## Timken Co. v. United States — January 11, 2005

After Polleys was written, and before consulting a fresh count or considering an author for this case, the legal form is one signed Court opinion following full merits submission. It has two genuine legal propositions: availability of Koyo's domestic statutory challenge despite the section 3512(c) defense, and permission for the identified zeroing method through actual delegated authority, including fair comparison and the considered international-law argument. The first is an antecedent review-channel question; the second resolves the method on its merits. The complete common ground supports affirmance on the granted zeroing issue and preserves the existing excluded cross-appeal disposition. All eight Associates support both propositions. The Chief expressly supports the merits proposition; his standing fallback supplies only agreement in the disposition of the separate section 3512(c) threshold component, on which Section II is silent. The opinion therefore needs an accurate partial-join topology but no separate Chief writing. One full-rationale author can carry both propositions; no split opinion or companion treatment is warranted. Counts have played no part in these choices.
