# Bates — pre-assignment opinion form

**Status:** Assembly draft; legal form fixed before any assignment count or author choice. The April 26 public-law refresh has been read and changes no material premise. The independent refresh receipt and sequential assignment remain pending. No Canonical Decision Record is written by this form.

**Event:** Bates v. Dow Agrosciences LLC, No. 03-388; April 27, 2005. All nine Justices participate. All support vacating the challenged preemption judgment and remanding. The governing warranty instruction has seven supporters and two opponents, as distinguished below.

## Form and governing propositions

One signed lead opinion resolves the properly presented FIFRA preemption question. Its divisions follow distinct legal questions rather than assignment considerations:

1. **Part I — actual substantive requirements.** A state damages duty may be a requirement, but section 136v(b) reaches only actual labeling or packaging duties different from or additional to federal duties. A manufacturer's possible commercial response to liability is not the legal duty. State sale/use authority retains section 136v(a)'s express federal limitation.
2. **Part II — independent conduct.** Genuine design, manufacture and testing duties fall outside the labeling category. The statutory definitions also exclude an actual duty governing oral sales representations, even when those representations repeat label content. Actual duty classification controls mixed claims. The complete referenced-publication exception accompanies the definition; genuine warning obligations remain subject to Part IV.
3. **Part III — voluntary express undertakings.** An enforceable voluntary express warranty is outside the state-imposed substantive labeling category even when the promise appears on the label. A DTPA theory shares this protection to the extent it remedies that surviving warranty. The contrary view would require comparison of label-based promises with federal obligations; the lead answers that the manufacturer's undertaking supplies the promised performance.
4. **Part IV — substantive equivalence.** Label-based fraud and nonwarranty DTPA duties require actual comparison with applicable federal misbranding duties. Additional recovery elements may narrow a claim without changing the conduct required. Directions, warnings and misleading statements retain their distinct statutory predicates. Authorized EPA regulations may specify the comparator within statutory and administrative limits. Jury instructions must confine recovery to genuinely equal label obligations. The express Appendix 192 disclaimer receives its effect under ordinary pleading rules; remand itself supplies no entitlement to amend.
5. **Part V — registration, independent conflict and operative remand.** Registration retains its statutory prima facie effect but is not conclusive immunity. Efficacy-data waiver does not eliminate the misbranding obligation. The proponent of independent preemption must identify an operative federal command or supported regulatory method and an incompatible state duty. A litigation preference or abstract uniformity objective is insufficient. The breach-of-contract abandonment and unappealed limitation-of-remedies ruling on the distinct presale representation remain effective.

One separate opinion, joined by Scalia and Thomas, concurs in part and dissents in part. It accepts the common textual rules and comparison remand, but objects that state liability for a federally compliant label promise can itself impose an additional labeling requirement. It would also compare warranty-derived DTPA duties. Its author is left for finalization; no historical authorship is adopted by default. Breyer's supported qualification about authorized agency specifications is incorporated in Part IV and creates no separate-writing function.

The lead omits reliance on a presumption against preemption or congressional silence because its common statutory reasoning fully supports the result. The omission answers Scalia's and Thomas's recorded reasoning boundary without changing either Justice's frozen outcome or warranty objection. It does not convert them into full-opinion joiners.

## Chief Justice compatibility and eligibility

Stone-Zsela's approved supplement expressly adopts the actual-duty, parallel-duty and independent-conflict framework; restores genuine nonlabel safety and voluntary-warranty theories; requires specific federal-source comparison; preserves independent dismissals; and requires protective jury instructions. Parts I–V implement that stated framework, with the oral-representation classification applying its expressly endorsed nonlabel rule to the statutory definition. No new failure-to-warn count is created. No standing fallback is used, and no unsupported policy or independent liability ground is attributed to him.

Eligible full-lead authors are Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Each supports every proposition of the integrated lead. Scalia and Thomas are ineligible to deliver the whole lead because their Part III objection remains. One eligible Justice can carry every part, so the legal form requires no division of authorship. The Chief assigns from the relevant judgment coalitions after the governing sequential count; this file chooses no author and states no tally from preceding opinions.
