## Proposition that receives support

If the lower court establishes an unlawful operation of the county policy and the requirements for the relief under consideration are satisfied, the relief must precisely correct that adjudicated unlawful operation and protect the persons governed by that operation unless the county establishes that narrower relief fully cures the proved violation. The decree must preserve independently lawful safety conditions. Every requirement for preliminary relief remains applicable at that stage.

The reference to persons governed by the policy is confined to the adjudicated unlawful operation being remedied. It is not a direction to protect every person from every application of an otherwise partly lawful policy. The county's narrower-relief showing concerns the adequate scope of a remedy after its predicate is established; it does not transfer the claimants' burden to establish standing, protected activity, the constitutional defect or the requirements for preliminary relief. The current remand establishes none of those still-unmade findings.

## Existing rule and the actual alternative

Anderson directs precise declaratory and injunctive relief against adjudicated unlawful policy operation. Its judgment requires protection of candidates and speakers governed by the challenged policies unless the State shows narrower relief fully cures the proved violation. Its opinion topology expressly distinguishes that general implementing remedy, joined by all eight Associates, from the separate polling-place rationale on which Scalia and Thomas withheld assent. It also requires preservation of independently lawful safeguards and rejects rewriting the governing statute through an unsupported universal construction.

The present conditional instruction retains that rule's actual trigger and remedial limit. It addresses a government policy's adjudicated unlawful operation, requires a remedy adequate to cure the proved defect, permits narrower relief when adequate, and preserves lawful applications and conditions. Its application to a county rather than a State does not change the legal function of those directions. Nor does its conditional place in an interlocutory remand establish the violation or dispense with the additional requirements of preliminary relief. The underlying Second Amendment standard still comes from Love, Wright and the other governing arms decisions, with the previously recorded differences in renewed rationale support.

The strongest objection is that Anderson followed actual merits invalidations whereas Nordyke presently corrects an erroneous rights premise and lacks burden and equitable findings. That distinction forbids an immediate Anderson-style injunction here. It does not prevent stating what follows if the lower court supplies the missing adjudication and satisfies the prerequisites for relief. The instruction is therefore supported only in its conditional form.

A materially broader proposition would be different: requiring relief for persons outside the adjudicated unlawful operation; invalidating every county-property application from the present legal error; presuming that all display or acquisition restrictions are unlawful; shifting liability or preliminary-relief burdens to the county; or rewriting exemptions without lawful authority. None receives support from this audit. Those propositions are not necessary consequences of Anderson, and the frozen Nordyke rows contain concrete record and scope objections to them.
