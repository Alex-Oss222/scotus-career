## Entering law, grant and common rule

Dodd v. Hood River County, November 19, 1998, unanimously establishes that reserving a federal cause does not erase an identical issue actually and necessarily litigated with a full and fair opportunity under the rendering State's preclusion law. Its issue-specific holding does not establish that every state compensation case necessarily extinguishes every federal claim. Exxon Mobil, March 30, 2005, keeps original jurisdiction, appellate review and state-law preclusion distinct. Lingle, May 23, 2005, displaces lack of substantial advancement as an independent taking predicate while respecting final judgments and the surviving property rules.

The granted question is the proposed categorical §1738 exception. The particular application of California preclusion law and wholesale reconsideration of Williamson County are outside that grant. The official opinion confirms that limited scope. Lingle does not itself create an exception to recognition or a new independently presented claim; the historical efficacy theory cannot be republished as current substantive takings law.

All eight Associates would affirm and endorse this operative rule: Section 1738 requires the federal court to give the state judgment the issue-preclusive effect it receives under the rendering State's law. Seeking state compensation under Williamson County, or reserving a federal cause, does not create a categorical takings exemption for otherwise binding common issues. England protects the appropriate return for a reserved federal claim following abstention; it does not authorize relitigation of identical issues submitted to and resolved by the state court. The rule applies the actual preclusion conditions rather than treating the state forum or the takings label as sufficient by itself.

## Change from provisional handoff and separate-writing function

No Associate judgment changes. The provisional statement that no additional separate-writing function was established is qualified for O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas. Full historical review supplies concrete evidence of a substantive objection that the frozen neutral jurisprudence does not answer: the justification for requiring the initial state litigation, even though its completed judgment must receive statutory recognition. Dodd and Exxon Mobil do not displace that premise. The three would express that objection and favor reconsideration in an appropriate case, without changing the result here or deciding an ungranted question.

Their separate grounds distinguish the administrative final-decision requirement from the subsequent state-compensation-litigation requirement. Ordinary competence of state courts to decide federal rights does not explain why takings claimants in particular must begin there. The historically grounded comity treatment of state taxation does not by itself establish a parallel rule for land-use takings. Requiring state compensation litigation and then applying ordinary preclusion can practically foreclose a later federal district-court merits adjudication; that consequence warrants examination of the prerequisite, not judicial suspension of §1738. The absence of a request to reconsider the prerequisite, the limited grant and the lack of a remedy for these petitioners from reconsideration prevent that examination from becoming a holding here.

This is a substantive concurrence function, not a public disclaimer invented from an internal join barrier. The three endorse every proposition in the common statutory rule stated above. A Court opinion confined to that rule may receive their joins with the separate concurrence; a portion affirmatively defending Williamson County on broader state-expertise or comity reasoning may not. Assembly must record the actual proposition-level scope instead of importing the historical five-member opinion wholesale or counting a broader join that has not been supported.

**Historical departure assessment:** There is no departure in any Associate's judgment or in support for the no-exception rule. Any difference from the historical formal majority membership depends on the circulated opinion's changed scope: the common rule here is supported by the independently controlling Dodd holding and omits the broader defense of Williamson County that the three historically criticized. That scope can support agreement on the statutory holding while their distinct reconsideration position remains expressed. It cannot justify erasing that position.
