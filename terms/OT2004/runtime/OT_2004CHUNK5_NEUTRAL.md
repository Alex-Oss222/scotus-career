## 37. Shepard v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Shepard v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-9168; 544 U.S. 13
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of a federal sentencing judgment after a second appellate remand.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-07; historical decision-calendar date
* **Question Presented:** Whether police reports or complaint applications may establish that a guilty plea under a nongeneric burglary statute necessarily admitted generic burglary for the Armed Career Criminal Act.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The First Circuit, 348 F.3d 308, required consideration of the disputed materials and application of the enhanced sentence; the District Court had declined the fifteen-year minimum.
* **Relief Requested:** Shepard seeks reversal of the appellate direction to enhance his sentence; the United States seeks recognition of sufficient qualifying burglary predicates and resentencing under section 924(e).
* **Material Record:** Shepard pleaded guilty to federal firearm possession. The Government invoked Massachusetts burglary convictions entered on guilty pleas. The state statutes and complaints covered vehicles or vessels as well as buildings. The Government offered police reports and complaint applications identifying buildings, although the guilty pleas did not expressly adopt those accounts. The District Court rejected the proposed predicates; the First Circuit twice required a broader inquiry into what the state prosecutor and defendant understood the pleas to encompass. The ordinary Guidelines range identified below was thirty to thirty-seven months; ACCA would impose a fifteen-year minimum. The complete federal charging instrument and any enhancement-specific waiver must be verified before treating an allegation or admission as established.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is direct review of sentencing, not collateral attack on the state convictions. The statutory evidentiary question is preserved. A guilty plea to firearm possession does not itself admit every proposed recidivist fact. The scope and preservation of the separate federal charging objection require record examination, rather than an assumption that all ACCA allegations were either present or absent.
* **Relevant Dates:** The federal sentencing proceedings began before the current sentencing decisions. The First Circuit judgment under review is from 2003. Argument occurred November 8, 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** United States v. Booker concerns federal Guidelines operation. Shepard concerns statutory ACCA predicates and the contents of earlier state pleas; neither matter supplies the other with a factual admission.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** If an effective earlier Booker decision changes sentencing remedies or Guidelines operation, apply that decision to the ordinary sentencing range. No such decision is identified; Gatewood remains independently controlling for ACCA increases.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Federal sentencing on direct review must observe the applicable indictment, jury and admission requirements already established by the Court. Statutory construction and the legal significance of conviction documents are reviewed as legal questions.

##### Issue 1: Permissible conviction records and proof of qualifying predicates

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Gatewood requires applicable federal charging and unanimous jury proof beyond a reasonable doubt of disputed adult-recidivist facts that increase a maximum or mandatory minimum, absent a valid admission and any necessary charging waiver. It covers existence, identity, historical qualification and chronology. United States v. Angle and Harris v. United States foreclose treating a binding floor as an exception. Almendarez-Torres retains no constitutional prior-conviction exception under the governing law.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate legal classification of established facts from finding a disputed enhancement fact. A preserved omitted indispensable federal allegation cannot be supplied by notice or judicial factfinding. Admission must actually encompass the material predicate; silence about a police narrative is not an admission. Authority to conduct another enhancement proceeding must exist independently of an appellate remand.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. sections 922(g)(1) and 924(e); the Fifth Amendment federal indictment requirement and Sixth Amendment jury guarantee. Taylor v. United States supplies the surviving statutory generic-burglary definition and conviction-centered inquiry, subject to the subsequent constitutional holdings.
* **Material Other Authority:** Taylor v. United States distinguishes the statutory elements from relitigation of underlying conduct. Its narrow use of charging instruments and instructions identifies what a conviction necessarily established. Whether analogous plea materials suffice is the statutory issue here, not a license to find new conduct by a preponderance.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Gatewood already governs the constitutional allocation of recidivist factfinding. Taylor already excludes reliance on the label burglary where the state offense is broader than generic burglary. Neither makes an allegation in an unadopted police account a necessary component of a plea.
* **Open Point:** Which records reliably identify the elements necessarily admitted in a guilty plea, and what sentencing proceedings remain permissible on this particular federal charging and waiver record.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The First Circuit allowed sufficiently reliable evidence of a shared understanding that the defendant admitted generic burglary. The United States emphasizes that the police descriptions concern buildings and that excluding them can prevent recognition of convictions whose real conduct was violent. Shepard answers that a plea admits the charge and agreed facts, not every investigative allegation, and that Taylor avoids unreliable retrospective mini-trials. The Government must also confront Gatewood as entering law; an argument resting on an unrestricted prior-conviction exception cannot control. Nothing verified establishes that Shepard expressly adopted all of the police accounts.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reversal may prohibit police-report reconstruction and remand for a lawful sentence on established predicates. If permissible judicial documents conclusively establish qualifying elements, the sentencing court must still resolve the independent Gatewood charging and proof questions. Affirmance of a limited document inquiry would not itself authorize judicial resolution of disputed facts under a lesser standard. A remand cannot guarantee the old sentence because ordinary sentencing, lawful departures and the federal charging record remain distinct.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A plea-record rule can extend Taylor without reopening the validity of old convictions. A broader constitutional exception for prior convictions is unavailable without expressly changing Gatewood. The Court may leave a concrete resentencing calculation to the District Court after stating all controlling constraints.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The indictment and any waiver colloquy must be examined on remand. The record identifies police allegations, not an express admission to them. Any later effective Booker decision must be checked before calculating the unenhanced sentencing range.

#### E. SOURCES

[Judicial opinion and procedural record](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/544/13/); [official United States Reports volume 544](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/544bv.pdf).

## 38. Ballard v. Commissioner

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Ballard v. Commissioner
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-184, consolidated with No. 03-1034; 544 U.S. 40
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of judgments affirming Tax Court deficiency and fraud determinations.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-07; historical decision-calendar date
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Tax Court may withhold a special trial judge's original report from the parties and appellate record while entering a final decision attributed to that judge under Rule 183.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit affirmed in Ballard, 321 F.3d 1037; the Seventh Circuit affirmed the related Estate of Kanter judgment, 337 F.3d 883. Both accepted exclusion of the original report.
* **Relief Requested:** The taxpayers seek disclosure and legally proper review of the initial report, with reversal of judgments resting on the undisclosed procedure. The Commissioner seeks affirmance without disclosure.
* **Material Record:** Special Trial Judge Couvillion heard extensive testimony concerning disputed income and alleged tax fraud involving Ballard, Kanter and related transactions. A regular Tax Court judge entered an opinion stating that the special trial judge's opinion was adopted. The original submitted report was withheld. The taxpayers challenged whether the public opinion accurately reflected the hearing judge's original findings and whether the required deference to credibility findings had been observed. The contents of the initial report are not established; allegations that it favored the taxpayers are not findings. The published final decision and the existence of an earlier submitted report are the relevant procedural facts.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The consolidated petitions concern the same procedure in separable tax judgments. Final appellate judgments permit review. The Court need not decide the ultimate tax deficiencies or fraud findings to determine whether the record permitted lawful review. Confidential taxpayer information may require targeted protection, but no such concern establishes a wholesale deliberative privilege over the operative Rule 183 report.
* **Relevant Dates:** The final Tax Court opinion was issued in 1999; the appellate judgments followed in 2003. Argument was December 7, 2004. Rule 183's earlier revision removed automatic party service language but retained submission, credibility and presumptive-correctness requirements.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Estate of Kanter is included only as a consolidated judgment component of this listed matter; it is not separately briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 26 U.S.C. sections 7443A and 7482 authorize the special-trial-judge structure and appellate review. Rule 183 distinguishes the hearing judge's report from the deciding judge's subsequent action. The Court can resolve the rule question before a broader constitutional challenge.

##### Issue 1: Original report, independent review and a reviewable decisional record

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling decision adopts the Tax Court's challenged secret-report practice or supplies a contrary construction of Rule 183. This rule-specific question remains open. General judicial review cannot convert an unproved allegation about an unseen report into a factual finding.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the legally required decision stages, the standard governing the second stage, and the record necessary to test compliance. A report required to carry a presumption and special credibility weight cannot be treated as if no distinct report existed. Confidentiality and harmlessness require an identified basis, not a conclusory assurance that the final result is correct.
* **Other Governing Law:** Tax Court Rule 183(b) requires a report containing findings and opinion. Rule 183(c) requires due regard for the hearing judge's credibility opportunity and presumes factual findings correct, while allowing the regular judge to adopt, modify or reject the report. Section 7460(b) separately concerns review of a regular judge by the full Tax Court.
* **Material Other Authority:** The statutory structures for magistrate reports and administrative initial decisions demonstrate familiar ways to preserve both initial findings and final decisional authority. They are analogies, not direct statutory commands governing the Tax Court. The taxpayer challenge also invokes due process, which need not be reached if the existing rule disposes of the practice.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The deciding Tax Court judge retains ultimate authority. Neither that authority nor the deletion of an automatic service provision answers whether a distinct report may be concealed when the governing rule gives it legal weight.
* **Open Point:** Whether Rule 183 permits undisclosed revision that leaves parties and appellate courts unable to identify the original findings or measure the required deference.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The taxpayers argue that an appellate court cannot enforce presumptive correctness or credibility deference without seeing the initial findings. They seek an actual reviewable report, not automatic victory on fraud. The Commissioner argues that the final opinion is the court's decision, that the 1983 rule amendment ended required disclosure, and that internal collaboration resembles other judicial deliberations. The courts below accepted the institutional explanation and distinguished the report from evidence. A material alternative is to demand compliance with Rule 183 without deciding due process.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reverse both appellate judgments and require disclosure followed by fresh Tax Court consideration and appellate review. It may conclude that the rule permits withholding and then address preserved statutory or constitutional objections. A confidentiality ruling may regulate access but cannot by itself resolve whether the findings received the required weight. A finding that the unseen report was necessarily harmless would require facts not presently established.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule-based holding can require preservation and disclosure of the submitted report while leaving the regular judge free to reject supported findings for stated reasons. A broader ruling on constitutional access to all internal judicial materials exceeds what this record necessarily presents.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The undisclosed report cannot be described as favorable or unfavorable as an established fact. The detailed tax liabilities and any changes after lawful reconsideration remain for the Tax Court. The record does not show that a narrower confidentiality order would be inadequate.

#### E. SOURCES

[Ballard and consolidated Kanter opinions, including Rule 183 and statutory text](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/544/40/); [official United States Reports volume 544](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/544bv.pdf).

## 39. Wilkinson v. Dotson

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Wilkinson v. Dotson
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-287; 544 U.S. 74
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of an en banc judgment allowing two prisoners' civil-rights actions to proceed.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-07; historical decision-calendar date
* **Question Presented:** Whether state prisoners may use section 1983 to seek new constitutionally adequate parole proceedings when success would not necessarily require immediate or earlier release.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit, sitting en banc, 329 F.3d 463, reversed dismissal of the actions brought by Dotson and Johnson.
* **Relief Requested:** Ohio officials seek dismissal in favor of exclusive habeas proceedings. The prisoners seek permission to litigate claims for prospective declarations and new parole consideration, without an order releasing them.
* **Material Record:** Dotson began a life sentence in 1981. A 2000 review applied Ohio's 1998 guidelines and deferred further consideration for at least five years. Johnson began a ten-to-thirty-year sentence in 1992 and was denied parole in 1999 under those guidelines. Dotson challenges retroactive guideline use; Johnson additionally challenges panel composition and the opportunity to speak. Their requested new reviews would leave the parole authority able to deny release in the exercise of lawful discretion. Neither complaint seeks restoration of earned credits or invalidation of a conviction.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The question concerns the available procedural vehicle, not whether either plaintiff has an enforceable parole liberty interest or has established an ex post facto violation. Their actual requested relief must remain prospective procedure, rather than a disguised order reducing sentence duration. Independent exhaustion of available prison remedies and other applicable defenses remain separate.
* **Relevant Dates:** The challenged guidelines were adopted in 1998, after both crimes and sentences. The actions were dismissed in 2000; the en banc judgment issued in 2003. Argument occurred December 6, 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Nelson v. Campbell involved a proposed execution procedure compatible with a valid sentence. These prisoners seek parole consideration compatible with continued custody, rather than a change to the execution method.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Section 1983 supplies an action for deprivation of federal rights under color of state law. The federal habeas statutes govern relief from unlawful custody. Classification depends on what success necessarily establishes, not the claimant's hope of eventual release.

##### Issue 1: Boundary between a procedural civil-rights claim and a custody challenge

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Heck v. Humphrey requires qualifying invalidation for an in-custody claim whose success necessarily invalidates the operative conviction or sentence. Edwards v. Balisok applies that rule to the actual effect on lost good-time credits while preserving genuinely compatible prospective relief. Muhammad v. Close rejects a prison-context shortcut where the claim leaves conviction, sentence and release-related determinations valid. Nelson v. Campbell permits a compatible procedure claim without bypassing independent remedial requirements.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Compare the elements and requested relief with the legal basis for confinement. Ask whether success necessarily invalidates the conviction, sentence or custody-producing determination, or necessarily commands release or reduced confinement. A possible later exercise of official discretion favoring the prisoner is insufficient. Procedural labels cannot save a claim whose necessary effect is invalidation.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. section 1983; 28 U.S.C. sections 2241 and 2254; relevant Prison Litigation Reform Act requirements. Preiser v. Rodriguez and Wolff v. McDonnell retain the distinction between relief in the core of custody and independent procedural claims.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Ex Post Facto and Due Process Clauses identify the asserted underlying rights, but the granted vehicle question does not establish their violation. The existence of a parole system does not itself prove an entitlement to release.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The cases already reject a categorical exclusion of all prisoner suits connected to duration. They require examination of the particular claim. The parole setting is an application needing an express answer about discretionary new hearings.
* **Open Point:** Whether these challenges to parole eligibility and suitability procedures necessarily invalidate confinement where the decisionmaker retains discretion to deny parole after a lawful hearing.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Sixth Circuit treated a discretionary new hearing as compatible with the existing sentence. Ohio argues that parole rules are part of the legal measure of confinement, that the prisoners seek earlier release in substance, and that section 1983 would evade habeas exhaustion. The prisoners answer that the requested judgment would leave both sentences and the parole authority's lawful discretion intact. Their position does not entail an entitlement to release or exemption from the PLRA. The materially limiting fact is discretion after a new hearing, not the wording of the complaints alone.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance permits the complaints to proceed to ordinary screening, exhaustion questions and adjudication of the asserted rights. It does not award new hearings immediately. A finding that a particular requested declaration necessarily invalidates custody would require dismissal or proper treatment under habeas procedures, rather than an automatic conversion that prejudices future petitions. The Court could distinguish eligibility from suitability only if state law makes their effects materially different; the present pleadings do not establish an automatic-release consequence for either.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The rule may cover procedures beyond parole only where success remains legally compatible with existing custody. A rule based simply on whether a prisoner desires earlier release would extend the habeas bar to claims that the current cases preserve.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The full parole rules matter to the eventual constitutional claims and any requested injunction. The record establishes the discretionary character relevant here, but does not prove a constitutional entitlement to a particular outcome or a permissible statewide remedy.

#### E. SOURCES

[Wilkinson pleadings, proceedings and opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/544/74/); [official United States Reports volume 544](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/544bv.pdf).

## 40. Back v. Hastings on Hudson Union Free School District

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Back v. Hastings on Hudson Union Free School District
* **Citation or Docket:** 365 F.3d 107 (2d Cir. 2004)
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of a federal appellate judgment vacating summary judgment in part.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-07; scheduled merits date
* **Question Presented:** Whether a public employer’s reliance on stereotypes that a mother of young children cannot remain committed to her job can establish intentional sex discrimination under the Equal Protection Clause without evidence of how similarly situated fathers were treated? Whether the prohibition against such sex stereotyping was sufficiently clearly established to deny the individual supervisors qualified immunity on the plaintiff’s version of the facts?
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit vacated summary judgment for supervisors Ann Brennan and Marilyn Wishnie and remanded for trial; it affirmed judgment for the school district and Superintendent John Russell.
* **Relief Requested:** The supervisors seek restoration of summary judgment, including immunity. Back seeks to preserve a trial on her individual equal-protection claims and the possibility of damages and appropriate employment relief.
* **Material Record:** Elana Back was hired as a school psychologist in 1998 on a three-year tenure track. The summary-judgment record includes strong early evaluations, maternity leave, and later criticism near the tenure decision. Back testified that her supervisors questioned whether a mother with young children could perform the job, predicted diminished commitment after tenure, and linked those assumptions to their recommendation against tenure. Defendants asserted organizational and interpersonal deficiencies. Tenure was denied in 2001. These are disputed evidentiary accounts, not findings that discrimination occurred; the reviewing court must credit Back's supported version at this stage.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Section 1983 requires intentional constitutional discrimination, personal involvement and causation. No special constitutional suspect class of all caregivers is necessary to a claim of discrimination because of sex. The appeal does not establish municipal policy or Russell's discriminatory involvement; the affirmed components must not be reopened without a proper review basis.
* **Relevant Dates:** The alleged comments occurred in 2000 and 2001, and tenure was denied in 2001. The Second Circuit decided April 7, 2004. Scheduled review date: March 7, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material to the question presented.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** An effective earlier Brosseau v. Haugen decision could alter the qualified-immunity defense and its transition rule. None is identified. Harlow and Anderson remain the governing framework.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Summary judgment cannot resolve supported credibility conflicts. Qualified immunity is assessed against clearly established law when the defendants acted. Later cases can explain the merits without retroactively providing conduct-date notice.

##### Issue 1: Intentional sex stereotyping and the need for a comparator

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Virginia forbids state action resting on overgeneralizations about women's capacities and roles and requires a genuine important purpose with a substantial relation when sex classifications are used. Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs recognizes the constitutional problem of state employment decisions grounded in assumptions about women's family responsibilities. Section 1983 still requires each defendant's responsibility for the intentional deprivation.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Back must produce evidence permitting a reasonable inference that each defendant caused materially adverse treatment because of sex. Under the indirect framework, a prima facie showing shifts only the burden of producing a legitimate explanation; Back retains persuasion by a preponderance. Direct sex-role statements and the surrounding record can establish the inference without a male comparator. Disbelief of an explanation permits, but does not compel, a discrimination finding.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Equal Protection Clause; 42 U.S.C. section 1983. Title VII evidentiary concepts may assist the analysis, but this case asserts an independent constitutional claim against state actors.
* **Material Other Authority:** Price Waterhouse v. Hopkins explains sex stereotyping in employment; United States v. Virginia supplies the constitutional prohibition. St. Mary’s Honor Center v. Hicks preserves employer production and employee persuasion without requiring additional direct evidence; Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products preserves reasonable inferences from the whole record. These evidentiary principles do not merge Title VII and §1983.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The constitutional prohibition already reaches official adverse action based on sex-role stereotypes. The evidentiary question is whether proof of differently treated fathers is mandatory despite direct evidence of the decisionmakers' assumptions.
* **Open Point:** Whether the combination of maternal-role statements and disputed performance explanations suffices without an actual male comparator.

##### Issue 2: Conduct-date qualified immunity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Hope v. Pelzer requires fair warning without an identical factual precedent. The Saucier v. Katz does not impose a mandatory merits-first sequence or create an additional reasonableness defense. Existing Harlow and Anderson principles remain operative.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the concrete prohibited conduct on the plaintiff-favorable record, then ask whether law effective in 2000 and 2001 gave reasonable officials fair notice. Later decisions cannot supply missing notice. Immunity does not authorize resolving contested facts in the defendant's favor.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1983 individual-capacity liability and the existing qualified-immunity defense; no statutory immunity specific to tenure recommendations is identified.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Virginia predates the conduct. Earlier sex-stereotyping employment law helps define fair warning. Hibbs, decided in 2003, is not an independent source of earlier notice.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The governing framework permits denial of immunity where the application of an established constitutional prohibition is apparent without a factually identical school case.
* **Open Point:** Whether the prohibition was sufficiently clear for supervisors allegedly making an adverse recommendation because motherhood would reduce commitment.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court viewed the absence of a similarly situated man as fatal. The Second Circuit treated maternal stereotypes as potential sex discrimination and found sufficient evidence against Brennan and Wishnie, but not the other defendants. The supervisors emphasize documented performance deficiencies, the absence of comparator evidence and asserted uncertainty about caregiver claims. Back emphasizes the explicit link between motherhood, anticipated commitment and the tenure recommendation. Her claim must remain one of sex discrimination, not a claim that all decisions affecting parents are unconstitutional.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirming the partial reversal permits trial against the two supervisors; it does not grant tenure or establish damages. Reversal on immunity could terminate their damages exposure while leaving any properly pleaded prospective claim to separate analysis. A no-comparator rule could dispose of the constitutional claim, but would need to justify why direct evidence cannot suffice. The unchallenged affirmed judgments remain intact.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The evidence rule reaches intentional sex-role discrimination without creating a general caregiver classification. The immunity ruling must be tied to conduct-date law and the supported facts, not to current hindsight.
* **Material Uncertainty:** Review is confined to the two identified issues concerning Brennan and Wishnie; the other defendants’ judgments remain outside those issues. Performance, motive and individual causation remain disputed.

#### E. SOURCES

[Second Circuit judicial opinion and record summary](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/365/107/608990/).

## 41. Muehler v. Mena

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Muehler v. Mena
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-1423; 544 U.S. 93
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of an appellate judgment sustaining a jury award against officers.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-22; historical decision-calendar date
* **Question Presented:** Whether officers executing a weapons search warrant may detain an occupant in handcuffs during the search and question her about immigration status without separate reasonable suspicion.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit, 332 F.3d 1255, affirmed a special-verdict judgment for Mena on unreasonable force and duration and treated immigration questioning as an additional constitutional violation.
* **Relief Requested:** The officers seek reversal of liability and immunity rulings. Mena seeks to retain the verdict or have the remaining force and prolonged-detention grounds considered under the correct standards.
* **Material Record:** On February 3, 1998, officers searched a residence under a warrant for weapons and gang evidence, believing an armed gang member lived there. A tactical team handcuffed Mena and three other occupants and held them in a converted garage guarded by one or two officers while the search continued. Mena remained cuffed for roughly two to three hours and was questioned about immigration status; her permanent-resident status was confirmed. A jury found excessive force and excessive duration and awarded $60,000 total damages against the two officers. Those findings are verdict facts subject to the appropriate review standard, not allegations that an appellate court may simply reweigh.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Separate the authority to detain, the intensity and continuation of restraints, the effect of questioning, and any detention after the search. Qualified immunity concerns clearly established law on February 3, 1998. The appellate court did not decide the alternative post-search detention theory. Neither a valid warrant nor lawful initial force determines every later moment of restraint.
* **Relevant Dates:** The search occurred in February 1998; the appellate judgment under review issued in 2003. Argument was December 8, 2004. Current merits law and conduct-date immunity must be distinguished.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Illinois v. Caballes concerns a traffic stop and a dog sniff. This case concerns residential search detention, handcuffs and questions; a later effective rule about added duration could matter, but no effective ruling is identified.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier effective Illinois v. Caballes decision must be checked for a prolongation rule, and any effective Brosseau v. Haugen decision for immunity and transition. Neither is identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The posture is review of a jury verdict and immunity ruling, not fresh factfinding. Legal errors may require remand, while supported verdict findings and preserved independent grounds require proper appellate treatment.

##### Issue 1: Detention authority, continuing force and search completion

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Michigan v. Summers permits detention of occupants during a proper premises search. Graham v. Connor separately governs objective reasonableness of force. Holland ex rel. Overdorff v. Harrington requires attention to planned force and continued restraint after control is secured; an initially justified safety measure does not justify unlimited continuation. It does not impose a least-intrusive-tactics requirement.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the lawful detention purpose, known safety risks, manner and duration of restraint, and changes as the search progresses. Continued restraints require an objectively reasonable relation to the actual conditions. Later-discovered evidence does not retroactively justify earlier force. Each defendant's causal role remains necessary.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourth Amendment; 42 U.S.C. section 1983. Summers is limited to detention incident to the lawful search and does not authorize custody after that justification ends without another adequate basis.
* **Material Other Authority:** The existing qualified-immunity framework requires conduct-date fair warning. Holland is current merits law but cannot itself establish what officers knew or should have known in February 1998.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Authority to keep an occupant present is distinct from authority to use handcuffs throughout. Holland already rejects automatic continuation of severe restraints after the factual need dissipates, while leaving ordinary safety judgments to objective review.
* **Open Point:** How those principles apply to this jury record, including the continuing weapons risk, four detainees, available guards and alleged detention beyond search completion.

##### Issue 2: Immigration questions and an additional seizure

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The Fourth Amendment regulates searches and seizures, not the topic of every question. Existing consensual-questioning principles do not demand reasonable suspicion for a request for information that adds no detention or other coercive intrusion. A person's already lawful detention does not authorize an extension for an unrelated investigation.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Ask what additional restraint, duration or compelled intrusion the questioning produced. A mere change of conversational subject is not an additional seizure. If questions prolong custody beyond its lawful justification, that added detention requires its own legal basis.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourth Amendment. Immigration status is not itself a substitute for individualized authority to extend a search detention. No independent Fifth Amendment compelled-testimony claim is adjudicated here.
* **Material Other Authority:** The record must identify the temporal and coercive effects of the questions.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing distinction between questioning and restraint supplies the general framework. Whether questioning actually extended detention is a factual application, not a presumed effect of asking about immigration.
* **Open Point:** Whether the Ninth Circuit could find a separate Fourth Amendment violation solely from unrelated questions without an added seizure.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Ninth Circuit reasoned that Mena should have been released from restraints once she posed no immediate threat and treated unrelated immigration questioning as independently unjustified. The officers invoke the weapons warrant, suspected armed gang occupants, limited guards and the operational need to maintain control. Mena emphasizes lengthy restraint of a compliant person, the jury's findings and detention allegedly continuing beyond completion of the search. The lawful alternatives are not limited to releasing her entirely or approving every hour in handcuffs: detention and force must be analyzed separately. No verified oral concession establishes that the questions never prolonged custody.

The United States, as amicus supporting the officers, urges reversal on the merits: the weapons-search risks justified handcuffs for the search’s duration, and questions that did not prolong an otherwise lawful detention caused no additional seizure. Its assertion that no added delay occurred must be tested against the actual record. Its position does not itself erase the jury’s force findings or supply a immunity rule.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Vacatur may correct the categorical questioning rule and require review of the force and duration findings under the appropriate standards. If a supported independent ground sustains liability, the court must consider its effect on the damages judgment; if an erroneous theory infected the award, a properly limited new trial may be necessary. Qualified immunity could defeat individual damages on a genuinely unresolved conduct-date legal question, but cannot be granted by retrospectively recasting disputed verdict facts.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court can distinguish lawful detention, reasonable force and added investigative detention without announcing a universal handcuff time limit. A broad warrant-based approval of all continuing force would require changing Holland rather than merely applying Summers.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The complete jury instructions, evidence and allocation of damages among theories are needed for the final remand disposition. The alternative overlong-detention ground was not resolved below. Those gaps preclude automatic restoration or cancellation of the whole award.

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Rancho Palos Verdes v. Abrams
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-1601; 544 U.S. 113
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of an appellate decision authorizing section 1983 relief for violations of the Telecommunications Act's siting provisions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-22; historical decision-calendar date
* **Question Presented:** Whether the limitations on local wireless-facility siting authority in 47 U.S.C. section 332(c)(7) may also be enforced through section 1983 despite the express expedited judicial remedy.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit, 354 F.3d 1094, allowed section 1983 enforcement after the District Court had granted relief under the Telecommunications Act but rejected the additional civil-rights remedy.
* **Relief Requested:** The City seeks reversal of the additional section 1983 route, including its consequences for damages and attorney's fees. Abrams seeks to retain that route alongside the statutory action.
* **Material Record:** Abrams used antennas at his home for amateur and commercial communications. The City required a conditional-use permit for commercial operation and denied his application after hearings, citing visual and land-use concerns. He challenged the denial under section 332(c)(7) and section 1983. The District Court granted statutory relief directing permit approval but declined the separate section 1983 relief. The question under review concerns that added enforcement route, not a fresh determination that the antenna denial complied with the statute.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Separate existence of an individual federal right from congressional foreclosure of section 1983 as the enforcement channel. The case does not supply an independent constitutional claim merely because the statutory route is contested. The underlying siting remedy and the additional damages or fee issues are separable judgment components.
* **Relevant Dates:** The conditional-use application followed the City's 1999 enforcement action; the appellate judgment issued in 2004. Argument occurred January 19, 2005. The 1996 Act's specific judicial remedy governs the claim.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Sabree concerns asserted Medicaid entitlements and federal funding oversight, rather than an express individual judicial action with a thirty-day filing period. Any later decision in Sabree cannot govern this earlier event.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Section 1983 presumptively enforces qualifying federal rights unless Congress expressly forecloses that action or creates an incompatible remedial scheme. The inquiry must use the particular statute and claimed remedy, not a categorical equation between an express action and exclusivity.

##### Issue 1: Individual rights and incompatibility of the additional enforcement route

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Blessing v. Freestone requires a particular intended-beneficiary right, a binding obligation and a sufficiently definite entitlement, followed by a distinct foreclosure inquiry. Gonzaga University v. Doe restates those factors in a two-question framework: Congress must create the individual right, and a defendant must show that Congress excluded section 1983; it holds FERPA's administrative scheme not incompatible with section 1983. Alexander v. Sandoval under the governing law does not establish a blanket prohibition on private regulatory enforcement.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the precise statutory right. If a right exists, presume section 1983 enforcement unless the defendant shows express exclusion or a remedial structure incompatible with that route. Administrative oversight alone is insufficient; a detailed individual judicial mechanism and conflicting timing or remedies can perform materially different work.
* **Other Governing Law:** 47 U.S.C. section 332(c)(7)(B) limits specified local siting decisions. Subparagraph (v) provides judicial action within thirty days of final action or failure to act and expedited consideration. Section 601(c)(1) of the 1996 Act preserves other law except as expressly provided. Sections 1983 and 1988 supply the asserted additional action and fees.
* **Material Other Authority:** Middlesex County Sewerage Authority v. National Sea Clammers Association and Smith v. Robinson illustrate incompatible remedial structures at their surviving scope. Their application must remain tied to statutory design, rather than an inference from silence about damages alone.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law supplies the right-then-foreclosure sequence. The personal protections against specified siting decisions support the asserted right, but do not answer whether Congress made the specially timed judicial action its exclusive statutory enforcement channel.
* **Open Point:** Whether the thirty-day, expedited judicial mechanism and its remedial design are incompatible with a parallel section 1983 action, and how the saving clause affects that conclusion.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Ninth Circuit treated the statutory rights as enforceable through section 1983 and read the saving clause to preserve that action. Abrams argues that an express remedy does not ordinarily displace section 1983, that the statute lacks an express exclusion, and that a compatible limitations period could be borrowed. The City responds that a second route would defeat Congress's chosen short deadline and expeditious correction mechanism and introduce remedies not specified there. The City must show actual structural incompatibility, not rely on the mere presence of a remedy. Abrams's proposed borrowing also requires a legal basis; it cannot reconstruct an entirely different action solely to avoid foreclosure.

The United States supports the City and argues that the express action supplies meaningful injunctive relief even without an enumerated remedy list, that a scheme need not be complex to be comprehensive, and that its timing and fee limits are incompatible with section 1983. That would reverse the additional civil-rights route while preserving available direct statutory relief. A general proposition that any meaningful express action is necessarily exclusive would go beyond the statute-specific incompatibility inquiry in the governing law.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reversal can eliminate the section 1983 route while preserving the underlying statutory permit relief. Affirmance can allow the additional claim only after addressing the timing and remedy conflict. A right-denial holding would be broader than necessary where an express action already protects the applicant; it should not be confused with the narrower foreclosure question. Neither path decides independent constitutional causes of action without a properly presented claim.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A provision-specific exclusivity holding distinguishes an individualized expedited court remedy from general funding oversight. A universal rule that express remedies always exclude section 1983 would materially change Blessing and is not compelled by this record.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The exact direct-TCA damages question is not necessary to resolve the section 1983 route. The record does not establish a separate constitutional injury. The treatment of any specific fee request must follow its actual statutory basis.

## 43. Brown v. Payton

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Brown v. Payton
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-1039; 544 U.S. 133
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of an en banc judgment granting penalty-phase habeas relief after reconsideration under the Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996 (AEDPA).
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-22; historical decision-calendar date
* **Question Presented:** Whether the state court unreasonably applied clearly established mitigation law in rejecting a claim that the charge and prosecutor's argument prevented consideration of postcrime rehabilitation, and what relief follows if the statutory gate is met.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit, en banc, 346 F.3d 1204, again sustained habeas relief after applying section 2254(d). The California Supreme Court had rejected the mitigation claim in 1992.
* **Relief Requested:** The warden seeks reversal of the habeas judgment. Payton seeks relief from the death sentence based on the treatment of his mitigation, not invalidation of the underlying convictions.
* **Material Record:** The penalty defense presented eight witnesses describing Payton's religious conversion, ministry and beneficial conduct during roughly twenty-one months in custody. The prosecution presented severe aggravation, including the murder and assaults, earlier violent conduct and damaging statements. California's factor (k) instruction referred to circumstances extenuating the gravity of the crime. The prosecutor repeatedly argued that postcrime evidence did not fall within that instruction, while also arguing that the conversion deserved little weight. Defense counsel objected and sought a clarification; the judge told jurors that counsel's remarks were argument but did not expressly correct the asserted legal exclusion. The charge also directed consideration of all the evidence. The significance of that entire context, rather than any isolated phrase, is disputed.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The federal application was filed after AEDPA's effective date although preparatory counsel proceedings began earlier. Woodford v. Garceau supplies the applicable temporal rule. Section 2254(d)(1) and actual constitutional error are separate requirements; harmless error remains a third question. The substantive clearly-established-law date is November 23, 1992, the state merits adjudication, not March 2005.
* **Relevant Dates:** The crimes occurred in 1980. The state merits decision is People v. Payton, issued November 23, 1992. Federal merits proceedings followed AEDPA; the operative en banc decision issued in 2003. Argument occurred November 10, 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Later mitigation decisions may explain current review method but cannot become Supreme Court holdings available to the state court in 1992. They also do not change this case into an ineffective-assistance claim.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** A habeas court applies the objective statutory limits in Williams v. Taylor and Lockyer v. Andrade, preserves independent procedural gates, and then applies the constitutional harmless-error rule if the claim is properly reached.

##### Issue 1: AEDPA applicability and the state-decision-date merits gate

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Woodford v. Garceau applies the pertinent chapter 153 adjudication provisions to a post-April 24, 1996 writ application despite earlier preparatory counsel or stay proceedings. Williams v. Taylor separates contrary-to and unreasonable-application grounds and rejects a head count of judges as the test. Lockyer v. Andrade distinguishes legal unreasonableness from ordinary error and fixes clearly established substantive law at the relevant state merits decision.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** First identify whether the pre-enactment filing actually sought adjudication of a habeas claim. Then identify the Supreme Court holdings available at the state merits date and examine the state court's whole adjudication for an objectively unreasonable application. A general governing rule can be clearly established without a factual replica; disagreement alone does not establish unreasonableness.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. section 2254(d)(1), with the independent functions of sections 2254(d)(2) and (e) preserved. AEDPA does not convert the legal inquiry into a clear-and-convincing evidentiary burden.
* **Material Other Authority:** Lockett v. Ohio, Eddings v. Oklahoma, Skipper v. South Carolina and Boyde v. California predate the 1992 decision. Boyde examines reasonable likelihood that the jury understood the charge to prevent constitutionally relevant mitigation. Penry v. Johnson and Beard v. Banks postdate the state decision and cannot supply new substantive law for this statutory comparison.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The applicable statutory framework is settled. The remaining question is whether the state court's assessment of this charge and argument stayed within the range permitted by the earlier mitigation cases.
* **Open Point:** Whether the combination of ambiguous wording, an express prosecutorial exclusion and the absence of a specific correction makes the state court's no-exclusion conclusion objectively unreasonable.

##### Issue 2: Effective mitigation, harmlessness and sentence-only relief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The mitigation decisions require a lawful route for the sentencer to give relevant evidence its mitigating force, not admission without usable effect. Brecht v. Abrahamson requires the State to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that a properly reached preserved constitutional trial error did not contribute to the verdict or sentence. O'Neal v. McAninch preserves that burden; the historical lesser habeas harmlessness standard is not law here.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine whether there is a reasonable likelihood of prohibited exclusion in the whole charge and argument. If so, review the actual sentencing record for harmlessness under the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden, rather than ask whether a hypothetical properly instructed jury could impose death. Procedural default, exhaustion and other independent statutory gates remain.
* **Other Governing Law:** Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments; the applicable California penalty statute and charge; federal habeas remedial authority. California's later amendment of factor (k) does not cure this trial retroactively.
* **Material Other Authority:** Penry v. Johnson confirms the current distinction between admitting mitigation and authorizing a lawful life verdict, without furnishing the 1992 statutory benchmark. Beard v. Banks preserves the independent old-law, AEDPA and harmlessness inquiries.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Postcrime rehabilitation is relevant mitigation under the preexisting law. The sentencer may discount its sincerity or weight, but may not be told it is legally unavailable. The harmlessness burden is already controlled by Brecht and O’Neal.
* **Open Point:** Whether the error was harmless on the complete actual penalty record and the precise conditional sentence remedy if the State cannot meet its burden.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The state court read Boyde to permit consideration of postcrime character evidence and found no reasonable likelihood of exclusion in the whole proceeding. The warden emphasizes the instruction to consider all evidence, defense counsel's explanation and the prosecutor's alternative argument about weight, together with AEDPA deference. Payton emphasizes that the prosecutor expressly told jurors his only mitigation was legally unavailable and the judge never corrected that legal assertion. The en banc court found the state application unreasonable. A disagreement about the instruction alone is insufficient under AEDPA; a finding of error also does not eliminate the separate harmlessness question.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reversal could deny relief because the state application was reasonable even if a reviewing court would independently find error. Affirmance of the statutory and constitutional conclusions could lead to a conditional penalty writ if harmlessness is resolved against the State. A limited remand can require that separate determination under the beyond-reasonable-doubt rule. No path on this issue warrants acquittal, a new guilt trial or immediate release.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A contextual holding may distinguish Boyde without declaring factor (k) invariably unconstitutional. The remedy must follow the actual sentencing error and preserve independent habeas gates. The count of judges agreeing with either result does not determine objective reasonableness.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The complete trial record is required for final harmlessness. The earlier remand did not decide the mitigation merits. The substantive cutoff remains 1992 even though current review law includes later decisions.

The State disputes the treatment of election data, emphasizes successful minority-preferred candidates and argues that incumbency and population considerations explain the lines. * **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-1672; 544 U.S. 167

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit, 309 F.3d 1333, affirmed dismissal on the ground that the statute did not authorize this retaliation claim and Jackson was outside the protected beneficiary class.

He alleged ensuing negative evaluations and removal from coaching in May 2001, while he remained employed as a teacher and lost the coaching supplement. The complaint attributes retaliation to the Board. * **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The question concerns the scope of an established implied statutory action, not creation of a cause of action from a regulation alone. Standing follows the alleged personal job and pay injury. The claimant need not prove the girls' underlying discrimination at the pleading threshold, but must identify a complaint about sex discrimination and retaliatory causation. The employer may assert a genuine nonretaliatory explanation later.

Rancho Palos Verdes concerns a distinct statutory remedy structure; no proposed result there controls this action.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier effective decision changing the general enforcement framework must be checked. No such OT2004 decision is identified; the established Title IX action and recipient-responsibility rules remain controlling.

Gwinnett County Public Schools permits appropriate compensation for proved intentional discrimination, with recipient responsibility, causation and loss distinct. Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District rejects an exclusive actual-corrective-official-notice route in teacher cases; Davis v. A regulation may confirm the statutory construction but does not alone create a new private damages duty. Third-party harassment attribution requirements must not be mechanically transferred to a direct official act.
* **Other Governing Law:** 20 U.S.C. section 1681(a) prohibits discrimination on the basis of sex in covered education programs. The implementing regulation, 34 C.F.R. section 106.71, incorporates the antiretaliation rule in section 100.7(e). The recipient must have adequate notice of the funding condition under Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Alexander v. Sandoval preserves bounded enforcement of valid Title VI effects regulations under Powell v. Ridge; its historical no-action formulation is not controlling here. The present claim can be resolved directly from Title IX's intentional-discrimination prohibition and established action without extending that regulatory holding.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The private action and compensation for intentional statutory discrimination are settled. Whether intentional punishment for reporting sex discrimination is itself within the prohibition, including when the reporter is not the initial victim, requires an express construction.
* **Open Point:** The statutory meaning of retaliation, the reporter's protected status and the claim that recipients lacked notice of such liability.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Eleventh Circuit read the absence of an express retaliation clause as decisive and treated Jackson as outside Title IX's beneficiary class. The Board argues that retaliation is discrimination because of conduct rather than sex, that a regulation cannot create an action, and that funding recipients lacked clear notice of damages exposure. Jackson answers that retaliation for demanding sex equality is a deliberate means of denying the statute's protection and falls within the existing action; he claims his own employment injury. The notice argument must be evaluated against the statutory prohibition, established intentional-discrimination action and longstanding implementing rule, not against the absence of a case with the same job title. No verified oral-argument concession is assumed.

The United States supports Jackson. It reads the statutory discrimination prohibition itself to include purposeful retaliation, relies on the longstanding implementing regulation as confirmation, and invokes Sullivan v. Little Hunting Park as a pre-enactment analogy protecting a person who advocated for another discrimination victim. Its requested judgment is reversal of dismissal, not a damages finding. This statutory route is distinct from inventing a private action based solely on a regulation.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reversal permits factual development and proof of the recipient's own intentional retaliation. It does not establish the underlying athletics discrimination, restore the coaching position immediately or set damages. Affirmance would exclude the retaliation claim from the statutory action; it would not adjudicate any independently preserved constitutional or other employment claim. A regulation-only route is unnecessary if the statutory prohibition reaches the conduct.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A statutory retaliation holding can protect reporters beyond the original victims while requiring the complaint to concern sex discrimination and the adverse act to be causally retaliatory. The Court need not create a general employee-grievance action or rewrite the separate harassment-liability frameworks.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The case arrives on pleadings. The Board's actual role in the decision, federal funding coverage, available employment remedy and amount of loss must be established. The record does not justify a finding that all unequal treatment alleged by Jackson actually occurred.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-29; historical decision-calendar date
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Oneida Nation's reacquired parcels within the reservation acknowledged by the Treaty of Canandaigua are immune from local property taxation, including the effects of the Treaty of Buffalo Creek and the Nation's continuing status.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit, 337 F.3d 139, affirmed the relevant judgments protecting the Sherrill parcels from taxation and related enforcement. It separately remanded a Madison County matter on procedural grounds; that different parcel dispute is not silently included here.
* **Relief Requested:** Sherrill seeks authority to impose and enforce its property taxes on the reacquired parcels. The Nation seeks preservation of the declaration and injunction against those taxes and related eviction measures.
* **Material Record:** The federally recognized Nation bought the Sherrill parcels in ordinary market transactions in 1997 and 1998 and used them for commercial enterprises. They lie within the reservation acknowledged in 1794. The title history presented below traces a tribal conveyance to an individual Oneida in 1805 and a reconveyance to a non-Indian in 1807 without federal authorization. Local government taxed and governed the surrounding area for generations. When the Nation refused the new assessments, the City pursued collection and eviction, including tax-sale purchases. The legal effect of that history is disputed; the long period of non-Indian possession is not itself an enacted congressional authorization.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Distinguish land-tax liability from sovereign immunity against a particular collection action and from broader regulatory jurisdiction. The reviewed parcels and judgments must be identified individually. Current federal recognition and succession are material; an asserted loss of tribal continuity cannot be assumed from historical population movement. Any new equitable theory must receive adversarial consideration rather than be treated as already governing law.
* **Relevant Dates:** Treaty of Canandaigua, 1794; Treaty of Buffalo Creek, 1838; purchases, 1997 and 1998; Second Circuit judgment, July 21, 2003; argument, January 11, 2005. The tax controversy concerns current assessments after reacquisition, not an ejectment demand against the prior private sellers.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The separate Madison County parcels require their own record. Ancient land-damages litigation and any future trust acquisition are different remedies and transactions, not alternative captions for this tax case.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Federal treaty and statutory questions are reviewed independently on the established parcel record. The United States supports the Nation as amicus. A reservation boundary, parcel alienability, tribal recognition and tax authorization are distinct legal predicates.

##### Issue 1: Reservation continuity, parcel status and federal tax authorization

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Cass County v. Leech Lake Band permits taxation of parcels actually disposed into ordinary non-Indian fee through the specified Nelson Act program, with a parcel-specific statutory chain; it rejects a general alienability rule. County of Yakima likewise requires attention to the particular federal authorization and tax incidence. Hagen v. Utah and South Dakota v. Yankton Sioux Tribe preserve congressional intent as the source of diminishment. Minnesota v. Mille Lacs Band preserves treaty rights absent legally sufficient abrogation.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the treaty-protected reservation, any later congressional or treaty alteration, the Nation's legally recognized succession and the federal source authorizing taxation of each tribal parcel. The taxing authority must identify the authorization; ordinary fee form or long local practice alone is insufficient. A boundary finding does not automatically decide every tax on every owner.
* **Other Governing Law:** Treaty of Canandaigua, 7 Stat. 44; Treaty of Buffalo Creek, 7 Stat. 550; Nonintercourse Act, 25 U.S.C. section 177; the then-numbered trust-acquisition provision, 25 U.S.C. section 465. No congressional termination or disposal statute for these parcels is identified comparable to that in Cass County.
* **Material Other Authority:** Oneida Indian Nation v. County of Oneida and County of Oneida v. Oneida Indian Nation address surviving federal protection and a damages action, not this precise tax dispute. The 1838 treaty exchanges Wisconsin interests for western lands and requires close attention to its actual New York provisions. Federal recognition supplies a present institutional status; disputed historical accounts cannot substitute for a lawful recognition decision.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering cases reject the inference that any alienable fee land is taxable when tribally reacquired. They also do not create an exemption for every parcel a tribe buys anywhere. This case requires application to protected reservation land transferred without federal authorization.
* **Open Point:** Whether the particular treaties and title history preserve the relevant federal protection upon reacquisition, and whether any legally sufficient federal authorization supports these taxes.

##### Issue 2: Delay, reliance and effective prospective tax relief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling holding creates a general equitable loss of tribal tax protection from nonuse, local governance or demographic change. Existing equitable remedial principles can address the form of relief and actual reliance interests without rewriting the congressional source of taxing authority. A reservation right and the remedy for an ancient dispossession remain different questions.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the right, the presently challenged governmental act, the specific remedy and the actual prejudice from delay. A defense to disruptive retroactive possession relief does not automatically authorize new unlawful taxes on land the claimant already owns. Any equitable limit must leave a lawful and effective response to an established ongoing violation.
* **Other Governing Law:** The federal treaty and statutory protections remain superior to inconsistent local taxation. Section 465 supplies a federal trust-acquisition mechanism but contains no governing rule making that procedure the exclusive means of protecting every existing reservation right.
* **Material Other Authority:** The United States argues that treaty protection and present succession support the Nation. The City and supporting local governments emphasize settled governance, tax expectations and fragmented administration. Those concerns are material to remedy and political solutions, but are not themselves federal enactments.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The law permits attention to the actual relief requested. It does not already hold that a tribe must surrender existing statutory protection until it completes a new trust application.
* **Open Point:** Whether equity may bar prospective tax protection on these reacquired parcels despite the absence of federal authorization, and how to confine relief to the current dispute.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Second Circuit treated the treaty reservation as continuing and the reacquired parcels as protected, rejected disestablishment by the Buffalo Creek Treaty and found sufficient tribal continuity. The City argues that the reservation lacks continuing federal status, that the later treaty and historical changes ended the relevant protection, and that ordinary fee purchases should not disrupt local governance. The Nation and the United States emphasize the 1794 federal guarantee, unauthorized transfers, current recognition and the absence of congressional tax authorization. The United States specifically supports the Nation as successor to the treaty party and distinguishes the Wisconsin exchange in 1838. Equitable concerns must be assessed as an actual alternative ground, not an assumed holding already present in law.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance can protect these parcels from the challenged tax without resolving all regulatory consequences. Reversal could follow an identified federal termination or taxable disposal, but not the mere analogy to fee ownership in Cass County. A new equitable bar would require an express doctrinal holding and a remedy analysis explaining the legal basis for continued assessments. A record-based remand is appropriate only for an actual unresolved parcel predicate, not to invent authorization absent from the City's case.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A source-specific ruling may distinguish unauthorized historical transfers from congressionally completed taxable disposals. It need not establish a universal revival of all sovereign powers or a universal equitable forfeiture of treaty protection.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The case involves several related actions, so the mandate must identify the Sherrill judgments and exclude the separately remanded Madison County dispute. Collection immunity and the existence of a tax are distinct; a ruling on one should not silently resolve every possible enforcement device.

## 47. Sabree ex rel. Sabree v. Richman

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Sabree ex rel. Sabree v. Richman
* **Citation or Docket:** 367 F.3d 180 (3d Cir. 2004)
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of an appellate judgment reversing dismissal of a statutory civil-rights action.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-29; scheduled merits date
* **Question Presented:** Whether Medicaid’s requirements that eligible individuals receive covered intermediate-care services with reasonable promptness create individual rights enforceable under 42 U.S.C. §1983, or leave federal withholding of funds as the exclusive remedy for state noncompliance?
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit reversed dismissal of the claims under sections 1396a(a)(8), 1396a(a)(10) and 1396d(a)(15), leaving the merits and particular remedy to the District Court.
* **Relief Requested:** The Pennsylvania Secretary seeks dismissal for lack of an enforceable individual federal right or an available section 1983 route. The beneficiaries seek to proceed for prospective relief addressing the assistance for which they qualify.
* **Material Record:** Hassan Sabree, Catherine Meade and Joseph Frazier, acting through next friends, were eligible for the intermediate-care benefit included in Pennsylvania's Medicaid plan and alleged years on waiting lists without the assistance. The appellate record treats eligibility and prolonged waiting as undisputed for the issue presented. The remaining dispute at that stage concerns enforceability, not a completed determination of the precise services, payment obligations, clinical arrangements or timetable each person must receive. The suit names the responsible state official in her official capacity and seeks relief for similarly situated persons; class scope and any operative certification order must be verified.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Identify the conduct-date statutory version and the particular entitlement before considering an injunction. The 2005 definition of medical assistance referred to payment of covered costs; later statutory additions cannot be read backward. A recognized right to assistance does not automatically establish a right to any preferred facility, unlimited home services or disregard of lawful program conditions. Prospective officer relief and retrospective treasury damages are distinct.
* **Relevant Dates:** The District Court dismissed in 2003; the Third Circuit decided May 11, 2004. Scheduled review date: March 29, 2005. Later amendments to the medical-assistance definition are not date-eligible law.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Rancho Palos Verdes concerns an express individual judicial action with a thirty-day filing period. Medicaid's federal funding oversight and state fair hearing are materially different mechanisms. Any effective earlier decision must be applied according to that distinction.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** If an effective Rancho Palos Verdes decision has entered before this event, apply its actual foreclosure holding. No such decision is identified; a proposed statute-specific position does not establish that all express remedies foreclose section 1983.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The action invokes section 1983 and prospective relief against the Secretary. Blessing requires a provision-specific right inquiry followed by a separate congressional-foreclosure inquiry; the presence of a state plan does not end either.

##### Issue 1: The individual entitlement in the operative Medicaid provisions

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Blessing v. Freestone requires intended beneficiaries, a binding obligation and a sufficiently definite individual entitlement. It rejects a categorical denial because a provision appears in a state plan and preserves the distinction between individual assistance and aggregate program adequacy. Gonzaga University v. Doe holds that the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) nondisclosure provision secures an individual right enforceable through section 1983, restating Blessing's factors in a two-question framework. No general requirement of a separately intended private remedy displaces section 1983.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read each claimed provision and its statutory setting to identify the person, benefit, eligibility conditions and required governmental performance. A qualitative reasonable-promptness standard is not automatically too vague when it concerns an identifiable individual entitlement. A definition must be connected to an operative command; it does not independently create every claimed right.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. sections 1396a(a)(8), 1396a(a)(10) and 1396d(a)(15), as effective at this event; sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 prevent state-plan placement alone from defeating enforceability. The operative definition concerns payment for covered care, including the specified intermediate-care category. The later expansion of the medical-assistance definition is excluded from this analysis.
* **Material Other Authority:** Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association and Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority provide surviving examples of particular enforceable federal benefit obligations. They do not establish that every Medicaid administrative requirement is a private right. Gonzaga restates Blessing’s general rule and confirms that a funding condition can secure an individual entitlement.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing law already separates beneficiary-specific commands from aggregate funding standards. The clauses directing assistance to all eligible individuals and reasonable promptness present a concrete benefit rather than an entitlement to overall program success.
* **Open Point:** Whether the selected provisions, read together, create the particular individual assistance and timing rights asserted, and what the pre-amendment definition permits a court to require.

##### Issue 2: Enforcement channel and workable prospective relief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Blessing presumes section 1983 enforcement once a particular right is shown, unless Congress expressly excludes it or provides an incompatible comprehensive remedial scheme. General audits, corrective processes and funding sanctions alone do not suffice. Ex parte Young permits appropriate prospective relief against a responsible official; Pennhurst does not authorize federal enforcement of state law alone.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The Secretary must identify actual congressional foreclosure, not simply federal oversight. The beneficiaries must establish breach, causation, the responsible official's capacity to comply and the requirements for prospective relief. The scope of an injunction must match the proved individual or systemic violation and the operative class record.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1396c federal funding oversight; section 1396a(a)(3) state fair-hearing mechanism; section 1983. No express exclusion of the asserted federal action is identified. A state administrative hearing does not by itself become an exclusive federal judicial-remedy scheme.
* **Material Other Authority:** The separate direct action in the Telecommunications Act illustrates why statutory structures must be compared in detail. This case supplies no comparable thirty-day exclusive judicial track or remedial design whose operation a section 1983 action would displace.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law rejects categorical foreclosure based on agency oversight. Recognizing an enforcement channel still does not award immediate placement or allow a federal court to administer the whole Medicaid program without a proved need.
* **Open Point:** Whether the actual Medicaid mechanisms rebut the presumption and what proceedings are necessary to produce effective, legally bounded relief for proved unreasonable delay.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The District Court treated the statutes as insufficiently individual after Gonzaga. The Third Circuit emphasized commands benefiting eligible persons and found no exclusive remedial scheme. The Secretary stresses spending-program structure, federal funding oversight, state hearings and limits on the meaning of medical assistance. The beneficiaries identify their conceded eligibility, the specified covered category and years of delay. Their strongest position is provision-specific assistance with reasonable promptness, not a right to judicial redesign of all disability services. The statutory definition and the remedy are material limits even if the enforcement channel exists.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance revives the action for breach and remedy proceedings. It does not itself order institutional placement. Reversal could follow a failure to identify an individual right or actual congressional foreclosure, but a general spending-statute or state-plan objection contradicts the controlling rule. A narrower remedy after proof may require payment or another legally sufficient assistance arrangement; it must not simply repeat a statutory promise while leaving a proved delay without a compliance path.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court can recognize the particular assistance and timing rights while distinguishing them from aggregate administration and later statutory language. An injunction may ultimately address a proved common practice, but this pleading-stage judgment does not establish that practice or its class scope.
* **Material Uncertainty:** Class certification, individual benefit determinations, the actual cause of delay and compliance options require further record development. The Third Circuit expressly left remedy questions to the District Court. No later amendment may be used to resolve that reserved question by hindsight.
