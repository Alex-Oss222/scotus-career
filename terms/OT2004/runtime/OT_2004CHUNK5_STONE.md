## 37. Shepard v. United States

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Conditions:** Subject to any intervening effective decision governing the issue.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated rule, disposition and remedy.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the First Circuit judgment and remand for sentencing consistent with the conviction-record restriction and Gatewood.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Do not direct a fifteen-year minimum on the police materials. The District Court must identify lawful predicates, applicable charging and admission facts, and its independent authority for any further enhancement proceeding. If the prerequisites are absent, sentence within the otherwise authorized range; do not automatically reinstate a numerical sentence whose full basis has not been reviewed.
* **Remedial Reach:** The criminal judgment concerns Shepard. No class or policy injunction is sought.
* **Practical Relief:** The sentencing record must list the actual documents and admissions supporting each proposed predicate and show the legal basis for the authorized range. Unsupported predicates cannot raise either boundary. A new enhancement trial is unavailable merely because the appellate court remands.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Permissible conviction records and proof of qualifying predicates

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory record question because it is the basis of the sentence under review, and enforce Gatewood when specifying the lawful remand. Do not adjudicate the validity of the old state convictions.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can unadopted police accounts turn a broad state burglary plea into a qualifying ACCA conviction, and can a judge supply the disputed qualification?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing constitutional law and extend Taylor to the plea-record setting through an express statutory holding.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The recurring plea-document problem warrants a rule for ACCA cases, with Gatewood supplying the constitutional allocation. A broader rule governing every use of criminal history is unnecessary. A case-only ruling would leave the same unsupported reconstruction available in the next plea case.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An ACCA court may identify what a prior guilty plea necessarily established from conclusive judicial plea records, but may not transform unadopted police allegations into admitted generic-burglary elements. The Government must identify the statute, charge, written plea terms, plea colloquy or a comparable express judicial finding assented to by the defendant that necessarily establishes the qualifying elements. Mere reliability of a police account does not satisfy that statutory inquiry. Any genuinely disputed recidivist fact indispensable to the enhanced floor or ceiling must also satisfy Gatewood, including federal charging and proof beyond a reasonable doubt or a valid admission. The First Circuit therefore cannot order enhancement on its proposed shared-understanding inference.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This rule governs ACCA qualification of convictions under statutes broader than generic burglary. It permits judicial legal classification of established elements; it excludes a second factual trial of the original burglary under a sentencing preponderance standard.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A plea expressly incorporating a building-specific charge differs from an undifferentiated plea accompanied by an unadopted police narrative. Courts may classify elements necessarily established by qualifying records, but must separately enforce Gatewood’s charging, jury and valid-admission requirements for indispensable disputed enhancement facts.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Taylor ties the enhancement to convictions, not a sentencing judge's reconstruction of likely conduct. The Massachusetts complaints leave the relevant distinction unresolved. Police applications may explain why a charge was filed without showing what the defendant necessarily admitted to obtain the disposition. Gatewood supplies a second, independent limit: even a practically persuasive account does not move an indispensable sentencing fact from the jury to the judge. The statutory rule and the constitutional rule therefore perform different work, and neither can be evaded by calling the inquiry an interpretation of the state plea.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Extend Taylor v. United States to guilty-plea records. Reaffirm United States v. Gatewood, United States v. Angle and Harris v. United States. Do not restore the displaced constitutional exception associated with Almendarez-Torres.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The complete federal indictment, preservation and waiver record remains necessary for resentencing. The authority for a new enhancement proceeding depends on that record and independently applicable law; this judgment does not create it.

## 38. Ballard v. Commissioner

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Conditions:** Subject to any intervening effective decision governing the issue.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated rule, disposition and remedy.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Eleventh Circuit judgment in No. 03-184 and the Seventh Circuit judgment in consolidated No. 03-1034; remand through those courts to the Tax Court.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the original submitted report to be placed in the reviewable record and disclosed to the parties subject to specific lawful confidentiality protections. Provide an opportunity to address departures, then reconsider the affected findings under Rule 183. Do not erase the deficiencies or fraud determinations without that review.
* **Remedial Reach:** Both consolidated taxpayer judgments receive this procedural relief. The interpretation governs future uses of the same rule, but the mandate does not reopen unrelated final tax judgments.
* **Practical Relief:** The Tax Court must identify the submitted report and material later changes, provide an orderly response schedule, and issue a decision that shows how the report was treated. Appellate review resumes on a complete record; an unexplained failure to provide the operative report cannot sustain the affected judgment.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Original report, independent review and a reviewable decisional record

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach both consolidated procedural judgments. The alleged divergence between reports need not be proved before requiring the record necessary to verify compliance with the rule.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the Tax Court's current rule permit an original hearing report to disappear into an undisclosed collaborative final opinion?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt a new, text-based construction of Rule 183; do not announce a freestanding constitutional right to all judicial drafts.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule applies to the recurring two-stage Tax Court procedure and preserves the procedural protection the rule expressly establishes. A broader constitutional disclosure regime is unnecessary. A taxpayers-only exception would fail to explain the legal status of the required report.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A special trial judge's submitted Rule 183 report must remain identifiable and available to the parties and reviewing court when the final decision is tested for the deference that Rule 183 requires. The regular judge may adopt, modify or reject that report, but must preserve it and make the disposition of material findings reviewable. The taxpayers need show that the legally operative report was excluded, not prove its undisclosed contents. The Tax Court must then provide the report, permit a meaningful response, and reconsider or explain its decision under the stated credibility and presumptive-correctness standards.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Submitted Rule 183 reports are covered. Ordinary preliminary drafts, informal discussions before submission, and all judicial deliberations are not made public by this holding. Appropriate protection of legitimately confidential tax information remains available without defeating party access or appellate review.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A final decision may disagree with the submitted report if the deciding judge applies Rule 183’s credibility and presumptive-correctness requirements and explains material departures. Preliminary drafts remain distinct from the completed report that the rule makes legally operative.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The rule assigns legal significance to a completed hearing report. Its presumption would be unenforceable if the report could be rewritten invisibly and the final text attributed solely to the hearing judge. Retaining final decisional authority does not eliminate the obligation to exercise it through the prescribed process. The full-court review statute addresses a different relation among regular judges and expressly directs exclusion; that text cannot silently erase Rule 183's distinct treatment of trial-level credibility. Disclosure also avoids deciding whether the same concealment independently violates due process.
* **Precedent Treatment:** No holding is overruled. The holding construes the specific Tax Court rule and distinguishes the full-court review procedure in section 7460(b).
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The original findings’ contents and correctness require the completed record on remand. A materially different future Tax Court rule is outside this dispute.

## 39. Wilkinson v. Dotson

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Conditions:** Subject to any intervening effective decision governing the issue.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit judgment allowing both section 1983 actions to proceed, and remand for further proceedings.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore the complaints for determination of independent procedural defenses and the actual constitutional claims. Do not order release, change either sentence, or compel a favorable parole decision.
* **Remedial Reach:** The immediate relief covers Dotson and Johnson's dismissed actions. No constitutional policy violation has yet been proved and no broader injunction is entered.
* **Practical Relief:** The District Court must decide the remaining barriers and merits on a defined record. If a violation is established, it can prescribe a lawful new proceeding while leaving the authorized parole decision to the responsible officials. Failure to provide an ordered proceeding is enforceable through the normal injunction process, not automatic release without a lawful basis.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Boundary between a procedural civil-rights claim and a custody challenge

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach only vehicle eligibility because that is the basis of dismissal and the en banc judgment. The pleadings request proceedings, not release.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the possibility that a new parole hearing could shorten custody place these actions exclusively within habeas?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply and restate the compatible-claim rule from Heck, Edwards, Muhammad and Nelson in the parole context.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. One administrable test should govern procedural prisoner claims whose relationship to release is contingent. A broader rule immunizing all claims labeled procedural would defeat Heck. A narrower rule confined to these two parole forms would recreate an arbitrary subject-matter distinction.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A prisoner may pursue prospective relief under section 1983 when success would require only a new lawful proceeding and would neither invalidate the legal basis of custody nor necessarily require release or shorter confinement. The plaintiff must identify the challenged procedure and relief with that limited effect; the court must examine the actual statutory consequences. Because lawful new consideration may again result in denial of parole, these claims do not require favorable termination before they can be heard. The prisoners retain the burden of establishing the underlying constitutional violation and satisfying independently applicable procedural requirements.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding reaches discretionary parole reconsideration and equivalent procedural claims compatible with existing custody. It excludes orders restoring credits that automatically shorten confinement, vacating a sentence, or directing release.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** If a favorable procedural ruling necessarily entitles the prisoner to release or shorter custody under state law, the same necessary-effect test directs the claim to habeas. Where officials retain lawful discretion to deny release, §1983 remains available subject to proof of a federal right and its violation.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Heck guards the continuing validity of a criminal judgment; it does not classify every hoped-for improvement in release prospects as invalidation. Edwards requires attention to the legal effect of the specific discipline and relief. Muhammad rejects confinement as a sufficient connection, and Nelson confirms that compatible procedural relief remains possible. These bounded rules should be expressed together as a necessary-effect inquiry, without overruling any. Ohio's position would substitute the prisoner's motive for the effect of the judgment and would exclude ordinary procedural rights even where the same lawful custody could continue unchanged.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm and restate together Heck v. Humphrey, Edwards v. Balisok, Muhammad v. Close and Nelson v. Campbell. Preserve Preiser v. Rodriguez for relief necessarily directed to custody.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether Ohio's particular guideline changes violate the Constitution and whether each plaintiff has a protected liberty interest were not decided below on the merits and are outside the vehicle question.

## 40. Back v. Hastings on Hudson Union Free School District

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Conditions:** Subject to any intervening effective decision governing the issue.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated rule, disposition and remedy.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Second Circuit's partial vacatur and remand as to Brennan and Wishnie; leave its affirmance for Russell and the district undisturbed.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the two individual claims for trial under the no-mandatory-comparator rule. Permit ordinary proof of damages and properly supported employment relief if liability is established; award neither at this stage.
* **Remedial Reach:** Back's individual claims against the two supervisors proceed. No unlawful policy has been proved and this judgment does not impose districtwide relief.
* **Practical Relief:** The trial court must allow the supported stereotyping evidence and performance evidence to be tested by the factfinder. Back must prove each defendant's responsibility and compensable harm. The remand itself does not compel reinstatement or tenure.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Intentional sex stereotyping and the need for a comparator

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the sufficiency question because it determined whether Back could obtain a trial. Treat the supported statements as true only for summary judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Must a mother identify a favored father to prove that official decisionmakers acted on a stereotype about women's proper family roles?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply current equal-protection law and reject an extra comparator requirement.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The principle governs proof of intentional employment discrimination across materially similar sex-stereotyping cases. A broader rule making every unfavorable caregiver decision unconstitutional would abandon the sex-causation requirement. A narrower comparator exception limited to these exact words would preserve an evidentiary barrier inconsistent with the right.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A public employee may prove intentional sex discrimination through decisionmakers’ sex-role statements and related evidence without identifying a similarly situated employee of the opposite sex. Back bears persuasion by a preponderance on intentional discrimination and each defendant’s causal responsibility. The indirect proof framework shifts only production of a legitimate explanation to the employer; the whole record must still support discrimination. Here the maternal-role statements tied to the tenure recommendation, together with disputed performance explanations, permit that inference. Their truth and causal significance remain for the jury.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns action because of sex, including maternal-role assumptions. It does not create strict liability for a comment, a general parental-status classification, or liability for defendants lacking personal involvement.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** An employer remains free to enforce genuine work requirements on equal terms and prove a nondiscriminatory basis. A mother and father comparison may strengthen or weaken an inference; its absence does not erase direct evidence. No issue is reserved within this evidentiary rule.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** United States v. Virginia rejects using generalized assumptions about women to determine an individual's opportunity. A supervisor who predicts that a mother will become less committed after tenure may be using precisely that forbidden assumption. Hibbs reinforces the constitutional significance of state employment stereotypes, but does not relieve Back of proving her own claim. The divergence between earlier evaluations and the stated later concerns is evidence for a factfinder, not conclusive proof. Requiring a father in the same workplace would make an otherwise identifiable constitutional injury depend on a fortuitous comparator.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply United States v. Virginia and Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs on sex stereotyping. Apply St. Mary’s Honor Center v. Hicks and Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products to preserve ordinary persuasion and permissible circumstantial inferences; no actual-father comparator is required.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None. The truth of the statements, actual performance and causation are factual applications of the stated rule for trial. The separate judgments for Russell and the district remain outside this appeal's disputed components.

##### Issue 2: Conduct-date qualified immunity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve immunity on the same supported plaintiff-favorable facts, not a finding that the defendants actually held those motives.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Did the absence of a prior case about this precise maternal stereotype reasonably permit the alleged discrimination?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing immunity rule; do not replace it with a new general immunity regime.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The clearly established prohibition controls this concrete alleged conduct. A broader revision of qualified immunity is unnecessary. A narrower insistence on an identical tenure case would contradict the fair-warning approach.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Officials are not entitled to qualified immunity on a supported account that they intentionally denied an employment opportunity because a woman's maternal role made her unsuitable, where preexisting law clearly forbade such sex-role discrimination. The defendants may contest the facts at trial, but cannot obtain summary judgment by demanding a case involving the same job and family circumstances. United States v. Virginia and the existing prohibition of deliberate sex discrimination supplied fair warning before the conduct.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This denies immunity for the specific alleged intentional conduct, not for every personnel error affecting a parent.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Disputed motive and conduct receive ordinary factfinding; they cannot be resolved against Back on summary judgment. Hibbs may explain current merits law but cannot supply notice for conduct preceding its decision.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The asserted uncertainty concerns evidence, not a reasonable understanding that intentional sex discrimination was lawful. A public supervisor needed no later decision to know that denying tenure because women should prioritize motherhood was constitutionally prohibited. Hope confirms that legal notice is not confined to a factual replica. The defense remains available when genuinely uncertain law, rather than a dispute about what happened, controls.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Hope v. Pelzer and the existing Harlow and Anderson framework. Preserve the treatment of Saucier v. Katz.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual motive and the occurrence of the alleged remarks remain disputed trial facts.

## 41. Muehler v. Mena

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Conditions:** Subject to any intervening effective decision governing the issue.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated rule, disposition and remedy.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand the appellate judgment for reconsideration under the separated detention, force and questioning standards.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject liability based solely on the topic of immigration questions. Require consideration of the preserved continuing-force and post-search detention grounds, proper deference to supported jury findings, conduct-date immunity and whether any error requires a limited new trial. Do not enter judgment for the officers on the entire case.
* **Remedial Reach:** The mandate concerns Mena's damages judgment against the two petitioning officers. No policy injunction or class relief is before the Court.
* **Practical Relief:** The Ninth Circuit must identify which verdict findings and theories remain legally sufficient and explain whether the award survives or must be retried in part. A remand cannot leave a damages award resting on an invalid theory or extinguish a supported independent injury without analysis.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Detention authority, continuing force and search completion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the legal standards but preserve the jury posture and the unaddressed alternative duration ground. The lawful search establishes a detention interest, not conclusive reasonableness of every restraint.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What must justify prolonged handcuffing during a search, and what remains of the verdict under that standard?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Summers and the current Graham and Holland framework; require proper review of the verdict rather than categorical approval or categorical rejection of search-long handcuffing.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule distinguishes detention from force and requires reassessment where conditions materially change. A broader ban on handcuffs during a weapons search would ignore real safety risks. A narrower rule approving all restraints until the search ends would disregard Holland and the jury's distinct findings.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A lawful search may justify detention of occupants, but the force used and its continued duration must remain objectively reasonable in light of the risks and control actually present. Officers may use handcuffs to secure a dangerous weapons search without individual suspicion that every occupant committed a crime, yet must account for material reductions in risk, restraint-related harm and available supervision as time passes. The court must assess the entire relevant record without reweighing supported jury findings, and determine separately whether detention outlasted the search or another lawful basis.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This reaches force incident to residential search detention. It does not create a fixed time limit, require the least intrusive feasible tactic, or permit detention beyond the lawful purpose.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Initial restraint while armed suspects remain unsecured and continued restraint after control present different safety circumstances under the same objective-reasonableness standard. Detention after the search ends requires an independent lawful basis.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Summers explains why officers may control occupants during a search; it does not collapse all additional bodily restraint into that authority. Holland makes temporal justification central: the reasons for initial force can change. The reported weapons risk and limited guards are material, but the jury's findings require examination under the correct standard rather than a new appellate preference. On remand, immunity must be analyzed using pre-February 1998 law and the supported factual account, not Holland's later date as a source of earlier notice.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Michigan v. Summers, Graham v. Connor and Holland ex rel. Overdorff v. Harrington. Do not alter the conduct-date qualified-immunity framework.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The appellate court did not resolve the alleged detention after completion of the search. The record summary does not permit a definitive assessment of all verdict evidence or immunity findings.

##### Issue 2: Immigration questions and an additional seizure

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the categorical questioning ground because it helped sustain the judgment; do not assume a factual finding of no added duration where the record leaves the matter unresolved.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do unrelated immigration questions alone require separate reasonable suspicion during an otherwise lawful detention?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the seizure requirement and expressly reject a subject-matter-only basis for liability.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The judgment can resolve the legal premise without approving coercive immigration questioning generally. A broader authorization to prolong detention would exceed that premise. A narrower refusal to answer would leave an incorrect independent basis for damages.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Questions about an unrelated subject do not create an additional Fourth Amendment seizure unless they add detention or a distinct coercive intrusion. Separate reasonable suspicion is therefore not required solely because officers asked about immigration status during an otherwise lawful search detention. The claimant may still establish that questioning prolonged the custody or involved an independently unlawful intrusion; that requires analysis of its actual effects.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Only the asserted additional Fourth Amendment violation from the questions themselves is resolved. The underlying force and length of detention remain separate.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts measure any added duration or coercive restraint against the lawful basis for custody. Unrelated questions that add neither do not create a separate seizure; an investigatory extension needs its own justification.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The constitutional injury must correspond to a governmental intrusion. Changing the subject does not necessarily alter an already justified restraint, but time spent holding a person after the authorized purpose ends does. Keeping those inquiries distinct prevents both an automatic liability rule based on topic and an unlimited investigatory extension.
* **Precedent Treatment:** No precedent is overruled. Preserve existing search, seizure and detention limits.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Any Fifth Amendment compulsion issue is outside the question presented. Added duration, if preserved and disputed, requires the lower court's record-based decision.

## 42. Rancho Palos Verdes v. Abrams

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit judgment insofar as it permits section 1983 enforcement of section 332(c)(7), and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject damages or section 1988 fees resting solely on that foreclosed statutory section 1983 claim. Preserve the separately granted TCA relief unless a properly presented independent ground requires reconsideration. Do not decide an independent constitutional claim or the full direct-TCA remedy question by implication.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment concerns Abrams's additional enforcement claim against the City and petitioning officials. The statutory construction governs equivalent siting claims but supplies no new injunction against other municipalities.
* **Practical Relief:** On remand the court must separate the existing permit remedy from requests depending exclusively on section 1983, deny the latter, and determine any remaining properly preserved relief under its actual statutory source. The City cannot use the enforcement-channel ruling to disregard the surviving permit order.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Individual rights and incompatibility of the additional enforcement route

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the enforcement-channel question, assuming the specifically protected applicant can invoke the statutory restrictions. Do not disturb the independently granted siting relief or decide an unpleaded constitutional injury.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does section 1983 provide an alternative route around the Telecommunications Act's express, expedited siting-review scheme?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Blessing and adopt a statute-specific foreclosure holding. Do not treat the nonmajority Gonzaga reasoning or historical Sandoval rule as governing law.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The holding governs this integrated siting-review provision and its parallel statutory claims. A broader rule making every express remedy exclusive would contradict the existing presumption. A narrower conclusion based only on Abrams's requested fees would fail to address the structural conflict between the two actions.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Telecommunications Act's specific thirty-day action for expedited judicial review of local wireless-siting decisions forecloses a parallel section 1983 action enforcing the same statutory restrictions. The City bears the burden of showing incompatibility, and satisfies it through the integrated short filing period, expedited adjudication and specially provided avenue for correcting the decision. An alternative action with ordinary section 1983 timing and remedies would materially displace that design. The saving clause preserves independently existing law and claims; it does not create a second enforcement mechanism for the newly enacted siting rights.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding is confined to claims enforcing section 332(c)(7)(B). It does not foreclose an independently established constitutional claim, restrict section 1983 elsewhere merely because a statute has an administrative remedy, or resolve every form of relief available within the TCA action itself.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** The foreclosure rule governs enforcement of §332(c)(7)(B), whose individual judicial action supplies a short filing period and expedited review. General funding supervision, audits or administrative hearings without a comparable incompatible judicial scheme do not establish foreclosure under Blessing.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Blessing makes foreclosure a separate question after identifying a right. Here Congress did more than authorize agency supervision: it gave the person injured by a final siting decision a direct, rapidly triggered court action and required expedition. A parallel route could make the filing deadline optional and detach the claim from the mechanism Congress chose. The saving clause sensibly preserves preexisting federal claims without giving every new statutory right a second and conflicting enforcement timetable. This conclusion does not depend on importing the historical Court's different treatment of implied rights or on assuming that section 1983 always enlarges damages.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Blessing v. Freestone. Preserve the limited controlling result in Gonzaga University v. Doe and the holdings in Alexander v. Sandoval and Powell v. Ridge. No general private-enforcement precedent is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Independent constitutional claims and the full direct-TCA damages question are outside the presented enforcement-channel dispute.

## 43. Brown v. Payton

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Conditions:** Subject to any intervening effective decision governing the issue.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated rule, disposition and remedy.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the determination that section 2254(d)(1) is satisfied and that the penalty proceeding contains constitutional error; vacate the final remedial component and remand for harmless-error review under the Brecht rule.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require whole-record review with the State bearing the beyond-reasonable-doubt burden. If the State fails, issue a conditional sentence writ requiring a constitutionally adequate penalty proceeding within a reasonable court-fixed period or replacement of death with a lawful noncapital sentence. Preserve convictions and unrelated claims.
* **Remedial Reach:** Relief concerns Payton's death sentence alone. No classwide relief or automatic reopening of other capital judgments follows.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must obtain the full existing sentencing record, permit focused submissions on harmlessness, and issue a reasoned determination under the correct burden. If relief is required, it must set and enforce an actual compliance deadline, while leaving the State a lawful choice of resentencing or noncapital punishment.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: AEDPA applicability and the state-decision-date merits gate

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Apply AEDPA because the record identifies a later writ application, not an earlier application hidden under a different caption. Reach the merits gate on the actual 1992 adjudication.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Did the state court reasonably apply the then-existing mitigation rule to a charge accompanied by an uncorrected statement that the only defense mitigation was legally irrelevant?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing statutory standard and find it satisfied on this record; do not replace it with de novo disagreement.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The combined charge and argument, not factor (k) in isolation, supplies the ground. A broader rule invalidating every use of that instruction would disregard Boyde. A narrower requirement of an identical prior prosecutorial statement would deny effect to clearly established general law.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 2254(d)(1) permits relief only when the state court’s contextual rejection of a mitigation-exclusion claim is objectively unreasonable, not merely incorrect. Payton meets that distinct burden here: the prosecutor expressly excluded the only affirmative mitigation from legal consideration, and the judge never corrected that proposition. The general direction to consider all evidence and the prosecutor’s alternative weight argument do not reasonably answer whether the jury understood itself legally authorized to choose life on that evidence. This conclusion concerns the combined record, not every ambiguity in factor (k).
* **Reach Within the Question:** The statutory holding concerns the combined penalty instructions and arguments on this state record. It does not establish that every ambiguous charge or erroneous argument is objectively unreasonable.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A direct correction explaining that postcrime character evidence may support a life verdict changes the material context. Courts assess the charge, objections, judicial responses and arguments together; an erroneous instruction alone does not automatically establish objective unreasonableness.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Skipper establishes that behavior in custody can bear on the capital sentencing choice. Eddings forbids legal exclusion, even when the sentencer may find the evidence unpersuasive. Boyde requires context, so the same printed instruction can operate differently when the prosecutor expressly narrows it. The state court relied on the fact that mitigation was admitted and debated; neither answers the legal-use problem created by the prosecutor's statement. Williams permits this objective application of an existing rule while preventing reliance on later holdings as if available in 1992.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Woodford v. Garceau, Williams v. Taylor and Lockyer v. Andrade. Apply the pre-1992 holdings in Lockett v. Ohio, Eddings v. Oklahoma, Skipper v. South Carolina and Boyde v. California. No precedent is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None concerning AEDPA’s application: the identified pre-enactment proceedings sought counsel and interim protection, and the writ application followed enactment. Final harmlessness remains a separate record question.

##### Issue 2: Effective mitigation, harmlessness and sentence-only relief

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Having found the statutory gate satisfied, reach the constitutional charge issue. The accessible record establishes the exclusion problem but is insufficient for a definitive whole-record harmlessness determination.
