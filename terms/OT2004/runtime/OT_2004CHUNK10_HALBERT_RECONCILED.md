## Statutory architecture and supported record

Michigan section 770.3a generally denies appointed appellate counsel after guilty, guilty-but-mentally-ill, and no-contest pleas, subject to two distinct kinds of exception. Subsection (2) requires appointment for an indigent plea defendant if the prosecutor seeks leave to appeal; the sentence exceeds the upper limit of the minimum range under the applicable sentencing guidelines; the Court of Appeals or Supreme Court grants the defendant's leave application; or the defendant seeks leave from a conditional plea under Michigan Court Rule 6.301(C)(2) or its successor. Each is an independent mandatory trigger.

Subsection (3) permits appointment only when all three conditions hold: the defendant seeks leave concerning an allegedly improper offense-variable or prior-record-variable score; the defendant objected or otherwise preserved the issue; and the imposed sentence departs upward from the upper limit of the minimum range the defendant alleges should have been scored. Eligibility leaves appointment discretionary. This alleged-correct-range comparison differs from subsection (2)(b)'s applicable-guidelines-range comparison.

Subsection (4) directs the court, before accepting the plea and while determining its understanding and voluntariness, to advise that acceptance ordinarily waives publicly appointed assistance for a leave application and other postconviction remedies, subject to the section's exceptions, and to determine understanding of that waiver. Upon sentencing it requires a nontechnical, easily understood application form. These statutory commands are distinct from proof that the actual advice or form was supplied and from the federal constitutional validity of a waiver. No statutory-exception entitlement is adjudicated without its matching facts. The complete enactment is copied in the final annex.

Halbert confirmed that he understood the loss of an appeal as of right. He separately confirmed the court's explanations of the mandatory and discretionary appointment categories. The quoted account does not include an express residual statement denying appointment in all other circumstances. Counsel reportedly answered affirmatively when asked about other promises or considerations and compliance with the no-contest plea rule. The source supplies no content for any additional promise; none is inferred. The trial court actually found the plea understandingly made, voluntary and accurate. That finding is accepted as reported, while its scope and evidentiary implications for the particular appellate-assistance right are disputed.

Halbert later twice sought appointed counsel, identified sentencing and representation issues, and described learning limitations and reliance on fellow prisoners. Those circumstances bear on the evidentiary inquiry; they neither establish incompetence nor make a valid waiver freely revocable. His initial application received a merits-based denial without a prior lawyer-prepared appellate presentation.

## Reconciled controlling-proposition candidates and remedy

**H1 - Initial appellate assistance.** The Fourteenth Amendment requires appointed advocacy for an indigent plea-convicted defendant seeking this initial appellate examination of alleged error in the Michigan Court of Appeals. An equivalent advocate-assisted screening mechanism may satisfy the function, and counsel need not advance frivolous arguments. The distinction from Ross rests on both the initial error-correcting function and the absence of prior counseled appellate presentation, rather than the word first alone.

Douglas supplies fair access to initial merits review. Robbins establishes that the advocate's actual legal-screening function, rather than a prescribed filing format, must be supplied. Michigan's mandatory exceptions and conjunctive discretionary scoring route provide real protection in their defined circumstances, but do not supply that assistance to this denied applicant. Post-grant counsel arrives after the initial screening decision. A form cannot itself perform legal identification and presentation. The State's resource interests and the narrower issues surviving a plea do not replace the function at this first merits stage.

**H2 - Actual waiver.** Entry of a plea does not automatically relinquish the distinct assistance required to seek first-tier appellate review. An actual knowing, intelligent and voluntary surrender may be shown from the whole record without prescribed words. The combined reported advice, affirmative responses, general counsel-compliance response and general plea-validity finding do not sufficiently establish intentional informed surrender of this appellate assistance.

Godinez separates capacity from actual waiver. Tovar rejects mandatory ritual warnings while requiring real understanding of the relevant assistance. Stumpf permits an actual specific explanation by counsel, confirmed by the defendant, to establish informed assent; its record contained that offense-element explanation. Here the court and defendant expressly discussed loss of appeal as of right and the affirmative appointment categories, while the counsel responses and finding do not identify an explanation or surrender of the distinct residual assistance. The six Associates give the affirmative evidence weight but reject the proposed inference as insufficient. This is an evidentiary conclusion on the whole reported record, not a rule demanding the precise words absent from it or assuming every statutory duty went unperformed.

**H3 - Restoration.** Vacate the judgment of the Michigan Court of Appeals denying Halbert's uncounseled leave application, and remand for constitutionally adequate first-tier consideration with appointed assistance. Robbins's missing-function rule does not require Halbert first to prove that an omitted argument probably would prevail. Ordinary preservation and adequate frivolousness screening govern that consideration. His conviction remains in force during the restored review.

The six supporting Associates join H1-H3 at the same level of generality. The independently sufficient no-entitlement and actual-waiver grounds below command the two opposing Associate votes only. No partial agreement with ordinary waiver principles establishes a join in the H2 application.

## Reconciled opposing grounds

**D1 - No entitlement in the selective process.** Scalia and Thomas would apply Douglas's appeal-as-of-right boundary and Ross's permission for selective review. Michigan's plea procedure permits an application rather than requiring a full appeal; the plea narrows the issues, and the statute supplies counsel at defined stages and for defined circumstances. They acknowledge the absence of an earlier counseled appellate presentation but do not regard that difference as enough to extend Douglas. Their position accepts real assistance where constitutionally required and does not deny Robbins's rule in its first-appeal-as-of-right setting.

**D2 - Actual waiver, assuming entitlement.** Independently, Scalia and Thomas would hold that Halbert knowingly and voluntarily accepted the ordinary absence of appointed counsel when entering his plea. The repeated confirmations of the change in appeal rights and finite appointment categories, counsel's plea-rule compliance response, and the court's understanding-and-voluntariness finding support that inference together. The statutory duty provides the proceeding's legal context; it is not substituted for evidence that unreported advice occurred. The known advice and confirmations themselves, reinforced by the finding, sufficiently communicate the ordinary residual consequence in their assessment.

This ground addresses the majority's strongest objection: the reported record contains no specific affirmative surrender or express residual exclusion. Under Tovar and Stumpf, an inferred but adequately demonstrated understanding can suffice without either formulation. The two Justices would give the described contextual evidence greater weight than the six Associates, while recognizing that loss of an appeal as of right and loss of assistance preparing an available leave application are legally distinct. Halbert's later requests do not negate the earlier knowing choice they find. The ground is an actual-record inference, not automatic waiver by plea status, presumed performance of every statutory duty, or an invented counsel explanation.

No separate harmless-error determination is added. The final dissent grounds are D1 and D2, and do not rest on an unproved finding about how additional advice would have changed the plea or the ultimate appeal. The controlling restoration rule addresses a function actually absent and not validly waived; D2 disputes that predicate rather than requiring a merits-success showing.

## Why the waiver reconciliation changes two provisional rows

The fresh independent model considered all the new evidence, but it retained only D1 for Scalia and Thomas and treated actual waiver as an analytically possible path they would decline to decide. It must nevertheless identify a concrete reason for omitting a material independent ground that a Justice actually supported on the same question and record.

The full historical dissent, 545 U.S. at 637-643, separates the existence and waivability of a right from actual waiver. Part III-B-3 relies on the relinquishment of appeal as of right, the enumerated appointment conditions and affirmative answers, counsel's compliance answer, and the trial court's plea finding. Those facts now appear in the clean neutral refresh. Its direct Stumpf analogy concerns contextual proof, not a rule that all plea findings necessarily prove waiver of every right.

Current Godinez, Tovar and Stumpf retain the demand for actual informed surrender, but they also permit proof without prescribed judicial words. The six Associates' distinction between specific and general advice is a substantial contrary argument; it does not identify a changed factual premise or new legal prohibition that prevents Scalia or Thomas from drawing the historical cumulative inference. Current Robbins changes the required initial assistance and restoration analysis for those who accept H1, but it does not determine whether a particular defendant knowingly waived that assistance. Neither Justice's recorded Robbins position supplies a reason to abandon D2.

The current six-Associate formulation expressly allows valid actual waivers and does not depend on saying an unrecognized state-law entitlement can never be waived. That removes the need for part of the historical dissent's broader response about categorical waivability and conditional waivers. It does not remove the live, separately sufficient disagreement over this record's actual surrender. Mere sufficiency of D1 does not itself explain declining D2; it was sufficient historically as well. After this comparison, the two affirmative waiver commitments are better supported than unexplained omission of the issue. This is a reconciliation correction of rationale following additional source review, without changing the neutral evidence or any judgment vote.

## Mechanical annex - complete operative statute

```text
Sec. 3a. (1) Except as provided in subsections (2) and (3), a defendant who pleads guilty, guilty but mentally ill, or
nolo contendere shall not have appellate counsel appointed for review of the defendant's conviction or sentence.

    (2) The trial court shall appoint appellate counsel for an indigent defendant who pleads guilty, guilty but mentally
ill, or nolo contendere if any of the following apply:

    (a) The prosecuting attorney seeks leave to appeal.
    (b) The defendant's sentence exceeds the upper limit of the minimum sentence range of the applicable sentencing
guidelines.
    (c) The court of appeals or the supreme court grants the defendant's application for leave to appeal.
    (d) The defendant seeks leave to appeal a conditional plea under Michigan Court Rule 6.301(C)(2) or its successor
rule.

    (3) The trial court may appoint appellate counsel for an indigent defendant who pleads guilty, guilty but mentally ill,
or nolo contendere if all of the following apply:

    (a) The defendant seeks leave to appeal a sentence based upon an alleged improper scoring of an offense variable or
a prior record variable.

    (b) The defendant objected to the scoring or otherwise preserved the matter for appeal.

    (c) The sentence imposed by the court constitutes an upward departure from the upper limit of the minimum
sentence range that the defendant alleges should have been scored.

    (4) While establishing that a plea of guilty, guilty but mentally ill, or nolo contendere was made understandingly and
voluntarily under Michigan Court Rule 6.302 or its successor rule, and before accepting the plea, the court shall advise
the defendant that, except as otherwise provided in this section, if the plea is accepted by the court, the defendant
waives the right to have an attorney appointed at public expense to assist in filing an application for leave to appeal or
to assist with other postconviction remedies, and shall determine whether the defendant understands the waiver. Upon
sentencing, the court shall furnish the defendant with a form developed by the state court administrative office that is
nontechnical and easily understood and that the defendant may complete and file as an application for leave to appeal.
```
