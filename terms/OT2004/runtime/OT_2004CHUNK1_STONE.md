* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2004, assessed for 2004-11-09; subject to intervening controlling decisions.
* **Conditions:** Subject to the stated record limits and any intervening controlling decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment sustaining the challenged aggravated-felony classification and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the agency and reviewing court to remove this §16 classification as a basis for the removal order and adjudicate any independently preserved basis under its own law. The criminal convictions remain valid.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment supplies corrective immigration relief to Leocal. The statutory construction governs other cases through ordinary precedent, without a class injunction or an order deciding other individuals' removability.
* **Practical Relief:** On mandate, the reviewing court must return the matter with a binding instruction that these DUI convictions cannot support this aggravated-felony ground. The agency must enter a conforming disposition and address the concrete means of correcting continuing consequences; a further removal ground requires its own process and proof.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The force requirement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The disputed offense classification is the predicate for both removability and the asserted review restriction, so it must be decided.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Florida offense qualifies under either part of §16.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing interpretation and review principles; adopt the presently unresolved construction of §16.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A categorical statutory rule is needed because removal turns on an offense definition. A broader rule covering all recklessness would exceed the presented elements. A narrower accident-specific result would improperly replace categorical analysis with conduct findings.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An offense that permits conviction for accidental or negligent injury without the employment of force against another person or another’s property is not a crime of violence under either subsection of 18 U.S.C. §16. The Government must establish a qualifying conviction through its legal elements and the applicable conviction record, not infer the missing requirement from injury alone. Section 16(b)'s risk concerns force use in committing the offense, not every risk of physical injury. Florida's offense therefore does not establish the charged aggravated felony.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Offenses whose minimum elements permit accidental or negligent injury; intentional force offenses remain subject to the actual statutory definition.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Bailey v. United States gives the statutory word use operative significance, while Taylor v. United States prevents the accident record from adding an element. The broader risk clause retains the same force-use concept, so the Government's causation argument would erase a limitation Congress repeated. Calcano-Martinez does not bar resolving an expressly disputed criminal predicate.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Bailey v. United States: apply its contextual active-use reasoning without extending its firearm holding as an automatic definition. Taylor v. United States: apply categorical analysis. Calcano-Martinez: distinguish conceded predicates.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A statute requiring reckless force use is outside the offense presented; other removal grounds lack an adjudicated record.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Lawful possession

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Mayzel's bailment supplies asserted control and duties over the entrusted money; that is his own claimed injury.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether his constitutional challenge can reach the whole forfeited fund.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend existing first-party injury principles to the identified bailment.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule covers lawful possessors whose own interests extend throughout the res. A broader stranger-standing rule lacks injury. A narrower legal-title requirement would erase the actual protected possessory interest.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A lawful possessor whose property rights or legally enforceable obligations are impaired by forfeiture may challenge the punitive exaction against the whole property to which that interest extends. The claimant must establish that interest through the applicable property law and record; ownership by another does not alone defeat it.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Bailments and comparable actual possessory interests, not unexplained possession or litigation on behalf of a stranger.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Eighth Amendment inquiry concerns punishment imposed through loss of a legally protected interest. Austin v. United States prevents form from concealing punitive operation. The asserted owner's default does not extinguish Mayzel's independently cognizable possession, though it continues to govern the owner's claim.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Austin v. United States: apply the functional fine classification without changing its scope.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 2: Proportionality

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Standing permits the inquiry but proves neither lawful source nor a disproportionate amount.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this full reporting forfeiture can survive the governing constitutional comparison.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the received gross-disproportionality rule without a new factor test.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The offense-sensitive rule reaches materially equivalent forfeitures. A broader immunity for lawful funds would contradict Bajakajian. A narrower statutory-maximum comparison would omit the constitutional inquiry.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A punitive reporting forfeiture is unconstitutional when the claimant proves it grossly disproportionate to the actual offense, assessed on supported culpability, harm, property relationship and authorized punishment. Legislative sanctions deserve weight but do not conclusively validate confiscation of the full fund. The supported lawful source, limited informational harm and absence of proved related illegal activity make $100,348 excessive here; the actual $10,000 exaction is not grossly disproportionate even using the lower plausible fine benchmark. This is a constitutional limit, not a judicial formula selecting the ideal fine.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Punitive reporting exactions, with material distinctions for actual proceeds, separate wrongdoing and culpability.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** United States v. Bajakajian requires a real offense-sensitive comparison and rejects both categorical immunity and automatic approval. United States v. Wagoner County Real Estate requires findings adequate for review, not a new hearing when existing findings suffice. The credited source and limited harm support rejection of the full fund, while the proven reporting conduct and false statements support a meaningful lesser exaction. The higher hypothetical fine is not treated as proved, and the appellate midpoint explanation is not adopted as a constitutional formula. Austin v. United States, United States v. Bajakajian and United States v. Wagoner County Real Estate retain their complementary coverage, comparison and proof rules.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States v. Bajakajian and United States v. Wagoner County Real Estate: reaffirm their actual rules; extend the supported-finding discipline to this currency proceeding.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None. The identified constitutional amount question is resolved; this disposition does not reopen separate forfeiture and fee determinations outside the grant.
