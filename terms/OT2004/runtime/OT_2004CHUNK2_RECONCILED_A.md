## Educational Credit Management Corp. v. Polleys — December 13, 2004

**Reconciled disposition:** All eight Associates affirm the Tenth Circuit judgment sustaining the hardship discharge. All eight join the complete statutory rule, burden, review standards and supported application in the refreshed handoff.

**Common operative ground.** The debtor bears the preponderance burden of establishing undue hardship to the debtor and dependents. The realistic Brunner inquiry requires inability to preserve minimal living needs while repaying, circumstances supporting persistence for a significant portion of the repayment period, and genuine good-faith efforts within actual abilities. Certainty of hopelessness, permanent total disability and a categorical expert forecast covering the entire loan are not enacted prerequisites. Ordinary evidence reliability remains applicable. Good faith examines actual work, expense and repayment efforts, including deliberate evasion; poverty alone does not dispense with proof.

The stipulation permits some retained employment and therefore excludes a finding of total inability to work. It does not contradict the supported impairment of income and employment stability. The twenty-year obligation, approximately $420 monthly payment and persistent earning limitations supply the temporal and financial application. The move from better-paid employment is considered with reduced housing costs, later accounting and nonaccounting employment efforts, consolidation, deferrals, attempted negotiation after the distinct earlier $800 demand, and the absence of an immediate discharge filing. These facts support the existing findings without an invented diagnosis, guaranteed lifetime incapacity or current income update. The lower concurrence's medical-proof and full-duration proposal is considered and rejected as a categorical statutory condition; competent evidence and ordinary burdens remain necessary.
