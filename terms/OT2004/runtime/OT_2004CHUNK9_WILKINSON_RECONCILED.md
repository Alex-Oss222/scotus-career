## Reconciled component result and governing terms

All eight Associates recognize a protected liberty interest under the actual Sandin rule, uphold the described New Policy's procedures, remove the unnecessary federally prescribed procedural additions, and remand for conforming disposition and any supported continuing remedy for the former policy. The formal disposition is affirmance in part, reversal in part, and remand. The appellate rejection of substantive placement restrictions remains effective and outside the grant. No separate writing is required by any reconciled commitment.

Ohio's substantive grounds meaningfully restrict placement authority, and the combination of exceptional isolation, indefinite duration and lost eligibility for otherwise available parole is a significant deprivation. Sandin's sufficient, nonexclusive state-created-interest rule therefore applies. Genuine placement predicates, rather than mandatory procedural wording alone, establish the substantive constraint. The Court's Sandin did not replace substantive-predicate analysis with the historical atypical-and-significant-hardship formula. The deprivation's severity establishes significance here; it is not an unannounced adoption of that different framework.

Mathews separately weighs the affected interest, error risks and value of further safeguards, and the State's security and administrative interests. The New Policy supplies factual notice, oral and written rebuttal, stated reasons, three review levels with a negative recommendation terminating placement, objections before final review, review within thirty days of arrival and annual reconsideration. That combination supplies adequate process in this classification setting. Hewitt and Greenholtz support informal review fitted to prison security; Wolff's distinct disciplinary procedures do not mechanically require the added adversarial features here. The prisoner's meaningful notice and opportunity to contest the actual basis remain central to the adequacy conclusion.

The injunction must reflect that adequacy holding and the actual need for any continuing correction of former-policy violations. Lewis, Benjamin and current Gates require relief fitted to proved injury; section 3626 requires need, narrowness, least intrusiveness and substantial consideration of public-safety and criminal-justice effects. Its additional conditions for relief contrary to state or local law remain distinct. Earlier unlawful assignments can support an appropriate continuing remedy on proved facts; their existence does not establish that every district-court addition to the replacement policy is necessary.

## 82. Wilkinson v. Austin

**Component dispositions:** All eight Associates recognize a protected interest in avoiding the OSP placement at issue and would uphold the New Policy's described procedures as constitutionally sufficient. They would affirm the liberty-interest conclusion on the controlling simulated ground, reverse the requirement for additional procedures beyond this adequate policy, and remand for conforming disposition of the injunction and any supported continuing implementation issue. The appellate rejection of new substantive placement restrictions remains effective.

**Protected-interest rule and application:** Sandin's governing rule is a sufficient, nonexclusive substantive-constraint rule: state law creates protected prison liberty when it meaningfully limits authority to impose a significant deprivation. Ohio requires a qualifying substantive placement basis, with evaluative discretion exercised within those grounds. That constraint, together with the severe isolation, indefinite duration and exclusion from otherwise available parole consideration, establishes a protected interest. Mere procedural promises are not the source. The combined record permits application rather than another abstract inquiry into whether any constraint exists.

**Process rule and application:** Mathews requires consideration of the interest, error risk and value of added safeguards, and governmental burdens. The described New Policy supplies notice of the factual basis, an opportunity for oral and written rebuttal, stated reasons and several levels of review, a terminating negative recommendation, a chance to object before final decision, prompt review after placement and annual reconsideration. In this security classification setting that combination reasonably guards against erroneous placement. Wolff and Hewitt permit procedures responsive to prison safety; full adversarial witness presentation and disclosure of every item are not constitutionally required by this record. Notice and reasons must still identify the actual basis sufficiently for the rebuttal the policy provides. The practical distinction between that requirement and disclosure endangering informants is part of the adopted adequacy analysis.

**Enhanced competing-path test:** The class's strongest path rests on demonstrated errors under the former process and the danger that general allegations or periodic paper review will continue those errors. Lewis v. Casey, Benjamin v. Fraser and current Gates v. Cook make actual proved injury and remedial necessity decisive and allow measures beyond a bare minimum when required to correct the violation. But the State's replacement incorporates meaningful checks, and earlier defects do not prove the additional witness, evidentiary and administrative specifications are necessary now. Ohio's strongest opposite path would deny any liberty in a classification left to professional judgment. The substantive predicates and magnitude of this particular restraint defeat that account even while officials retain substantial discretion within those predicates. The former Sandin dissent's hardship theory also supports an interest on these exceptional facts, but it does not replace the controlling sufficient rule.
