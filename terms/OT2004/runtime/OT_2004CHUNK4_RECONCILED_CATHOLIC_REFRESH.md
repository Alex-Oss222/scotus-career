### Record application and the controlling disagreement

The underlying action seeks a declaration of unconstitutionality and an injunction against enforcement. The superior court denied preliminary protection for lack of a reasonable likelihood of success. The Court of Appeal denied mandate, and the California Supreme Court affirmed that decision. Its opinion comprehensively rejects the presented federal and state constitutional theories, addresses conceded statutory ineligibility and concludes that Catholic Charities cannot qualify for the exemption in any event because of the unmet criteria, including the tax-status criterion. The supplied account identifies neither a disposition of the permanent-relief claims nor a particular further factual dispute or newly available claim.

The seven dismissal commitments acknowledge the full force of that comprehensive legal rejection. They nevertheless distinguish legal conclusions given in adjudicating entitlement to preliminary protection from an operative judgment conclusively disposing of the pleaded claims. On their reading, what remains is the adjudication of declaratory and permanent injunctive relief itself, not automatic execution of a claim-disposing order already entered. This is their legal assessment of the scope and effect of the supplied order. It asserts no undisclosed factual controversy, new theory, evidentiary hearing or additional procedural event.

Their answer to Stevens is that preordination must concern the actual remaining action, not merely the apparent strength of the legal prediction that the plaintiff will lose. The state's definitive discussion makes that prediction powerful, but the seven do not treat it as establishing the conclusive claim-disposing effect present in Richards. The preliminary order's function and the unchanged underlying claims matter together; neither the order's name nor the absence of a later decree supplies an independent bar. Johnson's burden allocates that legal determination and creates no production obligation.

Stevens draws the contrary inference from the same facts. Every presented theory supporting the requested constitutional relief has been rejected on accepted organizational facts and conceded ineligibility, with no identified issue requiring further substantive resolution. He regards permanent denial as practically preordained and the remaining adjudication as implementation. Richards supplies the first-category rule. His Jefferson dissent reinforces the objection to repetition serving no useful finality purpose. That dissent is not itself a first-category holding, and his position here does not rest on treating Ritchie as a substitute for Cox's first-category predicates.

Neither conclusion is a recovered historical finding about the effect of a later decree. The reconciliation retains a reasoned application disagreement over the existing judgment. It does not manufacture unanimity by treating all previous joins as an automatic command, or preserve the dismissal positions by concealing the strongest analogy against them.

### Shared rationale, dissent rationale, remedy and limits for assembly

The seven dismissal commitments support one shared statutory rationale: a highest state court's comprehensive rejection of constitutional arguments in reviewing a denial of preliminary protection does not, by itself, establish section 1257 finality. The actual judgment must conclude the relevant action or satisfy a supported Cox category. They find the operative preliminary adjudication here insufficient to establish first-category practical completion, for the reasons stated above, and find no independently sufficient other category. This is an application of the retained framework, not a categorical exclusion of preliminary injunctions or religious claims. The answer to the contrary position must acknowledge the exhaustive analysis and concessions and explain the operative-effect distinction; a bare reference to a missing decree is inadequate.

Their disposition is dismissal for want of jurisdiction. It ends this Supreme Court review and leaves the California disposition in its existing preliminary-relief posture. Further state adjudication follows that posture; any later federal review depends on its own judgment and applicable requirements. No research-return order or new production duty belongs to this disposition.

Stevens's threshold dissent would explain that Richards's first category turns on practical completion, and that exhaustive legal rejection on the accepted facts and concessions leaves no identified substantive question whose resolution could change the relief in this action. Requiring another adjudication of the same permanent claims therefore serves only implementation. His Jefferson dissent's acceptance of Ritchie's practical approach reinforces that conclusion. His joins in Florida and Johnson are distinguished by their identified, potentially outcome-changing substantive work. This dissent supplies no decision on constitutional validity, severability, exemption entitlement or ultimate relief; accepting review does not establish any of those propositions.

This handoff assigns no opinion form or author and supplies no private-position compatibility judgment. The seven threshold refusals and Stevens's contrary threshold conclusion are complete for a reasoned threshold disposition after assembly. Should later assembly propose to reach constitutional merits instead, no merits alignment may be inferred from these rows or recovered from superseded Catholic commitments; the affected ordinary stages must supply it.
