## American Civil Liberties Union of Nevada v. Heller

### Common legal conclusions and material divisions

**H1 — reach and statutory scope.** All eight accept standing on the concrete intended publications, affidavits and prior enforcement, rather than a generalized desire to challenge the statute. The requested express-advocacy construction cannot fairly replace coverage of any material or information relating to an election, candidate or ballot question. A request to certify that particular rewriting is not a reason to defer the federal question.

The duty identifies each actual payer or person responsible for payment, supplies a mailing or street address and states the payment responsibility. It does not invariably require a home address. Each statutory exception retains its own terms: candidate/party-paid advertising referring only to the candidate with the name prominently displayed; candidate-approved and paid material whose preparation/publication cost was reported as the specified contribution; independent natural persons not cooperating with or directed by the listed organizations/entities; and sufficient Communications Act identification under subsection 3. The action concerns the printed communications in this record. No additional statutory exception or narrowed definition of payer is invented.

**H2 — differentiated constitutional rule.** Stengel sustains payer name-and-address identification in its paid candidate-finance and organizational setting. McConnell sustains the challenged extension of on-message sponsor identification beyond express advocacy, with government-borne justification of a material electoral-information or source-tracing connection and protection for substantiated associational harm. On-message placement is relevant to burden but does not itself invalidate identification. The separate reporting provision's monetary threshold does not govern sponsorship. No fixed spending floor, election window or list of express-advocacy words is universally indispensable.

McIntyre continues to protect ordinary individual issue leaflets. Cooperation does not automatically eliminate every interest in anonymity; neither does an organizational identity automatically create a constitutional exemption. The actual statute reaches small cooperative speakers and neutral information remote from an election, while including a substantial legitimate field of paid electoral sponsorship. Source tracing may be materially useful without preventing every violator from lying. The candidate exceptions can avoid redundant identification when a candidate's own publication or reported payment already identifies the source; their existence does not by itself prove fatal underinclusiveness. Those points still do not establish justification of every Nevada application.

**H3 — facial judgment and remedy.** The principal division is whether this established statutory breadth and record already demonstrate substantial unjustified applications relative to the legitimate reach, or whether the lower court's erroneous treatment of the valid sponsorship field requires a fresh evaluation under H2. The choice concerns a legal and record-based overbreadth determination, not an automatic entitlement to additional government evidence.

## Granholm v. Heald — Nos. 03-1116, 03-1120 and 03-1274

### Components and competing grounds

**W1 — alcohol-specific constitutional and statutory authority.** The live question is producer-origin discrimination in access to an otherwise lawful direct-sale channel, not whether States may regulate importation and distribution. The Twenty-first Amendment's section 2 and the Wilson and Webb-Kenyon Acts require their own construction. Bacchus supplies controlling rejection of local-product favoritism; the earlier Young's Market line supplies the States' strongest contrary language about import authority. North Dakota concerns federal procurement and immunity, with differentiated rationales; its result does not automatically approve a local-producer exception.

The nondiscrimination path reads the Wilson Act's same-extent/same-manner language and Webb-Kenyon's removal of the interstate-shipment obstacle as enabling enforcement of otherwise valid state alcohol law, not an affirmative authorization to privilege local producers. Section 2 preserves that regulatory power without restoring authority to erect commercial preferences forbidden by Bacchus and the nondiscrimination component of Healy. A blanket authorization from the words concerning violation of state law would let the State supply the scope of its own constitutional exemption. The competing path treats section 2's affirmative import command, reinforced by Webb-Kenyon's broad state-law language, as displacing the judicial negative-Commerce restriction on delivery/use within the State, while retaining Congress's affirmative legislation and independent constitutional rights.

**W2 — the two actual preferences and justification.** Michigan permits local wineries to ship directly but requires outside wineries to use wholesalers; its compared licensing routes are not equal access. New York's local factory/office/storeroom and bonded-receipt path requires an outside producer to establish the local operation to obtain the direct-sales privilege already available to local wineries. Formal eligibility to become local does not eliminate that burden. The farm-winery privilege's own production cap and local-fruit condition remain separate text, not a universal description of the general license.

On the nondiscrimination path, the State must demonstrate a legitimate local purpose that cannot adequately be served through reasonable nondiscriminatory means. Tax collection, minors' protection and accountable distribution are legitimate, but must justify the difference itself. The record does not establish that evenhanded licenses, tax/reporting duties, records, age verification, jurisdictional accountability and sanctions cannot adequately serve those purposes. The comparison is regulated equal access, not unrestricted unlicensed shipment. Michigan's evidence remains insufficient even when assumed admissible and viewed favorably; another merits evidentiary opportunity is not due merely because the existing burden is demanding. New York's records/bonding/accountability provisions demonstrate possible regulatory tools without establishing that physical local establishment is indispensable.

**W3 — disposition and remedy.** Under the nondiscrimination path, affirm the Sixth Circuit judgments in Nos. 03-1116 and 03-1120; reverse the Second Circuit's Commerce Clause judgment in No. 03-1274 and remand for conforming equality relief. Preserve New York's independent advertising ruling and the independent citizenship disposition outside the common review question. Equality can be implemented only through lawful state-specific severability and remedial choices; the Court does not itself license unregulated shipments or automatically select extension of the local privilege instead of withdrawal.
