Any remaining preclusion and mootness issues require the actual claims, judgment and present controversy. Reversal of the specified dismissal ground does not conclusively adjudicate those distinct matters.

## Rule, precedent and remand limits

## Scalia's agency premise and separate position

**Reconciliation correction:** The initial commitment treated Scalia's agreement with compatible textual reasoning as a full join to independent judicial construction. No changed premise supports erasing that distinction. Reconciliation therefore corrects the join here without rewriting the initial handoff or claiming a historical departure.

His separate position explains that Congress authorized ADEA implementation through section 628 and that the EEOC exercised that authority in its longstanding interpretation recognizing adverse-impact restrictions. The national rule and its asserted statutory delegation differ from Christensen's advisory response and Mead's revisable transaction-specific classification letters. The entering Mead rule requires actual delegated authority and its exercise; formal rulemaking is evidence, not an exclusive test. Scalia considers that requirement satisfied here and independently adheres to his broader official-agency approach. The two propositions in section 1625.7(d) remain distinct. Its recognition that facially neutral practices can violate the ADEA is, for Scalia, a reasonable exercise of the delegated authority. Its business-necessity restriction on reasonable nonage factors conflicts with the enacted standard. Sharing a regulatory paragraph does not make the propositions legally inseparable. The source receipt and initial commitments already identify this institutional premise; reconciliation discovers no new operative factual premise requiring a new neutral packet.

## No-impact separate position and qualifications

O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas retain a substantive concurrence in the judgment. They read because-of-age language as requiring an age-based employment decision; the effects words in subsection (a)(2) identify harmful forms of classification rather than an additional unintentional-effects wrong. The reasonable-factors clause confirms a nonage safe harbor. The ADEA's distinction between prohibited arbitrary age discrimination and educational or other responses to age-related employment disadvantages, together with the possibility that legitimate tenure and compensation practices correlate with age, counsels against automatically transferring Griggs. Griggs followed the ADEA's enactment and its Title VII setting does not settle this statute's different structure.

They also reject Scalia's agency ground: in their view the regulation principally explains the reasonable-factors exemption without adequately exercising authority to construe the prohibitory subsection, and its business-necessity requirement misreads the exemption. They conditionally agree that, if impact claims exist, they require a particular identified practice and that reasonable nonage factors provide narrower liability than business necessity would allow. Their historical alternative also assigns the employee the burden of disproving a produced nonage explanation under their Wards Cove reading. That is a position of this separate writing, not a burden holding of the five Associates who join the lead application; no joined opinion may silently adopt it.
