## Paths and operative determination

**Subject-specific path adopted:** Section 14504's reference to a State registration requirement concerns the federal operating-authority registration system that subsections (a) and (c) specify. It does not preempt this separate Michigan-plated-vehicle safety-registration charge merely because the State collects vehicle information, requires a decal or waives its own otherwise available $10 federal-system fee. The supplied record contains no legal requirement that a carrier pay the $100 to complete the federally prescribed registration. Affirm the Michigan Court of Appeals' rejection of this federal statutory challenge.

The strongest competing path reads subsection (b)'s broad words to cover every state registration obligation imposed on an interstate motor carrier. The additional vehicle information, decal and $100 charge would then exceed the federal standards; treating a charge as safety administration could otherwise permit easy evasion. That reading fails because subsection (b) refers to the Secretary's standards, subsection (a) defines the standards by the proof-of-lawful-transportation function, and subsection (c) specifies the federal-registration evidence, insurance, agent and allocated fees. A limitation on the Secretary's required standards in subsection (c)(2)(B)(iii) does not by itself forbid every vehicle-identification device imposed through a distinct state program. Subsection (c)(2)(C) reaches fees under this section; subsection (B)(v)'s prohibition on charging for filing federal-registration evidence is a different command. Neither is a universal motor-carrier tax ceiling.

The competing actual-linkage path would enforce federal limits against an additional charge that conditions the covered registration itself. That legal principle is retained, but the factual premise is absent from this supplied record. The waiver alone cannot supply it. A program's age and asserted safety purpose are corroborative context, not categorical exemptions or substitutes for examining its actual obligation. A remand cannot be justified by inventing an unresolved linkage after the supplied facts establish separate obligations.
