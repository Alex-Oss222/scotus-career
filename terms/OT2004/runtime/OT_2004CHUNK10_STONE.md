## 91. Mid-Con Freight Systems, Inc. v. Michigan Public Service Commission

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22; current review request, with revisions authorized in advance.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling decisions and the stated record and jurisdictional limits.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording changes consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning and scope; no alternative disposition authorized.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of the §14504 preemption challenge to the separate §478.2(2) fee.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny invalidation and refund relief on this statutory ground. Continue enforcing the federal registration ceiling for charges actually within its subject; no new remedy is ordered for an unproved violation of that ceiling.
* **Remedial Reach:** The carriers' challenge to the identified Michigan fee. No universal exemption from other constitutional or statutory challenges follows.
* **Practical Relief:** Michigan may continue collecting the fee under the reviewed separate program. It may not rely on this judgment to add charges to the federally capped registration requirements or condition their completion on a fee not examined here.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The subject matter of the federal registration ceiling

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach statutory scope and the program's actual connection to covered registration, without deciding ungranted constitutional objections.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this separate $100 requirement is a covered federal-permit registration charge.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the federal statute to its specified subject and sustain the fee on the record of independent operation.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the holding must prevent both evasion of the federal ceiling and expansion of it into a general state-fee prohibition. A broader immunity for all separately labeled fees would invite evasion; a narrower literal focus on the word registration would ignore the statutory system it describes.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 14504 preempts additional state requirements for the federal-permit registration subjects Congress placed within the Single State Registration System, but does not cap a genuinely separate state regulatory fee merely because it applies to interstate carriers. Michigan's §478.2(2) charge is not shown to demand the covered proof or condition completion of that federal registration, so the asserted preemption claim fails.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The specified $100 program on this record, with federal-permit, insurance, service-agent and related covered registration charges still subject to the statutory ceiling.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts should examine actual requirements and conditions, including whether a carrier can complete federal-system registration without paying the separate charge. A fee's name, age or decal alone is not dispositive.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Yellow Transportation enforces the federal benchmark inside an expressly delegated system while preserving unrelated taxes and licensing policies. The present question is the boundary of that system. Reading the same registration phrase consistently with the listed information and fee duties gives Congress's prohibition full effect without assuming a broader occupation of all carrier regulation. The $10 waiver is evidence to consider, but does not establish that the $100 fee purchases the same registration.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan is applied at its express system-specific scope. No precedent is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## 92. Eulitt v. Maine Department of Education

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22; current review request, with revisions authorized in advance.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling decisions and the stated record and jurisdictional limits.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording changes consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning and scope; no alternative disposition authorized.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm judgment against the parents' requested integrated-tuition funding claim and reject their unsupported assertion of the school's separate rights.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny the requested tuition and injunction on this application. The school-rights disposition is without a merits adjudication of a claim the school has not brought. No order excludes separately controlled secular services or awards reimbursement.
* **Remedial Reach:** The named parents' and children's presented funding claims and the attempted school claim. The judgment does not validate every application of the statutory sectarian label.
* **Practical Relief:** Maine need not pay the requested ordinary tuition under this judgment. The parents retain their private school choice, and any distinct eligible secular-service application must be assessed under the governing function rules rather than denied solely for institutional identity.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Equal protection and the requested integrated devotional tuition

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the parents' own funding claim despite the futile formal application; decide the actual integrated purchase they request.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether equal protection requires Maine to finance this integrated religious instructional package.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing function-based funding rules, while preserving the separate prohibition on status-only exclusion.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the holding should resolve the recurring equality characterization of a restricted devotional purchase. A broader validation of every sectarian-provider exclusion would contradict existing limits; a narrower reliance on the parents' failure to submit a futile application would avoid the live substantive dispute.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Equal protection does not require a State to purchase integrated devotional instruction when its education program pays for eligible secular educational work, but religious identity alone cannot disqualify a reliably separated eligible secular service. The parents request ordinary St. Dominic's tuition specifically to obtain the integrated Catholic instruction absent from the public school, so the present funding refusal is valid.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The requested ordinary tuition and the parents' own equality claim; no categorical exclusion of every service offered by a religious institution is approved.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify the financed work in a later application for severable secular services and apply Agostini's existing separation and accountability requirements. Provider affiliation alone does not establish what the State would purchase.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Strout already evaluated ordinary tuition at this school as an integrated instructional purchase, and these parents identify the religious instruction itself as the educational need. Zelman and McCallum confirm that parental routing does not change the function purchased. Agostini, Strout, Zelman and McCallum provide multiple bounded rules for the same question; Stone would apply their existing unified function inquiry, treating the latter decisions as applications rather than layering a new test or overruling any of them. Equality protects participation in permissible public programs, but does not erase the limits on the public function being financed.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Strout v. Albanese is applied without pretending it decided the ungranted equal-protection claim. Agostini v. Felton, Zelman v. Simmons-Harris and Freedom From Religion Foundation v. McCallum are reaffirmed as a unified function-based framework. Locke v. Davey remains bounded to its actual holding.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 2: Assertion of the school's separate constitutional rights

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reject only the attempted assertion of the school's independent rights because the required obstacle is not established.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether these parents may litigate St. Dominic's separate institutional claim.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing third-party-standing requirements.
* **Reach Calibration:** Confined to this record: no hindrance is shown. A broader rule excluding all parent-school representation would exceed the facts; a narrower waiver of hindrance based on closeness alone would change the governing rule without justification.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Parents may not assert a school's separate constitutional rights merely because they purchase its services when no genuine obstacle prevents the school from asserting those rights itself. The school's independent claim must therefore be dismissed without deciding its merits.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The school's separate rights, leaving the parents' and properly represented children's own claims analytically intact.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The personal funding injury supplies a case for the parents, not automatic authority to litigate another entity's legal interests. The school is an identifiable institution capable of bringing its own claim, and the record identifies no barrier requiring substitution.
* **Precedent Treatment:** The established third-party-standing rule is applied; no anticipated Kowalski holding is treated as law.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## 93. Kelo v. City of New London

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling decisions and the stated record and jurisdictional limits.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording changes consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning and scope; no alternative disposition authorized.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Connecticut judgment against the federal public-use challenge.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny the requested federal constitutional injunction preventing these condemnations. Permit only acquisitions authorized by the applicable law and supported by the required compensation process; no valuation or transfer instrument is fixed here.
* **Remedial Reach:** The petitioners' challenged parcels and the public-use objection adjudicated in this action. No blanket authorization is issued for other redevelopment plans or later materially different takings.
* **Practical Relief:** The City may proceed through the lawful condemnation and compensation process for the adjudicated plan. The owners receive no injunction on this federal ground, but retain any preserved compensation and independent state-law claims within their proper proceedings.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Public purpose, private implementation and pretext

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the public-use challenge because identified homes face authorized condemnation and the owners seek prevention of an allegedly unlawful taking.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the actual comprehensive redevelopment purpose permits these condemnations despite private implementation and lack of parcel-specific blight.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing public-purpose rule and its express prohibition on naked private favoritism; sustain this plan on the established record.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the rule must cover genuine redevelopment while giving the anti-pretext limit practical force. A broader permission for any transfer promising more revenue would erase public use; a narrower public-ownership or blight-only requirement would depart from the existing public-purpose rule without a sufficient legal basis.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A compensated condemnation implementing an adopted comprehensive redevelopment plan satisfies public use when it serves a rationally supported actual public purpose rather than a pretext for private favoritism, even if private parties will implement portions and the particular homes are not blighted. New London's established planning purpose and record satisfy that rule; speculative economic benefits alone would not validate a sham transfer.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The challenged condemnations within this actual plan. An isolated transfer devised to benefit a favored private recipient is not approved by the judgment.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must permit a meaningful challenge to supported allegations that the asserted public purpose masks a private favor, examining the actual plan, process and beneficiary relationships under ordinary proof rules. They do not require a guarantee that a lawful plan will succeed.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp. establishes that private reconveyance is compatible with public use and expressly preserves the private-favor limit. Berman and Midkiff focus on the public objective rather than the identity of the eventual operator. The comprehensive plan here supplies a concrete objective beyond simply replacing one owner with another, while the absence of individual blight does not negate that plan's public character. The homeowners' property must still be acquired only through lawful authority and compensation.
* **Precedent Treatment:** National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., Berman v. Parker and Hawaii Housing Authority v. Midkiff are applied and reaffirmed at their public-purpose and anti-pretext boundaries.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## 94. Gonzalez v. Crosby

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22; current review request, with revisions authorized in advance.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling decisions and the stated record and jurisdictional limits.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording changes consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning and scope; no alternative disposition authorized.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm denial of the Rule 60 motion on the absence of extraordinary circumstances, while rejecting its classification as a successive habeas application.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** No reopening, writ, new trial or release is ordered. Clarify that this procedural category may be considered by the district court without §2244(b) authorization, subject to Rule 60's own requirements.
* **Remedial Reach:** Gonzalez's motion and the federal judgment it challenges.
* **Practical Relief:** The limitations judgment remains in force. The decision removes an erroneous categorical jurisdictional barrier for genuine procedural motions but does not restore Gonzalez's underlying plea claim to active litigation.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A procedural reopening motion versus a successive habeas claim

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Classify the actual requested relief before addressing extraordinary circumstances; this motion targets the limitations ruling alone.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether correction of that nonmerits ruling requires successive-application authorization.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Abdur'Rahman's binding distinction to the limitations-only motion; no new general process-versus-merits rule is proposed.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate application of an existing category: the same rule governs a limitations barrier preventing adjudication of an already pleaded claim. A broader exemption for every postjudgment filing would contradict Abdur'Rahman's merits exclusions; a narrower default-only rule would disregard its express coverage of genuine federal procedural barriers.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A Rule 60 motion that attacks only a nonmerits defect in the federal habeas proceeding, without adding a ground for relief from the state conviction or reopening a merits rejection, is not a second or successive application under §2244(b). Gonzalez's challenge to the limitations dismissal falls within that procedural category.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Genuine procedural attacks on the first federal judgment; added evidence or legal grounds seeking a different conviction-merits result remain governed by AEDPA's application requirements.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must separate mixed motions by their actual requested adjudication and cannot allow a procedural label to conceal a renewed merits claim.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Abdur'Rahman directly resolves the controlling classification: a federal procedural barrier to an already pleaded claim remains distinct from a new or renewed merits application, and reopening is not automatic. Gonzalez attacks only the limitations disposition; the federal court did not adjudicate his plea claim. The difference between default and limitations does not change that function. Calderon concerns a completed merits mandate, while Artuz may bear on the barrier's correctness rather than supply a new conviction claim.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Abdur'Rahman v. Bell is applied to a limitations-only barrier within its established procedural category. Artuz v. Bennett retains its filing-versus-claim-bar rule. Calderon v. Thompson remains distinct because it concerns a completed merits mandate.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 2: Extraordinary circumstances for Rule 60(b)(6) relief

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the Rule 60 ground because the asserted legal development and litigation history are identified and no unresolved material extraordinary circumstance is established.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this intervening interpretation and this diligence record justify reopening.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing extraordinary-circumstances requirement and deny relief on the presented showing.
* **Reach Calibration:** Confined to this record: the combination does not justify reopening. A broader ban on legal-change relief would exceed the Rule; a narrower automatic remand would serve no identified unresolved fact.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An intervening interpretation of a habeas limitations provision, without an additional extraordinary circumstance and after the movant abandoned available ordinary review, does not by itself require reopening under Rule 60(b)(6). Gonzalez's identified showing does not satisfy the Rule, even though his motion is procedurally cognizable.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This motion and the grounds actually asserted, not every procedural error or every later change in law.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Abdur'Rahman requires the court to examine this very combination of procedural correction, later law and omitted ordinary review. The absence of a merits adjudication does not eliminate the separate extraordinary-circumstances burden. Gonzalez identifies the later interpretation but no supported further obstacle explaining why his available review was abandoned or why this judgment must exceptionally reopen. On that showing the independent Rule 60 conditions are unmet.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Abdur'Rahman v. Bell is applied both for cognizability and for its independent reopening requirements. No categorical bar on every request involving a later legal change is adopted.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## 95. Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Allapattah Services, Inc.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22; current review request, with revisions authorized in advance.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling decisions and the stated record and jurisdictional limits.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording changes consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning and scope; no alternative disposition authorized.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** In No. 04-70, affirm the Eleventh Circuit's supplemental-jurisdiction ruling. In consolidated No. 04-79, reverse the First Circuit insofar as it excludes the relatives' related claims solely for their individual amount deficiencies, and remand.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit adjudication of the smaller dealer claims subject to Rule 23 and §1367. Restore consideration of the joined family claims under the same statute and Rule 20, without disturbing the qualifying child's claim. On each remand, resolve any remaining preserved jurisdictional predicates, subsection (c) discretion and merits issues through the ordinary proceedings.
* **Remedial Reach:** Both consolidated judgments, limited to the covered class and joined claims. Neither component awards new damages or determines a claimant's entitlement on the underlying contract or injury merits.
* **Practical Relief:** Dealers are not excluded from an otherwise proper class solely because their individual claims are smaller. The relatives regain the opportunity to litigate their related claims with the child's action, subject to the statute and unresolved merits. Neither group receives a payment amount from this jurisdictional disposition.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Smaller related class and joined-plaintiff claims under §1367

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach both consolidated components because each presents related claims failing only the separate amount requirement alongside a qualifying named claim. Preserve citizenship, relatedness, the applicable joinder rules and statutory discretion as independent requirements.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the same statutory grant reaches the smaller dealer claims and the related claims of the child's joined relatives.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the 1990 statutory grant and hold that it displaced the contrary pre-statute amount rule for this category.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the holding must state the text's rule for related diverse claims and its exclusions. A broader authorization despite incomplete diversity would erase an independent statutory condition; a narrower class-label exception to subsection (a) would add an exclusion Congress did not enact.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** When at least one named plaintiff satisfies §1332's amount requirement and the action otherwise meets original-jurisdiction requirements, §1367 authorizes related claims in the same Article III case that fall below the amount threshold unless an enacted exception applies. Neither the Rule 23 class membership in No. 04-70 nor the Rule 20 plaintiff joinder in No. 04-79 creates an exclusion Congress omitted; the smaller claims may proceed subject to the statute and applicable procedural rules.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The related diverse class claims in No. 04-70 and joined family claims in No. 04-79. The rule does not cure incomplete diversity, aggregate separate claims to manufacture the anchor or remove the exclusions Congress specified.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify the qualifying original claim, verify relatedness and citizenship, and then apply the actual subsection (b) and (c) provisions rather than a generalized contamination theory.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Subsection (a) would lose much of its function if original jurisdiction had to exist independently over every claim it supplements. Congress expressly included additional parties and then specified exceptions. City of Chicago applies that statutory structure, while Grupo Dataflux preserves the real citizenship requirement rather than converting every amount deficiency into destruction of all jurisdiction. Zahn's pre-statute rule yields at this defined scope because Congress supplied a broader authorization. The same text applies to Rule 20 plaintiffs: subsection (b) expressly distinguishes claims against joined parties from the specified situations in which added plaintiffs are barred. A court cannot rewrite those lists to preserve the entire pre-enactment amount regime.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Finley v. United States is superseded at the relevant scope by Congress's supplemental grant. Zahn v. International Paper Co. and Clark v. Paul Gray, Inc. no longer require separate amounts for the claims covered by that grant. Grupo Dataflux v. Atlas Global Group and City of Chicago v. International College of Surgeons retain their distinct requirements.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm dismissal of the surviving direct action for want of an applicable §390uu waiver, without adopting an unnecessary beneficiary-merits determination.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Dismiss the direct federal claim under the asserted waiver. Order no delivery of water, contract damages, specific performance or new beneficiary factfinding in a court lacking the required consent.
* **Remedial Reach:** The farmers' surviving action against the United States and its agents under §390uu.
* **Practical Relief:** The challenged suit cannot proceed on that statutory basis. The judgment leaves any independently authorized, properly filed alternative proceeding to its own legal requirements and does not revive the districts' dismissed complaint.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The scope of the reclamation-contract joinder waiver

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve consent first because the surviving action is against the United States and its agents and no qualifying nonfederal controversy requiring joinder is established.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §390uu authorizes this direct contract suit.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the enacted joinder limitation and leave beneficiary merits unresolved.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow: the case turns on a specific procedural form of consent. A broader ruling denying all beneficiary enforcement would decide an unnecessary substantive question; a narrower reliance on unproved contract intent would bypass the dispositive waiver boundary.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 390uu permits the United States to be joined as a necessary defendant in a qualifying reclamation-contract dispute between other parties, but does not itself authorize a stand-alone contract action against the United States and its agents. The farmers' surviving action therefore falls outside this waiver, regardless of their unresolved assertion of beneficiary status.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The asserted §390uu route in this action, not independently authorized contract claims in another proper forum.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Qualifying joinder requires an independent controversy between other parties in which the United States is necessary. An asserted contract benefit does not supply that procedural predicate.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Meyer separates the removal of immunity from the creation of a cause, and Blue Fox requires consent for the actual relief sought. Congress's reference to joining a necessary party describes a real procedural condition. Reading it as general consent to sue the United States alone would discard that limitation. The fact that the water ultimately benefits farmers cannot perform the missing jurisdictional work.
* **Precedent Treatment:** FDIC v. Meyer and Department of the Army v. Blue Fox are applied to the separation of consent and entitlement. No substantive rule about all third-party beneficiaries is adopted.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Alternative statutory waivers lie outside the grant. Beneficiary status requires no merits determination because the action fails on sovereign consent.

## 97. Halbert v. Michigan

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22; current review request, with revisions authorized in advance.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling decisions and the stated record and jurisdictional limits.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording changes consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning and scope; no alternative disposition authorized.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the uncounseled first-tier disposition and remand for constitutionally adequate appellate consideration with appointed counsel.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Michigan must appoint qualified counsel, provide access to the relevant record and restore a meaningful opportunity to prepare and submit the first-tier application under a new filing period. The appellate court must consider that application with the required assistance, using valid frivolousness procedures if appropriate.
* **Remedial Reach:** Halbert's appellate proceeding. The legal rule governs the challenged appointment practice, but no separate classwide injunction is entered in this individual criminal-review case.
* **Practical Relief:** The state court must supply counsel before the renewed application deadline runs and cannot rely on the earlier pro se merits denial as the completed review. No automatic plea withdrawal, new trial or reduced sentence follows.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Counsel for Michigan's first-tier plea review

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the right because Halbert personally requested and was denied assistance before a merits-based first-tier disposition.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the first-tier leave label removes the constitutional advocacy requirement.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the functional first-review principles and extend them expressly to this initial leave process.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the rule must cover the class of indigent plea defendants subjected to the same first-tier merits screen. A broader right to counsel at every discretionary stage would exceed Douglas's basis; a narrower rule limited to Halbert's learning difficulties would overlook the process's general denial of the first advocate function.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An indigent defendant is entitled to appointed counsel for a first-tier application that asks the intermediate appellate court to evaluate the merits of a plea-based conviction or sentence, even when state law calls review discretionary. Michigan must provide that assistance before resolving Halbert's application through a merits screen.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Indigent plea-convicted defendants seeking their first merits review in the Michigan Court of Appeals, subject to a valid waiver and constitutionally adequate frivolousness procedures. Later review remains governed by its distinct rules.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must examine whether the applicant has already received counseled appellate merits consideration and what the current tribunal actually decides. The label leave does not substitute for that examination.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Ross relied on the materials and assistance already supplied in an initial appeal. Halbert has none of that foundation. Smith v. Robbins rejects the proposition that judicial review and a personal submission cure the absence of an advocate at the decisive screen. A plea narrows issues but can leave legally difficult questions about the sentence, plea and representation, exactly the questions the intermediate court assesses.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Douglas v. California and Smith v. Robbins are applied and extended to first-tier leave screening. Ross v. Moffitt is distinguished by prior counseled review and the later court's function.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 2: Whether the plea waived appellate assistance

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach waiver because Michigan invokes it as an alternative ground for sustaining the completed denial.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Halbert knowingly surrendered this separate appellate assistance right.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing particularized waiver rule and reject the asserted waiver on this record.
* **Reach Calibration:** Confined to this record: the warnings and repeated requests do not establish intentional relinquishment. A broader prohibition on all counsel waivers would exceed the case; a narrower inference from the plea alone would dispense with the actual waiver standard.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A guilty or nolo contendere plea does not by itself waive the right to counsel for the first-tier appellate review that remains available, and the State must establish a knowing and intentional surrender of that distinct right. Halbert's colloquy and repeated appointment requests do not establish such a waiver.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Halbert's asserted waiver and similarly situated records lacking a knowing surrender, without deciding every possible express appellate waiver.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** An express waiver in a different record must satisfy the existing knowing and intentional relinquishment requirements for this particular appellate assistance. Neither the guilty-plea label nor a general warning that review requires leave supplies that waiver.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A defendant can accept guilt while contesting a sentence or the legality of the plea process through review the State preserves. The appointment discussion did not clearly identify an intentional surrender of constitutional assistance, and Halbert's later conduct demanded that assistance. Finality cannot replace the missing waiver.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Existing particularized waiver principles are applied without adding an automatic plea-based exception.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## 98. Mayle v. Felix

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22; current review request, with revisions authorized in advance.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling decisions and the stated record and jurisdictional limits.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's relation-back ruling and remand for proceedings consistent with the common-core requirement.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Treat the added interrogation claim as untimely absent a separately preserved and legally sufficient timeliness ground. Leave the original claim's unreviewed merits disposition intact; order no hearing on the new claim's substance merely because it concerns the same conviction.
* **Remedial Reach:** Felix's added claim and the amendment ruling before the Court.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must remove the trial-wide relation-back basis. If no independent preserved timeliness ground remains, it must dismiss the added claim as time barred without purporting to decide whether the statements were actually coerced.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The factual unit for relation back in habeas

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach relation back using the actual original and amended allegations; no separate timeliness basis is established in the granted issue.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the same trial and conviction alone connect these different factual claims under Rule 15.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Adopt the common-operative-facts construction required by the rule's text and the habeas pleading structure.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the rule must distinguish permissible elaboration from preservation of every unpleaded claim. A broader same-conviction approach would defeat the limitation; a narrower same-legal-theory requirement would improperly exclude amendments based on the same events.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An amended habeas claim relates back only when it shares a common core of operative facts with a timely pleaded claim; attacking the same trial or conviction is not enough. Felix's challenge to coercion during his own interrogation does not relate back to the distinct claim concerning a witness's videotaped testimony.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Post-limitations amendments under the operative Rule 15 provision, including new legal theories about the same facts but excluding distinct episodes linked only by the resulting judgment.

* **Precedent Treatment:** United States v. Mead Corp., Barnhart v. Walton and Yellow Transportation are reaffirmed within actual delegations. Neal v. United States is extended only through the stated proposed distinction, not recast as a universal prior holding. Chevron's general framework is not overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A general challenge to Chevron across unrelated statutory delegations lies outside the presented relationship between this earlier judicial construction and the FCC's later interpretation.

##### Issue 2: The offered transmission component under the statutory definitions

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory classification independently because it determines whether the challenged order can survive even apart from Portland's precedential status.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether integration with information capabilities removes the transmission service actually offered for a fee.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Independently construe the statutory boundary and reject the FCC's categorical exclusion on the described offering, even if controlling deference would otherwise be available.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the definition must apply to the actual bundled offering and resist evasion through packaging. A broader rule treating every use of telecommunications as a public transmission offering would overreach; a narrower rule requiring a separately priced transmission product would add words Congress did not enact.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A provider offers telecommunications service when it sells end users the defined transmission of their chosen information between their chosen points, even when that service is bundled with information-processing capabilities and not separately priced. The described cable broadband offering includes that transmission service, so integration alone cannot support the FCC's categorical exclusion from Title II.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The transmission component actually offered to cable broadband subscribers, not every information service that merely uses another carrier's transmission as an input.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Apply the definitions to the service actually offered. A facilities-based seller's public transmission offering differs from an information provider's mere use of a carrier's transmission as an input.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The definition focuses on transmission, user choice and an offering for a fee, not separate billing. The customer pays for the ability to send and receive selected information through the cable network; additional processing capabilities do not erase that offered function. Gulf Power expressly reserved this classification and cannot supply a contrary answer. Regulatory consequences can be addressed through Congress's forbearance mechanism, not by rewriting coverage to avoid them.
* **Precedent Treatment:** National Cable & Telecommunications Association v. Gulf Power Co. is applied at its express reservation. AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board and United States v. Mead Corp. preserve statutory limits on implementation. No previously settled Supreme Court classification is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 3: Reasoned treatment of cable and DSL and the agency remand

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Preserve the independent APA obligation but do not issue an unnecessary final arbitrariness holding after rejecting the classification's legal premise.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What the FCC may decide on remand within the corrected statutory boundary.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply existing reasoned-decisionmaking requirements and reserve the factual implementation choices Congress actually assigned.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow: the legal error requires a new agency implementation within the statute. A broader judicial regulatory plan would usurp assigned choices; a narrower remand leaving the rejected categorical exemption operative would fail to implement the statutory holding.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The FCC must implement the statutory classification through its lawful regulatory and forbearance powers, explaining material technological and market distinctions on the administrative record; policy reasons cannot preserve the invalid categorical exclusion. The independent cable-versus-DSL arbitrariness question need not be finally decided on an agency framework that must be reconsidered.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Implementation of the challenged order after correction of its statutory premise, not every broadband regulation.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Smiley permits reasoned administrative flexibility within an actual assignment. It does not permit classification by preferred policy outcome. The Commission should make the technical and market findings relevant to forbearance and particular duties itself, while the court ensures that the statutory category and enforceable review remain intact.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Smiley v. Citibank is applied within its existing authority and explanation limits; no new general administrative-law test is created.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.
