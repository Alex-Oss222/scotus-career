## 65. Lingle v. Chevron U.S.A. Inc.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded unanimously on May 23, 2005.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** O’Connor wrote the Court’s opinion. Kennedy filed a concurrence emphasizing the separate possibility of a proper substantive-due-process claim.
* **Historical Holding:** The historical Court removed the substantially-advances formulation from takings law and preserved the recognized takings categories and exaction precedents.
* **Historical Authority:** 544 U.S. 528.
* **Historical Sources:** [Historical Supreme Court opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/544/528/).

## 66. Johanns v. Livestock Marketing Association

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded, 6-3 in judgment.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Scalia wrote for Rehnquist, O’Connor, Thomas and Breyer. Thomas and Breyer concurred separately. Ginsburg concurred in the judgment. Kennedy dissented; Souter dissented with Stevens and Kennedy.
* **Historical Holding:** The historical Court held the campaign governmental speech and rejected the compelled-subsidy challenge while preserving other claims and distinguishing an unsupported individual-attribution theory.
* **Historical Authority:** 544 U.S. 550.
* **Historical Sources:** [Historical Supreme Court opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/544/550/).

## 67. Clingman v. Beaver

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 6-3.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Thomas wrote for Rehnquist, Scalia and Kennedy in full, and for O’Connor and Breyer except Part II-A. O’Connor concurred in part and in the judgment, joined by Breyer except her Part III. Stevens dissented with Ginsburg and, as to Parts I through III, Souter.
* **Historical Holding:** The historical Court sustained Oklahoma’s rule as a modest justified burden. Four Justices additionally minimized the association of voters unwilling to disaffiliate; that proposition did not command a majority.
* **Historical Authority:** 544 U.S. 581.
* **Historical Sources:** [Historical Supreme Court opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/544/581/).

## 69. Medellín v. Dretke

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Writ dismissed as improvidently granted, 5-4, on May 23, 2005; no historical merits holding resolving Avena’s domestic force was entered.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Per curiam. Ginsburg concurred, with Scalia joining Part II. O’Connor dissented with Stevens, Souter and Breyer. Souter dissented separately; Breyer dissented with Stevens. The five supporting dismissal were Rehnquist, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg.
* **Historical Holding:** The historical disposition relied on the new Texas proceeding and unresolved federal habeas hurdles. The written dismissal is the historical comparator for this listed merits matter.
* **Historical Authority:** 544 U.S. 660.
* **Historical Sources:** [Historical written dismissal and separate opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/544/660/); [official docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/Search.aspx?FileName=/docketfiles/04-5928.htm).

## 71. Arthur Andersen LLP v. United States

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

## 73. Tory v. Cochran

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Court vacated and remanded, 7-2, permitting substitution and finding a continuing controversy.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Breyer wrote for Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, dissented and favored dismissal as improvidently granted.
* **Historical Holding:** The Court relied on Cochran’s death and the resulting loss or substantial diminution of the decree’s rationale. It declined to decide the basic categorical defamation-injunction question or the decree’s constitutionality before death.
* **Historical Authority:** 544 U.S. 734.
* **Historical Sources:** [Tory v. Cochran, historical opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/544/734/)

## 75. Gonzales v. Raich

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Court vacated and remanded, 6-3.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens wrote for Justices Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Justice Scalia concurred in the judgment. Justice O’Connor dissented, joined by Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justice Thomas except for Part III; Justice Thomas also dissented separately.
* **Historical Holding:** The historical Court sustained federal application to local medical cultivation and possession, emphasizing the broader economic class and interstate drug regime. Justice Scalia emphasized the Necessary and Proper Clause. The dissents challenged the treatment of the local medical class and the adequacy of the federal connection.
* **Historical Authority:** 545 U.S. 1.
* **Historical Sources:** [Gonzales v. Raich, historical opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/545/1/)
