## 79. Johnson v. California, No. 04-6964

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22; current review request, with revisions authorized in advance.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling decisions and the stated record and jurisdictional limits.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording changes consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning and scope; no alternative disposition authorized.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the first-step ruling and remand for proceedings under the inference standard.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the state court to obtain and evaluate the actual reasons through a reliable Batson hearing if that remains possible. If purposeful discrimination is established, or a reliable adjudication cannot now cure the failure to conduct the required inquiry, set aside the conviction and provide a lawful new trial.
* **Remedial Reach:** Johnson's conviction and jury-selection objection. No classwide policy injunction is before the Court.
* **Practical Relief:** The state court must promptly determine whether a reliable hearing can be held, require the prosecutor's explanations, permit meaningful challenge and make an ultimate finding. It cannot simply reapply the rejected probability threshold or presume that facially neutral reconstructed reasons are true.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The showing required before the prosecutor must explain strikes

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the constitutional issue because the state judgment is now final and the threshold objection was preserved.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What showing requires the strike proponent to provide an explanation.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Clarify the existing Batson procedure by rejecting the proposed probability threshold.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: a uniform first-stage rule is needed for workable jury selection. A broader presumption that every racial pattern proves liability would collapse the later stages; a narrower correction confined to three strikes would leave the erroneous legal threshold in place.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A defendant triggers the duty to explain a peremptory strike by presenting facts that support an inference of purposeful discrimination, without first proving discrimination more likely than not. Johnson's showing that every eligible Black prospective juror was struck is sufficient in the circumstances to require explanations; the ultimate burden of proving purposeful discrimination remains his.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The first-stage showing for a preserved racial-discrimination objection, not a numerical quota or automatic new-trial rule for every disproportionate strike pattern.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** An initial inference triggers explanation; facial neutrality at the second stage does not establish credibility at the third. The objector retains the ultimate burden of proving purposeful discrimination.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The prosecutor controls the immediate explanation for the choice. Requiring the opponent to establish probable intent before obtaining that explanation defeats the procedure's evidentiary function. Franklyn and Gonzalez concern evaluation of the ultimate finding and therefore do not justify a preliminary probability test. The prior Johnson decision expressly left these merits unresolved because finality was absent.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Batson is applied and clarified at its first stage. Johnson v. California remains controlling on finality but no longer bars this completed state appeal. United States v. Franklyn and United States v. Gonzalez retain their ultimate-finding rules.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## 80. Bradshaw v. Stumpf

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22; current review request, with revisions authorized in advance.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling decisions and the stated record and jurisdictional limits.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording changes consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning and scope; no alternative disposition authorized.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the conviction-relief ruling based on inadequate understanding of intent, and remand for independent consideration of the death-sentence claim.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the plea against the adjudicated ground. Require the habeas court to determine the competing-account sentencing claim under the applicable pre-AEDPA standards; if a material constitutional sentencing violation is established, issue a conditional writ requiring lawful resentencing within a reasonable specified period or removal of the death sentence.
* **Remedial Reach:** Stumpf's conviction and death judgment only. No broader prosecution-policy injunction is before the Court.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must separately identify the factual findings and prejudice supporting its sentencing disposition. It may not treat a valid plea as a reason to skip that inquiry, and it may not order unconditional release from a valid aggravated-murder conviction based solely on a penalty-phase defect.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Understanding the aggravated-murder intent element

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the plea claim under the proper pre-AEDPA standard; the record contains counsel's explanation and an identified state-law basis for accomplice liability.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the refusal to admit personally firing the shots makes this plea constitutionally uninformed.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the established actual-understanding standard and distinguish intent from shooter identity.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow: this record supports rejecting the particular misunderstanding inference. A broader conclusive presumption from counsel's representation would defeat actual proof of misunderstanding; a narrower insistence on the judge's own formula would replace substance with ceremony.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A plea is knowing as to an offense element when the record establishes that counsel adequately explained it and the defendant understood it, even though the judge did not personally provide a prescribed recital. Stumpf's denial of being the shooter does not by itself negate understanding of purposeful aggravated-murder complicity, and the identified record does not support vacating the plea on that ground.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The understanding-of-intent claim, not every possible challenge to voluntariness or the subsequent death sentence.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Henderson protects a defendant who lacked notice of an essential element. It does not transform every dispute over a crime's physical details into absence of notice. The legal significance of Stumpf's participation and purpose must be assessed under Ohio's actual offense, not an assumed requirement that every guilty principal personally fire the fatal shots.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Henderson v. Morgan and the established plea cases are applied to actual notice; no precedent is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 2: Competing prosecution accounts and the death sentence

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the need for independent sentencing adjudication; the lower court's treatment did not resolve all necessary facts and legal consequences.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether validation of the plea disposes of the claim that the State's competing accounts undermine the death sentence.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing requirement of reliable, individualized capital sentencing and remand the unresolved factual application.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: distinguish guilt from punishment and require a genuine sentencing inquiry. A broader automatic reversal rule for any alternative theory is unsupported; a narrower refusal to examine the death sentence would leave the material claim unanswered.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An informed aggravated-murder plea does not dispose of a claim that the State obtained a death sentence through materially false or irreconcilable factual accounts affecting the defendant's individual culpability. The habeas court must identify the actual accounts, the State's knowledge, the sentencing use and the applicable prejudice before deciding whether the death judgment may stand.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The challenged death sentence and the pleaded constitutional grounds, without reopening guilt merely because shooter identity is disputed.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Knowing-falsity claims require proof of the State's knowledge and materiality under the governing due-process rule; lawful guilt and individualized selection of death remain separate inquiries.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The guilt element concerns purposeful participation. Selection of death can depend on a finer assessment of personal responsibility. Treating the first question as conclusive of the second would erase that distinction. A remand is warranted because the needed adjudication concerns evidence and actual sentencing reliance, not a legal assumption about accomplice liability.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Existing due-process and individualized-sentencing protections are applied; no new categorical inconsistent-theories prohibition is represented as entering law.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A general rule covering all differing theories across trials lies beyond the factual findings available here.

## 82. Wilkinson v. Austin

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22; current review request, with revisions authorized in advance.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling decisions and the stated record and jurisdictional limits.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording changes consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning and scope; no alternative disposition authorized.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Sustain recognition of the liberty interest on the Sandin ground, vacate unnecessary procedural additions, and remand for a decree ensuring meaningful application of the adequate New Policy to the affected class.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The district court must remove unsupported procedural and substantive commands while requiring prompt review of any continuing placement never subjected to constitutionally adequate process. It must identify any additional measure it retains by the proved defect it cures and the statutory requirements for prison relief.
* **Remedial Reach:** The policy and class it governs, because the challenged system and common deficiencies are before the Court. Ohio bears the showing that a narrower remedy fully cures an established continuing policy violation.
* **Practical Relief:** Ohio must provide the stated notice, rebuttal and review procedures and complete necessary corrective reviews on an enforceable schedule set by the district court. Compliance is shown by actual notices, reasons and review decisions, not an assurance that a policy exists. No automatic transfer of every OSP prisoner follows.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The protected interest in avoiding OSP placement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the liberty question because the State's defined placement predicates and the record of severe continuing restraint permit applying the existing rule.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Ohio has created a protected interest in avoiding OSP placement except on the substantive grounds it authorizes.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Sandin's substantive-constraint rule and Mathews's separate procedural inquiry.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the rule covers the actual constrained supermax assignment. A broader interest in every prison preference would erase significance and substantive limits; a narrower focus on regulatory wording would repeat the mechanical approach Sandin rejected.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A prisoner has a protected liberty interest when state law meaningfully limits the grounds for imposing the significant restraint of indefinite OSP confinement and loss of otherwise available parole eligibility. Ohio's identified placement predicates and the consequences proved here establish that interest; a promise of procedures alone would not.
* **Reach Within the Question:** OSP assignment under the substantive criteria before the Court, without a universal right to remain in a particular prison.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Sandin requires a genuine restriction on state authority and a meaningful deprivation. Ohio ties exceptional placement to identified security predicates rather than an unrestricted choice; those limits, indefinite isolation and loss of parole eligibility together establish the interest. The promised hearings alone do not create it.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Sandin v. Conner is applied at its controlling scope. No new atypical-hardship threshold is adopted.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 2: Process and the scope of the federal injunction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the challenged procedural additions, while preserving the need to cure established placements never subjected to adequate review.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the replacement policy provides constitutionally sufficient decision and review procedures.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing balancing and remedial-fit rules to uphold the New Policy's designed protections, subject to meaningful implementation.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the opinion should state what makes this process adequate and what would defeat that conclusion. A broader immunity for any written policy would ignore actual operation; a narrower endorsement of every court-imposed detail would displace a sufficient state remedy.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Due process permits Ohio's New Policy when prisoners receive usable notice of the actual placement grounds, a meaningful chance to rebut them, reasoned multi-level review and genuine periodic reconsideration. On the described design, additional mandatory witness procedures are not constitutionally necessary; ongoing placements never given that process must receive it promptly.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The class governed by the challenged OSP policy and the continuing consequences of proved deficient placements.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts apply this functional requirement to implementation claims; security-based limits must preserve a meaningful opportunity to answer the grounds rather than merely reproduce forms.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mathews requires weighing the prisoner's substantial interest against error risk, the likely value of added safeguards and their security and administrative costs. Usable notice, rebuttal, a terminating negative recommendation and recurring review substantially reduce erroneous placement. Mandatory witness procedures add limited value to predictive classification while exposing witnesses to retaliation. Lewis permits Ohio to implement this sufficient cure, subject to §3626 and proof that ongoing placements actually receive the required process.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Mathews v. Eldridge, the relevant prison-process precedents and Lewis v. Casey are applied without creating a right to witnesses in every classification hearing.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## 83. Miller-El v. Dretke

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22; current review request, with revisions authorized in advance.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling decisions and the stated record and jurisdictional limits.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording changes consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning and scope; no alternative disposition authorized.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit's merits judgment and remand with instructions to grant conditional habeas relief.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require Texas to retry Miller-El before a lawfully selected jury within a reasonable period fixed by the district court or release him from custody under the invalid conviction. The judgment does not require acquittal or bar a lawful retrial.
* **Remedial Reach:** Miller-El's conviction and sentence. No separate classwide prosecution-policy injunction is presented.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must enter a conditional writ with a definite retrial deadline and retain authority to enforce it. The death sentence cannot remain executable under the invalid conviction while the State decides whether to retry.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Purposeful exclusion on the combined record under AEDPA

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the merits now that the authorized appeal has produced a merits judgment. Apply the state-record restrictions and the factual presumption rather than recycling the earlier certificate result.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the state finding of nondiscrimination survives the statutory review on this combined evidence.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the settled prohibition and habeas standards to conclude that the finding cannot stand.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the rule must make whole-record review effective in recurring comparative-evidence cases. A broader automatic invalidation based on disparity or history would dispense with individual proof; a narrower strike-by-strike isolation would prevent the evidence from proving the intent it collectively establishes.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A state finding of nondiscriminatory jury selection rests on an unreasonable factual determination under §2254(d)(2) when the full state-court record renders the prosecutor's actual explanations untenable. Miller-El satisfies that condition and separately rebuts the relevant factual finding by clear and convincing evidence under §2254(e)(1): selective treatment of materially comparable jurors, disparate questioning, shuffles and corroborating office practices establish purposeful exclusion. Neither statutory showing is replaced by the earlier certificate ruling.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This preserved Batson claim on the state-court record; no requirement that all jurors be identical or that every strike pattern meet a fixed ratio.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must compare jurors against the reasons actually offered and evaluate supported alternative explanations in context. They may not supply new prosecutorial motives merely to preserve a finding. Apply Miller-El v. Cockrell's distinct statutory inquiries and their respective burdens without converting one provision into the other; the present claim satisfies both.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The prior Miller-El decision explained why the categories of evidence mattered but stopped at debate. At the merits stage, the comparisons expose reasons applied selectively, while questioning and selection practices corroborate that selective treatment. Historical office evidence is not doing the work alone. Franklyn and Gonzalez preserve the actual factual inquiry; they do not require accepting a reason that the relevant record defeats. Deference protects reasonable findings, not insulation from cumulative proof.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Miller-El v. Cockrell is applied at its express certificate-versus-merits boundary. Batson is enforced; United States v. Franklyn and United States v. Gonzalez retain their fact-specific approach.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## 84. Grable & Sons Metal Products, Inc. v. Darue Engineering & Manufacturing

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22; current review request, with revisions authorized in advance.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling decisions and the stated record and jurisdictional limits.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording changes consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning and scope; no alternative disposition authorized.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit's jurisdictional judgment within the question granted.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Sustain the federal court's authority to adjudicate the quiet-title claim. No remand to state court is required on the asserted absence of a federal cause of action, and no new damages or title remedy is ordered by this jurisdictional holding.
* **Remedial Reach:** The litigated quiet-title action between Grable and Darue.
* **Practical Relief:** The federal adjudication is not vacated for lack of jurisdiction. Any remaining preserved merits matter proceeds in the court possessing statutory authority; this ruling supplies no independent finding that a notice defect occurred.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A necessary federal issue embedded in a state cause of action

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide jurisdiction first; the complaint's title theory makes the federal notice question necessary and the parties genuinely dispute its meaning.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this narrow embedded federal tax issue supports §1331 jurisdiction.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** State a complete rule reconciling the surviving embedded-issue authorities and apply it to sustain jurisdiction.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: a usable jurisdictional rule is needed beyond a case-specific intuition. A broader federal-ingredient rule would absorb ordinary state litigation; a narrower categorical private-action requirement would erase the surviving Smith route without a sufficient statutory basis.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A state-law claim arises under federal law when it necessarily raises an actually disputed federal issue substantial to the federal system and federal adjudication is consistent with the allocation of judicial business Congress established. Grable's challenge to a federal tax-sale title satisfies those requirements; the absence of a separate federal cause of action does not by itself defeat jurisdiction.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The small class of state claims meeting every identified condition, including this tax-title claim. Ordinary fact-bound state tort claims borrowing a federal standard do not qualify merely for that reason.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify the federal issue required by the complaint and explain its institutional importance and jurisdictional consequences. A party seeking the federal forum bears the burden of establishing those grounds.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Smith preserves an embedded-issue route. Merrell Dow makes congressional remedial choices and the volume of displaced state litigation relevant rather than irrelevant. Here the legal validity of federal tax-sale procedures directly affects federal revenue administration, and recognizing jurisdiction does not create a general federal forum for state property disputes. The complete inquiry gives effect to both precedents.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Smith v. Kansas City Title & Trust is reaffirmed. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals v. Thompson is explained as a context-sensitive limitation, not overruled. Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Environment is applied to the order of adjudication.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## 85. Golden v. Golden

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22; current review request, with revisions authorized in advance.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling decisions and the stated record and jurisdictional limits.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording changes consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning and scope; no alternative disposition authorized.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the sufficiency of the pleaded jurisdictional amount and preservation of the already surviving personal claims; vacate dismissal of the challenged counts insofar as it rests on a general estate-interference theory, and remand for the stated claim-by-claim analysis. Preserve dismissal of any actual probate or estate-administration demand.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit jurisdictionally proper personal claims to proceed under the limited exception, retaining the supported amount determination. Exclude any requested federal probate, annulment, estate administration or control of property in state-court custody; apply any established preclusion and state-law failure separately. The remand awards no damages and itself changes no distribution.
* **Remedial Reach:** The named claimants, defendants and pleaded counts. No administration order binding the estate or absent beneficiaries is authorized.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must identify each requested remedy, retain any count meeting the stated requirements, and proceed through the usual pleading, evidence and merits stages. It may not retain a personal claim while assuming authority to redistribute property in probate custody.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Will-substitute trusts and the boundary between probate and personal liability

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the exception because it supplied the dismissal ground and the complaint contains materially different requests for adjudication.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether will-substitute status or inconsistency with probate findings alone bars the pleaded personal claims.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Clarify the unresolved boundary by preserving a limited probate exception and rejecting a general interference-with-estate-affairs category.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the rule must direct claim-by-claim handling of recurring tort suits. A broader abolition of the probate exception would disregard its settled core; a narrower permission for only a named tort would leave remedy-equivalent claims subject to arbitrary labels.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A will-substitute trust and an estate-related personal claim fall within the probate exception only to the extent the requested federal adjudication would probate or annul a will, administer an estate or exercise control over property in a state court's custody. Trust status does not itself expand or remove that exception. Fraud, undue-influence, forgery and fiduciary-duty claims seeking personal liability are not jurisdictionally barred merely because deciding them entails findings inconsistent with a probate determination; actual preclusion and state-law sufficiency must be assessed separately. Golden's dismissed counts therefore require classification by the adjudication and remedy demanded, not the circuit's general interference theory.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The inter vivos trust functioning as a will substitute and every pleaded personal theory within the accepted questions, including fraud, undue influence, forgery and fiduciary duty. The rule does not authorize removal of an executor, redistribution of an estate or control over property already in the state court merely because a claimant also requests damages.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify the relief each count actually seeks. A personal judgment for fraud or breach of duty may proceed when adjudication neither administers the estate nor takes property from the state court; a demand for those prohibited acts must be excluded even when attached to a permissible damages count.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Ankenbrandt rejects substitution of a broad family-subject exclusion for enacted jurisdiction, while Markham protects the concrete functions of probate and property custody. The trust's will-substitute role does not identify whether a federal personal judgment would perform one of those forbidden acts. Nor does the circuit's inferred acceptance of the addendum turn every inconsistent finding into federal annulment. Section 1738 must supply any genuinely applicable preclusion; Pennsylvania's allocation of claims to a specialized division cannot enlarge the federal exception. A demand to restore assets to a state-controlled estate remains different from recovery against a wrongdoer personally.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Markham v. Allen is applied to its probate and custody limits. Ankenbrandt v. Richards is extended as a jurisdictional method, not converted into a prior probate decision.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 2: Amount, state judgments and independent jurisdictional objections

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Address these objections because removing the probate bar does not supply missing statutory jurisdiction.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What must the district court decide before adjudicating the surviving personal claims.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing legal-certainty rule, preserve the pleaded amount's sufficiency and require separate treatment of any actual state-judgment effect.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow: the record supports the amount allegations through legally available punitive damages, while the state judgment's precise effect requires its actual terms. A broader conclusion that every pleaded tort succeeds would decide unadjudicated merits; a narrower remand reopening the sufficient amount on speculation would ignore its independent basis.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The pleaded amount satisfies §1332 when nonfrivolous, legally available punitive damages prevent a legal-certainty finding that the claim is below the threshold, even if the alleged compensatory loss alone is smaller. Golden's amount allegations meet that rule. Any actual Pennsylvania judgment receives its proper §1738 effect, but an unproved or merely inferred state adjudication cannot supply a blanket jurisdictional dismissal.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The claims remaining after the probate-remedy classification, not new unpleaded claims or absent beneficiaries' rights.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The amount requirement concerns a bona fide legally available claim, not proof of the eventual recovery. The intentional and reckless conduct alleged can support punitive damages under the state law identified in the record, so the tax-return arithmetic does not establish legal certainty below the threshold. Dodd v. Hood River County independently requires identification of the state issue actually and necessarily decided before preclusion operates. Those inquiries do not replace each other.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Ankenbrandt v. Richards and Dodd v. Hood River County are applied within their distinct jurisdictional and preclusion roles.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.
