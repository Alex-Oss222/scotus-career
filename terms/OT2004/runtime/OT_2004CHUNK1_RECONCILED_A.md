## 3. United States v. $100,348.00 in U.S. Currency — November 15, 2004

The source confirms the preflight's qualifications:

- Mayzel's own lawful possession and duties give the first-party argument legal substance. The entrusted bailment concerns $100,000; the res totals $100,348. Do not turn the amount distinction into an adjudicated finding that he owned the remaining $348, or that every dollar was entrusted by Amiel. The full-amount challenge rests on his protected possessory interest, including the established bailment, without a universal rule for unexplained custody. State law identifies the interest; federal law decides whether the punitive deprivation burdens his own protected interest.
- The unproved purported disclaimer does not establish that his duties disappeared. Delivery duties, slight care and possible gross-negligence responsibility are distinct from a completed personal damages judgment for the entire fund. No such judgment is required or found.
- The criminal reporting acquittal reflects failure to prove knowledge of the reporting requirement beyond reasonable doubt. It does not vacate the separate civil forfeitability determination or establish innocence for every purpose. The forfeiture is tied to the reporting offense; section 1001 supplies a conviction relevant to supported culpability, not a second forfeiture predicate.
- The lower hypothetical Guidelines maximum is $5,000 at level 6; the alternative is $30,000 at level 12. Level 6 requires no knowledge or belief that the funds were unlawful proceeds, no reckless disregard of their source, lawful proceeds, and intended lawful use. Only lawful source was explicitly found. Failure to inquire is evidence relevant to culpability, not a conclusive finding of Guidelines recklessness, unlawful purpose or another crime.
- The source's arithmetic-compromise description of $10,000 does not supply the constitutional rule. Neither $5,000 nor $30,000 is an adjudicated sentence in this civil proceeding. The actual lesser exaction can be sustained under either benchmark without inventing the missing findings; no fixed ratio or midpoint is adopted.
- CAFRA does not govern this proceeding. The lower majority's separate reliance on its later claimant-friendly policy is unnecessary to, and does not enter, the reconciled first-party holding. The owner's filing default, statutory forfeitability and fees remain outside the presented merits questions.

## 5. Anderson v. Spear — November 30, 2004

### Comparator status, statutory separation and remedy

There is no Supreme Court merits comparator. The [Sixth Circuit's opinion, 356 F.3d 651](https://openjurist.org/356/f3d/651/anderson-v-e-spear), checked against the [reporter transcription](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/356/356.F3d.651.02-5529.html), supplies the actual appellate judgment, the competing grounds and the statutory recitations. Batchelder's panel authorship supplies no Supreme Court authorship or alignment. Parts II, III, VI and VIII and the notes were checked for the presented matters. The other six claims are not additional Supreme Court questions.

Component A is the election-day electioneering prohibition at the polling place and within 500 feet of a county clerk's office or an entrance to a building containing a voting machine. Its speech coverage includes signs, campaign literature, cards, handbills, petition signatures and solicitation of votes. Exit polling is excluded. The private-property exception is confined to electioneering inside a private residence or establishment other than the polling premises, by persons with an ownership interest. It is not a general outdoor, invited-speaker or public-sidewalk exception. The supplied enforcement interpretation reaches write-in instructions. All eight reject this unsupported reach; the six common-method joins apply the controlling *Burson* strict-scrutiny rule. Scalia and Thomas preserve their distinct grounds as specified below.

Component B is the $50,000 personal-loan ceiling under sections 121.150(13) and (21). Component C is section 121.150(16)'s prohibition on candidates or their agents soliciting or accepting contributions after the general election. The initial use of a candidate's own money and a later donor's repayment contribution remain different transactions. A later contribution can create personal benefit and corruption risk; that does not establish a sufficient fit for either the initial personal-funding cap or the complete timing ban. The lower court's statements that a losing candidate necessarily presents virtually no risk, or that the ordinary donor ceiling eliminates every corruption concern, are not adopted as facts or rules.

All eight retain invalidation of A, B and C. The formal disposition affirms the pertinent invalidations, vacates the lower disposition insofar as it imposes a universal express-advocacy-only construction, and remands for precise declaratory and injunctive relief and lawful state-law severability. Part III actually imposed that construction; declining to carry it into the mandate does not invent a new lower-court event. Agreement that the challenged speech restriction fails does not authorize federal insertion of new Kentucky statutory words. No replacement distance, donor ceiling, payment deadline or debt-repayment code is selected.

The appropriation suspension does not abolish the three presented restrictions. No new repeal, candidacy, surviving debt amount, filing, individual waiver or mootness fact is presumed. The prepared live-controversy and timely-review premises retain their stated limits. Lawful donor amount, source, true-source and disclosure restrictions remain distinct, as do intimidation, vote-buying, valid polling-entrance safeguards and administration of the polling room.
