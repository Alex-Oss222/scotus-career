## Reconciled result and exact rule

Retain all eight Associate commitments to reverse the Ninth Circuit's authorization of an additional section 1983 action enforcing the same TCA siting restriction, and remand to remove that route and section 1988 fees resting only upon it. The direct TCA permit relief remains undisturbed. No Justice here supports reversal of that favorable permit judgment, a new merits decision on an unreviewed siting theory, or a decision resolving the availability of every direct-TCA remedy or independent fee basis.

For the reviewed claim, assume without deciding a qualifying individual statutory right. Blessing still separates that inquiry from congressional foreclosure. The express personal remedy in section 332(c)(7)(B)(v), its thirty-day filing condition measured from the specified final action or failure to act, and its compulsory expedited hearing and decision establish an enforcement arrangement incompatible with the additional ordinary section 1983 action claimed here. The conflict is concrete: ordinary section 1983 litigation does not inherit those requirements merely because the underlying right arises under the TCA. The invitation to borrow the thirty-day period cannot reconstruct a hybrid cause of action without a legal basis. The conclusion requires no choice between the otherwise potentially applicable longer section 1983 limitations rules.

The separate clause permitting an FCC petition for the radio-frequency violation specified in clause (iv) does not displace the competent-court route for the written-decision/substantial-evidence obligation in clause (iii). The court-action and agency-petition provisions retain their distinct subjects. Section 601(c)(1) leaves other law in operation but does not require a new TCA entitlement to acquire a second inconsistent enforcement route. Independently secured rights remain subject to their own normal enforcement requirements; the present case adjudicates no independent constitutional injury.

The absence of a direct express fee provision and the additional damages sought reinforce the statutory setting but do not independently prove a universal no-damages or foreclosure rule. Section 1988 fees fail here because the asserted section 1983 statutory predicate fails. The precise direct-TCA damages question is outside the remedial-route issue resolved; it is neither silently answered nor used as an indispensable premise. The source of the existing permit relief remains the direct TCA action.
