## 79. Johnson v. California, No. 04-6964

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Johnson v. California, No. 04-6964
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-6964; 545 U.S. 162
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-13 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a defendant must establish that purposeful discrimination is more likely than not before the prosecutor must explain challenged peremptory strikes under Batson.
* **Question Granted:** Whether a defendant must establish that purposeful discrimination is more likely than not before the prosecutor must explain challenged peremptory strikes under Batson.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The California Supreme Court sustained a first-step standard requiring a strong likelihood of discrimination, construed as more likely than not. After the earlier nonfinal appeal, the remaining state appellate proceedings concluded and the present petition followed.
* **Relief Requested:** Johnson seeks reversal of the threshold ruling and adjudication under a first-step inference standard. California seeks affirmance of the heightened threshold and the conviction.
* **Material Record:** The venire included three prospective Black jurors among forty-three eligible jurors. The prosecutor used peremptory challenges to remove all three. Johnson objected during jury selection, but the trial court did not require the prosecutor to provide reasons under the standard it applied. The racial pattern supports an inquiry; it does not itself establish the ultimate finding of intentional discrimination. The absence of contemporaneous explanations matters to what a reliable remand can accomplish.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is the Batson case, not the distinct prison-segregation case with the same caption. The prior Johnson v. California dismissed for lack of a final state judgment under §1257 and decided no Batson merits. Completion of the remaining state proceedings and denial of review on October 20, 2004 supply the materially different finality posture for this petition.
* **Relevant Dates:** The California Supreme Court's initial decision was June 30, 2003. The prior jurisdictional dismissal occurred May 3, 2004. State review of the completed appellate proceedings was denied October 20, 2004. The current merits event is June 13, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Miller-El v. Dretke concerns an ultimate discrimination finding on federal habeas review after extensive explanations and evidence. Johnson concerns the earlier duty to obtain explanations on direct review. Neither case's scheduled brief supplies an effective decision for the other.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** A relevant earlier effective same-term jury-selection decision would require incorporation. Miller-El is listed for the same date, but neither is effective in the current baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1257 requires a final state judgment. Batson separates an inference triggering an explanation, a facially neutral explanation, and the ultimate determination of purposeful discrimination; the defendant retains the ultimate burden.

##### Issue 1: The showing required before the prosecutor must explain strikes

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Batson's prohibition on purposeful racial exclusion remains operative. United States v. Franklyn and United States v. Gonzalez preserve review of the actual ultimate discrimination finding rather than replacing it with a categorical inference from one circumstance. The prior Johnson v. California decided only finality and supplies no heightened first-step rule.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing rules preserve purposeful-discrimination proof and the distinct roles of evidentiary thresholds and ultimate findings. A burden at an initial stage must not be represented as the burden governing the final factual adjudication.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Equal Protection Clause prohibits purposeful racial selection of jurors. The question is the administration of that prohibition, not a statutory entitlement to a fixed racial composition.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The ultimate prohibition and burden are settled. Entering law has not adopted California's more-likely-than-not requirement as the condition for obtaining an explanation.
* **Open Point:** Whether the first-stage showing is an inference of discrimination or preliminary proof exceeding a fifty-percent likelihood.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The showing required before the prosecutor must explain strikes

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** California treated a strong likelihood as requiring more than an inference and equated it with showing discrimination was more likely than not. The trial court consequently did not solicit explanations for the challenged strikes.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** Johnson argues that the initial stage must permit an inference from the surrounding facts; requiring probable discrimination before hearing the prosecutor's reasons defeats the explanatory procedure and shifts inaccessible information onto the opponent.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** California argues that a meaningful threshold protects legitimate peremptory challenges from routine explanation demands and that the defendant must present more than speculation based solely on the excluded jurors' race.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** An inference requires facts, not an automatic assumption that every strike against a minority juror is unlawful. Requiring an explanation does not decide whether that explanation is credible or whether discrimination occurred.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** No distinct material proposal is verified in the sources identified here.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** This is the Batson case, not the distinct prison-segregation case with the same caption. The prior Johnson v. California dismissed for lack of a final state judgment under §1257 and decided no Batson merits. Completion of the remaining state proceedings and denial of review on October 20, 2004 supply the materially different finality posture for this petition.

##### Issue 1: The showing required before the prosecutor must explain strikes

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Reverse the threshold and remand for a reliable Batson adjudication; affirm if the heightened standard is lawful; or identify an independently adequate final finding if the record actually contains one. Here the lack of required explanations prevents treating the first-step ruling as a completed neutral-reason adjudication.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule defining the first-stage burden governs peremptory-challenge objections while leaving ultimate proof and appellate deference to actual factual findings intact.

* **Record, Source, Posture, and Legal Uncertainty:** A retrospective hearing must be capable of reliable factual findings, not an invitation to accept untested reconstructed explanations. The record here supplies no final determination of intentional discrimination or of the feasibility of a hearing.

## 80. Bradshaw v. Stumpf

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Bradshaw v. Stumpf
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-637; 545 U.S. 175
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-13 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Stumpf's aggravated-murder plea was knowing and intelligent, and what consequence follows from the State's use of allegedly inconsistent accounts of who shot the victim in separate proceedings.
* **Question Granted:** Whether Stumpf's aggravated-murder plea was knowing and intelligent, and what consequence follows from the State's use of allegedly inconsistent accounts of who shot the victim in separate proceedings.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit granted habeas relief on the understanding-of-intent issue and treated the prosecutor's differing accounts as affecting the plea and death judgment. The State seeks review of the reversal of the district court's denial of relief.
* **Relief Requested:** Ohio seeks reinstatement of the judgment denying habeas relief. Stumpf seeks relief from his conviction and death sentence, or at least independent adjudication of the sentencing consequences of the competing accounts.
* **Material Record:** Stumpf and Wesley participated in an armed crime in which Mrs. Stout was killed. Stumpf pleaded guilty to aggravated murder while disputing that he fired the fatal shots. Counsel represented that the offense and its intent element had been explained to him; the plea proceeding also addressed his understanding. Ohio law permitted aggravated-murder responsibility without identifying Stumpf as the actual shooter if the requisite participation and intent were present. In Wesley's later trial, the State presented evidence identifying Wesley as the shooter, including an informant's account. The relative responsibility issue may matter differently to knowing guilt and individualized capital sentencing.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The plea's validity and the death sentence must be analyzed separately. A defendant can understand and admit purposeful participation without admitting he personally fired the shots. The nature of the later evidence, what prosecutors knew, the theories actually advanced and their effect on sentencing cannot be inferred from a conclusory inconsistency label.
* **Relevant Dates:** The crime and plea occurred in the 1980s. This habeas application preceded the April 24, 1996 enactment of the Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996 (AEDPA); the relevant pre-enactment merits rules apply under Lindh v. Murphy. The Sixth Circuit decision under review issued in 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material beyond the authorities addressed below.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** A valid guilty plea requires a voluntary and intelligent admission of the offense, including adequate understanding of its elements. Due process separately prohibits knowing use of false material evidence. Capital sentencing requires reliable individualized consideration; valid guilt does not automatically validate the death choice.

##### Issue 1: Understanding the aggravated-murder intent element

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The established plea rules of Brady v. United States, Boykin v. Alabama and Henderson v. Morgan require an informed plea but do not demand that the judge personally recite every element in a prescribed script. Lindh v. Murphy preserves the appropriate pre-AEDPA merits regime for this earlier application.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing inquiry concerns actual adequate notice and understanding, based on the whole plea record. The constitutional right cannot be reduced to the presence or absence of a particular judicial phrase.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment and the then-applicable Ohio aggravated-murder and complicity provisions determine the element Stumpf needed to understand. Pre-AEDPA habeas provisions govern the merits review of this application.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** An explanation by competent counsel may establish notice; it does not conclusively answer credible contrary evidence that the defendant lacked understanding.
* **Open Point:** Whether this plea record establishes a lack of understanding despite counsel's explanation and the distinction between intent and personal triggerman status.

##### Issue 2: Competing prosecution accounts and the death sentence

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The due-process prohibition on knowing use of false material evidence remains binding. Existing capital cases preserve a separate inquiry into death eligibility and death selection; a lawful conviction or surviving aggravator does not automatically cure a sentencing defect.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Materiality and prejudice must be assessed at the decision actually challenged. Claims about false evidence, contradictory advocacy and relative culpability require their own factual predicates and cannot be merged into a universal prohibition on alternative arguments.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment governs prosecutorial fairness; the Eighth Amendment and applicable Ohio sentencing law govern individualized capital punishment.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Knowing falsity and materiality are established grounds for relief. The consequences of these particular accounts require a reliable description of what was advanced and what the sentencer relied on.
* **Open Point:** Whether the State's later evidence and arguments undermine the lawful basis for selecting death for Stumpf, even though they do not invalidate his informed plea.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Understanding the aggravated-murder intent element

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The Sixth Circuit inferred an inadequate understanding of specific intent from the plea materials and Stumpf's denial that he shot Mrs. Stout.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** Ohio argues that counsel explained the elements, Stumpf affirmed understanding, and his refusal to admit being the shooter does not negate purposeful complicity under state law.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** Stumpf argues that the record does not show an informed admission of the required intent and that the State's shifting account exposes the uncertainty underlying the plea.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** A solemn plea is not irrebuttable proof of understanding. Conversely, disagreement about who pulled the trigger does not itself negate knowledge of an offense that can be committed through purposeful participation.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** No distinct material proposal is verified in the sources identified here.

##### Issue 2: Competing prosecution accounts and the death sentence

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The Sixth Circuit linked the changing account to the plea and sentence. The independent sentencing effect was not adequately separated from its erroneous understanding analysis.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** Ohio argues that both defendants could possess the required intent and that later evidence does not retroactively make the earlier plea uninformed or require automatic reversal.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** Stumpf argues that the State relied on materially incompatible factual claims to obtain death and that the later account changes the assessment of his personal responsibility.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** Alternative permissible inferences from uncertain evidence are not automatically knowing falsehood. The State cannot secure punishment through factual representations it knows are false or suppress the material basis for evaluating relative culpability.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** No distinct material proposal is verified in the sources identified here.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** The plea's validity and the death sentence must be analyzed separately. A defendant can understand and admit purposeful participation without admitting he personally fired the shots. The nature of the later evidence, what prosecutors knew, the theories actually advanced and their effect on sentencing cannot be inferred from a conclusory inconsistency label.

##### Issue 1: Understanding the aggravated-murder intent element

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Reject the asserted notice defect and preserve the conviction against that ground; grant plea relief if the actual record establishes misunderstanding of a necessary element; or remand for a material unresolved factual issue. The sentencing claim must not be disposed of solely by validating the plea.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule allowing proof of understanding through counsel and the whole plea record, while retaining relief for actual misunderstanding, does not authorize uninformed pleas or require ritual questioning.

##### Issue 2: Competing prosecution accounts and the death sentence

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Remand the distinct sentencing claim for findings and proper legal analysis; sustain the sentence if no actionable and prejudicial inconsistency is established; or grant conditional sentence relief if the constitutional violation is proved. Validating guilt alone cannot resolve these alternatives.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court can require phase-specific treatment without deciding a general rule for every inconsistency between separate criminal trials.

* **Record, Source, Posture, and Legal Uncertainty:** The remand must establish the precise prosecution presentations and sentencing effects rather than assume deliberate falsity from different witnesses. The record does not justify ordering immediate release or finding the capital claim ultimately successful.

## 81. Merck KGaA v. Integra Lifesciences I, Ltd.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Merck KGaA v. Integra Lifesciences I, Ltd.
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-1237; 545 U.S. 193
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-13 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether uses of patented inventions in preclinical research, including experiments whose results are not ultimately submitted to the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), fall within 35 U.S.C. §271(e)(1).
* **Question Granted:** Whether uses of patented inventions in preclinical research, including experiments whose results are not ultimately submitted to the FDA, fall within 35 U.S.C. §271(e)(1).
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Federal Circuit affirmed rejection of Merck's regulatory-use defense under a restrictive reading of §271(e)(1), while requiring further proceedings on damages. A jury had found infringement and rejected the defense.
* **Relief Requested:** Merck seeks vacatur of the safe-harbor ruling and review of the evidence under the correct statutory standard. Integra seeks preservation of infringement liability for research it characterizes as drug discovery rather than information development for FDA review.
* **Material Record:** Integra's patents concern RGD peptides. Merck sponsored research by Scripps investigators into peptide candidates affecting angiogenesis, including work relevant to cancer treatment. The disputed experiments concerned potential biological effects, mechanisms, efficacy and safety before human trials. Some candidates or experiments did not become part of an eventual FDA submission. The legal question does not establish that every challenged experiment had a reasonable regulatory relationship; the project and the particular use must be examined on the trial record.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The grant concerns the statutory defense, not a general judicial research exception. The Court must respect the trial evidence and the posture of Merck's challenge to the verdict. Whether patents covering research tools receive identical treatment is not established merely because patented compounds were used experimentally here.
* **Relevant Dates:** Congress enacted §271(e)(1) in 1984. The Federal Circuit decided the relevant appeal in 2003. The claim concerns the law governing the historical research, not later regulatory or patent amendments.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material beyond the authorities addressed below.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Patent protection and exemptions are statutory. Courts must give effect both to the protection of patented inventions and to the enacted exemption without converting a policy preference for research into an unbounded defense.

##### Issue 1: Reasonable relation to the development and submission of regulatory information

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling decision decides the disputed preclinical reach of §271(e)(1). Eli Lilly & Co. v. Medtronic remains relevant for the statute's coverage of inventions used in the federal drug-regulatory process; it does not itself decide whether each Merck experiment qualifies.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Existing law provides no exemption for all scientific research. Statutory use must be linked to the regulatory information-development function Congress described, assessed through the circumstances of the use rather than hindsight alone.
* **Other Governing Law:** 35 U.S.C. §271(a) defines infringement and §271(e)(1) excludes specified uses reasonably related to development and submission of information under federal drug regulation. The Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FDCA) and FDA investigational-new-drug and new-drug-application requirements include preclinical information relevant to safety and efficacy.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The statute covers uses reasonably related to developing and submitting regulatory information. Whether it includes unsuccessful candidates or results never submitted, and where basic research falls, remain the questions presented.
* **Open Point:** How to distinguish protected preclinical development from basic research lacking a sufficiently identified regulatory objective, and whether unsuccessful candidates or unsubmitted results are categorically excluded.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Reasonable relation to the development and submission of regulatory information

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The Federal Circuit treated the exemption as closely tied to information ultimately relevant to the regulatory submission process and regarded the disputed work as insufficiently connected to that process. Its construction substantially limited early research coverage.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** Merck argues that identifying and testing a viable candidate requires preclinical work and that a reasonable relationship cannot depend on whether the research ultimately succeeds or its results are submitted.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** Integra argues that an expansive exemption would permit commercial drug discovery to consume patent rights merely because the research might eventually yield a regulated product. It seeks a concrete limit separating basic discovery from regulatory development.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** A speculative possibility that any experiment could eventually assist drug approval is insufficient. Conversely, a researcher cannot know in advance which reasonable experiments will succeed or which results FDA will require. No oral-argument concession is assumed.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** The United States supports an exemption for preclinical experiments reasonably connected to development of information of the kind relevant to FDA approval, including unsuccessful research. That approach requires record-specific application rather than automatic immunity for all Merck-funded activity.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** The grant concerns the statutory defense, not a general judicial research exception. The Court must respect the trial evidence and the posture of Merck's challenge to the verdict. Whether patents covering research tools receive identical treatment is not established merely because patented compounds were used experimentally here.

##### Issue 1: Reasonable relation to the development and submission of regulatory information

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Vacate and remand under a reasonable regulatory relationship standard; affirm if the restrictive construction is correct or the trial record independently compels rejection under the proper standard; or direct judgment only if the preserved evidentiary posture permits it. A legal correction does not itself determine damages or every act of infringement.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The holding can cover preclinical safety, efficacy and mechanism work on candidate drugs without deciding all research tools, unrestricted basic science or later commercial sales.

* **Record, Source, Posture, and Legal Uncertainty:** The verdict cannot be replaced by a blanket judicial finding that all work was protected. The reviewing court must identify the challenged uses, the governing instruction, preserved evidentiary objections and what the record permits under the corrected construction.

## 82. Wilkinson v. Austin

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Wilkinson v. Austin
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-495; 545 U.S. 209
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-13 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Ohio's procedures for assigning prisoners to the Ohio State Penitentiary provide the process constitutionally required for the liberty at stake.
* **Question Granted:** Whether Ohio's procedures for assigning prisoners to the Ohio State Penitentiary provide the process constitutionally required for the liberty at stake.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit sustained the finding of a liberty interest and the district court's procedural additions to Ohio's New Policy, while setting aside substantive restrictions on Ohio's placement criteria.
* **Relief Requested:** Ohio seeks removal of the federally prescribed procedural additions and approval of its New Policy. The prisoner class seeks retention of protections against erroneous placement and retention at the supermax facility.
* **Material Record:** OSP imposes extreme isolation, rare visits through glass and severe restrictions on contact and activity. Placement is indefinite and disqualifies otherwise eligible prisoners from parole consideration. Ohio's New Policy identifies substantive grounds for review, including organized-crime involvement and prison-gang leadership; it does not authorize unexplained placement of any prisoner. The earlier system produced erroneous assignments. The New Policy provides factual notice, a rebuttal hearing, reasons through three review levels, objections before final review, a thirty-day review and annual reconsideration. A negative recommendation terminates the placement process. Witness calling is restricted.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This §1983 class action seeks equitable relief from a placement policy. The district court's proved deficiencies under the earlier system cannot be ignored, but they do not establish that every additional judicial procedure is required under the replacement policy. The grant does not authorize new substantive rules governing who may be housed at OSP.
* **Relevant Dates:** OSP opened in 1998. Ohio promulgated its New Policy before the district-court trial. The operative policy and the injunction must be evaluated as of the merits event, without assuming later implementation facts.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material beyond the authorities addressed below.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Due Process Clause first requires an identified liberty interest and then process suited to the deprivation. Mathews v. Eldridge weighs the affected interest, risk of error and value of additional safeguards, and governmental burdens. Prison security affects the process owed but is not a categorical exemption from it.

##### Issue 1: The protected interest in avoiding OSP placement

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Sandin v. Conner asks whether substantive state law meaningfully restricts authority to impose a significant deprivation. It rejects mechanical reliance on mandatory words alone, but does not adopt the historical atypical-and-significant-hardship formula as the exclusive test. Direct constitutional liberty and effects on sentence duration remain distinct possible grounds.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing rule preserves meaningful substantive constraints plus a significant restraint as a sufficient state-created-liberty basis. Procedure promised by a State is not, by itself, the substantive entitlement.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment and Ohio's substantive placement criteria are the relevant sources. Section 1983 supplies the vehicle; the Prison Litigation Reform Act governs the scope of prospective relief.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The entering rule requires examining what Ohio's substantive criteria actually constrain and the deprivation they govern. It does not authorize importing the historical Sandin test simply because the reported lower court used it.
* **Open Point:** Whether the identified criteria and exceptional consequences establish a protected interest under the governing rule, with any missing construction of the substantive limits resolved explicitly.

##### Issue 2: Process and the scope of the federal injunction

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Mathews v. Eldridge and the prison-process principles preserved in Wolff v. McDonnell and Hewitt v. Helms require meaningful notice and an opportunity to address the basis for deprivation, with safeguards fitted to security and the decision. Lewis v. Casey requires relief matched to a proved common institutional wrong and a genuine opportunity for the State to design an effective cure.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The interest, error risk, practical value of added safeguards and governmental burdens must be evaluated concretely. Court access or procedural adequacy is judged by effective operation, not formal paperwork alone. Prospective prison relief must satisfy the governing statutory need and scope requirements.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. §1983 and 18 U.S.C. §3626 govern equitable relief. The Due Process Clause does not itself authorize rewriting the State's substantive placement criteria through a procedural injunction.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Meaningful notice, rebuttal and review are required. Whether witness calling, prescribed evidentiary detail and each additional judicial measure are necessary depends on the actual replacement policy and proved risk.
* **Open Point:** Whether the New Policy adequately protects against error and what remains necessary to cure the earlier policy's continuing effects.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The protected interest in avoiding OSP placement

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The lower courts found a protected liberty interest using the historical characterization of OSP's exceptional conditions. Their conclusion must be tested against the governing substantive-constraint rule rather than copied with its historical rationale.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** Ohio disputes the need for the additional federal procedures and emphasizes discretion to classify dangerous prisoners. The severity of the actual placement remains material even if the State contests the legal source of the interest.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** The prisoners rely on the specified placement predicates, indefinite isolation and lost parole eligibility to establish an interest the State cannot remove through standardless classification.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** Not every transfer, adverse classification or mandatory procedural provision creates liberty. The actual substantive constraints and consequences of OSP placement must do the work.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** No distinct material proposal is verified in the sources identified here.

##### Issue 2: Process and the scope of the federal injunction

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The district court required additional procedures despite the New Policy. The Sixth Circuit sustained those procedural changes while rejecting substantive limitations as beyond the proper remedy.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** Ohio relies on multiple reviews, a terminating negative recommendation, reasons and recurrent review, and argues that additional witness and disclosure requirements impose unnecessary security risks.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** The class argues that vague allegations and limited access to the reasons for retention prevent effective rebuttal, and that the history of erroneous placements requires enforceable protections beyond a policy on paper.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** Security can justify protecting an informant or restricting testimony, but cannot justify notice so empty that the prisoner cannot contest the stated basis. The injunction must distinguish adequate future procedures from unresolved old placements.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** No distinct material proposal is verified in the sources identified here.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** This §1983 class action seeks equitable relief from a placement policy. The district court's proved deficiencies under the earlier system cannot be ignored, but they do not establish that every additional judicial procedure is required under the replacement policy. The grant does not authorize new substantive rules governing who may be housed at OSP.

##### Issue 1: The protected interest in avoiding OSP placement

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Find an interest from Ohio's substantive restrictions and significant deprivation and proceed to process; remand if the operative criteria require an unresolved legal or factual construction; or reject the interest only after addressing those constraints and any independent liberty source. Hardship alone does not satisfy the substantive-constraint inquiry.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A holding may apply Sandin to a constrained, indefinite supermax assignment without constitutionalizing every prison classification.

##### Issue 2: Process and the scope of the federal injunction

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Uphold the New Policy and vacate unnecessary procedural additions, with limited proceedings on any continuing unreviewed placements; sustain particular additions if their necessity is supported; or remand for findings on actual inadequacy. No path authorizes an unsupported new substantive classification code.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A functional process rule can approve this multi-level system while preserving challenges to sham notice, meaningless periodic review or demonstrated implementation failures.

* **Record, Source, Posture, and Legal Uncertainty:** The exact injunction must be checked against the operative New Policy and any unresolved placements made under the old system. Neither universal compliance nor the necessity of every additional measure may be presumed.

## 83. Miller-El v. Dretke

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Miller-El v. Dretke
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-9659; 545 U.S. 231
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-13 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the state court's rejection of Miller-El's purposeful racial-discrimination claim can stand under the governing federal habeas standards on the full jury-selection record.
* **Question Granted:** Whether the state court's rejection of Miller-El's purposeful racial-discrimination claim can stand under the governing federal habeas standards on the full jury-selection record.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** After the earlier certificate-of-appealability proceedings, the Fifth Circuit reached the merits and affirmed denial of habeas relief. The state court had accepted the prosecutors' explanations and found no purposeful discrimination.
* **Relief Requested:** Miller-El seeks reversal and a conditional writ requiring a trial before a lawfully selected jury. Texas seeks affirmance under the Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996 (AEDPA)'s restrictions on disturbing state findings.
* **Material Record:** Dallas prosecutors struck ten of eleven qualified Black prospective jurors. The evidence includes comparisons between struck Black jurors and accepted non-Black jurors expressing similar views, especially Billy Jean Fields and Joe Warren; different questioning about capital punishment; use of jury shuffles; and evidence of the office's historical exclusion practices. ## 85. Golden v. Golden

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Golden v. Golden
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-2184 below; 382 F.3d 348 (3d Cir. 2004)
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-13 (scheduled merits date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the probate exception to federal diversity jurisdiction extends to claims concerning an inter vivos trust that functions as a will substitute? Whether that exception bars fraud, undue-influence, forgery, or fiduciary-duty claims because adjudication would conflict with a probate court’s determinations, even when relief is sought against individuals?
* **Question Granted:** Whether the probate exception to federal diversity jurisdiction extends to claims concerning an inter vivos trust that functions as a will substitute? Whether that exception bars fraud, undue-influence, forgery, or fiduciary-duty claims because adjudication would conflict with a probate court’s determinations, even when relief is sought against individuals?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The district court dismissed the action under the probate exception. The Third Circuit affirmed in part and reversed in part, distinguishing claims that would affect probate determinations from personal tort claims it permitted to proceed.
* **Relief Requested:** The disappointed beneficiaries seek federal adjudication of claims against the alleged wrongdoers. The defendants seek dismissal of claims that would invalidate the testamentary distribution or interfere with estate administration; the amount in controversy and the effect of the probate decree also bear on jurisdiction.
* **Material Record:** Robert Golden acts under Leah Golden's power of attorney; Donald Earwood represents Helen Earwood's estate. A purported June 2000 addendum reduced the first two shares to $5,000 and $10,000 and left the remainder to David. The complaint alleges undue influence, fraud, forgery, fiduciary misconduct, slander and interference with expected inheritance. King died July 26, 2000; her will was probated in September. Apply the legal-certainty amount test to each plaintiff's claims and legally available punitive damages without improper aggregation. Personal damages do not automatically constitute an appeal from probate.
* **Relevant Dates:** The instruments were executed in 1999 and 2000; probate began in September 2000; the federal suit was filed March 28, 2001. The Third Circuit decided the appeal September 3, 2004, making the scheduled June 13, 2005 placement chronologically eligible.
