## 85. Golden v. Golden

**Disposition:** All eight Associates would affirm the sufficient diversity-amount ruling and the restoration of the independently cognizable personal claims, vacate the broader probate-exception dismissals, and remand for claim-and-relief-specific adjudication under the rule below. The same court may reject a legally unsupported tort or apply established preclusion after jurisdiction is correctly addressed; those are distinct tasks.

**Shared operative rule:** The probate exception prevents a federal court from probating or annulling a will, administering an estate, or taking control of property already under a state probate court's custody. It does not exclude an otherwise proper diversity claim seeking personal liability for wrongdoing merely because a trust functions as a will substitute, the controversy concerns an inheritance, or the adjudication could reach facts inconsistent with a probate determination. A personal-damages label is not controlling if the relief actually requires the prohibited probate, administrative or property-control act. A trust's label or practical testamentary function alone decides neither direction.

**Application to the actual claims:** The Third Circuit's inference that probate of the pour-over will necessarily accepted the trust and addendum cannot, by itself, turn every fraud, forgery or undue-influence damages claim into probate. The personal slander and interference-with-inheritance claims remain available for ordinary adjudication. The formerly dismissed claims require examination of their actual requested relief: a personal judgment can proceed within diversity jurisdiction, whereas an order vacating probate, allocating the corpus or administering the executor's accounting remains outside this forum's authority. The fiduciary-duty count particularly requires this distinction rather than a categorical executor label. State law may limit whether a freestanding tort exists; a State's internal assignment of cases to its Orphans' Court does not itself contract §1332.

**Amount, capacities and state judgments:** Leah Golden's claim uses her citizenship despite an attorney-in-fact; Helen Earwood's estate representative takes the decedent's citizenship under §1332(c)(2). The supplied New York and Georgia interests are diverse from the Pennsylvania defendants. Apply St. Paul Mercury's legal-certainty rule to each plaintiff, counting legally available punitive damages on bona fide claims and permitting aggregation of that plaintiff's claims against the proper opposing party. The pleaded intentional or reckless misconduct and Pennsylvania's available punitive remedies support the amount; the $188,946 tax-return valuation alone does not make each plaintiff's recovery legally certain to fall below the threshold. Section 1738 requires the actual rendering State's preclusive effect for an actual adjudication. Under current Exxon Mobil, an independent injury from alleged private misconduct does not become lower-federal appellate review merely because it conflicts with a state court's conclusion; a claim actually seeking rejection of judgment-caused injury retains the Rooker–Feldman bar's own requirements.

**Enhanced competing-path test:** Markham v. Allen, 326 U.S. 490, permits adjudication of personal rights while withholding probate and control of the state-court estate. Waterman v. Canal-Louisiana Bank & Trust Co., 215 U.S. 33, and Princess Lida v. Thompson, 305 U.S. 456, support distinguishing personal relief from administration and possession of a res. Ankenbrandt, June 15, 1992, preserves a bounded statutory decree exception without a general family-dispute exclusion; Quackenbush and current Exxon Mobil require actual predicates for abstention or appellate exclusion. Defendants' strongest path treats will-substitute trust litigation and the executor count as attempts to redirect the distribution under another label. That concern controls any actually administrative relief but cannot make mere factual inconsistency jurisdictional. Claimants' strongest broad path treats all damages requests or all inter vivos trusts as exempt. That fails where the requested judgment actually commands probate or corpus administration. The functional remedy rule answers both; actual state preclusion and tort sufficiency remain ordinary remand work.
