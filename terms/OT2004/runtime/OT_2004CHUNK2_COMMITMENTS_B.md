## 17. United States v. Booker and United States v. Fanfan

### Components and controlling baseline

**Constitutional component:** Angle already holds that a fact increasing either binding Guidelines boundary receives the applicable element protections despite a larger statutory maximum. Harris applies the floor rule; Blakely applies the binding-ceiling rule. Gatewood independently precludes a categorical recidivism exemption. Constitutional resolution cannot call the Federal Guidelines question undecided or distinguish Angle merely because the Commission promulgated the binding range. A valid admission must be an element admission, a lawful jury waiver retains proof beyond reasonable doubt, and federal charging requirements remain independent.

**Remedial component:** Whether to remove the Guidelines' binding force remains open. The enacted §3553(b)(1) command, its actual departure conditions and the §3742 review/remand provisions remain operative unless this decision supplies an express severability holding. The constitutional rule alone does not make the Guidelines advisory. Congress's general §3553(a) considerations do not themselves displace the mandatory command. No judicially invented enhancement trial follows from remand.

**Record consequences:** Booker's 360-month term depends on judicial quantity and obstruction findings that raised the 210–262-month verdict-supported range to 360 months–life. Fanfan's 78-month sentence withheld, rather than imposed, the additional findings that would yield 188–235 months. Their records cannot be assigned an identical constitutional error. The packet does not establish any different input error, waiver, harmlessness or inevitable replacement sentence.

### Alternative-path test

1. **Preserve binding Guidelines and enforce the lawful range.** The violation lies in using an unestablished aggravating fact to authorize more punishment, not in every application of §3553(b)(1). Mandatory ranges remain workable for verdict-established facts, valid admissions and lawfully adjudicated elements. Alaska Airlines requires examination of the remainder and the enacted arrangement, while Angle, Harris, Gatewood and Blakely supply charge-bound remedies that preserve lawful sentencing. **Strongest objection:** Congress designed a real-conduct system administered by judges; charging and jury proof may distort its upward/downward balance, create difficult evidentiary problems and transfer power to charging decisions. **Conclusion:** Those are serious reasons for legislative redesign and for the alternative construction, but they do not prove that the Court must remove the mandatory command in cases that can receive lawful sentences. Retaining lawful applications requires no automatic new trial and no prediction that every enhancement can still be imposed. A constitutional limit's effect on an enhancement does not itself authorize increased discretionary punishment. This path rejects systemwide excision on these records.

2. **Remove binding force and inconsistent mandatory review provisions, retaining guided discretion.** The competing severability position would remove §3553(b)(1)'s compulsory operation and the associated §3742(e) structure to the extent inseparable from mandatory ranges, while retaining §3553(a), the Guidelines as required considerations, reason-giving and review of the resulting lawful sentence. The rationale is preservation of Congress's real-offense proportionality and judicial sentencing architecture within constitutional limits. **Strongest objection:** Congress expressly selected binding ranges to replace the disparities of discretion, and the proposed deletion changes lawful sentences, including Fanfan's, although he suffered no enhancement violation. **Conclusion:** This is a plausible remedial judgment only if Congress's chosen manner of individualized sentencing is found more fundamental than mandatory force; it is not an automatic constitutional consequence. The dissenting conditional remedy below accepts that premise; the preservation supporters reject it. No mechanical preference for deleting fewer words settles the question.

3. **Preserve ordinary judicial enhancements by reconsidering Angle.** This is available only as an express request to overrule or limit the governing constitutional line. It cannot be presented as reconciliation with Angle's unchanged holding. The three constitutional dissenters preserve this preference; the other five reject it. Mistretta approves the Commission's institutional arrangement, while Witte and Watts permit judicial assessment within otherwise authorized punishment; none supplies a silent exception to Angle.

## 18. Jama v. Immigration and Customs Enforcement

### Alternative-path and scope conclusions

**Textually separate conditions:** Section 1231(b)(2)(E)(iv) identifies the country of birth without an advance-acceptance condition. The last fallback, (vii), expressly requires an accepting government after removal to each earlier connected country is impracticable, inadvisable or impossible. Reading that condition as clause-bound respects Congress's different wording in (C), (D) and (E). **Strongest counterargument:** The statutory sequence and international receiving-sovereign premise make acceptance a minimum condition of actual removal, so moving from nationality to birthplace should not circumvent the earlier safeguards. **Answer supporting affirmance:** Different stages have different predicates; the (C) authority to disregard a designation is permissive, and (D)'s conditions need not reappear in the textually different alternatives. Physical feasibility and independently available protection remain separate safeguards. No international rule identified here unambiguously overrides the enacted choice.

**Acceptance as a structural minimum:** Read the country-selection sequence as identifying destinations within an actual reception process, with the final accepting-country language expressing that premise rather than creating its first appearance. The absence of a functioning authority capable of accepting Jama therefore bars the selected removal. **Strongest counterargument:** This carries a condition backward that Congress placed expressly only in specified provisions and makes (iv)'s omission ineffective. **Answer supporting reversal:** A statutory country is a destination capable of receiving the person in the contemplated intergovernmental process, not merely a coordinate at which the Executive can deposit him; a clause-by-clause reading must preserve that enacted sequence as a coherent whole. This construction imposes no general judicial control of foreign affairs and leaves other lawful destinations available.

Barnhart v. Thomas, Souter's unanimous 2003 opinion, supplies a useful but nonconclusive analogy: a later qualification need not govern an earlier step, and the Court must consider the whole enacted design. Its actual delegated-choice holding is not a rule that the last-antecedent inference always wins. United States National Bank of Oregon, Souter's opinion joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, requires the enacted whole to control over isolated punctuation without authorizing policy revision. Zadvydas supplies no acceptance holding; St. Cyr supplies legal review without substantive entitlement.

## 19. Clark v. Martinez, with Benitez v. Rozos

### Separate components and alternative-path conclusions

**Threshold:** All eight Associates regard the contested custody authority and resulting conditional restraint as live. Benitez's temporary parole does not conclusively extinguish the authority asserted against him. Neither man's release constitutes admission. This conclusion does not presume that every case remains live after unconditional cessation; it follows this record's retained authority.

**Uniform statutory construction:** Section 1231(a)(6) uses the same operative detention authorization for its listed categories. Zadvydas has given that authorization a removal-related duration; extending that statutory construction to the reserved inadmissible class does not decide equal constitutional rights across all entry statuses. **Strongest counterargument:** Zadvydas expressly reserved the class and relied on concerns about previously admitted persons; Mezei and entry control permit a broader custody arrangement here. **Conclusion supporting extension:** A reservation means the issue remains open, not that the shared words already authorize permanent preventive detention for the reserved class. The statutory rule can govern all categories even where the constitutional pressure prompting its first adoption differs. Separate statutes remain available on their actual predicates.

**Category-specific authority:** Treat Zadvydas's admitted-person construction as confined to its expressly reserved constitutional setting, leaving broader entry-related detention and parole for inadmissible persons. **Strongest counterargument:** That gives a single detention phrase different durations based on categories Congress placed together. **Conclusion supporting rejection of extension:** The two dissenters regard initial exclusion and the admitted person's liberty as materially different legal settings and would preserve the statutory permission in the reserved class rather than extend a disputed implied limit. Kennedy retains his independent protection against arbitrary custody; Thomas does not adopt that framework. Neither dissent establishes actual compliance with every procedure or orders physical rearrest.

**Operative rule supported by the six:** After the presumptively reasonable six months, counted within the removal process, a detainee's good reason to believe there is no significant likelihood of removal within the reasonably foreseeable future requires sufficient governmental rebuttal. The horizon shortens as custody lengthens; the clock alone does not mandate release. On these accepted records the necessary removal prospect is absent and no independent custody authority is established. Section 1231(a)(6) therefore cannot support detention or its resumption. Martinez's release protection stands; Benitez must receive enforceable protection under the same rule notwithstanding his temporary parole. Supervision preserves the removal orders and immigration status.

**Exceptions in their own terms:** Section 1231(a)(1)(C) permits extension where the person fails or refuses timely good-faith application for necessary documents or conspires or acts to prevent removal; Cuba's refusal alone proves neither. Distinct criminal custody, lawful civil commitment or §1226a detention requires its own predicates and procedure. No special-security certification or factual predicate is established here. Paragraph (3) supervision retains periodic identification, necessary government-funded medical or psychiatric examination, sworn information and reasonable written conduct restrictions. A supervision violation or change in removal prospects must support actual renewed statutory authority and lawful procedure; a regulation cannot create perpetual custody. Release is neither admission nor immunity from later lawful removal.

## 20. Nordyke v. King

### Separate components and alternative-path conclusions

**Second Amendment:** Love's personal-holder, incorporation and necessary-acquisition holdings defeat the categorical no-right/no-jurisdiction premise. Wright requires actual coverage and burden analysis; Emerson confirms that justified regulation and personal protection coexist. The claimants still need actual personal injury, protected conduct and each prerequisite for preliminary relief. An ordinary market opportunity on public land is not automatically equivalent to the defensive home core.

**First Amendment:** Hurley protects an actual communicative enterprise without making every good or transaction speech. Texas v. Johnson and Spence distinguish sufficiently expressive conduct from ordinary possession. O'Brien addresses a genuine incidental conduct burden; City of Erie forbids using that label where justification and operation principally target communicative activity. The ordinance's facial phrasing alone cannot answer the latter inquiry. On this developed facial attack, ordinary possession and commercial transfer do not become speech merely because the show also includes advocacy or some message-bearing weapons. The record does not establish substantial unconstitutional expressive reach or a possession rule that principally operates as expression suppression. This permits rejection of the facial attack while preserving an actual supported display claim.

**Path one—mixed disposition and bounded remand:** Affirm denial of preliminary relief on the presented facial speech theories; vacate insofar as denial rests on the erroneous Second Amendment premise; return for actual personal injury, coverage, burden, justification and the unaddressed equitable requirements. **Strongest objection:** Plaintiffs bear the preliminary-relief burden, and missing proof of broader acquisition burdens might independently support denial. **Answer:** The lower court excluded the arms claim at the rights threshold and did not decide the necessary factual/equitable questions; an appellate court cannot substitute an unmade merits-and-balance determination. Correcting that error does not grant an injunction.

**Path two—immediate broad preliminary relief:** Treat practical destruction of the traditional gun-show venue and expressive uses as sufficient for an injunction. **Strongest objection:** Necessary acquisition is protected, but access to every seller or venue is not guaranteed; the record lacks the findings necessary to classify the actual restriction and justify relief of that breadth. **Conclusion:** No Associate supports that broad remedy on the supplied record. A narrower successful application remains possible after its actual premises are established, without presuming later accommodations or a protected-display exemption.

**Path three—affirm everything as public-property management:** The county's safety and proprietary interests are relevant. **Strongest objection:** Public ownership does not eliminate an incorporated right, and Love/Wright require an actual application. **Conclusion:** No Associate adopts ownership or the absence of militia enrollment as a sufficient ground. An independent developed merits/equity basis could support denial, but the supplied lower decision did not decide it.
