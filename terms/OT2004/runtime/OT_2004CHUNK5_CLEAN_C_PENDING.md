## City of Sherrill — supported paths

**Provisional disposition:** Each Associate supports affirming the reviewed Sherrill tax-protection judgments at their bounded scope. This follows independent evaluation of the real City paths below, not an assumption that equity is unavailable or that prior law already decided this dispute.

**Federal reservation status.** The City's extension of Venetie's federal set-aside/superintendence inquiry from section 1151(b) to all Indian-country categories is a serious legal proposal, but the unanimous entering holding preserves reservation and allotment categories. Even a functional federal-relationship inquiry does not favor the City here: Canandaigua Article 2 recognizes the particular retained reservation and protects use and enjoyment, Article 4 confirms that arrangement, and Articles 6–7 supply federal supervision and protection. State origin in 1788 therefore does not establish absence of a later federal relationship. Section 1151(a) cannot be reduced to the continuing status of each fee patent. Recognition alone would not suffice; the particular protected land relationship does.

**Treaty alteration.** Hagen and Yankton require clear federal intent from the whole operative instrument and context, without mandatory cession words or a fixed purchase price. The City's removal-policy, western-home and compensation argument must be read against Buffalo Creek's actual allocation. Article 1 cedes the specified Wisconsin interests, with its own geographic exception; Article 2 provides western land in consideration of that cession. Article 3 forfeits the new western interest if the required acceptance/removal does not occur within its period, as extended by the President; it does not forfeit the New York reservation. Article 13 makes Oneida removal dependent on satisfactory arrangements with New York for purchase of the Oneida lands. The Seneca and Tuscarora provisions and attached conveyances expressly complete different transactions. Their terms cannot be assigned to the Oneidas. The instrument establishes neither satisfaction of Article 13's condition for these parcels nor a completed federal Oneida cession. This is a whole-instrument conclusion, not a magic-words demand. Competing characterizations of later compensation in the briefs do not supply a new adjudicated New York surrender.

**Recognition and succession.** Current recognition and succession to the treaty party are supplied, while the City's historical-lapse argument rests on contested accounts. Movement of some members, local government and population change do not themselves establish federal termination of the Nation or retained parcel protection. The court below rejected the discovery showing. These commitments do not substitute an unprovided status document or find every historical assertion false; they conclude that the asserted lapse supplies no legally established extinction ground on this record.

**Tax source.** Yakima and Cass County require the identified federal disposal authority and parcel chain, preserving independent protections. Their holdings refuse to make alienability or governmental impact alone sufficient. The present parcels lack the completed federal disposal decisive there. Unauthorized state-era transfers do not acquire that statutory consequence through time or ordinary repurchase. The relevant combination is current Nation ownership of these retained reservation parcels and no established intervening federal removal of their protection, not tribal purchase anywhere.

Section 233's civil-jurisdiction grant supplies no tax authorization; its tax-preservation clause is not itself a universal exemption. Its tribal-member use/possession exception qualifies execution, not taxation or alienation. Former section 465 supplies discretionary trust acquisition and the attached exemption, without making it the exclusive source of existing reservation protection. Its Navajo funding limitation concerns the acquisition appropriation, not an exception to Oneida tax protection.

**Independent City equity path.** As an analytical alternative under Engine section 14—not an assertion that every formulation was actually filed here—a Justice could invoke federal equitable repose and territorial acquiescence to prevent abrupt judicial restoration of dormant sovereignty after generations of contrary governance. Stevens's Oneida II dissent develops the analogous argument: extraordinary delay, innocent reliance, changes in land character and destabilization can limit ancient common-law claims. Its Felix and Wetzel examples concern disruptive recovery of developed property. The Oneida II majority expressly reserves further remedial equity, although its Ewert discussion warns against validating restricted transfers through laches. The City actually identified equitable defenses below and invokes governmental and fiscal reliance. Their absence or waiver is not assumed.

The strongest response, adopted here, concerns the actual right and remedy. The Nation challenges new assessments on land it now owns by voluntary purchase. It does not seek cancellation of current possessors' titles, ancient rent from innocent owners or judicial restoration of a regional government. Defeating all present protection would require a new substantive limit on federal tax immunity or complete remedial foreclosure, rather than ordinary tailoring. Yakima refuses a governmental-impact substitute for tax authority; Cass confines nonrevival to a completed congressional disposal. They do not forbid every equitable limit, but make the City's proposed tax consequence require a further justification absent here. Coeur d'Alene allows prospective enforcement despite sovereign interests while reserving lawful remedial bounds. Even its dissent rejects general interest balancing and requires a comprehensive functional transfer of ownership and regulation. A tax-only decree on already purchased parcels is materially different.

The lower court found imminent financial collapse exaggerated, noted five years of surpluses and identified no service failure caused by nonpayment. These are preliminary-injury findings, not a conclusive prediction of all future fiscal effects. They nevertheless prevent treating governmental collapse as an established equitable predicate. Lost expected revenue and hypothetical fragmentation do not justify complete denial of prospective protection against an unauthorized tax on this record. Particular service agreements and payments are not assumed. A concrete implementation problem might affect relief, but no identified defect warrants an unguided remand to seek prejudice or a disposal statute.

## Jackson — supported paths

**Provisional disposition:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reverse the categorical dismissal and remand for ordinary proceedings on the recipient's alleged retaliation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas affirm the appealed statutory dismissal. Jackson's personal coaching/pay injury is distinct from the girls' injury under every position.

**Reversal path.** Section 1681 protects any person against discrimination on the basis of sex in covered education. Deliberately punishing a person because he complains of sex discrimination is unequal treatment causally linked to the sex-equality demand. The reporter's own sex need not be the trait used by the recipient. Sullivan supplies a pre-enactment analogy protecting an advocate personally injured for asserting another's equality; Cannon supplies the established action and Franklin compensation once intentional statutory discrimination is proved. Title VII's detailed separate enumeration does not control Title IX's broader formulation. The longstanding regulation confirms construction, without independently creating the action or automatically converting all its terms into statutory damages duties.

The strongest objection is that the immediate reason is complaining, not sex, so the proposal adds a distinct retaliation prohibition. The reversal supporters answer that the complaint's subject and the sanction's purpose provide the nexus: punishing efforts to secure equal educational treatment is a means of maintaining sex-based inequality. This is not every workplace grievance. The construction suffices for this pleading; it does not establish a comprehensive mistaken-complaint standard or a new causal quantum. The United States' proposed subject-neutral all-complainers exclusion is advocacy, not an automatically adopted defense.

**Affirmance path.** Retaliation can be intentional and objectionable without being discrimination because of sex. A broad protected-person phrase does not eliminate that causal limit; an express retaliation provision elsewhere demonstrates the distinct concept. Sullivan construed property-right protection in section 1982 and need not determine Title IX. Cannon establishes the action for the enacted right; Franklin supplies remedies after a statutory violation, not a new right against every retaliatory act. Pennhurst reinforces the objection to an inadequately specified funding duty. The strongest response is Title IX's lack of own-sex wording, Sullivan's background and retaliation's capacity to defeat enforcement. The affirmance supporters find those insufficient to substitute a protected-activity condition for the statutory causal one.

**Current-law corrections.** Powell/Sandoval/South Camden establish the actual bounded Title VI prospective regulatory action. Their historical categorical no-action counterpart is not law here. Neither their scope nor a regulation alone creates Title IX damages. Gebser/Davis concern distinct recipient-attribution routes; their third-party requirements do not automatically govern the recipient's own retaliatory decision. Alleging Board action does not prove attribution. Franklin rejects a categorical compensation/Spending Clause bar after intentional statutory discrimination is established, without predeciding retaliation's statutory status.

### Jackson remand and limits

The reversal supporters reject both categorical grounds: retaliation of the pleaded kind falls within section 1681, and the injured reporter need not be the original victim. Franklin permits appropriate compensation for proved intentional discrimination, including causal pay loss; ordinary employment relief requires its own legal and equitable basis. Coverage, legally attributable Board conduct, the complaint's sex-discrimination subject, retaliation, supported defenses and causal loss remain to be established. The athletics discrimination and the Board's liability remain allegations.

The complaint does not require a completed adjudication of the girls' underlying claim before surviving this categorical dismissal. That pleading conclusion does not decide every mistaken or bad-faith complaint, a new causal standard or a burden-shifting system. The regulation's distinct interference, testimony, assistance and participation language is not silently adopted as statutory damages law. Remand permits ordinary proceedings, not a compulsory trial, immediate reinstatement or a fixed award. Affirmance supporters would conclude only the appealed statutory retaliation theory without disposing of independent claims outside review.
