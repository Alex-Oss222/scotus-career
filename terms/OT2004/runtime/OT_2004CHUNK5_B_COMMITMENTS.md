## 41. Muehler v. Mena

### Components and shared premises

- **D — detention and initial control.** Summers authorizes detention of occupants during execution of this proper weapons warrant. Initial cuffs were reasonable given the anticipated armed gang risk and need to secure several occupants. Mere non-suspect status does not eliminate execution-related detention authority.
- **F — continuing search-period force.** Graham requires objective assessment of the actual continued cuffs. Holland rejects perpetual justification from an initial need, but does not demand the least restrictive method. Plaintiff-favorable supported historical facts remain distinct from the legal judgment of reasonableness; a general unreasonable-force verdict is not an express finding on every disputed safety predicate.
- **Q — immigration questions.** Reject a rule requiring separate suspicion solely because an ordinary information request concerns immigration. Questions alone, without additional restraint or compelled intrusion, do not add a Fourth Amendment seizure. Existing detention does not authorize an unrelated extension. Caballes supplies only its bounded rule about the particular contraband examination, not a universal nonprolongation safe harbor. No Fifth Amendment issue or independent purse-search claim is decided.
- **P — detention after the search.** Summers ends when its execution-related justification ends; independent lawful grounds or reasonable completion tasks must be identified. The preserved allegation of post-search custody requires adjudication on the actual record. The Court does not find a particular number of unlawful minutes or presume an immigration-related delay.
- **I — immunity.** Separate any surviving violation from particular notice on February 3, 1998. Current Holland and Brosseau explain the inquiry but cannot themselves create earlier notice. Graham, Summers, Anderson, Baker v. Monroe Township (1995), McDonald v. Haskins (1992), and Franklin v. Foxworth (1994), with factual distinctions, are relevant preconduct sources. Late-1998 Heitschmidt cannot supply February notice. Individual causation remains necessary; rank is insufficient.
- **R — judgment and relief.** Vacate the appellate judgment insofar as it rests on the erroneous categorical premises and remand. Preserve the actual jury findings and independent duration ground for their proper legal effect. The compensatory verdict was not allocated between force and duration; neither automatic restoration nor automatic elimination of all damages follows. Evaluate the actual instructions, verdict, causation, and valid remaining theories before choosing affirmance of an amount, reduction, or a properly limited new trial. Punitive relief retains its separate legal predicates. The defense verdicts on challenged premises searches and other officers' entry/property conduct remain undisturbed.

## 42. City of Rancho Palos Verdes v. Abrams

### Components and shared premises

- **S — asserted right and section 1983 channel.** The enforceable siting obligation is sufficiently particular for the present question; the disposition may assume the individual right without adopting a new general rights formula. Hold that section 332(c)(7)(B)(v)'s express personal remedy, thirty-day filing requirement, and compulsory expedited adjudication displace an additional section 1983 action enforcing these same statutory restrictions. The contrary ordinary limitations period and remedial route would defeat the legislative arrangement. No categorical inference follows merely because a statute provides some express action.
- **C — savings clause and independent claims.** Section 601(c)(1) preserves other law; it does not supply a second, incompatible enforcement route for the TCA-created obligation. Section 1983 continues to govern other rights under its ordinary rules. An independently secured constitutional right is not adjudicated or extinguished here. The separate FCC petition authorized for clause (iv) is not made the exclusive court route for the different written-decision requirement in clause (iii).
- **R — damages, fees, and mandate.** Reverse the additional statutory section 1983 ruling and remand to give effect to that foreclosure. Fees resting only on that section 1983 predicate fail with it. The already obtained direct TCA permit relief is undisturbed. Do not decide all remedies under the direct statutory action, every independent fee basis, or the merits of other siting theories not necessary to this channel question. The absence of an express damages or fee provision reinforces the actual statutory design but is not alone a universal foreclosure rule.

Blessing's two inquiries remain separate. Bruneau did not involve this mandatory express individual claim-processing route and controls against an argument that administrative oversight or an implied action alone defeats section 1983. Sea Clammers and Smith v. Robinson remain available for demonstrated incompatibility. Gonzaga did not obtain a majority for a general new rights/remedy equation. The retained Sandoval/Powell prospective-enforcement rule is not silently displaced.

## 43. Brown v. Payton

### Components and shared premises

- **A — governing review and cutoff.** Garceau makes the post-effective-date merits application subject to AEDPA despite preliminary earlier proceedings. Section 2254(d)(1) asks whether the November 23, 1992 state decision contradicted or objectively unreasonably applied then-established Supreme Court law. The present procedural framework is governed by Williams, Early, Visciotti, and Lockyer as actually decided. Reasonable disagreement is relevant to the objective range, not a judge-count test. Later constitutional holdings cannot manufacture earlier clearly established law.
- **E — constitutional issue.** Lockett, Eddings, Skipper, and Boyde require a lawful opportunity to consider and give effect to relevant mitigation. The question is a reasonable likelihood of impermissible application of the whole charge and trial. Admission alone is not enough; a defendant is not entitled to particular weight or belief. Postcrime conversion and adjustment can bear on character, future conduct, and a life sentence even when they do not diminish offense-time culpability. Their postcrime date does not categorically exclude them.
- **G — statutory gateway applied here.** This is distinct from the best independent answer under E. The material record includes the prosecutor's repeated legal exclusion of postcrime evidence, the defense objection and contrary closing, the judge's generic response, the all-evidence direction and factor (k), and the prosecutor's separate attack on sincerity and alternative balancing. The court's private broad understanding did not itself tell jurors the law. A state merits decision can be within the statutory range despite constitutional doubt, but not merely because it invoked Boyde.
- **H — harmlessness and remedy.** If G is not crossed, deny relief on this claim without deciding harmlessness or giving a general constitutional approval of the prosecutor's statements. If G is crossed and E is found, apply the governing State burden to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that this preserved error did not contribute to the sentence. No historical lower habeas prejudice burden is imported. Review the complete sentencing record and actual alternative arguments. A lawful harmlessness remand is not a finding that documents are missing. Any ultimate conditional writ is sentence-only, permitting a lawful new penalty proceeding within an appropriate period or a nondeath sentence; the conviction is not disturbed on this claim.

Bell's current rule requires identification of what the state court actually did and preserves the separate jury-guidance question; it does not presume that a valid general standard cured its use. Penry II's current effective-mitigation and procedural holdings remain binding at their proper dates, but its 2001 constitutional application is not treated as new 1992 notice. Boyde's 1990 opinion, including the treatment of counsel's arguments and postcrime evidence, is direct eligible authority. The jury never heard the judge's private explanation that the catchall was broad. The prosecution's express legal exclusion is acknowledged by every commitment below.

The strongest objection is that this prosecutor told jurors the evidence was legally unavailable, the very distinction that mattered to Boyde's context. Thomas acknowledges the stronger objection but concludes the entire presentation permitted a reasonable state court to find that jurors understood they could consider all mitigation while assigning it little weight. His alternate path is an objectively unreasonable judicial foreclosure of evidence protected by the law existing at the state decision. He refuses to make the 2001 Penry II application a 1992 holding or to announce that postcrime adjustment has no constitutional relevance. H is not reached. His mandate reverses relief on this claim, not every possible sentencing challenge.

**Ginsburg.** Applies A under Garceau despite her prior disagreement. Finds E and G satisfied and would affirm those conclusions, with H remanded for application of the correct State burden. Her Williams and Penry II joins insist that mitigation be legally usable, while her Early and Visciotti joins accept actual reasonable state determinations. Her Bell jury-guidance join requires a distinct answer to how the sentencing jury could use the charge. These current positions support rigorous application of the 1992 Boyde/Skipper rules, not an expansion of the substantive-law cutoff.

The strongest contrary case is that all participants knew conversion was the defense's case, the evidence was fully admitted, and both sides discussed its weight. She accepts those facts but finds that the specific legal exclusion and lack of authoritative correction gave the ambiguous factor a constitutionally impermissible application. Defense counsel's assurance is not the court's law. Her alternate path would deny relief if jurors received a clear lawful route notwithstanding counsel's misstatement; this charge did not supply a sufficient answer to the express exclusion. She refuses a categorical prohibition on jurors rejecting insincere conversion or assigning it little weight. H must address the difference between assigning little weight and being told the evidence cannot count at all. If the State fails its burden, only the sentence requires a new lawful proceeding or nondeath disposition.

**Breyer.** Applies A under Garceau notwithstanding his earlier dissent. Accepts E and treats the constitutional concern as serious, but on G concludes the state result remained within the objective range and votes to reverse relief on this claim. His Williams opinion on section 2254 distinguishes unreasonable application from independent disagreement. His Early and Visciotti joins apply that distinction to actual trial context. His Penry II and Bell jury-guidance joins show that he requires a workable mitigation route, but do not make every ambiguous catchall equivalent to Penry II's conflicting verdict mechanism.

The strongest objection is the repeated legal exclusion and the court's generic response, especially because the defense's main mitigation was postcrime. Breyer regards a clearer instruction as plainly preferable and does not endorse the prosecutor's legal reading. He nevertheless finds that the all-evidence direction, full defense explanation, and prosecutor's alternative treatment of the conversion as something to weigh permit the state court's conclusion under Boyde. His alternate path crosses G when the instruction or authoritative clarification prevents legal effect despite admitted evidence; merely reciting deference would not save such a result. He refuses an independent holding that no constitutional error occurred unless separately supported, a rule that admission alone suffices, and later-law retrojection. H is not reached because G independently disposes of this claim; other claims remain outside the mandate.

### Commitment tally and limits

All eight apply A and preserve the distinction between E, G, and H. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Breyer reject this claim at G and reverse the resulting habeas relief; their common ground does not require a separate no-error holding. Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg find E and G established, would affirm those conclusions, and require H under the governing State burden before the final sentence remedy. Their position does not direct automatic relief because an underlying document was unavailable, and does not permit the State to retain the sentence merely because aggravation was substantial. No commitment disturbs the conviction on this instructional claim.

## 44. Black Political Task Force v. Galvin

### Components and shared premises

- **L — route, live controversy, and current remedy.** Decide the properly presented section 1253 merits appeal from the final three-judge injunction. Replacement Plan C's enactment did not eliminate review of a continuing federal restraint and its liability predicate. The 2004 candidate-relief issues are separately moot. No ruling on every possible future challenge to Plan C is required.
- **S — statutory coverage.** Governing De Grandy, Valdespino, and Martinez apply section 2 to actual districting-based denial of equal electoral opportunity. Thomas and Scalia maintain their own contrary statutory construction, previously recorded in those cases. Their position is a threshold disagreement, not a finding that the Commonwealth actually afforded equal opportunity under the governing results test.
- **P — proof of causal electoral conditions.** Six Justices accept the supported findings of feasible compact alternative opportunity, Black political cohesion, and white bloc voting sufficient usually to defeat minority-preferred candidates. The first condition is not a mandate to maximize districts. Candidate identity is not voter preference; actual election evidence controls. The lower court could consider exogenous elections after explaining the lack of sufficiently informative endogenous contests and evaluate incumbency as a special circumstance without pretending it makes every victory irrelevant. Its acceptance of the revised alternative plan rested on known data and the absence of unfair surprise, not a new presumption against the State. Neither citizen voting-age estimates nor combined-race figures need be elevated into a universal method because the supported alternative sufficed under the material measures here.
- **T — totality and actual injury.** All six coverage Justices reject numerical-majority-only counting of electoral opportunity, any presumption of liability from P alone, retrogression as an independent section 2 command, and a racial seat entitlement. They divide over whether independent supported packing and unequal-opportunity findings suffice to affirm despite those errors, or the totality balance must return to the trial court. The disputed findings involve six predominantly Black precincts moved from the 11th and 12th into an already heavily Black 6th district, inconsistent with population needs and explained by protection of white incumbents. Race-conscious choices, feasible extra majority districts, or growth in Black population alone do not prove the statutory violation.
- **R — disposition and scope.** The four affirmers preserve the existing section 2 injunction and liability judgment on their independent lawful ground, not a new proportional allocation or replacement-map order. The two reassessment voters vacate the affected statutory liability and dependent remedy and remand for correct totality analysis, preserving supported subsidiary findings and allowing any necessary interim protection on ordinary equitable grounds. Neither result revives the old plan automatically or repeals enacted Plan C. Thomas and Scalia would reverse the section 2 liability, dissolve its dependent federal injunction, and remand for dismissal of that claim. The dismissed constitutional counts are not restored, and no additional damages, deadline, or candidate remedy is invented.

### Individual commitments

**Stevens.** Joins L, governing S, and P; on T votes to affirm liability on the independently supported actual packing and unequal-opportunity findings, rejecting the lower court's numerical and presumptive language. His De Grandy Parts II and III joins require causation beyond possible additional districts, treat proportionality as relevant but not conclusive, and reject cross-area offsets. His Valdespino majority join recognizes proved functional opportunity rather than an inflexible population label. His Martinez joins apply those rules to actual local electoral performance and supported packing evidence. His Laidlaw join supports attention to continuing judicial relief rather than a declaration of mootness from changed conduct alone.

The strongest counterargument is that excluding four claimed crossover opportunities from the proportionality numerator and beginning with a Gingles presumption could have affected the totality judgment. Stevens accepts that these propositions cannot stand. He nevertheless regards the independent detailed findings of racial precinct transfers, population inconsistency, protection of incumbents, actual polarization, and a workable alternative as a proved practical denial, not just an unfilled racial quota. Existing crossover successes are evidence and may overlap opportunity for more than one group; they do not automatically erase this proved local injury. His alternate lawful path is limited reassessment if the injury findings depend on the faulty count rather than the concrete precinct changes. He will not join a rule making racial purpose alone section 2 liability or guaranteeing another majority district. R affirms the existing injunction and leaves enacted Plan C in place without ordering fresh redistricting.

**O'Connor.** Joins L, governing S, and P, but on T votes to vacate for a correct totality assessment. Her De Grandy concurrence makes proportionality relevant evidence that must actually be considered, while rejecting both a safe harbor and a quota. Her narrower Valdespino position insisted on numerical ability-to-elect proof for the first condition; the alternative here satisfies the material numerical showing, so that prior disagreement does not decide this case. Her Martinez join requires actual local opportunity and proper weight for crossover and incumbent success. Her Laidlaw opinion distinguishes changed circumstances from elimination of a live controversy; the continuing injunction supplies one here.

The strongest case for affirmance is the unusually concrete precinct-transfer and incumbent-protection evidence, which can establish a local injury despite aggregate opportunity. O'Connor accepts that it could support liability after correct analysis. She cannot treat the legal error as inconsequential when the court explicitly refused to count demonstrated crossover opportunity in a major totality comparison and described Gingles satisfaction presumptively. Her alternate path is affirmance once the findings demonstrate unequal opportunity with that evidence properly included; no entitlement to victory follows from counting crossover districts. She refuses either dismissal because overall opportunity appears proportional or affirmance merely because another Black-majority district is feasible. R vacates the affected liability/remedy for reassessment, preserves supported factual findings, and leaves the State's enacted Plan C intact. Interim protection requires an ordinary equitable basis; no new legislative deadline is imposed.

**Scalia.** Joins L and would reverse under his contrary S position: section 2 regulates equal access to casting and counting votes, not a judicially selected measure of districting-based voting strength. His joins in Thomas's De Grandy dissent and subsequent Valdespino and Martinez threshold positions supply concrete continuity. He does not treat those minority views as current law for other Justices, but maintains them as his own ground for the judgment. He does not adopt P or T as an independent affirmative finding of equal opportunity.

The strongest counterargument is the settled governing coverage line, Congress's results language, and the concrete evidence that racial sorting made votes less effective. Scalia considers the absence of a statutory benchmark for the appropriate districting allocation decisive under his own interpretation. If the Court retains coverage, the lower court's exclusion of crossover opportunities and presumptive use of Gingles offer a narrower lawful reason to vacate rather than affirm; that is an alternative judgment path, not a joinder in the coverage rationale. He refuses numerical racial proportionality, a judicial maximization rule, or revival of dismissed constitutional claims through section 2. His committed R is reversal and dismissal of the statutory claim with dissolution of its federal injunction. Plan C remains enacted state law and is not replaced by the 2001 plan through this federal judgment.

**Kennedy.** Joins L, governing S, and P; on T votes to vacate for reassessment. His De Grandy concurrence joined the causal and nonmaximization holding but withheld the broader ratio application; he treated proportionality as relevant, rejected a safe harbor, and resisted an automatic racial allocation. His Valdespino narrower position and his Martinez joins likewise distinguish actual electoral opportunity from presumed demographic entitlement. His Laidlaw join does not require treating a continuing injunction as moot because a replacement was enacted.

The strongest affirmance argument is that the court independently found discriminatory packing with a genuine electoral effect, not merely inadequate demographic percentages. Kennedy accepts the significance of those supported findings and would not disturb them merely because crossover success exists elsewhere. He nonetheless considers it necessary for the trial court to evaluate actual opportunity under the correct legal framework when its opinion expressly excluded crossover districts from a principal comparison and used presumptive language. His alternate path is affirmance on concrete denied opportunity after that correction; aggregate parity would not itself immunize packing. He refuses an inference that protection of incumbents is necessarily lawful when achieved through racial exclusion, but equally refuses a racial entitlement to a particular number of representatives. R is limited reassessment with Plan C undisturbed, no automatic revival of the old map, and any interim federal restraint justified separately.

**Souter.** Joins L, S, and P and affirms T on the independently supported causal record. His De Grandy opinion supplies the distinction between necessary conditions, totality, and maximization; its proportionality discussion concerned actual opportunity and supplied no automatic offset for a proved local injury. His Valdespino majority join and Martinez opinion make functional electoral evidence central. His Laidlaw join permits review of this continuing injunctive restraint while recognizing that a past candidate-relief request may separately be moot.

The strongest contrary argument is that the trial court's express numerical-majority definition and presumption infected its ultimate assessment. Souter agrees those passages must be rejected, and an affirmance cannot adopt them as harmless stylistic shorthand. He finds a sufficient independent ground in the detailed transfer of Black precincts away from districts that needed population into an already concentrated district, the purpose and practical effect of protecting white incumbents, supported bloc-voting findings, and an effective compact alternative. The court also evaluated actual crossover success, even though it wrongly excluded it from the ratio calculation. His alternate path is remand if that independent evaluation does not establish the practical injury without the arithmetic; he commits here to its sufficiency. He refuses to require a racial quota, to discount a candidate merely for being white, or to regard every incumbent victory as meaningless. R affirms the existing statutory injunction and requires no new map process.

**Thomas.** Joins L and votes to reverse under S's noncoverage ground. His own De Grandy dissent, followed in Valdespino and Martinez, construes section 2 as protecting access to the ballot rather than authorizing a judicial rule for the proper distribution of voting strength among districts. His position is unchanged by whether the alternative map is compact or whether the challenged transfers were race-based; those matters could be relevant to a properly presented independent constitutional theory, but dismissed constitutional counts are not before the Court for revival.

The strongest opposing argument is that the governing section 2 line treats these facts as a practical denial of equal opportunity and does not require maximization. Thomas considers that framework incapable of deriving its baseline from the enacted access right. His alternative, if coverage governs the disposition, is to reject this totality analysis because it excludes demonstrated crossover opportunity and tends toward a presumption of racial entitlement; he can support vacatur on that narrower basis without joining the framework he rejects. He will not join an opinion suggesting that demographic proportionality is the required remedy or that every elected white candidate represents only white voters. His committed R is reversal, dissolution of the dependent federal restraint, and dismissal of the section 2 claim. It does not repeal Plan C or issue a substitute federal map.

**Ginsburg.** Joins L, S, and P and affirms liability under T's actual unequal-opportunity ground. Her De Grandy Parts II and III joins reject maximization and treat meaningful opportunity as the relevant inquiry. Her Valdespino majority join recognizes demonstrated crossover opportunity; her Martinez joins require a supported local link between districting and denied electoral opportunity. Her Laidlaw join distinguishes continuing relief from a mooted discrete request.

The strongest counterargument is the lower court's mistaken treatment of crossover districts and its presumptive formulation. Ginsburg rejects both, including any assumption that representation by a white candidate cannot be minority opportunity. She finds the independent precinct-level findings sufficiently concrete: the map moved Black precincts contrary to the ordinary population need, concentrated them in the already heavily Black district, and protected incumbents in circumstances where supported voting patterns and the alternative map establish an actual impairment. Mere ability to draw another majority district would not suffice. Her alternate path is a limited remand if the remaining findings cannot establish that effect without the numerical premise; she concludes they can here. She refuses a new constitutional racial-gerrymander holding, a section 5 retrogression rule imported into section 2, or treating successful crossover representation as nonexistent. R preserves the existing statutory injunction and enacted remedy without additional court-ordered redistricting.

**Breyer.** Joins L, S, and P and affirms T on the practical, supported packing findings. His Valdespino majority join makes functional ability to elect a legally available path requiring evidence; his Martinez joins demand actual local performance and a proved injury rather than demographic prediction. De Grandy governs as precedent; he did not participate in that 1994 decision. His Laidlaw join supports adjudicating the live injunction while separating completed candidate relief.

The strongest case for vacatur is that a material legal mistake in evaluating totality ordinarily should return to the trial court. Breyer accepts that discipline but regards these independent findings as sufficient without the disputed majority-only arithmetic: concrete precinct transfers and inconsistent population explanations, the supported political effect, and a workable alternative distinguish the case from a request simply to maximize districts. Crossover and incumbency remain part of the practical evidence, not automatic defenses or automatic proof of injury. His alternate path is reassessment where those independent findings leave the effect genuinely undecided; his committed reading is that the proved local denial survives correction. He refuses a numerical entitlement, a race-based assumption about preferred candidates, or rejection of cross-racial political success. R affirms the already tailored statutory injunction, preserves Plan C, and orders neither a new six-week response nor a new map.

### Commitment tally and limits

All eight accept L and the described procedural limits. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer apply governing coverage and accept P; Thomas and Scalia maintain noncoverage. On the operative liability disposition, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer affirm on independent actual injury; O'Connor and Kennedy vacate for corrected totality analysis; Thomas and Scalia reverse and direct dismissal on coverage. These grounds are not mechanically combined into a fictitious common statutory rationale. Every path leaves enacted Plan C as state law and the dismissed constitutional counts unrevived. No commitment authorizes a new racial quota, replacement plan, or elapsed 2004 remedial deadline.
