## Judgment and form

One signed opinion of the Court resolves the plenary merits case. Part I resolves the federal classification of an actual state application-filing deadline with exceptions and denies statutory exclusion. Part II separately assumes the broader equitable authority invoked, applies the necessary reasonable-diligence condition to the asserted obstacle and established chronology, and rejects that protection. These are independently necessary steps supporting one affirmance; neither is an alternative sufficient to affirm without addressing the other asserted route.

The judgment is affirmed, five to four. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support affirmance and fully join both parts at the stated scope. Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse the statutory ruling, exclude November 27, 1996 through July 29, 1999, and remand for exact calculation and remaining habeas requirements. They do not reach independent equity. A single statutory dissent states their common ground. Stevens is selected for that separate writing from his own Artuz and Carey joins, his Duncan concurrence and his Pliler treatment of the distinct statutory and equitable protections; historical authorship supplies no assignment premise.

The signed form reflects two contested questions with a shared reasoned majority and one reasoned dissent after plenary review. ## Governing propositions and complete limits

Part I applies Artuz's distinction between application delivery/acceptance requirements and claim-relief bars. Exceptions define additional routes to permitted filing rather than remove the deadline. The actual Pennsylvania one-year rule, each allegation-and-proof exception, sixty-day exception period, finality definition, defense-counsel exclusion and first-petition transition are stated in full substance. The state appellate untimeliness determination controls state law; the federal statutory classification remains the Court's task. The applicant must establish the dates and qualifying state pendency supporting the claimed exclusion. The need to adjudicate claim-dependent exception facts, clerical receipt and an earlier merits discussion do not establish proper filing.

Part II assumes broader equitable authority and the necessity of an extraordinary impediment and reasonable diligence. The asserted uncertain-state-law/exhaustion trap fails to establish diligence across the supported preceding years and the five months following state review. Old claims and the family meeting's 1985/1986 subject matter do not establish unverified affidavit execution or delivery dates. Pre-AEDPA years bear on pursuit of the claims, rather than the calculation of a federal period before enactment. Circumstances available when Pace acted govern; the opinion imposes no retroactive protective-filing duty derived from Rhines. It preserves Pliler's existing court-caused-loss protection and Johnson's distinct discovery trigger. No categorical noncapital bar or universal five-month rule is adopted.
