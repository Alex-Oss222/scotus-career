## Wilkinson v. Dotson

### Comparator and changed premise

History affirmed and remanded eight to one. Kennedy dissented because he treated the challenged parole denials as custody-producing decisions, analogized a new parole proceeding to resentencing after invalidation, and considered Preiser's specific habeas channel exclusive. Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurred in the historical majority while emphasizing the limits of habeas relief and the distinction between a valid sentence plus discretionary parole error and an invalid sentence.

Retain all eight provisional Associate votes to affirm the present §1983 cognizability judgment. Kennedy's departure is supported by a concrete prior simulated commitment, not a general invocation of precedent. Cavanaugh Part II already held that an order restoring a legally required occasion for discretionary parole consideration may proceed under §1983 where the judgment leaves the sentence and lawful denial intact; Kennedy personally joined that holding. Cavanaugh addressed loss of a mandatory reconsideration opportunity, not merely a condition of confinement, and expressly rejected the claim that the prisoner's hope of earlier release made habeas exclusive. The present Dotson eligibility and Johnson suitability relief have the same necessary legal consequence. His later Heck Part IV, Edwards, Muhammad and Nelson joins preserve claim-specific treatment and do not repudiate Cavanaugh.

Kennedy's historical sentencing analogy therefore encounters a changed legal premise: here his own controlling parole-specific precedent classifies opportunity-only relief as compatible with lawful custody. An invalid sentence is the legal authority being displaced by a new sentencing order; a discretionary parole denial leaves an independently valid sentence authorizing continued custody. This legal distinction answers the historical objection without reducing it to a mere probability of earlier release. No operative new position by Kennedy calls for overruling Cavanaugh, and no materially different automatic-release feature is established for either complaint.

**Historical departure:** Kennedy supports §1983 cognizability because he previously joined Cavanaugh's controlling rule permitting prospective restoration of discretionary parole consideration while the sentence and lawful continued confinement remain intact; that parole-specific premise was absent from his historical Wilkinson dissent and directly answers its view that every such denial must be attacked only in habeas.

### Reconciled rule, individual support and limits

An opportunity-only parole challenge may proceed under §1983 when its successful judgment neither invalidates the legal authority for existing custody nor requires release or a shorter custodial term. A possible favorable discretionary decision after lawful consideration is insufficient to invoke habeas exclusivity. Labels do not control: a process claim whose success necessarily displaces a custody-producing legal determination remains subject to the appropriate channel and invalidation rules. Actual legal consequence, not the prisoner's ultimate objective or the generic relationship of parole to duration, decides this case.

The same judgment covers Dotson's prospective retroactive-guidelines challenge and Johnson's prospective guidelines, panel-composition and speaking-opportunity claims at the vehicle stage. Both may proceed to ordinary adjudication. No constitutional parole entitlement or new-hearing injunction has yet been established. Screening, available administrative-remedy exhaustion, defenses and statutory and equitable limits on prospective relief retain independent effect; automatic conversion into habeas is not the remedy.
