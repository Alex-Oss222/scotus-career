# Kelo — proposed neutral ground supplement for fresh preflight

This is a proposed legal-source supplement for clean neutral validation and affected independent modeling before June 23, 2005. It supplies no Justice commitment, current-case comparison, opinion assignment, or new factual finding. The supplied condemnation record and existing public-use law remain unchanged.

## Constitutional source and competing legal meanings

The operative Fifth Amendment text is: “nor shall private property be taken for public use, without just compensation.” Public use and compensation are independent conditions. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/fifth_amendment .

One possible constitutional argument reads public use as government ownership or actual governmental use, or a legally enforceable public right to use the property. Public railroads and other common carriers illustrate private title combined with a public right of use. That argument would distinguish public use from incidental economic advantages of an ordinary privately occupied commercial property. It would require candid reconsideration of the broader public-purpose rationale in Berman and Midkiff; it cannot be described as the existing controlling rule. Its strongest objection is that the actual precedents expressly accept public purposes beyond public ownership or universal public access, and a lower court remains bound by those precedents unless this Court changes them.

The competing existing rule accepts an actual supported public purpose, including private implementation, and preserves the independent prohibition on a private-favor pretext. On that rule, the constitutional issue is the undertaking's public purpose, not the mechanics of title transfer. The existing Boston & Maine passenger-service holding permits private reconveyance while securing continuing passenger use. Its actual service application therefore differs from an ordinary private business transfer even though its rationale is broader. An agency-explanation objection to that order does not itself decide the constitutional scope of public use.

Two pre-divergence primary sources help separate the source of condemnation authority from the constitutional restriction; neither independently establishes that modern public-purpose precedents have already been displaced:

- Cole v. City of La Grange, 113 U.S. 1, 6–8 (1885), rejects municipal bonds supporting an admitted purely private manufacturing enterprise. Its discussion of Missouri's public-use compensation provision reasons that the provision presupposes a prohibition on taking for private use; otherwise private takings would escape even compensation. The opinion distinguishes railroad assistance serving public travel and a toll mill obligated to serve customers. Its actual holding concerns public financing under the identified state constitutional limits, so it is an analogy on public versus private use, not a holding resolving this redevelopment case. Full opinion and statutory footnotes: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/113/1 .
- Kohl v. United States, 91 U.S. 367, 371–374 (1876), recognizes federal condemnation authority to obtain property needed for constitutional governmental functions, including postal and judicial facilities. It discusses the source and necessity of federal power and the compensation limitation. That recognized governmental-use example does not itself decide whether every public-purpose taking for private implementation is forbidden. Opinion: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/91/367 .

The common-law distinction between regulation of harmful use and compensated acquisition must also remain intact. A power to prohibit a nuisance does not alone establish power to acquire ordinary property for a private successor, and an acquisition satisfying public use still carries its independent compensation requirement. Berman's area redevelopment and Midkiff's concentrated ownership must receive their actual terms rather than be redescribed as uncompensated nuisance regulation.

## Meaningful review and any proposed stronger review

An existing rational-public-purpose rule does not permit courts to disregard a supported accusation that the actual object is favoring a particular private recipient and the professed public benefit merely incidental or pretextual. The court can examine the actual record while retaining the ordinary presumption of legitimate public action. Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, 473 U.S. 432, 446–450 (1985), and Department of Agriculture v. Moreno, 413 U.S. 528, 533–538 (1973), provide equal-protection analogies for a rational-basis inquiry that does not accept an impermissible private-object justification. Those cases are not public-use holdings and do not themselves impose heightened scrutiny for redevelopment.

The separate question whether a narrower class of unusually suspicious transfers could warrant stronger scrutiny remains available for consideration as an unresolved boundary. It must not become a new presumption, burden, checklist, or categorical invalidity rule without an independently supported legal ground and a record presenting that question. The supplied comprehensive plan and absence of a finding of sham do not establish every conceivable factual predicate concerning unknown future transactions. The existing economic forecasts neither guarantee public success nor prove private favoritism.

For fresh modeling, test whether the broader original-use argument changes any supported rule or separate-writing function, and whether the existing anti-pretext rationale fully accommodates meaningful review while expressly leaving a stronger-review question open. Do not infer new parcel facts, new recipient identities, new procedural findings, or any anticipated judicial vote.
