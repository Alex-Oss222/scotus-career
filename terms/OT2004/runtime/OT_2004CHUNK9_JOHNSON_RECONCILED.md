## Reconciled result and governing terms

Seven Associates — Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer — reverse and remand on the full inference standard, its application and the completion of a reliable Batson adjudication. Thomas affirms on first-stage state procedural latitude. These are seven-to-one Associate commitments; the final Court count awaits assembly.

A defendant satisfies Batson's first step by producing facts sufficient to permit an inference of purposeful racial discrimination. California's more-likely-than-not requirement improperly moves ultimate persuasion ahead of the prosecutor's explanation. All three qualified Black prospective jurors were removed from the forty-three-person eligible venire, and the surrounding record supports the inference requiring explanations. The trial court must obtain the prosecutor's actual reasons and make the final purpose determination, with the persuasion burden remaining on Johnson. A reliable retrospective adjudication is the required remand work; if it cannot be supplied, a new trial supplies the effective remedy. That reliability instruction implements the frozen actual-finding rule and the existing remand, rather than treating the prima facie inference as a completed finding of discrimination.

Batson supplies the inference and three-stage sequence. Hernandez separates facial neutrality from the actual credibility/purpose finding. The actual Franklyn/Gonzalez holding requires a real ultimate finding before deference can sustain rejection; it supplies no substitute finding where explanations were never requested. The earlier Johnson finality dismissal decides no Batson issue. These rules answer the State's concern about routine challenges: the first stage still requires a supported inference, while its evidentiary function differs from final persuasion.

## Separate-writing substance and limits

Thomas's dissent explains that equal protection forbids intentional racial selection, while Batson's procedure is a means of enforcing that prohibition. He reads Batson's reservation of implementation procedures, reinforced by the state-procedure flexibility discussed in Finley and Smith v. Robbins, to permit California's substantial prima facie requirement. The seven reversing Associates reject that construction because the procedure must elicit the explanation before final persuasion can fairly occur. His dissent concerns step one, not a finding that the challenged strikes were race neutral.

Breyer joins the Court and retains the peremptory-challenge reform concern expressed in his paired same-date Miller-El concurrence: the present procedure can fail to expose pretext and stereotypes, and equal participation in juries warrants reconsidering the continued use of peremptories. His Johnson statement adopts that separate position, without making the companion merits holding authority here. The detailed basis belongs in the Miller-El separate-writing continuity account. This separate position does not withdraw any Johnson join or eliminate peremptories by judgment.

## Original independent commitment, preserved verbatim

## 79. Johnson v. California, No. 04-6964

**Question and disposition:** Seven Associates would reverse the first-stage threshold ruling and remand for completion of Batson's process. Thomas would affirm California's first-stage rule and the reviewed judgment. All reach the granted merits question on the supplied final-judgment posture.

**Common reversal ground:** A defendant triggers the prosecutor's explanation duty by facts sufficient to permit an inference of purposeful racial exclusion. Requiring proof that discrimination was more likely than not before the prosecutor explains the challenged strikes improperly demands an ultimate persuasion showing at the production stage. Striking all three qualified Black prospective jurors from this venire satisfies the required inference on the supplied record. The trial court must receive and evaluate the actual reasons and determine purposeful discrimination with the ultimate burden remaining on Johnson. A retrospective hearing must permit a reliable determination; if the lapse or state of the record prevents that constitutionally adequate adjudication, the state court must provide a new trial. This is a remedial consequence of the unsuccessful original inquiry, not a present finding that the strikes were discriminatory.

**Authorities and competing paths:** Batson, 476 U.S. 79, makes the surrounding circumstances and an inference the gateway to an explanation and reserves ultimate persuasion to the opponent. Hernandez v. New York, 500 U.S. 352, distinguishes production from the final credibility inquiry. Franklyn/Gonzalez, decided October 22, 1998, requires an actual final purpose finding; it cannot supply an alternative affirmance when reasons were never required. Miller-El v. Cockrell, February 25, 2003, preserves different thresholds for different procedural stages. California's strongest competing path invokes Batson's latitude for state implementation and the need to keep peremptories from becoming automatic challenges for cause. That path cannot justify making an objector prove concealed intent before the State discloses its actual explanation, but it supplies Thomas's narrower account of permissible prima facie administration. The meaningful inference requirement itself answers the concern that every strike triggers an explanation.
