## Ordinary-review sequence and contested rehearing attempt

The original federal petition was filed in June 1997. After a magistrate recommended dismissal on limitations grounds, Gonzalez objected and cited Lovasz v. Vaughn, 134 F.3d 146 (3d Cir. 1998), concerning statutory tolling. The final magistrate report acknowledged that the Eleventh Circuit had not yet decided the tolling issue and cited Lovasz in rejecting the claim. This shows an identified procedural issue was presented at the district-court stage; it does not establish that every later certificate application repeated that specific argument.

On September 23, 1998, Gonzalez applied for a certificate of appealability, which the district court granted. On October 28, 1999, the court of appeals dismissed that appeal and remanded for identification of the particular issues meriting appeal. Gonzalez submitted a new certificate application on remand. The district court denied it; Gonzalez timely appealed and received permission to proceed without prepaying fees. The court of appeals denied a certificate and dismissed the appeal on April 6, 2000.

Gonzalez alleges that on April 18, 2000, he submitted a timely rehearing petition, but the appellate clerk returned it unfiled with information that his appeal had been dismissed and closed on October 28, 1999. He contends that this information also led him to believe his time to seek further review had expired. The State gives a competing account: Gonzalez used the case number of the earlier dismissed appeal. The supplied material does not resolve which account is accurate, whether official misinformation prevented a proper filing, or how a renewed filing would have been handled. The petition's alleged submission is a disputed procedural fact; it is not a completed rehearing adjudication, an established external impediment, or an adjudicated excuse.

Sources for the competing allegations are the reopening motion at Appendix 16 and the reply at page 13, against the State's response at page 4, as recited in the authenticated report at 545 U.S. 542–544. The certificate chronology and Lovasz objection are recited at 545 U.S. 543–544 n.5. The original certificate denial is dated April 6, 2000, in the report; do not derive a different date by counting backward from a later Supreme Court event.

No timely petition for certiorari from that certificate denial is established. The supplied chronology places the Rule 60 motion about eight to nine months after Artuz became effective on November 7, 2000. Gonzalez was incarcerated and acting without counsel. The underlying claim alleges that inaccurate advice about actual time to be served made his guilty plea unknowing and involuntary; that is an allegation, not a finding that the plea was invalid or that habeas relief was warranted.

## Effect on neutral alternatives

Replace a categorical statement that Gonzalez made no effort at further review after the April 2000 denial with the precise distinction between an unaccepted alleged rehearing submission, the disputed reason for its rejection, and the absence of established certiorari review. Do not convert an absence of a proven external impediment into a finding that there was no contested diligence issue. Conversely, the allegation does not prove an excuse or require reopening.

The alternatives remain: decide that the asserted procedural legal change and this actual showing do not establish extraordinary circumstances even with the alleged rehearing attempt considered; or return the motion for an ordinary discretionary assessment, including resolution of a material disputed diligence fact if necessary. Any determination must distinguish the period after Artuz from the earlier appellate steps and must not treat a set number of months as a categorical bar. Reopening would not itself establish original petition timeliness or the plea claim's merit. The motion's process-only classification remains distinct and unchanged.
