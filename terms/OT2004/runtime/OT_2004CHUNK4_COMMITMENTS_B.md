## Cherokee Nation of Oklahoma v. Leavitt — March 1, 2005

### Components, legal conditions and competing paths

No. 02-1472 concerns Cherokee's FY1997 claims and Shoshone-Paiute's FY1996–1997 claims; No. 03-853 concerns Cherokee's FY1994–1996 claims. Both tribes have initial or expanded-program claims, and Cherokee also has ongoing-program claims. The fresh neutral supplement, the safe federal submission and the quoted transaction-effective text supply these distinctions. The governmental submission's characterization of legally available funds remains advocacy; its descriptions of the actual appropriations, agreements and lower rulings retain their identified source status.

**Path A — enforce the actual funding undertakings.** The statute and incorporated agreements promise the reasonable, allowable support costs of the contracted programs, subject to appropriations availability and their real terms. An unrestricted lump sum legally usable for that contract satisfies the availability condition; administrative distribution among competing demands cannot by itself convert authorized contract funds into legally unavailable funds. This is contract responsibility, not an equitable promise inferred from tribal status. Hercules supplies the actual-authorized-duty requirement, and Winstar distinguishes a financial obligation from a restriction on sovereign legislative authority. Lincoln permits administrative allocation where no enforceable boundary exists; a binding contract is an additional legal boundary.

The strongest objection combines the statutory no-reduction clause, the annual initial/expanded-program earmark, the agreement's congressional-adjustment language and §314. Each must receive its own answer:

1. Then-§450j-1(b)'s appropriations qualification remains real. The relevant FY1994–1997 lump sums were legally available for these undertakings; administrative support-cost budgets and committee-report expectations did not originally enact a total support-cost ceiling. Availability is a legal limitation on the undertaking, not whatever allocation the agency later elects to call available. A genuinely binding statutory limitation would control.
2. The separate clause that the Secretary is not required to reduce funding for programs, projects or activities serving another tribe protects against a demand to redistribute other tribal program funding. It does not require reductions, and it does not extinguish the United States' responsibility for an authorized promise made against legally sufficient appropriations. The tribes seek adjudication of the contract obligation; recognition of that obligation does not direct the Secretary to raid another tribe's program. A contractor need not monitor the agency's competing allocations to preserve its promise. The contrary reading would let the agency erase contract responsibility through the very allocations Lincoln leaves to its administrative judgment.
3. The $7.5 million provision for initial or expanded contracts makes that amount remain available until expended for the Indian Self-Determination Fund's transitional costs. It guarantees a designated fund and continued availability; it does not state that no other otherwise available annual money may satisfy the authorized support-cost undertaking. Read with the larger authorized lump sum, the text is not an exclusive ceiling. This answer applies to the initial/expanded claims independently of the committee-report answer for ongoing claims. The explicit overall not-to-exceed limitation beginning in FY1998 is not backdated to these earlier contracts.
4. Section 314 of Pub. L. 105-277, 112 Stat. 2681-288, limits the amounts remaining available from the identified FY1994–1998 appropriations and committee-report allocations. It does not expressly rescind accrued contractual obligations or the contract remedy. Its retrospective identification of fiscal-year accounts has work to do without converting a spending limit into destruction of already accrued claims. The agreement's adjustment language for congressional action affecting availability is read with this actual operation; it does not independently enlarge §314 into cancellation of accrued liability. This construction avoids attributing repudiation of a genuine governmental obligation to an accounting restriction without deciding a freestanding constitutional claim.

Under Path A, reverse the Tenth Circuit in No. 02-1472 and remand for contract adjudication consistent with those resolved defenses; affirm the Federal Circuit's favorable contract-liability disposition in No. 03-853 and return through the ordinary mandate for the genuinely remaining computation and implementation. Each claimant must establish the actual annual amount promised and still unpaid, reasonable and allowable costs, nonduplication with transferred program or administrative funding, causation and compensable loss under its agreement. No mandatory evidentiary trial is created: remaining matters follow ordinary contract procedure. The initial/expanded and ongoing categories and fiscal years remain distinct. The Court does not issue an agency-wide allocation order. That limitation is an answer to the Government's demanded no-reduction construction, not an excuse for incomplete payment-law resolution.

**Path B — treat the statutory and contractual qualifiers as aggregate program limits.** The Secretary's duty to serve noncontracting tribes, the express no-reduction clause and the $7.5 million designation could be read together as conditioning each promise on funds left within the program allocation after other statutory functions. On that reading, the authorized agreements incorporate rather than assume aggregate funding risk; §314 confirms the cap retrospectively. It would support affirming No. 02-1472 and reversing No. 03-853 for judgment on the relevant shortfall defenses, or a bounded remand if the legal limit's application to an annual claim remained unproved. It would preserve payment for any proven amount within the lawful allocation and would not itself find every cost disallowed.

The strongest objection is textual and contractual: the appropriation does not say that the $7.5 million is the sole source; the no-reduction language is permission not to reallocate, not a cancellation command; and committee reports initially were not enacted caps. Winstar requires enforcing actual conditions, but supplies no reason to add an aggregate no-liability condition to the particular promise. Section 314 speaks to available appropriation amounts rather than accrued claims. All eight Associates find those objections decisive on these supported annual terms. They reject Path B at this scope without denying Congress's capacity to enact an actual cap or condition.

## Petro-Hunt, L.L.C. v. United States — March 1, 2005

### Record, reach and two competing paths

This merits review is properly before the Court under the supplied review premise. The revised Fifth Circuit opinion, read in full, establishes 96 distinct servitudes arising from six mineral conveyances and eleven federal acquisitions. Nebo Oil's operative decree concerns the servitude on the approximately 800 acres described in its complaint. Its broader findings do not enlarge that decree to the other 95 servitudes. The United States accepts the earlier decree's continuing force for its adjudicated servitude. The district court's blanket declaration covered approximately 180,000 acres without resolving the different use histories. The Fifth Circuit held the other 95 servitudes subject to ten-year nonuse prescription and remanded to determine which had actually prescribed; its judgment is not a universal finding of nonuse.

**Path A — affirm the rejection of preclusion.** Federal common law determines this federal-question judgment's effect. Separate servitudes with different conveyances and use histories are different claims; one dispute over attempted drilling did not require adjudication of title to every other property held by the same parties. Issue preclusion remains separately available for an identical, actually litigated and necessary issue, but Nebo Oil assumed the applicability of Louisiana law and decided state-law retroactivity and constitutional objections. That does not actually adjudicate the distinct federal choice-of-law question. Nevada v. United States preserves property finality for the same claim; Sunnen and Montana require identity at the issue level; Semtek distinguishes the source and scope of preclusion without extending its diversity incorporation rule here.

The strongest objection is that the conveyances formed a connected arrangement and Nebo Oil necessarily accepted the common imprescriptibility premise. The answer rests on the actual operative facts and question adjudicated: common legal reasoning does not merge separate rights and histories, and an assumed choice of law does not become an actually litigated issue merely because the judgment depended on applying the assumed law. The decree's wording controls its property scope. The remedy on this path is affirmance of the appellate judgment, return for the already ordered servitude-specific prescription proceedings, and preservation of Nebo Oil's adjudicated title. Good-faith qualifying operations and any conceded interruptions must be considered for each servitude under the governing ten-year rule. The district court must resolve the second summary-judgment motion that it treated as moot, and determine title from the resulting histories. The fee cross-appeal remains without a present prevailing-party predicate; any later fee claim follows the eventual judgment.

**Path B — enforce a common issue while rejecting blanket claim identity.** A final decision can preclude an actually litigated legal issue in a later case involving a distinct property right. If Nebo Oil had necessarily resolved the same federal choice-of-law question, proof of distinct servitudes would not alone defeat mutual issue preclusion. Stauffer supplies the strongest defense against turning every similar later dispute into free reconsideration. Relief under that path would vacate the appellate preclusion ruling and require identification of the specific issue, its adjudicated scope and the affected servitudes; it would not award title across the entire acreage merely because a common issue exists. The decisive obstacle on the authenticated account is actual litigation: the earlier decision addressed retroactivity and constitutionality, not the federal source of the rule of decision. This path therefore does not survive on the present record.

**Alternative changed-law route.** Sunnen and Montana permit attention to a material change in the governing legal setting in a later action; Stauffer's unchanged-law setting is different. Little Lake Misere supplies the Government's asserted change. Its strongest opponent is the finality of adjudicated property rights, which is answered only by confining any exception to later distinct claims and leaving the original decree untouched. The chosen nonidentity and actual-litigation grounds resolve the preclusion controversy, so no Associate's commitment requires a new alternative changed-law holding. The exception for unrelated claims presenting an unmixed legal question is analytically separate from a material intervening change in law and is not substituted for it.

## Tenet v. Doe — March 2, 2005

### Components and adversarial alternatives

The Court reviews the certified orders allowing the surviving agreement-dependent estoppel and constitutional claims to proceed. The Does seek interim monthly support, a declaration prescribing internal review, and mandamus directing support and regulations. Their alleged recruitment, services, continuing-support promise, defection and resulting relationship remain allegations; the Government neither confirms nor denies them. The dismissed equal-protection count was not revived by the accepted interlocutory appeal. No disposition here reopens that branch.

**Path A — apply Totten at the outset.** Totten distinguishes the authority to make and honor a secret-service undertaking from its judicial enforceability. Its subject-matter bar applies when the asserted right and requested relief necessarily depend on proving the secret espionage relationship. Estoppel, a property entitlement and a demand for fair procedures do not supply an independent source when each depends on the same alleged promise. A court could not prescribe the requested process without first recognizing the asserted relationship and an entitlement arising from it. Pseudonyms and restrained initial allegations do not alter what must eventually be established. Reynolds preserves Totten's distinct pleading-stage application while governing evidence in a suit otherwise maintainable. The Director's personal invocation required for the evidentiary privilege is therefore not a prerequisite to this categorical bar.

The strongest counterargument is the lower court's precise procedural distinction: an internal administrative hearing may occur confidentially and judicial relief might require only that hearing, not public disclosure or a damages trial. Webster also preserves colorable constitutional claims arising from ordinary Agency employment decisions. The response is that the proposed judicial command still requires a cognizable entitlement to review of the alleged secret undertaking. Webster's independent constitutional challenge to a statutory employment decision does not silently eliminate Totten's preexisting limit on judicial enforcement of a secret bargain. The Does identify no independent public statutory entitlement establishing the benefits or review duty they seek. Their constitutional characterization is consequential to the promise, not independent of it.

Under Path A, reverse the appellate judgment and remand with instructions to dismiss the surviving claims founded on the alleged secret agreement. The district court implements that dismissal instead of undertaking the proposed Reynolds inquiry or ordering support, benefits procedures or regulations on those claims. The already denied preliminary injunction and dismissed equal-protection branch remain undisturbed. The disposition makes no finding confirming the relationship and creates no judicially enforceable administrative substitute.

**Path B — allow the process claims and require a proper Reynolds invocation.** A narrower reading of Totten as a contractual enforcement rule could distinguish a request for fair internal review and allow it to proceed, initially using pseudonyms and protective controls. This path would affirm the refusal to dismiss, remand for ordinary jurisdiction and waiver examination and identification of a cognizable entitlement, and require the Director's actual personal consideration and formal privilege claim before adjudicating a request to withhold evidence. Judicial assessment would protect secrecy without assuming that in-camera inspection must always occur. It would not award interim support or finally establish liability merely because the complaint survives.

The strongest objection is that this proposed distinction changes the label and forum of requested performance without changing the secret bargain that must establish entitlement. Totten's rationale expressly includes the existence of the relationship; Reynolds itself distinguishes dismissal on the pleadings from evidentiary privilege. No independent law supplies the missing entitlement here. Thus the procedural path cannot avoid the categorical rule on these pleaded claims. All eight Associates select Path A at this bounded scope.

## Catholic Charities of Sacramento, Inc. v. Superior Court — March 2, 2005

### Scope, statutory operation and independent claims

The constitutional merits are properly before the Court under the binding review premise. No additional finality, docket or filing verification is required. The reviewed disposition denies preliminary relief; the merits of the federal objections are presented within that posture, not as an existing permanent injunction. The provision material to the established group-plan coverage is Health and Safety Code §1367.25. The complete separate Insurance Code provision was not independently verified, so no clause unique to disability policies is added to these commitments.

For the stated contracts issued, amended, renewed or delivered from January 1, 2000, the statute requires a plan offering outpatient prescription benefits to cover a variety of approved prescription contraceptive methods. If none designated by the plan is medically appropriate to the patient's medical or personal history, it must cover another approved medically appropriate method prescribed by the provider. Covered spouses and nonspouse dependents receive the same outpatient prescription benefits. Specialized health-service contracts are excepted. The provision does not require experimental or investigational treatment and preserves regulatory compliance authority.

The religious-employer exception requires all four conditions: inculcation of religious values as the entity's purpose; primarily employing adherents; primarily serving adherents; and the specified nonprofit tax-return status for churches, their integrated auxiliaries, conventions or associations of churches, or exclusively religious activities of a religious order. A qualifying employer may request omission of approved methods contrary to its tenets; the plan must supply the requested exclusion, and the employer must give prospective enrollees written notice before enrollment. The separate preservation of coverage for noncontraceptive uses and for contraception necessary to preserve an enrollee's life or health continues. Permission to request an exclusion is not an obligation to request it.

Catholic Charities concedes it meets none of the four conditions. Its group plans cover 183 employees of varied religions, and it provides social services to the public. Those facts do not establish that its mission is irreligious. Its sincerely asserted obligation to provide adequate employee benefits is treated as a religious claim, not dismissed because another court might call charity philosophical. The fourth criterion is independently relevant: invalidating only the inculcation condition would not make this applicant eligible.

### Two competing legal paths and remedies

**Path A — sustain the insured-benefit requirement and bounded accommodation.** The governmental act is regulation of the insurance benefits attached to this organization's employment of a religiously mixed workforce. It neither decides Catholic doctrine nor organizes religious observance. Smith's retained rule covers neutral generally applicable conduct regulation; Lukumi requires objective targeting or substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm before its strict scrutiny applies. The law regulates prescription coverage furnished by religious and nonreligious employers alike. An exemption relieving a predominantly internal religious setting does not itself create a secular exemption or establish a plan to suppress charitable ministry. Not offering prescription coverage is outside the defined benefit relationship; the uninsured, unemployed and persons outside an employer plan do not receive the unequal prescription package the law regulates. The specialized-plan exclusion concerns a different product. Federal preemption of self-insured plans is a limit on state power, not a discretionary state indulgence to comparable secular objections. No actual individualized secular-exemption mechanism is established.

Under Agostini's unified actual-function inquiry, Amos permits accommodation of a religious organization's internal functions without requiring identical treatment of every associated legal entity and every employment relationship. Objective examination of organizational purpose, workforce, population served and specified tax status need not determine theological truth. Commack's compulsory official enforcement of Orthodox doctrine is materially different. Larson continues to condemn denominational preferences, including covert ones; its doctrine cannot be avoided merely by calling a classification an exemption. Here the four criteria distinguish a predominantly internal institutional setting from the public-service employment relationship, apply without selecting a denomination, and leave an applicant's religious identity and teaching intact. Locke supports the existence of bounded permissible accommodations, but its ministry-funding rule does not itself decide this insurance case. Boerne forecloses importing RFRA's statewide command.

The strongest objections are substantial: the same religious mission may require service to nonadherents, and a definition favoring inculcation can pressure that mission while resembling Larson's internal-support line. The fourth tax-status criterion also draws a religious institutional line. Path A answers the whole architecture rather than only the first criterion: the State is identifying the setting in which it will relieve its own insurance rule, not adjudicating whether the charitable mission is authentic religion. Catholic Charities remains religious, but its employee-benefit transaction involving a mixed workforce remains regulated. The exemption's relief for a more internal setting does not require an exemption that transfers the cost of the employer's belief to these employees. A material claim that officials actually judge religious truth would present different conduct, but no such finding is made from the statutory language alone.

On Path A, affirm denial of preliminary relief on the presented federal claims and return the state proceedings for their ordinary continuation. The federal constitutional objections supply no basis for an exemption or injunction on this record. The state court retains its remaining state-law proceedings; the federal decision does not convert a preliminary order into a final adjudication of every claim. The coverage provision, all four exemption criteria and their preserved statutory conditions remain operative for this case.

**Path B — reject the religious classification and remand the provisional remedy.** Larson and the actual-function prohibitions in Agostini and Commack can be read to forbid making the benefit of religious accommodation depend on whether a religious organization inculcates, predominantly employs coadherents, predominantly serves coadherents and falls within the specified inward institutional category. The objection is not that a statute mentions religion. It is that all four conditions together select the kinds of religious mission receiving protection and penalize a contrary mission of public charity. Equal regulation with secular employers does not answer unequal treatment among religious organizations; Larson's disfavored organizations also remained subject to an otherwise general law. The prior Locke dissent's equality ground makes this distinction material for its three joiners.

The strongest defense is that the State is drawing a legitimate institutional line to protect mixed-faith employees and need not grant every permissible accommodation. The Path B response recognizes that employee equality is a weighty and potentially compelling interest, but requires justification of this religious selection itself. Neither the status of a mission as inculcating nor the religion of those it serves establishes that the employer's burden may be disregarded. The fourth criterion cannot independently save the denial when used as part of that same exclusive religious-organization definition; this conclusion concerns its incorporation into this accommodation, not the validity of the federal tax-return rule. The State's claim that any broader exemption necessarily sacrifices the entire legislative objective is too general to establish the fit of these conditions. The previously considered public-financing alternative is an argument for examination, not an established costless substitute or a finding that Catholic Charities can self-insure.

Path B would vacate the preliminary-relief judgment and remand with the classification held impermissible at this scope. The state court must decide the severable remedy under state law: whether a constitutionally evenhanded accommodation can be extended or whether the invalid exemption architecture must be severed while a neutral coverage rule remains. It must reconsider interim protection under ordinary preliminary-injunction principles while that work is done, addressing the applicant's religious injury, employee consequences and feasible lawful implementation. It may not deny relief by relying on any of the four invalidated criteria or deem the applicant automatically eligible after striking only one. This path supplies no permanent universal religious exemption and no invented insurance or public-benefit scheme. No Justice on this path conditions a vote on a predetermined state-law severability answer unsupported by the record.

**O'Connor's independent Path A ground.** Her substantial-burden position requires a separate scrutiny conclusion. Assuming the sincerely claimed burden, she would sustain this application because protecting employees' equal prescription benefits is compelling and the requirement directly addresses the identified coverage disparity. The record reports the disproportionate out-of-pocket costs and the missing prescription coverage. An exemption here would place those costs on employees whose beliefs need not match the employer's. A wage supplement is not equivalent insured access; the State is not required on this showing to replace an employer's ordinary benefit obligation with a new publicly financed program. The law preserves an internal religious setting, reaches the particular benefit offered, and preserves medically necessary and noncontraceptive uses. The strongest objection is that a narrower alternative and a broader exemption might achieve the objective; she finds the proposed alternatives insufficiently equivalent for this existing mixed-faith employee-benefit relationship. This is a sufficient applied scrutiny ground, not a holding that every insurance mandate or every asserted equality interest passes scrutiny.

- **Free Exercise, Smith/Lukumi sufficient rationale:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support the complete Path A rule: the stated coverage requirement and objectively limited accommodation are neutral and generally applicable as applied to this insured employment relationship, and the claimed sincere burden does not itself establish an exemption.
