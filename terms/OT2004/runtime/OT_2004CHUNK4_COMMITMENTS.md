## Dowhal v. SmithKline Beecham Consumer Healthcare — February 23, 2005

### Material premises and components

The reviewed judgment rejects the state warning claim on conflict-preemption grounds. The operative California duty is clear and reasonable communication of reproductive toxicity, not compulsory use of one safe-harbor sentence. Labeling, point-of-sale notice, and advertising are distinct compliance routes. Section 379r(d)(2) exempts a qualifying pre-September 1, 1997 initiative from that section; the separate product-liability provision in subsection (e) and identical-requirement enforcement provision in subsection (f) are not substituted for it. Nonidentity or national uniformity alone cannot defeat the preserved initiative.

The August 17, 2001 citizen-petition response is the definitive action established by the published judicial recitation. Earlier communications required continued approved warnings while review remained pending; they are not retroactively treated as definitive determinations about Dowhal's proposal. The agency rejected the prior heart-rate formulation and the Habitrol formulation, required the comparative-risk communication, and allowed another supported proposal. That route for reconsideration does not authorize present disregard of an effective prohibition. The record supplies neither a new manufacturer dataset nor a final agency response to every conceivable combined warning.

The FDA's quoted reasons address uncertainty, comparative risk, and the misleading consumer implication of the proposed communication. They do not find nicotine harmless or establish categorical safety during pregnancy. The state court's inference that every complying warning conflicts is an inference to review, not an express sentence in the agency response. The off-label-notice argument involves obstruction of the particular federal method, not automatic extension of labeling jurisdiction to every public statement.

The separable legal components are: (1) whether the initiative saving provision forecloses an independent actual-conflict inquiry; (2) whether statutory misleadingness may include a literally accurate statement whose contextual implication is misleading, and whether the definitive labeling action is a legally material federal predicate; and (3) whether this record establishes conflict across the asserted state claim's available compliance routes. The formal judgment turns on component 3. No independent First Amendment claim is presented.

### Competing lawful paths

**Affirm the preemption judgment on the identified warning claim.** Geier Parts I–II preserve independently demonstrated actual conflict despite saving language, while its application recognizes a supported federal regulatory method. Here the agency did more than permit a product: its definitive action rejected the reproductive-harm warning in the course of regulating these products and required a different communication to prevent a misleading inference about smoking and replacement therapy. On this view, the mandatory state message defeats that specific method whether on a label or on a point-of-sale notice, and the ability to seek reconsideration with supporting data does not remove the present conflict. The strongest objection is that the state duty allows explanatory wording and channels beyond the label, so rejection of particular formulations does not prove incompatibility with every complying communication. An affirming commitment must adopt the substantive-incompatibility inference and explain its health basis; uniformity cannot supply it.

**Vacate the blanket preemption judgment and remand for the actual-duty comparison.** The operative federal action can bar the formulations it actually rejects, but the broader state duty permits a qualifying explanation addressing nicotine, product uncertainty, professional consultation, and relative risk. No such analytical possibility is treated as a proposal a party submitted or as a warning judicially approved. The point is that the defendants' summary-judgment theory must establish conflict at the scope of the obligation displaced. The strongest objection is that the FDA has already rejected the relevant substantive message and that a state court cannot evade the binding decision by adding words or changing the medium. The remanding commitments answer that objection by preserving the definitive decision and requiring genuine compatibility, rather than treating agency approval as irrelevant or authorizing the rejected warning. The permitted future proceedings are ordinary state adjudication of the actual claim and available forms of compliance, not a mandatory new agency proceeding or an evidentiary search detached from the pleaded duty.

Both paths reject the absolute-saving theory and the claim that literal accuracy alone defeats statutory misleadingness. A remand based on treating every FDA letter as legally ineffective would ignore this definitive approval-related action; no Justice adopts that categorical proposition. Neither path adjudicates a new constitutional speech claim.

## Johnson v. California, No. 03-636 — February 23, 2005

### Components, current law, and supported alternatives

The relevant period is roughly sixty days of reception and classification, repeated on transfer, rather than an established universal maximum. Officials describe confined double cells, limited visibility, gang organization, incomplete information, and violence risks; the policy also reaches transferees with prior institutional histories. Other integrated settings are relevant but are not factual twins. The record does not convert the United States' advocacy about alternative classification into adjudicated findings.

The standard-selection component is separable from application, individual responsibility and conduct-date immunity, and the prerequisites and scope of prospective relief. The Ninth Circuit's Turner-only judgment cannot be sustained merely by calling an intentional classification protective. The governing Adarand and Oneonta rule requires government to establish an important lawful objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship, and justification of the actual coverage and burdens, including its response to concrete practicable alternatives offering comparable effectiveness with materially less racial disadvantage. No universal least-restrictive-means or commissioned-study requirement follows. Lee v. Washington's treatment of racial segregation and genuine custodial circumstances fits that inquiry. Overton's Turner framework for compatible family association does not itself decide the standard governing purposeful racial classification.

**Correct the standard and remand.** Vacate the Ninth Circuit's judgment and require application of the common justification rule to reception and transfer assignments on the supported record, with further proceedings as ordinary rules require. The strongest objection is operational: reception staff must prevent immediate violence before completing individualized assessment, and a demanding alternatives inquiry may second-guess them after the fact. The answer is legal, not a contrary finding of safety: the common standard admits reliable experience, reasonable predictions, real uncertainty, and genuinely urgent precautions, but makes government connect them to the actual restriction. Courts must evaluate rather than disregard that evidence. The prior categorical Turner analysis did not perform that task.

**Affirm through prison-specific deference.** Turner and the particular reception risks provide a serious candidate rationale, especially for temporary precautionary assignments. Its strongest defect is that it replaces the specific, binding racial rule with a general operational rule and shifts practical justification away from government. Lee and the unanimous Oneonta classification rule leave no support for exemption based solely on protective motive or institutional difficulty. A limited safety accommodation can operate within the common standard; it does not require a different constitutional tier. None of the eight commitments adopts this affirmance path on the supplied record.

**Invalidate the entire policy now under the common standard.** Near universality, repeated transfers, existing inmate information, and possible individualized alternatives strongly support a merits challenge. The strongest objection is that the appellate judgment applied the wrong burden and did not adjudicate the fit of the whole policy under the governing rule. The supported custodial differences and disputed adequacy of alternatives make a present universal application finding unwarranted. The resulting remand is factual and remedial application of a resolved standard, not a reservation of the standard itself.

### Supported remand and immunity scope

All eight provisional commitments support vacatur of the Ninth Circuit judgment and remand under the common racial-classification standard. This is an Associate alignment only. The appellate court must determine the supported objective, the relationship of actual reception and transfer categories and durations to the identified threats, burdens, and responses to concrete practicable alternatives. Any additional factual proceedings must serve genuinely material issues unresolved by the existing record, not ritual repetition of evidence.

Personal damages require identification of each former director's own acts, causation, and the law at the relevant conduct dates under Harlow and Anderson. Oneonta and today's standard-selection disposition supply no retrospective notice for earlier acts merely because they now bind the merits inquiry. A genuine obvious application may supply notice without a factual twin. The Ninth Circuit did not decide the ultimate defense, and the supplied record does not support a conclusive all-defendant resolution here.

Prospective adjudication requires the proper responsible official and Johnson's actual ongoing exposure. Any decree must satisfy §3626: correction of the proved federal violation through narrow, necessary, least intrusive relief with the required findings and substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice effects. The statute's additional state-law conditions apply if the decree requires an official to exceed state authority. The remand does not establish those predicates merely by rejecting Turner as the governing racial standard.

## Cherokee Nation of Oklahoma v. Leavitt — March 1, 2005

### Components, legal conditions and competing paths

No. 02-1472 concerns Cherokee's FY1997 claims and Shoshone-Paiute's FY1996–1997 claims; No. 03-853 concerns Cherokee's FY1994–1996 claims. Both tribes have initial or expanded-program claims, and Cherokee also has ongoing-program claims. The fresh neutral supplement, the safe federal submission and the quoted transaction-effective text supply these distinctions. The governmental submission's characterization of legally available funds remains advocacy; its descriptions of the actual appropriations, agreements and lower rulings retain their identified source status.

**Path A — enforce the actual funding undertakings.** The statute and incorporated agreements promise the reasonable, allowable support costs of the contracted programs, subject to appropriations availability and their real terms. An unrestricted lump sum legally usable for that contract satisfies the availability condition; administrative distribution among competing demands cannot by itself convert authorized contract funds into legally unavailable funds. This is contract responsibility, not an equitable promise inferred from tribal status. Hercules supplies the actual-authorized-duty requirement, and Winstar distinguishes a financial obligation from a restriction on sovereign legislative authority. Lincoln permits administrative allocation where no enforceable boundary exists; a binding contract is an additional legal boundary.

The strongest objection combines the statutory no-reduction clause, the annual initial/expanded-program earmark, the agreement's congressional-adjustment language and §314. Each must receive its own answer:

1. Then-§450j-1(b)'s appropriations qualification remains real. The relevant FY1994–1997 lump sums were legally available for these undertakings; administrative support-cost budgets and committee-report expectations did not originally enact a total support-cost ceiling. Availability is a legal limitation on the undertaking, not whatever allocation the agency later elects to call available. A genuinely binding statutory limitation would control.
2. The separate clause that the Secretary is not required to reduce funding for programs, projects or activities serving another tribe protects against a demand to redistribute other tribal program funding. It does not require reductions, and it does not extinguish the United States' responsibility for an authorized promise made against legally sufficient appropriations. The tribes seek adjudication of the contract obligation; recognition of that obligation does not direct the Secretary to raid another tribe's program. A contractor need not monitor the agency's competing allocations to preserve its promise. The contrary reading would let the agency erase contract responsibility through the very allocations Lincoln leaves to its administrative judgment.
3. The $7.5 million provision for initial or expanded contracts makes that amount remain available until expended for the Indian Self-Determination Fund's transitional costs. It guarantees a designated fund and continued availability; it does not state that no other otherwise available annual money may satisfy the authorized support-cost undertaking. Read with the larger authorized lump sum, the text is not an exclusive ceiling. This answer applies to the initial/expanded claims independently of the committee-report answer for ongoing claims. The explicit overall not-to-exceed limitation beginning in FY1998 is not backdated to these earlier contracts.
4. Section 314 of Pub. L. 105-277, 112 Stat. 2681-288, limits the amounts remaining available from the identified FY1994–1998 appropriations and committee-report allocations. It does not expressly rescind accrued contractual obligations or the contract remedy. Its retrospective identification of fiscal-year accounts has work to do without converting a spending limit into destruction of already accrued claims. The agreement's adjustment language for congressional action affecting availability is read with this actual operation; it does not independently enlarge §314 into cancellation of accrued liability. This construction avoids attributing repudiation of a genuine governmental obligation to an accounting restriction without deciding a freestanding constitutional claim.

Under Path A, reverse the Tenth Circuit in No. 02-1472 and remand for contract adjudication consistent with those resolved defenses; affirm the Federal Circuit's favorable contract-liability disposition in No. 03-853 and return through the ordinary mandate for the genuinely remaining computation and implementation. Each claimant must establish the actual annual amount promised and still unpaid, reasonable and allowable costs, nonduplication with transferred program or administrative funding, causation and compensable loss under its agreement. No mandatory evidentiary trial is created: remaining matters follow ordinary contract procedure. The initial/expanded and ongoing categories and fiscal years remain distinct. The Court does not issue an agency-wide allocation order. That limitation is an answer to the Government's demanded no-reduction construction, not an excuse for incomplete payment-law resolution.

**Path B — treat the statutory and contractual qualifiers as aggregate program limits.** The Secretary's duty to serve noncontracting tribes, the express no-reduction clause and the $7.5 million designation could be read together as conditioning each promise on funds left within the program allocation after other statutory functions. On that reading, the authorized agreements incorporate rather than assume aggregate funding risk; §314 confirms the cap retrospectively. It would support affirming No. 02-1472 and reversing No. 03-853 for judgment on the relevant shortfall defenses, or a bounded remand if the legal limit's application to an annual claim remained unproved. It would preserve payment for any proven amount within the lawful allocation and would not itself find every cost disallowed.

The strongest objection is textual and contractual: the appropriation does not say that the $7.5 million is the sole source; the no-reduction language is permission not to reallocate, not a cancellation command; and committee reports initially were not enacted caps. Winstar requires enforcing actual conditions, but supplies no reason to add an aggregate no-liability condition to the particular promise. Section 314 speaks to available appropriation amounts rather than accrued claims. All eight Associates find those objections decisive on these supported annual terms. They reject Path B at this scope without denying Congress's capacity to enact an actual cap or condition.

## Petro-Hunt, L.L.C. v. United States — March 1, 2005

### Record, reach and two competing paths

This merits review is properly before the Court under the supplied review premise. The revised Fifth Circuit opinion, read in full, establishes 96 distinct servitudes arising from six mineral conveyances and eleven federal acquisitions. Nebo Oil's operative decree concerns the servitude on the approximately 800 acres described in its complaint. Its broader findings do not enlarge that decree to the other 95 servitudes. The United States accepts the earlier decree's continuing force for its adjudicated servitude. **Path A — affirm the rejection of preclusion.** Federal common law determines this federal-question judgment's effect. Separate servitudes with different conveyances and use histories are different claims; one dispute over attempted drilling did not require adjudication of title to every other property held by the same parties. Issue preclusion remains separately available for an identical, actually litigated and necessary issue, but Nebo Oil assumed the applicability of Louisiana law and decided state-law retroactivity and constitutional objections. The decree's wording controls its property scope. The district court must resolve the second summary-judgment motion that it treated as moot, and determine title from the resulting histories. The fee cross-appeal remains without a present prevailing-party predicate; any later fee claim follows the eventual judgment.

## Roper v. Simmons — March 1, 2005

### Readiness, law, and objective evidence

Fresh neutral validation was saved and read before this assessment. The federal constitutional question is whether death is permissible for an offense committed before eighteen. Simmons was seventeen at the crime; his planning and the murder's severity are supported record features, not a disputed claim of innocence. The Missouri state-postconviction judgment substituted life imprisonment without release eligibility. Its federal constitutional basis is reviewable; AEDPA is not this proceeding's gateway.

Atkins's seven-Justice rule applies to established intellectual disability and does not already settle age. Its full public explanation uses domestic enactments and practice, culpability, penal purposes, and the insufficiency of individual mitigation to solve the demonstrated categorical problem. Stanford's age-specific eligibility rule remains until this Court expressly changes it. Thompson's narrower under-sixteen holding and its separate rationales must not be described as an existing under-eighteen rule.

The fresh validated extraction supplies these March 1 aggregates: thirty States prohibit juvenile death eligibility, comprising twelve without capital punishment and eighteen retaining it but excluding juvenile offenses through enactment or judicial interpretation; six States have executed juvenile offenders since Stanford; only Oklahoma, Texas, and Virginia have done so in the preceding ten years; five States abandoned juvenile eligibility since Stanford, four by enactment and one by judicial decision. These are separate categories. The record does not establish thirty age-specific enactments, five legislative changes, a full state-by-state table, a complete sentencing dataset, or numerical international findings. None is inferred here. Rarity is not automatically rejection; the legal inference is contested.

### Competing paths and controlling propositions each would adopt

**Express categorical change and affirmance.** The death penalty is unavailable for anyone younger than eighteen when the crime was committed. The domestic exclusions and concentrated, rare use, considered with the general features of adolescent culpability recognized in Thompson and the supported youth arguments, justify the category. Immaturity, susceptibility to outside pressures, and a still-developing character weaken retribution and deterrence and make a capital sentencer's conclusion of irredeemable adult culpability unreliable for the class. These are legal judgments about the established category; no unverified neuroscience result or individualized psychiatric diagnosis is asserted. Atkins shows why individual mitigation need not cure that class-level problem. The strongest objection is the absence of an age-specific prohibition in a majority of capital-punishment States and the capacity of some older adolescents to plan a grave offense. The answer is not that planning is irrelevant; it is that culpability sufficient for criminal responsibility and severe confinement does not establish the adult culpability needed for this ultimate punishment, and domestic evidence is not a mechanical denominator test. This path expressly displaces Stanford insofar as it permits execution for crimes at sixteen or seventeen and affirms the existing noncapital judgment.

**Retain Stanford and reverse the federal categorical basis.** The eighteen age-excluding capital States and limited changes over fifteen years do not establish the necessary national rejection; the twelve general-abolition States made a broader decision. Rare executions can also reflect careful individualized selection. Youth's usual mitigation does not establish that every sixteen- or seventeen-year-old lacks the culpability needed for death, and the present record's deliberate conduct strengthens the challenge to a categorical conclusion. The strongest objection is that the same domestic-evidence method and individual-selection concern supported Atkins and that youth's transience undermines a reliable irreversible judgment. The answer differs by Justice: O'Connor accepts that method but finds this particular class and evidence materially different; Scalia and Thomas preserve their objections to the majority method and independently find its extension unsupported. Reversal would return the state case for proceedings under the surviving eligibility rule; it is not an order of execution or a finding that every independent sentencing condition is satisfied.

A lower court's obligation to follow controlling precedent does not disable this Court from reaching the granted reconsideration question. Neither side needs to approve a general lower-court power to anticipate overruling. No preserved independent mitigation defect supplies a narrower substitute for answering the categorical question. International practice supplies no necessary ground for the commitments here; no unsupplied international total is adopted.

## Tenet v. Doe — March 2, 2005

### Components and adversarial alternatives

The Court reviews the certified orders allowing the surviving agreement-dependent estoppel and constitutional claims to proceed. The Does seek interim monthly support, a declaration prescribing internal review, and mandamus directing support and regulations. Their alleged recruitment, services, continuing-support promise, defection and resulting relationship remain allegations; the Government neither confirms nor denies them. The dismissed equal-protection count was not revived by the accepted interlocutory appeal. No disposition here reopens that branch.

**Path A — apply Totten at the outset.** Totten distinguishes the authority to make and honor a secret-service undertaking from its judicial enforceability. Its subject-matter bar applies when the asserted right and requested relief necessarily depend on proving the secret espionage relationship. Estoppel, a property entitlement and a demand for fair procedures do not supply an independent source when each depends on the same alleged promise. A court could not prescribe the requested process without first recognizing the asserted relationship and an entitlement arising from it. Pseudonyms and restrained initial allegations do not alter what must eventually be established. Reynolds preserves Totten's distinct pleading-stage application while governing evidence in a suit otherwise maintainable. The Director's personal invocation required for the evidentiary privilege is therefore not a prerequisite to this categorical bar.

The strongest counterargument is the lower court's precise procedural distinction: an internal administrative hearing may occur confidentially and judicial relief might require only that hearing, not public disclosure or a damages trial. Webster also preserves colorable constitutional claims arising from ordinary Agency employment decisions. The response is that the proposed judicial command still requires a cognizable entitlement to review of the alleged secret undertaking. Webster's independent constitutional challenge to a statutory employment decision does not silently eliminate Totten's preexisting limit on judicial enforcement of a secret bargain. The Does identify no independent public statutory entitlement establishing the benefits or review duty they seek. Their constitutional characterization is consequential to the promise, not independent of it.

Under Path A, reverse the appellate judgment and remand with instructions to dismiss the surviving claims founded on the alleged secret agreement. The district court implements that dismissal instead of undertaking the proposed Reynolds inquiry or ordering support, benefits procedures or regulations on those claims. The already denied preliminary injunction and dismissed equal-protection branch remain undisturbed. The disposition makes no finding confirming the relationship and creates no judicially enforceable administrative substitute.

**Path B — allow the process claims and require a proper Reynolds invocation.** A narrower reading of Totten as a contractual enforcement rule could distinguish a request for fair internal review and allow it to proceed, initially using pseudonyms and protective controls. This path would affirm the refusal to dismiss, remand for ordinary jurisdiction and waiver examination and identification of a cognizable entitlement, and require the Director's actual personal consideration and formal privilege claim before adjudicating a request to withhold evidence. Judicial assessment would protect secrecy without assuming that in-camera inspection must always occur. It would not award interim support or finally establish liability merely because the complaint survives.

The strongest objection is that this proposed distinction changes the label and forum of requested performance without changing the secret bargain that must establish entitlement. Totten's rationale expressly includes the existence of the relationship; Reynolds itself distinguishes dismissal on the pleadings from evidentiary privilege. No independent law supplies the missing entitlement here. Thus the procedural path cannot avoid the categorical rule on these pleaded claims. All eight Associates select Path A at this bounded scope.
