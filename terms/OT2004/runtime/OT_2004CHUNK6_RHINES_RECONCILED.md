**Reconciliation correction:** The initial proposal treated a modest cause standard as enough to secure Souter's full join. His historical objection concerns the existence and cost of the inquiry, including difficult exhaustion classifications, rather than only its severity. No changed law, record or procedural premise answers that objection. The proposal is therefore corrected here to a partial join; it is not described as a historical departure or an invented circulation event. Initial commitments are preserved.

**Separate position to retain:** Souter, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, would omit good cause as an initial prerequisite. He accepts the power to retain the timely petition, the plainly-meritless and anti-abuse limits, reasonable deadlines, and the judgment; he would wait for evidence of intentional delay rather than require the prisoner to explain every failure to exhaust before obtaining a stay. The rationale is practical: exhaustion can be difficult even for courts, most prisoners lack legal training, and another threshold dispute may consume time without improving protection against abuse. Ginsburg and Breyer also join the lead opinion and its governing cause condition; the topology must state both joins. Authorship of this separate position follows the individual position, not an imported lead assignment. The lead opinion's express flexible formulation supplies Stevens's accepted qualification without a repetitive Stevens concurrence.

## Rule, precedent and remand limits

The initial common explanation remains adopted except for the corrections above. A stay preserves an existing timely action and does not exclude federal pendency under section 2244(d)(2), create a new filing period or revive a terminated action. Duncan's rule remains intact. Rose's exhaustion requirement remains operative, administered with bounded stay power after AEDPA. Section 2254(b)(2) permits denial on the merits despite nonexhaustion and does not compel it. Granting relief still requires exhaustion, absence of an available state corrective process, ineffectiveness of that process to protect the applicant's rights, or the State counsel's express waiver, as applicable under the statutory provisions. Pliler's actual notice and equitable-remedy rules retain their triggers and are not a substitute for the stay standard.

The seven-Associate lead rationale answers the broader position by preserving an incentive to exhaust before federal filing and permitting individualized assessment, while the flexible cause standard prevents a rigid second procedural-default obstacle. It answers the warden by excluding plainly meritless claims and abuse and requiring enforceable time limits. The remand must apply the actual adopted standard rather than reinstate the district court stay automatically. Cause, potential merit and delay on this record remain application work, not open doctrinal questions or presumed facts.
