# OT2004 chunk 9 — scoped approved supplements

## 77. Spector v. Norwegian Cruise Line Ltd.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22; current review request, with revisions authorized in advance.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling decisions and the stated record and jurisdictional limits.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording changes consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning and scope; no alternative disposition authorized.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit's categorical exclusion and remand.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require claim-by-claim adjudication under Title III and the particular maritime limitation. Any established discriminatory charge or policy must be stopped or otherwise remedied through relief authorized by the applicable cause of action; structural relief requires findings supporting the statutory duty.
* **Remedial Reach:** The present judgment reinstates these passengers' claims. If a challenged policy is proved unlawful and prospective relief is properly before the court, relief reaches that policy and those it governs unless Norwegian establishes that a narrower remedy fully cures the violation.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must identify each challenged practice, determine its statutory status and set an enforceable compliance measure for any proved violation. This remand does not itself award private damages under a provision authorizing only injunctive relief or set unsupported construction deadlines.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Domestic disability obligations aboard a foreign-flag cruise ship

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach statutory coverage because the blanket exclusion terminated claims that may impose no internal maritime interference. Reserve fact-dependent structural requirements for the lower court.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether foreign registry alone removes a cruise operator's domestic passenger accommodations from Title III.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve the unanswered statutory application by applying the ADA's text and retaining a specific maritime limitation for genuinely internal operations.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: coverage must reach the entire domestic commercial relationship, not just an isolated surcharge. A broader ruling compelling unspecified ship reconstruction lacks a record; a narrower policy-only answer would leave the granted coverage question artificially fragmented.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A foreign-flag cruise ship operating public accommodations in United States waters is subject to Title III, but a particular claimed obligation cannot be imposed without the congressional clarity required when it would regulate the ship's internal affairs. Foreign registry alone supplies no defense to discriminatory passenger charges or services. A claimant must establish the applicable ADA duty and violation; the operator must establish any statutory defense it invokes on the relevant evidence.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Domestic passenger accommodations and transportation activities covered by Title III, including nondiscrimination and otherwise required accessibility measures. The holding does not establish universal structural specifications for all foreign vessels.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Apply each claimed duty separately: passenger charges and services ordinarily concern domestic commerce; structural alterations require the statutory feasibility and safety assessment and any applicable maritime limitation.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The lower court's rule equates a ship's flag with the nature of every transaction aboard it. That prevents even removal of discriminatory charges without showing any interference with maritime governance. The internal labor concerns in Benz and McCulloch do not establish that every service sold to passengers belongs to the same category. The enacted defenses allow real safety and feasibility questions to be resolved on proof rather than presumed from nationality.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Benz v. Compania Naviera Hidalgo and McCulloch v. Sociedad Nacional de Marineros are distinguished as internal-affairs decisions, not overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The need for each structural modification, any particular conflict with international obligations, and the effect of absent design regulations require a preserved claim and developed record.

## 78. United States v. Kincade

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22; current review request, with revisions authorized in advance.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling decisions and the stated record and jurisdictional limits.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording changes consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning and scope; no alternative disposition authorized.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment insofar as it sustains the challenged DNA condition and a sanction based solely on refusal to comply with it; remand for correction of the release judgment.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The district court must identify and remove the invalid collection requirement and the consequence attributable solely to its refusal. If compelled material or a database profile was actually obtained under that demand, it must address deletion and return or destruction sufficient to cure that collection, subject to any independently established lawful basis.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment directly covers Kincade's release condition and sanction. No certified class or live claim for a programwide injunction is before the Court.
* **Practical Relief:** The supervising authority must cease enforcing the invalid condition against Kincade. The district court must correct the operative judgment and verify any necessary record or sample correction; remaining valid supervised-release obligations continue under their own authority.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Suspicionless DNA collection as a supervised-release condition

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the search requirement because refusal produced an enforceable supervised-release consequence. Do not adjudicate speculative unauthorized genetic uses.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this ordinary crime-investigation database search is constitutionally authorized without individualized suspicion.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing limits on supervision searches and special needs; decline to create a categorical suspicionless DNA exception from diminished privacy alone.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the rule addresses the statutory program's actual investigative collection, including its recurring release condition. A broader prohibition on all government DNA use would exceed the case; a narrower blood-draw-only rule would ignore the stated database purpose that justifies the demand.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A supervised-release condition does not authorize suspicionless biological sampling for a general criminal-investigation database merely because the subject has a qualifying conviction. The government must establish individualized suspicion plus lawful search authority, valid consent, or a constitutionally sufficient exception whose purpose and scope fit the search; it has established none for this demand.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Compulsory collection for the challenged ordinary investigative program during supervised release. Collection connected to identification in a different custodial setting or to a particular investigation remains subject to the applicable existing rules.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A release condition and diminished privacy do not alone authorize investigative collection. Particularized investigations remain governed by the search authority, consent or exception actually invoked.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Knights deliberately made the condition and particularized suspicion work together. Treating a conviction as authorization for every useful future search would discard that limitation. Edmond and Ferguson require examination of what the program actually does; matching biological traces to solve ordinary crimes does not become a separate administrative need by being described as identification. A narrow profile reduces one dimension of intrusion but does not supply the missing authority to take and retain it.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States v. Knights is applied without converting its reserved suspicionless question into permission. City of Indianapolis v. Edmond and Ferguson v. City of Charleston are applied to the actual program purpose. No governing controlling precedent is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Arrestee booking, prisoners not yet released, post-sentence retention and specific investigative collection are outside the presented record.
