## 1. Leocal v. Ashcroft

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22, current revision request.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning, scope and remedy.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2004, assessed for 2004-11-09; subject to intervening controlling decisions.
* **Conditions:** Subject to the stated record limits and any intervening controlling decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment sustaining the challenged aggravated-felony classification and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the agency and reviewing court to remove this §16 classification as a basis for the removal order and adjudicate any independently preserved basis under its own law. The criminal convictions remain valid.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment supplies corrective immigration relief to Leocal. The statutory construction governs other cases through ordinary precedent, without a class injunction or an order deciding other individuals' removability.
* **Practical Relief:** On mandate, the reviewing court must return the matter with a binding instruction that these DUI convictions cannot support this aggravated-felony ground. The agency must enter a conforming disposition and address the concrete means of correcting continuing consequences; a further removal ground requires its own process and proof.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The force requirement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The disputed offense classification is the predicate for both removability and the asserted review restriction, so it must be decided.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Florida offense qualifies under either part of §16.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing interpretation and review principles; adopt the presently unresolved construction of §16.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A categorical statutory rule is needed because removal turns on an offense definition. A broader rule covering all recklessness would exceed the presented elements. A narrower accident-specific result would improperly replace categorical analysis with conduct findings.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An offense that permits conviction for accidental or negligent injury without the employment of force against another person or another’s property is not a crime of violence under either subsection of 18 U.S.C. §16. The Government must establish a qualifying conviction through its legal elements and the applicable conviction record, not infer the missing requirement from injury alone. Section 16(b)'s risk concerns force use in committing the offense, not every risk of physical injury. Florida's offense therefore does not establish the charged aggravated felony.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Offenses whose minimum elements permit accidental or negligent injury; intentional force offenses remain subject to the actual statutory definition.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Bailey v. United States gives the statutory word use operative significance, while Taylor v. United States prevents the accident record from adding an element. The broader risk clause retains the same force-use concept, so the Government's causation argument would erase a limitation Congress repeated. Calcano-Martinez does not bar resolving an expressly disputed criminal predicate.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Bailey v. United States: apply its contextual active-use reasoning without extending its firearm holding as an automatic definition. Taylor v. United States: apply categorical analysis. Calcano-Martinez: distinguish conceded predicates.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A statute requiring reckless force use is outside the offense presented; other removal grounds lack an adjudicated record.

## 2. Smith v. Texas

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22, current revision request.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning, scope and remedy.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2004, assessed for 2004-11-15; subject to intervening controlling decisions.
* **Conditions:** Subject to the stated record limits and any intervening controlling decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the state court's rejection of the mitigation claim on its relevance and instructional grounds and remand for any genuinely remaining effect and procedural questions.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Hold that the heightened screen is impermissible and that this contradictory mechanism fails the effective-mitigation requirement. Do not order a new guilt trial. If the State cannot establish harmlessness under the governing standard, the state court must provide a lawful capital resentencing or noncapital sentence.
* **Remedial Reach:** Smith's penalty judgment. The record identifies no class or separate policy claim authorizing relief for other prisoners.
* **Practical Relief:** The state court must decide the complete-record effect question under the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden, without treating special-issue answers as an unconflicted mitigation verdict. If relief is required, it must fix a reasonable period for sentencing correction and specify the lawful noncapital consequence if that correction is not provided.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Effective consideration of supported mitigation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The state court reached the mitigation issue; the present charge and evidence permit resolution of the constitutional defect without deciding categorical disability.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the relevance screen and nullification mechanism adequately protect mitigation.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing simulated law unchanged.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The prior decisions already govern this recurring instruction defect. A broader structural-error or disability holding would answer absent questions. A narrower ruling on relevance alone would leave the identified verdict contradiction unresolved.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A capital jury must have a lawful, coherent means to give supported culpability mitigation effect even when it does not negate the factual special issues. The defendant need not prove a severe permanent handicap or causal connection to the crime to obtain that consideration. Smith's instruction required the same negative answer to express mitigation and a factual doubt that the juror might not hold, creating the prohibited reasonable likelihood of restriction.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Supported mitigation outside the factual issues; no prescribed weight or categorical eligibility rule follows.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Tennard v. Dretke resolves the relevance objection. Penry v. Johnson identifies why a direction to consider mitigation cannot cure a contradictory verdict certification. Graham v. Collins and Johnson v. Texas protect the independent culpability judgment, rather than making future-danger evidence the only relevant mitigation. The remand preserves the separate effect inquiry required by the received law.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Tennard v. Dretke and Penry v. Johnson: reaffirm and apply. No replacement test is proposed.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Missing record determination: ultimate effect on this complete penalty proceeding.

## 3. United States v. $100,348.00 in U.S. Currency

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22, current revision request.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning, scope and remedy.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2004, assessed for 2004-11-15; subject to intervening controlling decisions.
* **Conditions:** Subject to the stated record limits and any intervening controlling decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the bailee's entitlement to challenge the full forfeiture and affirm the $10,000 reduced forfeiture; reject the Government's demand for $100,348.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Maintain the reduced judgment and return the $90,348 balance to the legally entitled claimant through the existing forfeiture proceeding. No new proportionality hearing is required merely to choose between hypothetical fine brackets where neither changes the constitutional result. Independent claims outside the questions presented are not reopened.
* **Remedial Reach:** The identified fund and claimant's protected interest. This creates no general injunction against forfeiture proceedings and does not revive the owner's untimely independent claim.
* **Practical Relief:** The Government may retain the adjudicated $10,000 and must return the nonforfeitable $90,348 under the final judgment to the claimant legally entitled to receive it. The district court may enforce that return and resolve ministerial implementation; it may not retain the balance indefinitely or revive the owner's untimely independent claim.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Lawful possession

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Mayzel's bailment supplies asserted control and duties over the entrusted money; that is his own claimed injury.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether his constitutional challenge can reach the whole forfeited fund.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend existing first-party injury principles to the identified bailment.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule covers lawful possessors whose own interests extend throughout the res. A broader stranger-standing rule lacks injury. A narrower legal-title requirement would erase the actual protected possessory interest.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A lawful possessor whose property rights or legally enforceable obligations are impaired by forfeiture may challenge the punitive exaction against the whole property to which that interest extends. The claimant must establish that interest through the applicable property law and record; ownership by another does not alone defeat it.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Bailments and comparable actual possessory interests, not unexplained possession or litigation on behalf of a stranger.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Eighth Amendment inquiry concerns punishment imposed through loss of a legally protected interest. Austin v. United States prevents form from concealing punitive operation. The asserted owner's default does not extinguish Mayzel's independently cognizable possession, though it continues to govern the owner's claim.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Austin v. United States: apply the functional fine classification without changing its scope.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 2: Proportionality

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Standing permits the inquiry but proves neither lawful source nor a disproportionate amount.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this full reporting forfeiture can survive the governing constitutional comparison.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the received gross-disproportionality rule without a new factor test.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The offense-sensitive rule reaches materially equivalent forfeitures. A broader immunity for lawful funds would contradict Bajakajian. A narrower statutory-maximum comparison would omit the constitutional inquiry.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A punitive reporting forfeiture is unconstitutional when the claimant proves it grossly disproportionate to the actual offense, assessed on supported culpability, harm, property relationship and authorized punishment. Legislative sanctions deserve weight but do not conclusively validate confiscation of the full fund. The supported lawful source, limited informational harm and absence of proved related illegal activity make $100,348 excessive here; the actual $10,000 exaction is not grossly disproportionate even using the lower plausible fine benchmark. This is a constitutional limit, not a judicial formula selecting the ideal fine.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Punitive reporting exactions, with material distinctions for actual proceeds, separate wrongdoing and culpability.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** United States v. Bajakajian requires a real offense-sensitive comparison and rejects both categorical immunity and automatic approval. United States v. Wagoner County Real Estate requires findings adequate for review, not a new hearing when existing findings suffice. The credited source and limited harm support rejection of the full fund, while the proven reporting conduct and false statements support a meaningful lesser exaction. The higher hypothetical fine is not treated as proved, and the appellate midpoint explanation is not adopted as a constitutional formula. Austin v. United States, United States v. Bajakajian and United States v. Wagoner County Real Estate retain their complementary coverage, comparison and proof rules.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States v. Bajakajian and United States v. Wagoner County Real Estate: reaffirm their actual rules; extend the supported-finding discipline to this currency proceeding.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None. The identified constitutional amount question is resolved; this disposition does not reopen separate forfeiture and fee determinations outside the grant.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the uncapped TILA statutory-damages determination and remand for application of the $1,000 ceiling.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reduce only this statutory component to the amount permitted by the applicable version of §1640; address any consequential fee calculation under its own law. Preserve independently sustained liability and relief.
* **Remedial Reach:** The parties' credit transaction and judgment. No injunction against the dealer's operations or extinguishment of other consumers' claims follows.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must enter a conforming damages judgment on mandate, identify the untouched components and recalculate only those amounts actually affected. It may not treat the statutory cap as a reason to erase independent actual damages or fee entitlement.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Retained statutory cap

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The loan category and awarded amount are established; interpretation alone resolves this component.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the general bracket survives for this personal-property loan.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply ordinary statutory construction to an unresolved provision.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The enacted subparagraph requires a consistent reading across its covered transactions. A broader cap on every remedy would contradict separate provisions. A narrower transaction-only exception would leave identical clause (i) cases without an administrable rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Under the applicable version of §1640(a)(2)(A), the $100-to-$1,000 bracket continues to govern clause (i) transactions except where the statute expressly supplies another bracket. The clause (iii) real-property provision is a specific qualification, not an implied removal of the general ceiling for personal-property loans. Nigh's statutory award therefore cannot exceed $1,000 under this provision.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The version and individual-action category presented, with actual damages, class recovery and fees governed separately.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Lamie v. United States Trustee rejects judicial repair based merely on dissatisfaction with a remedy. Here no repair is needed: the retained reference to the subparagraph and the expressly specific bracket can coexist. Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank reinforces that a remedial policy cannot authorize an amount beyond the enacted entitlement.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Lamie v. United States Trustee: apply text and structure. Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank: apply source-based remedial limits.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## 5. Anderson v. Spear

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22, current revision request.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning, scope and remedy.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2004, assessed for 2004-11-30; subject to intervening controlling decisions.
* **Conditions:** Subject to the stated record limits and any intervening controlling decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm removal of summary judgment for officials on the three challenged restrictions and direct judgment against their unsupported categorical operation, with a bounded remedial remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Declare the unsupported 500-foot speech restriction, personal-loan ceiling and total debt-retirement contribution ban unenforceable at their adjudicated reach. Determine lawful severability without choosing a replacement distance or dollar ceiling.
* **Remedial Reach:** These are generally operative policies challenged through the responsible officials. Relief must reach their unlawful operation for the candidates and speakers governed by them unless the State shows a narrower injunction fully cures the proved violation.
* **Practical Relief:** Before the next application to protected activity, officials must conform written enforcement directions to the decree. The district court must specify prohibited enforcement, preserve separately lawful anti-intimidation and contribution rules, and permit enforcement proceedings if the invalid restrictions continue.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Polling-place speech

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The challenge identifies actual protected distribution on public forums and a 500-foot exclusion, not an abstract demand for unlimited access inside a voting room.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this perimeter and speech coverage are justified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Burson's controlling scrutiny proposition, without converting its fractured judgment into a numerical safe harbor.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. This defined statewide rule presents a recurring boundary problem. A broader ban on every polling-place restriction would disregard voting protection. A narrower speaker-specific exemption would leave the same unjustified rule operating against equivalent speech.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A campaign-specific restriction on ordinary public streets and sidewalks near polls must be necessary and narrowly drawn to protect voting; the State cannot justify a 500-foot exclusion merely by citing the validity of a smaller zone in another record. Kentucky has not supplied a sufficient basis for this broad suppression of write-in instruction and advocacy, so the challenged operation cannot stand.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The challenged public-forum perimeter and nonintimidating political expression; voting-room management and actual obstruction remain distinct.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Burson v. Freeman supplies strict scrutiny through an independently supported proposition, not a general polling-place exception. Mills v. Alabama makes election-day timing an inadequate reason to suppress advocacy. The State's genuine voter-protection interest does not explain why speech across this whole area must be prohibited.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Burson v. Freeman: apply its actual scrutiny rule and preserve its bounded judgment. Mills v. Alabama: apply protection of election-day advocacy.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 2: Campaign loans and debt retirement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The record distinguishes the candidate's initial use of his own resources from later contributors' repayment of debt.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether either categorical financing restriction is justified.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply received expenditure and contribution distinctions to these restrictions.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The statutes regulate recurring transactions. A broader exemption from donor regulation would ignore a real repayment-related corruption risk. A narrower exception for this candidate would leave unsupported prohibitions intact.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may regulate actual third-party contributions used to repay campaign debt under the governing contribution standard, but may not cap a candidate's own campaign financing or categorically prohibit all debt-retirement contributions without the constitutionally required justification. The asserted future repayment concern does not justify the initial $50,000 personal-loan ceiling, and the total post-election prohibition lacks the demonstrated close fit required here.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Personal campaign loans and post-election contributions for actual debt; ordinary donor limits, disclosure and corruption enforcement retain independent force.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Buckley v. Valeo separates spending one's own campaign resources from a donor's transfer. Russell v. Burris and Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC require close fit and meaningful advocacy, while Federal Election Commission v. Beaumont adds no categorical immunity or prohibition. These bounded rules govern different aspects of one transaction sequence; Stone retains their established distinctions and does not replace them with a new cumulative test.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Buckley v. Valeo, Russell v. Burris and Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC: apply and reaffirm.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.
