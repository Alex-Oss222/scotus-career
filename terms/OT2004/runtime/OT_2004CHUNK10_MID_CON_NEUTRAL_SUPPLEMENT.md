# Mid-Con — neutral statutory alternatives supplement

## Scope and source status

This supplement is for clean neutral validation and independent modeling of the June 20, 2005 merits event. It supplies no judicial commitment, proposed coalition, author identity, or resolution. It changes no existing adjudication. The full event-effective section 14504 text already copied into the entering-law slice remains the statutory source.

The original packet supplies the separate section 478.2(2) charge on Michigan-plated trucks operating entirely interstate, identifying information and decals, the section 478.2(1) local-haul charge, the lower court's regulatory-purpose ground, and the absence of an established requirement to pay the former fee to complete federal registration. Those facts remain intact. The following additional public recitations concern positions presented about the relationship between the two state subsections. They are positions, not findings establishing that the combined program is neutral.

The State contends that section 478.2(2) should be considered together with section 478.2(1), which imposes a $100 fee on trucks doing intrastate business in Michigan, so that the combined scheme imposes the same $100 fee on for-hire motor vehicles plated in Michigan without singling them out by interstate status. The carriers dispute that characterization. The record as supplied does not resolve the competing characterization of the combined scheme. The existence and disputed character of this argument are established by authenticated public recitations of Brief for Respondents 44–45, Reply Brief for Petitioners 10–14, and the United States' brief at 24–29. The original briefs themselves have not been examined for this supplement. No disputed premise is treated as established by the recitation.

## Three statutory constructions to test

1. **Defined subject matter.** Read section 14504(b) with the standards defined by subsection (a) and the particular federal-permit, insurance, service-agent and fee obligations in subsection (c). On that construction, the preemption rule addresses those particular registration subjects. A separate program outside those subjects is not brought within the federal system by vehicle information, a decal, or the apparent waiver alone. Actual conditioning of federal registration on an extra payment would remain a different application. This construction supports affirmance on the supplied record without treating a regulatory label as conclusive.

2. **Registration imposed because of interstate status.** Read the first sentence of subsection (b) as identifying registration demanded of an entity specifically because it is an interstate motor carrier, and the second as invalidating obligations beyond the federal standards within that class. Under this construction, a general neutral registration rule that only incidentally applies to interstate carriers remains outside subsection (b). Subsection (c) separately confines the designated registration State's demands for the listed federal-system information and fees, and prevents other participating States from requiring those listed matters of the carrier. The distinct subsection (c) limitations therefore continue to constrain covered demands even when they apply generally. This construction does not make all fees or every form of state registration subject to a universal $10 ceiling.

   Under this construction, the disputed relationship of sections 478.2(1) and (2) matters: the lower court's regulatory-purpose rationale alone does not answer whether the State's program imposes registration because of interstate-carrier status. A lawful remedy is to vacate and remand for consideration of the combined scheme under that legal standard, without declaring the $100 charge invalid or ordering any refund now. That is a statutory characterization remand, not a remand to invent a legal condition connecting the $100 payment to federal registration.

3. **All state carrier registration.** Read subsection (b) to reach every state registration obligation imposed upon an interstate carrier. This reaches further than construction 2 and must address ordinary tax and other neutral registration obligations. Do not treat objections to this broadest reading as sufficient answers to the distinct second construction.

## Competing objections and limits

The subject-matter construction relies on the same registration terminology in both subsection (b) sentences, their express reference to subsection (c)'s standards, subsection (a)'s definition, and the statute's specified system. The competing construction answers that the first sentence supplies an allowed subset while the second prohibits excess obligations, without an express exemption for other subjects. It uses the carrier's interstate status to limit subsection (b), and subsection (c)'s separate commands to protect the federal registration process. The inquiry must address whether that division has textual support or instead creates inconsistent treatment of the same registration obligation.

The former system's narrower scope does not by itself establish the enacted replacement's scope. Conversely, the replacement's general burden-reduction objective does not by itself establish preemption of every unrelated obligation. Yellow's preservation of qualifying reciprocity within the delegated fee system and its refusal to freeze unrelated taxes and licensing policies do not decide which new construction of subsection (b) governs this registration requirement. A State's regulatory purpose alone cannot decide whether an obligation is registration or whether federal law reaches it.

These arguments are analytical alternatives for assessment under the supplied law. The legal eligibility and scope of the second alternative require testing even if its application ultimately fails. No same-day decision concerning the local-haul fee supplies entering law; examining the existing two-subsection statutory scheme imports no disposition from that separate matter. No dormant-Commerce-Clause holding, exact-cost finding, or refund amount is supplied.
