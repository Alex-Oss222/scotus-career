# Granholm — opinion form before assignment

The legal form is fixed before a count or author is considered. One signed opinion resolves the consolidated common question in four connected parts: section 2 and the federal alcohol statutes (Part I); the two actual discriminatory mechanisms (Part II); the inadequacy of the States' offered justification under the reasonable-nondiscriminatory-alternatives rule (Part III); and separate judgments with effective state-specific remedies (Part IV). The shared constitutional question supports one opinion, while the three docket instructions remain distinct. The independent New York speech and Privileges and Immunities rulings remain outside the common review question.

The tentative majority is Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. They support affirming the Sixth Circuit in Nos. 03-1116 and 03-1120 and reversing only the Second Circuit's Commerce Clause disposition in No. 03-1274, with remand for effective equality relief. All six support the full common rationale at its stated discrimination scope and are full-opinion eligible authors before any recount. The Chief's express alcohol-statute and section 2 rule supports the construction that those sources enable substantial state control without authorizing local-producer protectionism. The Wilson/Webb-Kenyon relationship explains that same rule rather than adding a distinct Chief ground.

Scalia's frozen objection to a separate economic-extraterritorial-effects theory is honored by resting on discrimination. The opinion decides actual access rather than declaring every local-premises condition invalid. The proper remedy removes the established classwide inequality through lawful state severability and remedial choices, including lawful extension or withdrawal of local direct-sale privileges. Agencies must act under definite decrees that preserve operative neutral controls and prevent indefinite continuation of the preference; the merits justification inquiry is concluded rather than reopened to offer better proof.

Stevens, O'Connor and Thomas can join one shared dissent grounded in section 2's affirmative import authority, the Webb-Kenyon text and the competing treatment of Bacchus and Young's Market. The dissent preserves Congress's affirmative power and independent constitutional-right limits. Thomas's broader previously expressed criticism of negative Commerce Clause doctrine is unnecessary to this shared rationale and creates no separate writing here. No author is selected for either writing.

The Chief's remedy reaches each producer subject to the established policy and permits a narrower remedy only if the State shows that it fully cures the inequality. The Associates' effective-equality commitments support that application to the actual policy class and the ordinary prompt implementation of their decided merits rule. The opinion creates no unrelated remedial entitlement.

Chronology screening of completed April 26–27 law remains a condition of finalization. This form neither writes a Record nor performs an assignment. The sequential recount and final authorship statement belong to the operator after that condition closes.
