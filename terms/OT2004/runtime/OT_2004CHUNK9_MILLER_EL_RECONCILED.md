## Result, authority, and actual entering law

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer support reversal of the denial of relief and a conditional writ requiring a trial before a lawfully selected jury within a reasonable period fixed by the district court, or release from custody under this conviction. Scalia and Thomas support affirmance. These are six-to-two Associate commitments, not the final Court tally.

The six support three distinct conclusions on the properly considered state record: the no-discrimination determination is objectively unreasonable under §2254(d)(2); the relevant factual presumption is rebutted by clear and convincing evidence under §2254(e)(1); and purposeful racial exclusion violating Batson is established. The judgment does not follow from the earlier certificate or from a single statistical fact. Expressly satisfying both provisions here does not settle their relationship in every other habeas setting or impose a clear-and-convincing burden on legal unreasonableness.

The governing simulated authorities retain their actual scope:

- **Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79:** purposeful racial exclusion is prohibited; the objector retains ultimate persuasion, and the prosecutor's actual explanations are assessed with all relevant circumstances.
- **Hernandez v. New York, 500 U.S. 352:** actual discriminatory purpose and credibility remain factual questions receiving appropriate deference. Facial neutrality and ultimate credibility are different inquiries.
- **United States v. Franklyn / United States v. Gonzalez, October 22, 1998:** all eight Associates joined the requirement of an actual purpose finding after explanations, with clear-error review of a supported ultimate finding. The decision does not make every accepted facially neutral explanation conclusive and does not replace AEDPA's additional conditions here.
- **Miller-El v. Cockrell, February 25, 2003:** the eight-Justice certificate opinion required appellate debate about the combined record under the actual habeas framework and preserved the separate statutory tasks. Scalia's published concurrence regarded the State's individualized account as a substantial merits response. Thomas's dissent required combined-reason comparison and refused to infer present misconduct from office history or to withdraw deference automatically because the hearing occurred later.
- **Terry Williams and the actual Early/Visciotti framework:** objective statutory review, the whole state adjudication, the dated constitutional rule, and actual constitutional entitlement remain distinct.
- **Michael Wayne Williams and Holland v. Jackson, June 28, 2004:** the lawful state merits record must be identified before outside material supplies an unreasonableness finding; §2254(e)(2) requires applicant-attributed deficient diligence or greater fault, with its actual exceptions and cumulative innocence condition if triggered. Incompleteness does not itself establish default, deficient diligence, or a new claim. Holland was unanimous and therefore bears on each Associate, including all six supporting relief here.

The current-term Bell v. Cone and Brown v. Payton public propositions preserve attention to the actual state adjudication and independent statutory conditions. Neither supplies a categorical reason to accept this purpose finding or a new equal-protection rule. No same-date Johnson decision supplies entering law to this case. The substantive Batson question is third-stage purposeful discrimination on habeas, not the initial showing required before explanations.

## Published separate-writing functions and substantive grounds

**Breyer:** A separate concurrence may accompany his complete join. Its substantive function is to explain why even successful application of Batson leaves a practical institutional concern. A threshold inquiry may miss some discrimination; a readily supplied neutral explanation and an ultimate inquiry into subjective intent are difficult tools for exposing pretext and unconscious stereotypes. The continuing use of demographic stereotypes conflicts with excluded citizens' equality and the jury's participatory function. Peremptories have a historical role and can reassure litigants, but no constitutional right to peremptories outweighs the constitutional command against discrimination. A system without peremptories is a practicable subject for reconsideration, as the English experience illustrates. His position calls for reconsidering Batson and the peremptory system; it does not itself invalidate peremptories or provide a different ground for the present writ.

The verified historical authorities for this separate concern include Marshall's Batson concurrence, Goldberg's Swain dissent, Edmonson's rejection of racial stereotypes as the price of jury acceptance, Stilson and Ross on the absence of a constitutional entitlement to peremptories, and the broader juror-equality principles in Powers and the actual McCollum and J.E.B. decisions. The historical Purkett citation is not imported as an unverified simulated holding. The precise historical count of proceedings, opinions, or judges is not carried over into this timeline. The empirical literature and practice examples discussed historically remain supporting context, not new controlling constitutional tests or independently verified trial evidence. No other Associate is assigned a join to this concurrence.

**Thomas and Scalia:** Their shared dissenting ground is substantial, not a bare assertion of deference. The applicant must establish the distinct statutory predicates on the lawful record. The State offered a combination of reasons: rehabilitation beliefs and their possible effect on future-dangerousness answers, uncertainty about imposing death, relatives' criminal histories, available peremptories, and differences in accepted panelists' profiles. They would treat those differences together, rather than requiring any two venire members to be identical in every conceivable respect. They read Warren's stated inconsistent answers as the actual explanation and Fields's assurances as compatible with a genuine prosecutorial concern short of cause. In their assessment, different questioning tracks the jurors' death-penalty positions sufficiently to prevent the demanding statutory inference; shuffles without an adequate account of other visible characteristics and office history without a demonstrated link to these prosecutors do not establish the missing proof. The trial judge's opportunity to assess credibility remains relevant, and a delayed hearing does not automatically withdraw the presumption.

Their record-scope objection remains available if an eventual draft relies on federal-only questionnaires/cards without a lawful route. Removing that reliance does not compel a join: the alternative merits disagreement independently sustains their vote to affirm. A public dissent should address the reasons actually adopted in the assembled opinion, not accuse it of using materials it excludes. Likewise, it should not turn the majority's decision to rest on Fields and Warren into a purported affirmative adjudication that every other challenged strike was lawful; the historical dissent's characterization at p.286 is not the historical majority's actual disposition of those closer comparisons.

## Scope, remedy, and assembly constraints

The supported controlling explanation applies Batson to the actual offered reasons and the cumulative lawful record. A materially similar accepted juror is probative even when the individuals are not identical in every respect, but a relevant difference bearing on the offered explanation must be considered. An appellate court cannot cure a discredited explanation by substituting a new imagined rationale. That rule does not forbid considering the full content of an explanation actually offered; Warren's explanation is an express dispute on precisely that point.

The conditional writ reaches the conviction and consequent death judgment. The district court sets a reasonable retrial period after hearing the parties; failure to comply with the condition requires release from custody under that conviction. The remedy is not acquittal and does not adjudicate an independent basis for custody. The historical judgment's direction to enter judgment for the petitioner with appropriate relief, p.266, is consistent with the frozen conditional implementation and supplies no fixed number of days.

Internal limits are not automatically public negative holdings. The absence of a numerical threshold, a universal shuffle rule, or a presumption against every Dallas prosecutor need appear publicly only insofar as the offered rule, actual litigated alternative, or recorded join condition makes the qualification necessary. Breyer's reform concern is his separate position, not an unadopted theory to insert into the Court's holdings. The Court's scope rests on the adequate Fields/Warren ground; optional discussion of other strikes is not required merely because the historical opinion mentioned them.
