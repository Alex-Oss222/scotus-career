## 30. Johnson v. California, No. 03-636

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Conditions:** Subject to any intervening effective decision governing the issue.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated rule, disposition and remedy.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand for application of the common substantial-justification standard, with damages and prospective branches addressed separately.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require assessment of the policy’s coverage, duration, evidentiary support and concrete practicable alternatives under the correct standard. Further factfinding is necessary only where the existing record leaves a material dispute. Determine current official responsibility and standing, and separately decide immunity for damages using the law when each defendant acted.
* **Remedial Reach:** If the policy is proved unlawful, relief must correct its operation for the persons it governs unless California establishes that a narrower remedy fully cures the violation. Section 3626’s independent necessity and intrusiveness requirements remain. No damages award follows merely from an incorrect review standard.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must set a prompt schedule for evidence and any supported interim request, identify the responsible current officials and require a compliant housing practice if the common policy fails. An injunction must state permitted security exceptions and compliance dates rather than command indiscriminate cell reassignment. Individual loss and causal responsibility receive separate adjudication.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The governing racial-classification standard

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Johnson was repeatedly subjected to the challenged classification. The preserved standard-of-review issue can be resolved without deciding every personal-immunity or security fact.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether reception-center racial assignment escapes the common governmental racial-classification standard.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña to the prison setting; do not replace it with either historical strict scrutiny or general rational deference.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate, reaching the explicit housing policy and comparable custodial classifications. A universal ban on every emergency separation would exceed the security record. A rule confined to Johnson’s particular transfer would leave the recurring policy’s legal standard unresolved.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An express racial prison-housing classification must satisfy the common substantial-justification standard, and custody does not relieve the State of its burden to justify the classification’s actual coverage, duration, burdens and alternatives. Preventing substantiated violence is an important lawful objective, but generalized racial assumptions or administrative convenience cannot establish that a blanket entry-and-transfer policy substantially serves it. The judgment under a materially more deferential standard must be vacated.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Explicit racial cell assignments for new arrivals and transferees. Ordinary classification by individualized threats, gang activity and other lawfully relevant information remains available under its proper source.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts evaluate supported emergencies and genuinely unavailable information within the same standard. They do not presume that a sixty-day period or a transfer automatically requires racial separation.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña requires evidence-supported fit and actual-burden justification for every government’s racial treatment. Brown v. City of Oneonta prevents a benign explanation from avoiding that trigger. Security expertise can support factual judgments, but cannot shift the burden to a prisoner to disprove all conceivable dangers. Existing information about transferees and the integrated operation of other areas are relevant comparisons, not conclusive proof that every temporary precaution is unnecessary.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Extend the application of Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña to express prison housing classifications; preserve Turner v. Safley for matters its general framework governs.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Individual damages immunity and disputed security facts require adjudication on the proper record. Until those issues are decided, courts apply the conduct-date immunity rule and the common equality standard independently.

## 31. Dowhal v. SmithKline Beecham Consumer Healthcare

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Conditions:** Subject to any intervening effective decision governing the issue.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated rule, disposition and remedy.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the preemption judgment at the scope of the specifically prohibited warning demand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject the requested state enforcement and dependent monetary or injunctive relief insofar as they require that warning. Preserve genuinely different compatible duties for adjudication only if pleaded and preserved.
* **Remedial Reach:** The parties’ identified warning claim is resolved; no general injunction against Proposition 65 or judicial safety certification is entered.
* **Practical Relief:** The state court must enter or preserve judgment excluding liability based on failure to provide the conflicting warning. It must identify the actual covered products and demand, rather than dismiss independent claims solely because FDA approved a product.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Saved state requirements and actual conflict

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The actual warning demand and identified federal actions permit a focused supremacy determination.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §379r(d)(2) protects this warning from independently established conflict preemption.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply current actual-conflict doctrine.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow, because the dispute concerns a specifically rejected warning. Broad immunity for all nicotine-product claims would lack support. Restricting the decision to the particular manufacturer’s name would obscure the operative warning conflict.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 379r(d)(2) preserves qualifying state warning requirements from §379r’s express uniformity rule, but does not authorize a state duty that directly contradicts a valid, specifically applicable FDA labeling command. The party asserting preemption must identify that command and the incompatibility; approval or a generalized federal objective is insufficient. The demand for the specifically prohibited warning cannot be enforced while that operative federal decision remains valid.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The particular warning and covered products for which the actual federal command establishes conflict; compatible warnings and independent lawful duties are not extinguished by approval alone.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts identify the actual federal labeling command and assess any legally available alternative means of complying with the state duty. A different warning or communication is preempted only if the relevant federal action reaches and conflicts with it.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Geier v. American Honda Motor Co. gives independent work to both saving and actual conflict. Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick prevents an inference from silence. Here the asserted conflict rests on specific FDA consideration and prohibition, which must be distinguished from merely approving a minimum label. Preserving Proposition 65 has substantial effect across compatible applications without authorizing two irreconcilable commands.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Geier v. American Honda Motor Co.; preserve Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick and Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr’s operative-source and compatible-duty limits.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Different warnings, products and nonlabel communications require their own federal coverage and conflict showing.

##### Issue 2: FDA authority over literally true but misleading labeling

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The source and validity of the asserted federal command are necessary antecedents to preemption.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the FDA’s labeling authority can reach a truthful statement that materially misleads in context.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted misbranding provisions and independent review of authority.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate within drug labeling, because the statutory distinction between truth and misleading context recurs. A general government power to censor truth would exceed the source. A literal-falsity-only rule would omit the enacted misleadingness criterion.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The FDA may reject a proposed drug label that is literally accurate but materially misleading in its actual context when the misbranding statute covers the communication and the agency supports that conclusion through its authorized decision process. It may not substitute a bare preference for consumer behavior for that statutory finding. The comparative-risk explanation supports the particular labeling determination reviewed here.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Authorized drug-labeling judgments, not independent restrictions on all public discussion of product risks.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A different claim must establish what the communication conveys and why the FDA’s governing statute and actual action reach it; no general public-health exception to speech rights is announced.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The enacted reference to misleading labeling would be deprived of work if literal accuracy were conclusive. The concern here is the warning’s implication about relative harms and cessation, not denial that nicotine has risks. The conflict analysis therefore depends on a bounded labeling determination, not deference on the existence of federal power.
* **Precedent Treatment:** No precedent is overruled; the actual-conflict rule is applied only after identifying valid agency authority.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** An independent First Amendment challenge to a different speech restriction lies outside the question presented.

## 32. Roper v. Simmons

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Conditions:** Subject to any intervening effective decision governing the issue.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated rule, disposition and remedy.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment replacing Simmons’s death sentence with a lawful noncapital sentence.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the conviction and the state court’s noncapital disposition. Prohibit execution for the offense because Simmons was under eighteen when it occurred.
* **Remedial Reach:** Simmons receives direct judgment protection. The categorical rule applies to other qualifying offenders through their lawful review channels; this judgment issues no administrative order in unlisted cases.
* **Practical Relief:** Missouri must maintain a noncapital custody basis and remove any authority to execute Simmons for this offense. Other courts applying the rule must verify offense-date age and enter the noncapital correction authorized by their governing law, without disturbing valid convictions.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Capital punishment for conduct committed before eighteen

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The final state judgment directly presents the continued federal validity of juvenile death eligibility; the Court may reconsider its own precedent.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether persons under eighteen when they offended may be executed.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose changing the age-specific law by overruling Stanford v. Kentucky to the extent it permits execution for offenses committed before eighteen.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad within juvenile capital eligibility, because the identified defect is categorical rather than unique to Simmons. A prohibition of all capital punishment is outside the question. Case-by-case maturity screening would leave the identified unreliability of separating juveniles into a death-eligible subclass unresolved.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments prohibit the death penalty for an offense committed before the offender’s eighteenth birthday. Age at the offense controls; later adulthood, the crime’s severity and a judicial assessment of exceptional maturity do not create an exception. An existing death sentence within that class must be replaced through lawful noncapital sentencing procedures, while conviction and independent punishment remain subject to their ordinary rules.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Every offender whose crime occurred before eighteen, including cases on collateral review through an available lawful channel. No ruling is made on noncapital juvenile sentences or on adults with asserted developmental immaturity.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Age is measured at the offense, so later adulthood does not restore death eligibility. A genuinely disputed birth date requires reliable adjudication before execution can be authorized; the rule does not require reassessment of an individual juvenile’s maturity.
* **Transition:** The proposed rule removes a class from death eligibility and is substantive. It applies to Simmons’s state collateral proceeding and to nonfinal and final death judgments reached through lawful review mechanisms; it does not create new jurisdiction or erase independent procedural requirements.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Atkins v. Virginia recognizes that diminished culpability and unreliable death selection can require a categorical limit despite the availability of mitigation. Youth’s developmental differences concern responsibility, susceptibility and capacity for change, and the domestic legislative and practice evidence supports treating eighteen as the administrable boundary. The especially grave facts here test rather than defeat a categorical rule. The developments in domestic law and practice since Stanford, together with the unreliability of selecting exceptionally mature juveniles, undermine its reliance on individualized mitigation. Replacing that rule protects an identifiable class without unsettling convictions or any legitimate entitlement to continue an unconstitutional punishment. International practice may corroborate the domestic conclusion but is unnecessary as an independent source of binding law.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Overrule Stanford v. Kentucky only insofar as it permits execution for offenses committed before eighteen. Extend the categorical proportionality reasoning of Atkins v. Virginia; preserve its distinct disability definition and procedure.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Noncapital juvenile punishment and adult developmental claims fall outside the question presented. No general procedural reopening rule is adopted.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Tenth Circuit judgment in No. 02-1472; affirm the Federal Circuit judgment in No. 03-853; remand for the remaining contract proceedings.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Enforce the authorized support-cost undertakings subject to the actual amounts, conditions and statutory payment process. Do not command reallocation of every tribal program appropriation.
* **Remedial Reach:** The named contracting tribes and the contracts adjudicated in these consolidated disputes receive relief. The rule does not award money to nonparty tribes or contracts not proved.
* **Practical Relief:** The reviewing courts must determine any remaining amounts and direct the responsible federal contracting authorities to implement the resulting awards through the lawful payment channel. An unresolved accounting issue must be identified specifically; aggregate agency underallocation cannot again substitute for a contractual defense.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Contractual liability and appropriations availability

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The authorized contracts and statutory review channel present concrete payment disputes; liability can be decided without a general trust-duty damages theory.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Government may avoid the promised support costs on the asserted appropriations and allocation grounds.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the actual-undertaking rule and resolve the contract-support statute’s availability language.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate, covering binding support-cost contracts within legally sufficient appropriations. A general right to compel unappropriated spending would exceed the undertaking. Limiting liability to the agency’s discretionary allocation would make the promise unilaterally revocable.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When an authorized self-determination contract promises support costs and legally available appropriations are sufficient to satisfy that contract, an agency’s allocation of funds to competing demands does not by itself discharge the promise. The Government must identify an enacted limitation or contractual condition that actually defeats liability; a committee-report expectation or internal allocation is insufficient. Later restrictions on use of unspent appropriations do not retroactively cancel accrued contract obligations without enacted language requiring that result.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The fiscal-year undertakings and restrictions actually before the Court. A true statutory cap, lack of contracting authority or express valid condition remains material; no unlimited entitlement to every requested program amount is created.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts compare each contract and fiscal-year statute, determine legally available funds and then adjudicate the promised amount and lawful payment channel. They may not convert agency budget preferences into unexpressed conditions.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Hercules, Inc. v. United States prevents liability based on general fairness, but these claims rest on express authorized agreements. United States v. Winstar Corp. distinguishes contractual financial responsibility from power to prevent later lawful government action. Lincoln v. Vigil preserves allocation discretion, not immunity from the consequences of an agency’s binding promises. Reading availability as an agency option to withhold funds would deprive the undertaking of contractual force. The later appropriations text should be given its actual prospective funding work without silently annulling earlier liabilities.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Hercules, Inc. v. United States and United States v. Winstar Corp.; distinguish Lincoln v. Vigil’s allocation question.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other fiscal years, genuinely insufficient total appropriations and uncontracted costs fall outside these contracts and appropriation statutes.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the appellate rejection of blanket preclusion and remand for the remaining title proceedings.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the earlier final judgment for the property it actually adjudicated. Require parcel- and servitude-specific determination of the later claims, defenses and applicable federal choice-of-law question.
* **Remedial Reach:** The parties and servitudes in this action receive adjudication under the proper preclusion rule. No title decree for unrelated property or reopening of the old judgment follows.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must identify each disputed servitude, its instrument and claimed prescriptive history, determine any still-supported specific preclusion argument and then adjudicate title. It may not reject a claim solely because the litigants share predecessors, nor extend the old decree to land it did not decide.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Identity of adjudicated claims and issues

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The appellate ruling presents a concrete preclusion question; final title merits need not be resolved to determine the earlier judgment’s reach.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Nebo Oil precludes litigation of these distinct servitudes or the asserted unlitigated federal choice-of-law issue.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing judgment and preclusion distinctions.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow, tied to the actual servitudes and prior adjudication. A rule weakening final title judgments would be broader than necessary. Treating related transactions as identical without examination would erase preclusion’s essential predicates.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A final judgment concerning one mineral servitude does not preclude adjudication of distinct servitudes merely because they derive from related conveyances or common predecessors, and an issue not actually litigated and necessarily determined is not made conclusive by the judgment’s broader reasoning. The party invoking preclusion must establish the required identity from the actual instruments and prior adjudication. The record does not establish that identity for the later servitudes.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The disputed servitudes and federal choice-of-law issue actually presented. The original adjudicated servitude retains the protection of its judgment.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts compare the asserted property right and precise issue with the prior instruments and adjudication. Distinct servitudes receive ordinary title and prescription adjudication; a proved identical issue must receive the preclusive effect independently required by federal law.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. protects a completed judgment but creates no collateral expansion of its subject matter. Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp. warns against treating a procedural merits label as universal external preclusion. Here separate servitudes can have different creation and use histories, and the reported prior litigation did not decide the federal choice-of-law question now asserted. Those defects suffice without a general change-in-law exception to finality.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Preserve Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc.; distinguish Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp.’s diversity-specific incorporation rule. No final title judgment is reopened.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The ultimate title, nonuse and statutory-waiver facts require the remand record. No exception to an otherwise established identical-issue estoppel is necessary to this proposed disposition.

## 35. Tenet v. Doe

#### A. STATUS

* **Conditions:** Subject to any intervening effective decision governing the issue.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated rule, disposition and remedy.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand with instructions to dismiss the agreement-dependent claims.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** End the ordinary judicial enforcement action at the threshold. Enter no finding that an espionage agreement existed, no damages award and no order compelling intelligence disclosure.
* **Remedial Reach:** The pleaded claims of these Does are dismissed. The rule does not dispose of an unpleaded independent statutory entitlement or another person’s claim.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must identify the dismissed agreement-dependent counts and terminate further merits discovery on them. Any properly sought amendment must identify an actual independent legal source and satisfy ordinary procedural requirements; the mandate creates no benefits or new cause of action.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Scope of the secret-agreement bar

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The complaint’s own theory makes the covert undertaking indispensable. The legal bar can be applied without deciding whether the alleged relationship existed or its promises were breached.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether these agreement-dependent claims can proceed by invoking estoppel or due process.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Totten v. United States at its actual scope; do not create a general national-security immunity.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow, covering claims necessarily dependent on a secret espionage agreement. A rule immunizing all intelligence conduct would lack a source. A contract-label-only restriction would allow identical enforcement through relabeling.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A claim that necessarily requires judicial establishment and enforcement of a covert espionage agreement is barred by Totten v. United States even when pleaded as estoppel or denial of process, unless an applicable enactment supplies a different enforceable channel. An independent public legal duty must be assessed separately. The Does’ described claims depend on the alleged secret bargain and therefore cannot proceed through ordinary litigation.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Agreement-dependent covert-service claims. Independent public statutory benefits, ordinary public employment and tort or constitutional claims with a different necessary basis are not decided.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts identify the claim’s legal source and necessary proof. A claim founded on an independent public duty receives ordinary jurisdiction, waiver and merits analysis, with any properly invoked Reynolds privilege assessed separately.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Totten v. United States addresses the incompatibility between the secret undertaking and ordinary judicial enforcement. United States v. Reynolds does not purport to repeal that claim-category rule; using evidentiary procedures cannot eliminate the need to prove the relationship that supplies the entitlement. Christopher v. Harbury likewise preserves the need for an actual cause and remedy rather than allowing the seriousness of alleged wrongdoing to create them. The conclusion leaves independent legal duties for a case that pleads them.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Totten v. United States at its covert-agreement scope; distinguish United States v. Reynolds and Webster v. Doe.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No independent public statutory entitlement or nonagreement injury is established by the claims presented. Their availability is reserved for a record actually raising them.

## 36. Catholic Charities of Sacramento, Inc. v. Superior Court

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Conditions:** The threshold disposition rests on the preliminary-relief record. An actual final judgment or documented Cox exception must be evaluated before the federal merits may be reached.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated rule, disposition and remedy.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Dismiss for want of jurisdiction on the present finality record.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Issue no federal declaration, coverage exemption or merits injunction. Leave the parties to the ongoing state proceeding and a properly reviewable later judgment.
* **Remedial Reach:** The disposition concerns this attempted review only. It neither validates the statute for all employers nor forecloses a later federal merits challenge through the proper appellate route.
* **Practical Relief:** The case returns to the state process without a federal merits mandate. The organization may seek relief available there and must establish finality for any renewed Supreme Court review. No invented later judgment, enrollment change or benefit order is used to cure the gap.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Finality of the state preliminary-relief judgment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The current record identifies an ongoing action for declaration and injunction and appellate review of preliminary relief, not final adjudication of that action. The jurisdictional burden is unmet on the record.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this state judgment is presently reviewable under §1257.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply current finality law and withhold merits decision.
* **Reach Calibration:** Confined to this record, because the missing finality predicates prevent merits review. A rule forbidding every appeal from preliminary relief would disregard Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn. Treating constitutional importance as enough would abandon the statute.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A state appellate judgment denying preliminary injunctive relief is not reviewable under §1257 merely because it resolves the asserted federal legal theory; the party invoking jurisdiction must establish a final judgment or the actual predicates of a recognized practical-finality exception. The record establishes neither, so the Court must dismiss for want of jurisdiction without deciding the religion questions.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This preliminary-relief posture and the absence of established finality. A later final judgment or a proved Cox exception presents a different jurisdictional record.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A final state judgment or a demonstrated Cox exception permits review under §1257. A conclusive discussion of a federal issue alone does not establish finality while substantive proceedings and effective later review remain.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Johnson v. California rejected immediate review of an important federal right while substantive state proceedings remained. The same discipline requires an actual finality showing here; a broad constitutional discussion in the state opinion does not supply facts about what remains below. The organization’s continuing burden may support state interim relief but cannot independently enlarge this Court’s appellate jurisdiction.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Johnson v. California, Florida v. Thomas and Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn; no merits precedent is changed.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The free-exercise and exemption merits are not reached because §1257 jurisdiction is unestablished. They require a reviewable judgment and remain legally unresolved in this case.
