## 14. Educational Credit Management Corp. v. Polleys

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22, current revision request.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning, scope and remedy.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2004, assessed for 2004-12-13; subject to intervening controlling decisions.
* **Conditions:** Subject to the stated record limits and any intervening controlling decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the discharge judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Maintain discharge of the student-loan obligation adjudicated in the adversary proceeding. No new hardship hearing is needed because the existing supported findings satisfy the announced standard.
* **Remedial Reach:** Polleys's adjudicated debt to ECMC. The decision creates no class discharge or cancellation of other borrowers' obligations.
* **Practical Relief:** The bankruptcy discharge continues to bar collection of the covered obligation under the applicable discharge injunction. ECMC must treat that debt as discharged; ordinary bankruptcy enforcement addresses a violation of that injunction. No new payment schedule or administrative program is judicially created.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A workable statutory hardship inquiry

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** A final discharge and supported findings permit resolution of the full legal question and its application without speculative future facts.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether realistic present and foreseeable repayment inability, coupled with legitimate efforts, can satisfy undue hardship.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt a new statutory holding consistent with existing text-first bankruptcy law; do not call Brunner a prior Supreme Court command.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A coherent statutory test should govern comparable discharge claims. One step broader would decide partial discharge and later repayment regimes that are absent. One step narrower would reject one phrase while leaving the actual proof requirement unclear.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A debtor establishes undue hardship under §523(a)(8) by proving, more likely than not, that repayment prevents a minimal standard of living, that specific circumstances make that inability likely to persist for a significant part of the repayment period, and that the claimed hardship is consistent with reasonable good-faith efforts to earn income, keep expenses reasonable and meet the obligation when able. Courts may not require certainty of hopelessness, permanent disability or a payment the debtor lacked resources to make. Polleys satisfies that standard on the supported findings.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Covered student-loan debts under the operative statute, including genuine mental-health limits and dependents' essential needs. Mere inconvenience, voluntarily contrived inability and unsupported pessimism do not establish the exception.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Lamie v. United States Trustee requires enforcing the condition Congress enacted, which here is undue hardship rather than an additional certainty standard. Brunner usefully organizes the inquiry if each element remains tied to actual ability and legitimate effort. The findings describe a long-term earning constraint and a budget without payment capacity; lack of remittances does not independently establish bad faith. Hypothetical recovery cannot displace evidence, just as unsupported predictions of permanent inability cannot satisfy the debtor's burden.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Lamie v. United States Trustee: apply enacted conditions without judicial additions. Brunner v. New York State Higher Education Services Corp.: adopt the stated evidence-based formulation as a new Supreme Court rule, without adopting more restrictive glosses.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Partial discharge and later statutory or repayment-program changes are outside the question presented.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Remedial Reach:** Whitfield's and Hall's challenged §1956(h) convictions to the extent presented by this question; no adjudication of an unlisted defendant.
* **Practical Relief:** The courts apply the statute without an added overt-act element. #### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The elements Congress enacted

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved instruction dispute squarely presents the statutory requirement and supports resolution in both dockets.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §1956(h) incorporates an overt-act element from §371.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend the reasoning of Shabani through a new construction of the different statute.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A clear statute-wide element rule is required for charging and jury instructions. One step broader would announce an unsupported rule for every conspiracy statute. One step narrower would decide these instructions without clarifying the recurring statutory element.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1956(h) defines a conspiracy to commit the specified laundering offenses without an additional overt-act element. The Government must prove the unlawful agreement, the defendant's knowing participation and the required unlawful object beyond a reasonable doubt; association alone is insufficient. The same-penalties language does not incorporate §371, and a venue provision does not add an element omitted from the offense. The requested overt-act instruction was therefore properly refused.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Conspiracies prosecuted under §1956(h). Statutes expressly requiring an act, including §371, retain that requirement.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** United States v. Shabani separates the agreement offense Congress enacted from an overt-act condition Congress expressly includes elsewhere. That reasoning applies because §1956(h) directly describes conspiracy and contains no incorporation of §371. Nash v. United States and Singer v. United States explain the established meaning available to Congress. Ratzlaf v. United States preserves lenity, but ordinary text and structure resolve this question without a residual grievous ambiguity.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States v. Shabani: extend its text-based reasoning to §1956(h), respecting its original statutory limit. Ratzlaf v. United States: preserve lenity where ordinary interpretation leaves genuine ambiguity.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** An independent venue or sufficiency challenge is outside the question presented.

Commerce's anti-masking rationale is a permissible choice within that room. Charming Betsy warrants attention to compatible international obligations, but cannot give a report concerning a different proceeding overriding domestic force that Congress did not confer.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States v. Mead Corp. and United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: apply the delegation and statutory-boundary requirements. Barnhart v. Thomas: preserve the distinction between permissible implementation and exclusive statutory meaning.

## 17. United States v. Booker, consolidated with United States v. Fanfan

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22, current revision request.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Booker: affirm the setting aside of the enhanced sentence and remand for lawful resentencing. Fanfan: affirm the refusal to impose the unsupported enhancement and the resulting sentence against that challenge, leaving only any independently preserved calculation issue for ordinary disposition.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Retain the binding Guidelines. The sentencing court must calculate the range using only facts established with the applicable constitutional protections, then exercise the discretion lawfully available within it. No freestanding order for a new enhancement jury follows; further proceedings require independent legal authority and compliance with charging, waiver, proof and finality constraints.
* **Remedial Reach:** The two defendants' judgments and the governing rule for nonfinal federal cases to which it applies. Convictions are not vacated merely because a sentencing enhancement fails. No class resentencing order or automatic reopening of final judgments is entered.
* **Practical Relief:** Booker's sentencing court must enter a replacement sentence authorized by the lawful range, correcting any independently established calculation defect. Fanfan's 78-month sentence remains effective against the asserted entitlement to use the excluded findings. The Government may seek additional proceedings only if an actual procedural source permits them; the mandate does not supply that source. Ordinary appellate review continues under surviving enacted provisions.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The constitutional boundary already established

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The two records concretely distinguish an imposed judicial enhancement from refusal to impose one. Existing law answers the asserted federal-Guidelines distinction.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the additional findings can raise the binding range without the applicable safeguards.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply current law, restating Apprendi, Angle, Harris, Gatewood and Blakely as the single authorization inquiry they already support.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. The legal effect of a fact, rather than its label or rulemaker, defines the constitutional boundary. One step broader would regulate factfinding that does not change authorized punishment. One step narrower would recreate the statutory-label exceptions already rejected.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A fact that increases a binding sentencing ceiling or floor must be charged when the applicable grand-jury guarantee requires it and established to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt, validly admitted as an element, or determined beyond a reasonable doubt after a lawful jury waiver. The Government bears those charging and proof obligations; judicial discretion remains within the range already authorized. Booker's additional findings cannot support his increased range, and Fanfan's court properly declined the comparable enhancement.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Present-offense and recidivist facts that increase binding federal punishment boundaries, subject to the precise existing admission and waiver rules. Ordinary selection within a lawfully fixed range is excluded.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A sentencing report’s unchallenged assertion is not by itself a valid element-level admission.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Angle already rejects an outer-statutory-maximum escape from the jury guarantee for binding Guidelines. Harris establishes the same principle for a mandatory floor. Gatewood rejects a categorical recidivism carveout, and Blakely confirms that a plea authorizes only the range supported by the admitted elements. These are more than three bounded rules governing the same authorization question; Stone would state them as one inquiry without overruling any. Mistretta sustains the Commission's institution but supplies no exception to an independent constitutional guarantee.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apprendi, Angle, Harris, Gatewood and Blakely: reaffirm and restate their common authorization inquiry. Mistretta v. United States: preserve institutional validity while distinguishing the sentencing-fact question.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The validity of any particular unexamined admission, indictment or recidivist input needs the actual record.

##### Issue 2: Constitutional operation without an advisory rewrite

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The severability question is presented and requires a full answer because the Government seeks a different statutory system in both dockets.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Court should remove binding force or retain the enacted system subject to the constitutional safeguards.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the reserved remedial question by preserving the binding structure and rejecting the requested judicial advisory replacement.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad within the federal sentencing statute. The remedy must govern ordinary future application and both present judgments. One step broader would redesign substantive sentencing policy or authorize new prosecutions. One step narrower would leave courts unable to know whether the enacted ranges remain mandatory.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Federal Sentencing Guidelines remain binding to the extent a sentence is supported by constitutionally established facts; the constitutional defect does not authorize replacing Congress's mandatory system with an advisory one. Courts must withhold an unsupported enhancement and impose a lawful sentence under the remaining enacted framework. Severability removes only what cannot constitutionally operate, and existing law supplies no automatic authority to convene a new enhancement proceeding.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The Federal Sentencing Reform Act and challenged applications, with ordinary review of lawful ranges retained. The rule does not dictate what replacement system Congress may enact.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Further enhancement factfinding requires an independent procedural source and the governing charging, proof and waiver protections.
* **Transition:** Ordinary direct-review rules govern nonfinal cases; forfeited claims retain all Olano requirements. No categorical collateral reopening is proposed. Existing statutory deadlines, retroactivity rules and habeas gateways govern final cases separately.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Angle already demonstrated an operative binding framework with protected factfinding. The constitutional minimum constrains enforcement without requiring a new statutory policy. Alaska Airlines, Inc. v. Brock directs attention to the functioning enacted remainder; it does not allow a court to substitute a more discretionary arrangement because the Government predicts Congress would prefer it. Blakely and Gatewood require an actual source for additional proceedings after vacatur. The two defendants consequently receive different dispositions because only Booker suffered the identified enhancement.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Angle: reaffirm binding operation and decide its reserved system-level remedy. Blakely and Gatewood: reaffirm the limit on automatic additional enhancement proceedings. Alaska Airlines, Inc. v. Brock: apply severability without judicial replacement of the statutory design.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Independent record-specific calculation and preservation issues, if actually raised, require their existing records.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Place distinguishes the information acquired by a limited sniff from the separate seizure necessary to make it possible. Whren and Robinette preserve that second inquiry throughout a traffic encounter. Knowles bars a full citation search without an independent ground, which the later alert must supply here through ordinary probable cause. Edmond prevents the supposed nonsearch from justifying an antecedent suspicionless seizure. Kyllo protects concealed domestic information and does not turn every use of enhanced detection into a search. These rules are applied in sequence: authority for detention, classification of the canine examination, and justification for any physical search. Their distinct tests and burdens remain intact.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States v. Place: apply its limited detection principle. Whren v. United States, Ohio v. Robinette, Knowles v. Iowa, City of Indianapolis v. Edmond and Kyllo v. United States: reaffirm their distinct authority, scope and information limits.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Different canine access, disputed reliability or an actual added detention requires facts outside the accepted question.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Sixth Circuit's aggravator-based writ and remand for the corrected actual-adjudication and cure inquiry, without dismissing all remaining sentence claims.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The court must abandon the inference that an omitted citation proves nonapplication. It must determine the actual state merits adjudication and applicable threshold rules, then evaluate the narrowing and any necessary correction under the proper dated law. If a surviving constitutional defect warrants relief and lacks a lawful cure, enter a conditional sentence writ allowing an authorized constitutional correction within a reasonable specified period or vacation of the death sentence.

* **Practical Relief:** On return, the court must identify the state construction and what the state court actually did, rather than demand a citation or presume an omitted step. It must enter a reasoned disposition under §2254. If conditional relief is warranted, the order must set a concrete correction deadline and require the State to report a lawful replacement determination; failure requires removal of the unlawful death sentence, not indefinite continuation under an unimplemented declaration.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A citation is not the constitutional act

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The federal court's reasoning gives dispositive weight to the absent citation, while the state opinion contains substantive discussion requiring actual assessment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether lack of a Dicks citation establishes failure to narrow the aggravator.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing substantive-narrowing and AEDPA principles, with a confined corrective remand.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Courts need a rule distinguishing actual legal operation from citation form. One step broader would presume all unmentioned issues were decided or all appellate discussion cures a jury defect. One step narrower would correct a word choice without requiring examination of the actual adjudication.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A state court's failure to cite its settled narrowing precedent does not by itself establish that it applied an unconstitutional aggravator; federal review must assess the state construction, the actual reasoning and the relevant statutory gateway. The petitioner must establish the applicable §2254 ground, and a court may neither infer constitutional failure from the missing citation alone nor invent an adjudication or corrective step absent from the record. The present writ must return for that inquiry.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Actual application of authoritative narrowing law in a state merits adjudication. It does not create a universal silent-merits presumption.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Sochor v. Florida permits a sufficiently specific state narrowing application and rejects both mandatory verbal formulas and formula-only cures. Godfrey v. Georgia and Proffitt v. Florida supply the dated federal framework; the state opinion's discussion of conscious suffering must be assessed against it and Dicks. Bell v. Cone requires objective §2254 review but preserves distinct remaining claims. That combination forecloses the Sixth Circuit's citation shortcut without compelling blanket denial of every aggravator theory.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Sochor v. Florida: reaffirm substance over citation and actual correction. Bell v. Cone: enforce its dated review regime and preserved-claims mandate. Godfrey v. Georgia and Proffitt v. Florida: apply the relevant preadjudication narrowing principles.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Any actual absence of state merits adjudication, unresolved default predicate or unexamined curative operation requires the record-specific determination identified on remand.

##### Issue 2: Complete and lawful sentence correction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The scope of remand must preserve the difference between a narrowed legal definition and actual correction of a proved sentencing defect.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What follows if a properly reached jury-guidance defect remains after the corrected inquiry.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing actual-cure and State-borne effect rules, without backdating later law into the §2254 cutoff.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A usable remand must identify the path to relief as well as the gateway. One step broader would adjudicate an unpresented Ring claim or all sentence grounds. One step narrower would leave a proved uncured error without an enforceable correction.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A properly reached constitutional aggravator defect cannot be cured solely by counting remaining factors or presuming that independent judicial review removed its influence; the State must supply an authorized actual correction or establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute when the governing effect inquiry applies. If the statutory gateway and other relief requirements are met and neither showing exists, a conditional sentence writ must require lawful correction within a specified reasonable period or removal of the death sentence.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Richmond v. Lewis requires an actual constitutional sentencing determination or adequate harmlessness. Lambrix v. Singletary preserves the influence of a legally consequential jury recommendation and separates default from retroactivity. Brecht v. Abrahamson places the collateral effect burden on the State under controlling precedent. Schriro v. Summerlin rejects historical nonretroactivity for its defined Ring category, but does not remove AEDPA's independent date-sensitive gateway or add a claim absent from the grant.
