## 21. Illinois v. Caballes

* **Conditions:** Subject to the stated record limits and any intervening controlling decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Illinois judgment's categorical suspicion requirement and remand under the limited nonsearch rule.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The state court must apply the rule to the accepted lawful-stop premises and resolve only genuinely preserved independent issues. If no independent defect remains, the evidence is not excluded merely because the sniff lacked separate drug suspicion. The mandate does not itself adjudicate an unreviewed state-law ground or direct an unconditional conviction reinstatement.
* **Remedial Reach:** Caballes's suppression claim and dependent criminal judgment. No civil immunity, damages or systemwide injunction is before the Court.
* **Practical Relief:** The state court must determine the consequence of eliminating the categorical federal premise under the existing record. Officers receive no authority to delay release, enter a protected space or conduct a physical search without its own justification. The reliable alert may support the later search only under the ordinary probable-cause requirements.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Identify the actual added intrusion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The record and grant accept lawful traffic duration and present an exterior examination, permitting a legal answer without inventing a prolongation finding.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether separate reasonable suspicion is required for the specified limited sniff.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the surviving Place distinction together with the simulated traffic-stop limits.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The recurring vehicle setting requires a defined rule and actual predicates. One step broader would exempt all canine methods or all contraband investigations from the Amendment. One step narrower would make investigative purpose alone decisive despite lawful access and no protected added intrusion.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A trained canine's exterior examination that is actually confined to detecting contraband requires no separate drug suspicion when officers have lawful access and the examination adds no unlawful restraint or other protected intrusion. The State must establish those predicates and, for a later physical search, ordinary probable cause; a dog's label or subsequent discovery cannot substitute for either showing. On the accepted premises here, an unrelated investigative purpose alone does not invalidate the stop.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The specified exterior vehicle examination during independently lawful detention. Home access, physical entry, exposure of lawful private information and unsupported prolongation are excluded.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Ordinary search and seizure rules govern added detention, protected-area access and physical entry; the later vehicle search requires independently sufficient probable cause.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Place distinguishes the information acquired by a limited sniff from the separate seizure necessary to make it possible. Whren and Robinette preserve that second inquiry throughout a traffic encounter. Knowles bars a full citation search without an independent ground, which the later alert must supply here through ordinary probable cause. Edmond prevents the supposed nonsearch from justifying an antecedent suspicionless seizure. Kyllo protects concealed domestic information and does not turn every use of enhanced detection into a search. These rules are applied in sequence: authority for detention, classification of the canine examination, and justification for any physical search. Their distinct tests and burdens remain intact.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States v. Place: apply its limited detection principle. Whren v. United States, Ohio v. Robinette, Knowles v. Iowa, City of Indianapolis v. Edmond and Kyllo v. United States: reaffirm their distinct authority, scope and information limits.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Different canine access, disputed reliability or an actual added detention requires facts outside the accepted question.

## 22. Commissioner v. Banks

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22, current revision request.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning, scope and remedy.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2004, assessed for 2005-01-24; subject to intervening controlling decisions.
* **Conditions:** Subject to the stated record limits and any intervening controlling decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse both fee-exclusion judgments and remand for lawful tax calculations.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Include the contingent-fee portions of these otherwise taxable recoveries in the clients' gross income. Apply only deductions available under the actual tax-year law; do not extend §703 retroactively or decide an unpresented statutory-fee theory.
* **Remedial Reach:** Banks's and Banaitis's contested tax liabilities. No class refund, general attorney-fee award or change to the underlying employment settlements follows.
* **Practical Relief:** The tax courts must recompute any affected liabilities using the full taxable recoveries and lawful deductions, with the actual payment and return records. No precise deficiency is fixed by this judgment. Collection or refund then follows the ordinary tax judgment and statutory procedures.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Ownership of the claim and payment to its agent

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Both judgments squarely present gross-income attribution; the liens and contingent agreements are sufficiently identified to decide the claimed exclusions.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the attorney's contractual fee share removes part of the client's recovery from gross income.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing assignment principles to this new contingent-fee question.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A uniform attribution rule is needed where the relationship remains principal and agent. One step broader would decide genuine independent ownership or statutory fee awards absent from these records. One step narrower would make federal income attribution turn on lien terminology without examining the actual rights.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When a client retains the income-producing legal claim and counsel acts as the client's agent, an otherwise taxable recovery includes the contractual contingent fee paid to counsel, whether paid through the client or directly and whether secured by a state-law lien. The taxpayer claiming exclusion must identify an applicable statutory exclusion or a genuine different ownership arrangement; uncertainty about recovery value and counsel's labor do not alone transfer the claim. Both fee portions are included here.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Ordinary contingent-fee representation preserving the client's claim ownership and ultimate settlement control. A separately owned claim or independently awarded statutory fee is not adjudicated.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Lucas v. Earl and Helvering v. Horst tie attribution to the retained source rather than the destination of proceeds. The clients' claims arise from their legal injuries and remain theirs to settle; the fee satisfies their contractual obligations. Oregon's lien protects counsel's collection without transforming the relationship into a joint venture. Schleier and O'Gilvie require actual statutory treatment of particular receipts and provide no fairness-based exclusion. Congress's prospective deduction addresses a real mismatch while leaving earlier transactions subject to their enacted rules.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Lucas v. Earl and Helvering v. Horst: apply retained-source attribution to ordinary contingent fees. Commissioner v. Schleier and O'Gilvie v. United States: preserve receipt-specific exclusions without extending them to fee attribution.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Separately awarded statutory fees and unpreserved alternative tax theories fall outside the question and developed record.

## 23. Howell v. Mississippi

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22, current revision request.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning, scope and remedy.

* **Conditions:** Subject to the stated record limits and any intervening controlling decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Dismiss the writ as improvidently granted because this federal claim was not properly presented or addressed.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Do not adjudicate the constitutional instruction merits or order a new guilt or sentencing proceeding. Leave any independently available state or collateral channel to its own requirements without inventing an exception, deadline extension or entitlement.
* **Remedial Reach:** Howell's present Supreme Court review. The dismissal does not affirm the constitutional adequacy of the instructions.
* **Practical Relief:** The Court's review ends and the existing state judgment remains operative subject to lawful independent proceedings. No new hearing, resentencing, stay or execution authorization is created by this threshold disposition.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Meaningful notice without mandatory words

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The state submission and decision show a state evidentiary instruction claim, not an identified federal restriction on the capital verdict; the indirect citation chain does not supply actual presentation.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the federal claim is properly before this Court.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Baldwin's meaningful-notice reasoning to the direct-review context without treating its habeas holding as an already decided §1257 application.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The actual submission is sufficient to resolve this review. One step broader would decide every equivalence or indirect-incorporation case. One step narrower would dismiss without explaining why recognizable factual overlap does not itself identify the federal ground.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A federal claim is not properly presented for this review merely because a state-law argument concerns similar facts and a chain of citations eventually reaches a federal decision; the submission or actual state adjudication must meaningfully identify the federal issue. A constitutional citation is not indispensable, and an understandable description can suffice, but Howell's submission argued the State's evidentiary instruction rule without identifying the distinct federal ground. The writ should be dismissed without a merits ruling.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This state appellate submission and indirect-discovery theory. Properly incorporated material, actual federal adjudication and an otherwise understandable federal claim remain possible routes.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides the properly presented federal instruction claim, courts apply Beck’s requirement of a supported noncapital alternative, subject to Schad’s available-alternative analysis and Hopkins’s actual lesser-included-offense requirement.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Baldwin v. Reese rejects both a magic-words demand and an obligation to hunt elsewhere for an unidentified federal basis. Howell's all-or-nothing description supports his state instruction objection, but the filing did not tell the state court that the Federal Constitution independently constrained the capital verdict. The cited state rule also applies beyond the federal capital setting. The Court need not decide whether perfectly identical standards could establish notice on a different record or whether the presentation requirement is invariably jurisdictional.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Baldwin v. Reese: apply its meaningful-notice principle without enlarging its separate equivalence reservation. Beck v. Alabama: leave substantive operation unchanged because the claim is not reached.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The instruction merits are unavailable in this review. The general identical-standards and jurisdictional-character questions are unnecessary and outside the narrower actual-record ground.
