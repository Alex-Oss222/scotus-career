## Verified rule and date

Under 42 Pa. Cons. Stat. §9711(c)(1)(iii), the Commonwealth must prove aggravating circumstances beyond a reasonable doubt; the defendant must prove mitigating circumstances by a preponderance of the evidence. Paragraph (iv) requires a unanimous jury finding of at least one statutory aggravator and no mitigation, or a unanimous finding that one or more aggravators outweigh the mitigating circumstances, for a death verdict. In other cases the verdict is life imprisonment. Paragraph (v) separately permits the trial court, in its discretion, to discharge a jury when further deliberation would not yield unanimous agreement on sentence; after that discharge, the court must impose life imprisonment. The discretionary discharge decision and the mandatory consequence of discharge remain distinct.

Frey expressly explains that individual jurors may weigh mitigation they find established without unanimous agreement that the mitigating circumstance exists. One juror's assessment of mitigation can prevent the unanimous agreement necessary for death. That state construction applies the scheme to a 1980 penalty trial. The official amendment history identifies the 1978 enactment and subsequent amendments; its listed changes do not alter paragraphs (c)(1)(iii)–(v). In particular, the 1995 amendment of paragraph (c)(2) concerning other sentencing instructions is distinct from the three provisions verified here. The 1995 Rompilla direct appeal expressly identifies §9711(c)(1)(iv) as governing his 1988 penalty verdict. Its compressed reference to a November 1 sentence is used only to establish the statutory subsection, not to replace the separately supported penalty chronology.

The result is date-eligible state sentencing law governing the relevant penalty choice, not a later-created federal constitutional rule. Frey's 1989 interpretation precedes the relevant December 10, 1998 state collateral adjudication and describes the same operative statutory scheme already applicable before Rompilla's trial. The current official page serves to verify text and amendment history. No post-event substantive amendment enters the model.
