## Entering law and preserved statutory issues

The 1990 enactment of section 1367 follows pre-divergence Finley and is available. City of Chicago already distinguishes an original-jurisdiction anchor, the related-claim statutory grant, and lawful discretion to decline. Grupo Dataflux governs continuing-party citizenship, and Caterpillar governs an actual-party dismissal that creates diversity before trial and through judgment. Those decisions do not decide whether an otherwise diverse additional plaintiff's insufficient amount defeats jurisdiction over another plaintiff's independently sufficient claim. Grable supplies no alternative federal-question basis here.

The original named-claim, complete-diversity and same-case predicates are accepted as stated in the certified dispute; they are not supplied by the class verdict, expense, convenience, aggregation of separate claims or a new factual finding. The 2005 class-action legislation supplies no retroactive jurisdictional basis. The two dockets require separate dispositions despite their common interpretive question.

**A1, grant-and-exceptions rule.** When the other requirements of original jurisdiction are satisfied and at least one named plaintiff presents an independently sufficient amount, section 1367(a) authorizes related additional claims within the same Article III case, including claims involving additional parties. Subsection (b) does not exclude Rule 23 class-member claims or claims by the Rule 20 plaintiffs here. Its first category concerns claims by plaintiffs against persons made parties under Rules 14, 19, 20 or 24; the separate plaintiff categories concern persons proposed for joinder under Rule 19 and persons seeking intervention under Rule 24, when the exercise would be inconsistent with section 1332. The omitted categories cannot be inserted by judicial interpretation. This supersedes Zahn's and Clark's separate-amount requirements in the covered category, without authorizing aggregation to manufacture the original sufficient claim or discarding complete diversity.

**A2, original-action prerequisite.** The phrase requiring an action within original jurisdiction carries the established meaning of section 1332, including the separate amount required by Zahn and Clark. Section 1367(a) adds related-party power once that predicate exists; it does not itself redefine the predicate. The express additional-party sentence changes Finley's result, and subsection (b) prevents circumvention through otherwise supplemental additions, but neither requires treating the omission of Rule 20 plaintiffs or Rule 23 members as a new affirmative grant over their deficient claims. This reading preserves both earlier amount decisions. It acknowledges the textual force of A1 and does not falsely state that subsection (b) expressly lists either omitted category.

Under either construction, section 1367(c) permits declining otherwise authorized jurisdiction when a claim presents novel or complex state law, substantially predominates, remains after all original-jurisdiction claims are dismissed, or presents exceptional circumstances with other compelling reasons. Power does not compel retention, and discretion cannot supply missing power. No general rule concerning unrelated claims, nondiverse opposing parties or every procedural joinder is decided.
