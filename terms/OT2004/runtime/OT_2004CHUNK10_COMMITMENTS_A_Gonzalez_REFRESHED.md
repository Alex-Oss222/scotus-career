## Supported facts, assumptions and material distinctions

Gonzalez raised a tolling objection in the district court, including *Lovasz*. After a certificate application, an earlier appeal was dismissed on October 28, 1999 and returned for identification of appealable issues. He made a new certificate application and timely pursued its denial. The court of appeals denied a certificate and dismissed that appeal on April 6, 2000. He alleges a timely April 18 rehearing submission returned unfiled with information that his case had been dismissed and closed on October 28, 1999. The State attributes the return to use of the earlier appeal's case number. Gonzalez alleges that the information caused him to think the further-review period had expired. No timely certiorari filing is established. These are distinct facts and contested allegations, not proof of either an official error or a total absence of pursuit.

*Artuz* became effective November 7, 2000. The Rule 60 motion followed about eight to nine months later. That later interval requires separate evaluation: a supported explanation for the April rehearing episode does not automatically explain it, while that interval cannot prove that the April attempt never occurred. Incarceration and self-representation bear on reasonable pursuit but do not alone resolve diligence or extraordinary circumstances. The underlying plea allegation remains unadjudicated; neither its seriousness nor its existence establishes entitlement to a writ.

No Associate resolves the disputed case-number, clerk-information, timeliness, causation or correction-opportunity questions as historical fact in this handoff. The denial path below assumes the movant's asserted review episode and resulting misunderstanding for purposes of testing its independent sufficient ground. The remand path treats that episode as materially disputable and directs ordinary adjudication without presuming either side's account true.

## G1 — Classification remains a direct application of current law

All eight Associates directly join the rule and application from the original G1 commitment. Under *Abdur'Rahman* (December 10, 2002), a motion confined to the federal limitations barrier preventing adjudication of an already pleaded claim is not a second or successive application merely because reopening could eventually lead to custody relief. The supplied motion contains no new custody claim, factual merits supplement or request to replace a federal constitutional-merits determination. Section 2244(b) therefore does not require prior authorization for this process-only motion. A genuinely mixed motion would require separation of procedural correction from new or renewed merits demands, and independent certificate requirements retain their own work.

Florida's contrary eventual-relief argument would erase the controlling classification distinction. Scalia and Thomas preserve the distinction between this pure limitations motion and the express renewed merits and cumulative prayer their *Abdur'Rahman* writing opposed. O'Connor and Kennedy preserve the possibility of genuine procedural relief recognized in their prior writing. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer apply the process rule they directly joined. The new review chronology affects Rule 60 relief, not the substance of the present motion. No Associate proposes changing G1.

## G2 — Updated competing remedial paths

**G2-A — Deny extraordinary relief even on the assumed account.** Assume for this analysis that Gonzalez submitted the claimed timely rehearing paper, received the described returned-paper information and consequently misunderstood the remaining ordinary-review opportunity. That assumption defeats a categorical finding of simple abandonment. It nevertheless does not establish that the intervening procedural legal clarification, the initial nonmerits judgment and the later course of pursuit together meet Rule 60(b)(6)'s exceptional standard. The April episode concerns review before *Artuz*. The supplied account identifies no further circumstance explaining why the post-*Artuz* reopening request followed eight to nine months later. His custodial and unrepresented circumstances are considered rather than ignored, but do not themselves demonstrate reasonably sustained pursuit of this new reopening ground. The combination shown is insufficient; affirm denial on Rule 60 grounds while rejecting the circuit's categorical successive classification.

This path does not decide that eight or nine months always defeats reasonable timing, that a legal change never matters, or that the assumed official episode was harmless. It finds no established connection between that earlier assumed impediment and the later pursuit sufficient to justify reopening this completed judgment. The strongest counterargument is that categorical misclassification prevented ordinary consideration of a concrete allegation of obstructed review and that the later interval needs contextual assessment. G2-A answers that the motion still fails on the total showing even accepting the disputed account, so credibility resolution would not change the result on that sufficient ground.

**G2-B — Vacate and remand for ordinary discretionary adjudication.** The concrete asserted attempt to obtain rehearing and alleged official information may materially alter the Rule 60 assessment of omitted review and the original denial of any merits opportunity. *Abdur'Rahman* requires that assessment separately from classification. A categorical successive bar cannot stand in for it. Return the motion for consideration of the whole course of pursuit, resolving the specific disputed episode if material to the ruling, and separately examining the eight-to-nine-month interval after *Artuz*. The court may deny relief if the complete showing remains insufficient. The mandate grants neither automatic reopening nor a writ and prescribes no unsupported mandatory evidentiary procedure.

The strongest objection is that no factfinding is needed if the motion fails even assuming the asserted episode. G2-B rejects treating the later interval as an independently decisive premise on this supplied record, where no categorical monthly rule governs and the procedural barrier may have prevented the necessary individualized exercise of discretion. That is not a finding that the interval was excused. The alleged official episode, existing earlier efforts, procedural legal change, first-merits opportunity, incarceration and self-representation must be evaluated with the later delay and finality under the actual extraordinary-circumstances standard.

*Pace* (April 27, 2005) requires reasonable pursuit in actual circumstances; it supplies neither a fixed delay bar nor the first petition's tolling result. Its preserved *Pliler* protection concerns proved court-caused loss with causation, diligence and a lawful remedy. The alleged returned-paper episode cannot establish those predicates merely by assertion, and need not be forced into *Pliler*'s particular dismissal-election setting to be relevant to ordinary Rule 60 discretion. *Rhines* governs pending petitions rather than this completed judgment and imposes no retroactive protective-filing duty. *Lovasz* establishes an argument Gonzalez made, not binding Supreme Court law or automatic certificate entitlement.
