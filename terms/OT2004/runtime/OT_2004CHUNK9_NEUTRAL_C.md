## 86. San Remo Hotel, L.P. v. City and County of San Francisco

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** San Remo Hotel, L.P. v. City and County of San Francisco
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-340; 545 U.S. 323
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-20 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether federal courts must create an exception to 28 U.S.C. §1738 for issues decided in state takings litigation pursued to satisfy the state-compensation requirement for a federal takings claim.
* **Question Granted:** Whether federal courts must create an exception to 28 U.S.C. §1738 for issues decided in state takings litigation pursued to satisfy the state-compensation requirement for a federal takings claim.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed application of issue preclusion to the owners' federal takings litigation after the California courts rejected their challenge to the hotel-conversion requirements.
* **Relief Requested:** The hotel owners seek a federal adjudication unencumbered by the state court's resolution of overlapping issues. The City seeks affirmance giving the state judgment its ordinary California preclusive effect.
* **Material Record:** The owners sought to convert residential hotel rooms to tourist use. San Francisco required compliance with its residential hotel conversion ordinance, including a substantial mitigation payment. The initial federal litigation encountered abstention and the then-governing requirement to seek state compensation. The owners pursued state-law takings claims and attempted to reserve federal claims. The California courts decided issues overlapping the later federal theory. The dispute concerns whether that procedural route creates a categorical immunity from preclusion, not whether every possible federal element was necessarily decided by the state court.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The grant concerns a categorical exception to §1738. The application of California preclusion law and the state court's treatment of overlapping takings issues are not separately granted. A reserved federal cause differs from an actually litigated common issue. Wholesale reconsideration of the state-compensation prerequisite is outside review.
* **Relevant Dates:** The state litigation concluded before the renewed federal suit and the Ninth Circuit's 2004 judgment. The earlier Dodd v. Hood River County became effective November 19, 1998 and directly addresses the effect of reserving a federal claim on a necessarily litigated state factual issue.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Any effective same-term takings decision, including Lingle v. Chevron U.S.A. Inc., would require examining whether it changes the identity of an issue actually decided. No earlier decision is effective in the current baseline, and an anticipated doctrinal change cannot be assumed.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Potential dependence on an effective earlier same-term takings decision concerns issue identity and substantive doctrine, not a presently established exception to §1738.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Section 1738 requires the federal court to give a state judgment the effect it would receive in the rendering State. England v. Louisiana State Board of Medical Examiners protects a properly reserved federal claim in its abstention setting but does not automatically erase the consequences of deciding identical issues in state litigation.

##### Issue 1: Preclusion after required state compensation litigation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Dodd v. Hood River County holds that reserving a federal cause does not exempt an identical factual issue actually and necessarily litigated with a full and fair opportunity from lawful state issue preclusion. Its rule is issue-specific: a binding fact does not automatically extinguish every federal element or distinct legal assessment. No controlling decision creates a takings exception to §1738.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing rules preserve the difference between claim reservation and issue preclusion, and require the rendering State's actual preclusion conditions. England does not supply a unilateral license to contradict a necessary state finding in separate compensation litigation.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1738 supplies the federal recognition obligation. The Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments supply the underlying compensation claim. Williamson County Regional Planning Commission v. Hamilton Bank's entering state-compensation rule explains the procedural route but contains no enacted exception to §1738.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Dodd answers the core reservation argument for necessarily litigated facts. The present grant asks whether compulsory state litigation warrants a categorical takings exception; the lower courts' particular application of California preclusion law remains outside that grant.
* **Open Point:** Whether required resort to state compensation proceedings creates the requested categorical exception despite §1738's recognition command.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Preclusion after required state compensation litigation

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The Ninth Circuit gave preclusive effect to state determinations that it regarded as identical to those required by the federal claim and rejected a special takings exception.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** The owners argue that requiring state litigation and then treating its result as preclusive denies the federal forum that the ripeness rule appeared to postpone. They invoke their attempted reservation and seek an exception protecting access to federal adjudication.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** The City argues that §1738 applies to constitutional claims absent an applicable exception, that the owners actually litigated the relevant matters, and that a reservation cannot dictate the effect of a state judgment.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** Preclusion requires the actual state-law elements; neither a takings label nor participation in state court establishes them automatically. The owners' concern about the forum consequence is real, but it does not itself identify statutory authority to disregard a judgment.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** No distinct material proposal is verified in the sources identified here.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** The grant concerns a categorical exception to §1738. The application of California preclusion law and the state court's treatment of overlapping takings issues are not separately granted. A reserved federal cause differs from an actually litigated common issue. Wholesale reconsideration of the state-compensation prerequisite is outside review.

##### Issue 1: Preclusion after required state compensation litigation

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Affirm application of §1738 if the identified issues meet California's requirements; remand any materially distinct issue wrongly treated as resolved; or adopt a takings-specific exception and restore federal litigation, expressly explaining the legal basis and departure from Dodd. Reconsidering the ripeness rule is a separate path requiring a properly presented issue, not an unstated remedy here.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court can reject a categorical exemption while insisting on issue-by-issue analysis. It need not establish that every state compensation action bars every federal takings claim.

* **Record, Source, Posture, and Legal Uncertainty:** The limited grant does not reopen the state-law preclusion determinations. A genuinely intervening decision changing a dispositive issue would require separate analysis; none is effective in the current baseline. The mitigation payment does not itself establish a taking.

## 88. Rompilla v. Beard

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Rompilla v. Beard
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-5462; 545 U.S. 374
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-20 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether capital counsel rendered ineffective assistance by failing to examine the readily available file of a prior conviction they knew the prosecution would use in aggravation, and whether that omission prejudiced sentencing under the applicable habeas restrictions.
* **Question Granted:** Whether capital counsel rendered ineffective assistance by failing to examine the readily available file of a prior conviction they knew the prosecution would use in aggravation, and whether that omission prejudiced sentencing under the applicable habeas restrictions.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit reversed the district court's grant of sentencing habeas relief and sustained the state court's rejection of the ineffective-assistance claim.
* **Relief Requested:** Rompilla seeks restoration of conditional sentence relief and a lawful new penalty proceeding. Pennsylvania seeks affirmance on reasonable investigation, lack of prejudice and deference to the state adjudication.
* **Material Record:** Counsel knew the prosecution intended to use Rompilla's prior rape and assault conviction and details from its record in seeking death. The file was accessible at the courthouse, but defense counsel did not examine it adequately before sentencing. Counsel had interviewed Rompilla and family members and obtained mental-health examinations; those sources provided limited useful mitigation. The overlooked file contained leads to school, prison, psychological and family-history information, including severe childhood mistreatment, substance abuse and possible mental impairment. Postconviction investigation developed evidence not presented to the sentencing jury. The inquiry concerns the professional reasonableness of stopping before examining this known aggravation source and the cumulative effect of the omitted mitigation.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Section 2254(d)(1) requires an objectively unreasonable application of Supreme Court law established when Pennsylvania adjudicated performance. Wiggins illustrates Strickland's existing investigation duty but supplies no retroactively established rule. The state court did not reach prejudice, so that component receives independent review after the statutory gate is satisfied. State factual findings retain their separate presumption under §2254(e)(1). Relief concerns sentencing alone.
* **Relevant Dates:** The capital trial occurred in 1988. The relevant state postconviction adjudication preceded the 2003 Wiggins decision; the federal application is governed by the Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996 (AEDPA). The Third Circuit's reviewed decision issued in 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material beyond the authorities addressed below.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Strickland requires deficient performance and a reasonable probability of a different lawful result. Section 2254(d)(1) limits relief from an adjudicated state claim; factual presumptions retain their separate statutory role. Professional guidelines inform prevailing practice but are not freestanding constitutional commands.

##### Issue 1: Reasonable investigation of a known aggravation file

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Strickland requires reasonable investigation or an adequately informed decision that further investigation is unnecessary. Wiggins v. Smith rejects characterizing an uninformed stopping point as strategy and requires examination of the actual available leads. Terry Williams v. Taylor and Woodford v. Visciotti preserve the difference between unreasonable application and mere disagreement with a state judgment.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Investigation is assessed in context, without hindsight or a universal checklist. Known readily available information central to the prosecution's penalty case is materially different from an unbounded search for every conceivable mitigating record.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments and 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1) govern. The professional standards relevant to the trial period are evidence of reasonable practice, not later mandatory rules retroactively imposed.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The duty to make an informed investigation decision is settled. Whether the known prior-case file required examination is an application of that duty to a concrete, accessible source.
* **Open Point:** Whether the state court unreasonably applied Strickland in treating counsel's other efforts and the client's discouraging responses as sufficient reason not to inspect this file.

##### Issue 2: Cumulative penalty prejudice and conditional relief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Wiggins v. Smith requires weighing the omitted mitigation together with the aggravation and the evidence actually presented. A reasonable probability sufficient to undermine confidence is not a more-likely-than-not requirement, and lawful death eligibility does not itself answer individualized selection prejudice.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Assess the complete supported aggravation and mitigation, including what reasonable investigation would have revealed. An unadjudicated prejudice component receives independent Strickland review; §2254(d)(1) still governs the state court's adjudicated performance ground.
* **Other Governing Law:** Strickland and the applicable habeas provisions govern prejudice and relief. State capital sentencing law supplies the lawful alternative penalty and decision process.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The confidence standard is settled. Its application requires the actual omitted evidence and the entire aggravation case, not a presumption that any difficult childhood evidence changes a death verdict.
* **Open Point:** Whether the developed postconviction mitigation creates the required probability of a different penalty decision.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Reasonable investigation of a known aggravation file

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The Third Circuit emphasized the interviews and expert evaluations counsel had undertaken and concluded that the state court could reasonably find the investigation adequate despite the unexamined file.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** Rompilla argues that competent preparation required examining the very record the prosecutor announced would be used to secure death; doing so would also have revealed substantial mitigation leads.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** Pennsylvania argues that counsel pursued multiple sources, received unhelpful responses, and reasonably relied on family interviews and professional examinations. It invokes the deference due both counsel's choices and the state court's judgment.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** Counsel need not inspect every record in every capital case. The State cannot turn lack of cooperation into an automatic excuse for ignoring a readily available source known to be central to aggravation.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** No distinct material proposal is verified in the sources identified here.

##### Issue 2: Cumulative penalty prejudice and conditional relief

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The appellate rejection of relief relied on its favorable view of the investigation and disputed the basis for disturbing the state disposition. The omitted evidence must nevertheless receive a separate cumulative prejudice assessment.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** Rompilla argues that the records would have transformed a sparse mitigation case by exposing severe deprivation and impairment, giving the jury a materially different understanding of his life and responsibility.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** Pennsylvania emphasizes the serious aggravation, the potentially damaging aspects of the omitted material and the need to show a constitutionally significant effect rather than merely additional information.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** Mitigation is not an excuse erasing the crime. Prejudice does not require proof that every juror would reject death or that the aggravating evidence disappears.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** No distinct material proposal is verified in the sources identified here.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** AEDPA requires more than a federal judge's disagreement with the state court. The state adjudication must be tested against clearly established law at its relevant date. Wiggins explains the operating Strickland principles but cannot be treated as law already established when the state court ruled. Guilt and penalty are separate; this claim does not require vacating the conviction.

##### Issue 1: Reasonable investigation of a known aggravation file

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Find an unreasonable application of Strickland and proceed to prejudice; sustain the state ruling if the omitted inquiry was reasonably unnecessary in the actual circumstances; or remand only if material facts remain unresolved. A performance violation alone does not require a new penalty trial.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A context-sensitive duty to examine a known, accessible prior-conviction file that the State intends to use, without a universal record-collection checklist.

##### Issue 2: Cumulative penalty prejudice and conditional relief

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Grant conditional sentencing relief if confidence is undermined; deny if the cumulative lawful case does not meet that standard; or order a focused factual proceeding only if the necessary evidence remains unresolved. Conviction relief is not the remedy for this penalty-specific omission.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court can decide this complete mitigation record while preserving individualized assessment in other cases.

* **Record, Source, Posture, and Legal Uncertainty:** The exact resentencing procedure and lawful nondeath alternative are matters for the state court under governing law. The federal judgment must not prescribe an unsupported medical diagnosis or declare Rompilla ineligible for death on a claim not adjudicated here.

## 90. American Trucking Associations, Inc. v. Michigan Public Service Commission

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** American Trucking Associations, Inc. v. Michigan Public Service Commission
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-1230; 545 U.S. 429
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-20 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Michigan's flat annual $100 per-truck fee for engaging in intrastate commercial hauling discriminates against or impermissibly burdens interstate commerce.
* **Question Granted:** Whether Michigan's flat annual $100 per-truck fee for engaging in intrastate commercial hauling discriminates against or impermissibly burdens interstate commerce.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Michigan Court of Appeals sustained the fee. It rejected the proposition that a local-activity label alone removes Commerce Clause scrutiny, but found evenhanded regulation and no evidence establishing the asserted discriminatory burden.
* **Relief Requested:** The carriers seek invalidation of the fee and the appropriate consequences for its collection. Michigan seeks affirmance of its authority to charge the same local regulatory fee to all trucks undertaking point-to-point Michigan hauls.
* **Material Record:** Section 478.2(1) charges $100 annually for each truck engaged in intrastate commercial operations. A carrier that also hauls interstate freight pays the same amount as a wholly local carrier when it undertakes covered local hauling. Petitioners argue that mixed-operation trucks perform fewer local hauls and therefore pay more per local mile. The record identifies regulation of size, weight, insurance and safety, but contains no developed showing that the fee changes interstate routing, imposes a discriminatory practical burden or requires a particular mileage-based alternative.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The local nature of the assessed activity does not immunize the fee from constitutional scrutiny. The challenge must nevertheless be evaluated on the actual taxable or regulatory event and record rather than a hypothetical tax on interstate travel itself. The distinct fee litigated in Mid-Con is not this subsection's charge.
* **Relevant Dates:** The Michigan Court of Appeals judgment issued in 2003. The operative fee and record are those reviewed at the June 20, 2005 event; no later registration scheme is substituted.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Mid-Con Freight Systems, Inc. v. Michigan Public Service Commission concerns §478.2(2), a separate $100 fee on Michigan-plated trucks operating entirely interstate, and a federal registration-preemption claim. This case concerns local hauls under §478.2(1) and the Commerce Clause.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No same-term decision in Mid-Con is effective in the current baseline. Its separate statute and preemption theory cannot decide this constitutional challenge by assumption.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Commerce Clause protects interstate trade against discriminatory state burdens while preserving evenhanded state regulation and taxation of local activity. Complete Auto's requirements and ordinary burden analysis concern the actual operation of the charge, not its label.

##### Issue 1: Evenhanded local charges and practical interstate burden

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Jefferson Lines permits taxation of a distinct completed local transaction without mileage allocation where the actual event and practical burden satisfy the independent Commerce Clause requirements. Equal facial rates alone are not invariably sufficient. American Trucking Associations v. Scheiner addresses discriminatory flat charges on interstate highway use and must be compared to the activity charged here, not treated as invalidating every flat fee.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing rules preserve separate nexus, apportionment, nondiscrimination and fair-relation inquiries. Internal consistency asks what identical application by every State would tax; external consistency concerns the activity properly attributed to the taxing State. General governmental services can supply the required relation without exact cost reimbursement.
* **Other Governing Law:** Michigan Compiled Laws §478.2(1) imposes the challenged annual per-truck local-hauling fee. The Commerce Clause supplies the constitutional limitation; the distinct federal single-state-registration ceiling is not the granted ground in this docket.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** A genuine local business event can support an evenhanded fee, but a nominally local charge may still discriminate in operation. The factual burden asserted here must be proved rather than inferred from a per-mile quotient alone.
* **Open Point:** Whether the same flat local-hauling charge imposes the discriminatory or excessive interstate burden alleged on this record.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Evenhanded local charges and practical interstate burden

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The state appellate court found evenhanded regulation and no evidence that route choices or interstate operations were materially affected. It did not adopt the trial court's categorical exemption from Commerce Clause review.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** The carriers argue that trucks dividing their business among States obtain fewer local miles for the same fee, and that identical annual charges in many States cumulatively disadvantage interstate firms.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** Michigan argues that each fee regulates a distinct local-hauling operation, applies regardless of domicile, and supports per-truck or per-carrier regulatory tasks that need not vary with mileage. Firms conducting local business in several States undertake several locally regulated activities.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** A nominally evenhanded fee cannot conceal protectionism. The constitutional inquiry also does not guarantee every carrier the same fee per mile when the charge is for participation in a locally regulated activity rather than highway mileage.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** No distinct material proposal is verified in the sources identified here.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** The local nature of the assessed activity does not immunize the fee from constitutional scrutiny. The challenge must nevertheless be evaluated on the actual taxable or regulatory event and record rather than a hypothetical tax on interstate travel itself. The distinct fee litigated in Mid-Con is not this subsection's charge.

##### Issue 1: Evenhanded local charges and practical interstate burden

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Affirm on the actual local-event and evidentiary record; invalidate if proved practical discrimination or excessive burden defeats the governing requirements; or remand for a genuinely unresolved supported factual issue. Unsupported assumptions about every State charging for mere passage cannot decide a fee imposed on local hauls.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A record-based holding may sustain evenhanded local regulatory participation fees without immunizing flat highway-use taxes or charges on out-of-state activity.

* **Record, Source, Posture, and Legal Uncertainty:** The asserted practical discrimination must be proved on the record; the fee's relation to regulation does not require exactly $100 of services for each truck. No precise refund amount or alternative fee formula has been established.
