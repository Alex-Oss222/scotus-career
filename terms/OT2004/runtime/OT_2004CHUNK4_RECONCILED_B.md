## Cherokee Nation of Oklahoma v. Leavitt — March 1, 2005

### Reconciliation and fixed components

The historical judgment and the frozen commitments agree on both dockets. History supplies strong confirming evidence for each Associate: the eight historical participants all enforced the promises, reversed No. 02-1472 and affirmed No. 03-853. The original premise remains intact—actual promises backed by sufficient legally unrestricted appropriations, followed by nonpayment based on allocation and asserted statutory defenses. The governing Winstar rule in this timeline is the actual-authorized-undertaking and allocated-risk holding, not its noncontrolling complete unmistakability or substantial-contractual-effect theories. The present agreement therefore does not import a historical plurality rationale as current law.

All eight Associates support the following five components, at the same operative level of generality:

1. **Authorized promise and availability.** An authorized self-determination agreement promising reasonable, allowable contract support costs is binding when the relevant appropriation legally supplies sufficient funds for that undertaking. The availability proviso in then-25 U.S.C. §450j-1(b), the incorporated agreement's availability condition, and its adjustment language for congressional action remain real conditions. They do not authorize the agency to cancel an otherwise binding promise through its own allocation of funds. Congress supplied the relevant FY1994–1997 lump sums; administrative totals and competing demands do not establish a contrary enacted limitation. Hercules supplies the actual-duty and authority requirement; Winstar requires construction of the actual promise and conditions; Lincoln distinguishes administrative discretion from an enforceable statutory or contractual boundary.
2. **Separate no-required-reduction proviso.** The Secretary is not required to reduce funding for programs, projects or activities serving one tribe to make funds available to another tribe or tribal organization. That permission does not become either an absolute prohibition on reductions or cancellation of contract liability. Other legally unrestricted funds sufficient for these claims defeat the Government's asserted defense. Designating those funds for inherent federal functions is an administrative allocation, not an enacted earmark. Recognition of contractual responsibility supplies a contract remedy and does not direct redistribution of another tribe's program funding. A restriction on contracting out an inherently federal function is also distinct from the source used to pay a lawfully contracted activity.
3. **Initial and expanded programs and the $7.5 million fund.** The annual direction that $7.5 million shall remain available until expended for the Indian Self-Determination Fund's transitional costs of initial or expanded contracts identifies a fund with continuing availability. It does not make that fund the exclusive source for these support-cost promises or turn the agency's initial/expanded allocation into a contractual-liability ceiling. This textual issue is distinct from committee-report allocations for total support costs and from the sufficiency of the overall legally available appropriation. Both initial/expanded claims and Cherokee's ongoing-program claims receive the construction applicable to their own terms. The explicit overall not-to-exceed language beginning in FY1998 is not an original condition of the FY1994–1997 promises now reviewed.
4. **Section 314 and accrued claims.** Pub. L. 105-277, §314, 112 Stat. 2681-288, makes the amounts appropriated or earmarked in committee reports the total amounts available for the identified support costs in FY1994–1998. In this setting it limits use of unspent earlier appropriations; it does not extinguish the contractual claims that had accrued under the earlier undertakings. Its language can perform that account-limiting function without retroactively repudiating those obligations. The contrary repudiation reading raises serious constitutional concerns under Lynch and Perry; the adopted construction resolves the statutory defense without holding §314 unconstitutional. The Government's clarification theory fails because the original unambiguous lump sums did not already incorporate report allocations as legal ceilings. The agreements' congressional-adjustment clauses operate with §314's actual effect and do not independently turn it into rescission of accrued liabilities.
5. **Separate judgments and ensuing work.** In No. 02-1472, reverse the Tenth Circuit's judgment for the Government and remand for adjudication of Cherokee's FY1997 and Shoshone-Paiute's FY1996–1997 contract claims under these constructions. In No. 03-853, affirm the Federal Circuit judgment sustaining the Board's determination for Cherokee's FY1994–1996 claims; the Board determination thereby affirmed remains effective. Both proceedings return for the further proceedings their actual postures require. Ordinary proof of an unresolved annual amount, reasonable and allowable costs, nonduplication, causation or recoverable loss applies where genuinely remaining; it does not reopen a determination already sustained in No. 03-853. The frozen reference to remaining computation is confined accordingly. No special evidentiary trial is prescribed.

The Government's strongest contrary path combines the no-reduction proviso, the $7.5 million designation, the adjustment clauses and §314 into an aggregate limit incorporated in each promise. It fails for the distinct reasons in components 1–4: permission not to reduce is not rescission; a continuing-availability fund is not an exclusive source; the actual adjustment condition does not expand the later enactment; and the original allocation reports did not establish statutory caps. These are independent answers to the actual defenses, not a general rule that appropriation conditions never matter. The tribes' governmental relationship and statutory exclusion from procurement regulation do not remove the enacted contract and damages remedy or make the tribes bear the agency's allocation risk.

## Petro-Hunt, L.L.C. v. United States — March 1, 2005

### Reconciliation and fixed components

There is no historical Supreme Court merits vote or writing to reconcile. The comparator is the Fifth Circuit's revised judgment, not an implied decision by any Associate. The proper-arrival premise supplies the present merits posture. The clean neutral validation and frozen commitments preserve the lower judgment accurately: the 95 distinct servitudes are subject to ten-year nonuse prescription, and remand determines which actually prescribed. Neither appellate reversal of preclusion nor affirmance here establishes universal nonuse.

All eight Associates support these three components:

1. **Claim identity and the original decree.** Federal common law governs this federal-question judgment's preclusive effect. A completed title adjudication binds the same claim, including grounds that could have been advanced within that claim, but related conveyances, common owners and a common legal premise do not make distinct servitudes with different acquisition and use facts one claim. Nebo Oil's operative decree covers the servitude on the approximately 800 acres described in its complaint. Its broader findings do not extend that adjudication to the other 95 servitudes arising from the separate conveyances and acquisitions. The United States remains bound for the adjudicated servitude. Nevada v. United States supplies the same-property-right inquiry; Semtek requires attention to the source and true effect of the judgment without extending its state-law incorporation rule for diversity judgments to this federal-question case.
2. **Actual litigation of the issue.** Distinct claims can share a precluded issue, but mutual issue preclusion requires the identical issue actually litigated and necessarily determined. Nebo Oil addressed state-law retroactivity and constitutional objections while assuming Louisiana law's applicability; it did not actually adjudicate the federal choice-of-law issue now raised. Sunnen and Montana supply that issue-specific inquiry. Stauffer protects mutual issue preclusion where its predicates are satisfied; it does not transform an assumed rule of decision into an issue actually litigated. Claim nonidentity and failure of actual issue litigation are separate sufficient answers to their respective preclusion theories.
3. **Judgment and remand.** Affirm the reviewed Fifth Circuit judgment. Its determination that the other 95 servitudes are subject to Louisiana's ten-year nonuse prescription remains effective. The district court must determine actual prescription for each servitude from its governing acquisition and use history, including qualifying good-faith operations and interruptions, address the second summary-judgment motion previously dismissed as moot, and determine title from that application. The reported continued operations on five servitudes and the Government's concessions concerning some operations require their proper consideration; they are not a finding resolving every servitude. Nebo Oil's own adjudicated title remains intact. The fee cross-appeal has no present prevailing-party predicate after the blanket preclusion judgment falls; any later fee issue follows the eventual proper judgment.

The strongest alternative is actual common-issue preclusion despite distinct claims. Its legal premise is accepted, but its factual predicate fails: state retroactivity and constitutional adjudication did not actually decide federal choice of law. The Government's alternative changed-law route under Little Lake Misere therefore need not supply an additional holding. An exception for an unmixed legal issue in unrelated claims and an exception for materially changed governing law are distinct; neither is substituted for the actual-litigation inquiry. The second granted question about an otherwise preclusive issue is not reached because the asserted issue was not preclusive in the first place. The original final decree is not reopened. The prescription work is factual application on remand under a settled governing rule, not unresolved law.

## Tenet v. Doe — March 2, 2005

### Reconciliation and fixed components

The historical judgment and all eight frozen commitments agree that Totten bars the surviving agreement-dependent claims. The historical Court opinion was joined by every Associate. Stevens, joined by Ginsburg, also characterized the rule as modifiable federal common law and described its application as a merits resolution before the arguably antecedent jurisdictional question. Scalia joined the Court while rejecting that characterization, treating the particular Totten bar as jurisdictional and alternatively invoking the frivolous-claim basis. Those additional characterizations are distinct from their common agreement on Totten's categorical foreclosure of inquiry. They are not automatically required writings in this case.

The current prior public Steel Co. decision, June 2, 1997, forbids assumed Article III power to decide the contested EPCRA merits and expressly preserves legitimate nonmerits threshold sequences. O'Connor and Kennedy reserve the nonexhaustive nature of those sequences. Stevens and Souter's statutory-threshold position and Ginsburg's narrower statutory-authorization ground do not establish a general assumed-jurisdiction rule. The current unanimous Ruhrgas decision, May 17, 1999, permits the bounded straightforward-personal-jurisdiction-first route while retaining the prohibition on contested merits without jurisdiction. These actual positions, rather than the historical Steel Co. lineup, govern the comparison. They support treating Totten here honestly as a threshold prohibition on undertaking the inquiry; they do not turn every easy cause-of-action dismissal into a nonmerits threshold.

All eight Associates support these four components:

1. **The agreement-dependent category.** Totten bars a suit when success on the pleaded right and requested relief necessarily requires establishing a secret espionage relationship and enforcing the alleged undertaking arising from it. Estoppel and constitutional labels do not remove that necessary premise. Here the asserted entitlement to continuing support and to internal review derives from the alleged secret bargain; no independent public legal duty supplying the requested entitlement is established. The allegations of recruitment, service, promises, defection and support remain allegations, and the Government's defense does not confirm the relationship.
2. **The proposed procedures do not avoid the bar.** A judicial order directing interim monthly support, fair administrative review or regulations would still require recognition of the entitlement said to arise from the secret relationship. Pseudonyms, restrained initial disclosure and internal hearings do not remove that prerequisite. Reynolds governs an evidentiary privilege in an otherwise maintainable suit and expressly preserves Totten's dismissal on the pleadings. The Director's formal personal invocation required for the evidentiary privilege is therefore not a condition of this distinct categorical bar. Webster's acknowledged employment relationship and independent constitutional challenge to the statutory employment decision are materially different; an identity kept confidential is not an unacknowledged relationship whose very existence the suit must establish.
3. **Threshold character at the scope actually adopted.** The Totten ground prevents judicial inquiry into this category of action; it is not merely a conclusion that the plaintiffs lose an ordinary contested merits issue after jurisdiction is assumed. The Court may apply that threshold bar without conducting the inquiry into the alleged relationship that the bar forbids, or resolving the possible Tucker Act channel issue first. This limited order is consistent with Steel Co.'s preserved nonmerits distinction and Ruhrgas's recognition that thresholds have no universal hierarchy. It does not require classifying every application of Totten as absence of subject-matter jurisdiction. No common proposition declares that ordinary merits may precede jurisdiction, treats the Tucker Act objection as affirmatively resolved, or adopts Scalia's alternative frivolity characterization.
4. **Disposition and implementation.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit judgment sustaining continuation of the surviving agreement-dependent estoppel and due-process claims, and return the action for their dismissal. The mandate requires that dismissal instead of the proposed Reynolds inquiry, judicially ordered support, review procedure or regulations on these claims. The already dismissed equal-protection branch and denial of preliminary relief are undisturbed. The historical formal disposition was simply reversed; the frozen commitment's express remand instruction states the consequence of the present ruling rather than purporting to quote a historical remand clause.

The strongest competing route treats Totten as only a contract-enforcement rule and allows confidential administrative-process litigation under Reynolds and Webster. The objection is serious because the relief includes process rather than only damages. It nevertheless fails because the asserted right to that process is itself agreement-dependent; no separate legal source permits the Court to recognize the demanded entitlement while avoiding the relationship. The Court therefore resolves all the surviving forms of relief presented. No additional administrative benefits channel is established by the Government's mention of an opportunity to complain.
