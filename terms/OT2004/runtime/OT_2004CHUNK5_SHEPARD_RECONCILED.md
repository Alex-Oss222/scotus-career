## Historical comparison and intact statutory disagreement

Historically, the Court reversed and remanded by five to three among eight participating Justices. Souter's statutory opinion was joined by Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Thomas did not join Part III's constitutional-avoidance discussion and separately regarded the relevant factfinding as unconstitutional, rather than merely doubtful. O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer dissented: they would treat uncontradicted, internally consistent contemporary materials as evidence of the plea's actual shared basis, and regarded the police reports and complaint applications as sufficient on that approach. The replaced Chief's historical nonparticipation is not transferred to this Court.

The frozen five-to-three statutory division remains defensible. Taylor's text-based focus on qualifying convictions, its pragmatic refusal to retry old conduct and its concern about the actual basis of a plea survive here. The five favoring the conviction-record restriction do not manufacture an exclusion merely to secure constitutional relief. They resolve the distinct statutory question about the means by which a broader state conviction can count as generic burglary. Nor do the three favoring the broader evidentiary approach abandon it simply because independent constitutional law requires a bounded remand.

The strongest majority answer to reliable-evidence reasoning remains that a persuasive account of what happened is not necessarily an adjudicated or admitted element of the old conviction. The same investigative material could be entirely consistent with a jury record without having been a finding the jury had to make. Taylor accepted the cost of incomplete state records rather than replacing its conviction inquiry with retrospective factfinding. A charging document, written plea agreement, plea colloquy, explicit finding assented to by the defendant, or comparable judicial record can identify the necessarily admitted generic basis. Merely filing a police report or complaint application does not incorporate it into the plea. Express incorporation, if actually established by an appropriate judicial record, is different from mere presence in the file.

The strongest dissenting answer also remains: Taylor's listed trial records were illustrations of a confined inquiry, and equivalent guilty pleas should not have different federal consequences solely because recordkeeping differs. O'Connor's historical dissent considered the matching complaint, application and report, their consistency, and Massachusetts factual-basis requirements powerful evidence of actual shared understanding; Kennedy and Breyer joined that reasoning. The current neutral record has not disproved that position or transformed those Justices into supporters of an exclusive formal-document taxonomy. Their position remains an inquiry into what the plea actually concerned, not unrestricted redetermination of every historical act. The disputed affidavit and broader charge do not become conceded generic admissions by silence or by source unavailability.

## Changed constitutional and remedial premises

The historical opinions operated against an asserted surviving constitutional prior-conviction exception. Part III treated its boundary as sufficiently uncertain to support statutory avoidance. Thomas argued for reconsideration in an appropriate case. The historical dissent expressly defended the exception and insisted that Apprendi had disclaimed extension to adult recidivism.

That premise is displaced here. Gatewood already subjects necessary adult-recidivist existence, identity, historical qualification and chronology to applicable federal charging and proper factfinding beyond a reasonable doubt, absent valid admission and lawful waiver. It preserves judicial legal classification of established facts. Booker reaffirmed that allocation on January 12, 2005, and retained the binding Guidelines in their lawful applications. The present case cannot be resolved on an assumed unrestricted recidivism exception or an advisory-Guidelines remand.

The five statutory-restriction Justices support that constitutional rule on its merits as their Gatewood joins establish. Their shared rule here is direct application of settled law, not a four-Justice doubt rationale plus Thomas's more categorical position. Thomas can join the full direct-application explanation because it does not suggest that his constitutional objection is only speculative. Statutory qualification and constitutional authorization remain separate: even a permitted conviction document cannot itself supply a missing federal charge or a new disputed punishment-authorizing fact.

O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer retain their actual constitutional disagreement. Their recent Gatewood and Booker dissents are not erased. But their frozen present commitments apply those controlling holdings without seeking their renewed overruling in this review of conviction materials. That is supported by their own Booker treatment of sentence vacatur on the Court's constitutional premise, while preserving their disagreement with the rule. They can maintain their favorable assessment of the historical reports as evidence of shared understanding while recognizing that such an assessment no longer authorizes a judge to impose the fifteen-year floor by the former procedure. No change in the source record or newly imagined concession is needed for this limited remedial position.

**Historical departure:** O'Connor supports setting aside the unconditional enhancement command under Gatewood and Booker while retaining her broader statutory evidence rule and constitutional objection; the historical dissent relied on a surviving recidivism exception, whereas her own recent Booker position accepts sentence relief on the governing constitutional premise without adopting it as correct.

**Historical departure:** Kennedy supports setting aside the unconditional enhancement command because the governing adult-predicate charging and factfinding rule now applies to the disputed ACCA facts; he retains his historical statutory shared-understanding position and his joined Gatewood/Booker constitutional dissents, but his own Booker conditional remedial position supports relief under the Court's existing rule.

**Historical departure:** Breyer supports setting aside the unconditional enhancement command on the governing Gatewood/Booker premise while preserving his broader statutory evidence rule and objection to extending element protections to sentencing; his Booker concurrence in sentence vacatur supplies Justice-specific support for this remedial response without inventing a recidivist-floor exception or an advisory mandate contrary to current law.

The broader governing constitutional rule also changes the historical majority's rationale. Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg need not rely on uncertainty about the recidivism exception, because they already rejected that premise in Gatewood. Thomas's historical insistence on constitutional error now agrees with their existing controlling rule. This is a concrete doctrinal difference, not the importation of a historical plurality as present law.
