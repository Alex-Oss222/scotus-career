## 91. Mid-Con Freight Systems, Inc. v. Michigan Public Service Commission

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Mid-Con Freight Systems, Inc. v. Michigan Public Service Commission
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-1234; 545 U.S. 440
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-20 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the federal Single State Registration System preempts Michigan's separate annual $100 fee on Michigan-plated trucks operating entirely in interstate commerce.
* **Question Granted:** Whether the federal Single State Registration System preempts Michigan's separate annual $100 fee on Michigan-plated trucks operating entirely in interstate commerce.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Michigan Court of Appeals sustained the fee as a charge for administration of the Motor Carrier Act and state safety regulation, outside the federal registration requirements addressed by 49 U.S.C. §14504.
* **Relief Requested:** The carriers seek invalidation of §478.2(2) under the federal registration ceiling and appropriate relief from its collection. Michigan seeks affirmance of its separate regulatory fee.
* **Material Record:** Federal registration requires proof of operating authority, insurance and an agent for service, with a base State collecting participating States' permitted fees. Michigan separately charges $100 annually for each Michigan-plated truck operating entirely interstate. The State requires vehicle-identifying information and a decal for this separate program. It appears to waive its $10 federal-system fee for covered carriers choosing Michigan as their base State. The challenged charge predates Michigan's participation in the earlier federal-permit registration system. The record does not establish that paying it is necessary to complete the federally prescribed registration.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The issue is the scope of express statutory preemption, not whether all fees paid by interstate carriers are constitutional. The identity of the regulated information and legal obligation matters more than Michigan's label or the appearance of a decal. A real linkage that conditions federal registration on paying the additional fee would require separate analysis.
* **Relevant Dates:** Congress replaced the earlier registration system in 1991, with the replacement operating from 1994. Yellow Transportation became effective November 5, 2002. The state judgment under review issued in 2003.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** American Trucking Associations, Inc. v. Michigan Public Service Commission concerns the distinct §478.2(1) fee for local hauls and a Commerce Clause challenge. This docket concerns §478.2(2), Michigan-plated wholly interstate trucks and federal registration preemption.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No same-term decision in American Trucking Associations is effective in the current baseline. Its different fee and constitutional ground cannot be used as an existing resolution here.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Preemption follows Congress's enacted scope. A state regulatory label cannot defeat a federal ceiling that actually applies, but a federal system addressing defined registration subjects does not automatically displace all state carrier regulation.

##### Issue 1: The subject matter of the federal registration ceiling

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan upholds the delegated rule preserving qualifying reciprocity in the federal registration benchmark, subject to the $10 ceiling. It expressly does not freeze unrelated taxes and licensing policies. United States v. Mead Corp. preserves the need for actual delegated force-of-law authority when agency constructions are invoked.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing rules preserve the benchmark and reciprocity limits within the specified registration system, not a universal fee cap for interstate motor carriers. Statutory scope must be established before a ceiling is applied.
* **Other Governing Law:** 49 U.S.C. §14504(b) and (c), as operative at this event, regulate state registration of federal motor-carrier authority and the associated proof, insurance, service-agent and fee requirements. Michigan Compiled Laws §478.2(2) supplies the separate $100 fee. No later replacement registration statute governs by anticipation.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** A State may not add a prohibited charge to covered federal-permit registration. Whether this separate Michigan requirement concerns the same subject is the disputed application.
* **Open Point:** Whether the phrase State registration requirement reaches every state carrier registration or only the defined federal-permit registration system, and whether Michigan's actual program falls within that system.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The subject matter of the federal registration ceiling

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The Michigan appellate court treated the fee as supporting separate safety administration rather than registration of federal authority and therefore found no preemption.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** The carriers argue that registration of Michigan-plated interstate trucks, associated information and decals, and the connection to the $10 waiver make the $100 charge an additional registration burden Congress prohibited.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** Michigan argues that its charge does not demand proof of a federal permit, insurance or a service agent, can operate independently of federal-system compliance, and supports a separate regulatory program predating that system.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** Neither an old enactment date nor a regulatory label is conclusive if the actual charge duplicates or conditions federally covered registration. Conversely, a State's decision to waive a small covered fee does not by itself convert every separate charge into that fee.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** No distinct material proposal is verified in the sources identified here.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** The issue is the scope of express statutory preemption, not whether all fees paid by interstate carriers are constitutional. The identity of the regulated information and legal obligation matters more than Michigan's label or the appearance of a decal. A real linkage that conditions federal registration on paying the additional fee would require separate analysis.

##### Issue 1: The subject matter of the federal registration ceiling

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Affirm if §14504 is confined to the defined registration subject and the record establishes a separate program; reverse if the provision reaches the additional requirement or if the fee is actually a condition of covered registration; remand only for a material unresolved operational linkage. A preemption ruling must specify the displaced obligation and any lawful remaining fee.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A subject-specific construction can enforce the federal ceiling while leaving unrelated state safety and licensing powers subject to their own constitutional and statutory limits.

* **Record, Source, Posture, and Legal Uncertainty:** The disposition depends on the program as demonstrated in the record. If a preserved state rule actually makes federal registration contingent on the extra charge, that rule must be examined rather than ignored. The brief supplies no unsupported finding about precise regulatory costs or refund totals.

## 92. Eulitt v. Maine Department of Education

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Eulitt v. Maine Department of Education
* **Citation or Docket:** 386 F.3d 344 (1st Cir. 2004)
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-20 (scheduled merits date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Maine violates equal protection by excluding sectarian secondary schools from a program that permits publicly funded tuition at approved nonsectarian private schools when local public schools do not meet a student’s educational needs? Whether parents may assert a religious school’s separate equal-protection rights without showing any hindrance to the school’s bringing its own suit?
* **Question Granted:** Whether Maine violates equal protection by excluding sectarian secondary schools from a program that permits publicly funded tuition at approved nonsectarian private schools when local public schools do not meet a student’s educational needs? Whether parents may assert a religious school’s separate equal-protection rights without showing any hindrance to the school’s bringing its own suit?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The First Circuit affirmed summary judgment for Maine on the parents' equal-protection claims, using reasoning different from the district court's reliance on the earlier lower-court Strout decision. It separately addressed the attempted assertion of St. Dominic's rights.
* **Relief Requested:** The parents seek removal of the sectarian-school funding exclusion and public payment for their daughters' schooling at St. Dominic's. Maine seeks affirmance against those claims.
* **Material Record:** Minot operates elementary grades and contracts for at least ninety percent of its high-school students to attend Poland Regional High School. Up to ten percent may attend other approved nonsectarian schools when they demonstrate educational needs the public school cannot meet. The parents pay for St. Dominic's, a Catholic school, and identify Catholic instruction and viewpoint as the unmet need. They did not formally apply because the statutory sectarian exclusion would foreclose approval. The request is for the school's integrated ordinary tuition, not an identified separately controlled secular service. The parents also attempt to assert a distinct equal-protection claim on the school's behalf.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The categorical exclusion can make a formal application futile, but the parents must still show their own concrete injury and the relevance of the ordinary program's eligibility conditions. Their own claim differs from third-party assertion of the school's claim. No obstacle preventing the school from suing on its own behalf is established merely by the parent-school relationship.
* **Relevant Dates:** The First Circuit decision issued October 22, 2004. The June 20, 2005 event is scheduled merits date. Strout v. Albanese became effective February 28, 2000; Zelman and Freedom From Religion Foundation v. McCallum also precede this event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Strout concerns the same kind of ordinary St. Dominic's tuition purchase, but its granted claims were Establishment and Free Exercise, not a final disposition of these different parents' equal-protection claim. Kowalski v. Tesmer concerns attorneys' hypothetical future clients, not these parents' own live claims; no same-term Kowalski decision is effective in the current baseline.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any effective earlier same-term third-party-standing decision must be incorporated. It cannot eliminate the distinction between the parents' own rights and the school's separate rights.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Equal protection must be applied to the actual funding relationship. Agostini's unified governmental-act-and-function inquiry controls Establishment Clause predicates; the historical three-independent-prong approach is not entering law. Article III injury and third-party standing are separate questions.

##### Issue 1: Equal protection and the requested integrated devotional tuition

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Strout v. Albanese permits Maine to refuse the ordinary integrated devotional tuition presented there while preserving eligibility for genuinely severable secular work and rejecting religious-status disqualification alone. Zelman v. Simmons-Harris and Freedom From Religion Foundation v. McCallum hold that restricted public payments cannot purchase demonstrated integrated devotional functions merely through neutral eligibility and private choice. Agostini v. Felton supplies the unified function inquiry. Locke v. Davey preserves its bounded ministry-training exclusion and does not authorize every identity-based exclusion.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** These bounded funding rules operate within one inquiry into what the government actually purchases or performs. Religious affiliation, fungibility or private choice alone does not establish the answer; actual integrated devotional work and reliable separation matter. The constitutional equality of religious persons does not itself create an entitlement to public purchase of that work.
* **Other Governing Law:** Maine Revised Statutes title 20-A, including §§2951 and 5204, govern approved tuition arrangements, subject to the Religion Clauses and Equal Protection Clause. The local contract's educational-needs condition is distinct from the state sectarian exclusion.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The permissibility of refusing this kind of integrated tuition is established by Strout. The distinct equal-protection characterization and any request for status-neutral treatment of separate secular services require their own treatment; they cannot be silently treated as previously adjudicated.
* **Open Point:** Whether the parents' equality theory requires the very integrated devotional purchase the governing cases permit Maine to refuse, or identifies a distinct exclusion from eligible secular work.

##### Issue 2: Assertion of the school's separate constitutional rights

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The established third-party-standing requirements demand the claimant's own injury, a sufficiently close relationship and a genuine obstacle to the rights holder's own assertion. A close relationship alone does not supply the missing obstacle.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The parents' ability to litigate their own exclusion does not automatically authorize them to litigate every institutional claim of their chosen school. No effective same-term decision changes the entering requirements.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article III and the established limits on third-party assertion govern; the school's equal-protection theory is distinct from the parents' own pleaded injury.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The need for a real hindrance is settled. The record does not identify one preventing St. Dominic's from presenting its own institutional claim.
* **Open Point:** Whether any actual obstacle supports the parents' additional representative claim on this record.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Equal protection and the requested integrated devotional tuition

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The First Circuit held that equal protection did not require the requested funding. The district court had treated the earlier Strout litigation as fully dispositive; the appellate court considered the substantive equality theory rather than simply equating all claims.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** The parents argue that Maine supports private secular alternatives but excludes their chosen school because of religion and viewpoint, making the distinction unequal even if the State is not independently required to subsidize private education.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** Maine argues that it purchases a public educational substitute and may decline to pay for the requested sectarian instructional function, while leaving the parents free to choose and finance religious education.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** Religious identity alone cannot disqualify a provider from a genuinely eligible, reliably separated secular service. A parent cannot obtain an otherwise unavailable public devotional purchase simply by describing it as equal access to a general subsidy.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** No distinct material proposal is verified in the sources identified here.

##### Issue 2: Assertion of the school's separate constitutional rights

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The appellate analysis separated the parents' personal rights from the school's rights and found no adequate basis for the latter assertion.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** The parents rely on their direct relationship with the school and the financial effects of the funding exclusion on the educational choice they make for their children.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** Maine argues that the school can sue for itself and that the parents' relationship supplies no reason to dispense with the ordinary third-party requirements.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** Denial of the school claim cannot dispose of the parents' own claim. A future genuine obstacle could matter, but none may be invented here.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** No distinct material proposal is verified in the sources identified here.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** The categorical exclusion can make a formal application futile, but the parents must still show their own concrete injury and the relevance of the ordinary program's eligibility conditions. Their own claim differs from third-party assertion of the school's claim. No obstacle preventing the school from suing on its own behalf is established merely by the parent-school relationship.

##### Issue 1: Equal protection and the requested integrated devotional tuition

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Affirm against the actual integrated-tuition request under the governing function distinction; remand a supported separate secular-service claim if wrongly excluded by identity; or change the governing funding law expressly if the Court concludes equality requires broader public financing.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court can decide the actual tuition application without approving every possible categorical use of Maine's statutory wording or creating a general right to publicly funded religious instruction.

##### Issue 2: Assertion of the school's separate constitutional rights

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Reject the third-party claim without adjudicating the school's independent rights; permit it only on a supported hindrance showing; or resolve the parents' own claim separately, as the present record permits.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A record-specific application of third-party standing, not a categorical bar on parents litigating their children's rights or all school-related claims.

* **Record, Source, Posture, and Legal Uncertainty:** The record supports the integrated ordinary-tuition characterization because the parents specifically seek Catholic doctrinal instruction; it does not prove that every program at St. Dominic's is inseparable or ineligible. No damages amount, separate service application or actual institutional hindrance has been established.

## 93. Kelo v. City of New London

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Kelo v. City of New London
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-108; 545 U.S. 469
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-23 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Fifth Amendment's public-use requirement permits condemnation of private property for a comprehensive economic redevelopment plan implemented in part through transfer to private parties.
* **Question Granted:** Whether the Fifth Amendment's public-use requirement permits condemnation of private property for a comprehensive economic redevelopment plan implemented in part through transfer to private parties.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Connecticut Supreme Court sustained the challenged condemnations under the governing federal public-use requirement and state redevelopment law, reversing the trial court's partial relief for some parcels.
* **Relief Requested:** The homeowners seek to prevent condemnation of their properties for the challenged redevelopment. The City and development authority seek authority to proceed with the approved plan subject to compensation and other applicable legal requirements.
* **Material Record:** New London adopted a development plan for the Fort Trumbull area after prolonged economic distress. The plan combined several projected uses, including commercial, residential and public components, and contemplated private implementation. Petitioners' homes were not condemned as individually blighted. The City invoked expected jobs, tax revenue and coordinated redevelopment, not public access to every parcel. The record concerns an adopted planning process and the project's stated public purposes, with no finding that the condemnations were a sham solely to favor an identified private recipient. Forecasts remain forecasts; neither economic success nor failure after the event is part of this merits record.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The owners challenge the legality of the proposed taking, not merely the amount of compensation. The public-use issue is ripe through the identified condemnation decisions. State law can impose stricter limits than the federal minimum, but the federal question must be decided under the controlling precedents.
* **Relevant Dates:** The redevelopment planning and condemnation decisions preceded the Connecticut Supreme Court's 2004 judgment. The merits event is June 23, 2005. Later project outcomes and later legislation are ineligible as facts governing this decision.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** San Remo Hotel concerns preclusion after compensation litigation; Lingle concerns the boundary between takings and other challenges to regulation. Neither a proposed brief nor the historical result in either matter resolves this direct public-use challenge.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** An effective earlier same-term property decision would govern any overlapping proposition, but none is effective in the current baseline. The public-use rule entering this case remains the rule in the prior holdings.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Fifth Amendment permits takings only for public use and with just compensation. Public purpose, statutory authority, compensation and compliance with other constitutional limits remain separate requirements.

##### Issue 1: Public purpose, private implementation and pretext

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp. holds that compensated condemnation serving a rationally supported public passenger-service purpose satisfies public use despite private reconveyance. It applies Berman v. Parker and Hawaii Housing Authority v. Midkiff while expressly withholding permission for naked private favoritism. No controlling decision confines public use to public ownership or individual blight.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The entering rule examines the actual supported public purpose and preserves judicial review of a private-favor pretext. It does not require courts to select the best redevelopment policy or guarantee the project's eventual success.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments supply the federal limit. Connecticut's redevelopment statute and the authorized municipal plan supply the asserted condemnation power; compensation remains required independently.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Private transfer alone does not invalidate a taking. The unresolved application is whether comprehensive economic redevelopment, including nonblighted homes, is a sufficient actual public purpose on this record.
* **Open Point:** Whether the Court should exclude economic redevelopment categorically, or instead apply the existing public-purpose and anti-pretext boundary to this plan.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Public purpose, private implementation and pretext

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The Connecticut Supreme Court regarded the comprehensive plan and projected public economic benefits as sufficient public use, without requiring each property to be blighted or open to public access.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** The owners argue that predicted economic gains permit almost any home to be transferred to a more profitable private user, effectively erasing public use. They seek a meaningful boundary preventing condemnation for private economic development of ordinary homes.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** The City argues that the adopted comprehensive plan serves a genuine public redevelopment purpose, that private implementation is established practice under Berman and Midkiff, and that courts should not constitutionalize a preference among reasonable development methods.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** The City cannot justify a sham transfer by reciting jobs or tax revenue. The owners' legitimate concern about private favoritism does not establish that every privately implemented redevelopment plan is such a sham. Compensation cannot cure a taking whose purpose is constitutionally private.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** No distinct material proposal is verified in the sources identified here.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** The owners challenge the legality of the proposed taking, not merely the amount of compensation. The public-use issue is ripe through the identified condemnation decisions. State law can impose stricter limits than the federal minimum, but the federal question must be decided under the controlling precedents.

##### Issue 1: Public purpose, private implementation and pretext

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Affirm under the supported public-purpose rule with an express pretext boundary; reverse by adopting a categorical exclusion for this economic-development use, identifying the change from the existing rationale; or remand if material private-favor facts were improperly excluded or left unresolved. The record does not justify a judicial prediction of future project failure.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court may decide comprehensive, accountable redevelopment without validating isolated transfers to favored owners or declaring every projected tax increase sufficient by itself.

* **Record, Source, Posture, and Legal Uncertainty:** The judgment cannot certify that projected jobs or revenues will materialize. It also cannot adjudicate later conduct or transfer terms not in the record. Any preserved stricter state-law requirement remains distinct from the federal constitutional minimum.

## 94. Gonzalez v. Crosby

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Gonzalez v. Crosby
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-6432; 545 U.S. 524
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-23 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a Rule 60(b)(6) motion challenging only the federal court's prior limitations dismissal is a second or successive habeas application, and whether the asserted change in law warrants reopening the judgment.
* **Question Granted:** Whether a Rule 60(b)(6) motion challenging only the federal court's prior limitations dismissal is a second or successive habeas application, and whether the asserted change in law warrants reopening the judgment.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit, sitting en banc, treated the postjudgment motion as a successive habeas application requiring appellate authorization and affirmed denial of relief.
* **Relief Requested:** Gonzalez seeks consideration of his motion under Rule 60 and reopening of the original habeas proceeding. Florida seeks affirmance of the denial, invoking the Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996 (AEDPA)'s successive-application restrictions and the absence of extraordinary circumstances.
* **Material Record:** Gonzalez's first federal petition challenged the validity of his guilty plea but was dismissed on limitations grounds. The court denied tolling for a state postconviction application based on its understanding of properly filed proceedings. Gonzalez did not pursue further review after a certificate denial. Following Artuz, he moved under Rule 60(b)(6) to reopen the federal limitations judgment. The motion challenged the procedural barrier, not the federal court's resolution of the underlying plea claim on its merits. He waited months after Artuz before moving and identifies no separately established external impediment explaining the abandoned appellate route.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The court must classify the substance of the motion before imposing §2244(b). A request to add a conviction claim or reopen a merits rejection differs from a challenge to a nonmerits federal procedural ruling. Classification as a proper Rule 60 motion does not establish entitlement to relief under the Rule.
* **Relevant Dates:** The federal petition was filed in June 1997. Further certificate review was not pursued after the April 2000 denial. Artuz v. Bennett became effective November 7, 2000. Gonzalez filed the reopening motion months later; the reviewed en banc decision issued in 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Mayle v. Felix concerns amendment of an existing habeas petition before final merits disposition, not reopening a federal judgment. Bell v. Thompson concerns appellate mandate control; Calderon v. Thompson addresses recall of a completed merits mandate and has a different procedural predicate.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any effective same-term decision on state filing timeliness could affect the underlying tolling merits, but none is effective in the current baseline. The Rule 60 classification does not depend on assuming Gonzalez ultimately wins the tolling issue.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Rule 60 applies in habeas consistently with the habeas statutes and applicable rules. Abdur'Rahman v. Bell establishes that a genuine federal procedural motion preventing adjudication of an already pleaded claim is not successive merely because reopening might later permit relief. It separately requires reasonable timing, extraordinary circumstances and consideration of omitted ordinary review; classification supplies none of those conditions.

##### Issue 1: A procedural reopening motion versus a successive habeas claim

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Abdur'Rahman v. Bell holds that a request confined to correcting federal habeas procedure, including a barrier preventing adjudication of an already pleaded claim, is not a second or successive merits application. Its directly supported rule also requires separation of mixed procedural and merits requests and excludes new custody claims, factual merits supplements and renewed merits adjudication. Artuz v. Bennett distinguishes state filing conditions from bars to individual claims. Calderon v. Thompson governs the different act of recalling a completed merits mandate.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing rules preserve Abdur'Rahman's process-only classification, mixed-motion separation and independent Rule 60 requirements. A motion's label is insufficient, but the possible eventual benefit to a custody claim does not transform a true procedural correction into successive merits litigation.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2244(b), Rule 60(b), the then-applicable habeas Rule 11 and Civil Rule 81 govern consistency between civil procedure and habeas restrictions.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The process-only distinction is already binding, including barriers preventing adjudication of claims actually pleaded in the original application. This case applies it to a federal limitations dismissal rather than the default barrier previously presented.
* **Open Point:** Whether the actual motion is limited to the federal limitations barrier, as its terms indicate, and whether it independently satisfies Rule 60; adoption of the process-only distinction is not an open question.

##### Issue 2: Extraordinary circumstances for Rule 60(b)(6) relief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Abdur'Rahman v. Bell separately requires ordinary Rule 60(b)(6) adjudication of reasonable timing, extraordinary circumstances, failure to pursue the original procedural appeal and the legal effect of the asserted intervening change. Its procedural classification grants no reopening. Finality matters but cannot negate a rule expressly permitting exceptional relief.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing rules preserve those independent requirements and the movant's burden. A legal change and diligence must be assessed in their actual setting; a Rule 60 request does not become an ordinary substitute for an abandoned appeal.
* **Other Governing Law:** Rule 60(b)(6) and its reasonable-time requirement apply; the other specified Rule 60 grounds cannot be displaced simply to avoid their requirements.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The extraordinary-circumstances burden is settled. No controlling decision makes every intervening interpretation of a procedural statute sufficient to reopen a completed habeas judgment.
* **Open Point:** Whether Artuz, the procedural nature of the dismissal and Gonzalez's actual diligence together meet that burden.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A procedural reopening motion versus a successive habeas claim

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The Eleventh Circuit broadly treated Rule 60 motions, apart from a narrow fraud category, as successive applications and required precertification. That categorical approach conflicts with Abdur'Rahman's effective process-only rule.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** Gonzalez argues that he seeks correction of a federal procedural ruling that prevented any merits adjudication, not a second merits claim or new evidence supporting the conviction challenge.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** Florida argues that reopening would permit litigation of a conviction claim after a final federal judgment and could circumvent AEDPA's limits if postjudgment motions are freely permitted.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** A supposed procedural challenge that actually seeks reconsideration of the merits remains subject to §2244(b). A genuine procedural motion must still satisfy Rule 60's demanding requirements.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** No distinct material proposal is verified in the sources identified here.

##### Issue 2: Extraordinary circumstances for Rule 60(b)(6) relief

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The categorical successive-application rationale prevented a proper distinction between authority to consider the motion and whether the facts justify relief. The district court nevertheless denied the motion, and the available record permits consideration of the asserted reopening basis.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** Gonzalez argues that the intervening clarification exposes an erroneous threshold dismissal that prevented any adjudication of his conviction claim.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** Florida emphasizes finality, the failure to continue ordinary review and the absence of an additional extraordinary circumstance beyond the later legal development.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** A legal change is not categorically irrelevant, especially when combined with other compelling circumstances. Nor does an initial nonmerits dismissal guarantee perpetual access to reopening after ordinary review ends.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** No distinct material proposal is verified in the sources identified here.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** The court must classify the substance of the motion before imposing §2244(b). A request to add a conviction claim or reopen a merits rejection differs from a challenge to a nonmerits federal procedural ruling. Classification as a proper Rule 60 motion does not establish entitlement to relief under the Rule.

##### Issue 1: A procedural reopening motion versus a successive habeas claim

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Apply Abdur'Rahman and reject the successive classification for the identified limitations-only motion, then decide the Rule 60 showing. If the filing actually contains added or renewed merits demands, separate and process those components under the applicable restrictions. A court cannot sustain the categorical rule merely because reopening might eventually allow custody relief.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A substance-based distinction between conviction claims and genuine federal-process attacks can govern recurring Rule 60 motions without reopening ordinary merits review by another name.

##### Issue 2: Extraordinary circumstances for Rule 60(b)(6) relief
