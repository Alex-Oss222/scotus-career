#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the convictions against the presented statutory and revenue-rule challenge.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave guilt judgments effective on that ground. Any preserved sentencing or restitution matter must be adjudicated under its own authority and the law effective when addressed; no new tax-collection judgment is entered.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment resolves these defendants’ convictions, not a Canadian civil claim or the validity of other countries’ tax systems.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower courts implement the affirmance and identify any genuinely unresolved sentencing component without treating this decision as a command for additional restitution or as proof of a particular tax-loss amount.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Domestic wire fraud can target a foreign entitlement to money

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The charged domestic wire transactions and asserted money entitlement present the statutory issue. A separate tax-collection action is not before the Court.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this prosecution is excluded by the revenue rule.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve Cleveland v. United States’s unpaid-revenue reservation for this domestic criminal application, without expanding executive foreign-affairs power.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule must distinguish domestic property fraud from direct foreign tax enforcement. A broader rule making every foreign legal obligation federal property would exceed the statute. A narrower nationality exception would lack a basis in the enacted offense.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A scheme executed through domestic interstate wires to deprive a foreign sovereign of an existing entitlement to money may violate §1343 when the Government proves the statute’s fraud elements; the common-law revenue rule does not categorically bar enforcement of that domestic criminal prohibition. The prosecution must establish the actual money entitlement, material deception, fraudulent intent and requisite wire use beyond a reasonable doubt.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the presented domestic fraudulent scheme. It does not make foreign regulatory permission property or create a civil action to collect foreign taxes.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Domestic wire fraud requires a material scheme targeting property and the enacted culpability and wire-use elements. Restitution requires independent statutory authority and proof of the compensable loss.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Cleveland v. United States rejects treating licensing power as property but preserves actual public money and revenue questions. Here the object is a money entitlement, and domestic wires execute the deprivation. The independent federal offense supplies authority; executive choice alone does not. Neder v. United States requires materiality, preventing an untethered rule criminalizing any foreign-law noncompliance.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Cleveland v. United States: distinguish regulatory authority and answer the actual-revenue application. Neder v. United States: preserve the fraud elements.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Direct foreign tax collection, a specific restitution award and independently preserved sentencing issues are outside the presented statutory ground.

* **Remedial Reach:** Relief concerns Small’s challenged conviction and its dependent consequences. It establishes a statutory construction, not an individualized finding that Japan’s courts were unfair.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must examine the actual judgment and plea to vacate the affected count and dependent punishment. Any independent count or lawful restraint remains subject to its own basis; there is no freestanding gun-license order.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Congress defined a domestic predicate system

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved conditional plea allows resolution of the statutory category without a new evidentiary trial.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a foreign court’s conviction supplies the §922(g)(1) predicate.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve a previously open statutory reach question while preserving domestic restoration law.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule defines the statute’s conviction category. A broader constitutional rule about foreign judgments is unnecessary. A narrower fairness exception for Small would invent a standard Congress did not provide.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The phrase “convicted in any court” in §922(g)(1), read with §921(a)(20), refers to qualifying domestic convictions and does not include a foreign conviction. The Government must establish a predicate within that statutory category beyond a reasonable doubt; seriousness or perceived fairness of an excluded foreign judgment cannot substitute for coverage.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs this conviction-based firearm prohibition, not every statute mentioning a court or every legal use of a foreign judgment.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Other statutes require their own text and context; “any” is not mechanically limited in every enactment.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Beecham v. United States and Jones v. United States selects whose law controls restoration only after the relevant conviction is within the statute. Caron v. United States likewise assumes a domestic predicate and cannot expand the initial category. The federal-and-state exclusions and restoration framework identify the domestic system Congress regulated. A universal foreign reading would require courts to invent exceptions for political or otherwise incompatible convictions, while the statutory text supplies no such screening mechanism.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Beecham v. United States and Jones v. United States and Caron v. United States: preserve their domestic selector and restoration holdings.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the constitutional judgment and injunction against the Bible Education Ministry program as operated, on the governing governmental-function ground. Leave the supported anonymity and fee rulings undisturbed.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The school defendants must stop providing the challenged devotional classes as school instruction. Retain district-court enforcement, with wording confined to official religious instruction. No damages, replacement curriculum, or automatic exclusion of religious volunteers is ordered.
* **Remedial Reach:** The proved practice concerns a school-system program, so the injunction protects every child governed by that program rather than only the named families. The Board bears the burden of showing that a narrower remedy would fully cure the demonstrated violation; merely exempting named pupils would leave the official religious instruction intact.
* **Practical Relief:** Upon the mandate, the Board must cease scheduling or authorizing these classes as part of its school day and inform responsible principals. Compliance concerns the program’s operation and content, not individual pupils’ private expression. The district court can require a compliance report and address actual proposed changes through ordinary proceedings; it need not serve as a standing curriculum consultant.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Public adoption of devotional teaching

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The sealed enrollment evidence establishes live exposure and permits review without taxpayer-standing expansion. Undisputed lesson plans and the Board’s curricular authorization resolve attribution on summary judgment; no finding about every volunteer’s subjective motives is needed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether these public schools may give religious-college instructors the regular classroom period to teach Christian doctrine as truth.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing official-religious-function prohibition, using Agostini’s current framework rather than reviving independent Lemon prongs.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule answers the recurring outsourcing problem as well as this program. One step broader would improperly condemn neutral secular work by religious providers; one step narrower would allow the same official teaching merely by changing its contractor or descriptive label.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A public school may not make devotional instruction part of its own school-day program by assigning its delivery to a private religious provider. Plaintiffs must establish the governmental instructional relationship and religious function under ordinary civil proof; at summary judgment the record must leave no genuine dispute of material fact. Religious identity, location and moral vocabulary alone are insufficient. Here the regular classroom arrangement and actual lessons establish official teaching of religious truth, which the asserted character-education purpose cannot justify.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers actual governmental adoption of religious instruction, including nominally voluntary or privately delivered curricular programs. It excludes genuine independent private access and objectively secular study of religion.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Religious affiliation does not disqualify a person from teaching secular material. Courts examine what the authorized program actually teaches and who makes it part of the governmental educational function.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Agostini makes the actual function decisive and permits enforceably secular instruction even in religious settings. That holding cannot protect the inverse arrangement, actual devotional instruction within a public-school program. Lee rejects a requirement of separately quantified coercion after official sponsorship is established. Santa Fe likewise prevents private delivery from laundering the school’s own religious choice. These authorities work within the single current inquiry and need no replacement test. The Board can discontinue the unconstitutional program without receiving a prospective advisory ruling approving another one.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Agostini v. Felton is reaffirmed as the governing method. Lee v. Weisman and Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe are applied to the actual official instructional relationship; no existing holding is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A particular redesigned secular curriculum is outside the record.

## 60. Pace v. DiGuglielmo

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user's express preapproval.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** Current revision request.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the federal limitations judgment.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Dismiss this untimely federal application without adjudicating its underlying constitutional claims. Recognize no statutory exclusion for the late state application and deny the asserted equitable protection because the supported record does not establish reasonable diligence.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment governs Pace’s application. No unlawful policy or class claim is proved, and no broader remedial order follows.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court implements the limitations disposition. This ruling supplies no release, new sentencing or retrial. Future courts must calculate actual dates and address supported independent grounds rather than treating a state untimeliness ruling as a reason to skip the equitable inquiry altogether.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Deadline exceptions do not cure actual untimeliness

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Superior Court’s explicit ruling removes uncertainty about Pennsylvania’s disposition. The record establishes the intervals on which the federal calculation turns. No collateral merits adjudication is necessary.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether failure to satisfy a genuine state filing deadline and all of its exceptions prevents statutory tolling, and whether the asserted equitable basis saves this application.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve Artuz’s reservation by applying its delivery-versus-relief distinction to exception-bearing deadlines; preserve Pliler and assume broader equitable authority only as necessary to reject this record’s lack of diligence.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A rule about genuine deadlines must operate across different state exception structures. One step broader would improperly treat every claim default as a filing defect or deny all equitable protection; one step narrower would make clerical processing override the actual deadline.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A state collateral application rejected under an applicable filing deadline is not properly filed under §2244(d)(2) merely because that deadline contains exceptions the application does not satisfy. The federal court identifies the operative state filing rule and its authoritative application, distinguishing claim-relief bars; the applicant must establish the dates and qualifying state pendency on which the asserted exclusion depends. Separately, Pace cannot obtain the asserted equitable interval without showing reasonable diligence, which his unexplained years of delay and five-month post-review delay fail to establish.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This covers actual filing deadlines, whether absolute or subject to exceptions, while preserving Artuz’s distinction for rules directed solely at claim relief. The equitable conclusion concerns this asserted interval and record, not every possible ground for tolling.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A docket stamp or alternative merits discussion does not establish proper filing. Until this Court decides the asserted equitable-tolling authority beyond Pliler, courts apply its court-created-impediment rule where established and require reasonable diligence for the broader equitable theory assumed here.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Artuz preserves meaningful state filing conditions while preventing a claim’s weakness from changing the status of its application. An exception establishes another permissible route to timely filing; an application satisfying no route still fails that condition. Carey supports reliance on actual state timeliness rather than the sequence of state reasoning. The exhaustion concern is real but cannot rewrite Congress’s exclusion. Pliler requires an actual court-created loss and diligence, neither of which is established by Pace’s ordinary pursuit of a late state petition. Diligence is assessed against the circumstances and procedures available when Pace acted, without imposing a later protective-filing requirement retroactively.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Artuz v. Bennett is extended to resolve its express reservation. Carey v. Saffold and Duncan v. Walker are reaffirmed. Pliler v. Ford remains effective on its distinct predicates.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The full universe of equitable grounds, including facts not asserted here, is outside this record. Underlying habeas merits are unnecessary once this application is untimely.

## 61. Bates v. Dow Agrosciences LLC

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user's express preapproval.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** Current revision request.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Conditions:** The record and preservation limits stated below.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated disposition and substantive rules.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the categorical preemption judgment and remand for claim-specific proceedings.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore independently viable design, manufacture, testing and voluntary-warranty theories that impose no different labeling command. Require the lower court to compare each fraud or warning obligation with actual federal misbranding requirements and address any preserved actual conflict. Preserve independent state-law dismissals not disturbed by this ruling.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment restores these farmers’ claims against Dow to the extent the preemption error foreclosed them. No unlawful nationwide practice is proved and no general injunction or damages class is ordered.
* **Practical Relief:** On remand Dow must identify the specific federal duty and incompatible state command it invokes; the farmers must establish each surviving cause of action under ordinary state law. Jury instructions must prevent recovery based on a substantively different labeling requirement. The parties receive adjudication, not a predetermined liability or compensation award.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Substantive duties, not commercial incentives, determine displacement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The categorical inducement theory is a legal ground dispositive below and is reviewable without resolving disputed crop damage or whether each representation was false. The unresolved state-duty comparison justifies remand rather than a Supreme Court factual trial.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Which of these state product-liability duties FIFRA displaces and what proceedings survive.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply and restate the three existing bounded rules from Cipollone, Medtronic and Geier in one ordered statutory inquiry without erasing their distinct sources.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. The statutory distinction governs the recurring class of pesticide liability claims, not only Strongarm. One step broader would declare all tort claims immune from preemption; one step narrower would leave the erroneous inducement proxy operating under different claim labels.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** FIFRA preempts a state duty only when its actual substantive command imposes different or additional labeling or packaging requirements, or when a separately demonstrated conflict with operative federal law requires displacement. The proponent of preemption must identify that incompatibility; ordinary state-law burdens of liability remain with the claimant. Damages are not additional label content simply because a manufacturer might respond by changing its label. Nonlabel safety duties, voluntary warranties and genuinely parallel label duties therefore cannot be dismissed categorically, while an actual demand for different warnings cannot evade the clause through pleading terminology.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers state statutory and common-law requirements, including parallel claims. It supplies no private federal FIFRA cause of action and no immunity from proof of ordinary state-law elements.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A court must identify what conduct a verdict legally requires. When that duty concerns labeling, it compares the actual federal counterpart; when a party identifies an independent operative conflict, it decides that conflict on evidence and law rather than general regulatory preference.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Medtronic makes two propositions coexist: damages duties may be requirements, and equal substantive requirements are not additional because damages enforce them. Geier protects an actual federal regulatory choice only when the state duty defeats it; registration alone is not such a conflict. Cipollone directs attention to Congress’s clause and the actual duty. Folded into one inquiry, those rules foreclose treating a possible manufacturer reaction as a legal requirement. FIFRA’s preservation of state sale and use authority and its nonconclusive registration provision reinforce that reading. The incomplete Texas-equivalence briefing requires an actual comparison on remand, not a universal declaration about all warning litigation.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr and Geier v. American Honda Motor Co. are restated together and reaffirmed; none is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Texas duties’ precise content and equivalence were not fully determined below; those application questions return under the stated substantive-command and conflict rules.

## 62. Gates v. Cook

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user's express preapproval.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** Current revision request.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Conditions:** The record and preservation limits stated below.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of the exhaustion defense and the supported Unit 32-C protections. Vacate only the unrestricted incorporation of outside mental-health standards and remand to confine that provision to necessary correction. Leave provisions already vacated below undisturbed absent a preserved cross-request.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Maintain the supported cleaning, heat mitigation, screens, plumbing, lighting, and concrete mental-health protections. The district court must clarify the challenged blanket standards language using §3626’s findings, preserving necessary examinations, medication monitoring and housing measures supported by the record. The remand is not permission to suspend independent protective requirements.
* **Remedial Reach:** The certified death-row class receives correction of the common conditions in Unit 32-C. Officials must establish that any proposed narrower protection fully cures the proved classwide violation. The record does not justify extending these commands to all of Unit 32 merely because hazards might also occur there.
* **Practical Relief:** Officials must provide clean cells and adequate cleaning means, implement the supported heat and pest protections, repair the dangerous plumbing, and furnish the supported mental-health services. The district court retains reporting and enforcement authority and must set a prompt schedule for the confined clarification. Compliance is demonstrated by functioning facilities and services, not a promise to replace a vendor or efforts that leave the serious risk unabated. Security evidence can shape an equally effective lawful means; it cannot excuse continued known danger.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Representative exhaustion of the same prospective claim

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Russell presented the same systemic conditions through the emergency route and obtained a substantive final-level response before suit. The class’s common prospective claim is therefore available for adjudication without deciding unrelated unexhausted claims.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §1997e(a) demands that every absent member repeat the named representative’s completed grievance concerning the same conditions and relief.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve the open representative-action question while applying Booth and Porter’s mandatory, actually available exhaustion requirement.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule covers a certified class’s indivisible prospective claim. One step broader would excuse unexhausted individual damages or different conditions; one step narrower would turn representative procedure into a redundant series of identical administrative actions despite completion of the statutory opportunity to respond.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A named prisoner who exhausts the available process for a common prison-conditions grievance may represent a properly certified class seeking the same indivisible prospective relief without requiring each unnamed member to repeat that grievance. The court must verify actual prefiling completion and identity between the grievance, the class condition and the requested correction. This record meets those requirements because the Commissioner considered Russell’s emergency submission on its substance and prescribed no further available review.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers common prospective correction under Rule 23, not unrelated conditions, separate incidents or unproved individual damages claims. It creates no general futility exception.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A class complaint that adds a different condition must establish its own satisfied exhaustion predicate. Officials may insist on an actual remaining step but cannot invent one after completing review under the procedure they made available.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Booth and Porter enforce the enacted administrative sequence, not ritual repetition for its own sake. The responsible officials received the systemic complaint and an opportunity to correct it before litigation. Rule 23 then permits one exhausted claimant to seek the common remedy. Treating the emergency route as unfinished because it did not duplicate the ordinary route contradicts the operative policy and the substantive final-level response.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Booth v. Churner and Porter v. Nussle are applied, with a new holding on representative exhaustion; neither is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 2: Effective correction of proved interacting dangers

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The consent trial produced findings of serious continuing dangers and official awareness. The legal consequences and injunction’s scope are reviewable without finding new conditions outside Unit 32-C.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the proved combined conditions support the surviving targeted injunction and how its limits must operate.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Farmer, Helling and Wilson together: an identifiable serious deprivation, actual awareness and unreasonable response, followed by the separate statutory remedial limits. No new numerical constitutional code is proposed.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The governing rule addresses interacting harms while tying relief to proved conditions and responsibility. One step broader would constitutionalize every professional standard; one step narrower would artificially isolate mutually reinforcing dangers or wait for an avoidable injury.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Prison officials who knowingly disregard interacting conditions that expose prisoners to a substantial risk of serious harm must correct that danger through relief satisfying §3626’s necessity, narrowness and intrusiveness limits. Prisoners bear ordinary civil proof of the constitutional predicates; actual awareness may be inferred from persistent obvious risks and repeated notice but is not presumed from job title. The court must independently support each remedial requirement. The credited conditions justify the surviving targeted protections, while an undifferentiated command to obey every outside professional standard requires confinement to necessary correction.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule protects the affected class’s health, sanitation and mental-health needs without adjudicating capital sentences, ordering damages, or specifying an ideal prison system.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts examine interacting conditions by the identifiable deprivation they produce, apply Farmer’s knowledge and reasonable-response requirements, and support each remedial command under §3626.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Helling rejects waiting for disease after a dangerous exposure is proved. Farmer preserves actual knowledge and reasonable-response defenses, both addressed through trial evidence here rather than a categorical inference. Wilson permits consideration of mutually reinforcing conditions without replacing an identifiable human need with a general judgment that prison life is harsh. Those three bounded rules should be stated as one liability inquiry while keeping the enacted remedy inquiry distinct. Targeted cooling, sanitation and care measures directly answer the credited dangers. Wholesale external-code incorporation may reach practices never found dangerous and therefore requires a confined correction.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Farmer v. Brennan, Helling v. McKinney and Wilson v. Seiter are reaffirmed and restated together for interacting conditions; no culpability requirement is weakened.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Conditions outside the certified unit lack the findings necessary to extend this decree.

## 63. Granholm v. Heald

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit in Nos. 03-1116 and 03-1120. Reverse the Second Circuit in No. 03-1274 and remand for effective nondiscriminatory relief.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Enjoin enforcement of the geographic preferences. Each State may implement a lawful, genuinely equal distribution arrangement under its severability law, but cannot retain unequal direct access during an indefinite policy reconsideration. The lower courts must resolve the operative state-law remedy promptly; no federal license or damages award follows.
* **Remedial Reach:** The challenged systems govern the winery class as a matter of policy, so correction reaches every producer subject to the established preference. The States bear the burden of showing that a narrower remedy would fully eliminate that inequality. The Court does not command unregulated shipping.
* **Practical Relief:** The responsible licensing agencies must administer access on equal terms under the remedial decrees. Courts must specify what existing permissions and prohibitions remain enforceable while the States implement a lawful choice. Neutral age, reporting, licensing and tax requirements continue under their actual terms; geographic favoritism cannot be retained as a substitute for those controls.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Alcohol authority and interstate equality

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The concrete denial of equal market access permits merits review. The consolidated legal issue does not erase each docket’s separate judgment or require decision of an absent challenge to neutral alcohol distribution.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the alcohol-specific constitutional and statutory authority permits these discriminatory direct-shipping arrangements.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing nondiscrimination rule and the surviving alcohol-specific limit; reject an expansion of §2 into protectionist immunity.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. The reasoning governs discriminatory producer access across alcohol systems, not only the two statutory formulations. One step broader would unsettle neutral distribution systems without a presented defect; one step narrower would permit the same favoritism through a costly local-premises condition rather than an express ban.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A State may regulate alcohol importation and distribution, but may not give in-state producers a direct-sales advantage over comparable out-of-state producers unless it proves that a legitimate local purpose cannot adequately be served by reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives. Section 2 and the cited federal alcohol statutes do not authorize economic protectionism. The States bear that justification burden under ordinary proof, without a requirement to disprove fanciful alternatives. These records do not establish why workable evenhanded licensing, taxation and delivery controls are insufficient.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers geographic favoritism in access to a direct-shipping channel, including functional local-presence barriers. It preserves genuinely equal licensing, distribution and enforcement arrangements.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** The constitutional comparison concerns actual access and burdens rather than statutory labels. A materially different requirement supported by a distinct regulatory function receives that actual justification analysis; an interstate origin preference cannot be defended merely by restating the State’s power to regulate alcohol.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Oregon Waste supplies the demanding alternatives rule once origin discrimination is established. Carbone prevents government’s supervisory role from transforming a private competitive advantage into proprietary activity. Camps Newfound requires a genuine difference in the service rather than residence alone. These are three applications of a common discrimination rule, which Stone would restate as the single operative test above, preserving their actual-public-purchase boundaries. Bacchus keeps that principle operative in the alcohol field. The States’ interests are substantial, but the records do not connect those interests to the unequal treatment when comparable local direct sales are permitted.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Oregon Waste Systems v. Department of Environmental Quality, C & A Carbone v. Town of Clarkstown and Camps Newfound/Owatonna v. Town of Harrison are restated and reaffirmed. Bacchus Imports v. Dias is applied, not overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A neutral universal direct-shipping prohibition and unrelated features of three-tier distribution lie outside the presented discrimination claims.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Ninth Circuit’s categorical facial judgment and remand for the actual material-connection and associational-protection inquiry. Do not reinstate blanket validity without that inquiry.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The lower court must identify the challenged publication classes and financers, evaluate the State’s actual justification for name and address exposure, and decide whether supported invalid applications warrant protective, severable or facial relief. Existing interim protection may be administered under ordinary equitable standards; the Court does not invent a new statewide injunction or a confidentiality system.

Neither party receives an automatic entitlement to delay publication or compel complete donor disclosure pending a new trial.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Disclosure requires its own actual justification

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Specific intended communications establish injury. The mismatch between the appellate standard and controlling controlling holdings requires correction, while the incomplete application of the proper standard prevents an invented final finding about every covered publication.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether organizational financing may justify on-message sponsor identification despite an individual exemption, and how this Nevada law must be assessed.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply McConnell and Stengel without extending McIntyre’s individual application into a categorical organizational exemption. Require a genuine assessment of the statute’s additional reach.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule resolves the individual-versus-organizational distinction and supplies the standard for the entire challenged mandate. One step broader would approve all source exposure by label; one step narrower would ignore directly controlling sponsor-identification holdings and leave the recurrent statutory distinction unexplained.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A State may require factual identification of an organizational financer of electoral communication when it establishes a material electoral-information or source-tracing connection, subject to protection for substantiated associational injury; exempting independent individual leaflets does not itself invalidate that requirement. Government must justify the actual information and exposure demanded. A claimant seeking protective relief must substantiate the asserted injury, and government retains justification of the remaining exposure. Nevada’s broader coverage must be assessed on those terms, not invalidated merely because identification appears on the message or sustained merely because the speaker acts collectively.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the actual financer and covered electoral communication, not a general membership list, permission before speech, or ideological endorsement. It leaves McIntyre’s ordinary individual leaflets protected.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts cannot import reporting thresholds into separate attribution duties. A small association’s ordinary issue leaflet and a paid candidate advertisement may present different electoral connections and privacy burdens; the State must justify the class and information it actually regulates.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** McIntyre reserved the organizational financing setting. Stengel then sustained names and addresses in a bounded organized candidate-advertisement setting, recognizing that an address can identify the real financer but requires justification. McConnell extended the justified attribution duty beyond express advocacy and expressly kept its trigger separate from financial-report thresholds. These three bounded rules should be restated as the source-sensitive inquiry above, not collapsed into either absolute anonymity or automatic disclosure. Nevada’s actual wording may reach additional speech; that is a concrete coverage question the appellate court must evaluate under the correct rule. McConnell’s specific identification holding governs this point.
* **Precedent Treatment:** McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission is preserved at its individual-leaflet scope. Kentucky Right to Life v. Stengel and McConnell v. Federal Election Commission are applied and restated with it; no precedent is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.
