## 25. GDF Realty Investments, Ltd. v. Norton

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Conditions:** Subject to any intervening effective decision governing the issue.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated rule, disposition and remedy.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the constitutional judgment sustaining the challenged application.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny the requested Commerce Clause declaration and injunction. Preserve ordinary litigation over actual statutory elements and any properly presented permit or compensation claim.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment resolves these owners’ challenge; the controlling class rule has precedential force, but the judgment does not impose a new conservation injunction or authorize injury to the property.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court enters judgment rejecting the constitutional claim. Federal officials may proceed only through otherwise lawful enforcement and permitting mechanisms, with the required injury and causation showings. No court orders a development halt beyond an existing lawful restriction.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Necessary inclusion within interstate wildlife regulation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The concrete application and requested prospective relief permit decision of the preserved commerce question. No actual take or permit issue is necessary.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the absence of interstate trade in these cave species removes their taking from the Act’s valid interstate regulatory system.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply current law unchanged.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate, because the existing rule covers the statutory taking class, not merely one species. No broader federal police power is necessary. A species-only exception would contradict the entering rule and misdescribe its basis.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may include the taking of listed intrastate wildlife within the Endangered Species Act when that inclusion is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing the Act’s independently valid interstate wildlife regulation. The Government must identify the regime, covered class and genuine relationship; the court must assess those legal predicates independently. The landowners identify no material outside-class, invalid-regime or pretextual distinction here. The species’ confined range and lack of trade therefore do not defeat this application.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Unauthorized takes within the identified listed-wildlife regime, including habitat-caused statutory injury. The rule establishes neither liability for every land modification nor constitutional authority over all local environmental regulation.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts apply the same class-based inquiry to other intrastate listed species. They must adjudicate the separate statutory injury, causation, permit and remedy predicates before ordering relief for an actual take.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Rancho Viejo, LLC v. Norton sustained this functional relationship without a species-specific evidentiary remand. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt had already supplied its operational rule. United States v. Lopez excludes development’s downstream economic consequences as a substitute for a fitted connection, but does not prohibit necessary inclusion of local conduct. These three decisions form one already integrated inquiry; Stone would apply it, not add a competing ecological-benefit test. The distinction between power and liability preserves the owners’ ability to contest an actual enforcement claim.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt and Rancho Viejo, LLC v. Norton at their necessary-inclusion scope; preserve United States v. Lopez’s connection requirement.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual statutory injury, permit entitlement and compensation fall outside the constitutional-power question. Those claims retain their separate elements under Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon and the enacted permit provisions.

## 26. Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Taft

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Conditions:** Subject to any intervening effective decision governing the issue.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated rule, disposition and remedy.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Sixth Circuit judgment insofar as it categorically sustains the prohibition and eliminates necessary-care protection; remand under the complete entering standard.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require separate findings on actual D&E coverage, usable alternatives, residual interference, comparative serious health risks and grave mental-health necessity. Preserve or promptly issue interim protection for supported necessary-care applications while the court conforms final relief; do not automatically invalidate the entire statute.
* **Remedial Reach:** Relief must cover the proved unlawful policy applications and the patients and providers they govern. Ohio bears the showing that a narrower measure fully cures a proved common violation. Independent lawful applications survive under Ohio severability law.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must promptly identify protected clinical circumstances, the officials bound and the criminal, licensing and civil sanctions that cannot enforce those applications. It must set an expedited evidentiary schedule for genuinely unresolved facts and an effective interim order where the ordinary equitable predicates are met. Final relief must state the surviving duties clearly enough for clinical use and enforcement.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Scope of the method prohibition and complete access inquiry

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The threatened statute affects the providers’ available methods. Its actual scope must be decided before judging access consequences.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the D&E exclusion defeats the constitutional challenge without examining actual covered conduct and residual interference.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the complete entering standard; reject an obstacle-only substitute.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate, corresponding to the revised method law and the actual affected patients. A categorical invalidation of all method restrictions would exceed the record. Restricting review to whether D&E remains technically legal would omit controlling protections.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An express exemption for dilation and evacuation defeats a claim that the statute prohibits that method only to the extent the enacted definition and its actual operation preserve the clinically used procedure; it does not end the constitutional inquiry into remaining interference. The providers must establish actual coverage and attributable interference, and Ohio must meet the justification and fit burden that the degree of interference triggers. The lower court must apply the complete framework rather than treat an available alternative’s label as conclusive.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The revised statute’s previability applications and its actual fault and procedure definitions; lawful competence and safety regulation remains available.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must assess the medical procedure the statutory elements reach, the alternatives actually usable for the defined patients, and cumulative attributable burdens. No identical numerical burden threshold is required.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich requires examination of operative acts and alternatives rather than invalidity by nomenclature. Stenberg v. Carhart found an obstacle on a supported record under a different text; its factual conclusion cannot be imported. Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey nevertheless requires examination of severe and meaningful interference even if an ordinary-D&E obstacle is absent. Those rules can be applied together without changing their burdens.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey and Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich; distinguish Stenberg v. Carhart’s statutory-coverage finding.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The record does not resolve every clinically used procedure or affected patient group; those applications require the focused remand findings.

##### Issue 2: Necessary physical and mental health care

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The exception is directly challenged and determines whether the prohibition can lawfully operate in supported necessary-care circumstances.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the exception is adequate when its wording excludes necessary comparative-risk or grave mental-health applications.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing necessary-care law without making physician preference conclusive.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate, because the rule protects every supported necessary-care application of this prohibition. A universal right to the preferred method would exceed necessity. Limiting relief to imminent death would contradict the entering health protection.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may not enforce an abortion-method prohibition when competent individualized medical judgment establishes that substitution creates a material risk of death or serious health harm and the prohibited method is necessary for timely safe and suitable care. The protected judgment includes grave medically established mental-health danger, and a statutory defense must be practically available before catastrophic deterioration. Challengers must establish supported necessary-care applications; unsupported preference is insufficient, while professional disagreement alone does not defeat supported necessity. This civil constitutional rule does not alter the separate statutory burdens in a criminal prosecution.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Necessary-care applications before and after viability, with viability’s separate constitutional role preserved. Fraud, malpractice and otherwise lawful sanctions remain independently enforceable.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** When statutory wording cannot bear the required health protection, courts enjoin the incompatible applications and dependent sanctions rather than rewrite the defense. They must identify competent support for the need and preserve separable lawful applications.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stenberg v. Carhart distinguishes a life-only exception from usable serious-health protection. Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich independently protects grave mental-health necessity. The relevant harm may result from forced substitution, so an exception confined to a preexisting physical complication can miss the constitutional injury. The record must identify those applications rather than assume universal superiority of intact extraction.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm the necessary-care holdings of Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich and Stenberg v. Carhart.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Individual clinical necessity requires facts not resolved by this facial record; the stated necessity rule governs those determinations.

## 27. Smith v. Massachusetts

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Conditions:** Subject to any intervening effective decision governing the issue.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated rule, disposition and remedy.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand for removal of the firearm conviction.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Bar further adjudication of guilt on the acquitted count. Require correction of the judgment and any dependent sentence, preserving independent lawful convictions.
* **Remedial Reach:** Smith alone receives judgment relief. The completion rule governs analogous acquittals as precedent; no systemwide injunction is at issue.
* **Practical Relief:** The state court must vacate the firearm conviction and its dependent punishment when the mandate issues. Any resentencing must remain within authority supplied by surviving convictions and may not reintroduce punishment requiring guilt on the acquitted count.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Finality of the completed insufficiency determination

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The trial record identifies an actual insufficiency ruling, subsequent defense proceedings and reinstatement of the count. No threshold prevents deciding its double-jeopardy effect.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the completed acquittal could be reopened within the same trial.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the current acquittal rule.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate, because a completed acquittal receives the same protection across trial formats. No general prohibition on correcting every interlocutory ruling is necessary. A rule confined to signed or jury-announced acquittals would contradict existing law.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Once the trial court completes an unqualified determination that the prosecution’s proof is insufficient on a count, the Double Jeopardy Clause bars restoring that count for further adjudication of guilt, even within the same jury trial and even if the insufficiency ruling was legally wrong. The actual trial episode establishes completion here; an ordinary power to revisit interlocutory rulings supplies no notice that this acquittal remained provisional.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Completed substantive acquittals. Tentative observations and rulings expressly provisional under an applicable established procedure remain distinct.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts determine substantive completion from the ruling, applicable procedure and subsequent conduct as a whole. A genuinely tentative ruling remains open; a completed acquittal cannot be relabeled after the defense proceeds.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Price v. Vincent makes substantive completion decisive and separates habeas deference from the acquittal question. This direct-review record contains a granted motion ending a count, followed by defense proceedings, rather than an unsettled judicial exchange. Smalis v. Pennsylvania prohibits renewed guilt factfinding after acquittal. The prosecutor’s later legal authority shows a possible error in the acquittal, not authority to withdraw its final protection.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Price v. Vincent, Smalis v. Pennsylvania and United States v. Martin Linen Supply Co.; no overruling.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A clearly established rule expressly making an insufficiency ruling provisional is not this record and is reserved on that ground.

## 28. Stewart v. Dutra Construction Co.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Conditions:** Subject to any intervening effective decision governing the issue.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated rule, disposition and remedy.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the vessel-based judgment and remand for the remaining statutory and negligence issues.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Treat the Super Scoop as a vessel on the described undisputed capabilities. Determine Stewart’s employment connection and the proper defendant-capacity theory without the rejected primary-purpose exclusion.
* **Remedial Reach:** Stewart receives further adjudication of his own injury claims. No damages, compensation election or class relief is awarded at this stage.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must reopen the claims affected by the erroneous vessel premise and state which coverage and negligence issues remain. It may enter further summary judgment only on a legally sufficient independent ground; otherwise ordinary factfinding determines liability and loss.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Practical water-transport capacity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The legal vessel exclusion supplied a dispositive premise below. Correcting it does not require deciding contested negligence or every worker-status fact.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Super Scoop falls within the statutory vessel definition.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt a statutory construction consistent with, but not already decided by, the existing worker-status cases.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate, covering practically transport-capable watercraft under the statutory definition. A rule for everything that can float would erase practical capability. A primary-purpose or instantaneous-motion restriction would add conditions Congress omitted.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A watercraft is a vessel under the LHWCA when it is used or practically capable of being used to transport people or things over water, without requiring transportation to be its primary purpose or the craft to be moving when the injury occurs. Practical capability excludes permanently withdrawn or fixed structures whose maritime transport is merely theoretical. The Super Scoop’s actual waterborne movement with equipment and workers satisfies that definition.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Vessel status within the LHWCA and the linked seaman inquiry. Seaman connection, vessel negligence, causation and compensation remain separate.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts examine practical transport capability and service status, not industrial labels or a snapshot of movement. Permanent withdrawal requires evidence that transport capability is merely theoretical; the worker must separately satisfy Chandris’s substantial-connection test.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The text covers capability as well as current use; it does not say principally used for transportation. Chandris, Inc. v. Latsis already rejects a snapshot approach to employment connection and distinguishes temporary repair from withdrawal. Southwest Marine, Inc. v. Gizoni keeps the two statutory remedies coherent without making occupational designation dispositive. Applying the ordinary definition resolves the dredge premise while avoiding an automatic finding that every person working aboard is a seaman.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Clarify the vessel predicate used by Chandris, Inc. v. Latsis; preserve Southwest Marine, Inc. v. Gizoni and Harbor Tug & Barge Co. v. Papai.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Ultimate seaman status and capacity-specific negligence require factual application on remand; the vessel ruling does not decide them.

## 29. Karaha Bodas Co., L.L.C. v. Perusahaan Pertambangan Minyak Dan Gas Bumi Negara

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Conditions:** Subject to any intervening effective decision governing the issue.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated rule, disposition and remedy.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the recognition judgment on the presented annulment question.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the award recognized and enforceable through ordinary lawful procedures. Do not revive the prior foreign anti-suit injunction or its dependent contempt consequence.
* **Remedial Reach:** The recognition judgment binds the parties and award before the court. It supplies no decree against nonparties or automatic right to seize immune property.
* **Practical Relief:** The enforcing court proceeds with the existing judgment and separately determines any asset-specific execution objection. Pertamina cannot defeat recognition merely by producing the Indonesian annulment; Karaha Bodas must still use the lawful execution process for any collection.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Country of governing arbitral law under Article V(1)(e)

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The prior decision expressly reserved the separate recognition appeal, so this judgment is reviewable without reopening the anti-suit disposition.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Indonesian annulment satisfies Article V(1)(e).
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the reserved Convention construction while applying the prior decision’s recognition-versus-coercion distinction.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate, covering the Convention’s country-of-governing-law provision. A rule deciding all foreign judgments would exceed the treaty question. A rule treating this single award as exceptional would leave the contractual distinction unexplained.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** For Article V(1)(e), the country under whose law an award was made is the country whose arbitration law governs the proceeding, not every country whose substantive contract law the tribunal applies. The party resisting recognition must establish annulment or suspension by a competent authority within that provision. The Swiss seat and arbitral procedure govern here; the Indonesian substantive-law clause does not make the Indonesian annulment a qualifying defense.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Recognition of awards where substantive governing law differs from the seat’s arbitral procedural law. Express selection of another arbitral procedural regime must be established from the actual agreement and applicable law.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Where substantive contract law and arbitral procedure differ, the enforcing court identifies the governing procedural law before evaluating Article V(1)(e). Article VI permits a discretionary adjournment for proceedings before a competent primary authority and, on the enforcing party’s application, suitable security. An invalid secondary-country annulment supplies no defense under Article V(1)(e).
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Convention’s structure assigns supervisory annulment and secondary recognition different roles. Equating substantive law with arbitral law would create multiple annulment forums from ordinary contract clauses and disregard the parties’ chosen arbitral seat. The earlier Karaha Bodas Co., LLC v. Perusahaan Pertambangan Minyak Dan Gas Bumi Negara rejected extraordinary restraint because ordinary recognition rules could address the foreign result. Applying those rules now is consistent with that decision rather than an evasion of it.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Karaha Bodas Co., LLC v. Perusahaan Pertambangan Minyak Dan Gas Bumi Negara’s separation of recognition and anti-suit relief; resolve its express primary-authority reservation at the scope presented.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The effect of an annulment by a competent primary authority is outside these facts. Execution immunity and particular assets are outside the recognition question.
