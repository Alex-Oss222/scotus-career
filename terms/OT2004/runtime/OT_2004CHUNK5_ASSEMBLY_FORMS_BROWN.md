# Brown — form and propositions before assignment

Fixed before the March 22 Brown recount: one signed plenary opinion resolves the review-law issue and the independently sufficient section 2254(d)(1) ground. Part I states the applicable post-enactment application rule, the November 23, 1992 substantive cutoff and the distinct statutory inquiry. Part II concludes that the state court's complete contextual application remains within the objective statutory range and reverses relief on this claim. It expressly preserves the qualified all-evidence direction, repeated prosecutorial legal exclusion, absence of an express judicial correction, full mitigating presentation and alternative weight arguments. It assumes the governing mitigation protection required for the statutory inquiry and supplies no independent no-constitutional-error holding.

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer support the full statutory lead. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg agree with review law but would affirm sentence relief because constitutional error, objective unreasonableness and failure of the State's separate actual-effect burden are established. Their final remedy is controlled by the frozen BROWN_REMEDY_CORRECTION, not the superseded remedial return. The Chief agrees with the statutory-gateway and constitutional-error grounds of that dissent but requires a separate full-record harmlessness adjudication and a conditional sentence remedy only if the State fails its burden. His different mandate requires its own partial concurrence and dissent. The three-Justice dissent may be joined by him only in its gateway and constitutional portions, not its completed harmlessness determination or affirmance.

The common statutory opinion already resolves the case without expanding mitigation doctrine or deciding whether the trial was independently constitutional. That formulation honors Scalia and Thomas's recorded constitutional reservation without manufacturing a separate writing to repeat it. The distinction that matters to Breyer is part of the Court's common ground; no redundant separate concurrence is needed. No historical writing or author is automatically imported. These form choices concern the actual legal work, before counts or author selection.

No Associate commitment changes in assembly. The three alternative mandates remain reversal, affirmance and partial affirmance with remedial vacatur and remand. They will not be combined into a fictitious majority on constitutional error or harmlessness. One author can carry the complete statutory lead.
