#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm summary judgment on the disparate-impact claim on the reasonable-factors ground, while rejecting the categorical exclusion of that statutory theory.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** No impact-based backpay, compensation or injunction is awarded. The judgment concerns the impact claim under review; separately surviving intentional-treatment litigation keeps its own posture.
* **Remedial Reach:** The participating employees’ impact claim against this pay plan is resolved. No unlawful policy has been proved, so no class-wide compensation or change to municipal pay schedules is ordered.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court enters the mandate on the impact branch and may proceed on any independently surviving claim. The City need not recalculate salaries on the rejected theory.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: ADEA impact liability and this pay plan

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The employees challenge their own compensation and the statutory question is preserved. The alternative defense is a legal basis for judgment only insofar as the record leaves no material factual dispute.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether neutral employment practices producing age-related disadvantage can violate the ADEA, and whether this plan does so.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve the question Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins expressly left open, applying the enacted reasonable-factors limitation without changing intentional-treatment law.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule must govern the statutory claim category and its defense. A broader rule importing Title VII’s amended necessity requirement would rewrite the ADEA. A narrower rule confined to police pay would leave the same statutory question unresolved for other employers.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The ADEA permits a claim that an identified neutral employment practice causes a disadvantage to older employees, subject to the employer’s defense that the differentiation rests on reasonable factors other than age. The employees must prove the practice, protected-age disadvantage and causal connection by the ordinary civil standard; the employer bears the statutory defense. Reasonableness concerns the actual factor and its relation to the employment objective, without a universal least-restrictive-alternative requirement. Here rank, tenure and the adjustment of entry salaries to the surrounding market establish that defense on the reviewed record.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers effects-based claims under §623(a)(2), not a free-standing entitlement to equal percentage raises or compensation based on age alone. Deliberate use of tenure as an age proxy remains subject to intentional-treatment law.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Ordinary job criteria remain challengeable on identified impact and causation, but reasonable nonage factors need not be indispensable or the least disparate conceivable choice.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins separates years of service from chronological age and preserves liability when one masks the other. That distinction supports treating this compensation plan’s tenure component as nonage while leaving the separate effects inquiry available. The reasonable-factors clause limits the effects-oriented prohibition without making it redundant. The record identifies a concrete compensation objective and a factor reasonably fitted to it; no finding of animus is necessary to decide this branch.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins: reaffirm its treatment rule and answer its impact reservation. General Dynamics Land Systems, Inc. v. Cline: preserve directional protection. No overruling.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The separately proceeding intentional-treatment claim lies outside the granted disparate-impact question.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Eighth Circuit’s categorical restriction and remand for review of the actual stay under a bounded discretion standard.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Retain the federal proceeding during prompt consideration of the stay’s predicates. The district court must identify the reason for nonexhaustion, the claims’ potential merit and any deliberate delay, and set reasonable exhaustion-and-return conditions if the stay remains warranted.
* **Remedial Reach:** Relief preserves Rhines’s pending application only. It creates no writ, damages, release or automatic reopening for other prisoners.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must decide whether the existing sixty-day conditions and actual state-court progress satisfy the rule. If a stay is refused, it must permit a meaningful election to delete the unexhausted claims, with the neutral warning required by Pliler v. Ford only if its unrepresented-prisoner and qualifying court-directed-election predicates are met. Rhines had appointed counsel for the amended federal petition, so that notice holding does not automatically govern this represented election. Any dismissal must identify its actual effect on timely federal review.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A bounded stay preserving exhaustion and review

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Rhines filed timely, and dismissal after judicial processing could eliminate review. The question concerns disposition of that pending action, not creation of a new application after expiration.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the district court can retain jurisdiction while Rhines promptly exhausts the identified claims.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve reserved stay authority as a new procedural holding, leaving Duncan v. Walker’s statutory interpretation and Pliler v. Ford’s separate protections intact.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule addresses recurring mixed-petition management and supplies a workable standard. A broader automatic stay would excuse strategic nonexhaustion. A narrower remedy for Rhines alone would leave identical filing traps to arbitrary discretion.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A district court may stay a timely mixed habeas petition when the prisoner shows a reasonable justification for nonexhaustion, the unexhausted claims are not plainly meritless, and the record does not show abusive or intentionally dilatory litigation. The prisoner bears the factual showing by the ordinary civil standard. The court must impose reasonable deadlines for seeking state relief and returning, accounting for delay outside the prisoner’s control. Where those predicates are met and dismissal would defeat timely review, refusal of a stay requires a reason compatible with the statute rather than a categorical prohibition.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs retained timely mixed applications. It does not toll every federal filing, disregard state procedural requirements, guarantee relief on any claim or reopen a terminated case.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts retain only timely applications under this stay rule and require prompt exhaustion and return. Reopening a closed action requires an independently valid procedural ground.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Duncan v. Walker holds that Congress excluded federal pendency from statutory tolling; retaining an existing action does not contradict that holding. Pliler v. Ford recognizes that courts must not turn a procedural election into avoidable loss of timely review, but expressly reserves stays. A bounded retention rule gives state courts their first opportunity while preserving the federal filing Congress allowed. Rhines’s actual reasons and progress must be found rather than supplied by appellate assumption.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Duncan v. Walker: reaffirm the tolling rule and answer its stay reservation. Pliler v. Ford: preserve its distinct notice, causation, diligence and lawful-remedy rules.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Ultimate constitutional merits and any future amendment are outside this procedural question.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the jurisdictional dismissal and remand.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore the federal action for determination of genuinely live jurisdictional defenses, state-law preclusion under §1738, and any remaining merits issues. No duplicative recovery or reconsideration of the Delaware judgment is authorized.
* **Remedial Reach:** The relief restores these parties’ federal proceeding only. It does not invalidate the state judgment or resolve other joint-venture litigation.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must identify each remaining claim, the relief it seeks and the preclusive effect of the state judgment. It may dispose of claims on established defenses, but cannot repeat the rejected jurisdictional extinction rationale.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: No jurisdictional extinction from a parallel judgment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The federal suit preceded the state judgment and asserts injury from the underlying royalty conduct. Exxon’s state-court success does not turn that complaint into an appellate challenge.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a later state judgment automatically terminates original federal jurisdiction.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Jordahl’s distinction between judgment-based and independently actionable injury to the unresolved filing-sequence question, preserving Quackenbush’s separation of jurisdiction and abstention.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A rule about the source of injury and filing sequence resolves a recurring jurisdictional error. A broader exemption from preclusion would contradict §1738. A narrower case-only rule would leave the same confusion in every parallel proceeding.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A federal district court does not lose original jurisdiction merely because a parallel state action reaches judgment while the federal action is pending. The prohibition on district-court appellate review applies when a state-court loser brings a federal action seeking relief from injury caused by an already rendered state judicial judgment and invites its rejection. An independent claim remains governed by jurisdictional statutes, preclusion and any properly applicable abstention doctrine, each under its own requirements.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This governs civil original actions, including overlapping state and federal litigation. It does not create a district-court appeal, excuse an absent jurisdictional basis or displace congressionally authorized habeas review.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A complaint nominally styled as damages still fails if its injury and requested relief actually require appellate rejection of an earlier state judgment. A contrary judgment on an independently arising claim instead invokes preclusion.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Jordahl barred damages counts whose asserted wrong was the legal error of state injunctions, while preserving claims based on independently actionable injury. Exxon’s royalty claims concern underlying conduct and preceded the state judgment. Section 1257 does not convert that original action into an appeal when parallel litigation ends; this resolves the timing question Jordahl did not decide. Quackenbush likewise requires a lawful source before surrendering federal adjudication. SABIC’s finality concerns belong in preclusion or mootness analysis, not automatic jurisdictional extinction.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Jordahl v. Democratic Party of Virginia: reaffirm its claim-specific appellate boundary and resolve the previously unanswered pending-action timing question. Rooker v. Fidelity Trust Co. and District of Columbia Court of Appeals v. Feldman: preserve their appellate-review boundary. Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co.: reaffirm source-specific analysis. No overruling.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** SABIC’s remaining immunity arguments and Delaware preclusion issues were not resolved by the appellate jurisdictional ruling and require their own adjudication.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment permitting removal notwithstanding §22(a) and remand for return of the covered individual Securities Act actions to state court.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Direct the lower courts to identify the covered actions and issue the corresponding state-court remand orders. No securities damages, bankruptcy distribution or adjudication of contribution liability is awarded.
* **Remedial Reach:** Relief covers the actions participating in the reviewed forum judgment. It does not dissolve the separate federal class litigation or adjudicate unlisted investors’ claims.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must transmit the covered actions to their originating state courts and cease merits adjudication of them after remand. Federal bankruptcy protections, including any independently applicable stay, remain governed by their own terms.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The express nonremoval command remains effective

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The certified appeal properly presents a forum question before securities liability is determined. An order denying remand is not an order remanding that the statutory review bars protect.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §1452 authorizes this removal despite §22(a).
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve an open statutory interaction; preserve both enactments rather than imply an unstated bankruptcy exception to the specific nonremoval command.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule addresses all individual Securities Act cases within the same provisions. A broader immunity from bankruptcy jurisdiction would exceed the removal question. A narrower preference confined to these pension funds would lack a textual basis.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 1452(a) does not remove an individual Securities Act action from §22(a)’s express prohibition on removal when no enacted exception applies. Bankruptcy subject-matter jurisdiction and removal authorization are distinct; a statute empowering removal generally can operate alongside a claim-specific command preserving the state forum. Defendants therefore cannot establish removal authority merely by proving a potential effect on the debtor’s estate.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding governs the statutory category and leaves §22(a)’s express exceptions, original federal actions, bankruptcy claims administration and independent stay provisions intact.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** The ruling preserves the express nonremoval protection for covered individual Securities Act actions; other removal prohibitions require their own text and context.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress used a direct prohibition addressed to removal of a defined class of cases and retained a particular exception. The omission in §1452 of §1441’s saving phrase does not itself expressly repeal that prohibition. Things Remembered, Inc. v. Petrarca treats bankruptcy procedure as capable of coexistence with generally applicable limits, rather than a self-executing override. Centralization may be efficient, but statutory efficiency does not create the omitted exception.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Things Remembered, Inc. v. Petrarca and Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co.: preserve their review-channel rules; neither is extended into a new jurisdictional prohibition.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The exact sufficiency of defendants’ estate-related claims is unnecessary after the dispositive nonremoval ruling and remains outside the holding.

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the categorical no-new-fact ruling and remand for consideration of Johnson’s timely vacatur-based motion, subject to the actual notice date and independent merits requirements.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the district court to determine the order’s scope and whether the removed conviction actually supported the enhanced sentence. If the enhancement lacks a necessary predicate under the applicable sentencing law, vacate the sentence to that extent and resentence through the authorized §2255 procedure.
* **Remedial Reach:** Only Johnson’s federal sentence is before the Court. State convictions are already governed by the state vacatur; no other prisoner’s final judgment is reopened.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must compare the vacatur order, the original predicate findings and the lawful sentencing range. It must enter a reasoned merits disposition; it may not dismiss solely because Johnson failed to create the vacatur event earlier. No immediate release is ordered.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The fact is the operative vacatur event

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Johnson has obtained independent state invalidation and promptly filed federally. The legal premise excluding every vacatur from the factual trigger is dispositive and properly presented.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the later order can trigger paragraph 6(4), and what diligence that paragraph requires.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve the open statutory question without adding a separate deadline for prosecuting state collateral litigation.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule covers a class of later predicate-removal events under the enacted trigger. A broader rule restarting time whenever law changes would erase the separate new-right provision. A narrower rule limited to Georgia orders would lack statutory support.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An order vacating a conviction used to enhance a federal sentence is a fact supporting a challenge to that sentence, and the limitation period runs from when the prisoner could discover that order through due diligence. The statute requires diligence in discovering the supporting event and timely filing after it; it does not impose an additional federal deadline for obtaining a state order that did not yet exist. The movant must prove the order, the discovery date and the predicate’s material connection to the asserted claim.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers actual changes to the status of a particular predicate conviction. It does not convert a new judicial interpretation, a repeated legal argument or an unsuccessful collateral filing into a newly existing fact.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A new order changing a particular predicate conviction’s status differs from a new legal interpretation. Ordinary discovery diligence and the remaining §2255 prerequisites govern.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Custis v. United States and Daniels v. United States require the ordinary state review channel and preserve only authorized later federal relief. Johnson used that channel. Those decisions do not promise success, but neither supplies a statutory diligence deadline for creating a state judgment. Congress measured discovery of supporting facts. Reconstructing when a state court might have granted an earlier hypothetical petition adds a different condition and invites speculation about a proceeding that never occurred. Finality is protected by the actual one-year trigger and remaining collateral-review restrictions.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Custis v. United States and Daniels v. United States: preserve the channel rule and its separate procedural prerequisites; neither is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The exact sentence reduction requires the vacatur’s scope and the original sentencing record; those factual and remedial matters remain for remand.

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The trustee’s objection turns on the legal character of actual IRAs, so eligibility can be resolved without finding a universal support amount.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether these IRAs are similar retirement plans providing an age-related right to payment.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve the exemption question left open by Patterson v. Shumate through the enacted text and actual account restrictions.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A common statutory category needs an administrable interpretation. The exemption remains limited by the statute’s support requirement and applicable exceptions, with the trustee bearing the objection burden under the bankruptcy rules.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Patterson v. Shumate protects an independently qualified exclusion and deliberately does not answer this exemption. The ordinary meaning of similarity asks for the shared retirement-income function rather than identical restrictions. A ten-percent loss is a meaningful condition on full access; its removal at the statutory age supplies the causal link. The exemption’s reference to tax-qualified accounts in its exception confirms that qualifying accounts are not categorically outside the broader provision.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Patterson v. Shumate: preserve the exclusion rule and resolve a distinct reserved exemption question.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The reasonably necessary support amount and any preserved account-specific objection require findings not supplied by categorical eligibility review.

* **Precedent Treatment:** New York v. FERC and California ex rel. Lockyer v. FERC: apply their distinct authority and reasoned-review rules without extending jurisdiction.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Outside the question presented: direct regulatory authority over foreign facilities. Alleged discriminatory transactions or later rate injury require ordinary application to their actual records, not a new legal reservation.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand the judgment sustaining the device claim on inflation alone.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require identification of the claimed economic loss and causal connection under the existing pleading rules. Permit consideration of a properly supported amendment; do not enter a factual no-loss judgment or impose a mandatory proof model.
* **Remedial Reach:** The disposition concerns the reviewed device-related claim and the represented purchasers to the extent that claim is properly asserted. It awards no damages and does not determine the separate profitability branch.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must explain the pleading defect and address any timely amendment under the applicable rules. If no sufficient causal-loss theory is pleaded after lawful amendment proceedings, dismiss that branch; if it is, proceed to proof under the statutory burden.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Purchase-price inflation does not substitute for causal loss

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The complaint’s asserted rule and the appellate holding present a legal question. No finding about the truth of the representations or final amount of loss is needed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether inflation alone supplies the substantive and pleading requirement of loss causation.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing causal-injury requirement and clarify its operation without withdrawing the controlling existing private action or adding a heightened pleading standard.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule separates two recurring elements of securities fraud. A broader exclusive-disclosure test would foreclose causal mechanisms not before the Court. A narrower device-specific rule would leave the inflation error intact elsewhere.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A private securities plaintiff must identify and ultimately prove an economic loss caused by the alleged fraud; payment of an inflated market price alone does not establish that element. At pleading, the plaintiff must give fair notice of the loss and the causal mechanism, not prove an event study or eliminate every competing cause. At adjudication, the plaintiff bears causation and recoverable loss by a preponderance, with recovery excluding losses attributable solely to independent causes.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs the reviewed public-market damages claim and comparable causal-loss allegations. It does not abolish qualifying assistance liability, presume an efficient market or decide a special measure of damages in a different transaction.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Loss may follow gradual disclosure or materialization of a concealed risk if alleged and proved; this record establishes neither. Courts require actual causal loss and cannot presume it from either purchase-price inflation or a later price decline alone.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. expressly retains causation and recoverable loss even for culpable assistance. A broader group of liable actors does not broaden the compensable injury. Swierkiewicz v. Sorema N.A. prevents turning that element into a trial-proof pleading checklist. Because the complaint connects the device fraud only to inflation, the appellate rationale cannot sustain it; the distinct earnings announcement is not an interchangeable causal fact.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A.: preserve private action and injury requirements. Swierkiewicz v. Sorema N.A.: apply fair-notice discipline.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Outside the accepted question: independent complaint branches and a comprehensive final damages methodology. Ordinary pleading amendment and causation factfinding are governed by the rule announced here.

##### Issue 2: Antitrust elements require their own adjudication

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The antitrust claim was dismissed on a categorical patent-scope ground; the appeal squarely presents its validity, and the alternative separate-product ground was not resolved below.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether staying within patent scope necessarily defeats the tying claim.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend CSU, L.L.C. v. Xerox Corporation’s rejection of conclusive patent-scope immunity from monopolization to the presented tying defense, applying Kodak’s separate §1 requirements. No presumption of liability follows.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. CSU’s reason for rejecting conclusive patent-scope immunity applies to the asserted tying defense, while Kodak supplies the independent §1 elements. A broader duty to license would discard those elements. A crop-specific exception would lack a legal basis for distinguishing otherwise comparable ties.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A restriction within the patent grant does not, for that reason alone, defeat a Sherman Act §1 tying claim. McFarling must establish separate products by consumer demand, conditioning, sufficient tying-market power, a substantial amount of affected tied commerce, and the competitive injury and causation required for his claim. Lawful patent exclusion retains substantial justificatory force; a preferred licensing arrangement or royalty expense alone establishes no violation.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule reaches the independent tying counterclaim and analogous patent-based defenses, without converting refusal to license into liability.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts may grant renewed summary judgment for actual failure of an element, but may not substitute patent scope for that inquiry.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** CSU holds that lawful exclusion does not conclusively immunize independently unlawful monopolization. The same statutory coexistence supports rejecting conclusive immunity for a properly established §1 tie, without importing §2’s offense elements. Kodak applies Jefferson Parish’s demand-based product distinction and requires the remaining tying elements. Courts must examine the asserted tie under that framework before invoking a categorical tying rule; patent scope alone establishes neither per se legality nor per se liability. Because the appellate court bypassed the separate-product issue, it must address the preserved record on remand.
* **Precedent Treatment:** CSU, L.L.C. v. Xerox Corporation: extend its rejection of conclusive scope immunity to the presented tying defense. Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services, Inc.: apply its distinct-product and remaining tying requirements.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.
