# Sabree — form and propositions fixed before assignment

Event: Sabree ex rel. Sabree v. Richman, March 29, 2005. This receipt precedes any assignment recount or authorship selection for this event.

Form: one signed Opinion of the Court after full merits submission. All nine Justices join every governing proposition and affirm the Third Circuit's reversal of dismissal. Their differences in prior statutory cases yield no remaining partial join or separate-writing function at the agreed scope.

The governing propositions are: (I) sections 1396a(a)(8) and (10), read with the dated section 1396d(a) payment definition and paragraph (15), create personal covered-assistance and reasonable-promptness rights for eligible beneficiaries of the adopted intermediate-care benefit, subject to the actual coverage, clinical and benefit conditions; (II) the combined section 1396a(a)(3) fair-hearing and section 1396c funding mechanisms establish neither express exclusion nor a comprehensive remedial scheme incompatible with this section 1983 action under Blessing and completed Rancho; and (III) prospective enforcement against the responsible official proceeds through findings on the assistance owed, unreasonable delay, causation, lawful compliance and individual or properly established representative scope, with effective measurable relief after proof.

The current payment-based definition controls. The lower court's express financing-versus-furnishing legal reservation remains for the District Court on the developed record; neither nominal willingness to pay nor a universal entitlement to services is preselected. Benefit, clinical and review qualifications operate at their actual statutory stages. Accepted eligible waiting-list claimants need not already be receiving the allegedly withheld treatment before they may challenge delay. The pre-1975 public-institution expenditure provision is not a current eligibility obstacle. Funding administration cannot erase the statutory entitlement.

Actual Blessing supplies the general rights and foreclosure rules. Suter's particular result and the enacted state-plan corrections retain their scopes; Gonzaga's unjoined general reformulation does not displace Blessing. Medicaid's individual hearing must be considered together with federal supervision. Rancho's actual personal judicial action, filing period and expedited adjudication supply a concrete structural distinction, not a categorical rule that administrative remedies can never foreclose section 1983.

The Chief's approved core is fully compatible with each proposition and the remand. No standing fallback is used. No Associate commitment changes in assembly. This form and scope are fixed on legal grounds and will not be changed to affect assignment counts.
