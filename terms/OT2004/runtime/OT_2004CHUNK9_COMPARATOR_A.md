# OT2004 chunk 9 — COMPARATOR A

## 76. Alaska v. United States

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Court rejected Alaska's exceptions on June 6, 2005. The Alexander Archipelago conclusions were unanimous; the Glacier Bay retention conclusion was 6-3.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Kennedy wrote for the Court. Justice Scalia dissented from the Glacier Bay portion, joined by Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justice Thomas.
* **Historical Holding:** The archipelago claims did not establish the asserted inland-water title, and the federal government retained the Glacier Bay submerged lands. The distinct Tongass claim had been removed by disclaimer.
* **Historical Authority:** 545 U.S. 75; No. 128, Original.
* **Historical Sources:** [Official United States Reports opinion](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep545/usrep545075/usrep545075.pdf).

## 77. Spector v. Norwegian Cruise Line Ltd.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Court reversed and remanded. Five Justices supported the full judgment; Justice Thomas additionally supported application to matters outside internal affairs but would exclude structural obligations. Three Justices favored affirmance across the board.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Kennedy wrote the Court's Parts I, II-A-1 and II-B-2 for himself and Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; other passages had only Stevens and Souter, while Part III-A also had Thomas. Ginsburg concurred in part and in the judgment with Breyer. Thomas concurred and dissented in part. Scalia dissented with Rehnquist and O'Connor, and Thomas joined only Part I-A of that dissent.
* **Historical Holding:** Title III applies to foreign cruise ships in United States waters, but particular applications implicating internal affairs require further analysis. The different opinions disagreed about the breadth and operation of that limitation.
* **Historical Authority:** 545 U.S. 119; No. 03-1388.
* **Historical Sources:** [Reported opinion and separate writings](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/545/119/).
