### GDF Realty Investments, Ltd. v. Norton, Fifth Circuit No. 01-51099

Merits decision, January 24, 2005

#### Chronology and Posture

On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, No. 01-51099, 326 F.3d 622; rehearing denied, 362 F.3d 286. Submitted on full merits briefing; decided January 24, 2005.

The question is whether Congress may apply the Endangered Species Act's taking prohibition to six listed, noncommercial cave species confined to two Texas counties as an integral part of the Act's interstate wildlife regulation. The owners seek declaratory and injunctive protection for their proposed commercial development. The court of appeals affirmed summary judgment rejecting their constitutional challenge.

The full Court participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Court affirms the rejection of the owners’ Commerce Clause challenge and denies their requested constitutional declaration and injunction.

#### Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS and GINSBURG, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Necessary inclusion of intrastate listed wildlife

**The Court holds:** Congress may include unauthorized taking of listed intrastate wildlife in the Endangered Species Act when the taking class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of carrying out the Act's independently valid interstate wildlife regulation. The Government must identify that regulation, the covered class and a genuine functional relationship, which the court independently assesses; an outside-class application, invalid underlying interstate regime, or nominal or pretextual relationship cannot obtain authority from this rule, but a species' local range or absence of trade does not itself defeat inclusion.

**Authority:** Breyer's opinion, joined in full by all eight other participating Justices, controls the rule, this application and the remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the rule already established in National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt and applied to a single-state listed species in Rancho Viejo, LLC v. Norton. The Act regulates interstate exploitation, movement, importation, exportation and commercial disposition of listed wildlife. Protecting those populations against unauthorized depletion preserves both the objects and the enforceability of that regulation. The taking class, the valid interstate regime and their operative relationship are identifiable independently of a general conservation objective.

United States v. Lopez requires a fitted interstate connection before aggregation; the necessary-inclusion relationship supplies that connection here. Courts determine its legal boundaries while respecting reasonable congressional judgments within them. The owners' demand for a separate market or independent substantial interstate effect for each protected species would replace the established class rule with the species-by-species requirement Rancho Viejo rejected. Their six cave species occupy the same protected class, and the accepted record identifies no material defect in the statutory relationship. The functional statutory ground resolves the requested constitutional exemption without an additional species-specific evidentiary proceeding.

Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon supplies the separate statutory requirements for habitat-caused injury, proximate causation and ordinary foreseeability. Officials applying the Act must establish those requirements, and any permission remains governed by section 10. Constitutional authority and statutory liability therefore retain their separate work.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- **National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt:** Reaffirmed and applied at its necessary-inclusion scope; its identification of an independently valid regime, covered class and genuine relationship remains controlling.
- **Rancho Viejo, LLC v. Norton:** Reaffirmed and applied to these listed cave species; its single-state application and outside-class, invalid-regime and nominal-or-pretextual limits retain their force.
- **United States v. Lopez:** Applied to require a fitted constitutional connection before aggregation; the identified necessary-inclusion mechanism satisfies that requirement.
- **Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon:** Preserved as the separate statutory rule governing actual habitat-caused injury, causation and foreseeability.

The necessary-inclusion rule governs other intrastate listed species within the same valid interstate wildlife system. A local range or absent species-specific market does not entitle a species to a constitutional exemption or require an independent species-effects hearing. Courts must identify the actual class, valid interstate regulation and genuine population-protection or enforcement relationship, preserving challenges to outside-class applications, invalid underlying regimes and nominal or pretextual relationships. Statutory liability, permitting and any properly presented compensation claim retain their distinct requirements.

#### Source Notes

U.S. Const., Art. I, §8; 16 U.S.C. §§1532(19), 1538(a)(1)(B), 1539; GDF Realty Investments, Ltd. v. Norton, 326 F.3d 622, 362 F.3d 286. The accepted record identifies six listed subterranean species in Travis and Williamson Counties and cross-motions for summary judgment without a material factual dispute. Scientific travel and specimen transfers are distinct from the absence of a commercial market.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The judgment rejecting the owners’ Commerce Clause claim remains in force, and the lower courts must implement it. The requested constitutional declaration and injunction are denied. Enforcement and permitting proceed under the Act’s governing elements, exceptions and remedial requirements. Any otherwise proper statutory, permitting or compensation proceeding follows its own governing channel and requirements.

**End of entry: GDF Realty Investments, Ltd. v. Norton, merits decision, January 24, 2005.**

### Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Taft, Sixth Circuit No. 01-4124

Merits decision, January 24, 2005

#### Chronology and Posture

On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, No. 01-4124, 353 F.3d 436. Submitted on full merits briefing; decided January 24, 2005.

The questions concern the actual reach of Ohio's revised procedure prohibition despite its express dilation-and-evacuation (D&E) exclusion, and whether its life-and-health provision protects necessary comparative-risk reduction and grave mental-health care. The court of appeals reversed the district court's permanent injunction. Providers seek effective declaratory and injunctive protection; Ohio seeks enforcement of the revised law.

The full Court participated at submission and decision.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The revised prohibition turns on actual acts and distinct exclusions

**The Court holds:** Ohio Rev. Code §2919.151 reaches the complete intentional procedure it defines, with each act and mental element satisfied in sequence; its express exclusions preserve ordinary D&E, suction curettage and suction aspiration. The Court rejects the contention that overlapping labels, removal of a fetal part, or an intact delivery alone subjects ordinary D&E to the prohibition, while conduct satisfying the complete defined procedure remains governed by the actual prohibition and its life-and-health terms.

**Authority:** Part I’s eight-Justice statutory-coverage coalition, identified above, controls that disposition. Stone-Zsela also joins the legal propositions requiring all actual elements and giving each exclusion effect, but withholds the completed coverage application.

**Controlling explanation:** Voinovich requires examination of the actual procedure, fault and alternatives rather than invalidation by nomenclature. The revised definition requires intentional cervical dilation; intentional extraction of an intact fetus beyond the vaginal opening, to at least the lower torso through the navel for breech presentation or the complete head for head-first presentation, while some of the body remains inside; then an intentional partial evacuation of intracranial contents, intentional compression of the head, or another intentional act, in each case known by the performer to cause death; and completion of vaginal delivery. Each condition performs independent work.

Division (F) expressly excludes ordinary D&E and the two suction methods. Division (A)(1) separately distinguishes D&E from dilation and extraction. Division (G) protects performance or attempted performance of a legal abortion when the death-causing act precedes partial birth even if death follows it; that timing exclusion is distinct from the method exclusion. Division (E) separately protects the pregnant woman from criminal responsibility under these prohibitions. The revised text thus supplies protections absent from the different statute considered in Stenberg. A prosecutor's assurance supplies none of this construction's force: the enacted acts, intentions and exclusions do. Actual use of a clinically suitable alternative and the health consequences of substitution remain material to the separate constitutional inquiry below.

##### II. Necessary care includes serious comparative risk and grave mental-health danger

**The Court holds:** A State may not enforce an abortion-method prohibition when competent individualized medical judgment establishes that substitution creates a material risk of death or serious health harm and the prohibited method is necessary for timely, safe and suitable care. Challengers must establish supported necessary-care applications, including grave medically established danger of severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury; unsupported preference is insufficient, professional disagreement alone does not defeat supported necessity, and usable protection must operate before catastrophic deterioration.

**Authority:** Part II and its necessary-care mandate control through the seven-Justice coalition identified in the opinion table.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey preserves the necessary-care baseline. Voinovich includes grave medically established mental danger, and Stenberg protects against serious risk imposed by compelled substitution when the prohibited method is necessary for safe and suitable care. Those rules distinguish supported necessary treatment from a marginal advantage or a physician's unsupported preference. They retain viability's separate role while protecting necessary care before and after viability. The civil constitutional inquiry leaves the separate statutory criminal burdens in their proper place.

Ohio's separate previability and postviability provisions prohibit knowing performance when the procedure is unnecessary under their reasonable-medical-judgment life-or-health criterion. Their defined serious-risk category concerns a medically diagnosed condition complicating pregnancy and substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The court of appeals offered a prospective construction addressing future procedural risks, but its explanation's disjunctive wording cannot replace that enacted conjunction. A construction available under Ohio law must actually supply the required protection.

The reported findings identify conditional risks from repeated instrument passes, clotting disorders, blood loss, anesthesia, retained tissue, induction, major surgery and methods of causing death before partial delivery. They support inquiry into actual necessary-care applications rather than universal superiority of one method. The mental-health question likewise concerns grave danger from continued pregnancy combined with the physical method necessary for the indicated abortion, not only distress produced by choosing one technique. Detailed final findings on that question remain below. The new exclusions answer the scope objection but do not conclusively establish that every supported patient retains safe and suitable care. The health validation must therefore be vacated for application of the settled rule.

##### III. The complete framework governs remaining previability interference

**The Court holds:** After determining actual coverage and necessary-care protection, courts must apply Casey's complete six-step framework to remaining challenged previability applications. The challengers must establish the operative duty, actual affected population, causal connection and triggering interference; an independent baseline violation, obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is invalid, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring, and remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated justification and fit, including an answer to a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective.

**Authority:** Part III and its additional remand command control through the six-Justice coalition identified in the opinion table. The rule rests on those direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey's framework requires six connected inquiries: identify the actual obligation, exceptions and protected baseline; assess its real function, purpose and asserted permissible justification; establish attributable interference for a stable affected population, including supported subgroups and genuinely usable exceptions; apply the independent prohibitions and then the appropriate scrutiny to remaining burdens; protect clinical judgment through competent evidence and patient circumstances; and identify the violation and fit the relief to it. Cumulative effects matter without double counting, and alternatives count only when actually usable for the affected patients.

A substantial obstacle seriously impairs the practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary. Remaining meaningful interference is concrete, nontrivial added burden, not automatic invalidity. Below that threshold the heightened access inquiry ends, while independently prohibited purpose and baseline injury retain their force. Reliable clinical evidence, experience and reasonable predictions can support prospective injury and justification. A professional association has no veto, bare preference supplies no necessity, and neither scientific certainty nor a new study in every case is required.

Kennedy's narrower approach would permit reasonable method regulation outside lost protected choice or necessary care. But the controlling Casey rule also protects against severe or meaningful interference even where some timely care remains available. Treating an alternative's label or the absence of a substantial obstacle as conclusive would erase those distinct protections. The court must therefore assess the remaining actual applications under the full rule, with the State's burdens arising only after the challengers establish the corresponding interference.

##### IV. Relief follows the proved violation and the actual procedural scope

**The Court holds:** When an application fails the governing constitutional rule, the decree must protect the affected patients and providers and bar the enforcement consequences dependent on that violation; a proved common-policy violation receives that coverage unless Ohio shows narrower relief fully cures it. The lower court must preserve independently lawful applications under Ohio severability law, promptly supply necessary interim protection when the ordinary equitable predicates are established, and expedite genuinely unresolved material facts while respecting the existing unappealed rulings.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice necessary-care coalition joins these remedial principles and their health application. Six also require their implementation for the complete residual-interference inquiry; Kennedy opposes only that additional command.

**Controlling explanation:** Voinovich requires lawful construction and relief fitted to the actual violation, including independent operation and Ohio severability limits. A court may apply a construction Ohio law permits; when the text cannot bear the required necessary-care protection, the remedy is protection against incompatible applications and their dependent sanctions. Anderson v. Spear requires relief adequate to correct a proved unlawful policy, while permitting a narrower remedy upon the State's showing of a complete cure. That rule operates after a violation and its scope are proved; it does not shift the providers' burden to establish constitutional injury.

The decree must identify the officials bound and the actual criminal, licensing and civil enforcement consequences within the court's lawful reach. The independent third-party civil-action standing dismissal and the unappealed recklessness construction remain effective. Expedition addresses material factual uncertainty; the current record may itself permit decision where the required predicates are established. Effective interim protection depends on the ordinary equitable findings, and final relief must make the surviving clinical and enforcement duties clear.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- **Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey:** Reaffirmed and applied. Its necessary-care baseline and complete six-step previability framework retain their respective controlling coalitions and burdens.
- **Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich:** Applied to actual acts, fault, usable alternatives, grave medically established mental-health danger and Ohio-law severability. The revised enactment receives its own construction rather than the former statute's alternative-method defense and burdens.
- **Stenberg v. Carhart:** Its necessary-care protection and treatment of supported serious comparative risk are reaffirmed. Its ordinary-D&E coverage determination is distinguished because the present sequential definition and express exclusions differ.
- **Anderson v. Spear:** Its corrective-policy remedy is applied to a proved common violation: relief protects the people governed by that violation unless the State establishes a fully effective narrower cure, while lawful provisions retain independent operation.

The categorical ordinary-D&E objection is resolved against the providers. The seven-Justice necessary-care rule requires usable protection for supported serious comparative risk, including grave mental danger from continued pregnancy combined with a method necessary for safe treatment; a preexisting physical complication cannot be an exclusive constitutional prerequisite. The six-Justice complete previability framework also governs remaining applications, with each State burden arising only after the challengers establish the corresponding interference. These legal rules are settled. Clinical application, lawful Ohio construction, severability and relief fitted to the proved violation remain for the lower court. The unappealed fault construction and independent civil-standing disposition remain effective.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part:** The Chief Justice joins the actual-elements rule, necessary-care holding, complete framework and implementing remedy. He would also vacate the conclusive statutory-coverage disposition. Voinovich makes clinically performed acts decisive, and the statutory D&E label supplies an answer only to the extent actual operation preserves the procedure used. He would require focused findings on that operation, usable alternatives and the affected patients instead of conclusively closing the coverage branch. He agrees that Stenberg's different definition cannot be imported and that neither a blanket method entitlement nor automatic invalidation follows. His disagreement concerns the additional coverage inquiry, not the health or full-framework remand he joins.

**Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part:** Kennedy joins the statutory disposition and the necessary-care vacatur and remedy. His Casey baseline position and Voinovich health joins preserve necessary life-and-health care, including grave medically established mental danger; his Stenberg position distinguishes supported comparative risk from an entitlement to a preferred technique. Ohio's new safeguards must receive their actual construction, but the conjunction and proper mental-danger theory require further application before conclusive validation. He would permit reasonable method regulation outside a denial of protected choice or necessary care and therefore opposes the additional command to justify residual meaningful interference under the full framework. His dissent is confined to that operative direction and its rationale; he supports the construction, clinical, severability and prompt interim-protection work required by the necessary-care holding.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas:** Both join the complete-elements interpretation and the rejection of categorical ordinary-D&E coverage. They would affirm the health disposition because they reject the constitutional abortion and necessary-health requirements imposed by Casey, Voinovich and Stenberg, continuing the grounds of their published separate opinions in those cases. Under their view, the defined intentional procedure may receive reasonable regulation for potential-life and medical-ethics purposes. Their disagreement concerns the governing constitutional rule; it does not turn conditional medical evidence into a finding that every substitute is equally safe. They acknowledge ordinary severability and remedial principles but oppose using the Court's constitutional premises to order health and residual-interference remands.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for the directed construction, clinical application and relief. Relief must protect the patients and providers governed by a proved unlawful common-policy application unless Ohio establishes that narrower relief fully cures the violation. The lower court must identify the officials bound and the dependent criminal, licensing and civil enforcement consequences within its lawful reach, preserve independently lawful applications under Ohio severability law, and define the surviving obligations and prohibited enforcement in terms usable by patients, clinicians and officials.

The lower court must promptly decide interim protection under the ordinary equitable predicates and enter necessary protection when those predicates are met. It must set an expedited evidentiary schedule for genuinely unresolved material facts. An injunction against dependent sanctions must match the proved constitutional application and lawful party scope. The unappealed recklessness construction and independent third-party civil-action standing dismissal remain in force.

### Karaha Bodas Co., L.L.C. v. Perusahaan Pertambangan Minyak Dan Gas Bumi Negara

Merits decision, February 22, 2005

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, 364 F.3d 274 (March 23, 2004). Submitted on full merits briefing; decided February 22, 2005.

The question is whether the Indonesian annulment defeats recognition under Article V(1)(e) of the Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards when the contracts selected Geneva arbitration, UNCITRAL rules and Indonesian governing law with particular Indonesian procedural references. The Fifth Circuit affirmed the United States recognition judgment and the denial of Rule 60(b) reopening. Pertamina seeks to set aside recognition on the annulment ground; Karaha Bodas seeks to retain it. The Court's March 9, 2004 decision concerned the separate foreign anti-suit injunction.

The full Court participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Court affirms the United States recognition judgment and the denial of Rule 60(b) reopening against the Indonesian-annulment challenge.

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The country whose arbitral law governs the award

**The Court holds:** For Article V(1)(e), the country under whose law an award was made is the country whose national arbitral procedural law governs the proceeding, rather than every country whose substantive law supplies the parties' contractual rights; the party resisting recognition must establish annulment or suspension by a competent authority in the country of making or that governing-law country. The enforcing court must examine the actual agreement and applicable law to determine whether a different national arbitral regime was selected, giving particular incorporated provisions their stated scope; the combined terms here establish Geneva arbitration without selecting Indonesian judicial supervision, so the Indonesian annulment supplies no Article V(1)(e) ground to refuse recognition or reopen the judgment.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion for the unanimous Court controls the rule, contractual application and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Convention distinguishes the legal system supervising an arbitration from the courts deciding whether its award will be recognized elsewhere. Its reference to the law under which an award was made concerns the arbitral proceeding, not every substantive law the tribunal applied. Treating an ordinary governing-law clause as an additional national annulment regime would erase that distinction. Section 207 requires confirmation unless a Convention ground for refusal or deferral is established.

Pertamina's strongest submission relies on the combined Indonesian references, not merely the general governing-law clause. The contracts choose Geneva and UNCITRAL arbitration. Their waivers prevent arbitrator appointments from terminating after six months and remove the specified arbitration-completion period under Articles 650.2 and 620.1. Their Section 641 arrangement renounces appeal and waives the specified appellate provisions. The affirmative Section 631 clause permits the tribunal to depart from strict legal rules inconsistent with the contract's spirit and the parties' intent, directing a correct and just interpretation and enforcement of its terms. Each provision performs that particular contractual work without assigning Indonesia national judicial supervision. The Paris hearing does not displace Geneva as the selected legal place and stated place of the award.

The earlier Karaha Bodas decision required assessment of ordinary Convention remedies before extraordinary restraint of foreign proceedings. That approach now permits a direct determination of the Indonesian order's domestic effect. Article VI likewise operates only upon an application to a qualifying authority: adjournment remains discretionary, and suitable security additionally requires the enforcing party's application. The country's qualification is therefore a necessary legal predicate, not a presumption supplied by comity.

**Question outside this review:** The effect of annulment by an authority satisfying Article V(1)(e) is outside the granted dispute as resolved on this record. The other Convention defenses were adjudicated below and are outside the granted review.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- **Karaha Bodas Co., LLC v. Perusahaan Pertambangan Minyak Dan Gas Bumi Negara (March 9, 2004):** Applies its separation of ordinary recognition adjudication from extraordinary foreign anti-suit relief. The present decision resolves the previously reserved allocation of annulment authority at the scope of the actual clauses and Indonesian order; the earlier injunction judgment and its necessity rule retain their force.

Recognition courts must identify the national arbitral procedural regime before giving an annulment effect under Article V(1)(e). Substantive-law clauses and particular incorporated provisions have their actual agreed scope; they do not alone confer national supervisory authority. A genuine selection of another arbitral procedural regime must be established from the agreement and applicable law. Under Article VI, a qualifying application permits, but does not require, adjournment when the enforcing court considers it proper; suitable security additionally requires the enforcing party’s application. The Indonesian order fails the qualifying-country condition here.

#### Source Notes

Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards, Arts. V(1)(e), VI; 9 U.S.C. §§201, 203, 207; Karaha Bodas Co., L.L.C. v. Perusahaan Pertambangan Minyak Dan Gas Bumi Negara, 364 F.3d 274 (CA5 2004). Joint Operation Contract, Art. 13.2(a), (b), (d), and Energy Sales Contract, §8.2(a), (b), (d). The contracts selected Geneva; the hearing occurred in Paris and the award stated Geneva as its place. The Swiss challenge ended for untimely payment of costs, rather than on the award's merits.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The United States recognition judgment and denial of Rule 60(b) relief remain in force. The award remains enforceable through ordinary lawful procedures, and the enforcing court determines any particular-asset execution objection in its appropriate proceeding. Review on the Indonesian-annulment ground is concluded. The foreign anti-suit injunction and its dependent civil-contempt consequence remain vacated under the Court’s March 9, 2004 judgment.

**End of entry: Karaha Bodas Co., L.L.C. v. Perusahaan Pertambangan Minyak Dan Gas Bumi Negara, merits decision, February 22, 2005.**

### Smith v. Massachusetts, No. 03-8661

Merits decision, February 22, 2005

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 1, 2004; decided February 22, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the Massachusetts Appeals Court.

The question is whether the Double Jeopardy Clause permits restoration of a count after an unqualified midtrial finding that the prosecution's evidence was insufficient and ensuing defense proceedings. The trial judge allowed Smith's required-finding motion on firearm possession, then retracted it before closing arguments after the prosecutor supplied additional legal authority. The same jury convicted Smith on that count and two assault counts. The Appeals Court affirmed on the view that ordinary reconsideration remained permissible during the continuing trial. Smith seeks removal of the firearm conviction and its punishment; Massachusetts seeks affirmance.

The full Court participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed in part and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The reversal concerns the firearm conviction and its dependent punishment. The two independent assault convictions remain operative.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, SOUTER and THOMAS, JJ., joined. BREYER, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which KENNEDY and GINSBURG, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### A completed insufficiency determination ends guilt adjudication on the count

**The Court holds:** Once a trial court completes an unqualified determination that the prosecution's proof is insufficient on a count, the Double Jeopardy Clause bars restoring it for further adjudication of guilt, including in the same jury trial and despite legal error. Completion is determined from the ruling, applicable procedure and subsequent trial conduct as a whole; an ordinary power to revisit interlocutory rulings does not keep an implemented, unqualified acquittal provisional after the defense phase proceeds without a reserved sufficiency question or an established procedure plainly giving that type of ruling provisional status.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined in full by the other five Justices supporting reversal, directly controls the rule, its application and the remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Price v. Vincent requires a substantive inquiry into completion and distinguishes that inquiry from federal habeas eligibility. This direct review presents the acquittal question itself. United States v. Martin Linen Supply Co. makes a failure-of-proof determination an acquittal regardless of its label; Smalis v. Pennsylvania bars renewed guilt factfinding after such an acquittal; Fong Foo v. United States preserves that protection despite error. United States v. Scott distinguishes a termination unrelated to factual guilt.

Rule 25(a) required a ruling at the close of the prosecution's evidence. The judge unqualifiedly allowed the motion, and the allowance was endorsed and entered. The prosecution rested without seeking reopening, the codefendant presented a witness, and both defendants rested. The judge reserved neither further argument nor a later sufficiency determination. This completed transition differs from Price's continuing consideration of a genuinely unsettled question. Smith's own testimony was unnecessary to completion.

The Commonwealth and dissent rely on the state's general common-law power to reconsider during a pending trial. That procedural rule does not itself make this completed finding of not guilty provisional. State procedure supplies the setting; federal law determines the acquittal's constitutional effect. Rule 42's correction of clerical mistakes likewise does not authorize reconsideration of a legal judgment. The later handgun authority showed a possible error in evaluating the evidence, not an unfinished ruling. The same jury's continued presence and the absence of demonstrated tactical harm do not permit renewed adjudication after completion. The firearm conviction and punishment must therefore be removed even though its sentence ran concurrently with the assault sentences.

**Question outside this record:** A clearly established procedure expressly making an insufficiency ruling provisional presents a different completion question and is outside the procedure presented here.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- **Price v. Vincent:** Applies its whole-episode substantive completion rule. Its independent federal habeas limitation retains its own scope; this direct review determines completion without that statutory barrier.
- **United States v. Martin Linen Supply Co.:** Applies the rule that substance, rather than a ruling's label, identifies an acquittal.
- **Smalis v. Pennsylvania:** Applies the prohibition on renewed guilt factfinding after an actual insufficiency acquittal, including further proceedings in the same prosecution.
- **Fong Foo v. United States:** Applies the finality of an actual acquittal despite legal error.
- **United States v. Scott:** Preserves the distinction between an acquittal and a termination unrelated to factual guilt.

Completed substantive acquittals receive the same protection in a continuing jury trial as in other trial forms. General interlocutory reconsideration authority cannot retrospectively reopen an accomplished insufficiency determination after the defense has proceeded. A genuinely tentative ruling remains subject to completion; a completed acquittal forecloses renewed guilt adjudication without a separate showing of tactical injury.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Breyer, dissenting, joined by Kennedy, Ginsburg.** The dissent would affirm the firearm conviction because the trial judge's midtrial ruling remained subject to Massachusetts's general common-law reconsideration power. It accepts Martin Linen's substantive acquittal inquiry, Fong Foo's protection despite legal error, and Price's rejection of a signature or jury-announcement prerequisite. Its disagreement concerns whether this proceeding had reached a completed acquittal at all.

Rule 25(a) required an immediate ruling, but the dissent reads the state court's account of Haskell as permitting reconsideration of that partial ruling while the trial remained pending. It would not demand an additional rule specifically naming insufficiency determinations or an express reservation by the trial judge. The same jury remained empaneled, correction preceded closing arguments and instructions, and the prosecutor relied on evidence already presented. The Appeals Court reported no asserted change in defense strategy, and Smith did not request reopening after correction. In the dissent's view those features show a continued original adjudication with an opportunity to defend.

The unqualified allowance, docket implementation and ensuing defense proceedings are the strongest contrary evidence. The dissent nevertheless distinguishes a conclusive statement of the judge's present assessment from an irrevocable disposition under the governing procedure. It reads Smalis as addressing further proceedings after appeal from a final acquittal. It does not permit reconsideration of an acknowledged completed acquittal merely because the ruling was wrong or no prejudice was shown; it finds the antecedent finality absent here.

#### Source Notes

U.S. Const., Amends. V, XIV; No. 03-8661; Massachusetts Rule of Criminal Procedure 25(a); Rule 42; Commonwealth v. Haskell, 438 Mass. 790, 784 N.E.2d 625 (2003); Commonwealth v. Sperrazza, 372 Mass. 667, 363 N.E.2d 673 (1977). The motion allowance was endorsed and entered, the jury was not notified, and the codefendant then presented a witness before both defendants rested. The Appeals Court's account that Smith claimed no changed strategy is a characterization of his litigation position.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

When the mandate issues, the Massachusetts courts must vacate the firearm conviction and its dependent punishment, correct the operative judgment, and determine any lawful sentencing consequences of the two surviving assault convictions. Any resentencing must rest on lawful surviving convictions and may not restore punishment requiring guilt on the acquitted count. Further adjudication of guilt on the firearm count is barred; it may not be tried or resubmitted.

**End of entry: Smith v. Massachusetts, merits decision, February 22, 2005.**

### Stewart v. Dutra Construction Co., No. 03-814

Merits decision, February 22, 2005

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 1, 2004; decided February 22, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit.

The question is whether the Super Scoop, a dredge practically capable of carrying equipment and workers over water, is a vessel under the Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act (LHWCA) and the linked Jones Act inquiry although transportation is not its primary purpose and it was temporarily stationary at the injury. The First Circuit sustained Jones Act summary judgment under its primary-business and injury-time-movement approach. It later affirmed a separate section 905(b) judgment on employer capacity rather than vessel-owner negligence, assuming vessel status. Stewart seeks reversal and further adjudication of his maritime injury claims; Dutra seeks to preserve summary judgment.

The full Court participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jones Act vessel-based judgment | Reversed and remanded, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | — | Treat the Super Scoop as a vessel and adjudicate the genuinely remaining Jones Act issues under the corrected premise and the motion's actual acknowledgments. |
| Independent section 905(b) capacity judgment | Left undisturbed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | — | The judgment survives; its capacity merits are outside the granted vessel question. |

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Practical water-transport capability determines vessel status

**The Court holds:** A watercraft is a vessel under the LHWCA's integrated vessel provisions and the linked seaman inquiry when it is used, or practically capable of being used, to transport people or things over water; transportation need not be its primary purpose, the craft need not propel itself, and motion at the moment of injury is unnecessary. Permanently fixed or withdrawn structures whose transportation capacity is merely theoretical fall outside this rule, but the Super Scoop's actual waterborne movements carrying equipment and workers satisfy it.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined in full by all eight other Justices, controls this vessel rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 3 of Title 1 covers use or capability as a means of water transportation. Its terms do not restrict a vessel to a transportation business or require a voyage at the instant of injury. The LHWCA identifies the vessel's relevant owners and operators without supplying a different physical-characteristics definition. Practical capability gives the statutory term a concrete limit: flotation or an imagined possibility of moving a permanently fixed structure is insufficient.

The Super Scoop regularly moved thirty to fifty feet while dredging, carrying its machinery and crew, and tugs moved it over long distances. Its anchors and cables supplied shorter movement. Its construction function and reliance on external propulsion therefore do not remove its actual transport capacity. The stop associated with a scow's repair was temporary, rather than withdrawal from service.

Dutra argues that this reading erases the distinction between maritime crews and people employed on floating workplaces. The distinct worker and claim requirements preserve that distinction. Southwest Marine, Inc. v. Gizoni separates occupational labels from actual Jones Act status. Chandris, Inc. v. Latsis directs attention to the employment relationship and distinguishes temporary repair from withdrawal; it supplies no primary-purpose exclusion for this operational dredge. Harbor Tug & Barge Co. v. Papai likewise separates the vessel relationship from a job label or momentary docking. Those precedents retain their respective worker-status functions while the Court now resolves the craft-definition question. The vessel premise supporting the Jones Act judgment is legally erroneous.

##### The mandate follows the ground of each claim judgment

**The Court holds:** The Jones Act judgment must be reversed because it rests on the erroneous vessel exclusion, and the lower court must decide the genuinely remaining employment-connection, proper defendant-capacity, negligence and remedial issues under the corrected classification while honoring Dutra's summary-judgment acknowledgments. The independent section 905(b) judgment remains operative because it assumed vessel status and its separate capacity ground lies outside the granted question.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion directly controls this disposition and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Harbor Tug requires separate treatment of seaman-dependent claims and a distinct vessel-negligence judgment. Its vessel-negligence appeal remained unaddressed below; here the First Circuit already decided that separate appeal. Correcting the vessel premise therefore reopens the Jones Act branch while leaving the independent capacity judgment in place.

For its Jones Act motion, Dutra acknowledged Stewart's crew membership, ninety-nine percent service aboard the dredge and contribution to its function. The lower court must give those acknowledgments their procedural effect when identifying what remains to be adjudicated. A renewed vessel exclusion or invented dispute contradicting those motion facts cannot support summary judgment. Any remaining independent legal ground may be addressed, and genuinely disputed liability or remedy facts receive ordinary adjudication. This gives Stewart the further proceedings warranted by the identified error while preserving the separate judgment's actual scope.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- **Southwest Marine, Inc. v. Gizoni:** Applies its distinction between occupational labeling and Jones Act status; its compensation and genuine-dispute rules retain their stated scope.
- **Chandris, Inc. v. Latsis:** Clarifies the vessel predicate used in its employment-connection inquiry by adopting practical water-transport capability. Its existing contribution, connection and temporary-repair rules retain their respective controlling scope.
- **Harbor Tug & Barge Co. v. Papai:** Applies the distinction between vessel or worker classification and the separate vessel-negligence judgment. Its Part III requires claim-specific appellate treatment; the already adjudicated capacity branch here retains its judgment while the erroneous Jones Act vessel ruling is reversed.

Practical water-transport use or capability, assessed in the craft’s actual service condition, governs statutory vessel status. Industrial purpose, external propulsion and temporary immobility do not exclude an operational craft that carries people or equipment over water; permanently fixed or withdrawn structures with merely theoretical capacity remain outside the definition. Worker status, actionable defendant capacity and negligence retain distinct requirements. Correcting one claim’s vessel premise warrants relief for that claim while independently adjudicated grounds retain their procedural force.

#### Source Notes

1 U.S.C. §3; 46 U.S.C. App. §688(a); 33 U.S.C. §§902(3)(G), 902(21), 905(b); Stewart v. Dutra Construction Co., 230 F.3d 461 (CA1 2000), 343 F.3d 10 (CA1 2003). Dutra's crew-membership, ninety-nine-percent service and mission-contribution acknowledgments were made for the Jones Act summary-judgment motion. Vessel status was assumed for the separate section 905(b) capacity judgment.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for further Jones Act proceedings. The lower court must identify and adjudicate the genuinely remaining employment-connection, proper defendant-capacity, negligence and remedy questions with the Super Scoop treated as a vessel and Dutra’s motion acknowledgments given their proper procedural effect. A legally sufficient independent ground may support further summary judgment; otherwise ordinary factfinding determines the remaining liability and loss issues. The separate section 905(b) capacity judgment remains in force, with its merits outside the granted vessel question.

**End of entry: Stewart v. Dutra Construction Co., merits decision, February 22, 2005.**
