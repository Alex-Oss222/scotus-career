## Decisions and Dispositions

### Clingman v. Beaver, No. 04-37

Merits decision, May 23, 2005

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 19, 2005; decided May 23, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, which reversed the judgment for Oklahoma and directed relief against its semiclosed-primary restriction. The question is whether Oklahoma may prevent a political party from inviting voters registered with other parties into its primary while permitting invitations to independents. The Libertarian Party sought to invite all registered voters; the State excluded those retaining another party registration. The completed original primary leaves the continuing rule and prospective relief in controversy.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer.

The judgment invalidating the continued-registration exclusion is affirmed under contextual review. The mandate confines prospective relief to qualified parties' voluntary invitations and otherwise eligible voters, with implementation as stated in Part III.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, unanimous as to Part I and joined by STEVENS, KENNEDY, SOUTER and GINSBURG, JJ., as to Parts II and III. O'CONNOR, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part, in which SCALIA, THOMAS and BREYER, JJ., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | All eight Associates in Part I; Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg in Parts II–III | Affirmance and prospective remand | I: protected association and contextual review; II: insufficient justification; III: implementing relief |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | O'Connor | Scalia, Thomas, Breyer | Would reverse | Joins Part I; sustains the limited registration-choice condition |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Protected association and the governing inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** A qualified party's invitation and an otherwise eligible voter's acceptance are protected political association even when the voter retains another party registration. A court must assess the actual burden, the State's precise interests and their fit under Anderson and Burdick: severe burdens require compelling justification and narrow tailoring; lesser burdens still require a sufficient regulatory connection.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine Justices, directly controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Participation in an official nomination is legally effective association. Permission to campaign or endorse outside that process does not replace it. Timmons protects a consenting party's meaningful nomination choice; Tashjian protects invitations to independents while leaving other-party registrants for decision. Jones protects an objecting party against compelled outsiders. These decisions require attention to the parties' actual choices and the restriction's operation. Registration remains an administrable way to define an electorate, but its label does not eliminate the association that it burdens. The State may preserve a single nominating vote and genuine election administration through rules fitted to those purposes.

##### The continued-registration exclusion lacks sufficient justification

**Holding and operative rule:** Oklahoma may not exclude an otherwise qualified voter from a consenting party's primary solely because the voter retains another party registration when the State has not shown a sufficient regulatory need for that exclusion. On this record the burden is substantial but not severe, and the categorical disaffiliation condition fails contextual review without automatic strict scrutiny.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the five-Justice coalition identified above, supplies five direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Timmons requires a real connection between an electoral burden and the State's asserted stability interests. The trial findings rejected confusion, insuperable administrative difficulty and protection of the inviting Libertarian Party from its own choice. The legitimate interests in identifiable parties, orderly nominations and other parties' continuity remain, but the evidence does not connect this additional exclusion adequately to their preservation. The close-primary statistic shows that invited votes could change results; it does not demonstrate disorganization or corrupted nominations. Other parties may exclude unwanted voters from their own primaries, but that protection does not entitle them to prevent their registrants' willing association elsewhere. The dissent properly recognizes the legal significance of nomination and the ease of a registration choice. Those considerations do not explain why an exclusive primary vote and ordinary verification are insufficient here, or why continuing affiliation itself establishes a threat. The Court therefore affirms the constitutional judgment on the existing record while rejecting the appellate court's severe-burden classification.

##### Prospective relief follows the common exclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** The prospective decree must protect qualified inviting parties and their otherwise eligible invited voters against the adjudicated categorical exclusion; Oklahoma may obtain narrower party-specific relief only by showing that it fully cures that same policy defect. The decree must preserve an unwilling party's choice, a voter's single primary vote, lawful deadlines, ordinary verification and independently valid eligibility conditions.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the five Justices supporting Part II, directly controls the remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson v. Spear requires relief that corrects the unlawful operation for those governed by the policy unless the State establishes a fully effective narrower cure. Here the common defect is the demand to surrender an existing registration before accepting a qualified party's invitation. Naming only the plaintiffs does not itself establish that a smaller decree will cure it. Election officials must accept timely invitations and administer a single-primary choice without recreating the forbidden exclusion. The lower court must settle the implementing terms and any supported narrower-cure contention under this standard. The relief operates prospectively.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Anderson v. Celebrezze and Burdick v. Takushi supply the contextual burden, precise-interest and fit inquiry; their graduated scrutiny remains controlling.
- Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party is extended from meaningful consensual nomination to reciprocal primary invitations, preserving its substantial-but-not-severe analysis.
- California Democratic Party v. Jones retains its protection against compelled outsider nomination; the party's consent distinguishes this case.
- Tashjian v. Republican Party of Connecticut remains controlling for independent-voter invitations; its reserved other-party question is resolved at the consent-based scope stated here.
- Anderson v. Spear supplies the common-policy remedy and the State's opportunity to establish a fully curative narrower decree.

#### Law After Decision

Beginning with this decision, voluntary invitations to otherwise eligible voters retaining another affiliation receive the same contextual protection as other legally effective political association. Continued registration alone cannot justify the exclusion on the record adjudicated here. Genuine, distinct electoral threats remain subject to the burden-and-fit inquiry; ordinary one-primary administration and the autonomy of unwilling parties remain operative. The lower court's remaining work is implementation of the settled rule.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Thomas and Breyer, concurs in Part I and dissents from invalidation and its remedy. The writing accepts real reciprocal and overlapping association and applies Anderson and Burdick, but regards the available formal choice of nominating affiliation as a limited burden supported by identifiable parties, campaign planning, nomination-system operation and other-party continuity. Preventive regulation need not await a proved corrupted election. Timmons's protection of a consenting candidate designation does not, in their view, eliminate a distinct affiliation choice for selecting a party's nominee. They accept the lower factual findings and do not rely on the rejected confusion or raiding grounds. Their position depends on reasonable access to the registration choice presented here. They would reverse and sustain the challenged condition.

#### Source Notes

No. 04-37; Oklahoma Statutes, title 26, §1-104; Beaver v. Clingman, 363 F.3d 1048 (10th Cir. 2004). The trial findings and the distinction between changed nomination results and political instability govern the record application. Tashjian v. Republican Party of Connecticut, 479 U.S. 208, addresses invitations to independents and reserves the different-party question.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Tenth Circuit for a fitted prospective decree. Election officials must provide the lawful single-primary means of participation described in Part III. Any supported request for a narrower decree must establish a complete cure of the adjudicated common-policy violation.

**End of entry: Clingman v. Beaver, merits decision, May 23, 2005.**

### Deck v. Missouri, No. 04-5293

Merits decision, May 23, 2005

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 1, 2005; decided May 23, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Missouri, which affirmed a death sentence imposed in a replacement penalty proceeding after the earlier sentence was set aside. Deck appeared before the sentencing jury in handcuffs, leg irons and a belly chain. Counsel repeatedly objected; the trial court relied on the conviction and juror reassurance without identifying a particular current security need. The questions are whether visible shackling throughout capital sentencing requires defendant-specific justification and which prejudice or harmlessness rule governs relief.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The reviewed affirmance of the death sentence is reversed. Missouri must establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt on the full penalty record; otherwise the death sentence must be vacated and the State must provide a lawful new penalty proceeding or a lawful noncapital sentence. The convictions remain intact.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA, J., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Visible restraint during capital punishment selection

**Holding and operative rule:** A court may not require a defendant to appear visibly shackled before a capital sentencing jury unless circumstances particular to the defendant and proceeding establish an essential security or comparable courtroom need. The State must justify the measure; the trial judge retains reasonable discretion to address actual danger, escape risk and courtroom order, considering supported history and reasonable less prejudicial alternatives without a prescribed hearing format or proof quantum.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion, joined by the seven-Justice coalition identified above, supplies seven direct joins. That same coalition controls Parts II and III.

**Controlling explanation:** Allen preserves the court's power to maintain order, while Holbrook distinguishes ordinary security from an inherently prejudicial display. Riggins shows why a compelled courtroom condition requires actual justification and why its effects on participation may resist reconstruction. Their principles extend to the jury's capital punishment decision. Conviction settles guilt; it does not settle punishment. Visible chains can convey the court's own judgment of present dangerousness at that remaining choice, interfere with participation and consultation, and compromise the dignity and reliability of the proceeding. The dissent's distinction between an accused and a convicted defendant therefore does not answer the due-process concern. The Court preserves real security authority: a judge may use the defendant's supported history and need not await a new incident. Necessity, rather than a routine practice tied to conviction, supplies the constitutional justification.

##### The restraints in this proceeding lacked the required justification

**Holding and operative rule:** Conviction alone and generalized juror reassurance do not justify deliberately imposed visible shackling throughout a capital penalty proceeding. Deck's preserved challenge establishes unconstitutional restraint because the trial court identified no individualized necessity and this record supplies no exceptional, indisputable need curing that omission.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the seven-Justice Court coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The objections and the trial court's responses establish that the jury saw Deck's restraints. Estelle's distinction between compelled appearance and an unobjected choice therefore presents no preservation obstacle. The court's reasons addressed the general condition of a convicted defendant and reassurance, rather than circumstances showing why these restraints were needed for Deck. A conclusory reference to security cannot replace the actual judgment Part I requires.

##### The State bears the direct-review harmlessness burden

**Holding and operative rule:** Unjustified visible shackling in capital jury sentencing is constitutional trial error. On a preserved direct-review claim, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt from the full penalty record that the error did not contribute to the death sentence; absent that showing, the defendant receives a lawful new sentencing proceeding or a lawful noncapital sentence.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the seven-Justice Court coalition, adopts the rule and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Chapman supplies the governing burden for preserved constitutional trial error. Smith v. Texas applies that burden to a preserved capital sentencing restriction and distinguishes correction of the penalty proceeding from disturbance of guilt. Deck need not reconstruct the jury's unshackled deliberations or prove each juror's actual response before relief becomes available. The inquiry remains a genuine full-record harmlessness review, rather than automatic reversal. Riggins's medication-specific holding supports attention to unreconstructable effects but did not previously decide this restraint error's Chapman treatment. Missouri's contrary demand that Deck establish actual prejudice placed the burden on the wrong party. The reviewing court must apply the proper burden to the actual sentencing record.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Illinois v. Allen preserves necessary courtroom control and supplies the individualized security authority applied at capital sentencing.
- Holbrook v. Flynn supplies the distinction between ordinary security arrangements and inherently prejudicial measures; that distinction governs the visible restraints here.
- Riggins v. Nevada is extended by analogy concerning actual justification and effects that cannot reliably be reconstructed; its medication holding remains confined to that subject.
- Estelle v. Williams preserves the distinction between compelled appearance and a measure accepted without timely objection; Deck repeatedly objected.
- Chapman v. California now governs the preserved visible-restraint error on direct review.
- Smith v. Texas supplies the existing capital-error allocation and sentence-specific remedy; its mitigation rule remains distinct from the restraint prohibition adopted here.

#### Law After Decision

Due process now expressly protects capital punishment selection against unjustified visible restraints. Particular necessity permits appropriate security; routine reliance on conviction does not. A preserved violation receives State-borne Chapman review, with sentence-specific relief if harmlessness is not established. The rule separates the trial judge's security determination from the reviewing court's inquiry into the error's effect.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Thomas, joined by Scalia, dissents. The guilt-stage restraint cases protected the presumption of innocence, and the medication cases addressed distinct liberty and defense effects. A convicted capital defendant stands in a different posture. In the dissent's view, those authorities do not establish that visible restraints at punishment are constitutionally prejudicial without proof of actual unfairness or serious impairment of assistance in the defense. The writing acknowledges that chains can suggest danger but rejects treating that possible incremental effect as a sufficient constitutional violation on this showing. It would affirm. Its disagreement precedes harmlessness: it does not excuse an acknowledged constitutional error under a different Chapman burden.

#### Source Notes

No. 04-5293; State v. Deck, 136 S.W.3d 481 (Mo. 2004). The objections and trial-court responses establish visible restraints and preservation. Fourteenth Amendment; Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337; Holbrook v. Flynn, 475 U.S. 560; Estelle v. Williams, 425 U.S. 501; Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18. The proceeding under review selected punishment after the convictions had become settled.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Missouri courts for full-record harmless-error review under the State's burden. If that showing fails, the State must provide lawful resentencing or the lawful noncapital alternative. At a new penalty proceeding the judge must determine actual security needs before exposing the jury to restraints and reconsider changed circumstances; the prior general rationale cannot substitute for that determination.

**End of entry: Deck v. Missouri, merits decision, May 23, 2005.**

### Johanns v. Livestock Marketing Association, Nos. 03-1164 and 03-1165

Merits decision, May 23, 2005

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 8, 2004; decided May 23, 2005. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit. Both dockets concern the certified First Amendment judgment declaring the Beef Act and Order unconstitutional and restraining all further checkoff collection, while other claims remain pending. The questions are whether compulsory promotion assessments violate the First Amendment when the Government actually controls the advertising and whether asserted producer attribution establishes the facial defect.

The Act imposes a dollar-per-head assessment for promotion and other projects. The Secretary appoints the Beef Board, retains removal authority over the relevant committee, approves content, and participates through department representatives in message development. Producers otherwise make their own marketing decisions; some object to generic promotion and messages promoting imported as well as domestic beef. Government-speech and commercial-justification defenses were presented below.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No. 03-1164: reviewed constitutional judgment and dependent permanent collection injunction | Vacated and remanded, 6–3 as a formal disposition | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stevens, Kennedy, Souter | Ginsburg seeks a different commercial-application remand; the other five adopt the controlling instructions below |
| No. 03-1165: same reviewed judgment | Vacated and remanded, 6–3 as a formal disposition | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stevens, Kennedy, Souter | Same distinct grounds and remedial scopes as No. 03-1164 |
| Both dockets: governmental-message ruling and operative remand instructions | Compulsory-financing claim rejected and its dependent declaration and whole-checkoff restraint removed, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Breyer | Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg | Remaining claims and independently supported relief retain their own proceedings |

The three Justices opposing complete vacatur would affirm protection against compulsory private advertising, vacate the categorical nonseverability determination, and retain collection protection during a genuine severability and remedy inquiry. Ginsburg would vacate the permanent decree but return the constitutional justification itself for application of her proposed commercial standard; she does not join the five-Justice mandate resolving this funding challenge for the Government.

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA and BREYER, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed a concurring opinion. GINSBURG, J., filed an opinion concurring in the formal vacatur and remand. SOUTER, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part, in which STEVENS and KENNEDY, JJ., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Breyer | Government-ground vacatur and remand in both dockets | Parts I–IV: actual control, targeted financing, facial attribution, complete operative remedy |
| Concurrence | Thomas | — | Joins the Court completely | Distinct affirmative protection against objectively established involuntary personal attribution |
| Opinion concurring in the formal vacatur and remand | Ginsburg | — | Judgment-only agreement in that formal act; different remand work | Withholds governmental classification and resolved-claim mandate; proposes independent commercial justification and application below |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | Souter | Stevens, Kennedy | Partial affirmance, partial vacatur and remedial remand; opposes the Court's complete vacatur | Accountability ground, current private-support rule, retained protection and actual severability inquiry |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Actual governmental control identifies the speaker

**Holding and operative rule:** A promotional message is governmental for the presented compelled-subsidy objection when government determines its policy and actually selects, supervises and finally approves its content. The Government must establish that operational direction. Appointment power, a governmental label or ordinary compliance review alone is insufficient; the operational direction established here makes the challenged beef campaign the Government's message.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion, joined by the five-Justice coalition identified above, supplies five direct joins. That same coalition joins Parts II–IV in full.

**Controlling explanation:** Keller requires examination of the particular expressive function rather than a body's general legal status. The Secretary's actual participation in development and final control over content establish more than a dormant veto over an independent private speaker. Producer drafting and the producers' freedom to run their own businesses do not transfer the final message choice from the officials who exercise it. The accountability objection would additionally require public identification of each campaign as governmental. For this funding claim, however, the relevant responsibility follows actual official message selection; a generic identification of the source of funds does not itself turn the controlled campaign into private authorship. United Foods reserved this operational governmental defense because it had not been developed there. The defense is preserved and supported here, so the Court resolves that reservation on the actual control record.

##### A lawful targeted assessment may fund that governmental message

**Holding and operative rule:** Compulsory financing of a message actually selected and controlled by government does not, merely because assessed producers disagree, violate the First Amendment protection against compelled support of private expression. Financing through a lawful industry assessment rather than general revenue does not change the speaker; compelled personal expression and independently applicable constitutional or statutory constraints retain their own predicates.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the five-Justice Court coalition, directly controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Glickman permits germane promotion within its qualifying collective private program, while United Foods forbids selected private advertising from supplying its own compulsory-association justification. These are different funded relationships. Here actual governmental selection supplies the sufficient distinction, rather than industry benefit, majority approval or the opportunity to counteradvertise. The First Amendment does not require the Government to replace a lawful targeted exaction with general taxation whenever an assessed person opposes its message. Ginsburg's commercial proposal addresses an independent justification for private promotion. The government-message ground resolves this challenge before an application of that proposed alternative becomes necessary; the existing private-support rule continues to govern its category.

##### Generic funding identification does not establish the facial attribution claim

**Holding and operative rule:** The statute's compulsory financing and generic identification of producers as the funding source do not establish that every objector must personally endorse the advertising. Because the statute does not require a named dissenter's endorsement, this facial attribution objection supplies no independent basis for the reviewed statutory bar; a properly presented individual-attribution claim requires its own facts and adjudication.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the five-Justice Court coalition, supplies the limited facial holding and express reservation.

**Controlling explanation:** Governmental authorship, the source of money and a particular person's apparent endorsement are distinct questions. Barnette and Wooley protect against compelled personal expression, but compulsory payment and a broad funding label do not themselves establish the necessary personal attribution. The Court's ruling therefore disposes of the presented facial theory while preserving the distinct claim of an identified person or organization whose unwanted message is attributed to it. That claim's governing application remains unadjudicated because its factual predicate has not been established here.

##### The dependent whole-program decree loses its constitutional premise

**Holding and operative rule:** Both dockets require vacatur of the reviewed constitutional judgment, its dependent declaration against the Act and Order, and the whole-checkoff collection injunction resting on the rejected funding theory. The lower court must remove those dependent restraints while adjudicating the separately pending producer-communications, referendum, statutory-power, administration and accounting claims under their own law, preserving independently supported relief.

**Authority:** Part IV, joined by the five-Justice Court coalition, supplies the operative mandate; Ginsburg supports the formal vacatur and remand but not this resolution of the constitutional claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The actual decree restrained all further collection, not merely advertising expenditures. Once the adjudicated compulsory-financing ground fails, it cannot sustain either the advertising or nonadvertising collection restraints. A severability ruling is unnecessary to remove a remedy whose constitutional premise has been rejected. The three opposing Justices' proposed continued restraint depends on sustaining that advertising defect. Their concern about lawful survival of other functions does not preserve the defect after the Court's governmental-message ruling. The lower court must identify the restrictions resting solely on the rejected ground and remove them, while addressing the actual remaining claims.

#### Precedent Treatment

- United States v. United Foods, Inc. retains its independently sufficient private-support rule; its reserved operational government-speech question is resolved for an actually controlled governmental message. Its treatment of the preserved commercial alternative remains distinct from its refusal to reach an undeveloped governmental defense.
- Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott retains its permission for germane promotion within the qualifying collective private program and its conditions.
- Keller v. State Bar of California supplies the functional inquiry; a body's general governmental status alone does not classify each financed message.
- West Virginia State Board of Education v. Barnette and Wooley v. Maynard retain protection against compelled personal expression; the generic funding identification does not establish personal endorsement here.

#### Law After Decision

Actual governmental selection and control defeat the presented private-subsidy objection to financing that message, including through a lawful industry assessment. Nominal supervision of independent private speech is insufficient. The existing rule for compulsory private advertising remains controlling; governmental authorship, financial support and personal attribution remain distinct. The reviewed funding claim is resolved, while the independent pending claims proceed under their own requirements.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Justice Thomas, concurring.** He joins the Court in full and would also recognize a distinct constitutional injury when an unwanted message is objectively and involuntarily attributed to an identified individual or organization. Government control does not answer that personal injury. His position extends beyond the Court's reservation while respecting the absence of an established attribution predicate in this case.

**Justice Ginsburg, concurring in the formal vacatur and remand.** She withholds the governmental-message classification for this producer-presented campaign, without adopting every part of the dissent's affirmative accountability rule. Instead she would permit a commercial subsidy to be justified by a substantial governmental interest, direct advancement and reasonable fit. That approach expressly proposes modifying United Foods's independent-private-support rule; it does not treat economic regulation as outside the First Amendment. Her United Foods dissent supplies the proposed framework, and the consistent presentation of the commercial defense permits its consideration here. Industry welfare identifies the asserted interest, but does not establish all the test's predicates. She would vacate the permanent constitutional decree and return genuinely unresolved application to the actual bench-trial record, supplemented only as ordinary procedure requires, with the Government bearing the burden. Her proposal prescribes neither scientific or quantitative benchmarks nor an automatic new trial. Any interim protection would require ordinary equitable support. She therefore agrees in formal vacatur while withholding the Court's instruction that this constitutional funding question is resolved for the Government.

**Justice Souter, joined by Stevens and Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Actual executive control is accepted, but hidden governmental selection does not provide the publicly identifiable responsibility needed to claim government speech for a campaign presented as the industry's message. Private drafting and targeted financing are not categorically forbidden; accountable governmental authorship must first exist. The writing expressly leaves open whether a discrete group's compelled support could raise another objection even when the Government openly identifies its speech.

Without the governmental justification, Abood, Keller and United Foods require an independently sufficient common function: advertising cannot justify its own compelled association. Producers retain their marketing autonomy and promotion is the principal object. Research spending does not by itself establish an adequate independent function. The preserved commercial defense fails as an independent legal cure even assuming effective promotion and sensible design; the objection is not an empirical finding that advertising failed. Glickman's qualifying collective program and Southworth's separate educational function remain distinct permissible relationships.

The writing would affirm protection against compelled advertising, vacate the categorical nonseverability determination and remand for actual statutory survival and remedial scope. The integrated collection restraint would continue until a conforming order permits a lawful, legally separable operation. Advertising's centrality and the deleted savings clause matter, but the deletion is not an affirmative legislative command resolving every remainder. The lower court would determine whether lawful operations can survive without rewriting the assessment. If they can, relief must permit them while fully protecting against the advertising defect; otherwise the whole-collection restraint may continue on that determination. The research allocation alone authorizes no fractional collection.

#### Source Notes

Nos. 03-1164 and 03-1165; Beef Promotion and Research Act, 7 U.S.C. §§2901 and following; implementing Beef Order; Livestock Marketing Association v. United States Department of Agriculture, 335 F.3d 711 (8th Cir. 2003), Parts I, III and IV. The First Amendment judgment followed a January 14, 2002 bench trial and was certified under Rule 54(b). The decree covered all further checkoff collection. The reported allocation findings concern assessments collected and paid to the Beef Board; they do not establish an individual's endorsement. The lower nonseverability conclusion is an interpretation of the deleted clause and program structure.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Both dockets return through the Eighth Circuit. The lower court must dissolve the declaration and collection restraints resting solely on the rejected compulsory-financing ground, and proceed with the actual remaining claims and independently supported relief. Any separately established attribution injury receives its own appropriate adjudication and remedy.

**End of entry: Johanns v. Livestock Marketing Association, merits decision, May 23, 2005.**

### Lingle v. Chevron U.S.A. Inc., No. 04-163

Merits decision, May 23, 2005

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 22, 2005; decided May 23, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, which affirmed relief against Hawaii's limit on oil-company rents charged to dealer-operated service stations. Chevron's surviving facial theory alleged that the cap failed substantially to advance the State's objective. The lower courts evaluated competing economic predictions about the cap's effects. The question is whether that efficacy inquiry independently identifies a taking and supports the judgment.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The lower court must remove the declaration or injunction resting solely on the rejected taking theory and enter judgment consistent with its rejection. Any independently preserved matter must satisfy its own substantive and procedural predicates.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the unanimous opinion of the Court.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Regulatory efficacy does not independently establish a taking

**Holding and operative rule:** Failure substantially to advance a legitimate governmental objective is not, by itself, a taking requiring compensation under the Fifth Amendment. A claimant must establish the relevant property burden or demanded dedication under the applicable taking rule; an economic-efficacy trial cannot substitute for that showing, whether the challenged regulation is general or an individual permit denial.

**Authority:** Part I of the unanimous opinion supplies nine direct joins; the same coalition joins Parts II and III.

**Controlling explanation:** The compensation command addresses the nature and burden of governmental interference with property. An ineffective regulation may impose little loss, while an effective one may take property and require payment. Efficacy cannot distinguish those cases. Loretto identifies a compelled permanent occupation; Lucas addresses complete loss of beneficial use; Penn Central and Tahoe-Sierra examine the continuing property interest and the actual regulatory burden. Nollan and Dolan address the distinct demand to surrender identified property as a condition of permission. None makes ordinary economic disagreement the constitutional event. Chevron's strongest objection is reliance on the real purpose-relationship holding in Del Monte Dunes. Restricting that holding to individual denials would retain the same source error: ineffective means do not become a taking merely because they concern one parcel. Independently unlawful or arbitrary regulation remains subject to applicable constitutional protections, including due process, with their own elements and remedies. Compensation is not a license to undertake action forbidden by those independent protections.

##### The earlier efficacy holding is overruled at its precise scope

**Holding and operative rule:** Agins and Del Monte Dunes are overruled insofar as they recognize lack of substantial advancement as an independent taking predicate; Del Monte Dunes's allocation of that displaced substantive issue likewise ceases to govern. Its final judgment and award, independent complete-deprivation ground, municipal compensation rules, finality rulings, and constitutional jury entitlement and task allocation for surviving legal claims remain in force.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion expressly changes the earlier controlling rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Del Monte Dunes sustained an undifferentiated award on sufficient evidence supporting both submitted alternatives. Its purpose-relationship ground was an actual holding. The Court therefore confronts and withdraws that ground instead of dismissing it as dictum. Stare decisis carries particular weight where property and transactions are arranged under judicial rules, but it does not justify retaining a liability predicate unrelated to the burden that the compensation command identifies. Preserving final judgments and independent valid grounds respects those interests without perpetuating the mismatch in pending and future cases. Rejecting the substantive efficacy issue also removes the occasion for assigning that issue to a jury; it does not abolish a jury right for an otherwise valid legal damages claim. The replacement rule governs this case and other cases still open on direct review where the issue is properly presented. The completed Del Monte Dunes compensation judgment remains final.

##### The surviving property rules govern distinct interferences

**Holding and operative rule:** Compelled permanent physical occupation follows Loretto; total elimination of beneficial use follows Lucas, subject to substantiated background property or nuisance limits already inherent in ownership; other regulatory burdens follow Penn Central's inquiry into economic impact, interference with distinct investment-backed expectations and the character of governmental action. For an individualized development-permit condition demanding identified property interests, the government must establish Nollan's essential nexus and demonstrate Dolan's rough proportionality between the dedication's nature and extent and the development's effects, allowing reasonable estimates without exact mathematical equivalence or unique attribution to the project.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion preserves these rules as the operative alternatives to the rejected predicate.

**Controlling explanation:** The court must classify the actual interference before choosing its test. Tahoe-Sierra preserves examination of a continuing property interest rather than manufacture of total loss by temporal segmentation. Lucas requires the State to substantiate an antecedent ownership limitation rather than rely on a new legislative harm label. Dolan requires a connection between the nature and extent of demanded property and the development's effects, a distinct burden that ordinary scrutiny of regulatory effectiveness cannot replace. These rules retain their own triggers, proof and consequences.

##### Chevron's selected theory cannot sustain the judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** A judgment resting solely on failure of substantial advancement must be reversed, and relief based on that predicate must be removed. Chevron's surviving efficacy theory therefore fails; independently preserved claims require their own legal and procedural basis.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous opinion supplies the judgment and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The economic testimony addressed whether Hawaii's cap would achieve its objectives. That inquiry establishes neither the nature nor the magnitude of a taking. Chevron's reviewed theory does not invoke a physical occupation, total deprivation or demanded conveyance. The lower court must reject the chosen predicate and identify any independently preserved matter actually remaining. A distinct due-process or compensation claim is not presented for decision here, and a remand cannot create one by relabeling the failed theory.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Agins v. City of Tiburon is overruled only insofar as lack of substantial advancement supplies an independent taking ground.
- Del Monte Dunes at Monterey, Ltd. v. City of Monterey is overruled at its accepted purpose-relationship alternative and the assignment of that displaced issue. Its final award, independent complete-deprivation holding, municipal compensation route, damages and finality rules, and jury entitlement and allocation for surviving claims remain controlling.
- Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV Corp. retains the categorical compelled-permanent-occupation rule.
- Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council retains the total-beneficial-use rule and substantiated background-title limitation.
- Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City retains the contextual property-burden inquiry.
- Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency retains its continuing-property treatment and rejection of automatic temporal segmentation.
- Nollan v. California Coastal Commission and Dolan v. City of Tigard retain their distinct nexus and rough-proportionality requirements for demanded property dedications.

#### Law After Decision

Regulatory-taking liability can no longer rest solely on a mismatch between a regulation and its asserted objective. That change reaches general rules and particular denials, while the recognized occupation, deprivation, regulatory-burden and dedication rules remain available on their own predicates. Independently arbitrary regulation remains subject to applicable due-process law. The new rule applies in this case and properly presented cases still open on direct review; final compensation judgments retain their finality.

No. 04-163; Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments; Hawaii's service-station rent-cap legislation. The surviving claim concerns regulatory efficacy. Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV Corp., 458 U.S. 419; Agins v. City of Tiburon, 447 U.S. 255; Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, 438 U.S. 104. Del Monte Dunes, decided June 16, 1997, sustained sufficient evidence on both taking alternatives and a single completed compensation award.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Ninth Circuit for removal of relief resting on the rejected test and entry of judgment rejecting Chevron's sole surviving efficacy claim. Hawaii may enforce the rent cap subject to other valid orders or independently preserved claims.

### Lofton v. Secretary of the Department of Children & Family Services

Merits decision, May 23, 2005

#### Chronology and Posture

Submitted on the merits; decided May 23, 2005. On review of the Eleventh Circuit's January 28, 2004 judgment, 358 F.3d 804, sustaining Florida's categorical adoption exclusion, with rehearing denied July 21, 2004. The questions concern equal protection and the alternative claim that the bar impermissibly burdens established foster and guardianship families. The plaintiffs seek removal of the statutory barrier and genuine consideration for adoption, rather than a general entitlement to adopt.

Florida permits gay adults to foster children or serve as guardians but excludes them from adoption, while unmarried heterosexual applicants remain eligible. Lofton raised Doe from infancy. Houghton cared for Roe from age four and became co-guardian after the biological father's placement; the father later consented to termination of parental rights. Lofton declined guardianship unless it led toward adoption because it would sacrifice foster-care payments and threaten Medicaid coverage. These circumstances explain the claimed importance of permanency without settling any particular adoption's merits.

All nine Justices participated at merits submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

Florida must accept and evaluate applications without the categorical orientation bar in §63.042(3), under otherwise lawful child-specific eligibility and best-interest criteria. Relief corrects the statewide eligibility policy for all applicants governed by it unless Florida establishes that a narrower order fully cures the same defect. Existing placements and guardianship rights retain their present legal scope.

#### Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER and BREYER, JJ., joined. SCALIA, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which THOMAS, J., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The actual classification must satisfy existing intermediate scrutiny

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may not categorically exclude gay and lesbian adults from adoption unless it demonstrates a substantial relationship between that classification and a genuine important child-welfare objective. Intermediate scrutiny governs sexual-orientation classifications, including deliberate conduct proxies; ordinary civil evidence and supported professional judgment may carry the State's burden, while stereotype, private bias, moral disapproval and merely conceivable post hoc reasons cannot.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by the seven-Justice coalition identified above, applies the binding Shahar and Equality Foundation standard. The same seven join Parts II and III.

**Controlling explanation:** Shahar requires genuine justification for the actual distinction and extends its protection to deliberate status proxies. Equality Foundation applies that rule outside employment. Describing adoption as a statutory benefit or construing the bar in terms of same-sex conduct does not remove the classification from that law. Child welfare is an important responsibility, and predictive professional judgments deserve consideration. The standard requires neither scientific certainty nor least restrictive means or an individualized exception in every rule. It does require substantial fit. The dissent would replace this existing standard with ordinary rationality. Its conceivable future-household rationale does not answer the burden under the governing rule, and the particular adoption setting supplies no sufficient reason to abandon that rule.

##### Florida's categorical bar fails on the developed record

**Holding and operative rule:** Florida's categorical orientation exclusion lacks the substantial relationship to child welfare that intermediate scrutiny requires and is unconstitutional. The State must evaluate applicants using supported parenting-capacity, safety, stability and child-specific welfare criteria, without treating orientation or a deliberate status proxy as categorical incapacity.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the seven-Justice Court coalition, resolves the classification on the developed record.

**Controlling explanation:** A preference for a presently two-parent, mother-and-father household does not describe a statute that permits unmarried heterosexual applicants but excludes every gay applicant. The possibility that a heterosexual single applicant may later marry does not substantiate why each excluded applicant is unsuitable for consideration. Florida's actual reliance on gay foster parents and guardians also prevents treating orientation itself as demonstrated incapacity, while leaving each adoption to its own evidence. Foster care and adoption differ, especially in permanence; that distinction does not turn an unsupported orientation inference into substantial fit. Longstanding care relationships sharpen the mismatch without proving any applicant entitled to a particular placement. The developed justifications can be resolved under the governing standard, so the remand implements the settled equality ruling. Lawrence's reservation of civil status does not suspend independently established equal-protection law.

##### Equal consideration and effective policy relief complete the decision

**Holding and operative rule:** Prospective relief must remove the categorical bar for applicants governed by the policy and provide genuine consideration under lawful individual adoption criteria; Florida bears the burden of showing that narrower relief fully cures the policy defect. Because that equality relief affords the complete requested eligibility opportunity, the alternative substantive-due-process claim concerning existing families is unnecessary to the judgment and is neither accepted nor rejected.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the seven-Justice Court coalition, supplies the remedy and the ground for alternative-claim nonreach.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson v. Spear requires a remedy that corrects the policy's unlawful operation rather than only grants a named plaintiff an exception that leaves the defect intact. The Department must remove the disqualifying criterion from its instructions and decisions, notify responsible personnel and provide prompt lawful reconsideration of operative rejections resting only on that basis within ordinary applicable procedures. The district court must set and enforce a compliance schedule. Officials retain responsibility for actual fitness, existing parental rights and the particular child's interests, but cannot restore the invalid categorical assumption as an unexamined fitness rule. Troxel and Smith v. Organization of Foster Families address the legal sources, expectations and interference relevant to family liberty. Here equality affords the requested opportunity in full, making an independent family-liberty ruling unnecessary.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Shahar v. Bowers is applied: existing intermediate scrutiny and its deliberate-status-proxy rule govern the adoption classification.
- Equality Foundation of Greater Cincinnati v. City of Cincinnati is applied: the same equality protection extends beyond employment and rejects unsupported categorical disability.
- Romer v. Evans remains an independent ordinary-review floor, not a replacement for the governing tier.
- Lawrence v. Texas retains its private-intimacy protection and civil-status reservation; that reservation does not curtail the independently applicable equality rule.
- Anderson v. Spear supplies effective common-policy relief and the State's opportunity to establish a fully curative narrower decree.
- Troxel v. Granville and Smith v. Organization of Foster Families retain their distinct family-relationship principles; complete equality relief makes the alternative liberty ground unnecessary here.

#### Law After Decision

The categorical adoption disqualification and deliberate proxies for it cannot govern eligibility. Florida must offer real, prompt consideration under supported child-specific criteria. The ruling applies the existing intermediate-scrutiny rule to the statewide exclusion; it preserves professional evaluation, relevant individual differences and ordinary adoption procedures. The established-family due-process theory remains undecided because the equality judgment supplies the requested eligibility relief.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia, joined by Thomas, dissents on both claims. The writing acknowledges that Shahar makes intermediate scrutiny the current rule and expressly proposes rejecting that tier to apply its maintained ordinary-rationality position. Under that proposed standard, responsibility for permanent placements and the possibility that single heterosexual applicants may later form mother-and-father households sufficiently justify the distinction despite imperfect coverage. The dissent does not represent conceivable reasons as satisfying intermediate scrutiny.

On the independent family-liberty claim, the dissent distinguishes protection of an existing family's care and custody from a constitutional command to create adoptive status. Troxel protects a fit parent's established authority; Smith v. Organization of Foster Families requires attention to the statutory origins and expectations of foster relationships. The challenged law does not itself remove the children or dissolve current care arrangements. The biological father's consent in Houghton's case removes an obstacle but does not establish every legal adoption condition. Longstanding bonds and the practical financial and permanence interests are substantial, yet in the dissent's view they do not require conversion to a new status. It would affirm both claims on those grounds.

The stipulated absence of a general right to adopt did not withdraw the distinct claims of equality and existing family integrity. The record concerns an eligibility barrier, not a custody-removal order or a final adoption determination.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for a declaration and an injunction enforcing equal eligibility. The district court must establish and enforce the compliance schedule, including corrected instructions, notice to responsible officials and prompt lawful reconsideration within ordinary procedures. Actual applications remain subject to child-specific determinations, existing parental rights and lawful adoption requirements.

On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, which denied a certificate of appealability from denial of federal habeas relief. ### Arthur Andersen LLP v. United States, No. 04-368

Merits decision, May 31, 2005

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 27, 2005; decided May 31, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, which affirmed the Southern District of Texas obstruction conviction at 374 F.3d 281. The questions are whether the instructions required knowing corrupt persuasion and the necessary connection to an official proceeding under the version of 18 U.S.C. §1512(b)(2) applicable to the charged October–November 2001 conduct.

Andersen's directions to follow its document policy preceded extensive destruction of Enron-related records amid SEC contacts, anticipated litigation and internal legal discussions. The charge permitted conviction despite an honest belief in lawfulness and defined the forbidden purpose through interference with government factfinding. The preserved challenges concern that charge and its contemplated-proceeding requirement.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The conviction and its dependent sentence are set aside for prejudicial instructional error. Further proceedings must apply the correctly stated elements, with any independently preserved sufficiency issue and the ordinary double-jeopardy limits governing the relief then available. This trial-error disposition directs neither an acquittal nor a mandatory retrial.

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Knowing corrupt persuasion

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1512(b)(2)(A) and (B), as applicable to the 2001 conduct, requires awareness of the wrongful character of persuasion, together with the specified intent to cause withholding of evidence from an official proceeding or impairment of an object's integrity or availability for use there. Merely intending to impede government factfinding is insufficient; knowledge of the statutory citation or of the precise criminal prohibition is unnecessary.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices, controls this construction and the following proceeding and verdict holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Persuasion is often lawful. Advice to assert a valid privilege or to comply with an ordinary document policy does not become corrupt merely because it makes information unavailable. The enacted mental-state language distinguishes that conduct from consciously wrongful interference. Equally, a policy or counsel-participation label does not protect knowing obstruction. Investigative warnings, timing, internal advice and anticipated evidentiary use can establish the required state of mind under a correct charge. Here the instruction affirmatively permitted an honest belief that the conduct was lawful and reduced corrupt purpose to interference with factfinding. It therefore failed to require the wrongful awareness Congress made part of this offense. The Government's interest in preventing anticipatory destruction cannot remove that enacted limit.

##### A particular contemplated official proceeding

**Holding and operative rule:** The persuader must contemplate an identifiable official proceeding in which the evidence might be material and act with the statutory withholding or impairment purpose toward that use. The proceeding need not already be pending, about to begin, certain to occur or assigned a known date; no subpoena or successful obstruction is required.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1512(e)(1) gives the offense anticipatory reach. It eliminates pendency, rather than the contemplated official object to which the specific intent relates. Aguilar supplies the need to respect the enacted connection to official work, but its section 1503 pending-proceeding requirement cannot be transferred to a statute expressly reaching future proceedings. Abstract awareness that records might interest government someday does not supply this connection. Conversely, the SEC contacts and legal discussions could support contemplation under correct instructions; their significance belongs to a properly instructed jury. The challenged charge removed the necessary connection by allowing the prospect of indefinite future government factfinding to suffice.

The applicable section 1512(e)(2) permits the offense even if the evidence is inadmissible or subject to a privilege claim. Section 1512(f) removes a need to prove knowledge of a proceeding's federal character; it does not eliminate contemplation and purpose concerning the proceeding itself. Section 1512(d) supplies a separate affirmative defense, proved by the defendant by a preponderance, when the conduct was solely lawful and the sole intention was to encourage, induce or cause truthful testimony. That defense does not replace the Government's obligation to prove the offense's elements. The separate contention that an informal inquiry is outside the statutory definition of an official proceeding was not preserved and is not decided.

##### The errors contributed unreliably to this verdict

**Holding and operative rule:** On this charge and contested mental-state record, the Government has not established beyond reasonable doubt that the instructional errors did not contribute to the verdict. The conviction must be set aside and the matter remanded for lawful further proceedings.

**Controlling explanation:** Neder requires attention to the actual verdict, rather than an appellate conclusion that a properly instructed jury could have convicted. Here the charge permitted a disputed mental state insufficient under the statute, the verdict supplies no actual equivalent findings establishing the required culpability and proceeding relationship, and the evidence leaves competing mental-state inferences. Those features together defeat reliable noncontribution beyond reasonable doubt. A finding of deliberate withholding under this charge does not itself establish conscious wrongful persuasion toward contemplated official use. The reported deadlock does not independently prove prejudice; the defect is the absence of a reliable connection between this verdict and the required offense. Another harmlessness inquiry cannot supply that missing connection on this record. This conclusion resolves these instructions without adopting a new general classification or inferential rule for other instructional errors.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Aguilar:** Applied for offense-specific connection and knowledge concerning official work. Its section 1503 pendency rule remains confined to that provision; section 1512's express anticipatory coverage is preserved.
- **Neder v. United States:** Applied for the Government's beyond-reasonable-doubt noncontribution burden and attention to the actual verdict. Its existing qualified method retains its stated scope; the shared conclusion here rests on absent actual equivalent findings and competing mental-state inferences.

#### Law After Decision

Knowing corrupt persuasion under the charged statute requires conscious wrongdoing and the specified intent concerning a particular contemplated official proceeding. The construction protects lawful persuasion while allowing prosecution of proved anticipatory obstruction before formal process. Andersen's conviction cannot stand under instructions that omitted those requirements. The decision applies the governing version of section 1512.

#### Source Notes

18 U.S.C. §§1512(b)(2), (d)–(f), 1515 (2000); 374 F.3d 281; 544 U.S. 696. United States v. Aguilar, No. 94-270 (June 21, 1995); Neder v. United States, No. 97-1985 (June 10, 1999).

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for lawful further proceedings under the correct elements. The challenged conviction and dependent sentence cease to support punishment; ordinary sufficiency and double-jeopardy rules determine the proceedings available on remand.

**End of entry: Arthur Andersen LLP v. United States, merits decision, May 31, 2005.**

### Cutter v. Wilkinson, No. 03-9877

Merits decision, May 31, 2005

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 21, 2005; decided May 31, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, which reversed an interlocutory ruling rejecting a facial Establishment Clause challenge to section 3 of the Religious Land Use and Institutionalized Persons Act, 349 F.3d 257. The question is whether that institutionalized-persons protection is facially invalid because it specially protects religious exercise.

Current and former Ohio inmates allege restrictions on literature, worship, dress, ceremonial objects and religious assistance. Officials stipulated for this stage to the bona fides and sincerity of the asserted beliefs. The appeal concerns the categorical constitutional ground for dismissing their statutory claims.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The statutory claims return for adjudication of coverage, actual burdens, application-specific defenses and appropriate claimant-specific relief, together with properly preserved distinct constitutional defenses.

#### Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed a concurring opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Neutral accommodation of private religious exercise in custody

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3's generally available protection against substantial government-imposed burdens on religious exercise in covered institutions is not facially invalid under the Establishment Clause. Government may relieve exceptional constraints its custody imposes on private exercise even when the Free Exercise Clause would not independently require that accommodation or comparable nonreligious interests receive less statutory protection.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices, controls this holding and the statutory-administration and remand holdings below.

**Controlling explanation:** Custody limits ordinary means of obtaining materials, associating and observing religious practices. Relieving that government-created dependence restores private exercise; it does not necessarily make the exercise an official religious program. Section 3 selects no denomination, requires no religious adherence and assigns no civil authority to a sect. Kiryas Joel permits accommodation beyond the constitutional minimum while forbidding denominational allocation of government power. Agostini directs attention to the actual governmental act and function, with purpose, effects and entanglement as evidence within its unified inquiry. This provision performs an accommodation function. Doe v. Porter concerns the different act of adopting devotional instruction as a public institution's own program.

The categorical objection that religion receives greater protection than other interests overlooks that distinction. The possibility that accommodation makes private religious practice more attractive does not itself establish coercion or sponsorship. At the same time, accommodation remains subject to neutrality, other persons' constitutional rights and the prohibition on governmental devotion. Those limits and the statute's justification defense require adjudication of concrete demands rather than dismissal of the entire protection.

##### The enacted standard accounts for institutional interests

**Holding and operative rule:** A claimant must establish statutory coverage and a substantial burden on sincere religious exercise. Government then must demonstrate, through production and persuasion, that imposing that burden on that person furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. Institutional security, order, resources and effects on others receive concrete evidentiary weight within that test.

Section 3 covers a substantial burden imposed in a program or activity receiving federal financial assistance, or one that affects—or whose removal would affect—commerce with foreign nations, among the States or with Indian tribes. When the latter commerce claim is the only jurisdictional basis, section 4(g) makes the provision inapplicable if government demonstrates that all substantial burdens on, or removal of all substantial burdens from, similar religious exercise throughout the Nation would not lead in the aggregate to a substantial effect on that commerce. That conditional coverage limitation is distinct from section 3's compelling-interest defense. Section 4(b)'s separately worded burden provision concerns Free Exercise or section 2 claims; section 3's own language governs the defense here.

Religious exercise need not be compelled by or central to a belief system; sincerity remains a legitimate inquiry. Section 5 permits government to eliminate the burden by changing the policy or practice, retaining it with an exemption for the burdened exercise, exempting applications that substantially burden exercise, or another means that eliminates the burden. The Act neither creates nor precludes a right to religious funding or assistance, although avoiding a substantial burden may require expense in government's own operations. Its broad-construction direction operates within the text and Constitution, its severability provision preserves unaffected provisions and applications, and section 6 leaves the Establishment Clause's independent limits intact.

**Controlling explanation:** A dangerous item, a proposed gathering and harmless literature can present different institutional risks. Officials' experience and supported evidence of security, staffing, expense, coercion and burdens on other persons matter; the court must assess them in the actual setting. Neither a general warning about prison management nor sincerity alone settles the claim. Congress's compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means language cannot be replaced by rationality review, and the accommodation cannot compel unconstitutional religious activity or disregard others' constitutional rights. Courts assess substantial burden and justification without deciding theological truth. The statutory sequence addresses excessive demands while retaining a meaningful protection against unsupported restrictions.

##### The claims and distinct defenses return for ordinary adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** Rejection of the facial Establishment Clause ground requires reversal of that dismissal and adjudication of the statutory claims under the stated prerequisites, defense and constitutional limits. Relief must correspond to a proven violation and the claimant's continuing interest; Article III and the Prison Litigation Reform Act, including applicable exhaustion requirements, retain their force.

**Controlling explanation:** The sincerity stipulation is stage-specific and does not decide a particular practice's burden, risk or remedy. The existence of live claims likewise leaves former inmates' requests for prospective relief subject to their own continuing-interest requirements. The preserved Spending and Commerce objections and distinct federalism defenses fall outside the facial Establishment question decided here. Boerne invalidated RFRA's universal state and local command as an exercise of Fourteenth Amendment section 5 power; that holding neither resolves these different power predicates nor disappears because neutral accommodation is constitutionally permissible. The lower courts must address genuinely preserved defenses in their proper order.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Kiryas Joel:** Applied to distinguish permissible accommodation beyond the Free Exercise minimum from denominational allocation of civil authority or particular sectarian privilege.
- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied through its unified governmental-act/function inquiry. Purpose, effects and entanglement remain evidence within that inquiry; the former independent Lemon prongs are not a separate fallback.
- **Doe v. Porter:** Distinguished because official adoption of devotional instruction differs from restoring private religious exercise constrained by custody.
- **City of Boerne v. Flores:** Preserved at its section 5 scope. Its rejection of RFRA's universal state/local command does not determine the distinct Spending or Commerce objections reserved here.

#### Law After Decision

Section 3 remains available to covered institutionalized persons seeking relief from substantial burdens on sincere religious exercise. Its religious focus is permissible accommodation, while each application remains governed by the enacted defense, constitutional limits, standing and applicable procedural requirements. The facial Establishment ruling supplies no resolution of the separately preserved congressional-power defenses.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Thomas, concurring.** Thomas joins the opinion of the Court in full and adds that federal legislation concerning religion is not necessarily legislation establishing religion. A general accommodation provision does not necessarily compel a State to impose religious observance or support, or to prefer a sect. A categorical assertion that religion is exclusively a state subject therefore does not establish this facial violation. That distinction does not supply unlimited congressional power: Spending, Commerce and other unadjudicated power objections retain their separate questions. His additional ground is consistent with the controlling accommodation analysis and changes neither the statutory standard nor the remand.

Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet, 512 U.S. 687; Agostini v. Felton (June 23, 1997); City of Boerne v. Flores (June 25, 1997); Doe v. Porter (April 26, 2005).

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for ordinary adjudication of the inmates' statutory claims and preserved defenses. The courts must determine the actual substantial burdens, supported institutional justifications and lawful relief for claimants with a continuing interest, under the statutory and constitutional limits stated above.

### Tory v. Cochran, No. 03-1488

Merits decision, May 31, 2005

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 22, 2005; decided May 31, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the California Court of Appeal, Second Appellate District, which affirmed a permanent injunction obtained by Johnnie Cochran against Ulysses Tory and Ruth Craft. The questions concern a prohibition on all future public speech about an admitted public figure and the consequences of his death for the continuing controversy and relief.

The trial court found false statements, threatening letters, picketing and demands intended to coerce payment of money Cochran did not owe. Its decree nevertheless prohibits public picketing, signs and oral statements about Cochran and his firm by petitioners and their agents or representatives. Cochran died on March 29, after argument. The order has no automatic death-termination term, and respondent continues to assert its enforceability. The firm was not a party to the original action.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

The unopposed motion to substitute Sylvia Dale Mason Cochran as respondent is granted.

**Vacated and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas, who would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted.

The unsupported all-public-topic prohibition and enforcement dependent on it cannot continue unchanged. The lawful liability findings and independently valid unreviewed relief are preserved. An appropriate party may seek new relief in state court supported by a continuing claim and fitted to the changed circumstances; the mandate neither supplies a replacement decree nor establishes a successor's substantive entitlement.

#### Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER and GINSBURG, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion. THOMAS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA, J., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The continuing command preserves a live controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** When an injunction remains operative by its terms and is asserted to remain enforceable, the beneficiary's death does not eliminate the restrained speakers' live challenge merely because the original substantive interest has changed. The agreed substitution is permitted for this appeal, without determining a successor's independent entitlement to relief.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice coalition identified above joins this holding, the present-overbreadth holding and the corresponding vacatur and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The command still regulates petitioners' speech, and respondent continues to claim its enforcement. A speaker need not risk contempt to obtain review of that burden. California procedures allowing later challenges do not make the current command disappear. Death changes the available justification and potential claimants; it does not, on this record, eliminate the restraint. The Court can correct that present injury without settling the independent state-law ownership, survival and successor-entitlement questions outside this appeal.

##### The present complete topic ban lacks a sufficient remedial justification

**Holding and operative rule:** In the present circumstances, the injunction's prohibition on all public statements about Cochran and his firm is an unjustifiably broad prior restraint. Cochran's death has eliminated or materially diminished its stated objective of preventing coercion of him into paying money, and the existing record supplies no new adjudicated justification for maintaining the same complete prohibition to protect the practice.

**Controlling explanation:** A prior restraint requires a justification fitted to the speech it forbids and the injury it prevents. Near, Keefe and Nebraska Press supply that protection against unsupported advance suppression. Madsen and Schenck require a remedial connection between wrongdoing and each restriction; their content-neutral formula does not itself validate this subject-based decree. Alexander distinguishes punishment for past conduct from an order actually forbidding future expression, which this injunction does.

The trial court's serious findings remain facts. They do not supply a current reason to suppress every truthful, unrelated or otherwise lawful statement after the central coercion-prevention objective has disappeared or materially diminished. An asserted interest in the firm cannot substitute for an adjudicated basis supporting this order's full reach. The continuing, express restraint also answers the argument for leaving all correction to California: present constitutional relief is available on the changed circumstances and existing record. State proceedings remain the place for any new supported remedy.

##### Relief follows the defect in the existing command

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment sustaining the present public-topic prohibition is vacated, and its dependent enforcement must be removed with it. On remand an appropriate party may seek a different, precisely supported remedy for independently unlawful conduct under the applicable substantive and constitutional requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** Vacatur responds to the prospective restraint and preserves the lawful liability findings. A future decree must identify the party entitled to relief, the continuing wrong, the responsible persons and the speech or conduct properly restricted. Madsen and Schenck preserve the requirement of notice and actual concert before an injunction binds persons through concerted conduct; neither a general label nor the substitution makes the nonparty firm an original claimant. A prohibition on the same protected discussion cannot survive merely through a differently titled dependent clause. The constitutionality of a different injunction confined to adjudicated false statements requires an order and remedial record absent from this case; independently lawful remedies retain their own elements.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Near v. Minnesota:** Applied for the constitutional protection against unsupported advance suppression of publication.
- **Organization for a Better Austin v. Keefe:** Applied for the protection against an unsupported injunction suppressing public criticism.
- **Nebraska Press Association v. Stuart:** Applied for the demanding justification required for a prior restraint.
- **Madsen v. Women's Health Center:** Applied for provision-specific remedial fit and responsibility of the persons bound. Its content-neutral injunction standard is not a safe harbor for this topic-based prohibition.
- **Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network:** Applied for fitted restraints, notice and actual-concert limits, and removal of enforcement dependent on an invalid speech restriction.
- **Alexander v. United States:** Distinguished because this decree directly forbids future speech, whereas the prior-restraint classification there concerned punishment without a future-publication ban.

#### Law After Decision

The existing complete public-topic restraint cannot survive its present lack of a sufficient remedial justification after Cochran's death. An operative judicial command asserted to remain enforceable is reviewable while it continues to burden the speakers. Relief removes that prohibition and dependent enforcement while preserving lawful liability and the opportunity for an appropriate party to seek a supported, constitutionally confined new remedy.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief Justice joins the Court's continuing-controversy, present-overbreadth and remedial grounds. He further concludes that the decree's blanket topic prohibition violates the First Amendment independently of Cochran's death. Findings of coercive defamation justify an appropriate remedy for proved unlawful conduct, but they do not establish that every future public statement about Cochran or his firm is actionable. The command suppresses truthful criticism, discussion of this lawsuit and other protected expression without differentiating those statements from the adjudicated wrong. Madsen and Schenck require a supported remedial connection, while Alexander confirms the significance of an express future-speech ban; the content-neutral standard in the former decisions is not itself the standard for this content-based order. Death further weakens the stated objective of preventing Cochran's personal coercion. An interest in the practice does not supply findings justifying the decree's entire breadth. He would remove that public-topic prohibition, including dependent enforcement, while preserving lawful liability findings and independently valid relief. A different injunction confined to established unlawful conduct requires a concrete order and continuing remedial record; his ground does not decide every injunction following adjudicated defamation or supply immunity for threats, coercion or actionable false statements.

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Scalia.** These Justices would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted. Cochran's death removed the original vehicle for the broad question on which review was granted. The new need to interpret, modify or dissolve the decree can be addressed in California, where constitutional objections remain available. A case-specific need to correct the existing command does not, in their view, restore the reason for retaining this changed vehicle or justify reaching a new constitutional issue. Their position neither affirms the decree's constitutionality nor decides the contested mootness question; it leaves the parties to seek appropriate state-court correction.

The case returns to the California courts for proceedings consistent with vacatur of the present topic prohibition and its dependent enforcement. Any request for new relief must rest on the requesting party's own entitlement, a supported continuing wrong and a precisely fitted decree. Lawful liability determinations and independently valid unreviewed relief remain operative.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Cumulative burdens require adjudication under the complete access standard

**Holding and operative rule:** Specific evidence of added expenditures, lost provider capacity and consequential delay precludes summary judgment for the State when it creates material disputes about attributable interference or justification under the complete previability framework. The lower court must examine each surviving obligation and the challenged interacting package, including supported affected subgroups, and apply the protections in their prescribed order.

**Authority:** The six-Justice complete-opinion coalition identified above joins the complete rule and additional mandate, supplying six direct joins. Kennedy separately joins the remand concerning protected choice, a substantial practical obstacle and timely necessary care; his seventh judgment vote does not join the residual severe- and meaningful-interference requirements. The six-Justice holding adopts the following complete operative standard:

**Current rule:** Before viability the patient decides whether to continue pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons. The State may not prohibit that choice, confer effective control on another person, or prevent timely necessary life-and-health care. After viability, prohibition requires the life-and-health safeguard, including individualized physical and mental health considerations. Viability is individualized and medically supported, not a fixed constitutional week. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons; not every preference is a medical emergency. The following complete standard governs throughout previability:

1. **Identify the duty and protected baseline.** Identify the enacted command, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances. Distinguish an actual obligation from assumed future administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care violates the baseline and cannot be rescued by justification. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Defining all noncompliant treatment as unsafe does not establish medical necessity.
2. **Identify actual function and purpose.** Identify what the obligation does and the asserted permissible objective, including safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration or financial accountability. The State may prefer childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstruction, punishment of protected choice or burdens imposed for their deterrent effect. An obstructive purpose established by objective evidence of text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the requirement; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Voluntary persuasion differs from prevention through barriers. Evaluate each justification on its terms: medical claims need medical support, and deliberative claims need an explanation linking the particular obligation to informed decisionmaking.
3. **Establish actual interference, comparison and affected population.** Challengers must establish concrete additional interference caused by the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include extra appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion and disclosure consequences affecting choice or access. Compare the actual system with and without the obligation while holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. For a challenged interacting package, identify the package and compare the system without it. Consider cumulative effects without counting the same burden twice. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding constitutional injury, include evidence-supported subgroups, and keep the population stable. Do not average the burden across everyone pregnant or define the group as only those who prevail. Poverty, distance, transportation, employment and abuse matter insofar as they explain the State's added condition; identify that causal link. Exceptions, bypasses and alternative providers count only if actually usable. Meaningful interference means concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review rather than establishing invalidity. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly threshold applies. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony and reasonable predictions can establish sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury before access is lost.
4. **Apply the substantive protections in order.** First, a baseline violation, unconstitutional obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the affected population's practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and success by some patients does not establish adequate access for others. A compelling interest cannot rescue an established obstacle. Second, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Identify the severe intrusion and explain why the independent prohibitions did not already decide the issue; overlapping protections require no artificial boundary, but an obstacle may not be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical judgment, health or protected confidentiality may require this scrutiny despite usable access. Third, for remaining meaningful interference the State must substantiate a permissible justification and its fit to the added obligation. Abstract interests and conclusory assertions are insufficient. When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it; inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences may answer that showing. The State need not select the cheapest imaginable policy or disprove every hypothetical alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; particular legislative findings, a new study in every case, scientific certainty and numerical benefit calculations are not required. Once justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy. Below meaningful interference this heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary lawful-regulation requirements remain available.
5. **Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication.** Evaluate medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight, without a professional association's veto or conclusive weight for a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. Necessary care must remain timely; unnecessary committee vetoes and documentation functioning as prior permission may not displace appropriate clinical judgment. Duties must be sufficiently definite and applicable fault requirements respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every element of a punishable offense; lawful enforcement against fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remains. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accuracy does not alone validate burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Distinguish useful confidential oversight records from public disclosure and records enforcing invalid obligations.
6. **Identify the violation and tailor relief.** Specify the duty or application that fails, why, and the corresponding relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law and expressly address dependent certifications, report fields and sanctions. An individual claim does not fail because others comply; broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it. Voluntary compliance in some circumstances does not alone validate compulsory compliance. Otherwise confine relief to the established applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not remove proof of causation, scope or inadequate justification. For cumulative burdens, remedy the challenged obligation's or package's contribution without automatically invalidating all regulation. Replacing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision; the mandate must specify the resulting duties.

Challengers establish the duty, stable population, causation and triggering interference before the State bears the corresponding justification and fit burden. Gestational age may support a medically justified safety requirement, but a trimester boundary does not itself change the standard. Necessary-care protection includes competent individualized medical support for a material risk of death or serious health harm, including grave medically established mental danger, when the method is necessary for timely, safe and suitable care; preference or marginal comparative advantage alone is insufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey supplies the whole rule; Lawall, Greenville and Taft preserve its independent operation for practical access and clinical protection. The providers offered concrete mechanisms: equipment expenses, nurse staffing, overtime and follow-up costs, predicted departures and loss of physician capacity, and resulting delay and clinical risk. These are evidence-supported forecasts requiring adjudication, not findings of completed closures. A general safety label and the absence of one exact predicted fee increase do not eliminate those disputes. Conversely, the providers' request for trial and the contested causation and justification record require factual proceedings under the rule.

The comparison must include the practical costs of surviving requirements, including privilege applications, meetings, on-call work and the actual on-site duty. The on-site privilege holder need not personally perform every procedure, but must remain until patients are stable and ready to leave recovery. Duties removed by the independent holdings cannot be counted as retained lawful obligations. The State's serious safety concerns receive their evidentiary force; they must answer the actual interference at the applicable level. The dissent's rational-regulation approach does not account for the protected constitutional choice governing this case, and limiting review to substantial obstacles would omit the framework's distinct protections for remaining severe and meaningful interference.

##### Ordinary provider categories and independent patient sex equality

**Holding and operative rule:** The professional category and exemption for very small first-trimester practices survive the presented equal-protection challenges because targeted safety oversight and administrative burden provide rational relationships to legitimate ends. The independent patient sex-equality claim must be adjudicated separately: an actual sex classification in terms or administration requires a genuine important objective and substantial relationship, while a genuinely neutral classification challenged for discrimination requires a choice made at least partly because of sex or its adverse consequences; disparity and awareness alone are insufficient.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join these results and Klinger’s classification-and-purpose rule. The direct common proposition requires no automatic classification of every abortion regulation as a sex distinction.

**Controlling explanation:** The law covers five or more first-trimester abortions in a month or any later-trimester abortion. Practice volume can rationally bear on regulatory expense and the scale of potential harm, and the profession is not a suspect class. Those conclusions do not answer the separately pleaded comparison of women's health services with comparably risky services for men. Klinger requires the court to identify the actual governmental distinction and relevant opportunity before choosing review. A medical label cannot extinguish that independent inquiry, and an actual sex classification does not require an additional finding of hostile motive before intermediate scrutiny applies. The lower court's rejection of particular animus evidence under rational review did not resolve the classification and comparator questions it treated as absorbed into access analysis. On remand the court must identify the actual classification or purposeful administration, make the required findings and apply the corresponding standard.

##### Immediate compulsory entry requires constitutionally adequate authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Arizona may not compel the challenged open-ended immediate clinic entry without a warrant or an established exception merely by newly imposing extensive regulation. The State must establish the exception on which it relies. In a genuinely closely regulated industry, Burger additionally requires a substantial governmental interest, necessity for warrantless inspection and a constitutionally adequate substitute constraining official discretion; this scheme does not establish those predicates for its full mandatory entry command.

**Authority:** All nine join the rule and remedy on Burger and protected-premises grounds. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer also adopt Ferguson's operation-specific and no-general-waiver reasoning as corroboration.

**Controlling explanation:** Commercial medical premises retain constitutional protection. The newly imposed regulatory density cannot itself establish the historical regulated-industry premise or eliminate the privacy of examination rooms, patients and records. The operating-hours restriction supplies a time limit but not adequate control of the authorized intrusion. Legitimate urgent health oversight and professional licensure therefore do not sustain this breadth. Ordinary consent, an appropriately obtained warrant and a genuine exigency remain lawful sources for an inspection. Ferguson likewise requires attention to the actual medical operation and rejects treating attendance for care as general consent, while its coordinated prosecution setting differs from this administrative scheme. The restraint reaches compulsory entry without adequate authority and the applications and sanctions dependent on that entry command.

##### Recipient-specific limits govern department and contractor information access

**Holding and operative rule:** Official access to intimate patient information requires a sufficient connection among the information, actual recipient, regulatory function, scope, uses, retention and safeguards. The established department access and copying under R9-10-1511(A)(4)(b),(c), and APEX identified-ultrasound access and retention under §36-2301.02(B),(C), lack that connection and must be restrained at their unjustified scope.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete privacy rule, these applications and corresponding relief in the Court's opinion. Scalia and Thomas dissent from these two privacy dispositions.

**Controlling explanation:** Greenville, Lawall and Herring protect against unjustified authorized exposure, while allowing functionally justified identified audits, complaints and corroboration. Each identifier need not be indispensable. Here department access includes broad unredacted histories and copying without supported need-to-know restrictions; vague training assurances and uncertain sanctions do not adequately constrain its recipients and uses. APEX receives identified ultrasound prints with seven-year retention, minimal secure-storage terms for the first two years and inadequate later-storage and employee-access limits. A bare nondisclosure rule does not cure those permissions, and a proposed coding policy is not operative protection.

The constitutional injury concerns actual authorized access, not a presumption of unlawful leakage. Redaction, coding, consent and targeted follow-up are concrete alternatives when adequate to the task, rather than universal prerequisites. The dissent's constitutional-source objection cannot displace the recipient-specific protection established by Greenville and Lawall. The remedy must remove the proved excessive access and redistribution while preserving justified confidential oversight, with exact recipients, tasks and dependent consequences specified below.

##### Bounded serious-incident reports serve a distinct disciplinary function

**Holding and operative rule:** The limited report under R9-10-1504(B),(C) concerning an abortion-related death or serious injury to a patient or viable fetus may supply the patient's name, incident description and follow-up action to the responsible professional board under the distinct confidentiality protections of §§32-1451.01(C) and 32-1855.03(D). That defined disciplinary function, limited subject and protected disclosure provide the required connection on this record.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices join the complete privacy rationale. Scalia and Thomas join the rejection of this challenge and the permission for lawful bounded board reporting, giving the disposition nine votes without adding their joins to the substantive-privacy rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** A serious injury creates a concrete disciplinary inquiry for an identified professional board. The limited information and the board-specific protections materially distinguish that channel from department-wide access and contractor retention. Those other provisions' defects cannot be transferred to different recipients and safeguards. The amended appellate court entertained this particular issue and sustained the report; that ruling is affirmed at its actual scope.

##### Undefined dignity language cannot independently support punishment

**Holding and operative rule:** R9-10-1507's unconfined requirement of consideration, respect and full recognition of dignity and individuality cannot independently support civil, criminal or adverse licensing sanctions without an ascertainable operative standard. Relief removes that independently punishable branch and dependent consequences while preserving definite clinical and professional duties.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this confined rule and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Greenville distinguishes contextual professional standards from undefined additional duties selected by administrators. The value of respectful care does not identify the additional conduct required for full recognition of dignity and individuality. This record supplies no authoritative limitation or recognized clinical criterion that makes an independent sanction ascertainable. A warning, mitigation or promise of careful enforcement cannot create the omitted standard. The defect differs from a recognizable professional-core obligation: it concerns punishment based on an inspector's or complainant's subjective judgment under this abstract additional requirement. Removing that sanction branch leaves voluntary aspirations and independently ascertainable care requirements intact.

##### Constrained hospital qualifications do not establish the alleged private veto

**Holding and operative rule:** The facial standardless-delegation challenge fails where Arizona law supplies hospital-related legitimate standards, notice and hearing, and judicial review for reasonableness and substantial evidentiary support, and the record establishes no actual or supported impending arbitrary refusal based on abortion practice. The costs and operational burdens of the qualification requirement remain part of the access proceedings at the scope described above.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join the qualification ruling and rejection of the present delegation claim. The access consequences retain the six-Justice complete-framework and Kennedy narrower joins already stated.

**Controlling explanation:** Greenville permits objective professional qualifications and emergency-care arrangements while forbidding arbitrary, legally uncontrolled private licensing consent. Arizona's Holmes, Peterson and Blende rules provide actual constraints and review. The possibility of private discretion does not establish an uncontrolled veto under this record and those enforceable limits. A supported impending arbitrary exclusion can be challenged before a clinic closes; public-hospital restrictions cannot simply be assumed to govern every private institution. The present rejection concerns the private-control theory. Staffing, applications, meetings, on-call duties and the on-site requirement's effects still must be considered under the governing access rule.

##### Remedy follows the precise violation and its proved scope

**Holding and operative rule:** The lower court must apply Arizona severability law to the identified unconstitutional duties and applications, remove their dependent enforcement consequences, preserve independently lawful obligations, and promptly specify the stipulated restraint's continuing operation. Further factual and equitable determinations must identify the actual violation, affected population and scope before selecting corresponding relief.

**Authority:** All nine join those common severability and implementation requirements for the independent defects they support. The complete-framework majority of six joins their application to all access grounds; Kennedy joins effective relief on protected-choice, obstacle, necessary-care and independent grounds; Scalia and Thomas retain their stated objections to relief resting on abortion or substantive informational privacy.

Once a common-policy violation and its scope are proved, the seven Justices supporting the protected-choice/necessary-care and independent privacy grounds also apply Taft's corrective-policy rule: relief must protect the governed patients and providers unless the State establishes that a narrower remedy fully cures the violation. Six join its application when the predicate is the residual access framework. Challengers retain the initial burden to prove injury and scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The unresolved access and patient-equality questions require further adjudication before a final severability package can be fixed. The stipulation remains governed by its terms pending a prompt reasoned order; partial affirmance does not itself authorize blanket enforcement. The court must identify retained duties, prohibited applications, bound officials and dependent civil, criminal and licensing consequences. Any continuation or modification of protection requires the existing stipulation and ordinary equitable predicates. Taft's application of Anderson requires a remedy matching a proved common-policy injury, while preserving the State's opportunity to establish a complete narrower cure. The court should expedite genuinely material factual work and state the resulting obligations precisely.

### Gonzales v. Raich, No. 03-1454

Merits decision, June 6, 2005

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 29, 2004; decided June 6, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, which reversed denial of preliminary relief and directed an injunction on the as-applied Commerce Clause theory, 352 F.3d 1222. The question is whether Congress may apply the Controlled Substances Act to intrastate cultivation and possession of marijuana for personal medical use recommended by a physician and permitted by California law.

Monson grows marijuana for her own treatment. Raich receives locally cultivated marijuana without charge from two caregivers. Their physicians supplied evidence of serious conditions, unsuccessful conventional treatment and the asserted medical importance of cannabis. Federal agents seized and destroyed Monson's six plants on August 15, 2002. The complaint also asserts distinct patient-liberty, necessity and other constitutional theories. Review concerns the Commerce foundation for preliminary relief.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Commerce Clause basis for the preliminary-injunction direction is rejected. The lower courts must remove that basis and promptly adjudicate genuinely live, preserved independent claims and any request for interim relief under their own law and evidence, including actual medical-risk evidence where legally relevant.

#### Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Local production and possession are integral to this interstate supply regime

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may include unauthorized local marijuana cultivation, caregiver supply and possession in the Controlled Substances Act's independently valid interstate drug-control regime because that inclusion is reasonably adapted and integral to control of the same fungible substance's supply and distribution. The Government must identify the valid interstate regime, enacted covered class and genuine functional relationship; courts independently determine those boundaries while respecting reasonable congressional enforcement judgments within them.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices, controls this necessary-inclusion rule, its application and the following statutory-boundary and remedial holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Lopez requires an eligible interstate connection before aggregation. Home Builders supplies the necessary-inclusion route, and Gibbs, Rancho Viejo and GDF apply it without demanding border crossing, commercial motive or a separate substantial interstate effect for each local member of a genuinely connected class. Those decisions supply the rule, not facts about drugs.

The CSA controls production, distribution and possession through an integrated supply-and-authorization system for the substance traded interstate. Cultivation creates that substance, caregiver delivery transfers it, and possession retains it. Local unauthorized supply and its substitution for market acquisition bear directly on the regime's objects and enforceability. Section 801's supply and practical-differentiation findings support that concrete mechanism; generalized health costs or the description of the Act as comprehensive cannot supply the constitutional connection by themselves. The accepted local cultivation and possession remain within the mechanism even without a sale or interstate movement by these patients. The rule preserves independent judicial scrutiny of an application outside the enacted class, an invalid interstate regime or a nominal or pretextual relationship. It requires no least-restrictive-means showing or proof that every conceivable exemption would cause measurable enforcement failure.

##### Medical purpose and state permission do not remove the functional connection

**Holding and operative rule:** The accepted local, unpaid, physician-recommended and state-permitted setting does not sever the relationship between these production and possession activities and the federal substance-control system. California permission and a physician's recommendation do not amend federal authorization; the CSA's enacted definitions, authorization provisions and state-law limits retain their actual operation.

Section 841(a) prohibits knowing or intentional manufacture, distribution, dispensing and the related possession-with-intent conduct except as authorized by the subchapter. Section 844(a) governs knowing or intentional possession, preserving substances obtained directly, or under a valid prescription or order, from a practitioner acting professionally, and other authorization under the applicable subchapters. Those are federal statutory conditions, rather than an exemption created by state permission alone.

Section 802 includes planting, cultivation, growing and harvesting in production. Distribution is delivery other than administering or dispensing; delivery includes actual, constructive or attempted transfer regardless of an agency relationship and does not require payment. The manufacture definition excludes preparation, compounding, packaging or labeling in conformity with state or local law by a practitioner as an incident to professional administration or dispensing; that limited exception is not a general cultivation exemption. Marijuana remains governed by Schedule I and the federal authorization, research, registration and scheduling routes in force. Section 903 leaves otherwise valid state law in place unless a positive conflict prevents the federal provision and state law from consistently standing together. Direct federal regulation of private conduct does not require California to criminalize the same conduct or administer the federal program.

**Controlling explanation:** The patients' strongest boundary would define a separately administered local medical class and require proof that excluding it would defeat interstate enforcement. Their medical needs, lack of sales and state supervision are accepted at their proper record status. But medical purpose explains why production or possession occurs; it does not change the substance produced or remove the activity from the authorization and supply controls. A State's ability to administer an exception may inform the functional inquiry without obliging Congress to adopt that State's alternative system. The Court need not find patient-specific diversion or failure of California safeguards to identify this direct relationship. Household substitution alone would be too broad; the holding depends on the actual class and same-substance supply mechanism within an independently valid regime. The statutory operation and accepted activity facts settle that relationship here, so a further Commerce evidentiary proceeding is unnecessary.

##### Independent patient claims retain their own adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** The preliminary-injunction direction cannot stand on the rejected Commerce theory. The case returns for genuinely preserved independent patient claims and any request for interim relief under the substantive law, evidence and equitable requirements governing those claims.

**Controlling explanation:** Oakland Cannabis rejected the distributor's statutory medical-necessity exemption and an equitable recreation of it, while leaving individual possession, patient necessity and independent constitutional claims outside that disposition. The present grant resolves federal power, rather than the distinct due-process, Ninth Amendment, patient-necessity or commandeering theories. Those matters remain because they fall outside the question decided and require their own legal and factual adjudication. The physicians' evidence keeps its proper significance for medical risk and any applicable claim or interim-relief requirement; the Commerce decision does not decide those claims' merits or guarantee relief. The lower courts must address the actual preserved requests promptly under their proper standards.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Lopez:** Applied to require an identifiable connection fitted to the enacted class before aggregation; generalized national health, crime or productivity consequences remain insufficient substitutes.
- **National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt:** Applied at its reasonably-adapted and integral necessary-inclusion scope, with independent judicial identification of the valid regime, actual class and genuine relationship.
- **Gibbs v. Babbitt:** Applied for class-based inclusion without individual border crossing, commercial motive or a separate substantial interstate effect.
- **Rancho Viejo, LLC v. Norton:** Applied for the genuine regime/class/relationship inquiry and its outside-class, invalid-regime and nominal-or-pretextual limits.
- **GDF Realty Investments, Ltd. v. Norton:** Applied for the same necessary-inclusion rule and rejection of a routine member-specific effects hearing when the actual statutory relationship is established. Its wildlife facts do not establish this drug relationship.
- **United States v. Oakland Cannabis Buyers' Cooperative:** Preserved at its distributor-specific statutory and equitable scope, including its individual-patient and independent constitutional reservations.

#### Law After Decision

The existing necessary-inclusion rule permits the CSA to reach unauthorized local medical cultivation, caregiver supply and possession within its genuine same-substance interstate control system. State permission and absence of payment do not create the claimed Commerce exemption. Courts must continue to identify a valid regime, enacted class and actual functional connection; statutory authorization and the distinct preserved patient claims retain their own requirements.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Ninth Circuit for removal of the Commerce-based injunction direction and prompt consideration of any genuinely live, preserved independent claims and interim-relief requests. Federal officials may enforce the otherwise applicable CSA without this Commerce exemption, subject to the governing statutory provisions and any independently valid judicial relief.
