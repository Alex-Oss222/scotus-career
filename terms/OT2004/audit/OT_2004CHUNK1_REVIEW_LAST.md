## Decision findings

**Brosseau.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer reverse and remand on individual qualified immunity. Those seven adopt Kennedy's entire opinion. Stevens and Stone-Zsela dissent separately, with no cross-join. Stevens applies the existing defense and treats Garner as sufficient notice on the adverse account. Stone-Zsela proposes replacing the objective legal-notice defense with actual reasonable reliance on directly authorizing binding precedent or enacted authority. His preponderance burden, actual-reliance requirement, source-specific treatment of policies and instructions, legal-silence exclusion, individual-capacity scope and bounded return all remain intact.

The majority retains the frozen plaintiff-favorable assumptions: a stationary Jeep, clear escape route, unobstructed windshield, no identified person in the path and no supported immediate weapon threat. It distinguishes prospective danger from a found collision threat. The retained Harlow–Anderson defense, the absence of mandatory Saucier sequencing, Hope's obvious-application principle, and the February 21, 1999 notice cutoff are correctly separated. The holding answers both the existing-law dissent and the Chief's duplication objection without adopting either dissent. It resolves the reviewed personal damages claim by immunity; force merits remain outside the limited grant and the other branches keep their existing status. Routine direct-review transition language remains in the approved internal core and is properly omitted from the published dissent under section 4.

**Devenpeck.** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer reverse and remand. Stone-Zsela would vacate the closely-related-offense ruling and remand. All nine adopt the substantive objective rule, complete custody qualification and remand grounds. The Chief does not join the formal reversal label. The public judgment and topology preserve that distinction without attributing affirmance to him, converting vacatur to reversal, or creating a separate rationale he did not approve.

The alternative offense must be real and supported by contemporaneously known facts satisfying its elements and ordinary probable cause. Initial authority remains distinct from the restraint imposed. The Ricci/Atwater actual-enforcement requirement, its nonexclusive possible functions, and its rejection of automatic or universal custody alternatives are preserved at their existing scope. The unresolved impersonation and obstruction applications, necessary factual work, any independently presented detention-scope issue, and conduct-date personal immunity remain for adjudication. The officers' request for present judgment on their defenses is denied. The Chief's conditional instruction to adjudicate responsibility, immunity and relief if no lawful ground supports the actual seizure is carried into the return.

**Florida v. Nixon.** All nine reverse and remand and join Breyer's opinion in full. The rule requires adequate discussion, an unresponsive defendant, continued meaningful adversarial representation and an actual trial. It rejects automatic deficiency and presumed prejudice from absence of express consent alone while retaining separate ordinary performance and reasonable-probability-prejudice burdens. Trial-era investigation, consultation and the integrated guilt-and-penalty strategy remain material; the strength of the prosecution's case is not conclusive professional justification.

Bell, Cronic, Strickland and Williams keep their distinct functions. Boykin and Brookhart distinguish an actual plea or a materially abbreviated proceeding imposed contrary to the defendant's trial choice. The express-objection question is outside the decided question. The ordinary preserved allegations, including unexamined evidentiary inconsistencies, remain for supported adjudication. A properly supported dispositive prong may resolve a claim; relief follows the proved defect and applicable state postconviction remedy. Section 1257 review is not confused with section 2254's federal-habeas gate. No blanket finding of effective representation or automatic new corrective proceeding is added.

**Kowalski.** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer reverse on the attorneys' third-party claim and join the Chief's complete opinion. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg oppose that disposition and join Ginsburg's dissent. The majority assumes the pleaded personal stake and decides the independent relationship defect. It does not describe the attorneys as lacking an economic-injury allegation. The recurring strict-rotation practice, intended continued participation and alleged reduced paid appointments remain allegations, distinct from adjudicated loss and distinct from a concrete client relationship.

The controlling rule is confined to unidentified future clients without the required concrete connection. It preserves contextual existing-client and prevented-representation cases, seriously considers the practical obstacle, and explains why that obstacle alone does not supply the missing relationship. The counsel merits and the separate individual-plaintiff abstention disposition remain distinct. The mandate dismisses the deficient attorney claim, removes dependent relief, and requires identification of any independent surviving basis before changing other relief. The dissent gives its substantive prospective-relationship and access grounds; it neither demands new injury allegations nor orders an evidentiary return merely to repair an earlier source omission. The ancillary Douglas/Ross discussion is preserved internally and creates no unnecessary separate writing or unadopted Court holding.
