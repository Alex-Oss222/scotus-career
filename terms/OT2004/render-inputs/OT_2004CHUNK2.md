## Event

Educational Credit Management Corp. v. Polleys; lower-court No. 02-8059, 356 F.3d 1302. Merits decision, December 13, 2004. On full merits submission, reviewing the Tenth Circuit's February 4, 2004 affirmance of the discharge judgment. The question is whether §523(a)(8) requires certainty of hopelessness or permits discharge under a realistic assessment of present and foreseeable repayment ability and good-faith efforts, and whether the supported findings satisfy that standard.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Tenth Circuit is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The adjudicated ECMC student-loan obligation remains discharged. The existing findings satisfy the governing rule and support affirmance.

## Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

## Holdings

### A realistic statutory undue-hardship inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** A debtor establishes undue hardship under the operative 11 U.S.C. §523(a)(8) by proving, more likely than not, that repayment would prevent a minimal standard of living for the debtor and dependents, that specific circumstances make that inability likely to persist for a significant portion of the repayment period, and that the claimed hardship accords with reasonable good-faith efforts to earn income, keep expenses reasonable and meet the obligation when able. Courts may not demand certainty of hopelessness, permanent disability or a payment the debtor lacked resources to make; mere inconvenience, deliberately contrived inability and unsupported pessimism do not establish the exception.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined in full by the other eight Justices identified in Opinion Topology. All nine adopt the complete rule, burden, review standards and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory exception requires a realistic assessment of repayment ability. Lamie requires enforcement of Congress's enacted conditions: fresh-start policy cannot erase undue hardship, and an added impossibility requirement cannot erase the exception. Hood preserves the debtor's hardship burden. Grogan supplies the ordinary preponderance standard. Brunner usefully organizes present resources, likely persistence and genuine effort; the Court adopts that evidence-based formulation as its own rule.

Specific circumstances must support the forecast. Ordinary evidentiary reliability applies, but the statute does not require medical disability, a universal expert-testimony prerequisite or a medical prediction spanning every year of the loan. Courts review factual findings for clear error and statutory meaning and the findings' legal sufficiency independently.

Polleys stipulated that no medical or physical condition prevented her from retaining work. That stipulation concerns capacity for some employment, not sufficient earnings. Supported findings establish enduring emotional limitations, medication-related memory and communication difficulties, employment instability and insufficient income even with modest improvement. At trial, she and her dependent lived below poverty, relied on family housing and had no discretionary income to meet approximately $420 monthly on a $51,000 consolidated debt over twenty years. Those findings establish present inability and likely persistence for a significant repayment period; neither imagined recovery nor unsupported hopelessness substitutes for evidence.

ECMC relies on nonpayment and departure from better-paid work to move to Wyoming. The whole record answers that objection: the move reduced housing expenses; Polleys attempted accounting employment and self-employment, tried the CPA examination, and sought work outside her training. She consolidated the debt, used deferrals, attempted negotiation after a distinct earlier $800 demand and did not immediately seek discharge. These concrete efforts establish good faith within her limitations. Requiring an unaffordable remittance would turn inability itself into disqualifying misconduct. The supported findings satisfy all three requirements, and the discharge stands.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Lamie v. United States Trustee: applied to enforce the enacted bankruptcy condition without adding or removing an eligibility requirement.
- Tennessee Student Assistance Corp. v. Hood: applied for the debtor's obligation to establish the education-debt exception; the present case supplies its substantive hardship standard.
- Grogan v. Garner, 498 U.S. 279: applied for the ordinary civil proof standard in discharge litigation.
- Brunner v. New York State Higher Education Services Corp., 831 F.2d 395: its realistic three-part organization is adopted as a new Supreme Court rule; more restrictive certainty and permanent-disability glosses cannot govern this statutory exception.

## Precedent Treatment

Lamie is applied for fidelity to enacted bankruptcy conditions, Hood for the retained hardship burden, and Grogan for the ordinary civil proof standard. Brunner's realistic three-part organization becomes the Court's own statutory rule; the certainty-of-hopelessness and permanent-disability additions cannot govern it.

## Law After Decision

For education debts covered by the operative §523(a)(8), lower courts apply the three requirements stated above under the debtor's preponderance burden. They assess actual resources, supported prospects and genuine efforts within the debtor's abilities. Supported findings receive clear-error review; the statutory rule and the findings' legal sufficiency receive independent review. This decision supplies the substantive hardship standard that Hood left to merits adjudication.

## Event

Timken Co. v. United States; Federal Circuit Nos. 03-1098 and 03-1238, 354 F.3d 1334. Merits decision, January 11, 2005. ## Holdings

### I. The domestic statutory challenge may be heard

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3512(c) does not bar a challenge brought under the domestic antidumping statute merely because the challenger invokes international obligations as an aid to interpreting that statute. Koyo's statutory challenge is therefore properly considered on its merits.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, Part I, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. These eight Justices adopt the threshold rationale; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition of that component without joining its rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a domestic statutory claim and an independent claim to enforce an international agreement are distinct. Koyo invokes the review Congress provided for Commerce's determination and argues that the domestic margin and fair-comparison provisions require offsets. Its reliance on international obligations is interpretive support for that claim. Section 3512(c)'s restriction on private agreement-based claims does not transform such a domestic challenge into a prohibited cause of action. The Government and Timken's threshold objection would prevent consideration of an argument within an otherwise available statutory appeal. The statute does not require that result.

### II. The identified zeroing method is permitted within the actual delegation

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the operative 19 U.S.C. §§1675(a), 1677(35) and 1677b(a), Commerce may, in an authorized administrative review, treat transactions whose export price or constructed export price exceeds normal value as zero when aggregating positive dumping margins, provided it observes the prescribed comparison adjustments and gives a reasoned explanation. Zeroing is permitted rather than compelled; a challenger must establish a statutory prohibition or a defect under the governing review standard, and neither the fair-comparison argument nor the cited international report establishes the requested offset requirement here.

**Authority:** Part II and the affirmance remedy are joined by all nine Justices. This unanimous direct agreement supplies controlling force for the complete merits rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the final review determination exercises Congress's actual assignment to Commerce to fix duty liability under §1675(a). Mead requires that assignment and its exercise; Haggar requires courts first to enforce statutory boundaries. This duty-setting mechanism satisfies those requirements. Expertise or longstanding practice alone would not suffice.

Section 1677(35) defines individual dumping margins through normal value's excess over export price and then aggregates margins. Positive excesses fit that text, but a signed mathematical difference also fits it. The Government and Timken therefore cannot turn statutory permission into a command to zero. Barnhart v. Thomas preserves that distinction. Within the remaining choice, Chevron permits Commerce's reasonable construction. Treating above-value sales as zero prevents them from masking below-value sales and fits the prescribed entry-based duty administration.

Koyo argues that considering all transactions is necessary for the fair comparison required by §1677b(a). That command and the prescribed adjustments remain binding. Their text and relationship to aggregation do not compel negative offsets in this administrative review. The method's stated anti-masking purpose supplies a reasoned choice within that room.

Charming Betsy requires attention to interpretation consistent with international obligations. The EC–Bed Linen report's concern that all transactions be accounted for supports Koyo's alternative and receives consideration for its persuasive force. Its nonbinding status alone does not answer the argument. But the report addressed a European Community investigation, and its reasoning does not conclusively establish the asserted obligation for this distinct administrative review. Koyo's claimed functional equivalence does not eliminate that difference in the governing proceedings or overcome the domestic definition and entry-based administration. The report consequently does not make Commerce's permissible choice unlawful. The reviewed method survives the legal objection.

**Limit of the decision:** Timken's separate entered-value versus sales-value adverse-facts cross-appeal is outside the grant; its existing disposition remains intact.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Mead Corp.: applied through the actual statutory assignment and final duty-setting determination; the classification-letter holding retains its distinct scope.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: applied for enforcement of statutory boundaries before reasonable-choice review.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.: applied to the reasonable aggregation choice within that actual delegation.
- Barnhart v. Thomas: applied to distinguish a permissible implementation from the only permissible statutory meaning.
- Murray v. Charming Betsy: applied as an interpretive aid after considering the asserted international obligation and the report's persuasive reasoning; it supplies no contrary result on these premises.

## Precedent Treatment

Mead and Haggar govern the actual-delegation threshold and enforcement of statutory limits. Chevron governs the permissible choice remaining within that authority. Barnhart v. Thomas preserves the distinction between permission and exclusive meaning. Charming Betsy informs construction through the asserted international obligations and the report's persuasive reasoning. These precedents retain their existing force.

## Law After Decision

Domestic statutory review remains available when international obligations support an interpretive argument. Under the operative provisions, the identified zeroing method is a lawful administrative-review choice if Commerce observes statutory comparison requirements and explains its choice. Courts enforce those conditions under the governing review standard. The statutory permission does not compel zeroing.

## Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS and BREYER, JJ., joined.

## Holdings

### Section 1956(h) requires the unlawful agreement, without an additional overt act

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1956(h) independently defines conspiracy to commit an offense specified in §1956 or §1957 and requires neither allegation nor proof of an additional overt act. The Government must prove the actual unlawful agreement, the defendant's knowing participation and the required unlawful object beyond a reasonable doubt; association alone is insufficient, and the requested overt-act instruction was properly refused in both cases.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined in full by the other eight Justices identified in Opinion Topology. All nine adopt the complete statute-specific construction and both docket applications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress made the agreement itself the conspiracy offense. Section 1956(h) directly identifies the persons who conspire and the specified laundering offenses that are their object. It contains no additional-act requirement and no cross-reference incorporating §371. The latter statute expressly requires an act in furtherance of its conspiracy. That enacted difference must be given effect.

Shabani supplies the close analogy: its construction of §846 preserves proof of the knowing unlawful agreement without adding an overt act. The Court independently reaches the same conclusion for §1956(h)'s materially equivalent agreement-and-penalty language. Nash supplies the traditional meaning under which the agreement completes conspiracy, and Singer explains the significance of Congress's omission of a further-act condition.

Petitioners contend that §1956(h) merely increases punishment for a conspiracy otherwise defined by §371. A sentence can define the prohibited conduct and prescribe its punishment together. The same-penalties language therefore does not supply the missing incorporation. The penalty-oriented title and placement apart from the substantive laundering offenses do not negate the operative agreement offense. Legislative silence about eliminating an act requirement cannot insert one into that text.

The later venue provision identifies geographic connections for prosecution. Its reference to an act in furtherance does not itself make such an act an element of guilt under the earlier offense. That distinction answers petitioners' attempt to turn a venue provision into an element of guilt. Ratzlaf preserves lenity when ordinary construction leaves genuine ambiguity; the text, structure and settled agreement meaning resolve the present question. The refusal to add an overt-act instruction thus supplies no ground for reversal or a new trial in either docket.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Shabani: its text-based reasoning is extended from §846 to the independently construed §1956(h); its original holding remains statute-specific.
- Nash v. United States, 229 U.S. 373: applied for the traditional agreement-based understanding of conspiracy.
- Singer v. United States, 323 U.S. 338: applied for the significance of omitting an additional-act condition from the enacted conspiracy provision.
- Ratzlaf v. United States: applied for the residual-ambiguity condition on lenity; its distinct structuring willfulness rule retains its own scope.

## Precedent Treatment

Shabani's reasoning is extended to §1956(h) through independent construction of that statute. Nash and Singer supply the traditional agreement meaning and the significance of omitting an additional-act condition. Ratzlaf's residual lenity principle is applied; its structuring willfulness holding retains its distinct scope.

## Law After Decision

Section 1956(h) charges and jury instructions require proof beyond a reasonable doubt of the actual unlawful agreement, knowing participation and required unlawful object, without allegation or proof of a further overt act. Statutes expressly requiring such an act, including §371, retain that requirement. The rule governs the offense's recurring charging and instruction question.

## Event

Clark v. Martinez, No. 03-878, consolidated with Benitez v. Rozos, No. 03-7434. Argued October 13, 2004; decided January 12, 2005. On writs of certiorari to the Ninth and Eleventh Circuits. The question is whether §1231(a)(6)'s removal-related duration limit also governs inadmissible persons who have never been admitted, and what relief the two custody records require. Both men arrived from Cuba in 1980, were paroled without admission, and later became subject to final removal orders. The district courts accepted that removal to Cuba was not reasonably foreseeable. Martinez obtained release protection; Benitez's habeas petition was denied. Benitez's temporary parole during this review leaves the challenged authority to resume custody in dispute.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Court announces its judgments and the opinion construing the common removal-detention provision.

## Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed a dissenting opinion.

## Holdings

### 1. The common removal-detention provision has one temporal meaning

**Controlling proposition:** Section 1231(a)(6) permits detention of every category it covers, including inadmissible persons who have never been admitted, only for the period reasonably necessary to accomplish removal. When removal lacks a significant likelihood within the reasonably foreseeable future and no independent lawful custody ground applies, the person is entitled to enforceable release under lawful supervision; release does not grant admission.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies eight direct votes for this holding and Holding 2.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the same statutory words authorize the same kind of detention for each covered category. Zadvydas v. Davis construed those words to link custody to removal and expressly reserved their application to previously unadmitted persons. Today's holding resolves that reservation. Different constitutional positions at entry cannot turn one undifferentiated detention grant into two different statutory powers. Constitutional avoidance selects a permissible meaning of a statute; it does not allow its meaning to vary with the constitutional claim of each person detained. The contrary reading mistakes the reason for choosing the statutory construction for a restriction on what the chosen words mean. Mezei concerned a different statutory and procedural setting and does not construe this provision. Parole regulates release without supplying perpetual detention authority. The accepted findings in both cases establish that removal to Cuba is not reasonably foreseeable, and the Government has supplied no sufficient rebuttal. Martinez's release protection therefore stands, and Benitez is entitled to equivalent enforceable protection despite his temporary parole.

### 2. The existing presumption and burdens govern the previously reserved category

**Controlling proposition:** Six months, counted within the removal process rather than added after the ordinary ninety-day removal period, is a presumptively reasonable detention interval. After it, the detainee must give good reason to believe that removal lacks a significant likelihood within the reasonably foreseeable future, and the Government must sufficiently rebut that showing; the interval alone does not require release, and supported evidence that a longer period is reasonably necessary, including relevant evidence about an inadmissible class, may justify continued detention.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Zadvydas's workable removal-prospect inquiry to the statutory category now decided. As detention continues, the reasonably foreseeable horizon shortens. Actual removal efforts, foreign responses, negotiations, documents, receiving arrangements and material detainee conduct bear on that inquiry; permanent impossibility need not be proved. Neither dangerousness alone nor a foreign government's refusal substitutes for the statutory removal purpose. Section 1231(a)(1)(C) retains its separate extension for failure or refusal to make a timely good-faith application for necessary travel documents, or conspiracy or action preventing removal; foreign refusal alone establishes none of those grounds. Section 1231(a)(3) permits periodic identification, necessary medical or psychiatric examination at government expense, sworn information and reasonable written conduct restrictions. Renewed custody requires changed removal facts or a separately applicable lawful source and its required process. Section 1226a supplies a distinct statutory source with its own required predicates and process, not a dangerousness exception to the removal-purpose limit. Temporary parole cannot displace effective judicial protection from custody forbidden by the removal statute. Both records therefore require prompt supervised release protection under the existing, rebuttable rule.

## Precedent Treatment

- Zadvydas v. Davis: extended to the previously reserved inadmissible category under the same statutory grant; its removal-purpose limit, rebuttable six-month presumption and supervised-release implementation retain their force.
- Shaughnessy v. United States ex rel. Mezei: distinguished because its statutory and procedural setting does not determine the meaning of §1231(a)(6); the present statutory holding does not equate constitutional entry statuses.

## Law After Decision

Section 1231(a)(6) now has the same removal-related temporal scope for all its covered categories. The qualified foreseeable-removal inquiry governs the inadmissible category as well as previously admitted persons. Effective judicial release protection, lawful supervision and independently supported renewed custody remain distinct legal operations.

## Separate Writings

Thomas dissents. He would reconsider Zadvydas because the statute authorizes detention beyond the removal period without the implied general duration limit. Alternatively, he would preserve that decision's admitted-person scope and express reservation: the constitutional concerns supporting its construction do not apply identically to persons never admitted. He rejects using the earlier avoidance construction to resolve a materially different constitutional setting. He would reverse Martinez's statutory release entitlement and affirm Benitez's denial of this general duration claim, with implementation under the governing custody procedures rather than an automatic rearrest command.

## Procedure After Action

Both matters return for implementation of enforceable supervised-release protection. The courts must give effect to the unrebutted removal-prospect findings and retain the lawful supervision authorized by the statute. A later custody action must have an actual new lawful basis and available review; repeated short parole periods cannot defeat the adjudicated duration limit.

The accepted findings establish the absence of reasonably foreseeable Cuban removal; neither record establishes an independent security-detention predicate.

## Event

Jama v. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, No. 03-674. Argued October 12, 2004; decided January 12, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit, 329 F.3d 630. Jama was born in Somalia, entered as a refugee, and later lost that status following a criminal conviction. He designated no destination; the agency selected Somalia under 8 U.S.C. §1231(b)(2)(E)(iv). The question is whether that birthplace clause requires advance acceptance by the receiving government. The district court granted acceptance-based habeas relief, and the Court of Appeals reversed.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the rejection of the claimed advance-acceptance requirement.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The acceptance-based habeas ground fails. Officials may proceed under the birthplace clause subject to the actual selection sequence, the existing removal order and any operative stay or independently applicable protection. Continued detention remains subject to its own statutory authority and foreseeable-removal limits.

## Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA and KENNEDY, JJ., joined. SOUTER, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which STEVENS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

## Holdings

### Birthplace selection does not require advance acceptance

**Controlling proposition:** Section 1231(b)(2)(E)(iv) permits selection of the country of a person's birth without an additional advance-acceptance condition when the preceding statutory steps and that clause's own predicates are satisfied. Clause (vii)'s express acceptance requirement governs its residual destination, not clause (iv); the agency remains bound by independently applicable removal protections and custody limits.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy, supplies five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress attached different conditions to different destination choices. Subparagraph (A) ordinarily requires the person's designated country, subject to the territorial limitation in (B) and the grounds in (C). The territorial limitation permits designation of contiguous territory, an adjacent island, or an island adjacent to contiguous territory only if the person is its native, citizen, subject or national, or has resided there. Under (C), officials may disregard a designation for failure to designate promptly, failure to answer finally within thirty days of inquiry, unwillingness to accept, or prejudice to the United States. Under (D), removal to the country of nationality is required unless its government fails to answer within thirty days or another period reasonably allowed, or is unwilling to accept. Subparagraph (E) then supplies connected-country destinations, including birthplace. Only its residual clause (vii), available when removal to every preceding listed country is impracticable, inadvisable or impossible, expressly requires acceptance.

Barnhart v. Thomas supports reading a terminal qualification with its immediate antecedent when context permits. Here the differing statutory conditions reinforce that grammar. The dissent's concern that birthplace selection can follow an unsuccessful nationality stage does not justify making Congress's distinct routes identical. In particular, (C) permits disregard rather than invariably commanding it; the statute does not establish a universal acceptance rule. Jama's birth in Somalia supplies the connection required by clause (iv). Somalia's lack of an accepting government therefore does not establish his asserted statutory bar. St. Cyr preserves legal review of that question, while Zadvydas keeps continuing detention tied to actual foreseeable removal. Whether (D)'s exceptions leave residual discretion is outside the birthplace question presented and unnecessary to its resolution. Independent withholding, torture-protection and actual-removal-feasibility claims require their own records.

## Precedent Treatment

- Barnhart v. Thomas: applied as a context-sensitive rule for a terminal qualification; the whole statutory structure confirms the local reading here.
- INS v. St. Cyr: its legal-review protection remains effective and permits resolution of this statutory issue without supplying substantive destination immunity.
- Zadvydas v. Davis: its qualified foreseeable-removal inquiry remains distinct from authority to select a destination; lawful selection does not establish indefinite detention power.

## Law After Decision

The birthplace route in §1231(b)(2)(E)(iv) carries no additional advance-acceptance requirement. Each preceding destination stage and each express acceptance condition keeps its own operation. The construction resolves country-selection authority while preserving the independent rules governing actual removal and custody.

## Separate Writings

Souter, joined by Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer, dissents. Reading the destination provisions together, they understand the accepting-country description in (E)(vii) as identifying a shared feature of the connected-country routes. Otherwise officials could use (E) to send a person to the same nationality country whose lack of acceptance defeats (D), undermining the integrated scheme. They acknowledge that (C) permits disregard of the person's designation and does not command it whenever a listed condition occurs; their position is an acceptance requirement for the connected-country route presented here, not an absolute rule for every statutory step. United States National Bank of Oregon supports giving the enactment's whole structure priority over an isolated grammatical inference. They would reverse and preserve the acceptance-based bar to removal to Somalia under the asserted birthplace route.

## Procedure After Action

The Eighth Circuit judgment remains effective, and the acceptance-based bar is removed. Implementation must satisfy the actual destination sequence and any operative protection or stay. Independently presented protection and detention claims proceed through their applicable channels and on their own records.

## Holdings

### I. The personal arms right governs this county-property challenge

**Controlling proposition:** Individuals may invoke the Second Amendment, as incorporated through the Fourteenth Amendment, against a county restriction burdening protected possession or acquisition necessary to protected keeping. Public ownership does not extinguish the right; the lower court must assess the claimants' actual injuries, covered conduct, practical burdens, required justification and remaining preliminary-relief requirements under the governing law.

**Remedial rule:** If the lower court establishes an unlawful operation of the county policy and the requirements for the relief under consideration are satisfied, relief must precisely correct that adjudicated unlawful operation and protect the persons governed by that operation unless the county establishes that narrower relief fully cures the proved violation. The decree must preserve independently lawful safety conditions; the county's narrower-relief showing concerns remedial scope, while the claimants retain their existing burdens concerning standing, liability and preliminary-relief prerequisites.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine adopt the existing-law application, conditional remedial rule and bounded remand. Part II has the separate five-member framework coalition identified below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a collective-right or nonincorporation premise cannot defeat the claim. Love v. Pepersack established personal protection, application against the States and acquisition necessary to protected keeping. United States v. Hale distinguishes that coverage from the validity of particular regulations. The county's ownership informs the actual setting and justification; it does not eliminate the constitutional inquiry. The organizers must identify protected conduct and their own concrete injury. The court must examine the challenged fairgrounds applications, meaningful acquisition routes, possession rules, actual exemptions and safety evidence. The lower court stopped at an erroneous merits premise and did not perform the required burden analysis or reach the hardship balance. The record therefore supports vacatur of the arms component and prompt application of existing law. It does not establish an entitlement to the immediate preliminary injunction the organizers seek. An independently unsatisfied indispensable prerequisite may support denial with reasons; the rejected categorical rights premise may not. Anderson v. Spear requires effective, precise relief against adjudicated unlawful policy operation. Anderson followed established merits violations; this preliminary record still requires the predicate findings and applicable equitable determinations. Its remedial rule therefore applies conditionally here, preserving lawful safety conditions and limiting protection to the unlawful operation actually proved.

### II. Wright's coverage and practical-burden framework supplies the standard

**Controlling proposition:** The claimant must first identify protected keeping, bearing, acquisition, necessary ammunition or transportation through text, history and precedent; the county must then justify the regulation at the level required by its actual burden. A severe core burden requires a compelling interest, narrow tailoring and no comparably effective materially less restrictive alternative; a substantial burden requires an important objective, a substantial relationship and no unnecessary or effective elimination of protected activity; an incidental, nonobstructive regulation receives reasonableness review while remaining subject to prohibitions against arbitrariness, discrimination and cumulative destruction of the right.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg, supplies five direct rationale votes reaffirming the complete framework. Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer accept its binding application on remand without joining this renewed doctrinal endorsement.

**Controlling explanation:** Wright requires the practical operation of a rule to determine the necessary justification. Calling a restriction a safety measure or an administrative condition cannot reduce review when it materially obstructs protected conduct. Its independent categorical rule also forbids abolition of meaningful possession of ordinary functional defensive arms in the home; that meaningful core does not immunize every weapon, person or use. The fairgrounds activity cannot simply be equated with that home setting. Emerson shows how a supported safety record and a suitably confined restriction can satisfy the required justification; a safety assertion by itself does not do that work. The county's reliance on other channels must therefore be tested against the actual acquisition burden, while the organizers' commercial interest in a familiar venue does not itself prove a severe core deprivation. The missing practical-burden, alternative-channel and fit findings are matters for application on remand, not uncertainty about the governing standard.

### III. The categorical facial speech attack does not establish preliminary-relief entitlement

**Controlling proposition:** Ordinary firearm possession and exchange do not become protected speech in every application because a show also includes advocacy, and isolated expressive displays do not establish a basis to invalidate the entire possession ordinance. A demonstrably communicative display receives the protection appropriate to its actual context; the claimant must establish that expressive application and the requested breadth of relief, and the county must satisfy the scrutiny applicable to a proved burden, with viewpoint discrimination separately prohibited.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices and supplies nine votes for the facial result and these operative distinctions.

**Controlling explanation:** Hurley protects genuine expression without converting ordinary goods into speech. Texas v. Johnson and Spence require attention to what conduct communicates in context. Some message-bearing rifles may communicate, but those examples do not establish that all possession or exchange at a gun show is expression or that the entire ordinance must fall. O'Brien permits justified incidental regulation of expressive conduct. Under City of Erie, however, that treatment is unavailable when a law's justification and operation principally target communication; general wording alone does not establish neutrality. The record does not establish the expressive breadth or actual targeting needed for the requested facial relief. The facial denial therefore stands, while a properly preserved, concrete expressive-display claim remains subject to its own record and applicable conduct or forum rules. The time, place and manner theory was not pressed on appeal and is outside the question decided; application-specific display and exemption issues require facts absent from this facial preliminary record.

## Holdings

### I. Facts increasing a binding punishment boundary require element protections

**Controlling proposition:** A fact legally necessary to increase a binding sentencing ceiling or floor must receive applicable grand-jury charging protection and be proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt, validly admitted as an element, or determined beyond a reasonable doubt after a lawful jury waiver. This rule governs present-offense and necessary recidivist facts, including those that increase a binding Federal Sentencing Guidelines range; judicial selection within punishment independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions remains permissible.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the established punishment-authorization inquiry. Apprendi protects facts that authorize greater punishment. Angle already applies that rule to binding federal Guidelines ceilings and floors, Harris confirms the mandatory-floor component, Gatewood rejects a categorical recidivism exception, and Blakely identifies the range authorized by a verdict or valid plea admission. A larger outer statutory maximum does not make an enforceable inner range discretionary. Mistretta sustains the Commission's institutional arrangement, not an exemption from independent constitutional guarantees; Witte and Watts preserve ordinary assessment only within otherwise authorized punishment. The argument that Commission rules permit ordinary judicial enhancement therefore conflicts with the existing rule rather than distinguishing it. Nor does the dissent's concern about judicial administration justify withdrawing safeguards from facts that legally compel greater punishment. An unchallenged assertion in a sentencing report is not by itself a valid element admission. A jury waiver retains the proof burden and the applicable charge boundary. Booker's additional judicial findings raised a 210-to-262-month calculation to 360 months to life and produced a 360-month sentence. Those findings cannot authorize that increase. Fanfan properly refused to use the comparable unsupported findings. The stated ranges describe the sentencing records; other disputed inputs must satisfy their own applicable requirements.

### II. Constitutional applications preserve the binding statutory system

**Controlling proposition:** The Federal Sentencing Guidelines remain binding to the extent their ranges rest on constitutionally established facts; an unsupported enhancement must be withheld rather than used to replace Congress's mandatory system with an advisory one. Section 3553(b)(1), §3742(e) and the enacted review and remand provisions remain effective in their lawful applications, with judicial discretion exercised within the independently authorized range and lawful statutory departures.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter and Thomas, supplies five direct votes for this holding and the mandate in Holding III. Ginsburg joins Part I only.

**Controlling explanation:** Alaska Airlines v. Brock asks whether the enacted remainder can function consistently with Congress's design. Severability can remove unconstitutional applications as well as invalid provisions; the necessary question is what cannot lawfully operate. Here a binding system can operate on facts established with the required protections. Section 3553(b)(1) commands the applicable kind and range of sentence unless an aggravating or mitigating circumstance of a kind or degree not adequately considered by the Commission warrants a different sentence. Adequate consideration is assessed from the Guidelines, policy statements and official commentary. Those departure conditions remain subject to the constitutional authorization rule.

The Government and the remedial dissent emphasize real-conduct sentencing, information available to judges, charging-driven disparities and unequal effects of upward and downward adjustments. Those concerns identify consequences of constitutional safeguards, not an inability of the enacted mandatory command to govern lawful applications. The Court cannot preserve every feature that conflicts with those safeguards, but it can preserve Congress's binding directions and actual appellate-review provisions. Removing both mandatory force and the review structure would substitute a materially different sentencing policy. Angle demonstrated the legal availability of protected factfinding within binding ranges while expressly leaving this system-level remedy open; today's holding answers that reservation. The presumption favoring lawful surviving applications is not overcome by the asserted administrative inconvenience.

### III. The two records require different relief under the retained framework

**Controlling proposition:** Booker's appellate sentence vacatur is affirmed, and his case is remanded for a replacement sentence within a lawfully established binding range, subject to correction of independently established calculation errors. Fanfan's refusal to enhance and resulting 78-month sentence are affirmed against the asserted entitlement to use the excluded findings; any independently preserved calculation issue remains for ordinary disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Booker received the unsupported increased punishment; Fanfan did not. Preserving the binding statute therefore requires correcting Booker's sentence while sustaining Fanfan's refusal to impose the enhancement. Blakely and Gatewood distinguish sentence relief from authority to institute new aggravated proceedings. This mandate supplies no automatic enhancement jury or new charging authority: further proceedings require an independent procedural source and compliance with charging, proof, waiver and finality constraints. Booker's convictions remain intact. Griffith governs application to nonfinal cases on direct review. For a forfeited claim, Olano separately requires error, plainness, an ordinary showing of effect on substantial rights, and a discretionary finding that the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. An assumed identical sentence cannot replace those inquiries or make an unauthorized binding range lawful. Existing deadlines, retroactivity rules and collateral-review gateways govern final judgments separately.

## Holdings

### I. Federal review turns on the state court's legal work, not a case citation

**Holding and operative rule:** A state court's failure to cite its settled narrowing precedent does not itself establish that it applied an unconstitutionally vague capital aggravator. Federal review must examine the authoritative construction and the state court's actual reasoning and result under the applicable review law, without inventing an adjudication or corrective step the record does not establish.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's per curiam opinion, supported by all nine participating Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** *Godfrey v. Georgia*, 446 U.S. 420, requires meaningful guidance in applying a heinousness aggravator; *Proffitt v. Florida*, 428 U.S. 242, recognizes the work performed by an authoritative narrowing construction. The constitutional inquiry concerns the rule actually applied. *Sochor v. Florida* permits a sufficiently specific judicial narrowing application without treating citation form as the constitutional act. *Early v. Packer* and *Woodford v. Visciotti* likewise require substantive review of the complete state adjudication. An omitted name cannot replace that inquiry. Conversely, presumed familiarity with state law cannot establish an otherwise unperformed application or correction. The Sixth Circuit's missing-citation inference therefore cannot sustain its sentence-relief judgment.

### II. Tennessee actually applied the narrowed category; exhaustion uncertainty does not prevent rejecting that theory

**Holding and operative rule:** Tennessee's 1984 opinion actually applied its authoritative narrowed heinousness category to Cone's killings, and the asserted failure of judicial narrowing is conclusively rejected. Under 28 U.S.C. §2254(b)(2), a court may deny a habeas claim on a sufficient merits ground despite nonexhaustion; that permission does not require denial, establish a state merits adjudication or dispose of a distinct constitutional theory.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, supported by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela does not join this conclusive application or the §2254(b)(2) rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** *State v. Dicks* confines the category to a conscienceless or pitiless crime unnecessarily torturous to the victim. *State v. Pritchett* requires predeath torture or sufficiently grave depravity and excludes instantaneous death from the torture branch. *State v. Melson* applies that construction through defensive injuries, awareness, fear, resistance and repeated crushing blows. The Tennessee Supreme Court identified the corresponding circumstances here: defensive wounds and resistance, multiple crushing blows, noninstantaneous deaths and terror from the sequence of the two killings. That explanation applies the narrowed category rather than merely repeating the statutory adjectives. The parties' references to the state authorities corroborate the setting; the court's actual reasoning supplies the application.

The claim that the reviewing court failed to narrow the category therefore fails on its substance, without reliance on a disputed presumption that every federal theory was adjudicated. Section 2254(b)(2) permits that sufficient denial despite a dispute about exhaustion. The provision removes the need to settle exhaustion solely to reject this unsuccessful theory; it does not supply the answer to the distinct jury-guidance issue addressed in Part III.

### III. The distinct jury-guidance and weighing claim requires its own lawful disposition

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit's aggravator-based sentence-relief judgment is vacated, and the case is remanded to dispose of Cone's existing, distinct jury-guidance and weighing claim under its actual state adjudication, the applicable federal-law cutoff, §2254 and any preserved valid default. Judicial narrowing is settled; if no distinct available theory survives, relief on this ground must be denied, while an available constitutional sentencing error requires an authorized actual correction or the governing State-borne harmlessness determination.

**Authority:** Part III of the per curiam opinion, supported by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six adopt the complete confined mandate and its explanation. Stone-Zsela supports vacatur and remand on the broader inquiry stated in his separate opinion; Scalia and Thomas would finally reject the reviewed aggravator claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The jury found and weighed the heinousness aggravator. Its reported definitions include extreme wickedness, outrageous vileness and cruelty expressed through high pain, indifference, enjoyment of suffering or pitilessness. The state court's later finding that the killings fit its narrower construction does not itself establish that the jury used that construction or that an unconstitutional broader influence, if proved, was corrected. Different formulations alone also do not prove error.

Lewis* requires an actual constitutional sentencing determination or adequate harmlessness when a weighing error is established. *Lambrix v. Singletary* preserves the legal significance of a consequential jury contribution despite independent judicial review. Those distinctions answer the argument that valid judicial narrowing necessarily ends this already asserted jury claim.

Relief still requires the correct statutory and temporal predicates. *Bell v. Cone*, 535 U.S. 685, applies amended §2254 to this 1997 application and preserves remaining sentence claims. The 1984 direct adjudication must be assessed against Supreme Court holdings then established; *Godfrey* and *Proffitt* qualify, while later holdings do not become 1984 law merely by explaining it. The 1993 previously-determined description and 1995 affirmance do not themselves establish a new merits date. *Stringer*'s 1985-finality and *Lambrix*'s 1986-finality rulings do not settle that earlier cutoff. *Mitchell v. Esparza* permits final denial when the actual claim and dated law supply it; here judicial category-fit alone leaves a distinct asserted operation unresolved.

An independently sufficient denial may be entered without unnecessary exhaustion litigation or factual proceedings. If an available nonstructural sentencing violation is established, *Brecht v. Abrahamson* places harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt on the State when that effect inquiry governs. An authorized actual cure is a separate route. Surviving-factor counts, proportionality or the state harmlessness ruling on the different risk-to-others factor cannot automatically correct a proved heinousness error. The convictions remain intact; other properly pending sentence claims retain the 2002 mandate's protection.

## Event

Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. Banks, No. 03-892, and Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. Banaitis, No. 03-907; 543 U.S. 426. Merits decision, January 24, 2005. Argued November 1, 2004. ## Holdings

### I. A contingent fee remains part of the client's otherwise taxable recovery

**Holding and operative rule:** When a client retains ownership and control of an income-producing legal claim and counsel acts as the client's agent, the client's gross income under 26 U.S.C. §61 includes the contractual contingent fee paid from an otherwise taxable recovery, whether the payment passes through the client or goes directly to counsel and whether a state-law lien secures it. A taxpayer seeking exclusion must identify an applicable statutory exclusion or a genuinely different ownership arrangement; uncertainty about the recovery's value and the lawyer's labor do not alone transfer the claim.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined in full by every other participating Justice. The common coalition identified in Opinion Topology adopts the rule, both docket-specific applications and the explanation below.

**Controlling explanation:** The claim arising from the client's legal injury produces the recovery. The client retains ultimate control, including whether to settle, while counsel acts for the client. *Lucas v. Earl*, 281 U.S. 111, prevents advance allocation of earnings from changing their attribution; *Helvering v. Horst*, 311 U.S. 112, attributes diverted proceeds to the person who retains the income-producing source and obtains the benefit of its disposition. Payment to counsel satisfies the client's contractual obligation.

The taxpayers emphasize that recovery was uncertain and that counsel supplied the skill, work and risk needed to obtain it. Those features explain the compensation arrangement. They do not change ownership of the claim or turn this agency into a partnership. The attribution rule does not depend on a known recovery amount when the contract is signed or on proof that a particular taxpayer sought to avoid tax.

The same inquiry resolves Banaitis's Oregon argument. State law gives counsel a superior lien securing compensation, but the accepted rights leave prosecution and ultimate settlement control with the client. Priority in payment therefore does not transfer the income-producing claim. Banks's contractual fee and Banaitis's directly paid, lien-protected fee are both included.

The mismatch between inclusion and restricted deductions can impose a substantial tax burden. *Commissioner v. Schleier* and *O'Gilvie v. United States* require the actual statutory conditions for excluding particular receipts; they provide no equitable exclusion for this fee payment. Inclusion and deductibility perform separate statutory functions.

**Material limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the principal-agent relationship established in these records. The Court leaves separately awarded statutory fees unresolved because Banks's settlement produced a contractual contingent fee, without a separate statutory award or an established payment in its place. Supplementary partnership, capital-expense and reimbursed-employee-expense theories were not developed below and are not reached on that preservation ground.

### II. Each tax liability is calculated under the law for its tax year

**Holding and operative rule:** Both fee-exclusion judgments are reversed, and each docket returns for the lawful tax calculation using the full otherwise taxable recovery and deductions available under the applicable tax-year provisions. Section 703(c) of the American Jobs Creation Act of 2004 makes both payment of the fees and costs after October 22, 2004, and a judgment or settlement after that date necessary to the new deduction; these earlier transactions remain governed by their tax-year law.

**Authority:** The same whole-Court opinion and common coalition identified in Opinion Topology.

**Controlling explanation:** Gross-income attribution answers whose taxable recovery includes the fee. The deduction and alternative-minimum-tax provisions then determine their separate consequences. Congress placed the new deduction in §62 but expressly made its operation prospective through two temporal conditions. Both matters involve earlier transactions. The courts on remand must apply the old-year provisions to the actual payment and return records, giving effect to the inclusion rule in each docket.

## Precedent Treatment

- *Lucas v. Earl*, 281 U.S. 111: Applied to advance diversion of contingent proceeds while the taxpayer retains the source; uncertainty of the eventual amount does not defeat attribution.
- *Helvering v. Horst*, 311 U.S. 112: Applied to a client's retained legal claim and the benefit obtained when its proceeds satisfy the client's fee obligation.
- *Commissioner v. Schleier*, 515 U.S. 323: Preserved as requiring the actual statutory conditions for a receipt-specific exclusion; those conditions supply no fee-payment exclusion here.
- *O'Gilvie v. United States*, 519 U.S. 79: Preserved for receipt-specific causation under its applicable exclusion provision; the present attribution rule does not alter that inquiry.

## Law After Decision

The full otherwise taxable recovery is attributed to the client in ordinary contingent-fee representation that preserves client ownership and ultimate control, including arrangements with direct payment and a superior payment lien. Exclusions and deductions depend on their own statutory requirements. Each liability is calculated under its governing tax-year rules.

## Event

Illinois v. Caballes, No. 03-923; 543 U.S. 405. Merits decision, January 24, 2005. Argued November 10, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Illinois, 207 Ill. 2d 504, 802 N.E.2d 202.

A trooper stopped Caballes for speeding. During the traffic business another officer walked a narcotics dog around the car; its alert led to a trunk search and discovery of marijuana. The trial court found the duration lawful and the alert sufficiently reliable for probable cause. The Illinois Supreme Court reversed the conviction because the canine examination expanded the investigation without reasonable, articulable drug suspicion. The granted question accepts lawful traffic duration and asks whether the Fourth Amendment independently requires that suspicion for this exterior examination. Illinois seeks removal of that requirement; Caballes seeks preservation of suppression.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Illinois Supreme Court's judgment is vacated, and the case is remanded.

## Holdings

### The limited exterior canine examination during an independently lawful traffic stop

**Holding and operative rule:** An exterior examination by a properly trained narcotics dog that is actually confined to detecting contraband requires no separate drug suspicion when officers have lawful access and the examination adds no unlawful restraint or other protected intrusion. The State must establish those predicates and ordinary probable cause for any later physical search; the dog's label and the later discovery of drugs do not substitute for them.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court. The common whole-opinion coalition identified in Opinion Topology adopts the rule, its qualifications, this application and the vacatur-and-remand remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** *United States v. Place*, 462 U.S. 696, distinguishes a canine examination confined to contraband from the detention that enables it. *United States v. Jacobsen*, 466 U.S. 109, distinguishes detection of contraband alone from disclosure of lawful private information. Those distinctions govern the exterior examination here.

Authority must exist for each stage. The exterior-examination rule applies only during an independently lawful initial detention: *Whren v. United States* makes the unchallenged speeding stop presumptively reasonable, and nothing rebuts that presumption here. *Ohio v. Robinette* requires independent grounds for restraint after traffic justification ends. *Knowles v. Iowa* requires an independent basis for a physical search following a citation. The later alert must satisfy ordinary probable-cause requirements. *City of Indianapolis v. Edmond* requires antecedent authority for a seizure before a canine examination; here the traffic offense supplies that authority. *Kyllo v. United States* protects concealed domestic information acquired by a targeted examination; the limited method here is confined to contraband.

The grant accepts the lawfulness of the traffic detention's duration. The trial court separately found this alert sufficiently reliable for probable cause. The dissenters object that a dog can err and that a compulsory drug investigation changes the encounter's character. Reliability for probable cause and the information revealed by the examination are distinct questions. A possible erroneous alert does not itself expose lawful private information; the later physical search remains subject to its own justification. On the accepted exterior-examination and lawful-duration premises, an unrelated investigative aim alone supplies neither a protected privacy intrusion nor unlawful added restraint.

The Illinois Supreme Court therefore erred in requiring separate drug suspicion solely because this examination added a drug-investigation purpose to the lawful traffic encounter.

**Operative limits:** The rule requires actual informational confinement, lawful access and lawful restraint. It does not authorize delay after traffic justification ends or entry into a protected area; physical searches retain their independent constitutional predicates. Reliability rests on the trial court's finding about this alert.

## Precedent Treatment

- *United States v. Place*, 462 U.S. 696: Applied to contraband-confined detection while retaining its independent limits on detention.
- *United States v. Jacobsen*, 466 U.S. 109: Applied for the distinction between contraband-only information and lawful private contents; constitutional classification remains separate from a later physical search.
- *Whren v. United States*, 517 U.S. 806: Applied to the independently justified traffic stop, preserving separate manner, scope and duration limits.
- *Ohio v. Robinette*: Preserved for the requirement of independent authority when traffic justification ends; lawful initial detention supplies no continuing authority by itself.
- *Knowles v. Iowa*, 525 U.S. 113: Preserved for the independent justification required for a physical search following a traffic citation.
- *City of Indianapolis v. Edmond*, 531 U.S. 32: Distinguished because the present traffic offense supplies the seizure authority absent from the narcotics checkpoint; the canine-classification question reserved there is resolved here for this defined setting.
- *Kyllo v. United States*, 533 U.S. 27: Distinguished because its examination acquired concealed lawful domestic information. Its protection of that information and its separate warrant requirement remain in force.

## Law After Decision

A contraband-confined exterior canine examination during an independently lawful traffic detention requires no separate drug suspicion when the State establishes the stated access, information and restraint predicates. Existing search and seizure rules independently govern detention duration, protected intrusions and the physical search following an alert.

## Separate Writings

**Souter, dissenting:** The dog was used to investigate concealed effects and to establish grounds for opening the trunk. Souter would treat that investigative examination as a search requiring an independent lawful basis beyond the speeding detention. *Place* supplies a special privacy distinction only when the method is genuinely confined to contraband; sufficient reliability for probable cause does not establish the absolute informational exemption asserted here. *Jacobsen* concerned a test of material already lawfully obtained, whereas this examination began an investigative process directed at a closed enclosure. *Kyllo* supports scrutiny of acquisition of concealed information, and *Edmond* requires authority for the actual intrusion. Souter accepts the individual reliability finding but rejects its use to settle constitutional classification. He would affirm suppression and also joins Ginsburg's separate seizure ground.

**Ginsburg, dissenting, joined by Souter:** A traffic seizure is limited in scope as well as time. The compulsory canine investigation materially expands that seizure from traffic enforcement to a drug inquiry, even though the accepted duration remains lawful. *Robinette* preserves the limited authority conferred by the traffic encounter, *Edmond* distinguishes ordinary crime investigation from an independently justified seizure, and *Knowles* rejects treating a citation as general search authority. Ginsburg agrees that probable-cause reliability does not by itself establish an all-purpose contraband-only exemption, but her sufficient ground concerns the scope of this seizure. She would require articulable drug suspicion for the added investigation and affirm the Illinois judgment. Her agreement with the limited privacy objection is not a whole-opinion join in Souter's separate search rationale.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Illinois courts to apply the corrected federal rule to the accepted lawful-stop premises and resolve genuinely preserved independent grounds. If no independent defect remains, the absence of separate drug suspicion supplies no basis for suppression.
