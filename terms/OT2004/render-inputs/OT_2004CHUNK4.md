## Event

Dowhal v. SmithKline Beecham Consumer Healthcare; California Supreme Court No. S109306, 32 Cal. 4th 910. Merits decision, February 23, 2005. On review of the California Supreme Court after full merits briefing. The questions are whether the saved Proposition 65 warning requirement remains subject to an independently established conflict with federal drug law, whether the FDA may reject a literally accurate warning that materially misleads in context, and whether the warning demanded in this claim actually conflicts with the operative federal decision. The state supreme court sustained summary judgment against the identified nicotine-replacement-product warning claim.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The California Supreme Court's judgment is affirmed at the scope of the identified warning claim.

Judgment for defendants remains effective insofar as the requested enforcement and dependent monetary or injunctive relief require the warning that conflicts with the operative federal decision. Any genuinely different compatible duty that was pleaded and preserved retains its own adjudicative basis. The judgment concerns the identified message and covered products.

## Holdings

### Saved initiative requirements and actual conflict

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 379r(d)(2) exempts a state requirement adopted by a public initiative or referendum enacted before September 1, 1997 from that section's express displacement of nonidentical requirements. A saved requirement nevertheless yields when the party asserting preemption establishes incompatibility with a valid, specifically applicable federal command or supported regulatory method under other operative federal law.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine Justices, controls this construction.

**Controlling explanation:** Geier gives independent effect to both saving language and demonstrated conflict. The words limiting this exemption to the particular section preserve Proposition 65 against the section's uniformity rule while leaving other valid federal obligations operative. Myrick requires an actual federal source; regulatory silence supplies none. Medtronic requires comparison of the duties themselves and preserves compatible obligations. Thus, nonidentity alone cannot defeat the saved initiative, while its preservation does not compel obedience to incompatible federal and state commands. The separate product-liability saving in subsection (e) and identical-requirement enforcement provision in subsection (f) perform their own statutory work; neither substitutes for the initiative exemption applied here.

### Contextually misleading labeling

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §§321(n), 352(a) and 355, the FDA may reject drug labeling that is literally accurate but materially misleading in context, considering its representations and material omissions concerning consequences under prescribed or customary use. That authority requires an authorized labeling determination supported by its actual reasons; a bare preference for how consumers should behave is insufficient.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine Justices, controls this rule and the identification of the definitive federal action.

**Controlling explanation:** The enacted distinction between falsity and misleadingness requires attention to the message a label conveys as a whole. Here the agency considered the implications of reproductive-risk language alongside the relative dangers of smoking, replacement therapy and failed abstinence. The August 17, 2001 citizen-petition response establishes the definitive determination, distinct from earlier interim communications requiring continued approved warnings. Its reasons concern a misleading risk comparison and the uncertainty relevant to that comparison. They supply a statutory labeling ground, rather than power to suppress accurate discussion generally. The agency's allowance for another data-supported proposal preserves the lawful reconsideration process while the current decision remains effective.

### Conflict in the warning claim presented

**Holding and operative rule:** The reproductive-harm warning demanded for the covered nicotine-replacement products conflicts with the operative FDA decision because it restores the misleading comparative-risk message that decision rejected. The claim and its dependent remedies therefore cannot be enforced while that decision governs; the party asserting displacement must establish the relevant action's coverage and conflict for a materially different warning or duty.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer, supplies a five-Justice controlling application.

**Controlling explanation:** The federal determination did more than approve a minimum label. It rejected the added message in the course of requiring pregnancy advice that addressed smoking, inability to stop without assistance and the comparative risks of replacement therapy. On the present demand, repeating the rejected implication through a label, a point-of-sale notice or the asserted advertising route would frustrate the same supported method. The dissent rightly identifies the need to examine the full state obligation, which permits different forms of clear and reasonable warning. But a hypothetical reformulation does not remove the substantive conflict established for the message this claim demands. This conclusion applies the federal determination to the actual claim; it does not attribute an express prohibition of every conceivable formulation to the agency. A genuinely compatible independent duty and a supported later labeling proposal remain subject to their own requirements.

## Precedent Treatment

- Geier v. American Honda Motor Co. is applied: saving language and actual conflict remain separate inquiries, and displacement requires the actual federal choice and incompatible state obligation.
- Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick is applied: this case contains an operative federal determination, unlike an inference based on regulatory absence.
- Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr is applied: approval alone supplies no general immunity, and genuinely compatible duties retain their force.
- FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp. supplies independent examination of statutory delegation; preemption does not enlarge the agency's authority.

## Law After Decision

Qualifying initiative requirements remain protected from §379r's express uniformity rule. Courts must separately determine actual conflict with valid federal law by identifying the action, the covered product and the state duty. The current warning claim is displaced because of the supported substantive conflict, while contextual misleadingness remains bounded by the enacted labeling authority. Later supported agency reconsideration and genuinely different duties receive the same actual-coverage inquiry.

## Separate Writings

Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, Souter and Thomas, agrees that the initiative saving provision permits independently demonstrated conflict and that a true statement can materially mislead in context. The dissent would vacate and remand because the agency's rejection of identified formulations does not establish incompatibility with the entire state obligation, which permits explanatory language and several communication methods. Myrick and Medtronic require an operative source and precise comparison, and the Geier disagreement concerns whether that comparison proves exclusivity. The definitive response establishes more than mere approval, but its force cannot be enlarged into the missing claim-wide showing. Their proposed remand would preserve the operative federal decision while testing genuinely compatible compliance, rather than order a rejected warning. The distinction is the scope of displacement on this record, not a general prohibition on regulation of misleading labeling.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for implementation of the affirmed preemption judgment at its stated scope. The state court must identify the covered products and warning demand when giving effect to that judgment and adjudicate any independently preserved compatible duty on its own basis.

## Event

Johnson v. California, No. 03-636. Argued November 2, 2004; decided February 23, 2005. On certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 321 F.3d 791. The question is whether California's express racial assignment of reception-center cellmates is governed by the common governmental racial-classification standard or by Turner's general prison-regulation framework. Johnson repeatedly experienced the policy after transfers; the reception and classification period ordinarily lasted roughly sixty days.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the judgment and remands for application of the common substantial-justification standard and separate adjudication of the damages and prospective-relief branches.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 6–3. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Stevens, who would hold the categorical policy unconstitutional now and remand for remaining relief, and Scalia and Thomas, who would affirm under Turner.

The lower court must evaluate the policy's coverage, duration, evidentiary support, burdens and concrete practicable alternatives under the government's burden. Further evidence is required only for genuinely unresolved material issues. Damages require separate determinations of individual conduct, causation and conduct-date immunity. Prospective relief requires current exposure, proper responsible officials and the statutory remedy findings. If the policy fails, relief must correct its operation for the persons it governs unless California establishes that a narrower remedy fully cures the violation, consistently with §3626.

## Holdings

### The State must substantially justify express racial cell assignments

**Holding and operative rule:** An express racial prison-housing classification must satisfy the common substantial-justification standard: the State must establish an important lawful objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship between the classification and that objective, justified coverage and actual burdens, and a supported response to concrete practicable alternatives that offer comparable effectiveness with materially less racial disadvantage. Custody makes credible security experience, urgency and genuinely incomplete information relevant within that inquiry, while a protective purpose, general racial assumptions or administrative convenience alone cannot satisfy it.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Adarand supplies the common government burden for express racial treatment. Oneonta makes deliberate racial selection subject to that burden even when officials assert a benign explanation. Preventing substantiated violence is an important lawful objective, but equal separation of racial groups does not establish the fit of the means chosen. Turner gives operational judgment substantial weight in the prison matters it governs; it does not replace the more specific racial-classification rule. Overton demonstrates why actual security conditions and professional experience matter, without converting them into an exemption. The State must explain both the information deficit for new arrivals and repeated treatment of transferees about whom information already exists. Integration elsewhere and available individualized information are relevant comparisons, not conclusive answers. The standard demands a practical supported assessment, rather than a commissioned study, idealized alternative or requirement to adopt the least restrictive imaginable means. Supported emergency precautions are evaluated within that same rule.

### The policy's validity must be decided under the correct burden

**Holding and operative rule:** A judgment sustaining the reception policy under materially more deferential review must be vacated for application of the common standard to the actual policy and record. Additional factfinding serves genuine material disputes about that application; the court must resolve issues the existing record already permits it to decide.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies the six-Justice judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The Ninth Circuit's Turner analysis did not require California to justify the racial policy's actual coverage and burdens under the governing rule. An ordinary reception period of roughly sixty days does not itself establish either necessity or a universal duration limit. The record permits resolution of the governing legal issue while leaving disputed security fit for its first adjudication under the proper burden. Stevens persuasively identifies the significance of repeated segregation of known transferees and integrated activities, but the Court does not regard those comparisons as resolving every material operational question. Remand calls for a decision under the existing evidence where possible, and focused evidence where a genuine dispute remains. Further proceedings must resolve the supported disputes under that burden. Scalia and Thomas's operational concerns receive weight under the common rule without shifting the burden to the prisoner to disprove every conceivable danger.

### Damages and prospective correction require independent adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** Individual damages depend on actionable conduct, causal responsibility and the qualified-immunity law governing when each defendant acted; prospective correction depends on continuing exposure and a responsible current official. Under 18 U.S.C. §3626(a)(1), a prison decree must be narrowly drawn, extend no further than necessary to correct the federal-right violation, use the least intrusive means necessary, and give substantial weight to adverse effects on public safety or operation of the criminal justice system; relief requiring or permitting an official to exceed state or local authority, or otherwise violate state or local law, is permissible only if federal law requires it to correct the violation, it is necessary, and no other relief will correct the violation.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls these independent requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson supply the conduct-date notice inquiry preserved by Saucier and Brosseau. Existing law may make an application obvious without a factual twin; today's merits rule cannot itself supply retrospective notice. Conversely, a damages defense cannot dispose of a properly supported claim for prospective correction. Section 3626 supplies remedy requirements distinct from the common constitutional merits standard. Its least-intrusiveness rule governs the decree, not the State's merits showing. If the common practice is unlawful, correction must reach the persons it governs unless a narrower remedy fully cures the violation, with the statute's necessity and findings independently satisfied. The district court must identify responsible officials and actual continuing exposure before fashioning relief.

## Precedent Treatment

- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña is applied to express prison-housing classifications: its common substantial-justification burden governs actual racial treatment.
- Brown v. City of Oneonta is applied: benign purpose does not remove deliberate racial selection from that burden.
- Turner v. Safley is distinguished as the source of general prison-regulation review; its deference does not replace the governing racial-classification rule.
- Overton v. Bazzetta retains force for the prison matters it decides and for the practical relevance of institutional evidence.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton supply the individual conduct-date immunity inquiry.
- Saucier v. Katz and Brosseau v. Haugen preserve separate adjudication of the constitutional merits and the existing personal defense, including obvious applications of existing law.

## Law After Decision

Express racial reception-cell assignments, including assignments after repeated transfers, require the State's substantial justification under the common rule. Courts assess the actual coverage, duration, burdens, security evidence and practicable alternatives while respecting supported urgent conditions. The reception policy's validity remains for adjudication under that burden. Personal damages and prospective correction each require their own factual and legal predicates, and §3626 independently limits a prison decree.

## Separate Writings

Stevens agrees that the common standard governs and that personal immunity and prospective relief are distinct. He would hold the categorical policy invalid on this record because its racial proxy and repeated application to known transferees remain insufficiently justified even under Turner. Integrated activities and available individual information expose the inadequacy of the asserted blanket need. Adarand and Oneonta strengthen that conclusion. A changed review standard does not itself entitle the State to another merits opportunity. He distinguishes genuine emergency precautions and would remand only for personal defenses and appropriate relief, rather than enter an immediate damages award or an unsupported decree.

Thomas, joined by Scalia, would affirm under Turner. Confined reception cells, limited monitoring, changing gang affiliations and incomplete information make this temporary classification an operational security judgment reasonably related to protecting inmates and staff. General racial-classification decisions outside confinement do not silently displace the specific prison line. The integrated activities and existing information about transferees make the application closer but do not negate the particular risk in cells. Their argument does not validate racial allocation for its own sake or permanent system-wide segregation. If the Court imposes the common standard, they would permit genuine factual application rather than invalidate the policy immediately, and would apply the ordinary independent remedy restrictions. That alternative position does not change their primary affirmance vote.

## Procedure After Action

The district court must set a prompt schedule for resolving the relevant evidence and any supported interim-relief request under the applicable statutory conditions. It must identify current responsible officials and continuing exposure, decide any personal damages defenses and causal responsibility separately, and require a compliant housing practice if the common policy fails. Any injunction must specify permitted security exceptions and compliance dates consistently with §3626, so that it corrects the established violation through a workable decree.

## Event

Cherokee Nation of Oklahoma v. Leavitt, Nos. 02-1472 and 03-853. Merits decision, March 1, 2005. Argued November 9, 2004. On writs of certiorari to the United States Courts of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit and the Federal Circuit.

No. 02-1472 concerns Cherokee Nation's fiscal-year 1997 contract claims and the Shoshone-Paiute Tribes' fiscal-year 1996–1997 claims; the Tenth Circuit sustained judgment for the Government. No. 03-853 concerns Cherokee Nation's fiscal-year 1994–1996 claims; the Federal Circuit sustained the Board of Contract Appeals' determination for the Nation. The claims include initial or expanded programs and Cherokee's ongoing programs.

The question is whether the United States must pay promised self-determination contract support costs when legally available appropriations were sufficient for the contracts but the agency allocated less to support costs, and whether the statutory funding qualifications and subsequent section 314 defeat those obligations.

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Each holding below is part of the unanimous Opinion of the Court identified in Opinion Topology. All nine Justices join every rule, explanation, qualification and docket-specific application at the stated level of generality.

### I. Legally sufficient appropriations support the authorized payment promise

**Holding and operative rule:** When an authorized self-determination agreement promises reasonable, allowable contract support costs and the relevant appropriation legally provides sufficient funds for that undertaking, the agency's allocation to competing demands does not discharge the promise. An enacted funding limit or an actual contractual condition controls, but a committee-report expectation or internal budget allocation does not create such a limit.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Hercules's requirement of an actual authorized undertaking and Winstar's insistence that the undertaking be enforced with its actual qualifications. These agreements supply the payment promises; the statutory review channel does not create a promise merely from fairness. Then-25 U.S.C. §450j-1(b), the agreements' availability clauses and their adjustment language for congressional action remain operative. Availability concerns the legal sufficiency of the appropriation, rather than whatever amount the agency elects to allocate to contract support.

The fiscal-year 1994–1997 lump sums were legally sufficient for these undertakings. Their original terms did not enact the administrative support-cost totals or committee-report allocations as aggregate ceilings. The Government's contrary reading would permit the agency to cancel its authorized promise by the allocation decision that produces the shortfall. Lincoln preserves discretion in allocating an unrestricted lump sum, while recognizing enforceable statutory boundaries; it does not discharge the consequences of these additional binding promises. The tribes' governmental status and exclusion from procurement regulation do not displace the enacted contract remedy.

**Operative limits:** Courts must examine the particular agreement, fiscal-year appropriation, contracting authority and actual conditions. Costs must be reasonable and allowable, and support-cost recovery must not duplicate transferred program or administrative funding. Causation and recoverable loss retain their ordinary contractual roles. Other fiscal years, genuinely insufficient total appropriations and uncontracted costs lie outside the undertakings and statutes decided here.

### II. The no-required-reduction clause preserves allocation discretion without cancelling liability

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 450j-1(b)'s separate provision that the Secretary is not required to reduce funding for programs, projects or activities serving one tribe to make funds available to another tribe or tribal organization permits the Secretary to decline that reduction. It neither prohibits every reduction nor defeats liability on these authorized contracts when other legally unrestricted funds suffice.

**Controlling explanation:** The Government treats protection against compelled redistribution as a condition cancelling each promise whenever the chosen program allocations exhaust the support-cost budget. The text supplies permission not to reduce another tribe's program funding, rather than that additional condition. Other legally unrestricted funds were sufficient for these claims. Assigning those funds administratively to inherent federal functions does not turn the assignment into an enacted earmark; restricting which functions may be contracted out is a different question from which funds may pay for an authorized contract. Lincoln's allocation discretion and Winstar's contractual responsibility can therefore both operate. Recognizing the contractual obligation provides the contract remedy without directing redistribution of another tribe's program funding.

### III. The initial and expanded-program fund is not an exclusive payment ceiling

**Holding and operative rule:** The annual provision making $7.5 million remain available until expended for the Indian Self-Determination Fund's transitional costs of initial or expanded tribal contracts establishes a designated fund with continuing availability. It does not make that fund the exclusive source for the support-cost promises at issue or cap liability at the agency's initial or expanded-program allocation when other legally available appropriations are sufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** The language gives the designated amount a specified purpose and preserves its availability beyond the fiscal year. It does not state that only that amount may fund the promised support costs. Reading a continuing-availability provision as an exclusive ceiling would add a restriction absent from the annual text and the authorized agreements. This question is distinct from the legal effect of committee-report allocations for total support costs. The construction governs the initial and expanded-program claims on their own terms; the ongoing-program claims retain their separate annual terms. Congress's explicit overall not-to-exceed language beginning in fiscal year 1998 is not an original condition of the fiscal-year 1994–1997 promises under review.

### IV. Section 314 limits unspent appropriations without extinguishing accrued contract claims

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 314 of Pub. L. 105-277 makes the amounts appropriated or earmarked in committee reports the total amounts available for the identified support costs in fiscal years 1994–1998. In these disputes, it limits use of remaining appropriations without retroactively extinguishing claims accrued under the earlier contractual undertakings; the agreements' congressional-adjustment clauses operate at that statutory scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Identifying the amounts still available from earlier fiscal accounts performs real statutory work without repudiating the Government's accrued contractual obligations. The Government's clarification theory fails because the original unrestricted appropriations did not already incorporate the committee-report allocations as legal ceilings. Nor does an agreement's adjustment clause enlarge the subsequent law: the clause responds to what Congress enacted, and section 314 governs the remaining appropriations rather than cancellation of the accrued claims. Lynch and Perry recognize the legal significance of genuine government contractual obligations and reinforce the serious constitutional concern that an interpretation repudiating them would raise. The text permits resolution of the asserted statutory defense through its account-limiting operation, without adjudicating a constitutional invalidity claim.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hercules, Inc. v. United States supplies the actual authorized undertaking requirement; the express support-cost promises satisfy that requirement rather than resting on general fairness.
- United States v. Winstar Corp. supplies enforcement of the actual financial undertaking and its real conditions while preserving Congress's legislative authority; its rule applies to the promises and funding qualifications here.
- Lincoln v. Vigil retains its rule for discretionary allocation of unrestricted lump sums. The additional statutory and contractual duties here distinguish that allocation question from liability for a binding promise.
- Lynch v. United States and Perry v. United States retain their protection of genuine government obligations and support the construction of section 314 that avoids attributing repudiation of accrued contractual claims to an appropriation-account limit.

## Law After Decision

Authorized tribal self-determination support-cost promises backed by sufficient legally available appropriations remain enforceable despite administrative underallocation. Courts must give separate effect to appropriations availability, the permission not to reduce another tribe's program funding, an annual designated fund and a later restriction on unspent accounts. The fiscal-year provisions decided here supply neither the asserted exclusive $7.5 million ceiling nor cancellation of accrued claims under section 314. Genuine statutory limits and actual contract conditions continue to govern.

## Event

Petro-Hunt, L.L.C. v. United States, No. 04-190. Merits decision, March 1, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, reviewing its judgment reported at 365 F.3d 385, issued March 31, 2004 and revised May 28, 2004.

The questions are whether the final Nebo Oil judgment protecting one mineral servitude precludes litigation over distinct servitudes derived from related conveyances, and whether intervening changes in federal choice-of-law principles permit renewed litigation of an otherwise precluded issue. The district court extended the earlier judgment across the disputed acreage; the Fifth Circuit rejected that preclusion, held the other 95 servitudes subject to ten-year nonuse prescription, and remanded to determine which had prescribed.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in the merits review and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the Fifth Circuit's judgment and remands for the remaining title proceedings.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Nebo Oil's adjudication of its original servitude remains effective. The Fifth Circuit's determination that the other 95 servitudes are subject to Louisiana's ten-year nonuse prescription remains effective; the district court must determine actual prescription and resulting title for each servitude.

## Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion for a unanimous Court.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Scalia | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Supports affirmance and remand | Entire opinion, both preclusion holdings and the remand instructions |

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Both holdings and the remand instructions are part of the unanimous Opinion of the Court identified in Opinion Topology. All nine Justices join them at the stated level of generality.

### I. A judgment about one servitude does not merge distinct property claims

**Holding and operative rule:** Federal common law governs the preclusive effect of the earlier federal-question judgment. A final title judgment binds the claim it adjudicated, including grounds that could have been advanced within that claim, but distinct servitudes with different acquisition and use histories do not become the same claim merely because they share owners, related conveyances or a legal premise; the party invoking preclusion must establish the required identity from the actual instruments and adjudication.

**Controlling explanation:** Nevada v. United States requires attention to the same property right before the conclusive effect of a title judgment can control a later dispute. The 96 servitudes here arose through six mineral conveyances and eleven federal land acquisitions. Nebo Oil's operative decree concerns the servitude on approximately 800 acres described in its complaint. Its broader findings do not extend that decree to the other 95 servitudes. The United States remains bound as to the adjudicated property.

Petro-Hunt's strongest argument treats the related acquisitions as one connected transaction and the earlier ruling as settling a common right to retain the minerals. That connection does not eliminate the distinct property rights or their individual creation and use histories. One dispute concerning the original servitude did not adjudicate every other servitude held through the same predecessors. Semtek requires examination of the source and actual effect of a federal judgment, while its ordinary state-law incorporation rule concerns diversity judgments and does not govern this federal-question decree. Plaut protects a completed adjudication at its actual scope; it does not enlarge the adjudicated property. The district court's blanket claim-preclusion ruling therefore fails.

### II. An assumed choice of law is not an actually litigated issue

**Holding and operative rule:** Mutual issue preclusion requires the identical issue to have been actually litigated and necessarily determined. Nebo Oil's adjudication of state-law retroactivity and constitutional objections, while assuming Louisiana law supplied the rule of decision, does not preclude the distinct federal choice-of-law issue now asserted.

**Controlling explanation:** Different claims may share an issue that has binding effect, so the distinct servitudes do not by themselves defeat Petro-Hunt's issue-preclusion theory. Sunnen and Montana require examination of the precise issue actually decided. The earlier litigation addressed whether the state statute operated retroactively and whether its application violated constitutional protections; it did not litigate the federal choice-of-law issue. A judgment's dependence on an assumed legal rule does not transform the assumption into an actually contested and resolved question.

Stauffer protects mutual issue preclusion when its predicates are established. It does not supply the missing actual litigation here. The claim-identity and issue-litigation defects independently answer the corresponding preclusion theories. Because the asserted issue never acquired preclusive effect, the granted question about a changed-law exception to an otherwise established issue estoppel does not arise on this record. The Court therefore does not reach that question or adopt the Government's Little Lake Misere exception theory. The original final decree retains its binding disposition.

## Precedent Treatment

## Event

Roper v. Simmons, No. 03-633. Argued October 13, 2004; decided March 1, 2005. On certiorari to the Supreme Court of Missouri, 112 S.W.3d 397, which granted state collateral relief from Simmons's death sentence. The question is whether the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments permit execution for a crime committed before the offender's eighteenth birthday and whether Stanford v. Kentucky should continue to govern that question. Simmons committed the murder at seventeen.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the Missouri judgment replacing the death sentence with a noncapital sentence.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 6–3. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas.

Simmons's conviction and sentence of life imprisonment without eligibility for probation, parole or release except by gubernatorial action remain effective. Missouri must maintain that noncapital custody basis and remove authority to execute him for this offense. His established offense-date age supplies the categorical ground for relief.

## Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ. O'CONNOR, J., filed a dissenting opinion. SCALIA, J., filed a dissenting opinion, joined by THOMAS, J.

| Dissent | Scalia | Thomas | Would reverse and remand | Methodological and domestic-evidence grounds for retaining Stanford |

## Holdings

### Offenders under eighteen at the time of the crime are ineligible for death

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments prohibit the death penalty for an offense committed before the offender's eighteenth birthday. Age at the offense controls; later adulthood, the crime's severity or planning, and an assessment of exceptional individual maturity do not create an exception.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls every holding and its explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** Atkins supplies the method: domestic enactment and practice inform proportionality, and diminished culpability coupled with unreliable capital selection can require a category even when mitigation is available. Thirty States exclude this punishment—twelve reject capital punishment generally and eighteen exclude under-eighteen offenses by legislation or judicial interpretation. These are not thirty age-specific enactments. Six States have executed juvenile offenders since Stanford, only three in the preceding decade. Five States have changed since Stanford, four legislatively and one judicially. The movement is slower than the change considered in Atkins, but the combined exclusion and concentrated use support the age boundary.

Immaturity and irresponsible judgment, susceptibility to pressure and limited control of circumstances, and a character still developing reduce the class's culpability. Those features weaken retribution and deterrence as grounds for execution. Planned cruelty can establish serious criminal responsibility without reliably identifying an adolescent with the settled culpability claimed to justify death. O'Connor's variability objection therefore identifies a central selection problem rather than a dependable exception. Individual mitigation cannot reliably separate an exceptionally mature, irredeemably culpable juvenile from the protected class. Eighteen is an administrable legal boundary, not a finding that all development ends on a birthday. The domestic evidence and the Court's proportionality judgment together support the rule.

### Stanford is displaced and the substantive restriction governs lawful review

**Holding and operative rule:** Stanford v. Kentucky is overruled insofar as it permits execution for offenses committed at sixteen or seventeen. Because the new rule removes a class from death eligibility, a death sentence within that class must be replaced through lawful noncapital sentencing procedures in nonfinal cases and final cases reached through an available review channel, preserving valid convictions and independent procedural requirements.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice opinion controls both the express precedent change and the application to Simmons's state collateral judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Stanford relied on then-prevailing domestic evidence and the adequacy of individualized mitigation. The developments since that decision, the reduced culpability of youth and the continuing unreliability of identifying an adult-like juvenile for irreversible punishment undermine those premises. Atkins explains why available mitigation can fail to cure a class-wide selection defect; it does not itself decide the age question or equate youth with its distinct disability definition. Retaining a rule that permits execution of this class cannot be justified by an entitlement to continue that punishment. The change leaves convictions and lawful noncapital punishment in place. Thompson's under-sixteen outcome remains consistent with the broader age boundary now adopted. This Court directly reconsiders Stanford in the case presenting that question. The substantive restriction supplies Simmons's relief in his existing collateral proceeding and governs other qualifying judgments when lawfully reached. Offense-date age, rather than an individual maturity hearing, determines its application.

## Precedent Treatment

- Stanford v. Kentucky is overruled to the extent it permits death for offenses committed before eighteen; that permission no longer has force.
- Atkins v. Virginia is extended in its categorical proportionality method, including domestic evidence, culpability, penal purposes and unreliable selection. Its separate disability definition and procedures retain their own scope.
- Thompson v. Oklahoma remains consistent with the result; its under-sixteen protection is encompassed by the present under-eighteen rule.

## Law After Decision

Offense-date age below eighteen categorically excludes a person from capital punishment. Courts applying the rule must reliably resolve a genuinely disputed birth date and, through their lawful review and sentencing procedures, replace a qualifying death sentence with authorized noncapital punishment. The category applies to final and nonfinal judgments reached through those procedures. Serious criminal responsibility, valid convictions and independent lawful punishment retain their force.

## Separate Writings

O'Connor accepts evolving standards and independent proportionality review, as she did in Atkins, but finds this extension insufficiently supported. The changes since Stanford are fewer and slower, continuing authorization remains substantial, and older adolescents vary in maturity. In her view, the evidence does not establish that individualized consideration cannot identify an offender sufficiently culpable for death. Atkins's distinct disability category and its reliable-status inquiry therefore do not decide this age question. She would preserve Stanford's eligibility rule, give youth its substantial mitigating force and return the case for lawful state proceedings. Her position does not reject independent proportionality judgment or treat legislatures as its exclusive source.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, objects both to the Court's method and to its account of domestic evidence. Stanford leaves the disputed eligibility rule in force unless the Constitution supplies a sound basis for change. The twelve States rejecting all capital punishment cannot be counted as twelve age-specific enactments; limited movement and continuing permission among capital States do not establish the claimed rejection. Rare use may reflect careful selection rather than repudiation, and grave planned crimes challenge a universal account of insufficient culpability. They oppose using independent judicial penal judgment to create the category and regard the showing as inadequate even within the prevailing evolving-standards inquiry. Atkins remains binding for its actual disability class, and youth must still receive its lawful mitigating consideration. They would reverse the federal categorical ruling and remand for lawful state proceedings, rather than direct execution.

## Procedure After Action

The affirmed Missouri judgment governs Simmons's custody and bars execution for this offense. His noncapital sentence remains subject to its stated gubernatorial-action qualification. Other qualifying death judgments receive the substantive age rule through their existing lawful review channels and the noncapital correction authorized by governing law.

## Source Notes

112 S.W.3d 397; Stanford v. Kentucky, 492 U.S. 361; Thompson v. Oklahoma, 487 U.S. 815; Atkins v. Virginia. The domestic counts distinguish general abolition, age-specific exclusion, legislative change, judicial change and actual executions. The state judgment specifies the qualification for gubernatorial action in the noncapital sentence.

## Event

Catholic Charities of Sacramento, Inc. v. Superior Court; California Supreme Court No. S099822, 32 Cal. 4th 527. Decided March 2, 2005, after full merits briefing. On review of the California Supreme Court's affirmance of the denial of mandate concerning preliminary injunctive relief. The underlying action seeks declaratory and injunctive relief against the Women's Contraception Equity Act. The threshold question is whether this judgment is reviewable under 28 U.S.C. §1257 despite the action's preliminary-relief posture.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Court dismisses for want of jurisdiction.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Dismissed for want of jurisdiction, 8–1. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Stevens.

The dismissal ends this attempted review and leaves the California preliminary-relief disposition in its existing posture. Because §1257 jurisdiction is unestablished, the Court does not reach the free-exercise, establishment, exemption or related constitutional merits.

## Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ. STEVENS, J., filed a dissenting opinion on jurisdiction.

## Holdings

### Final resolution of arguments does not by itself conclude the action

**Holding and operative rule:** A highest state court's comprehensive rejection of federal theories in reviewing denial of preliminary protection does not, by itself, establish a final judgment under §1257. Review requires a judgment concluding the relevant action or an established Cox exception, including practical completion with the further outcome preordained; the operative effect of the judgment governs rather than its interlocutory label.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls both holdings and every stated exception analysis.

**Controlling explanation:** Richards permits review when a ruling conclusively bars the remaining claims and leaves implementation preordained. Jefferson distinguishes a conclusively discussed issue from an action still requiring substantive adjudication. California has exhaustively rejected every presented constitutional theory and addressed Catholic Charities' conceded statutory ineligibility. Stevens therefore presents a substantial argument that permanent denial is inevitable.

The Court nevertheless reads the actual judgment as adjudicating entitlement to provisional protection, rather than conclusively disposing of the pleaded declaratory and permanent-injunction claims. The remaining adjudication of those rights is meaningful under that order's operative scope, not automatic execution of an existing claim-disposing judgment. The force of the legal reasons supporting denial makes the prediction of ultimate defeat powerful; it does not supply the conclusive effect present in Richards. This conclusion rests on the scope of the adjudication itself, not a presumed new factual dispute, a demand for a later decree or a categorical objection to interim orders. Johnson, No. 03-6539, supplies the invoking party's burden to establish finality or an exception. The present record does not satisfy ordinary finality or the first Cox category on that legal assessment.

### The other practical-finality routes do not establish review here

**Holding and operative rule:** Once the federal issue has been finally decided, the remaining Cox routes permit review when it necessarily survives and requires decision regardless of further proceedings; when effective later review cannot be obtained whatever their outcome; or when federal reversal would end the relevant litigation and delay seriously threatens federal policy. The invoking party must establish the actual predicates of a route, and useful later protection—not the abstract possibility of another appeal—governs the effective-review inquiry.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice opinion controls the distinct analyses of the second, third and fourth categories.

**Controlling explanation:** The continuing importance of the coverage question does not establish that it must survive every remaining disposition. Its asserted inevitability here depends on the practical-completion premise rejected above; no independently sufficient second-category predicate is established.

An adverse judgment on the permanent claims can present the continuing coverage controversy and permit useful prospective correction. Catholic Charities alleges present religious pressure to provide objectionable coverage or withdraw prescription benefits. That is a real alleged interim burden, but its continuing effects do not establish that meaningful review will be unavailable whatever the outcome. Ritchie requires attention to useful protection, while Jefferson and Florida v. Thomas preserve later review when it can effectively correct the actual dispute. The record identifies no specific event eliminating that protection.

Under the fourth category, correcting preliminary-injunction analysis or invalidating one exemption criterion need not end the relevant litigation. Even assuming a federal ruling broad enough to resolve the entire dispute, serious erosion of federal policy from waiting remains independently necessary. The importance of religious exercise and the ordinary interim effects established here do not meet that requirement. Johnson rejects constitutional importance alone as an immediate-review exception. These grounds leave the merits beyond the Court's jurisdiction in this review.

## Precedent Treatment

- Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn supplies four distinct practical-finality routes, each requiring its actual predicates.
- Richards v. Jefferson County is distinguished on operative effect: its conclusive claim bar left implementation preordained, while the present provisional adjudication does not have that established effect.
- Jefferson v. City of Tarrant supplies the distinction between a settled federal issue and a practically completed action. Its remaining liability, causation and claim proceedings supplied a distinct application of that rule.
- Florida v. Thomas supplies separate evaluation of each Cox route and effective later review; its express alternative-ground remand illustrates a different basis for substantive proceedings to continue.
- Johnson v. California, No. 03-6539, decided May 3, 2004, supplies the jurisdiction-invoking party's burden and rejects constitutional importance alone as an exception.
- Pennsylvania v. Ritchie retains its practical qualification: a merely conceivable later appeal does not establish useful later protection.

## Law After Decision

Section 1257 finality depends on the judgment's actual claim-disposing effect and the established predicates of a Cox category. A comprehensive constitutional discussion supporting provisional relief does not alone establish practical completion. Courts must examine effective later protection against the actual asserted burden. This preliminary-relief judgment fails that framework's jurisdictional requirements; the religion merits remain undecided here because the Court lacks jurisdiction to reach them.

## Separate Writings

Stevens would exercise jurisdiction under Cox's first category. Richards makes practical completion decisive even when the state order bears an interlocutory label. California rejected every presented constitutional theory on accepted organizational facts and conceded statutory ineligibility. No identified substantive issue remains whose resolution could alter the requested relief. In his view, denial of permanent relief is therefore preordained, and another adjudication would implement an already conclusive legal result.

His Jefferson dissent explains why repetition serving no useful finality purpose should not postpone review. Ritchie's practical approach reinforces that concern, although the first Cox category supplies the ground here. He distinguishes his joins in Florida v. Thomas and Johnson because those cases identified substantive proceedings capable of changing or eliminating the federal dispute. The majority's distinction between provisional entitlement and permanent rights gives insufficient effect to the exhaustive resolution of the legal claims in this record. His dissent concerns jurisdiction and expresses no disposition of the constitutional merits.

## Procedure After Action

The parties remain in the state action under its existing preliminary-relief posture and may pursue the relief available there. Any renewed Supreme Court review must rest on its own judgment satisfying §1257 or an established practical-finality exception.

## Event

Tenet v. Doe, No. 03-1395. Merits decision, March 2, 2005. Argued January 11, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit.

The Does allege that the Central Intelligence Agency recruited them to perform espionage abroad in return for continuing support, and later reduced or stopped that support. They seek interim monthly support, a declaration prescribing internal administrative review, and mandamus requiring support and regulations. The Government has neither confirmed nor denied the alleged relationship.

The district court dismissed the equal-protection count and denied a preliminary injunction, but permitted the surviving estoppel and constitutional claims to continue and later denied the Government's summary-judgment request. The Ninth Circuit accepted the Government's certified interlocutory appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1292(b), declined the Does' cross-petition, and sustained continuation of the surviving claims. The question is whether Totten bars these agreement-dependent claims despite their estoppel and due-process labels and requested administrative procedures.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remands with instructions to dismiss the surviving agreement-dependent claims.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded for dismissal, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The surviving estoppel and due-process claims founded on the alleged secret undertaking must be dismissed. The already dismissed equal-protection branch and denial of preliminary relief remain undisturbed.

## Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion for a unanimous Court.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Supports reversal and dismissal | Entire opinion, all three holdings and the implementing mandate |

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Each holding is part of the unanimous Opinion of the Court identified in Opinion Topology. All nine Justices join every rule, explanation, qualification and application at the stated level of generality.

### I. Totten bars claims necessarily dependent on a secret espionage undertaking

**Holding and operative rule:** A claim is barred when success on the pleaded right and requested relief necessarily requires judicial establishment of a secret espionage relationship with the Government and enforcement of the alleged undertaking arising from it. Estoppel or due-process labels do not remove that indispensable premise; a claim founded on an independent public legal duty or an applicable enacted enforcement channel must be assessed on its own source and requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** Totten protects the secrecy of the undertaking itself, including whether the relationship existed. The Does' claimed entitlement to continuing support and internal review derives from their alleged recruitment and promise. Taking that theory as pleaded permits the Court to identify the bar without confirming the relationship or adjudicating whether its asserted promises were breached.

The Does argue that a fair internal process differs from ordinary enforcement of a secret contract. But a judicial command for that process would first require recognition of an entitlement arising from the same alleged bargain. No independent public legal duty supplies the claimed support or review right here. Christopher v. Harbury preserves the requirement of an actual underlying entitlement and suitable remedy; the seriousness of an alleged injury cannot supply a missing judicial channel. Intelligence-agency identity or classified evidence alone is insufficient to trigger the rule: its necessary condition is the claim's dependence on the secret espionage undertaking.

### II. Evidentiary protections and the requested procedures do not remove the necessary premise

**Holding and operative rule:** Reynolds's state-secrets privilege governs withholding evidence in an otherwise maintainable action; it does not replace Totten's distinct bar on maintaining an agreement-dependent suit. The Director's formal personal invocation required for that evidentiary privilege is therefore not a prerequisite to dismissing these claims under Totten, including their requests for interim support, administrative review and regulations.

**Controlling explanation:** Reynolds expressly preserves Totten's dismissal on the pleadings when the suit's very subject is the secret undertaking. Pseudonyms and restrained initial disclosure may protect names, but they cannot eliminate the relationship that must be established to obtain the requested relief. An order to pay monthly support, prescribe review or issue regulations would still enforce the asserted entitlement arising from that relationship. The Ninth Circuit's proposed privilege inquiry therefore addresses evidence without resolving the antecedent bar to this category of suit.

Webster concerned an acknowledged employment relationship and an independent constitutional challenge to a statutory employment decision. That setting differs from a relationship whose very existence the Government has not acknowledged and whose alleged secret terms supply the asserted entitlement. The Court preserves Webster's independent public-law setting while enforcing Totten's agreement-dependent boundary. The Government's mention of an opportunity to complain within the Agency does not establish an enforceable benefits remedy.

### III. The Court may enforce this threshold bar before the possible Tucker Act channel objection

**Holding and operative rule:** Totten prevents the judicial inquiry necessary to maintain this category of action and may be applied at the threshold without first resolving the possible Tucker Act channel objection. This sequence rests on a prohibition against undertaking the inquiry, rather than a decision of ordinary contested liability on assumed jurisdiction.

**Controlling explanation:** Steel Co. forbids assuming Article III power to decide contested merits while preserving legitimate nonmerits threshold sequences. Ruhrgas recognizes that threshold objections do not have a universal order and preserves the distinction between declining to entertain a case and adjudicating liability without authority. Totten supplies the specific threshold here: further inquiry into the alleged secret relationship is precisely what the rule forbids.

The Court therefore resolves the pleaded category without confirming its factual premise or choosing among possible contractual judicial channels. Whether the Tucker Act would otherwise supply an exclusive channel is unnecessary to this antecedent resolution and remains undecided. The limited sequencing holding does not establish that every application of Totten defeats subject-matter jurisdiction or that ordinary merits may be adjudicated on assumed jurisdiction.

## Precedent Treatment

- Totten v. United States retains its categorical prohibition on ordinary judicial enforcement necessarily dependent on a secret espionage undertaking; it applies to the agreement-dependent support and process claims here.
- United States v. Reynolds retains its evidentiary privilege and its formal-invocation requirements for that privilege. Its distinct function does not displace Totten's pleading-stage rule.
- Webster v. Doe retains constitutional review in its independent statutory employment setting; that acknowledged relationship differs from the secret undertaking necessary to these claims.
- Christopher v. Harbury supplies the requirement that an asserted injury and requested remedy connect to an actual underlying entitlement; it supplies no independent channel for the agreement-dependent claims.
- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. retains its prohibition on contested merits adjudication upon assumed Article III power and its distinction for legitimate nonmerits thresholds.
- Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co. supplies the absence of a universal hierarchy among threshold objections while preserving the jurisdiction-before-merits boundary.

## Law After Decision

Totten governs the full category of claims whose success necessarily depends on establishing and enforcing a secret espionage undertaking, including claims relabeled as estoppel or due process and demands for judicially ordered support or internal review. Reynolds remains the evidentiary rule for an otherwise maintainable suit. Courts must identify the claim's legal source and necessary proof; genuinely independent public duties receive ordinary jurisdiction, waiver and merits analysis, together with any properly invoked evidentiary privilege. The bar permits threshold dismissal of the agreement-dependent claims without resolving the alleged relationship or the possible Tucker Act channel objection.
