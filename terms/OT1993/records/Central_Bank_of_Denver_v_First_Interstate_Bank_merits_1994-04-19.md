## Controlling holdings

### Private secondary liability under §10(b)

**Controlling proposition:** A private plaintiff may not recover under §10(b) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 or Rule 10b-5 merely because the defendant aided and abetted another person’s securities fraud. The defendant must itself engage, directly or indirectly, in the prohibited manipulative or deceptive conduct and satisfy the elements of a primary claim; professional status supplies no immunity, but culpable assistance alone does not establish that claim.

**Authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices adopt the statutory boundary and its application to the pleaded secondary claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that assistance to a violation is not itself the conduct Congress prohibited. Hochfelder makes statutory language the starting point and reserved secondary liability; Huddleston likewise did not decide it. Santa Fe requires manipulation or deception rather than misconduct alone. The word indirectly reaches indirect commission of prohibited conduct, but does not erase that conduct requirement. Express securities remedies specify liable defendants; assistance cannot bypass the primary action’s applicable reliance requirement. Criminal aiding-and-abetting liability under 18 U.S.C. §2 does not supply a general private civil action. Common-law accessory principles cannot replace the enacted predicate. Musick, Peeler permits contribution among persons already jointly liable within the recognized action; it does not bring an additional defendant within that liability. Longstanding circuit acceptance deserves weight, but no controlling decision adopted this reserved extension, and congressional silence does not enact it. Rejecting that consensus reduces private recovery against some knowing facilitators; enforcement utility nevertheless cannot supply missing statutory authority. The bank’s appraisal deferral may be culpable assistance, but the pleaded theory does not establish its own primary violation. The Court does not decide the contested secondary scienter standard or an unpresented primary claim.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder, 425 U.S. 185 — applied for the statutory conduct inquiry; its reservation of aiding and abetting is now resolved for private claims.
- Herman & MacLean v. Huddleston, 459 U.S. 375 — its reserved private secondary-liability question is resolved; its treatment of overlapping express and implied remedies is not overruled.
- Santa Fe Industries, Inc. v. Green, 430 U.S. 462 — applied: unfairness or culpability alone does not replace manipulation or deception.
- Pinter v. Dahl, 486 U.S. 622 — applied by analogy for fidelity to the defendants and conduct specified in express securities liability; its §12 holding is unchanged.
- Musick, Peeler & Garrett v. Employers Insurance of Wausau — distinguished: contribution allocates existing shared liability and does not create the underlying secondary cause.
- Superintendent of Insurance v. Bankers Life & Casualty Co., 404 U.S. 6 — preserved as recognition of the private primary action; it did not settle this secondary action.
