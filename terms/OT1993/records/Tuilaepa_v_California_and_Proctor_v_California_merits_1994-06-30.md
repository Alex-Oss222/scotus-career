## Controlling holdings

### Relevant individualized selection does not require fixed labels or numerical weights

**Controlling proposition:** On the unchallenged eligibility premise presented here, the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments permit consideration of understandable, relevant subjects for individualized penalty selection without requiring every consideration to be a yes-or-no factual element, exclusively aggravating or mitigating, or assigned a fixed numerical weight. This rule does not authorize forbidden or wholly irrelevant adverse considerations, exclusion of effective mitigation, or disregard of a separately established selection error.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight directly adopt this bounded rule; Stevens and Ginsburg do not join Part I's additional framework discussion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that individual sentencing judgment cannot be reduced to a required arithmetic formula. Zant permits consideration of relevant characteristics of the crime and offender once eligibility has been lawfully narrowed, while Lockett and Eddings protect the defendant's opportunity to give mitigation meaningful effect. The present eligibility findings are unchallenged; the Court assumes their sufficiency for these questions. Petitioners argue that flexible subjects allow almost any fact to be urged toward death. That possibility does not make every relevant subject vague or require the State to assign it an exclusive direction. It also does not license an unconstitutional adverse inference. Graham and Johnson v. Texas require an effective route for independent culpability mitigation; these California mechanisms permit that assessment without forcing a false answer to another factual question. Boyde requires attention to the instructions in context, not favorable weight for every item. The Court sustains the presented factors within those limits and decides no actual forbidden use unsupported by these records.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983): applied to relevant individualized selection after unchallenged narrowing; it supplies no exemption for forbidden adverse considerations.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 (1978), and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 (1982): preserved; relevant mitigation must have effective consideration, without guaranteed favorable weight.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 (1990): applied to contextual review and individualized moral judgment, not mechanical factor counting.
- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993, and Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: distinguished in application; their effective independent-mitigation requirement remains fully operative.

**Framework and continuing safeguards:** Lawful eligibility narrowing and individualized selection perform distinct functions under Gregg, Woodson and Lowenfield. Arave's authoritative limitation on an eligibility aggravator remains necessary in its setting. Eligibility does not cure a later unconstitutional weighing factor under Stringer and Espinosa. Romano's separate-death-decision responsibility rule and Simmons's future-danger/parole-ineligibility rebuttal rule retain their distinct operation. This decision neither extends nor retreats from those rules and does not approve the entire California capital scheme.

### The circumstances-of-the-crime factor survives both presented challenges

**Controlling proposition:** Section 190.3(a), directing consideration when relevant of the circumstances of the crime of conviction and the existence of special circumstances found true under §190.1, identifies an understandable and relevant selection subject and is not unconstitutional on the facial and instructional theories presented by either petitioner. The Court affirms each judgment on that ground without adjudicating §190.2 eligibility or any independently preserved claim of actual forbidden use.

**Authority:** Part III of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight find no error on each docket's factor-(a) challenge; none of these joins is a harmlessness vote.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the circumstances of this crime identify relevant subject matter rather than an undefined judgment that a murder is especially heinous. Godfrey and Maynard require meaningful limits on the vague aggravating labels at issue there; their cautions do not automatically invalidate this direction to consider the actual offense. Zant's individualized inquiry permits facts that can carry different significance in different cases. Tuilaepa's jury received relevant factors, nonmechanical weighing and a requirement that aggravation be sufficiently substantial to warrant death. Proctor's separate jury received the full list and older mandatory language, together with nonmechanical weighing and directions permitting mitigation, pity, sympathy and mercy while excluding prejudice and conjecture. The Court preserves that difference and evaluates the presented objection in each setting. Proctor's venue-requested new jury establishes neither bias nor a surrender of responsibility. Petitioners' concern about possible impermissible uses remains reviewable on a proper record, but broad relevance alone establishes neither those uses nor constitutional error here.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 (1980), and Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 (1988): distinguished; their vague pejorative aggravators differ from identification of the actual crime's circumstances.
- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983): applied; relevant individualized consideration does not require an exclusive aggravating or mitigating label for every circumstance.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 (1990): applied to each complete reported charge; no ordinary word or boilerplate direction automatically cures an actual contradiction.

**Application and nonreach:** Both factor-(a) challenges fail without harmless-error review. The judgment does not decide unrelated guilt-phase comments, every other factor, racial discrimination, the lawfulness of treating absence of a minor-accomplice role as aggravation, or the whole California eligibility scheme. ### The violent-criminal-activity factor is sufficiently definite in Tuilaepa

**Controlling proposition:** Section 190.3(b), when relevant, permits consideration of the presence or absence of criminal activity involving use or attempted use of force or violence, or an express or implied threat to use force or violence. As construed to concern punishable offenses involving force or threatened force against a person, this is an understandable selection subject and defeats Tuilaepa's presented vagueness challenge; Proctor presents no factor-(b) question.

**Authority:** Part IV of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight directly sustain factor (b) on no-error grounds; Blackmun dissents on this factor as well as the others.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that criminal acts involving violence or threatened violence identify a discernible subject for sentencing judgment. The direction concerns conduct, including the conduct in custody and during trial reported here, rather than only acts preceding the capital offense. Zant permits relevant information about the offender, and Boyde does not require the State to assign a numerical moral value to each proved act. Tuilaepa's jury was instructed to find the acts beyond a reasonable doubt before considering them in aggravation. That reported safeguard addresses whether the conduct occurred; the Court does not convert it into a constitutional burden for every sentencing evaluation. The competing argument correctly distinguishes proof from sentencing weight, but the factor's defined violent subject and the individualized assessment do not become vague merely because weight remains for the jury. The state court's no-request and no-prejudice reasoning about instructions defining particular offenses is a separate matter. The Court resolves no independent elements-instruction claim or categorical objection to all unadjudicated conduct.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983): applied to relevant offender conduct without requiring a uniform assigned weight.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 (1990): applied; individualized sentencing is not mechanical factual counting.

**Qualification:** The trial instruction's proof burden is a record fact, not a new statutory or universal constitutional rule. This holding supplies no finding about an unreported act, no general permission to use unreliable or forbidden evidence, and no factor-(b) ruling in Proctor.

### Age is a relevant contextual subject, not an automatic aggravator

**Controlling proposition:** Section 190.3(i)'s direction to consider, when relevant, the defendant's age at the time of the crime is sufficiently understandable on Tuilaepa's presented challenge and requires no universal numerical weight or exclusive aggravating or mitigating label. The absence of youth does not automatically aggravate the crime, and the possibility of improper argument does not establish a facial defect or a proved as-applied violation; Proctor presents no age-factor question.

**Authority:** Part V of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight adopt the rule and no-error application; Part VI gives the corresponding affirmances without a new penalty hearing.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that age has intelligible but contextual significance in assessing the person who committed the crime. Zant permits relevant individual consideration without demanding that every ambiguous circumstance favor only one side. Lockett and Eddings, as implemented by Graham and Johnson v. Texas, still require a genuine route for youth's independent mitigating significance. California's selection mechanism permits that moral assessment rather than making a finding of deliberateness or future danger the exclusive expression of culpability. The objection that a prosecutor might characterize any possible age as aggravating raises a real boundary, not a reason to presume that every use of the subject is invalid. Absence of youth is not automatically a reason for death. An argument treating every age as independently sufficient aggravation would need examination on its own preserved record. The supplied facts establish no such adjudicated misuse here. The Court therefore affirms on no-error grounds and reaches neither the lower separate harmlessness theories nor a general approval of prosecutorial age arguments.

- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 (1978), and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 (1982): preserved as protection for meaningful mitigation.

**Controlling proposition:** On the unchallenged eligibility premise presented here, the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments permit consideration of understandable, relevant subjects for individualized penalty selection without requiring every consideration to be a yes-or-no factual element, exclusively aggravating or mitigating, or assigned a fixed numerical weight. This rule does not authorize forbidden or wholly irrelevant adverse considerations, exclusion of effective mitigation, or disregard of a separately established selection error.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight directly adopt this bounded rule; Stevens and Ginsburg do not join Part I's additional framework discussion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that individual sentencing judgment cannot be reduced to a required arithmetic formula. Zant permits consideration of relevant characteristics of the crime and offender once eligibility has been lawfully narrowed, while Lockett and Eddings protect the defendant's opportunity to give mitigation meaningful effect. The present eligibility findings are unchallenged; the Court assumes their sufficiency for these questions. Petitioners argue that flexible subjects allow almost any fact to be urged toward death. That possibility does not make every relevant subject vague or require the State to assign it an exclusive direction. It also does not license an unconstitutional adverse inference. Graham and Johnson v. Texas require an effective route for independent culpability mitigation; these California mechanisms permit that assessment without forcing a false answer to another factual question. Boyde requires attention to the instructions in context, not favorable weight for every item. The Court sustains the presented factors within those limits and decides no actual forbidden use unsupported by these records.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983): applied to relevant individualized selection after unchallenged narrowing; it supplies no exemption for forbidden adverse considerations.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 (1978), and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 (1982): preserved; relevant mitigation must have effective consideration, without guaranteed favorable weight.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 (1990): applied to contextual review and individualized moral judgment, not mechanical factor counting.
- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993, and Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: distinguished in application; their effective independent-mitigation requirement remains fully operative.

**Framework and continuing safeguards:** Lawful eligibility narrowing and individualized selection perform distinct functions under Gregg, Woodson and Lowenfield. Arave's authoritative limitation on an eligibility aggravator remains necessary in its setting. Eligibility does not cure a later unconstitutional weighing factor under Stringer and Espinosa. Romano's separate-death-decision responsibility rule and Simmons's future-danger/parole-ineligibility rebuttal rule retain their distinct operation. This decision neither extends nor retreats from those rules and does not approve the entire California capital scheme.

### The circumstances-of-the-crime factor survives both presented challenges

**Controlling proposition:** Section 190.3(a), directing consideration when relevant of the circumstances of the crime of conviction and the existence of special circumstances found true under §190.1, identifies an understandable and relevant selection subject and is not unconstitutional on the facial and instructional theories presented by either petitioner. The Court affirms each judgment on that ground without adjudicating §190.2 eligibility or any independently preserved claim of actual forbidden use.

**Authority:** Part III of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight find no error on each docket's factor-(a) challenge; none of these joins is a harmlessness vote.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the circumstances of this crime identify relevant subject matter rather than an undefined judgment that a murder is especially heinous. Godfrey and Maynard require meaningful limits on the vague aggravating labels at issue there; their cautions do not automatically invalidate this direction to consider the actual offense. Zant's individualized inquiry permits facts that can carry different significance in different cases. Tuilaepa's jury received relevant factors, nonmechanical weighing and a requirement that aggravation be sufficiently substantial to warrant death. Proctor's separate jury received the full list and older mandatory language, together with nonmechanical weighing and directions permitting mitigation, pity, sympathy and mercy while excluding prejudice and conjecture. The Court preserves that difference and evaluates the presented objection in each setting. Proctor's venue-requested new jury establishes neither bias nor a surrender of responsibility. Petitioners' concern about possible impermissible uses remains reviewable on a proper record, but broad relevance alone establishes neither those uses nor constitutional error here.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 (1980), and Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 (1988): distinguished; their vague pejorative aggravators differ from identification of the actual crime's circumstances.
- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983): applied; relevant individualized consideration does not require an exclusive aggravating or mitigating label for every circumstance.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 (1990): applied to each complete reported charge; no ordinary word or boilerplate direction automatically cures an actual contradiction.

**Application and nonreach:** Both factor-(a) challenges fail without harmless-error review. The judgment does not decide unrelated guilt-phase comments, every other factor, racial discrimination, the lawfulness of treating absence of a minor-accomplice role as aggravation, or the whole California eligibility scheme. No actual juror mental state is found.

### The violent-criminal-activity factor is sufficiently definite in Tuilaepa

**Controlling proposition:** Section 190.3(b), when relevant, permits consideration of the presence or absence of criminal activity involving use or attempted use of force or violence, or an express or implied threat to use force or violence. As construed to concern punishable offenses involving force or threatened force against a person, this is an understandable selection subject and defeats Tuilaepa's presented vagueness challenge; Proctor presents no factor-(b) question.

**Authority:** Part IV of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight directly sustain factor (b) on no-error grounds; Blackmun dissents on this factor as well as the others.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that criminal acts involving violence or threatened violence identify a discernible subject for sentencing judgment. The direction concerns conduct, including the conduct in custody and during trial reported here, rather than only acts preceding the capital offense. Zant permits relevant information about the offender, and Boyde does not require the State to assign a numerical moral value to each proved act. Tuilaepa's jury was instructed to find the acts beyond a reasonable doubt before considering them in aggravation. That reported safeguard addresses whether the conduct occurred; the Court does not convert it into a constitutional burden for every sentencing evaluation. The competing argument correctly distinguishes proof from sentencing weight, but the factor's defined violent subject and the individualized assessment do not become vague merely because weight remains for the jury. The state court's no-request and no-prejudice reasoning about instructions defining particular offenses is a separate matter. The Court resolves no independent elements-instruction claim or categorical objection to all unadjudicated conduct.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983): applied to relevant offender conduct without requiring a uniform assigned weight.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 (1990): applied; individualized sentencing is not mechanical factual counting.

**Qualification:** The trial instruction's proof burden is a record fact, not a new statutory or universal constitutional rule. This holding supplies no finding about an unreported act, no general permission to use unreliable or forbidden evidence, and no factor-(b) ruling in Proctor.

### Age is a relevant contextual subject, not an automatic aggravator

**Controlling proposition:** Section 190.3(i)'s direction to consider, when relevant, the defendant's age at the time of the crime is sufficiently understandable on Tuilaepa's presented challenge and requires no universal numerical weight or exclusive aggravating or mitigating label. The absence of youth does not automatically aggravate the crime, and the possibility of improper argument does not establish a facial defect or a proved as-applied violation; Proctor presents no age-factor question.

**Authority:** Part V of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight adopt the rule and no-error application; Part VI gives the corresponding affirmances without a new penalty hearing.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that age has intelligible but contextual significance in assessing the person who committed the crime. Zant permits relevant individual consideration without demanding that every ambiguous circumstance favor only one side. Lockett and Eddings, as implemented by Graham and Johnson v. Texas, still require a genuine route for youth's independent mitigating significance. California's selection mechanism permits that moral assessment rather than making a finding of deliberateness or future danger the exclusive expression of culpability. The objection that a prosecutor might characterize any possible age as aggravating raises a real boundary, not a reason to presume that every use of the subject is invalid. Absence of youth is not automatically a reason for death. An argument treating every age as independently sufficient aggravation would need examination on its own preserved record. The supplied facts establish no such adjudicated misuse here. The Court therefore affirms on no-error grounds and reaches neither the lower separate harmlessness theories nor a general approval of prosecutorial age arguments.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983): applied; contextual relevance does not require an exclusive label or numerical weight.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 (1978), and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 (1982): preserved as protection for meaningful mitigation.
- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993, and Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: their independent-culpability-mitigation rules remain binding; the different California mechanism supports a different no-error application.

**Limits and remedies:** The Court finds no constitutional error on any presented factor challenge and orders no new penalty hearing. It makes no harmlessness finding and adds no alternative cure holding. If an independent properly presented claim establishes a weighing error, Stringer and Espinosa still require genuine state-authorized reweighing or constitutional harmless-error review with the State carrying Chapman's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden, and a new sentencing proceeding if no lawful cure exists. Nothing here resolves Romano's distinct further-remedy disagreement or alters Simmons's case-specific mandate. Guilt remains outside these sentencing questions.

## Precedent Treatment

The holding-specific treatments govern. Gregg, Woodson and Lowenfield retain the distinct requirements of lawful narrowing and individualized selection. Zant, Lockett, Eddings and Boyde permit these relevant subjects within the stated constraints. Godfrey and Maynard are distinguished; Arave's limited eligibility holding is preserved. Graham and Johnson's actual independent-mitigation requirements, Stringer and Espinosa's weighing-error correction, Victor/Sandoval's contextual instruction review, Romano's responsibility rule and Simmons's rebuttal rule remain in force. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Effective June 30, 1994, the challenged crime-circumstances, violent-criminal-activity and age subjects survive the presented selection challenges. Relevant individualized assessment need not use fixed numerical weights, universally exclusive labels or only factual yes-or-no elements. The ruling is no-error affirmance, not harmless-error approval. Existing mitigation, responsibility, rebuttal and weighing-error safeguards continue independently; no general exemption from constitutional selection review, automatic absence-of-youth aggravation or comprehensive approval of California's scheme follows.

## Separate Writings

Justice Stevens, joined by Justice Ginsburg, concurs in part and in the judgments. They join Parts II–VI and rely on the narrower Zant/Lockett ground: with eligibility unchallenged, the State may identify relevant matters that inform an individual sentencing decision without labeling every ambiguous item exclusively aggravating or mitigating. Identifying those subjects may reduce the risk that prejudice substitutes for individual consideration. They reject authority to rely adversely on constitutionally forbidden or wholly irrelevant matters and preserve effective mitigation. Their votes find no error; they do not use Zant as a harmlessness shortcut for an actually invalid California weighing factor. Their limited joins do not adopt Part I's full framework discussion.

Justice Scalia concurs, joining the Court's opinion while reserving his broader objection to distinctive Eighth Amendment regulation of weighing after lawful eligibility narrowing. His present join accepts the narrow sustaining result and leaves existing selection holdings intact; it does not expand them or make his broader view controlling. Neither Stringer and Espinosa nor Graham and Johnson is overruled by implication. No other Justice joins this separate position, including Justice Thomas.

Justice Souter concurs, joining the Court throughout. Factor (b) is essentially factual in form but sufficiently clear, while the broader crime-circumstances and age subjects can guide individual judgment without becoming yes-or-no eligibility elements. The distinction is functional: nonpropositional phrasing is not immunity from review, and factual phrasing is not itself a defect. The present no-error conclusions preserve meaningful mitigation, sentencing responsibility and review of actual impermissible use. No other Justice joins the additional explanation.

Justice Blackmun dissents from both judgments and from rejection of each challenged factor. His concern is the guidance governing aggravating use on a legally consequential weighing scale. Proof that violent conduct occurred does not determine the principles controlling its weight; understandable references to crime circumstances, violent activity and age can still permit arbitrary selection. His Arave dissent demanded a coherent limiting formulation, and Stringer and Espinosa establish that eligibility does not cure a later weighing defect. He regards California's nonmechanical directions and the violence proof instruction as insufficient answers to that different problem. He does not demand numerical weights, find any unreported act or forbidden argument proved, or announce a general ban on unadjudicated conduct. Independently, he retains his published Romano and Simmons position that capital punishment as presently administered fails to reconcile consistent selection, individualized fairness and reliability. His preferred relief is vacatur of the death-sustaining judgments and lawful noncapital disposition through the state courts, without disturbing guilt. As minimum case-specific relief he accepts vacatur and correction of the factor errors through genuine state-authorized reweighing or constitutional harmless-error review with the State bearing Chapman's burden; a new sentencing proceeding follows if no lawful cure exists. He makes no current harmlessness finding and does not accept another capital proceeding as an answer to his independent broader objection. No other Justice joins his dissent.

## Procedure After Action

The California judgments remain affirmed against the questions decided, with no remand or new penalty hearing on these theories. The decision does not disturb guilt, newly approve the unchallenged eligibility system, foreclose an independently available properly presented claim of actual sentencing error, or order an execution or its date.
