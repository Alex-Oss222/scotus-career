## Event, posture, participation and entering law

Research cutoff: immediately before May 23, 1994. No. 93-609; argued April 26, 1994; certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. First RepublicBank Corp., 997 F.2d 39. O'Connor takes no part; the public source gives no reason. Eight participate at decision, quorum six and majority five. Ginsburg participates. Only Pacific Mutual's final dismissal is this docket; the separate Simmons/TGX facts discussed in the Fifth Circuit's opinion are not adjudicated here.

Pacific Mutual filed in March 1991 over approximately $8 million in 1987 securities transactions. Lampf and Beam, June 20, 1991, led to an August 1991 dismissal with prejudice under the federal one-year/three-year limitations rule. It did not appeal. The dismissal was final before §27A's December 19, 1991 enactment. Its January 31, 1992 reinstatement motion was within sixty days. Section 27A(b)'s conditions are cumulative: commencement on or before June 19, 1991; dismissal as time barred after June 19; timeliness under the jurisdiction's law as it existed June 19, including retroactivity principles; and motion within sixty days of enactment. Subsection (a)'s rule for the specified commenced actions is distinct. The presented question is constitutional power to apply subsection (b), not jurisdiction to review the Fifth Circuit's mandate.

The entering law includes Robertson, Harper, Landgraf, Rivers, Central Bank and Kokkonen. Robertson permits genuine legislative amendment governing pending litigation and leaves the completed-judgment question unresolved. Harper's judicial-retroactivity rule does not itself reopen final cases. Landgraf and Rivers separate express statutory temporal direction from constitutional authority. Central Bank's treatment of the private aiding theory does not itself reopen a judgment or establish liability. Kokkonen requires a legal source for jurisdiction; the existence of a statutory source does not alone establish its constitutionality.

## Stone, assignment and joins

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela votes to reverse. His position, not a holding and not published with the judgment, is that Article III protects the conclusive disposition of a private case by a federal court once its judgment has become final through the ordinary judicial process. Congress may change the law governing cases still pending, but it may not, solely by changing the law previously applicable to a completed private action, require an Article III court to vacate that final judgment and reinstate the same litigation. Finality is itself an Article III protection; it does not disappear because Congress leaves statutory eligibility and the underlying merits for a court to decide, and the independent work a court would do after reinstatement does not restore the completed dismissal itself. Because §27A(b) expressly directs reinstatement, ordinary antiretroactivity construction cannot avoid the constitutional question, and Robertson left the final-judgment question open. He would reverse and remand with directions to deny the §27A(b) motion as applied to Pacific Mutual's completed dismissal, leaving the original judgment undisturbed and deciding no severability question beyond withholding that application. Pending appeals, continuing prospective injunctions and ordinary judicial reopening under valid procedures differ; Congress may not in any event declare a completed judicial finding erroneous, compel a contrary finding or prescribe the liability of identified parties. He decides nothing on §27A(a), the underlying fraud allegations, liability or damages. No fallback vote is used.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas also vote to reverse; Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg vote to affirm. O'Connor takes no part. The Court is equally divided, 4–4. Five votes were required for any disposition other than affirmance, so the Fifth Circuit's judgment is affirmed by an equally divided Court. No opinion issues and no separate writing is filed. There is no assignment, no Opinion of the Court and no lead opinion counted for any Justice. The judgment states the alignment.
