## Current findings

### R08 — Medium — Posters ’N’ Things' tobacco exception is narrowed to sales in the Standards candidate

**Location:** [Standards and Tests candidate](STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.candidate.md), line 1788, “Exceptions and limits.”

The candidate describes the item exception as applying to “an item sold in the normal lawful course of business” that is traditionally intended for tobacco use. The [Posters ’N’ Things Record](../records/Posters_N_Things_Ltd_v_United_States_merits_1994-05-23.md), lines 18 and 44 and its Public Projection at lines 155 and 217, supplies the full alternatives: an item that, in the normal lawful course of business, is **imported, exported, transported, or sold through the mail or by any other means**, and traditionally intended for use with tobacco products, including pipes, papers, and accessories.

The candidate drops importation, exportation, and transportation from the reusable exception. Those alternatives are not supplied elsewhere in the entry. A sale-only formulation materially narrows the exception on the face of the register, even though the entry correctly preserves “traditionally,” rejects an additional “primarily” condition, and separately states the authorized-person exception. The missing activities are distinct from that person-based exception and cannot be supplied by reading the two together.

**Required resolution:** Restore the complete item-exception alternatives and their conditions in the Standards summary. The current Record and its controlling proposition already supply them; no new adjudication is needed. The correction must preserve the independent person exemption and the distinction between traditional tobacco use and primary customer use. No content was changed during this audit.

### R09 — Low — Carter's Standards summary changes a partial-failure qualification into failure of every requirement

**Location:** [Standards and Tests candidate](STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.candidate.md), line 2451, “Current rule.”

The candidate permits reimbursement even if the private placement “fails every requirement” in §1401(a)(18)'s definition. The [Carter Record](../records/Florence_County_School_District_Four_v_Carter_merits_1993-11-09.md), lines 42 and 135, instead says the placement **“does not satisfy every requirement.”** The latter means that satisfaction of all public-program requirements is unnecessary; the former says that every requirement may fail. Those propositions are not equivalent.

The same candidate paragraph correctly requires an otherwise appropriate private education, proof of the public offer's inadequacy, and equitable assessment. Those retained safeguards limit the harm and prevent reading the paragraph as an automatic reimbursement entitlement. They do not make the altered quantifier an accurate statement of the Record's qualification. In particular, the source does not replace the continuing private-appropriateness requirement with permission to fail every element of the public definition.

**Required resolution:** Preserve the Record's “does not satisfy every requirement” qualification, together with the existing independent appropriateness and equity conditions. The Holdings candidate and canonical public rule retain the correct distinction. This is a candidate wording discrepancy, not an error in the affirmance or an instruction to revisit reimbursement.

### R10 — Low — Irvine's Standards reservation introduces an unsupported 1917 cutoff

**Location:** [Standards and Tests candidate](STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.candidate.md), line 3784, “Open question.”

The candidate reserves whether the regulation covers “every pre-1917 inter vivos interest.” The [Irvine Record](../records/United_States_v_Irvine_merits_1994-04-20.md), lines 58, 131, and 160, reserves applicability to **this 1917 inter vivos trust**, created before the 1932 gift-tax enactment. It explains the unresolved regulatory references to taxable pre-1977 transfers and to property transferred from a decedent. Neither the Record nor the actual trust date supplies a pre-1917 legal boundary.

The summary's invented boundary excludes the very 1917 trust whose regulatory coverage remains undecided. It also substitutes a different temporal class for the actual pre-Act reservation. The preceding current-rule paragraph correctly retains conditional applicability, the 1932 distinction, and the independent statutory-transfer ground; the taxability holding and reversal therefore remain intact. The open-question statement is nevertheless inaccurate and incomplete as navigation for later cases.

**Required resolution:** Identify the actual unresolved applicability question for the 1917 pre-Act inter vivos trust, preserving the Record's regulatory qualifications. Do not replace it with a categorical ruling about all pre-1932 interests, a new numerical disclaimer period, or any later §2518 rule.
