## Standards and Tests selection: Effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation

### Effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation

**Governing question:** What instructions govern effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation?

**Current rule:** When record-supported mitigation has material culpability significance that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the charge, evidence and trial as a whole prevent that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Admission and partial relevance to future danger are insufficient if a truthful finding of deliberateness and danger prevents giving effect to youth's independent mitigating force. Disturbed upbringing need not involve a uniquely severe permanent handicap or lack any relation to future danger. The sentencer need not give favorable weight, and mere possibility of a restrictive reading is insufficient. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated this functional requirement before Graham's September 10, 1984 finality; no new Teague exception follows. Adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence can provide correction without disturbing guilt. No general positive-character rule follows from Graham's separate fractured component.

**Authority by component:**

- The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality: [Graham v. Collins — The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/Graham_v_Collins_merits_1993-01-25.md), 1993-01-25. Parts I–III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this prefinality-law holding, its application to youth and background, and the conditional sentencing remedy.
- Youth requires an effective route for its independent mitigating force: [Graham v. Collins — Youth requires an effective route for its independent mitigating force](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/Graham_v_Collins_merits_1993-01-25.md), 1993-01-25. Parts II and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground for sentencing relief and its conditional remedy.
- The submitted background evidence also requires effective consideration: [Graham v. Collins — The submitted background evidence also requires effective consideration](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/Graham_v_Collins_merits_1993-01-25.md), 1993-01-25. ## Standards and Tests selection: Nonemployee organizers' access under a nondiscriminatory exclusion policy

### Nonemployee organizers' access under a nondiscriminatory exclusion policy

**Governing question:** When the Board may balance a nonemployee organizer's need for entry against the employer's property rights.

**Current rule:** Nonemployee organizers must demonstrate that employees are practically beyond reasonable offsite communication before balancing may begin. Greater convenience or improved organizing success does not establish that threshold. The Board may assess actual obstacles through its expertise, but it may not replace the controlling statutory inaccessibility requirement with universal balancing that treats alternatives as merely another factor. The inquiry requires neither organizing success nor exhaustion of every imaginable contact technique. Employee solicitation, discriminatory exclusion, and lawful public-property advocacy remain governed by their distinct rules; genuine isolation by living or working location remains within the inaccessibility inquiry.

**Authority by component:**

- Antecedent inaccessibility, communication-versus-success distinction, and rejection of universal *Jean Country* balancing: [Lechmere v. NLRB — Nonemployee access requires demonstrated inaccessibility before balancing](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Lechmere_v_NLRB_merits_1992-01-27.md), January 27, 1992; Stone's opinion joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** *Lechmere*, January 27, 1992.

## Standards and Tests selection: An agency interpretation necessarily embodied in its decision

### An agency interpretation necessarily embodied in its decision

**Governing question:** Whether an agency's decision contains its own sufficient statutory ground when later counsel states that ground in more explicit words.

**Current rule:** A reviewing court may recognize an interpretation necessarily embodied in the agency's own decision even when the agency did not use the exact words later employed by counsel. The actual decision must disclose that operative premise. This permits recognition of the agency's ground, not judicial invention of a new ground, missing findings or a substitute policy; appellate rationalizations cannot sustain a rationale absent from the agency decision.

**Authority by component:**
- Discernible-agency-ground boundary: [National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp.](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/National_Railroad_Passenger_Corp_v_Boston_and_Maine_Corp_merits_1992-03-25.md), March 25, 1992, holding 2, “The agency's discernible ground”; Kennedy for Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Souter, applying Chenery while preserving State Farm.

**Present operation effective:** National Railroad Passenger Corp., March 25, 1992.

## Standards and Tests selection: Medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change

### Medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change

**Governing question:** What instructions govern medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change?

**Current rule:** Section 1395x(v)(1)(A)(ii) commands suitable retroactive corrective adjustments when aggregate reimbursement for a provider and period proves inadequate or excessive. The Secretary may construe that command as reconciling interim payments to sums payable under lawful reasonable-cost methods and ceilings, without an individualized exception whenever actual costs exceed the ceiling. Authorized exceptions and independent challenges to unlawful methods remain. Interpretive change can reduce weight without automatically invalidating a considered permissible construction; text, delegation and reasons must be examined. Unsupported litigation positions neither replace required agency explanation nor gain automatic controlling weight.

## Standards and Tests selection: Operational assets and the unitary-business limitation on apportioned income

### Operational assets and the unitary-business limitation on apportioned income

**Governing question:** Whether a State may apportion income from an asset held by a nondomiciliary corporation doing business there.

**Current rule:** The State may apportion income connected to the corporation's unitary business but may not include discrete, unrelated investment value merely because the corporation does business in the State or owns the asset. A unitary relationship between payor and recipient is sufficient but unnecessary when the income-producing asset or capital transaction itself serves an operational function in the taxpayer's unitary business, subject to territorial connection and fair apportionment. A general acquisition strategy and intended later use of sale proceeds cannot replace an identifiable operational function where the enterprises lack functional integration, centralized management and economies of scale. A valid taxable base and a fair allocation formula remain separate requirements; the taxpayer's existing clear-and-cogent burden survives.

**Authority by component:**
- Unitary relationship and operational-function routes: [Allied-Signal, Inc. v. Director, Division of Taxation, “Holding 1: The unitary-business limitation and operational-asset route remain governing law”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Allied_Signal_Inc_v_Director_Division_of_Taxation_merits_1992-06-15.md), June 15, 1992; Kennedy, joined by White, Stevens, Scalia and Souter (five).
- Insufficiency of general acquisition strategy and later proceeds use without either connection: Allied-Signal, “Holding 2: The stipulated record requires exclusion of the ASARCO gain,” June 15, 1992; same five Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Allied-Signal, Inc. v. Director, Division of Taxation, June 15, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** No holding makes every state-law category of business income constitutional or reduces the inquiry to investment duration.

**Holdings navigation:** [Allied-Signal, Inc., as successor-in-interest to Bendix Corp. v. Director, Division of Taxation, 504 U.S. 768 (1992) — State-tax apportionment and investment income](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#allied-signal-inc-as-successor-in-interest-to-bendix-corp-v-director-division-of-taxation-504-us-768-1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Container importation exemptions and local lease taxation

### Container importation exemptions and local lease taxation

**Governing question:** What instructions govern container importation exemptions and local lease taxation?

**Current rule:** The 1956 and 1972 Container Conventions prohibit direct or indirect taxes by reason of or in connection with importation of qualifying temporarily admitted containers, not every container transaction. The 1972 definition excludes fees and charges limited to approximate service cost. A nondiscriminatory local transfer-of-possession lease tax is not importation taxation; the conventions, §1322 and regulations neither occupy all container taxation nor preempt a tax that does not obstruct temporary admission. Complete Auto's nexus, apportionment, nondiscrimination and fair-service-relation requirements remain necessary. Foreign-commerce taxation additionally must avoid substantial risk of international multiple taxation and interference with national one-voice authority; same-transaction credits and actual federal arrangements matter, while Executive support informs but does not control. Michelin's uniformity, federal-revenue and interstate-harmony concerns remain distinct. Whether Michelin changed Richfield for direct taxation of goods in transit remains unresolved.

**Authority by component:**

## Standards and Tests selection: Actual receipt and the Interstate Agreement on Detainers trial period

### Actual receipt and the Interstate Agreement on Detainers trial period

**Governing question:** What instructions govern actual receipt and the Interstate Agreement on Detainers trial period?

**Current rule:** For a prisoner serving a term with an untried charge subject to a detainer in another participating State, Article III(a)'s 180-day period begins when both the prosecuting officer and appropriate court actually receive the required written notice and disposition request, not delivery to the custodian alone. Necessary or reasonable continuance is permitted for good cause shown in open court with prisoner or counsel present. Article VI(a) separately tolls time during inability to stand trial as determined by the court with jurisdiction. Article V(c) requires dismissal with prejudice for violation of the applicable period. Prompt forwarding and Article III(c) notice remain duties; the receipt rule supplies no remedy for their breach or automatic grace period.

**Authority by component:**

- Article III(a): actual receipt starts the trial period: [Fex v. Michigan — Article III(a): actual receipt starts the trial period](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/Fex_v_Michigan_merits_1993-02-23.md), 1993-02-23. Scalia's opinion, joined by White, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies six votes for this rule and its application.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-02-23; Fex v. Michigan, for the components identified above.

## Standards and Tests selection: Preserved protected-silence error on federal habeas review

### Preserved protected-silence error on federal habeas review

**Governing question:** What instructions govern preserved protected-silence error on federal habeas review?

**Current rule:** Use of post-Miranda silence to impeach a trial explanation violates Doyle and constitutes constitutional trial error rather than a nonconstitutional rule or structural defect. Permitted prewarning impeachment remains distinct. For preserved Doyle error properly before a federal habeas court, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that it did not contribute to the verdict, considering the whole record. Collateral posture alone does not substitute Kotteakos; relief depends on failure of the State's Chapman showing and is not automatic. Other habeas gates and structural-error rules remain independent; this does not prescribe every collateral-review procedure.

**Authority by component:**

Abrahamson — The protected-silence error remains constitutional trial error](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/brecht_merits_1993-04-21.md), 1993-04-21. ## Standards and Tests selection: Prolonged negligent post-accusation delay and presumed defense impairment

### Prolonged negligent post-accusation delay and presumed defense impairment

**Governing question:** Whether extraordinary governmental negligence after indictment can violate the speedy-trial right without identified trial prejudice, including when the accused was free and unaware of the charge.

**Current rule:** After official accusation, defense impairment remains part of Barker's balance even without incarceration or anxiety. First determine whether delay warrants inquiry; then weigh its duration, reasons, assertion of the right and prejudice together. Sustained governmental negligence weighs less than deliberate obstruction but increasingly against the Government as unjustified delay persists. Extraordinary negligent delay can establish a violation without particularized trial prejudice when the accused did not knowingly acquiesce and the resulting inference of defense impairment is not persuasively rebutted. Possible prejudice alone is insufficient. Once a violation is established, dismissal of the indictment and vacatur of the resulting conviction, rather than sentence adjustment, implement it.

**Authority by component:**

- Scope and the specified negligence/presumed-impairment operation: [Doggett v. United States, “An outstanding indictment protects fair adjudication before arrest” and “Extraordinary negligent delay and unrebutted impairment require dismissal here”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Doggett_v_United_States_merits_1992-06-24.md), June 24, 1992; Souter's opinion joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy.
- Dismissal consequence: the second holding applies Strunk without changing that remedy.

**Present operation effective:** *Doggett*, June 24, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Diligence, justified obstacles, evasion, knowing acquiescence or materially shorter neglect may yield a different balance. No fixed deadline, automatic constitutional-violation presumption, general transfer of the ultimate constitutional burden, preaccusation rule or independent right of repose is adopted.

**Holdings navigation:** [Doggett v. United States, 505 U.S. 647 (1992) — Speedy trial](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#doggett-v-united-states-505-us-647-1992-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: Complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use

### Complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use

**Governing question:** Compensation for a land-use restriction eliminating all economically beneficial use of the relevant property.

**Current rule:** Complete deprivation effects a taking requiring just compensation unless background state property or nuisance law already excluded the prohibited use from the owner's title. After the owner establishes complete deprivation, the State must identify and substantiate a background limitation that independently prohibits the use in the property's circumstances. A new legislative declaration that the use is harmful is insufficient. Apply ordinary adjudicative proof requirements, without a special heightened quantum. Changed knowledge may affect application of genuine nuisance law; legislative labeling cannot substitute for an antecedent title limit. Compensation concerns any actual taking period, not an assumed permanent loss.

**Authority by component:**

- Categorical compensation, background-law exception and allocation: [Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, “Complete economic deprivation requires compensation unless background ownership law already foreclosed the use”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Lucas_v_South_Carolina_Coastal_Council_merits_1992-06-29.md), June 29, 1992; Scalia, Stone, White, O'Connor and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** *Lucas*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Penn Central's contextual analysis remains for partial deprivation. No percentage shortcut, parcel-definition rule, automatic valuation, right to anticipated profits or finding that every environmental restriction is invalid follows. Valid authority to regulate remains distinct from responsibility to compensate.

**Holdings navigation:** [Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, 505 U.S. 1003 (1992) — Complete economic deprivation of land](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#lucas-v-south-carolina-coastal-council-505-us-1003-1992-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: Boundary-preserving conditions for artificial coastal extension

### Boundary-preserving conditions for artificial coastal extension

**Governing question:** The Corps' authority under Rivers and Harbors Act §10, 33 U.S.C. §403, to protect federal submerged-land rights when permitting an artificial coastal extension.

**Current rule:** Section 10 permits consideration of an artificial extension's prospective effect on federal submerged-land rights and permits conditioning approval on preservation of the existing federal-state boundary for additional entitlement attributable to the project. The Submerged Lands Act does not compel unconditional approval or invalidate such a condition when existing state property and claims remain intact. The condition must address the same boundary harm supporting withholding of approval; this holding supplies no power to demand unrelated existing territory or approve different conditions.

**Authority by component:**
- Statutory power, attributable-extension limit and preserved state interests: [United States v. Alaska](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/United_States_v_Alaska_original_summary_judgment_1992-04-21.md), April 21, 1992, “Statutory authority for the boundary-preserving condition,” White's Part II for all nine Justices. The source adopts this construction through the Nome condition's stated limits; it does not grant unconditional approval to all future disclaimers.

**Present operation effective:** Alaska, April 21, 1992.

**Open question:** Natural accretion, compulsory surrender of existing state territory, different permit conditions and a general rule governing States' constitutional takings remedies remain undecided.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Alaska, 503 U.S. 569 (1992) — Federal and state submerged-land boundaries](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-alaska-503-us-569-1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Miranda habeas review and fair litigation of independent coercion claims

### Miranda habeas review and fair litigation of independent coercion claims

**Governing question:** What instructions govern miranda habeas review and fair litigation of independent coercion claims?

**Current rule:** A full and fair state opportunity does not itself bar federal habeas review of a claim that a conviction rests on statements obtained in violation of Miranda; Powell's Fourth Amendment exclusion restriction does not extend to that claim. Ordinary habeas gates, merits and harmlessness remain. A court may not grant relief on an independent postwarning involuntariness theory neither pleaded nor tried by express or implied consent when the State lacked an opportunity to present relevant evidence. Litigation of custody and warnings alone is not consent; later presentation remains subject to pleading, exhaustion, retroactivity and other applicable requirements.

## Standing State selection: Standing Practices

## 3. Standing Practices

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). It ordinarily issues without opinion; a brief per curiam and noted dissents are available. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. #### Wyoming v. Oklahoma, 502 U.S. 437 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 112, Original.
**Decided:** January 22, 1992.

For the separate questions concerning Wyoming's Article III injury and exercise of original jurisdiction, see *Wyoming v. Oklahoma*, January 22, 1992, No. 112, Original, under Federal Courts — Standing and original jurisdiction.

##### Whether Oklahoma's ten-percent in-state-coal requirement is lawful discrimination against interstate commerce

**Holding:** Oklahoma's express in-state-coal quota discriminates against interstate commerce and cannot stand unless the State establishes a legitimate local purpose that cannot adequately be served by reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives; it has not done so here. The Federal Power Act's general preservation of state utility regulation does not clearly authorize this geographic discrimination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices support this proposition. Scalia and Thomas cast no merits vote.

**Material application:** The Act requires covered public and private coal-fired utilities to obtain at least ten percent of coal heat input from Oklahoma coal. The small reserved share does not cure facial origin discrimination. The record does not show that an origin quota is necessary to secure reliable energy supplies when reasonable neutral diversification measures are available; retaining benefits for the local coal industry is not a sufficient justification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neutral energy regulation remains available; the evenhanded-regulation balance does not govern this express origin quota.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Summary judgment is entered for Wyoming, Oklahoma's exceptions are overruled, and the Act is declared unconstitutional. From January 22, 1992, Oklahoma and its officials are enjoined from enforcing the ten-percent requirement against covered utilities. No tax damages are awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Philadelphia v. New Jersey*, 437 U.S. 617 — applied to invalidate facial geographic discrimination regardless of the favored market share's size.
- *Hughes v. Oklahoma*, 441 U.S. 322 — applied for legitimate purpose and the absence of reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives.
- *New Energy Co. of Indiana v. Limbach*, 486 U.S. 269, and *Bacchus Imports, Ltd. v. Dias*, 468 U.S. 263 — local commercial advantage does not justify discrimination.
- *New England Power Co. v. New Hampshire*, 455 U.S. 331 — general federal permission to regulate does not clearly authorize discriminatory restrictions.
- *Pike v. Bruce Church, Inc.*, 397 U.S. 137 — its evenhanded-regulation balance is not the governing branch for this express origin quota.
- *Maine v. Taylor*, 477 U.S. 131 — a proved need without reasonable neutral alternatives may justify discrimination; that showing is absent here.

##### Whether the integrated Act can be severed to retain only its Grand River Dam Authority application

**Holding:** The Act's integrated purchasing mandate cannot be preserved solely for the state-owned Grand River Dam Authority without an adequate state-law basis for severing applications and retaining that different legislative scheme. The Court invalidates the enacted regulatory program while leaving the validity of an independently adopted state procurement policy open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices support this proposition.

**Material application:** The Act's severability language concerning parts does not adequately establish legislative intent to remove all private applications and preserve a different GRDA-only scheme. State ownership of one regulated utility does not establish that an independent procurement policy was enacted or would be retained alone.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The market-participant doctrine is preserved. The validity of a separately adopted state procurement preference is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Wyoming's severability exception is sustained. The injunction covers the enacted quota as a whole, including the proposed GRDA-only application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Reeves, Inc. v. Stake*, 447 U.S. 429, and *Hughes v. Alexandria Scrap Corp.*, 426 U.S. 794 — the market-participant doctrine is preserved; no independent procurement policy is adjudicated.
- *South-Central Timber Development, Inc. v. Wunnicke*, 467 U.S. 82 — distinguishes market participation from regulatory restrictions beyond the relevant participation.
- *Dorchy v. Kansas*, 264 U.S. 286, and *Champlin Refining Co. v. Corporation Commission*, 286 U.S. 210 — severability depends on legislative intent and a workable retained enactment, not judicial preference for a substitute policy.

#### Chemical Waste Management, Inc. v. Hunt, 504 U.S. 334 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-471.
**Decided:** June 1, 1992.

##### Whether Alabama may impose an additional disposal charge on equivalent hazardous waste because it originated outside the State

**Holding:** A State may not impose an additional disposal charge on out-of-state hazardous waste that it does not impose on equivalent in-state waste unless it establishes a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve. Alabama's $72-per-ton additional charge fails because the record supplies no origin-linked difference in the relevant risks or costs and adequate evenhanded means remain available; commercial hazardous-waste disposal receives Commerce Clause protection despite the material's dangers.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine adopt the rule, application and remedial limits. Stone's additional compensatory-tax observation is not part of the Court's holding.

**Material application:** The accepted long-term disposal, transportation, monitoring and capacity concerns do not establish that an equivalent imported ton is more dangerous or costly than a domestic ton. Evenhanded charges, total-volume limits and direct safety regulation address those mechanisms; these examples expose the missing justification rather than require a replacement program. Allowing the same waste upon payment of an extra fee does not establish a quarantine justification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The grant concerns the additional fee alone. The base fee, independent volume cap, other permitting provisions, state constitutional claims and other federal challenges are not adjudicated. The State did not press its fair-share or compensatory-tax theory; amici's congressional-authorization theory was neither decided below nor briefed or argued by the parties. No unlimited right to dispose of hazardous waste, new proof quantum, perfect-certainty requirement or least-imaginable-alternative rule is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Alabama Supreme Court's judgment sustaining the additional fee and remand for appropriate state relief from the discriminatory exaction, consistently with *McKesson* where its coerced-payment and postpayment-review prerequisites apply. Neither those prerequisites nor an automatic refund entitlement or amount is determined. The independent base-fee, cap and other rulings outside the grant remain undisturbed; no replacement fee, operating permit or compulsory acceptance requirement issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Philadelphia v. New Jersey*, 437 U.S. 617, applies Commerce protection and nondiscrimination to hazardous-waste transactions. *Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission*, 432 U.S. 333, preserves the State's purpose-and-alternatives burden. *Maine v. Taylor*, 477 U.S. 131, is distinguished because this record lacks distinctive imported risks and unavailable adequate alternatives. *New Energy Co. of Indiana v. Limbach*, 486 U.S. 269, applies to discriminatory charges defended on environmental grounds; complete exclusion is unnecessary. *Wyoming v. Oklahoma*, January 22, 1992, supplies the express-geographic-discrimination rule without extending its other holdings. *Pike v. Bruce Church, Inc.*, 397 U.S. 137, governs a separate evenhanded-regulation branch. *McKesson Corp. v. Division of Alcoholic Beverages and Tobacco*, 496 U.S. 18, supplies the conditional state-remedy principles without fixing a refund.

#### Fort Gratiot Sanitary Landfill, Inc. v. Michigan Department of Natural Resources, 504 U.S. 353 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-636.
**Decided:** June 1, 1992.

##### Whether county-origin restrictions at private landfills escape Commerce Clause scrutiny because they also exclude other intrastate counties

**Holding:** A State's prohibition on a private landfill's acceptance of outside-county waste unless a county plan affirmatively authorizes it discriminates against interstate commerce even when it also excludes other counties within that State and some counties admit imports. The restriction cannot stand unless the State establishes a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve; Michigan has not done so for these Waste Import Restrictions, and planning waste services does not convert regulation of a private facility into market participation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt the same rule, application and limits. Blackmun's proposed evidentiary remand supplies no controlling join.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the restrictions on privately owned and operated facilities.

**Material application:** The challenged veto gives local waste access that equivalent outside waste lacks. Neutral quantity limits, appropriate charges and facility standards can address the planning concerns; respondents identify no distinct imported hazard or inadequacy of those alternatives and neither identify further necessary proof nor request an evidentiary remand. The alternatives illustrate the failed justification, rather than require a replacement program. No statewide total exclusion or disadvantage to every outside supplier is required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Only the 1988 Waste Import Restrictions in §§299.413a and 299.430(2), as applied to private landfills, are invalidated. The earlier planning statute, public-facility practices, neutral health, safety and capacity rules, different waste-risk classifications, unconditional operation and genuinely applicable congressional authorization remain outside the decision. No hazardous-waste question arises. Distinct due-process, taking and ownership-transfer licensing matters are not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit's judgment sustaining dismissal and remand through that court for appropriately tailored declaratory and prospective relief consistent with invalidation. Independent neutral planning and permitting remain; the judgment requires neither unconditional permit issuance, acceptance of particular waste nor operation beyond neutral capacity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Philadelphia v. New Jersey*, 437 U.S. 617, applies to origin-only exclusion at private waste facilities. *Brimmer v. Rebman*, 138 U.S. 78, and *Dean Milk Co. v. Madison*, 340 U.S. 349, prevent a substate boundary and exclusion of some intrastate commerce from curing geographic discrimination. *New Energy Co. of Indiana v. Limbach*, 486 U.S. 269, and *Wyoming v. Oklahoma*, January 22, 1992, distinguish the existence of discrimination from its extent. *Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission*, 432 U.S. 333, preserves the purpose-and-alternatives burden. *Maine v. Taylor*, 477 U.S. 131, is distinguished for lack of a distinctive imported hazard and unavailable adequate alternatives. *Hughes v. Alexandria Scrap Corp.*, 426 U.S. 794, and *Reeves, Inc. v. Stake*, 447 U.S. 429, preserve market participation while leaving private-facility regulation outside it. *Pike v. Bruce Church, Inc.*, 397 U.S. 137, remains the separate rule for evenhanded regulation.

#### Allied-Signal, Inc., as successor-in-interest to Bendix Corp. v. Director, Division of Taxation, 504 U.S. 768 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-615.
**Decided:** June 15, 1992.

##### Whether the unitary-business limitation remains controlling and permits taxation of genuine operational assets without a unitary payor and recipient

**Holding:** A State taxing a nondomiciliary corporation may apportion income connected to its unitary business, but may not include discrete, unrelated investment value merely because the corporation does business in the State or owns the asset. A unitary relationship between income payor and recipient is sufficient but not necessary: an income-producing asset or capital transaction serving an operational function in the taxpayer's unitary business may qualify without that relationship, subject to the required territorial connection and fair apportionment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Stevens, Scalia and Souter; five Justices adopt the territorial limitation and operational-function qualification. The opinion independently controls without aggregating separate writings.

**Material application:** The existing boundary remains workable despite fact-sensitive applications, and States and businesses have organized tax systems and investments around it. A corporation's general profit purpose or common ownership cannot substitute for an operational connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A valid tax base and a fair apportionment formula are separate requirements. The Court does not constitutionalize every state-law definition of business income or make investment duration decisive. Because ASARCO and Woolworth are retained, the conditional reargument questions about retroactivity and replacement principles are not reached.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The ASARCO gain must be excluded for the reasons in the following application holding; state courts determine calculations and appropriate refund treatment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mobil Oil Corp. v. Commissioner of Taxes, 445 U.S. 425 (1980): followed for the minimum connection, rational relationship, and unitary-business framework; a separate enterprise can remain outside the base.
- ASARCO Inc. v. Idaho Tax Commission, 458 U.S. 307 (June 29, 1982): reaffirmed; generalized corporate profit purpose does not establish a unitary connection, and capital gains receive the same constitutional treatment as dividends.
- F. W. Woolworth Co. v. Taxation and Revenue Department, 458 U.S. 354 (1982): reaffirmed; functional integration, centralized management and economies of scale remain practical indicia, while investor oversight alone is insufficient.
- Container Corp. of America v. Franchise Tax Board, 463 U.S. 159 (1983): followed and clarified; concrete operational value can support apportionment without a flow of goods or a unitary payor/payee relationship, but passive investment is insufficient.

##### Whether Bendix established that its ASARCO capital gain falls outside the apportionable tax base

**Holding:** Where the stipulated facts establish unrelated enterprises without functional integration, centralized management or economies of scale, and do not show that the stock itself performed an identifiable operational function in the taxpayer's unitary business, a general acquisition strategy and intended later use of sale proceeds cannot justify apportioning the capital gain. Bendix has met its burden on this record; New Jersey must exclude the approximately $211.5 million ASARCO gain from the challenged apportionable tax base.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Stevens, Scalia and Souter; five Justices adopt the application and remand. Stone separately supports reversal; Blackmun, O'Connor and Thomas dissent, making the judgment 6–3.

**Material application:** The stipulated absence of shared operational management, services, facilities or financing, and the merely minor arm's-length dealings, negate integration. Two noncontrolling board seats do not show centralized management. The record identifies no working-capital use or other operational role for the shares; intended later use of sale proceeds is insufficient.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Container's clear-and-cogent taxpayer burden and review for use of the correct legal standard remain unchanged. Genuine operational investments and fair-apportionment challenges to a proper base remain open. UGC gain, related interest and separate allocation-factor issues decided below are outside this proceeding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The New Jersey judgment is reversed only as to the ASARCO gain and remanded for its exclusion, calculations and appropriate refund treatment. No refund amount or replacement allocation factor is fixed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- ASARCO Inc. v. Idaho Tax Commission, 458 U.S. 307 (1982): applied to reject the business-purpose rationale and to treat capital gain under the same nexus rule as dividend income.
- F. W. Woolworth Co. v. Taxation and Revenue Department, 458 U.S. 354 (1982): applied; no actual integration is shown, and two noncontrolling board seats do not establish centralized operational management.
- Container Corp. of America v. Franchise Tax Board, 463 U.S. 159 (1983): distinguished on the facts; its operational finance and services are absent. Its taxpayer burden and review for use of the correct legal standard remain governing; duration alone is not made a constitutional test.

#### Itel Containers International Corp. v. Huddleston, 507 U.S. 60 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-321.

**Decided:** February 23, 1993.

##### The Container Conventions exempt importation charges, not every container transaction

**Holding:** The 1956 and 1972 Container Conventions prohibit direct or indirect taxes imposed by reason of, or in connection with, importation of qualifying temporarily admitted containers, rather than every tax involving those containers. Tennessee's nondiscriminatory tax on the transfer of possession under a local lease is not such an importation charge; the 1972 definition also excludes fees and charges limited to the approximate cost of services rendered.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's treaty portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas: eight Justices adopt this construction and application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Air France v. Saks, 470 U.S.392 (1985): applied for treaty interpretation beginning with text while considering relevant context.
- Factor v. Laubenheimer, 290 U.S.276 (1933): applied for consideration of treaty parties' practical construction; the practice asserted here does not displace the operative importation limitation.

##### Federal container regulation does not preempt this local lease tax

**Holding:** The Container Conventions, 19 U.S.C.1322 and their implementing regulations do not occupy all container taxation or preempt a nondiscriminatory local lease tax that neither taxes importation nor obstructs the federal temporary-admission policy. Tennessee's tax therefore survives both the asserted field-preemption and obstacle-preemption grounds; the distinct federal rules governing bonded warehouses do not confer the same exemption on these lease transactions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's regulatory-preemption portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas: eight Justices adopt the stated rejection of both grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hines v. Davidowitz, 312 U.S.52 (1941): applied for conflict with federal purposes; no such obstacle established here.
- McGoldrick v. Gulf Oil Corp., 309 U.S.414 (1940): distinguished because the bonded-warehouse benefit and supervision differ from container leasing.
- Xerox Corp. v. County of Harris, 459 U.S.145 (1982): distinguished on the same specific federal warehouse purpose; its protection remains.
- R. J. Reynolds Tobacco Co. v. Durham County, 479 U.S.130 (1986): applied against inferring complete field occupation from detailed customs regulation.

##### The local lease tax satisfies the additional foreign-commerce requirements

**Holding:** In addition to Complete Auto's substantial nexus, fair apportionment, nondiscrimination and fair relation to state services, a tax affecting foreign commerce must avoid a substantial risk of international multiple taxation and interference with the Nation's ability to speak with one voice. With Complete Auto compliance conceded here, Tennessee's discrete local transaction and credit for tax properly paid to another domestic or foreign jurisdiction on that same transaction, together with the demonstrated federal arrangement, satisfy those additional requirements; Executive support informs but does not decide their application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's foreign-commerce portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Thomas: seven Justices adopt this rule and application. Scalia agrees only in the judgment on this issue; Blackmun dissents.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Complete Auto Transit, Inc. v. Brady, 430 U.S.274 (1977): its four requirements remain mandatory; their satisfaction is conceded in this case.
- Japan Line, Ltd. v. County of Los Angeles, 441 U.S.434 (1979): retained and distinguished; foreign-owned property taxed fully abroad presented a different multiple-taxation and uniformity problem.
- Container Corp. of America v. Franchise Tax Board, 463 U.S.159 (1983): applied to distinguish inevitable duplication from potential foreign taxation and to assess foreign-policy evidence.
- Wardair Canada Inc. v. Florida Department of Revenue, 477 U.S.1 (1986): applied for the significance of a discrete local transaction and the actual federal arrangement.

##### A local lease transaction is not the import or export taxed in Richfield

**Holding:** Under Michelin, this nondiscriminatory local lease tax does not offend national commercial uniformity, divert federal import revenue or disrupt interstate harmony, and therefore does not violate the Import-Export Clause. The tax attaches to temporary possession transferred in Tennessee, rather than to the containers or their cargo as imports or exports in transit; the Court leaves unresolved whether Michelin otherwise altered Richfield's rule for direct taxation of goods in transit.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Import-Export portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Thomas: seven Justices adopt this rule and application. Scalia concurs only in this component's judgment on a distinct export-status ground; Blackmun does not supply a separate Import-Export disposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Michelin Tire Corp. v. Wages, 423 U.S.276 (1976): applied through its three constitutional concerns.
- Department of Revenue v. Association of Washington Stevedoring Cos., 435 U.S.734 (1978): applied for distinct local services and the interstate-harmony inquiry.
- Richfield Oil Corp. v. State Board of Equalization, 329 U.S.69 (1946): distinguished; its direct-export rule is not overruled or finally reconciled with every implication of Michelin.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The temporary-admission regime requires reexportation within three months unless customs authorities extend it. Domestic ownership does not remove Japan Line review; Executive support is informative, not dispositive. Complete Auto compliance is conceded rather than independently adjudicated. No general apportionment exemption, field occupation, express-conflict-only rule or overruling of Richfield follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of the treaty, preemption and constitutional challenges to this nondiscriminatory Tennessee lease tax. The constitutional analyses retain the distinct proposition coalitions stated above.

#### Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, 505 U.S. 1003 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-453.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the compensation rule, see Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, June 29, 1992, No. 91-453, under Property and Economic Rights — Complete economic deprivation of land.

##### The original categorical prohibition presents a reviewable earlier-period claim

**Holding:** A later discretionary permit procedure does not bar review of compensation for an earlier categorical building prohibition where permission under that prohibition was unavailable and the state supreme court finally rejected the compensation claim on its merits. Any compensable taking must be remedied for its actual duration; the later procedure and unresolved factual issues remain relevant to future use, duration and valuation, and the permanent-value award is not automatically reinstated.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor and Thomas, controls with five votes. Kennedy separately concurs in the judgment and agrees that the earlier interval is reviewable, but does not join the Court's writing. The holding rests on the express five-Justice opinion without combining separate rationales.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the no-compensation judgment and remand to the Supreme Court of South Carolina. The new permit avenue, remaining defenses, extent and duration of loss, and valuation remain for further proceedings; the permanent-value award is not automatically restored.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Williamson County Regional Planning Commission v. Hamilton Bank, 473 U.S. 172 (1985): preserves ordinary finality requirements; distinguishes an absolute prohibition for an already elapsed period.
- MacDonald, Sommer & Frates v. Yolo County, 477 U.S. 340 (1986): preserves the need to determine permitted development; an unavailable original permit need not be sought to test the earlier prohibition.
- First English Evangelical Lutheran Church v. County of Los Angeles, 482 U.S. 304 (1987): applies compensation for an actual temporary taking; does not decide whether this regulation effected one or set the amount.

#### Yee v. City of Escondido, 503 U.S. 519 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1947.
**Decided:** April 1, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *Yee v. City of Escondido*, April 1, 1992, No. 90-1947: whether restrictions on voluntarily created pad tenancies compel permanent physical occupation (Property and Economic Rights — Physical takings and voluntary tenancies).

##### Whether the unpreserved substantive-due-process claim is reviewable

**Holding:** The Court declines a substantive due-process claim neither properly raised nor decided in the state courts; presentation only in a denied request for discretionary review does not supply the missing state-court decision. The Court need not decide here whether this review limitation is jurisdictional or prudential.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the California judgment rejecting the physical-taking claim; award no compensation on that theory. Decline the due-process and regulatory-taking merits without deciding their validity; any properly available regulatory or as-applied claim requires an appropriate proceeding, not a new merits remand here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Illinois v. Gates, 462 U.S. 213 — applied: an unpresented state-court claim is not decided on review; the jurisdictional/prudential classification remains open.
- People v. Triggs, 8 Cal.3d 884 — applied only to the effect of denied California discretionary review, which expresses no merits determination.

##### Whether a new taking argument and this facial theory satisfy preservation and ripeness

**Holding:** A party that properly presents a federal taking claim below may offer a different supporting argument on review, subject to the separate limitation imposed by the question presented. Under the then-governing standards, this facial allegation that the ordinance never substantially advances a legitimate state interest is ripe without a rent-increase request because it does not depend on the particular property’s economic loss or compensation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-B–C of O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; six Justices adopt this procedural proposition. Stone does not join this rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding uses the then-governing facial substantial-advancement theory and does not decide regulatory-taking merits or override the separate question-presented limit.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the California judgment rejecting the physical-taking claim; award no compensation on that theory. Decline the due-process and regulatory-taking merits without deciding their validity; any properly available regulatory or as-applied claim requires an appropriate proceeding, not a new merits remand here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bankers Life & Casualty Co. v. Crenshaw, 486 U.S. 71 — applied: new arguments may support a preserved federal claim.
- Illinois v. Gates, 462 U.S. 213 — applied: argument and claim preservation are distinct.
- Williamson County Regional Planning Commission v. Hamilton Bank, 473 U.S. 172 — distinguished: the asserted facial theory does not require a property-specific rent determination.
- Agins v. Tiburon, 447 U.S. 255 — applied only to the then-current facial-ripeness proposition; no merits determination follows.
- Keystone Bituminous Coal Assn. v. DeBenedictis, 480 U.S. 470 — applied to distinguish facial allegations from property-specific application.

##### Whether the physical-taking question fairly includes regulatory-taking merits

**Holding:** Under Rule 14.1(a), a question asking whether the ordinance causes a physical taking does not fairly include the related but independent question whether it causes a regulatory taking. No exceptional urgency or economy justifies departing from that prudential limit here, so the regulatory merits remain undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor, II-D, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: seven votes; this section adopts no regulatory merits or ripeness conclusion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Preserving a general taking claim does not enlarge the grant. Physical and regulatory taking questions are related but not subsidiary to one another, and no exceptional urgency or economy warrants enlargement here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the California judgment rejecting the physical-taking claim; award no compensation on that theory. Decline the due-process and regulatory-taking merits without deciding their validity; any properly available regulatory or as-applied claim requires an appropriate proceeding, not a new merits remand here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Stone v. Powell, 428 U.S. 465 — applied: departure from the question-presented rule remains exceptional.
- Lytle v. Household Manufacturing, Inc., 494 U.S. 545 — applied: developed arguments and lower-court consideration support disciplined review.
- American National Bank & Trust Co. v. Haroco, Inc., 473 U.S. 606 — applied by analogy: related questions are not necessarily fairly included within one another.

#### Wyoming v. Oklahoma, 502 U.S. 437 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 112, Original.
**Decided:** January 22, 1992.

For the separate questions whether the coal quota violates the Commerce Clause and whether it can survive solely as a public-utility purchasing directive, see *Wyoming v. Oklahoma*, January 22, 1992, No. 112, Original, under Constitutional Structure — Interstate commerce.

##### Whether Wyoming's severance-tax loss establishes its own Article III injury

**Holding:** A documented loss of severance-tax revenue caused by a mandatory substitution away from the State's taxed coal establishes Wyoming's own Article III injury where the record supports causation and an injunction would remove the challenged purchasing mandate. Redressability does not require a guarantee that every displaced purchase will return, but generalized hopes of future tax growth do not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices support this proposition.

**Material application:** The purchasing record, the extraction tax, and evidence of available production capacity support an inference of lost severance receipts from compelled substitution away from Wyoming coal. The fiscal inference is supported on the summary-judgment record; it is not itself a stipulated fact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary injury, causation and redressability requirements apply. No sovereign exemption or standing based on unrelated future fiscal effects is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Maryland v. Louisiana*, 451 U.S. 725 — applied to a State's demonstrated injury from another State's commercial restriction.
- *Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission*, 432 U.S. 333 — geographic market exclusion is a concrete commercial injury; Wyoming still proves its own fiscal stake.
- *Warth v. Seldin*, 422 U.S. 490, and *Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization*, 426 U.S. 26 — ordinary personal-injury and causation requirements are preserved, not relaxed for States.

##### Whether this interstate fiscal controversy warrants exercise of original jurisdiction

**Holding:** The Court exercises original jurisdiction over this substantial interstate controversy because Wyoming's own fiscal injury and the challenged statewide mandate warrant direct resolution and no equivalent pending private action would fully resolve the State's claim. Exclusive jurisdiction under §1251(a) does not eliminate the Court's established discretion in administering its original docket.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices support this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The possibility of a producer's or utility's suit does not require surrender of this distinct state injury, but the holding does not promise an original forum for every indirect effect on tax receipts. Acceptance of the bill does not bar jurisdictional reconsideration on the developed record.

**Material judgment:** Original jurisdiction is exercised by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter. Scalia and Thomas would dismiss and do not reach the Commerce Clause merits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

#### Withrow v. Williams, 507 U.S. 680 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1030.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### A fair state opportunity does not itself bar Miranda habeas review

**Holding:** Powell’s prudential restriction on Fourth Amendment exclusionary claims does not extend to a state prisoner’s claim that the conviction rests on statements obtained in violation of Miranda. A full and fair state opportunity therefore does not itself foreclose that claim; ordinary habeas requirements, merits analysis and the governing harmless-error inquiry remain necessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, I–II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Powell, 428 U.S. 465 — distinguished and not extended; its actual prudential scope remains.
- Miranda, 384 U.S. 436 — preserved on habeas for a conviction resting on improperly obtained statements.
- Kimmelman, 477 U.S. 365; Jackson, 443 U.S. 307; Rose v. Mitchell, 443 U.S. 545 — applied as limits on extending Powell to distinct trial protections.
- Miller v. Fenton, 474 U.S. 104, and Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 — preserved: independent voluntariness review remains available on its own predicates.

##### An unpleaded, unconsented coercion theory requires fair litigation

**Holding:** A habeas court may not grant relief on the independent postwarning involuntariness theory presented here when it was neither pleaded nor tried by express or implied consent and the State lacked an opportunity to present relevant evidence. Litigation of Miranda custody and warning issues does not by itself supply consent; any later presentation remains subject to ordinary pleading, exhaustion, retroactivity and other applicable requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 — preserved: actual police coercion is necessary to due-process involuntariness; absent warnings alone do not establish it.
- Miller v. Fenton, 474 U.S. 104 — preserved: voluntariness requires its own legal determination on the proper record.
- Teague, 489 U.S. 288 — reserved here; no new exception or merits resolution of the retroactivity objection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Miranda and due-process involuntariness are independent inquiries. Rule 15(b) consent is absent on this record; custody/warning litigation alone does not provide it. Coercion, exhaustion and Teague questions remain for any lawfully presented claim. No automatic writ, universal prophylactic-safeguard review rule or reopening of Powell-barred search claims follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm availability of Miranda review; reverse the independent postwarning voluntariness ruling for lack of pleading, consent and fair opportunity to litigate; remand for proper merits and remedial analysis.

#### United States v. Williams, 504 U.S. 36 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1972.
**Decided:** May 4, 1992.

For the other questions in *United States v. Williams*, May 4, 1992, No. 90-1972, see Federal Courts — Preservation and questions passed upon below (reviewability) and Criminal Procedure — Grand-jury presentation and supervisory dismissal (disclosure duty and dismissal authority).

##### What known evidence a federal prosecutor must disclose to avoid materially distorting a grand-jury presentation

**Holding:** Before seeking an indictment, a federal prosecutor must present or otherwise disclose to the grand jury personally known, substantial evidence directly negating the charged offense when its omission would materially distort the grand-jury presentation. This duty requires neither an investigative search for undiscovered favorable evidence nor presentation of every possible defense; a violation alone does not establish entitlement to dismissal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Parts II and III, joined by Blackmun, O’Connor and Thomas, adopt the personally-known/direct-negation duty. Stone’s separate merits opinion adopts that duty where omission materially distorts the presentation. Under Marks, these five judgment supporters establish the stated common requirement; the four-Justice formulation does not remove Stone’s material-distortion qualification from the controlling rule.

**Material application:** The duty concerns substantial evidence personally known to the prosecutor, not an investigative search or a preliminary trial of every defense. Misconduct and charging prejudice are separate inquiries.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The grant concerns authority, not the accounting documents’ specific character. Affirmance does not establish that every intent-related item meets the duty or that the prosecutor fabricated evidence. Negligent discovery, state-grand-jury duties, constitutional disclosure, immunity and fabrication claims remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit’s dismissal without prejudice is affirmed, 5–4: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor and Thomas. A lawful new indictment remains available; no acquittal, dismissal with prejudice or reindictment command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Calandra, 414 U.S. 338 — its protective function and enforceable process limits support the confined duty; ordinary evidentiary freedom remains.
- Bank of Nova Scotia v. United States, 487 U.S. 250 — its misconduct and prejudice inquiries remain separate; violation does not automatically warrant dismissal.
- Costello v. United States, 350 U.S. 359 — preserved for competence and sufficiency challenges to an otherwise lawful presentation; distinguished from the specified prosecutorial omission.
- Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 — applied to the judgment supporters’ shared direct-negation duty with the material-distortion qualification retained.

##### When a materially misleading omission permits supervisory dismissal without prejudice

**Holding:** Federal supervisory authority permits dismissal without prejudice when a prosecutor withholds personally known, substantial evidence directly negating the charged offense, the omission materially distorts the presentation, the evidence would plainly preclude a finding of probable cause, and the violation substantially influenced the indictment or leaves grave doubt that the indictment was free from that influence. This establishes the stated sufficient ground for dismissal; it requires neither a search for undiscovered favorable evidence nor presentation of every potential defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s merits Parts II and III, joined by Blackmun, O’Connor and Thomas, supply the narrower judgment-supporting rule. Stone concurs in affirmance under the broader material-distortion formulation, covering known evidence substantially undermining an essential element with established Bank of Nova Scotia prejudice. Under Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188, the four-Justice rule is the controlling logical subset: every known, directly negating omission that plainly defeats probable cause, materially distorts the presentation and causes the stated prejudice also satisfies Stone’s broader rule. The five judgment supporters together establish its force. Stone is not represented as formally joining the narrower qualification. The four-Justice and broader one-Justice applications are not combined to remove that qualification from the sufficient ground recognized here. Neither the plurality nor the five judgment supporters adopt an exclusive rule forbidding relief in every other circumstance.

**Material application:** The narrower rule adds evidence plainly precluding probable cause to the shared known-evidence, direct-negation and material-distortion requirements, and independently requires Bank of Nova Scotia prejudice. The Government presented a categorical authority objection rather than renewed review of the particular evidence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is a sufficient dismissal class, not an exclusive prohibition on relief outside it. The plurality does not find the accounting documents plainly defeat probable cause; the evidentiary application was outside the grant. Stone’s broader application does not become general law. Guilt, negligent discovery, state grand juries and constitutional disclosure remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit’s dismissal without prejudice is affirmed, 5–4: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor and Thomas. A lawful new indictment remains available; no acquittal, dismissal with prejudice or reindictment command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bank of Nova Scotia v. United States, 487 U.S. 250 — its prejudice requirement is retained; limited supervisory power is extended to the specified known-evidence omission. Its evidence-reliability prohibition survives for lawful presentations.
- Costello v. United States, 350 U.S. 359 — distinguished: the confined misconduct inquiry is not a challenge to the competence or sufficiency of evidence in an otherwise lawful presentation. Its hearsay and ordinary adequacy rules remain.
- United States v. Calandra, 414 U.S. 338 — preserved for grand-jury independence and freedom from trial evidence rules; no exclusionary-rule extension occurs.
- United States v. Mechanik, 475 U.S. 66 — its completed-trial harmlessness holding is not extended to erase a prejudicial violation challenged and resolved before trial; the charging-prejudice formulation adopted in Bank remains operative.
- Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83 — not transplanted into grand-jury proceedings; its trial disclosure duty remains unchanged.
- Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 — applied to the genuine logical subset of the five judgment supporters’ rules; the shared authority supports the sufficient dismissal ground, without creating a single majority merits opinion or an exclusive boundary on other relief.
- United States v. Page, 808 F.2d 723 — the judgment applying it in Williams is sustained against the categorical no-authority challenge; Page’s full disclosure formulation and any rule requiring every fact relevant to a possible defense are not adopted.

#### Graham v. Collins, 506 U.S. 461 (1993)

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality

**Holding:** Teague does not bar Graham's claim that Texas's submitted sentencing questions prevented effective use of his youth and unstable background as material mitigation independent of deliberateness or future danger. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated that functional requirement before his conviction became final on September 10, 1984; no new procedural-rule exception is created.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — preserved as qualified approval; it does not immunize every application of the special issues.

##### Youth requires an effective route for its independent mitigating force

**Holding:** When the record supports a material mitigating judgment about culpability that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the submitted mechanism prevents that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Graham's youth at seventeen has that independent significance notwithstanding deliberate conduct or predicted future danger, and the three submitted special issues provide no adequate route for it here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground for sentencing relief and its conditional remedy.

- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied beyond a permanent-handicap limitation; a mitigating trait need not have only aggravating relevance to future danger to require an effective vehicle.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 — applied as the reasonable-likelihood method of assessing instructional operation, not as the source of a new substantive rule at 1984 finality.

##### The submitted background evidence also requires effective consideration

**Independent alternative holding:** A capital sentencing system may not prevent a jury from acting on the material culpability significance it finds in a defendant's disturbed upbringing merely because that evidence also bears on future dangerousness or lacks a uniquely severe permanent handicap. The evidence of Graham's mother's recurring mental illness and hospitalizations and his shifting family care supports such a mitigating judgment, which the submitted special issues cannot adequately express; this independently requires the same sentencing remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Parts III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independent background-evidence ground and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 — applied to relevant background proffered as a basis for less than death.
- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied; disturbed upbringing cannot be excluded from mitigating effect as a matter of law.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied; neither a uniquely severe permanent handicap nor complete absence of relevance to the special issues is an exclusive trigger.

##### Positive character as an independent ground

**Authority of the judgment:** The separate contention that Graham's household assistance, religious observance and financial support of his children independently require additional sentencing relief is rejected by all nine Justices, on different grounds. Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter regard its asserted independent culpability significance as insubstantial on this record and its future-behavior use as available under the second issue; White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would bar the requested mitigation relief under Teague. Stone-Zsela joins the denial of this independent component in the judgment only. These grounds do not yield a common general rule or a Marks subset; the youth and background holdings independently require sentencing relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule about positive character evidence or its value apart from the asserted culpability and future-danger uses is decided. The jury remains free to hear constitutionally relevant evidence at the new sentencing proceeding. Neither denial of this independent ground nor the sentencing remedy disturbs the murder conviction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere admission or some future-danger relevance is insufficient when supported culpability mitigation cannot receive effect. The ruling requires neither favorable weight nor a categorical fourth question, finds no psychiatric disability, and imposes no juvenile-execution prohibition or facial invalidity of the scheme. Texas’s later statute is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse sentencing relief denial on youth and background and remand for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate sentencing or a lawful noncapital replacement. Preserve the conviction; no automatic acquittal or immediate release.

#### Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, 505 U.S. 1003 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-453.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For reviewability of the original categorical-prohibition period, see Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, June 29, 1992, No. 91-453, under Federal Courts — Finality and earlier-period takings claims.

##### Complete economic deprivation requires compensation unless background ownership law already foreclosed the use

**Holding:** A land-use regulation that removes all economically beneficial use of the relevant property effects a taking requiring just compensation unless the prohibited use was already outside the owner's title under background state property or nuisance law. After the owner establishes complete deprivation, the State must identify and substantiate an applicable background limitation that would independently prohibit the claimed use in the property's circumstances; a new legislative harm declaration alone does not suffice, and ordinary adjudication supplies the proof requirements without a special heightened quantum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor and Thomas, supplies five votes for the complete rule and its explanation. Kennedy supports reversal through an expectations-sensitive analysis, without joining this categorical formulation. No Marks combination is required or used.

**Material application:** Total loss is the trial finding assumed for the legal question, not a Council concession or appellate affirmance of value. A new legislative declaration of harm cannot replace substantiated background law; changed knowledge may affect application of genuine nuisance principles.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes the trial court's total-loss finding to decide the presented legal issue; it makes no new appraisal and does not resolve the Council's preserved challenge to that finding. No unconditional entitlement to the trial award, final application of state nuisance law, right to anticipated profits, new parcel rule, or disposition of post-amendment permission is adopted. Legitimate environmental purposes remain sufficient to justify regulation; compensation is a distinct consequence. The Court overrules no earlier decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The no-compensation judgment is reversed and remanded for background property and nuisance law and the remaining defenses, including the sustained extent and duration of loss. Any compensation covers the actual taking period; the $1,232,387.50 trial award is not reinstated automatically.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pennsylvania Coal Co. v. Mahon, 260 U.S. 393 (1922): applies its recognition that regulation can go too far despite valid public purposes; does not attribute today's exact categorical rule to that decision.
- Agins v. City of Tiburon, 447 U.S. 255 (1980): specifies and extends its complete-economic-use formulation into the rule and necessary exception stated here; does not adjudicate its distinct legitimate-interest formulation.
- Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, 438 U.S. 104 (1978): preserves contextual analysis for partial deprivation, including economic impact, expectations and the character of governmental action; no percentage automatically defeats or establishes compensation.
- Mugler v. Kansas, 123 U.S. 623 (1887), Hadacheck v. Sebastian, 239 U.S. 394 (1915), Miller v. Schoene, 276 U.S. 272 (1928), and Goldblatt v. Hempstead, 369 U.S. 590 (1962): limits a broad noxious-use reading that would make any legislative harm characterization conclusive despite complete beneficial-use deprivation; preserves their actual outcomes and lawful protective regulation.
- Keystone Bituminous Coal Association v. DeBenedictis, 480 U.S. 470 (1987): distinguishes its productive-enterprise and partial-interest setting; no reversal of its actual holding or adoption of a new denominator follows.
- Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972): applies existing rules and understandings to identify the protected property interest; does not decide a procedural due-process claim.
- Scranton v. Wheeler, 179 U.S. 141 (1900): applies its antecedent-title-limit principle; a previously existing servitude is distinct from a newly imposed public burden.
- Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV Corp., 458 U.S. 419 (1982), and Kaiser Aetna v. United States, 444 U.S. 164 (1979): preserve physical-occupation and exclusion protections and illuminate the difference between existing limitations and a new imposed servitude; this decision concerns regulatory loss.
- Webb's Fabulous Pharmacies, Inc. v. Beckwith, 449 U.S. 155 (1980): applies the prohibition on converting private property into public property by governmental assertion alone.

#### Yee v. City of Escondido, 503 U.S. 519 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1947.
**Decided:** April 1, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *Yee v. City of Escondido*, April 1, 1992, No. 90-1947: whether the unpreserved substantive-due-process claim is reviewable (Federal Courts — Preservation and question-presented limits); whether a new taking argument and this facial theory satisfy preservation and ripeness (Federal Courts — Preservation and question-presented limits); whether the physical-taking question fairly includes regulatory-taking merits (Federal Courts — Preservation and question-presented limits).

##### Whether restrictions on voluntarily created pad tenancies compel permanent physical occupation

**Holding:** Regulating rents and continuation of voluntarily created pad tenancies does not impose a permanent physical occupation where the law permits the owner to change the land’s use and no contrary application is established. A tenant’s resale premium and the owner’s reduced ability to select incoming tenants do not themselves convert that regulation into a compelled occupation.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor, I, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: seven votes control. Blackmun and Souter separately agree with rejection of the physical-taking claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The owners did not attempt the change-of-use procedure; no unsuccessful withdrawal attempt or actual law forbidding withdrawal is adjudicated. A separate regulatory-taking claim and other applications remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the California judgment rejecting the physical-taking claim; award no compensation on that theory. Decline the due-process and regulatory-taking merits without deciding their validity; any properly available regulatory or as-applied claim requires an appropriate proceeding, not a new merits remand here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- FCC v. Florida Power Corp., 480 U.S. 245 — applied: invitation to occupy remains distinct from compelled occupation despite regulated rent.
- Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV Corp., 458 U.S. 419 — applied and distinguished: its permanent-occupation rule requires compulsion absent here.
- Nollan v. California Coastal Commission, 483 U.S. 825 — distinguished: a required easement cannot substitute for proof of forced occupation here.
- Kaiser Aetna v. United States, 444 U.S. 164 — preserved: the right to exclude is important, but the facial scheme has not compelled this occupation.

#### National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., 503 U.S. 407 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-1419 and 90-1769.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp.*, March 25, 1992, Nos. 90-1419 and 90-1769: whether §562(d) permits passenger-service acquisition followed by private reconveyance (Administrative Law — Passenger-rail acquisition and agency review); whether an agency interpretation necessarily embodied in its decision supplies its own reviewable ground (Administrative Law — Passenger-rail acquisition and agency review); whether the record satisfies §562(d)’s inability-to-agree prerequisite (Administrative Law — Passenger-rail acquisition and agency review); whether the railroad rebuts §562(d)’s presumed passenger-service need (Administrative Law — Passenger-rail acquisition and agency review).

##### Whether the passenger-service condemnation satisfies public use despite private reconveyance

**Holding:** A compensated condemnation serving a rationally supported public passenger-service purpose satisfies the Fifth Amendment’s public-use requirement even when title is reconveyed to another private railroad. The ICC’s determination that this transaction facilitates Amtrak service meets that requirement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Souter; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The condemnation power belongs to the ICC and remains subject to compensation. The decision does not authorize naked private favoritism or determine the proper valuation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The D.C. Circuit’s judgments in both consolidated dockets are reversed and remanded for resolution of compensation challenges and other review issues not previously decided. The Court sustains the ICC order only on the reached acquisition, agency-ground, prerequisite and public-use issues; it fixes no new valuation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hawaii Housing Authority v. Midkiff, 467 U.S. 229 — applied: rational public purpose can support condemnation followed by private transfer.
- Berman v. Parker, 348 U.S. 26 — applied: private implementation does not defeat an otherwise public purpose.
- United States v. Carmack, 329 U.S. 230 — distinguished: the condemnation power here is exercised by a government commission.
