**[Serious future health risks in prison conditions](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#serious-future-health-risks-in-prison-conditions)** — Effective: 1993-06-18; Helling v. McKinney, for the components identified above.

A prisoner may establish an Eighth Amendment violation through deliberate indifference to involuntary exposure posing unreasonable risk of serious future harm, without waiting for existing disease. The prisoner must prove objective gravity, including seriousness, likelihood and contemporary society's unwillingness to tolerate the risk, and the separate subjective culpability requirement under Wilson. Current conditions govern prospective relief; no later Farmer formulation or automatic relief from exposure alone enters.

**[Physical injury in a nontrivial malicious custodial beating](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#physical-injury-in-a-nontrivial-malicious-custodial-beating)** — Effective: *Hudson*, February 25, 1992.

Injury remains relevant evidence and bears on compensation; accidental harm or merely technical battery is insufficient. This rule identifies a sufficient constitutional violation and does not impose a universal mental-state standard for all prison-force claims.

**Unresolved boundary:** A comprehensive mental-state rule for other force settings, psychological injury alone, and a general definition of punishment covering isolated unauthorized assaults remain unresolved. ##### Issue 1: Litigation and settlement fees

**Controlling Simulated Law / Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the case-specific inherited-law rules and limits above to this issue. Other eligible authorities below remain effective only to the extent consistent with those simulated holdings and any actual earlier effective OT1993 ruling.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** CERCLA §107(a)(4)(B), 42 U.S.C. §9607(a)(4)(B), covers necessary response costs consistent with the national contingency plan. Section 101(25) defines response to include enforcement activities; §113 supplies contribution provisions, and §120 addresses federal facilities. Alyeska Pipeline Service Co. v. Wilderness Society, 421 U.S. 240 (1975), and Runyon v. McCrary, 427 U.S. 160 (1976), require a sufficient statutory basis to depart from the American rule. SARA expressly authorizes fees in other provisions, but the language invoked here does not expressly make private cost-allocation litigation a fee-shifting action. [P30](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/809/)

**Open Point:** Whether “enforcement activities” and “response” authorize private cost-recovery litigation fees or the legal expense of protecting the settlor’s own position before EPA.

##### Issue 2: Nonlitigation identification work

**Controlling Simulated Law / Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the case-specific inherited-law rules and limits above to this issue. Other eligible authorities below remain effective only to the extent consistent with those simulated holdings and any actual earlier effective OT1993 ruling.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Section 107(a)(4)(B)’s necessity and national-contingency-plan requirements govern work undertaken as part of a response. The claimant must establish what work was performed, its response function, necessity, and the recoverable amount. The American rule governs the costs of litigating, not every professional activity a lawyer might perform. [P30](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/809/)

**Open Point:** Whether identifying responsible parties is an independent response activity rather than a disguised litigation fee.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Key Tronic reads the added enforcement language to include private actions essential to making polluters pay and argues that all three categories advance CERCLA. The United States invokes the American rule and distinguishes cleanup from shifting the client’s legal expenses. A functional intermediate construction would distinguish identification work that is independently a response activity from private litigation fees. The Court must decide whether the text and record support that distinction, broader recovery, or rejection of the challenged costs. [P30](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/809/)

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm rejection of litigation and negotiation fees while remanding identification costs; allow broader fees only on a sufficient statutory construction; or reject a particular investigation charge where its independent response function or necessity is not established. No blanket fee entitlement follows from liability under CERCLA. [P30](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/809/)
