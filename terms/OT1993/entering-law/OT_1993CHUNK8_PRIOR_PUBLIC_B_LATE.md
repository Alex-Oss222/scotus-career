### Forsyth County v. Nationalist Movement, No. 91-538

Merits decision, June 19, 1992

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 31, 1992. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, 913 F.2d 885 and 934 F.2d 1482. Case reference: [505 U.S. 123–143](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep505/usrep505123/usrep505123.pdf). The court of appeals reversed the District Court's rejection of the facial challenge to the County's demonstration fees, reasoning that only a nominal fee was permissible.

The Court considers whether the fee mechanism violates the First Amendment, including the asserted nominal-fee ceiling and the independently pleaded defects in the licensing scheme. The Nationalist Movement seeks an injunction against enforcement. The separate City and School Board permit controversies are outside this review. All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

The Court affirms the judgment invalidating the County's challenged fee mechanism because it gives officials unbounded discretion and permits charges based on a demonstration's content. The Court rejects a universal nominal-fee ceiling.

| County fee mechanism | Affirmed, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter | White, Scalia, Thomas | Facial relief against enforcement stands. The dissenting Justices would reverse the nominal-fee basis and remand the other claims. Proceedings below implement the affirmed judgment. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Facial review and bounded licensing discretion

**Controlling proposition:** A licensing rule directed at protected demonstrations is facially invalid when neither its text, authoritative construction, nor established administration supplies narrow, objective, definite standards controlling whether and how much officials charge. A challenger need not prove discriminatory administration of its own application, and a maximum fee does not supply the missing standards.

**Authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela separately concurs in the judgment on compatible grounds and does not formally join that opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the County may coordinate competing uses of its public property, but it must constrain the officials who license protected expression. Cox permits reasonable adjustment of administrative costs under a binding nondiscriminatory construction. Shuttlesworth and Niemotko require definite licensing standards, and Lakewood permits a facial challenge to the censorship risk created by their absence. Here the administrator decides whether to charge, what time and support costs to include, and whether to discount them, without a binding method or an explanation requirement. Different fees alone would not establish a violation; the absence of constraints governing those choices does. The District Court's finding that the particular $100 charge reflected administrative work therefore does not answer the facial claim. Nor does the $1,000 ceiling control the choices below it. The Court leaves room for neutral, sufficiently bounded coordination and cost rules and does not decide a concrete indigency claim.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941): retained and distinguished; its authoritative nondiscrimination construction supports flexible lawful fees, not unbounded discretion.
- Shuttlesworth v. Birmingham, 394 U.S. 147 (1969): applied; expressive licensing requires narrow, objective, definite standards.
- Niemotko v. Maryland, 340 U.S. 268 (1951): applied; officials licensing protected expression must act under definite standards.
- City of Lakewood v. Plain Dealer Publishing Co., 486 U.S. 750 (1988): applied; the risk inherent in unbounded expressive licensing supports facial review despite one unobjectionable application.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989): applied as to authoritative constructions and actual administration; a binding narrowing construction matters, a presumed benign discretion does not.

##### Charges tied to audience hostility

**Controlling proposition:** A permit fee that varies with anticipated hostile reaction to a demonstration is content based and violates the First Amendment when justified by recovering the costs of protecting speakers from that reaction. Neither calling those expenses a secondary effect nor imposing a small numerical ceiling makes the charge constitutional.

**Authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter: an independently sufficient five-Justice holding. Stone-Zsela's separate concurrence agrees with the rule without a formal join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the price of protected expression cannot rise because listeners oppose its message. Ward's content-neutrality requirement and Boos's treatment of listeners' reactions prevent the County from treating hostility as an independent, neutral side effect. Under the ordinance as the County defends it, an administrator estimates the opposition a message will provoke and then estimates the protection required. That method makes unpopular speakers pay for the threatened misconduct of their opponents. The legitimate public responsibility to maintain order does not justify that content-based burden. Cox supplies no contrary authorization: its permissible adjustment for the size and demands of an event did not approve a premium for a controversial message. The record establishes the County's construction, so a remand to determine whether it might use a different construction is unnecessary. This conclusion concerns the fee mechanism; it does not find the actual $100 assessment retaliatory, fix municipal police expenditures, or forbid genuinely content-independent administrative charges.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989): applied; the justification must be independent of the message.
- Boos v. Barry, 485 U.S. 312 (1988): applied at its supported content-classification level; listeners' hostile responses are not neutral secondary effects.
- Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941): distinguished and retained; coordination costs do not include a message-dependent hostility premium.
- Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991 decision: consistent application of the prohibition on unjustified content-based financial burdens; its separate remedial limits are unchanged.
- Terminiello v. Chicago, 337 U.S. 1 (1949): applied in its protection against suppressing speech because of hostile response; no new police-protection entitlement is inferred.

##### No universal nominal-fee ceiling

**Controlling proposition:** The First Amendment does not make a demonstration fee invalid merely because it exceeds a nominal amount, or valid merely because it is small. Cox's allowance for lawful, nondiscriminatory regulatory cost recovery survives; a fee's lawful purpose, content neutrality, and bounded administration govern the defects presented here.

**Authority:** Blackmun's five-Justice Court opinion expressly rejects both proposed numerical shortcuts. White's dissent, joined by Scalia and Thomas, agrees that Cox rejects a universal nominal ceiling; Stone-Zsela's concurrence also rejects that ceiling. The Court opinion independently supplies controlling authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that amount alone does not decide the constitutionality of this licensing mechanism. Cox upheld a fee calibrated to administration and public coordination under a binding nondiscriminatory construction. Murdock invalidated a flat charge imposed on protected religious distribution and distinguished a genuine regulatory fee. Its reference to a nominal fee did not establish a universal numerical limit for parades, and did not authorize censorship so long as its price was small. The Court of Appeals therefore used the wrong reason when it treated the possible $1,000 assessment as dispositive. The County makes the converse mistake by treating that same ceiling as protection against content discrimination. The judgment nevertheless stands because the ordinance lacks adequate constraints and permits hostility-based pricing. The Court establishes neither a permissible dollar figure nor a comprehensive schedule of recoverable costs. Those questions must be addressed under lawful neutral standards in a concrete case.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941): retained; flexible, nondiscriminatory regulatory fees are not limited to nominal amounts.
- Murdock v. Pennsylvania, 319 U.S. 105 (1943): distinguished; its nominal-fee reference does not establish an amount-only rule or displace Cox.

#### Limits and Current-Law Effect

The fee mechanism cannot be enforced against protected demonstrations. Unbounded discretion in licensing expression and permit pricing based on audience hostility each independently support that result. A cap cures neither defect; fees exceeding a nominal amount remain permissible if otherwise lawful. The County may adopt lawful neutral coordination rules and a sufficiently bounded cost policy. No precedent is overruled. A five-Justice Court opinion controls every holding, so no rule must be drawn from competing separate opinions.

The Court does not decide concrete indigency rules, the validity of fixed neutral fees or particular content-independent administrative costs, or municipal funding duties. It prescribes neither a fee amount nor a mandatory spending formula and awards no damages or refund amount. The City and School Board claims remain outside this review.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice White, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, would correct the court of appeals' nominal-fee rule under *Cox*, which permits lawful nondiscriminatory regulatory fees, and remand the other challenges for initial findings below. The dissent does not approve fees based on hostile audiences or unlimited discretion; it disputes whether the Court should resolve those defects on this review. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment, applying the same facial-review and neutrality rules without regard to the speaker's viewpoint. He reserves concrete indigency, fixed neutral fees, and municipal spending questions and does not formally join the Court's opinion.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Supreme Court merits review is complete. Affirmance of the County portion leaves the facial protection against enforcement in place, with implementation returned to the lower courts through the ordinary mandate. No further Supreme Court merits action is ordered, and no separate review of the City or School Board controversy is added.

**End of entry: Forsyth County v. Nationalist Movement, merits decision, June 19, 1992.**

### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902

Merits decision and cross-review, June 29, 1992

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 22, 1992. Decided June 29, 1992. On writs of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit, 947 F.2d 682, reviewing the District Court's decision at 744 F. Supp. 1323. Providers seek broader declaratory and injunctive relief in No. 91-744; state officials seek restoration of spousal notice in No. 91-902. The Third Circuit sustained the appealed requirements except spousal notice.

Review concerns the protected abortion choice and reconsideration of *Roe v. Wade*; medical emergency; consent information, materials, delivery, waiting and certification; parental involvement and judicial bypass; spousal notice; and the appealed reporting and public-release provisions. The separately presented constitutional grounds and limits on review are stated below.

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

In No. 91-902, the Court affirms the invalidation of compulsory spousal notification, 6–3. Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter support that judgment; White, Scalia and Thomas dissent. In No. 91-744, the Court affirms in part, reverses in part and remands. It invalidates the fixed waiting period, exclusive physician delivery of routine disclosures, blanket referring-source identification, and the notice-compliance and nonnotification-reason report fields. It grants relief from the challenged public-release conditions as applied to the plaintiff facilities and their covered reports. Independently lawful consent, parental, reporting and collection duties survive at the scopes stated below. No special materials or parental-attendance proceedings are ordered.

Six Justices retain the protected previability choice, individualized viability and necessary life-and-health care. Five—Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter—adopt the complete six-step framework, including the independent obstacle prohibition, residual severe-interference protection, lesser-burden substantiation rule, proof allocation and remedial principles. They replace the trimester timetable only together with those operative protections. All nine reject continuation of the former timetable in result, but only those five adopt the integrated replacement rationale. The other four votes do not enlarge the framework majority.

#### Statutory Provisions and Qualifications

The following terms describe the Act as enacted. Their inclusion does not restore provisions invalidated below. The Court removes the fixed interval, exclusive routine physician delivery, blanket referring-source reporting, compulsory spousal notice and notice-report dependencies, and the challenged public-release conditions as applied to the plaintiff facilities. Each surviving duty remains subject to its stated qualifications.

- **Medical emergency, §3203.** The condition must, on the physician's good-faith clinical judgment, necessitate immediate abortion to avert death, or make delay create a serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The Third Circuit's construction that compliance must not create a significant threat to life or health, including preeclampsia, inevitable abortion and premature membrane rupture, is a protective judicial construction, not additional statutory language. Section 3205(b) requires the physician to explain the supporting medical indications **before the abortion if possible**; it does not make prior explanation indispensable where the emergency prevents it.

- **Medical consent and the separate timing and speaker duties, §3205(a)(1).** Voluntary informed consent is the general condition. Except for medical emergency, the performing or referring physician must orally provide, at least 24 hours before the abortion, the nature of the procedure or treatment, risks and alternatives a reasonable patient would consider material, probable gestational age when the procedure will occur, and medical risks of carrying the pregnancy to term. Content, the physician-only delivery requirement and the 24-hour interval are distinct obligations; each receives its own constitutional disposition.

- **Ancillary notices and qualified delegates, §3205(a)(2).** The performing or referring physician, or a qualified physician assistant, health-care practitioner, technician or social worker to whom either physician delegates the responsibility, may give the notices. The Act prescribes separate 24-hour timing and emergency terms; the fixed interval is invalidated below and the emergency protection remains. The patient is informed that free state materials describing fetal development and alternatives are available for her review if she chooses; that medical assistance **may** be available for prenatal care, childbirth and neonatal care, with details in the materials; and that the father is liable to assist in support even if he offered to pay for abortion. Liability is not a guarantee of collection. “In the case of rape, this information may be omitted.” That permission requires no police report.

- **Paternal-support rape permission, §3205(a)(2)(iii).** The statute states: **“In the case of rape, this information may be omitted.”** Omission is permitted, not required. This qualification has **no police-reporting condition** and does not extinguish the support obligation. It is not the reported-spousal-sexual-assault exception to spousal notice.

- **Materials, certification and speech, §§3205(a)(3)–(4), 3208.** Materials are supplied if the patient chooses to view them; acceptance, reading and ideological agreement are not compelled. Before the abortion, the patient certifies in writing that the information required under paragraphs (1), (2) and (3) has been provided. Section 3208 requires easily comprehensible materials in English, Spanish and Vietnamese within 60 days after the chapter becomes law, annual updating and legible type. The geographically indexed directory includes public and private pregnancy, childbirth, dependent-child and adoption services, their services and contact information, including telephone numbers. The Department may instead supply a toll-free 24-hour number giving that list and service information for the caller's locality. Fetal-development materials describe probable anatomy and physiology from fertilization to full term in two-week increments and relevant survival information; pictures must state fetal dimensions, be realistic and suit the woman's stage of pregnancy. Information must be objective, nonjudgmental and scientifically accurate. The materials include objective information about common abortion methods and risks, possible detrimental psychological effects and childbirth risks, and are available free upon request in appropriate number to any person, facility or hospital. They state that coercing abortion is unlawful; that performing an abortion without informed consent or a private medical consultation may expose the physician to civil damages; that the father remains liable for support even if he offered to pay for abortion; and that adoptive parents may pay prenatal, childbirth and neonatal costs. Those statements and the assistance information do not guarantee financial availability. District findings 181–189 identify directory defects and misinformation by some listed organizations; they do not establish that every pamphlet is false.

- **The separate disclosure defense and enforcement, §3205(c).** A physician is not guilty of violating the section for failing to furnish subsection (a) information if the physician establishes by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. This defense concerns omitted information; it is not a general waiver of waiting and cannot substitute for effective emergency treatment. Violations constitute unprofessional conduct subject to license suspension or revocation through the governing procedures. Performing an abortion without the required written certificate, or with knowledge or reason to know that informed consent was not obtained, is a summary offense initially and a third-degree misdemeanor for subsequent offenses. Section 3205(d) protects a complying physician against the patient civil-liability claim for failure to obtain informed consent; it is not general immunity from malpractice.

- **One-parent consent and substitutes, §3206(a)–(b).** The developed challenge concerns a pregnant minor under 18 who is not emancipated. The separate rule for an adult adjudged incompetent under §5511 is statutory background, not a newly developed claim. Except for medical emergency and the section's alternatives, the physician obtains informed consent from both the minor and one parent. A parent or guardian considers only the child's or ward's best interests. If pregnancy results from incest and the father participated in the incestuous act, maternal consent alone supplies the required parental consent; the minor's own informed consent remains required. If both parents are dead or otherwise unavailable to the physician within a reasonable time and manner, guardian consent suffices; after divorce the custodial parent's consent suffices; when neither a parent nor guardian is available within a reasonable time and manner, an adult standing in place of a parent may consent.

- **Independent bypass and enforcement, §3206(c)–(i).** A minor may choose judicial authorization without first asking a parent or guardian. The proper common pleas court is in the district where she lives or where abortion is sought. Maturity, capacity for informed consent and actual informed consent require authorization. Alternatively, abortion in her best interests requires authorization; maturity and best interests are not cumulative conditions. The court advises her of the right to counsel and provides counsel unless she chooses private counsel or knowingly and intelligently waives representation. A guardian ad litem may assist. Proceedings and records are confidential, her name is excluded from a public docket, and other persons are excluded unless their presence is specifically requested by her or her guardian. The court makes written findings and conclusions and maintains a sealed record. The trial court must rule within **three business days of application**; an appellate court must rule within **five business days of filing the appeal**. These are separate ruling periods, not a single eight-calendar-day clock. The statutory text does not expressly require another parental clinic visit: that is the district court's operational finding, and Third Circuit footnote 23 reserves its statutory correctness. The penalty provision combines knowledge, recklessness or negligence regarding covered minor/incompetent status with an intentional, knowing or reckless failure to conform to the section; it specifies unprofessional conduct and at least three months' license suspension through the applicable mechanism.

- **Spousal notice and substitute statements, §3209.** A married patient supplies a signed statement that she notified her spouse. It need not be notarized but must warn that false statements are punishable. Instead she may sign a statement, also nonnotarized and carrying that warning, certifying at least one condition: her spouse is not the father; diligent effort failed to locate him; pregnancy resulted from spousal sexual assault under §3128 personally reported by the victim or her agent to a law-enforcement agency with jurisdiction within 90 days of commission of the offense; or she has reason to believe notification is likely to cause bodily injury to her by the spouse or another individual. Medical emergency independently excuses notification. The statute supplies no general exception for psychological or economic coercion, threatened injury to a child or another person, publicity, divorce or custody retaliation.

- **The incorporated spousal-assault elements and 90-day clock, §3128.** Sexual intercourse or deviate sexual intercourse with a spouse must involve forcible compulsion, a threat of forcible compulsion that would prevent resistance by a person of reasonable resolution, or an unconscious spouse. Under §3128(c), the victim or her agent must personally report the crime to a law-enforcement agency with jurisdiction **within 90 days of the commission of the offense**. The clock begins with the offense, not discovery of pregnancy, a request for abortion or notification. These statutory terms come from **1984 Act 230, §2, P.L. 1211**; district finding 268 also quotes the offense elements. **District finding 297** separately explains that the notice exception excludes coercive sexual behavior other than penetration and that the reporting limit narrows the exception because victims may be psychologically unable to report for years after the incident. The finding is not a verbatim quotation of subsection (c). None of these reporting conditions attaches to the rape-information permission in §3205(a)(2)(iii).

- **Spousal enforcement, §3209(e).** A violation is unprofessional conduct subject to license suspension or revocation through governing procedures. A knowing violation additionally creates civil liability to the spouse who is the father of the aborted child for resulting damages and $5,000 punitive damages; a prevailing plaintiff receives a reasonable attorney fee as costs. These triggers are not interchangeable with §3205(c)'s information defense or culpability rules.

- **Individual reports and confidentiality, §3214(a).** Forms may not identify the patient by name. District finding 357 describes §3214(e)(1)–(2): these are not public records; law-enforcement access requires a common pleas court order after an application showing good cause, and the Department's statistical publication must not disclose any physician, facility or patient's identity. Adequacy of these confidentiality safeguards is accepted in this litigation. Public patient-identity disclosure is not a finding.

- **Distinct confidential fields, §3214(a)(1), (8), (10), (11), (12).** Referring physician, agency or service identification, if any, is distinct from performing physician, statutorily required concurring or second physician and facility identification in subsection (a)(1). Subsection (a)(8) reports the basis for medical necessity when abortion occurred under §3211(b)(1): prevention of death or substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. Subsection (a)(10) reports the basis for a medical-emergency judgment excusing compliance with the Act. Subsection (a)(11) incorporates §3210(a)'s account of the inquiries actually made, examinations and tests actually used, and diagnostic basis for gestational age; the underlying assessment calls for what a prudent physician considers necessary and is excused when a medical emergency **prevents compliance**. It is distinct from subsection (a)(5)'s resulting gestational-age field and does not specify an additional fixed test. Subsection (a)(12) separately asks marital status, whether spousal notice occurred and, if not, why. These are three separable data fields, not a single indivisible demand.

- **Facility information and public access, §3207(b).** Within 30 days after the chapter's effective date, each abortion facility files its name and address; the names and addresses of parent, subsidiary or affiliated organizations, corporations or associations; and those of such entities having contemporaneous common ownership, beneficial interest, directorship or officership with any other facility. Changes require immediate updating. Its public-access condition concerns state-appropriated funds received during the **12 calendar months before the request to inspect or copy**. Reports of nonfunded facilities are available only to law-enforcement officials, the State Board of Medicine and the State Board of Osteopathic Medicine for official duties. Failure to comply carries a $500-per-day assessment. Collection, disclosure and sanctions for an unlawful disclosure-related duty must be distinguished.

- **Quarterly totals and public access, §3214(f).** A facility performing an abortion during a quarter reports the quarter's total and totals by trimester. The public-access condition uses state-appropriated funds received during the **12 calendar months before filing the report**. A declaration that no such funds are received requires confidential treatment unless other evidence causes the Department to conclude that funds were received. This filing-based lookback differs from §3207(b)'s request-based lookback. Both concern facility information, not patient-identifying reports.

- **Severability.** Act 64, §6 permits severance of a word, phrase, provision or application when the remainder can operate without it. Pennsylvania's general severability statute is 1 Pa. Cons. Stat. §1925. The Third Circuit quotes and applies those state-law sources. Independent operation must be tested; neither wholesale invalidation nor judicial rewriting follows automatically.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The framework retaining protected choice

**Controlling proposition:** Before viability, the patient retains the right to decide whether to continue a pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons; the State may not prohibit that protected choice, give another person effective control over it, or prevent timely care necessary for life or health. After viability the State may prohibit abortion only with the required life-and-health protection, while competent practice, genuinely informed consent, safety and lawful administration remain regulable within these boundaries.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the decision whether to continue a pregnancy remains a protected liberty. Roe locates that decision within the Fourteenth Amendment and distinguishes the patient's choice before viability from the State's authority afterward. Griswold and Eisenstadt establish that constitutional protection of reproductive decisions concerns the individual, including an unmarried individual. The protection is therefore neither a privilege of marriage nor a permission that officials may withhold when they find the patient's reasons insufficient. The personal consequences of pregnancy make her decision constitutionally significant; disagreement with that decision does not transfer it to the State.

This liberty protects an effective opportunity to choose. Carey explains that a government can interfere with a reproductive decision by restricting access to its means even without prohibiting the decision in words. Danforth supplies the corresponding limit on another person's authority: the State may not give a spouse a controlling power over the patient's protected choice. Accordingly, the Court considers what Pennsylvania's requirements actually demand and how they operate. A formal description as information, notice or administration cannot answer evidence that the condition gives another person effective control. Conversely, a condition does not become unconstitutional merely because it concerns abortion or may persuade a patient who freely considers accurate information.

The State has legitimate interests in health, competent medical practice and potential life. Roe recognizes those interests, and the retained right does not promise access to demonstrably unsafe treatment. The clinician must still determine what care is appropriate, and the State may establish lawful professional standards. But clinical suitability is different from an official judgment about whether a patient has an acceptable reason to end a previability pregnancy. A safety label cannot itself establish that otherwise appropriate care is unsafe. The actual medical function, burden and constitutional limits must be considered under the law applicable to the challenged requirement.

Life-and-health protection must operate when treatment is needed. Doe requires attention to individualized clinical judgment and relevant physical and mental conditions; it does not reduce health to danger of immediate death. Pennsylvania's emergency definition is sustained under the prospective construction protecting significant threats before damage becomes catastrophic. That construction, together with the requirement of good-faith clinical judgment, answers the developed facial health challenge. It does not make every preference an emergency or settle an independent vagueness claim. The separate defense for omitting harmful information cannot substitute for permission to provide timely necessary treatment.

The protected decision also explains why adult spousal control differs from permissible parental involvement. Danforth prevents an effective spousal veto. Bellotti, Ashcroft and the later minors' cases address the distinct responsibility of parents and require adequate independent routes for a minor to obtain authorization. Pennsylvania's one-parent structure must be considered with its emergency exception, substitute-consent provisions and elective, confidential, expeditious bypass based on maturity and informed consent or, alternatively, best interests. Recognizing that structure does not authorize an adult spouse to control another adult's medical choice.

The principal contrary argument would return the entire subject to ordinary legislative discretion because abortion is not expressly named in the Constitution. The Court rejects that argument under the established protection of reproductive liberty and the reasons for retaining Roe stated separately. Recognition of legitimate state interests does not answer who holds the protected decision. Neither does continued disagreement erase the individual liberty those precedents secure. The foundation survives the Court's separate treatment of regulatory rules.

This six-Justice foundation is distinct from the five-Justice complete framework stated next. The trimester timetable is replaced together with that framework; the adoption is not inferred from the foundation votes. Nor does the Court decide constitutional fetal personhood, a general entitlement to public funding, a conscience claim, every postviability restriction, or admission of a particular drug. States may provide greater lawful protection. The Court decides the requirements actually presented and preserves the limits necessary to make the patient's protected choice real.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Roe v. Wade — Retains the protected previability decision, viability boundary and required postviability life-and-health safeguard; this holding does not itself decide the separate treatment of the trimester timetable.
- Griswold v. Connecticut — Applies protection of reproductive decisionmaking as a foundation of the retained liberty.
- Eisenstadt v. Baird — Applies the individual character of reproductive choice; marital status does not supply official control over the decision.
- Carey v. Population Services International — Applies the principle that substantial restrictions on access can burden the protected decision itself, with no general rule about every law implicating sexual freedom.
- Doe v. Bolton — Retains individualized clinical judgment and meaningful physical-and-mental-health protection; no general professional immunity follows.
- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth — Applies the prohibition on State-conferred spousal control and preserves the distinction between voluntary informed consent and interference with protected choice.
- Bellotti v. Baird, Planned Parenthood Association of Kansas City v. Ashcroft, Hodgson v. Minnesota, and Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health — Preserve their particular parental-involvement and independent-authorization safeguards; they do not supply an adult spouse's veto or a universal cure for every added parental burden.

##### The standard governing previability regulation after this decision

**Controlling proposition:** The Court adopts a complete six-step standard governing previability abortion regulation. Substantial obstacles and violations of the protected baseline are independently invalid. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires the State to substantiate its justification and fit and answer identified practicable, substantially less burdensome alternatives. The complete standard replaces the independent trimester timetable in the same holding.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** The six steps organize one constitutional inquiry. They do not establish six competing standards. A court must identify the obligation, its function, its actual interference, the governing substantive protection, the clinical and communication safeguards, and the appropriate relief.

**Step 1. Identify the legal duty and protected baseline**

Identify the enacted command, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences, and relevant medical circumstances. Distinguish an actual requirement from assumed future administration.

A previability prohibition, an effective third-party veto, or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care violates the protected baseline. No later justification inquiry can cure that violation. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration makes intervention unavoidable.

Clinical appropriateness means safe and suitable care. It does not require official approval of the patient's reasons for obtaining a previability abortion. Nor may the State establish a restriction's medical necessity merely by defining all noncompliant treatment as unsafe.

**Step 2. Identify actual function and purpose**

Identify what the obligation does and the objective asserted for it: patient safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration, financial accountability, or another constitutionally permissible function.

The State may express a preference for childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstructing protected choice, punishing its exercise, or imposing burdens for their deterrent effect. A purpose to obstruct, established through objective evidence of text, design, context, and operation, independently invalidates the requirement. A legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose.

Persuasion through voluntary understanding differs from prevention through barriers. Fewer abortions resulting from informed decisions may further the State's permissible objective; fewer abortions resulting from blocked access cannot justify the blockage.

Evaluate each justification on its own terms. A medical claim requires medical support. A deliberative claim requires an explanation of how the particular obligation improves informed decisionmaking. Neither label establishes the necessary connection.

**Step 3. Establish actual interference, the baseline, and the affected population**

Challengers must show concrete additional interference attributable to the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include additional appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion, and disclosure consequences affecting protected choice or access.

**The comparison.** Compare the actual system with and without the challenged obligation, holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. If an interacting package is challenged, identify that package and compare the system with the package removed. The baseline is neither ideal access nor a world without unrelated regulation.

Consider cumulative operation. A twenty-four-hour interval requiring another visit to a clinic operating weekly may produce a week of actual delay. That is an illustration of the inquiry, not a finding about every clinic here. Examine the practical consequence without counting the same delay twice.

**The population.** Define the affected group from the duty's operation before determining constitutional injury. For a second-visit requirement, examine patients required to make an additional visit, including evidence-supported subgroups. Keep the group stable throughout the analysis. Do not average its burdens across everyone who is pregnant or define it circularly as only patients whose claims succeed.

Poverty, distance, transportation, work obligations, and abuse matter when they explain how the State's added condition operates. Their relevance does not make the State responsible for creating every underlying hardship. Explain the causal connection. Exceptions, bypasses, and alternative providers count only when actually usable.

**The threshold.** Meaningful interference means a concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review; it is not itself a finding of unconstitutionality. A routine receipt checkbox completed during an existing consultation ordinarily does not cross the threshold without an additional consequence. A required extra trip ordinarily supplies a concrete basis for examining expense and delay.

No universal dollar, mileage, or hourly cutoff governs. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony, and reasonable predictions may establish interference. Courts need not wait for patients to lose access before recognizing a sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury.

**Step 4. Apply the substantive protections in sequence**

**A. Independently forbidden interference.** First determine whether the requirement violates the protected baseline, has an unconstitutional obstructive purpose, or creates a substantial obstacle. If it does, the inquiry ends in invalidity.

A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the practical opportunity to obtain timely, clinically appropriate protected care in the circumstances of the affected population. It need not make abortion absolutely impossible. That some patients overcome a restriction does not establish adequate access for others. Once an obstacle is established, an asserted compelling interest cannot rescue it.

**B. Other severe interference.** If the preceding prohibitions do not resolve the case, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. The court must identify the severe intrusion and explain why the first inquiry did not already dispose of it.

These protections may overlap. They operate in order and do not require an artificial dividing line. A court may not shrink the obstacle prohibition to absolute impossibility or relabel an established obstacle as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical decisionmaking, health, or protected confidentiality may require exacting justification even where practical access remains usable.

For example, a medically supported specialist referral for a narrowly defined high-risk group may impose a serious but justified burden. The State must substantiate the particular risk, the tailoring, and the realistic availability of timely receiving care. A nominal referral or an unsupported safety label cannot justify blocking otherwise appropriate treatment.

**C. Meaningful lesser interference.** For remaining meaningful interference, the State bears the burden of substantiating the permissible justification and the fit between its objective and the additional obligation imposed. An abstract interest or conclusory assertion is insufficient.

When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative that would adequately serve the objective, the State must answer it. The State may establish that the alternative is inadequate, unavailable, or carries material countervailing consequences. It need not adopt the cheapest imaginable policy, disprove every hypothetical alternative, or produce perfect legislation.

Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may substantiate a justification. We require no particular legislative findings, new study in every case, scientific certainty, or numerical benefit calculation. Once the necessary justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy.

Crossing the meaningful-interference threshold triggers this inquiry; it does not predetermine its result. Below that threshold, this heightened access inquiry ends. Prohibited-purpose claims, independently applicable constitutional protections, and ordinary requirements of lawful regulation remain available.

**Proof allocation.** Challengers establish the duty, population, causation, and triggering interference. The State then carries the applicable justification and tailoring or fit burden. This allocation does not alter ordinary proof rules for disputed historical facts or a separate statutory defense.

The residual severe-interference protection draws on the treatment of serious burdens on reproductive liberty in [Carey v. Population Services International, 431 U.S. 678, 686–689](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/431/678/). The scrutiny of unnecessary restrictions has support in [Akron v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health, 462 U.S. 416](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/462/416/). We expressly adopt the general lesser-burden substantiation requirement today; we do not represent that every element of this formulation was already settled law.

**Step 5. Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication**

Medical justifications must be evaluated against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances, and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight. No professional association has a legislative veto, and neither a clinician's bare assertion nor an official's unsupported preference is conclusive.

Necessary care must remain timely. Appropriate clinical judgment cannot be displaced by an unnecessary committee veto or documentation functioning as prior permission. Duties must be sufficiently definite, and applicable fault requirements must be respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement does not alone establish every element of a punishable violation. Fraud, malpractice, and professional misconduct remain subject to lawful enforcement. [Doe](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/410/179/); [Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/439/379/).

Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading, and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without requiring ideological agreement. Clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accurate content does not automatically justify burdensome timing, delivery, or certification conditions.

Useful confidential records can serve legitimate oversight. Their scope, use, and burden must be distinguished from public disclosure and from records enforcing an invalid condition. [Planned Parenthood v. Danforth, 428 U.S. 52, 79–81](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/428/52/).

**Step 6. Identify the violation and tailor relief**

State which duty or application fails, why it fails, and what relief addresses the violation. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law. Address dependent certifications, reporting fields, and sanctions expressly.

An individual claim does not fail because other patients can comply. Broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated where its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State has failed to justify that condition. Occasions on which patients would voluntarily do the same thing do not alone justify compulsory compliance. Otherwise, relief should be limited to the applications or groups for which the violation is established.

This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach follows from the substantive right: successful compliance by some cannot alone answer a demonstrated constitutional defect in the obligation. It does not excuse proof of causation, scope, or inadequate justification. The distinct basis for invalidating the spousal-notice requirement is addressed in Part VI.

For cumulative burdens, relief addresses the challenged obligation's or package's contribution. It does not automatically invalidate all existing regulation. Removing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision. The mandate must specify the resulting duties.

Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter join every substantive branch, the proof allocation, the clinical safeguards and the remedial principles. Kennedy joins the protected core and particular applications but not this complete framework. White, Scalia and Thomas reject the abortion-specific standard. A lower court therefore applies this direct five-Justice holding without constructing a further rule from separate opinions under Marks.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Roe and Doe: preserve the protected decision and clinical safeguards while replacing the independent timetable.
- Carey and Akron: support protection against serious and unjustified interference; the general lesser-burden substantiation rule is expressly adopted here.
- Marks: no aggregation of dissenting rationales is necessary because five Justices directly join the complete rule.
- Ohio v. Akron: its facial-review formulation is qualified at the scope expressly stated in Step 6 and the spousal-notice holding.

##### Stare decisis and the constitutional foundation

**Controlling proposition:** Roe's protected reproductive liberty, viability boundary and necessary life-and-health protection are retained because renewed disagreement with the constitutional judgment supplies no sufficient justification for withdrawing that settled protection. The Court may distinguish or alter a separable regulatory rule for a demonstrated legal reason, but such treatment does not silently overrule the liberty that the rule implements.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** The Court retains Roe's constitutional foundation. Respect for precedent does not make constitutional error beyond correction. It requires a legal reason for correction sufficient to justify the disruption that overruling causes. Arizona v. Rumsey supplies that discipline: a conclusion that an earlier constitutional decision was wrong does not alone provide the special justification for abandoning it. The issue is therefore not whether every Member of the present Court would have written Roe in the same way. It is whether the reasons now presented warrant withdrawing the protected liberty on which later decisions and individual choices have rested.

That inquiry must identify the particular rule under examination. Roe recognizes a protected decision before viability and permits prohibition afterward only with necessary life-and-health protection. Its regulatory machinery addresses how the State's interests interact with that liberty. A criticism of one implementation does not establish that the liberty itself is unworkable. Nor does disagreement about the permissible content of a consent notice demonstrate that the patient may constitutionally be deprived of the ultimate decision. The Court accordingly separates the retained foundation from each disputed regulatory proposition and states the treatment of those propositions where they are decided.

The foundation remains capable of judicial application. Courts can distinguish a protected previability choice from an unsafe procedure, voluntary persuasion from an effective prohibition, and permissible parental guidance from an adult spouse's controlling authority. Difficult factual applications do not establish that the underlying right lacks meaning. Danforth, Doe and the later regulatory cases have required examination of identifiable legal duties and their operation. Those decisions leave contested questions, as constitutional rules often do, but the present record does not show that preserving the patient's decision is impossible to administer.

Reliance also bears directly on the requested overruling. The ability to decide whether to continue a pregnancy affects intimate relationships, education, work, family responsibilities and the course of a person's life. Such reliance cannot be reduced to the terms of a commercial agreement made in express reference to a judicial opinion. Nor does the possibility of precautions before pregnancy eliminate the significance of the legal choice once pregnancy occurs. The Court need not assume that every person has arranged every part of life in reliance on Roe to recognize that withdrawal of its protection would disturb a settled and consequential freedom.

The underlying constitutional decisions have not lost their force. Griswold and Eisenstadt continue to protect individual reproductive decisions; Carey explains that restrictions on the means of exercising such a choice may burden the choice itself. The State's interest in potential life is substantial, but Roe acknowledged that interest rather than overlooking it. Its continued assertion is not a newly discovered premise requiring abandonment of all protection. Medical developments may bear on factual applications and the timing or usefulness of particular rules. They do not establish that the patient's liberty disappears or that necessary life-and-health care may be withheld.

The strongest contrary argument is that a constitutional decision cannot ordinarily be corrected by legislation and therefore deserves less rigid adherence. That consideration makes reconsideration possible; it does not make settled constitutional liberty temporary at the preference of each newly constituted Court. A change in membership is no legal defect in a precedent. Continued public disagreement likewise neither proves the precedent wrong nor makes it immune from reconsideration. The Court's duty is to give reasons tied to law, workable protection and the consequences of change, not to treat either resistance or acquiescence as a substitute for judgment.

This conclusion does not freeze every statement in Akron or Thornburgh, nor does it adopt every argument offered to preserve Roe. Particular disclosure requirements, physician delivery, fixed delay and reporting receive their own grounds and precise precedent treatment. A majority for a particular change must support that change at its actual scope; it cannot be inferred from votes retaining the constitutional foundation. The Court rejects both wholesale abandonment by implication and the claim that any refinement necessarily abandons the right. Roe's core protection remains law for the reasons given here, subject to the distinct regulatory holdings the Court actually adopts.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Arizona v. Rumsey — Applies the requirement of a special justification beyond a belief that a constitutional precedent was wrongly decided.
- Roe v. Wade — Reaffirms its protected-choice foundation, viability boundary and necessary life-and-health safeguard; regulatory implementation receives separate treatment.
- Griswold v. Connecticut and Eisenstadt v. Baird — Their protection of individual reproductive choice remains a doctrinal foundation for retaining Roe.
- Carey v. Population Services International — Retains the connection between protected reproductive choice and meaningful access to its exercise; its existence undercuts a claim that Roe's foundation has been doctrinally displaced.
- Doe v. Bolton and Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth — Their clinical-judgment, consent and third-party-control rules demonstrate separable applications of the protected liberty and remain subject to the specific holdings stated here.
- Akron v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health and Thornburgh v. American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists — No wholesale overruling follows from this stare-decisis holding; each actual qualification, retained protection or changed rule is identified in the provision-specific holdings.

##### The trimester timetable and the timing of regulation

**Controlling proposition:** The independent trimester timetable is displaced together with adoption of the complete six-step replacement. Five Justices adopt that integrated holding. All nine reject continuation of the former timetable in result, but the other four do not join the replacement standard.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. The separate result of rejecting the former timetable has nine supporters; only these five adopt its integrated replacement.

**Controlling explanation:** Roe's initial timetable generally left the first-trimester decision and its effectuation to the patient and physician, permitted health-related regulation after that stage, and permitted prohibitions after viability subject to necessary life-and-health protection. The first two timing rules are replaced; the viability boundary and necessary exception remain. Viability is individualized and medically supported, not a fixed constitutional week or a conclusive calendar presumption.

The calendar alone does not reliably identify the function or effect of a requirement. Accurate consent information may be appropriate early; an unnecessary barrier may be unconstitutional later. The same complete standard therefore governs throughout previability. Gestational age may support a particular safety requirement when medical evidence connects the duty to relevant risks, but crossing a trimester boundary does not independently change the standard.

Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter adopt removal and replacement together. The substitute is operative law, not a minority assurance. Kennedy separately agrees that the timetable should end while retaining the protected core and a less demanding approach to ordinary regulation. White, Scalia and Thomas reject the timetable because they would reject Roe's underlying entitlement. Their four votes do not add supporters to the five-Justice replacement rationale.

The State may regulate but may not require medical necessity or official approval of the patient's reasons for previability choice. Clinical appropriateness means safe and suitable care. Necessary life-and-health care remains available throughout pregnancy before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. The timetable's removal neither dissolves existing injunctions nor validates a requirement that fails the replacement framework.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Roe: independent trimester timing restrictions displaced; protected choice, individualized viability and necessary life-and-health care retained.
- Doe: individualized clinical judgment and relevant physical and psychological health remain protected.
- Webster: its criticism of rigid timing supports reconsideration of implementation, without treating its fractured opinions as an earlier complete overruling.

##### Medical emergency and the protective construction of §3203

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3203 survives this facial challenge under the prospective construction that compliance with the Act cannot create a significant threat to the patient's life or health. Its good-faith clinical standard permits prompt treatment of preeclampsia, inevitable abortion and prematurely ruptured membranes at onset, without waiting for catastrophic deterioration; the ruling does not adjudicate every other postviability restriction or every degree of clinical risk.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** The Court sustains the emergency definition as prospectively construed. The statute asks whether delay will create a serious risk; it does not require the physician to await the injury that timely treatment would prevent. Its reference to good-faith clinical judgment directs attention to the patient's condition when treatment is needed. The court of appeals applied that reading to preeclampsia, inevitable abortion and prematurely ruptured membranes, allowing treatment before shock, infection or irreversible injury occurs. The providers' contrary argument draws force from the consequences of a narrower construction. That construction is not the basis of today's judgment. Webster supports evaluating a medical requirement under a workable construction preserving appropriate professional judgment rather than assuming medically useless or dangerous administration. The separate information-omission defense cannot repair an inadequate emergency rule because it concerns disclosures, not postponement of treatment. The Court decides the developed construction and health-protection challenge. It does not issue a separate vagueness holding or foreclose a challenge to materially different authoritative construction or enforcement.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Webster v. Reproductive Health Services: applies its attention to a medical rule's workable, prudent construction; does not adopt every part of its fractured abortion analysis.
- Roe v. Wade and Doe v. Bolton: their retained necessary-care protection is stated separately at its own proposition-level coalition; this unanimous statutory construction does not create unanimous adherence to their entire reasoning.

**Statutory terms and construction:** Section 3203 defines medical emergency through the physician's good-faith clinical judgment: a condition necessitating immediate abortion to avert death, or one in which delay creates serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The significant-threat interpretation and application to the three obstetric conditions are the Third Circuit's construction, not additional statutory words or a binding Pennsylvania Supreme Court interpretation. Section 3205(c)'s separate disclosure defense is not an emergency-delay exception.

##### Emergency explanation under §3205(b)

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3205(b) requires an explanation of the medical indications supporting emergency abortion before the procedure only if possible. It does not condition necessary immediate care on completing an explanation or obtaining advance permission, and it survives the facial challenge on that limited operation.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** The Court sustains the emergency explanation requirement according to its express limit. Before treatment, a physician must explain the medical indications when circumstances permit. When they do not, the phrase 'if possible' prevents the duty from becoming a reason to postpone necessary care. The State may require information supporting an informed treatment decision without requiring that an emergency patient wait while a physician completes an ordinary counseling process. Danforth supplies the distinction between permissible informed consent and a condition interfering with the patient's decision; the statute's own qualification resolves the alleged timing conflict here. The required explanation concerns the actual medical indications supporting the emergency judgment. It is not an application to a public official and creates no power to withhold advance authorization. Nor does this subsection replace the emergency definition or the separate disclosure defense. The Court does not decide a hypothetical enforcement practice demanding an explanation when the emergency makes one impossible; such a practice would contradict the operation sustained today.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth: applies the permission for appropriate informed-consent administration while keeping interference with the treatment decision distinct.

**Statutory terms and construction:** When medical emergency compels an abortion, the physician shall inform the woman, prior to abortion if possible, of the medical indications supporting the judgment that abortion is necessary to avert death or substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The statutory text appears in the appendix at 505 U.S. 903–904.

##### The distinct information-omission defense in §3205(c)

**Controlling proposition:** A physician is protected from liability for failing to furnish §3205(a)'s information if the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would have a severely adverse effect on the patient's physical or mental health. This statutory disclosure defense does not supply a general waiting-period waiver, authorize delay of necessary emergency treatment or replace §3203's emergency protection.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** The Court gives the disclosure defense its separate statutory function. It protects a physician who reasonably believes that furnishing the prescribed information would severely harm this patient's physical or mental health and proves that belief by a preponderance of the evidence. The defense does not require proof that the feared injury actually occurred; the inquiry concerns the reasonable belief supporting the omission. It also does not excuse all inconvenience or every unfavorable reaction to information. Danforth permits appropriate informed-consent rules, and the defense limits their operation where the specified serious health concern exists. The principal contrary argument wrongly asks this provision to do the work of an emergency exception. A defense concerning what information is furnished cannot authorize postponing care that the emergency rule permits immediately. Conversely, the defense's separate mental-health terms cannot be erased because another provision uses a different formulation. The Court preserves this enacted allocation for this defense alone. This statutory defense retains its enacted burden; it neither supplies nor displaces the constitutional allocation adopted in the six-step standard and does not decide every possible information-related health challenge.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth: ordinary informed-consent administration remains permissible subject to the actual statutory and constitutional limits; no general proof rule is derived from it.

**Statutory terms and construction:** The paragraph identified by the District Court and Third Circuit as §3205(c) protects a physician from liability for failure to furnish subsection (a)'s information when the physician proves, by a preponderance of the evidence, a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health.

##### Accurate material medical information

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3205(a)(1)(i)–(iii)'s accurate, medically material information concerning the procedure, material risks and alternatives, probable gestational age and childbirth risks may be required for informed consent. Content is sustained independently of the separately invalidated exclusive routine-delivery and fixed waiting requirements, with the statutory disclosure-health defense preserved.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** An informed decision requires material information about the treatment being authorized and its alternatives. Danforth permits a State to require voluntary and informed consent; Roe does not make the patient dependent on whatever facts a provider elects to omit. The required procedure, risk, gestational-age and childbirth information bears directly on this medical decision. The reasonable-patient materiality limitation supplies a concrete connection between the disclosure and consent.

The Court does not assume that every prescribed statement is accurate because the legislature calls it medical. False or exaggerated risk descriptions and ideological assent fall outside the sustained obligation. Nor does approval of these facts resolve who must personally deliver them or how long the patient must wait; those are separately reviewed duties. Section 3205(c) protects the reasonable belief, proved by a preponderance, that furnishing the information would severely adversely affect physical or mental health. The principal objection to imposed counseling therefore does not justify facially invalidating this bounded factual content. Concrete misleading implementation remains open to challenge.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: applied to ordinary voluntary and informed consent.
- Akron and Thornburgh: their protection against misleading or coercive communication survives; they do not forbid this isolated accurate medical content.

**Application of the governing standard:** Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter additionally apply the complete framework: separated from the invalid interval and exclusive routine-speaker condition, the bounded medical information supplied during the existing encounter establishes no demonstrated meaningful additional access burden on this record. Accuracy alone would not answer a concrete burden from materially different delivery, timing or implementation. The other four Justices join the bounded medical-content permission without adopting the complete framework.

##### Notice of available free materials

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3205(a)(2)(i) may require truthful notice that state materials describing fetal development and alternatives exist, that the patient has a right to review them, and that a copy will be supplied free if she chooses. The duty requires notice, not acceptance, reading or endorsement of the materials or listed agencies.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. The judgment is 8–1: White, Scalia and Thomas concur in the judgment on their broader reasonable-regulation grounds; Blackmun dissents from the mandatory notice.

**Controlling explanation:** The fact that information is available differs from a command to accept its advice. This provision requires a concise description of the patient's option to review free materials. It does not require her to read a pamphlet, contact an agency, or agree with a governmental preference. Danforth's informed-consent principle permits a relevant explanation of available choices. The clinician may correct the directory, recommend another agency, or explain deficiencies.

The district findings about omissions and misinformation from some listed organizations must be taken seriously. They do not establish that the distinct statement about availability and the right to a free copy is false, or that all state materials convey unlawful information. The contrary argument treats the mandatory notice and optional receipt as the same obligation. They are separate. Akron and Thornburgh are qualified only to the extent their disclosure reasoning categorically excludes this narrow accurate notice merely because it might encourage childbirth. An identified deceptive or coercive implementation remains reviewable.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: applied to relevant informed-choice information.
- Akron and Thornburgh: qualified as to categorical exclusion of this concise accurate notice; their protections against falsehood, coercion and actual burdens remain.

**Application of the governing standard:** Separated from the invalid interval and exclusive routine-speaker condition, the bounded information supplied during the existing encounter establishes no demonstrated meaningful additional access burden on this record. Accuracy alone would not answer a concrete burden from materially different delivery, timing or implementation.

##### Notice of potentially available medical assistance

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3205(a)(2)(ii) may require concise accurate notice that medical assistance benefits may be available for prenatal care, childbirth and neonatal care, with more information in state materials. It does not promise eligibility or payment, and remains subject to the disclosure-health defense.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. The judgment is 8–1: White, Scalia and Thomas concur in the judgment on their broader reasonable-regulation grounds; Blackmun dissents from the mandatory notice.

**Controlling explanation:** A patient's understanding of available assistance can inform her decision without transferring that decision to the State. The statute speaks conditionally: benefits may be available. It does not guarantee eligibility, a particular award, or successful collection. That limitation matters because an inaccurate promise could distort consent. Danforth permits informed choice, and this concrete financial option bears a sufficient relationship to the alternatives being considered.

The State may favor childbirth through truthful information; the resulting possibility of persuasion does not alone make this concise notice unconstitutional. The Court rejects a categorical reading of Akron and Thornburgh that would exclude such a notice solely on that ground. It preserves their protection against misleading communication and coercion. Qualified delegated personnel may give the notice, and section 3205(c)'s separate health defense remains available on its stated preponderance showing. The Court does not hold that any truthful financial fact can be imposed regardless of relevance, manner or burden. A concrete unlawful implementation remains a distinct question.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: applied to relevant information supporting voluntary choice.
- Akron and Thornburgh: qualified for this concise conditional assistance notice; no general permission for compulsory advocacy follows.

**Application of the governing standard:** Separated from the invalid interval and exclusive routine-speaker condition, the bounded information supplied during the existing encounter establishes no demonstrated meaningful additional access burden on this record. Accuracy alone would not answer a concrete burden from materially different delivery, timing or implementation.

##### Paternal-support notice and the rape omission permission

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3205(a)(2)(iii) may require accurate notice that the father is liable to assist in supporting the child even if he offered to pay for an abortion; the statute provides, “In the case of rape, this information may be omitted.” No police report is required. Legal liability cannot be represented as guaranteed payment, and the omission permission changes no support obligation. This holding governs the disclosure alone; a married patient's separate question of notifying her spouse of the pregnancy is governed by the spousal-notice holding below, not by this provision.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. The judgment is 8–1: White, Scalia and Thomas concur in the judgment on their broader reasonable-regulation grounds; Blackmun dissents from the mandatory notice.

**Controlling explanation:** The disclosure identifies a legal responsibility relevant to the patient's alternatives. It does not establish that a particular father will pay, that collection will succeed, or that the State guarantees support. Danforth permits relevant information that assists voluntary consent. The Court sustains the concise statement of liability at that scope, while preserving the patient's decision and the clinician's ability to explain its practical limits.

The rape qualification is a permission. The statute states: “In the case of rape, this information may be omitted.” It neither requires omission nor conditions it on a report to police. This holds whether or not the assailant is the patient's spouse: the qualification asks only whether the pregnancy resulted from rape, and a spousal assailant does not remove the patient from that class. The distinct spousal-sexual-assault reporting condition in section 3209 governs a different question — whether the patient must notify her spouse of the pregnancy at all — and cannot be imported into this disclosure provision or its terms. A patient whose assault does not qualify for section 3209's reported-assault exception, because she did not report within its 90-day period, is unaffected by that fact for purposes of this holding: the paternal-support omission permission carries no reporting condition of its own and remains available to her on the same terms as any other rape survivor. Section 3205(c)'s physical-or-mental-health defense also retains its separate terms. The contrary argument against childbirth-favoring notices does not justify categorical invalidation of this accurate legal information. Akron and Thornburgh are qualified only at that narrow point. Misleading claims about payment, compelled moral endorsement, and concrete coercive implementation remain outside the holding.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: applied to truthful consent-related information.
- Akron and Thornburgh: qualified for accurate concise paternal-liability information with the statutory permission; their anti-coercion and accuracy protections remain.

**Application of the governing standard:** Separated from the invalid interval and exclusive routine-speaker condition, the bounded information supplied during the existing encounter establishes no demonstrated meaningful additional access burden on this record. Accuracy alone would not answer a concrete burden from materially different delivery, timing or implementation.

##### Statutory publication and chosen receipt of materials

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3208's publication duties and section 3205(a)(3)'s provision of a copy when the patient chooses to review it are sustained at their objective, accurate and nonjudgmental statutory scope. The patient need not accept, read or endorse the materials, and the ruling does not establish that every publication or listed agency actually complies.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** The State may publish information about fetal development and services available during pregnancy and after childbirth. Section 3208 regulates the content and format of that information: it requires objective, nonjudgmental scientific material and accurate descriptions, with pictures appropriate to gestational stage. The woman receives a copy under section 3205(a)(3) if she chooses to review the materials. Her decision to receive them does not become a duty to read or endorse them.

Danforth permits information that assists voluntary choice. Thornburgh's objection to compulsory persuasion does not invalidate state publication and genuinely chosen receipt as such. The directory findings show deficiencies that may warrant correction; they do not prove that all statutory publication is unconstitutional. Clinicians may supplement or criticize the information. The Court therefore sustains these severable functions without declaring the actual materials universally accurate or immunizing a concrete deceptive practice. The separate mandatory availability notice receives its own disposition, and its validity is not inferred merely from the patient's ultimate choice about receipt.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: supports information serving voluntary choice.
- Thornburgh: distinguished at voluntary publication/receipt scope; misleading or coercive implementation remains reviewable.

##### Certification of lawful receipt

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3205(a)(4) may document actual receipt of lawful information without ideological assent or certification of unchosen reading. Its particular mandatory ancillary, timing and physician-delivery links stand or fall with the separately counted duties; a nine-Justice agreement on basic documentation is not a nine-Justice vote for every retained prerequisite.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author. The basic receipt principle is unanimous. Ancillary dependencies survive 8–1, with Blackmun dissenting; Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter join the five-Justice ancillary rationale, while White, Scalia and Thomas concur in those judgments. Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter sever both timing and exclusive-delivery dependencies, each 5–4.

**Controlling explanation:** A written record of informed consent may document compliance without compelling an ideological pledge. Danforth permits consent documentation, and the present certification confirms that required information was given. The distinction between receipt and agreement controls. A patient need not certify agreement with the State's preference, the advice of a listed organization, or any belief about fetal life.

The form must also respect the statutory option concerning materials. If a patient does not choose to review them, the statute does not require a copy, and the certification cannot be used to create that missing duty. The contrary compelled-assent argument therefore does not justify invalidating accurate documentation of lawful receipt. Conversely, a form cannot restore an obligation that the Court has held invalid or expand one beyond its sustained terms. The Court sustains certification at this administrative scope, leaving a materially different coercive or false implementation for a concrete challenge. The fixed timing and physician-delivery duties receive independent judgments and are not decided by this documentation holding.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: applied to written consent documentation, with receipt distinguished from assent.

**Dependency disposition:** Forms and sanctions may not require compliance with the invalid fixed interval or exclusive routine-speaker condition. Those links are removed with the underlying obligations; accurate documentation of the surviving duties remains.

##### No additional materials remand or interim injunction

**Controlling proposition:** The record does not sufficiently identify an unlawful state communication or compulsory distribution practice to justify the requested additional materials vacatur, focused remand or interim injunction. The facial dispositions stand at their stated scopes, while a concrete misleading or coercive implementation remains challengeable.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** Relief must address a legal obligation and a demonstrated defect of corresponding scope. Brockett requires attention to severable lawful operation rather than indiscriminate invalidation. The district found omissions in the directory and misinformation supplied by some listed organizations. Those findings do not identify every state statement as false, establish that the patient must accept the materials, or prove a compulsory distribution practice contrary to her statutory choice.

The Court therefore does not order an additional inquiry or interim injunction directed at an unspecified class of materials. That conclusion does not dismiss the findings or pronounce every publication accurate. Clinicians remain able to warn against a listed agency and to supply better information; an identified unlawful communication can be challenged through the appropriate proceedings. The requested special remedy lacks the necessary connection between its proposed scope and an established defect. The ordinary mandate implements the actual component judgments, including the distinct treatment of mandatory notices, voluntary publication and chosen receipt.

##### Physician delivery of medical information

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3205(a)(1) is invalid insofar as it excludes qualified personnel from communicating routine consent information under appropriate physician responsibility. Accurate content remains required; individualized diagnosis, clinical assessment and professional responsibility are not eliminated.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent from invalidation. The judgment is 5–4.

**Controlling explanation:** We invalidate §3205(a)(1) insofar as it excludes qualified personnel from communicating routine consent information under appropriate physician responsibility. The record establishes meaningful additional demands from requiring a physician personally to deliver information that trained personnel can competently communicate. The State's general interest in professional responsibility does not substantiate that exclusion.

Qualified delivery with physician availability and responsibility is a concrete, practicable alternative. The State has not adequately explained why that alternative fails to serve informed consent. We retain Akron's protection against unnecessary physician exclusivity. Individual diagnosis, assessment, treatment recommendations, and physician responsibility remain distinct; today's holding does not authorize unqualified medical practice.

The remedy removes the exclusive-speaker condition and its dependent sanctions and certifications. It preserves the information requirement and otherwise applicable professional qualifications.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Akron: its protection against unnecessary exclusive physician delivery is retained and applied.
- Ohio v. Akron: parental notification does not independently resolve adult clinical counseling.
- Danforth: permits informed consent without validating every staffing restriction.

##### The mandatory 24-hour interval

**Controlling proposition:** The compulsory twenty-four-hour interval in §3205(a)(1)–(2) is invalid under the meaningful-interference branch. The State has not substantiated the justification and fit of a uniform interval or adequately answered practical, substantially less burdensome means of securing informed and voluntary consent. Timing-dependent certification and enforcement are severed.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent. The judgment is 5–4; no finding that every application is a substantial obstacle is required for this distinct ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The mandatory interval in §3205(a)(1)–(2) imposes concrete delay and, in its demonstrated operation, additional travel, expense, and scheduling difficulties. These effects exceed trivial inconvenience for the affected patients.

The State may ensure that consent is informed and voluntary. It has not adequately justified a universal compulsory interval after the required information has been received. The gravity of the decision does not establish that every informed patient requires another day, another journey, or another disruption of work and family responsibilities.

Individual counseling, an opportunity for questions, and time when the patient needs or requests it provide concrete alternatives. The State has not substantiated why those alternatives inadequately serve informed decisionmaking. The emergency exception does not answer the burdens imposed on patients who do not meet its terms.

The interval therefore fails the meaningful-interference branch independently of whether every application creates a substantial obstacle. Where the interval does create such an obstacle, it is independently invalid. We retain Akron's holding against an unjustified fixed waiting requirement.

The defect concerns the compulsory interval itself, which cannot be made valid by identifying patients who would voluntarily wait. We sever that condition, including its incorporated certification and enforcement consequences, while preserving valid consent duties. Genuine clinical need or unresolved consent may require additional time; the State may not prescribe this unsupported universal delay. [Akron, 462 U.S. at 446–451](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/462/416/).

**Precedent treatment:**

- Akron: its fixed-waiting protection is retained and applied.
- Hodgson and Ohio v. Akron: parental-involvement decisions do not supply a justification for universal adult reconsideration.
- Roe and Doe: the protected decision and necessary timely care remain independently binding.

##### One-parent informed consent and independent judicial bypass

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3206's one-parent informed-consent structure survives this facial challenge with its emergency and family-substitution provisions and an effective independent judicial route. A minor may seek court authorization without first approaching a parent; maturity, capacity and actual informed consent, or alternatively her best interests, support authorization through confidential, expedited proceedings with counsel.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** The Court sustains the one-parent structure and independent bypass presented here. Bellotti identifies maturity and best interests as distinct routes to judicial authorization; Ashcroft sustains a parental-consent arrangement with effective judicial safeguards. Pennsylvania permits the minor to choose the judicial route without first requesting a parent's consent. It supplies counsel, confidentiality and prompt rulings, and requires authorization when either applicable route is established. The statute also accounts for emergency treatment, incest involving the father, parental unavailability and custodial arrangements. These features answer the claim that the structure necessarily delegates an unreviewable parental veto. The record establishes no present failure to provide the bypass's required operation; a past concern about preparations cannot be substituted for such a finding. Sustaining this structure does not declare every safeguard constitutionally indispensable or resolve every conceivable administration dispute. The ancillary information required for parental consent and the asserted attendance operation are treated separately. No independent adult-incapacity claim is decided, and medical emergency care cannot await parental or judicial proceedings.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bellotti v. Baird: applies the distinct maturity/informed-consent and best-interests routes rather than merging them into cumulative conditions.
- Planned Parenthood Association of Kansas City v. Ashcroft: follows its sustention of parental consent supported by effective expedited judicial safeguards.
- Hodgson v. Minnesota and Ohio v. Akron Center: apply their treatment of usable independent bypass without converting parental responsibility into an adult spouse's authority.

**Statutory terms and construction:** Unemancipated minors under eighteen ordinarily need their own informed consent plus one parent's. Only the mother is required where the father participated in incest. If both parents are deceased or unavailable within reasonable time and manner, a guardian may consent; after divorce the custodial parent's consent suffices; if neither parent nor guardian is available within reasonable time and manner, an adult standing in place of a parent may consent. Parent/guardian considers only the child's or ward's best interests. Medical emergency excuses compliance. A minor may elect court without first requesting parental consent. Maturity/capacity plus actual consent and best interests are alternative judicial routes. Counsel is provided absent private counsel or a knowing intelligent waiver. Trial ruling is due within three business days of application; appeal ruling within five business days of filing. Proceedings and records are confidential. The separate adult-incompetence text presents no developed independent challenge here. These are the terms of §3206(a)–(f) and (h), including the provisions quoted by the Third Circuit.

##### Accurate information used for informed parental consent

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3206 may use accurate material medical information and concise, nonmisleading notices concerning available materials, possible assistance and paternal support to inform a parent's consent. This sustention creates no independent parent-specific waiting period, physician-only parental-delivery command or compulsory clinic visit; optional materials need not be read or endorsed, and the paternal-support provision states: “In the case of rape, this information may be omitted.” No police report is required.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: 7 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** The Court permits the limited accurate information described here to inform a parent's consent. The parental role concerns the minor's best interests, and reliable information about medical choices, possible support and available assistance can serve that role. Ashcroft and the minors' cases supply the distinct parental-participation setting; they do not create a husband's comparable authority over an adult wife. The required notices remain factual and qualified. Benefits may be available, paternal liability does not promise payment, and the paternal-support provision states: “In the case of rape, this information may be omitted.” No report to law enforcement is required. Optional materials require neither acceptance nor endorsement. The contrary argument treats every mention of childbirth-related resources as impermissible persuasion. That conclusion does not follow when the actual command supplies accurate, limited information for informed participation and leaves the independent judicial route available. The district court's use of §3205 to explain informed parental content does not put every adult timing or delivery mechanism into §3206. The Court therefore decides content separately and supplies no new parent-specific waiting period, nondelegable medical-speaker rule or attendance command.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Planned Parenthood Association of Kansas City v. Ashcroft, Hodgson v. Minnesota and Ohio v. Akron Center: apply the distinct permissible parental-guidance setting and actual bypass safeguards.
- Akron and Thornburgh: categorical opposition to accurate concise assistance or alternative notices merely because they favor childbirth does not control this narrow factual-content permission; other delay and medical-delivery questions remain separately counted.

**Statutory terms and construction:** The district court used §3205 to interpret informed parental consent at 744 F. Supp. 1382–1384. That is a lower-court construction, not an express §3206 incorporation of every §3205 timing/delivery mechanism. Assistance may be available, not guaranteed. Paternal liability includes the father who offered to pay for abortion; “In the case of rape, this information may be omitted.” This permission has no law-enforcement reporting condition and does not guarantee collection. Materials are furnished if chosen, without required acceptance, reading or ideological assent. The §3206 emergency, incest, unavailability, custody and bypass terms remain as stated in the preceding holding.

Under the Court's separately counted five-Justice waiting and exclusive-delivery holdings, those invalid requirements cannot survive through incorporation into parental consent. This conforming consequence does not add the dissenters from those judgments to their rationale coalitions. The parental-content holding creates no additional parental timing, speaker or attendance obligation.

##### The parental-attendance objection and the denial of special relief

**Controlling proposition:** The Court denies an attendance-specific vacatur, remand or interim injunction, 9–0. Five Justices rest on the absence of an established enacted or authoritative compulsory-visit command. Four additionally would sustain the assumed operation with the available bypass. Only the first ground commands a majority.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on their additional assumed-operation ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 3206 does not expressly require an additional parental clinic visit. District findings 232 and 238–259 describe anticipated practices and resulting burdens; the Third Circuit reserved the correctness of that statutory interpretation in footnote 23. Those findings do not establish the additional legal command necessary for the requested distinct relief. The Court therefore denies a special attendance order.

The five-Justice rationale does not approve a later compulsory visit and does not treat bypass as a universal cure. If an enacted or authoritative practice requires an additional visit, its actual burdens, available alternatives and justification must be examined under the complete standard. A minor need not first seek parental consent before using the confidential and expedited maturity/informed-consent or best-interests route. That route's practical availability matters, but its mere existence cannot substitute for analysis of a different challenged duty.

White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas also deny relief. They would additionally sustain the assumed attendance operation on this facial record because of the independently usable bypass. Their four-person alternative rationale is not a holding of the Court.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bellotti, Hodgson and Ohio v. Akron: preserve effective independent authorization at their actual scope.
- The general six-step standard: an additional legal obligation must first be established; no invented attendance condition is approved.

##### Spousal notice and effective control over the protected decision

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3209 is invalid because its compulsory notice and certification condition gives spouses practical control over protected previability decisions for a large fraction of the independently defined women whom it requires to disclose against their wishes; voluntary consultation does not cure that operation. Its exceptions do not adequately protect that group, so the notice requirement and dependent enforcement are severed and enjoined; no general numerical facial-review formula is adopted.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** The State cannot place an adult patient's protected decision under her spouse's practical control. Danforth forbids a spousal veto over abortion. The present law formally requires notification rather than consent, but the distinction does not answer what the compulsory disclosure does to women whose decisions it changes. A condition that predictably enables prevention of the protected procedure cannot become valid simply because it stops short of giving the spouse a written power of refusal. The Court applies Danforth to the operation established in this record, not to an assumption that every notice requirement has the same effect.

Most married patients voluntarily discuss abortion with their spouses. Their willingness to communicate supports voluntary consultation, but it cannot measure the constitutionality of a duty imposed on those who would not disclose. The relevant group is identified independently of the constitutional conclusion: married women whom the statute requires to notify against their wishes after its actual exceptions are taken into account. The district findings establish substantial physical, psychological, economic and family pressures within that group. For a large fraction of these women, the mandatory notice would expose the decision to effective obstruction. The Court does not invent a percentage, classify every marriage as coercive, or treat an isolated difficult application as sufficient for categorical relief.

The exceptions must be considered as they operate. A woman can provide a signed substitute statement that her spouse is not the father or could not be located after diligent effort. She can also invoke pregnancy resulting from spousal sexual assault described in section 3128 and reported to law enforcement with jurisdiction. That incorporated law, section 3128(c), requires the victim or her agent personally to report the assault to an agency with jurisdiction within 90 days of commission of the offense; a survivor who does not report within that window does not qualify for this particular substitute statement, though the not-located or anticipated-injury substitutes may still be available to her. District finding 297 explains how the reporting restriction further narrows the exception for women unable to report the assault promptly. That a survivor did not meet the 90-day condition does not establish that notifying her husband is safe or that he will not obstruct her decision; the condition measures only whether one particular substitute statement is available, not the risk notice actually poses. The remaining substitute concerns reason to believe that notice is likely to cause bodily injury to the woman by her spouse or another person. A medical emergency independently excuses notice. The notice and exception statements need not be notarized, but must warn that false statements are punishable. The 90-day condition explains why these exceptions do not adequately protect the compelled group; it is not itself a separate holding, and the Court does not adjudicate the validity of section 3128(c) as a criminal-law reporting requirement, which is not before it. A patient's separate paternal-support disclosure under section 3205(a)(2)(iii) is unaffected by whether she qualifies for this notice exception; that disclosure's rape-omission permission carries no reporting condition of its own, as stated above.

Those provisions do not generally excuse threatened financial retaliation, psychological coercion, injury to children or others, exposure of private information, or divorce and custody threats. The record also shows why some endangered women cannot safely use the available certification routes. The Court neither denies the protection those exceptions provide nor rewrites likely bodily injury as every form of coercion. Their incomplete coverage matters because the State itself makes disclosure a prerequisite to lawful care. Poverty or an abusive relationship alone is not the challenged governmental act; the additional legal condition activates the demonstrated obstacle.

The State's interests in marital communication and potential life do not supply a spouse with supervisory authority over an adult patient. The minors' cases rest on parents' responsibilities for children and on independent bypass safeguards. They therefore do not support treating a wife as a minor or requiring her to establish an acceptable reason for secrecy. Protecting voluntary communication remains permissible. Enforcing an ideal of marital consultation through a condition that produces practical control over the protected choice does not.

Ohio v. Akron Center's facial-review discussion does not require counting women who voluntarily notify as constitutionally valid restrictive applications of this law. The Court confines this conclusion to the proved operation of the notice condition and the group it actually compels; it does not adopt a numerical cutoff or a universal numerical facial-challenge formula. Adding judicial exceptions for every uncovered threat would reconstruct the law and still force endangered patients to qualify for secrecy. Severance provides effective relief without that reconstruction. The judgment in No. 91-902 is affirmed, and the notice-linked report fields are separately removed. Independent spousal speech, equality and generalized privacy grounds are unnecessary to this controlling liberty judgment and remain undecided by the Court.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth, 428 U.S. 52 (1976): extended from formal spousal consent to notice proved to give effective control; voluntary consultation remains lawful.
- Ohio v. Akron Center, 497 U.S. 502, 514 (1990): its no-valid-applications formulation is qualified for this notice condition; voluntary disclosure by unaffected women does not defeat the demonstrated facial defect.
- Bellotti II, Ashcroft and Hodgson: distinguished because parental responsibility and effective bypass for minors do not establish marital authority over an adult.
- Roe and Doe: supply the protected decision and its life-and-health boundary.

For the five Justices adopting the complete framework, the established substantial obstacle independently ends the justification inquiry. Kennedy joins this notice-specific effective-control rationale and the affected-group rule, without joining the full framework. No compelling-interest assertion rescues the obstacle, and no universal numerical percentage is established for other cases.

##### Confidential referring-source identification

**Controlling proposition:** The referring-source component of §3214(a)(1) is invalid insofar as it demands identification solely because a physician, agency or service made a referral. The remaining identification duties and otherwise lawful inquiries based on actual clinical responsibility are preserved.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent. The judgment is 5–4.

**Controlling explanation:** The referring-source component of §3214(a)(1) requires a separate result. The record identifies physicians who will stop referring patients if their names must be reported, even under the confidentiality safeguards. That loss of access to a patient's existing source of referral information is concrete meaningful interference; the patient need not prove the disappearance of every alternative provider.

We recognize that a referring physician may sometimes perform a clinical duty. That possibility does not justify collecting every referring physician's, agency's, or service's identity solely because it made a referral. The performing provider and existing clinical records supply a practical source for ordinary follow-up. Where a particular person's clinical responsibility or suspected misconduct requires investigation, appropriately targeted lawful inquiry remains available.

The State has not substantiated the additional value of blanket referral identification or adequately answered those narrower means. We sever the referring-source reporting component. Identification of the performing physician, other independently required clinical participants, and the facility remains outside that invalidation. This holding does not create anonymity for clinical misconduct or prevent otherwise lawful investigation.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: useful confidential reporting remains permissible; confidentiality does not answer every demonstrated access burden.
- Thornburgh: actual deterrence and information demanded must be examined separately.
- Brockett and Pennsylvania severability law: remove the referring-source component without reopening independently lawful identification duties.

##### Confidential reporting of medical necessity under §3214(a)(8)

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3214(a)(8) may require a confidential account of the physician's basis for invoking §3211(b)(1)'s medical-necessity predicate. Recording that judgment does not establish a new substantive necessity test, require advance approval, adjudicate every other postviability restriction or create an offense merely because another physician later disagrees.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** The Court sustains a record of the reason an existing medical-necessity predicate was invoked. Danforth permits appropriately directed confidential health reporting; a physician's explanation can support both medical knowledge and lawful review of compliance. The challenged field asks why §3211(b)(1) applied. It does not itself change that provision's substantive requirements or transfer the initial treatment judgment to an official. The district court's concern that retrospective scrutiny may chill professional judgment deserves attention, but documentation alone does not establish the anticipated unlawful interference. This decision neither authorizes punishment merely because a predicted harm failed to occur nor supplies a new general immunity from enforcement. Liability continues to depend on the actual lawful offense and its required predicates. The report remains confidential under the safeguards accepted here and omits the patient's name. The Court does not decide the independent constitutionality of every later-abortion restriction, substitute this basis field for gestational-age reporting, or foreclose a concrete challenge to administration that exceeds the duty sustained today.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth: applies the permissibility of appropriately directed confidential reporting rather than inferring invalidity from the existence of documentation.
- Thornburgh v. American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists: its attention to actual burdens and disclosure consequences is preserved; it does not automatically invalidate this distinct confidential medical-basis field.

**Statutory terms and construction:** Section 3214(a)(8) requests the basis for the judgment that an abortion under §3211(b)(1) was necessary to prevent death or substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. That predicate concerns the referenced rule for abortion at twenty-four or more weeks, not merely a viability/gestational-age result. The individual report excludes patient names; actual confidentiality safeguards are accepted in this litigation. These terms appear in §§3214(a) and (a)(8); the confidentiality findings are stated in the lower-court reporting discussions.

**Application under the complete standard:** Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter additionally hold that, even assuming meaningful interference from documenting this judgment, the State has substantiated the requirement's fit to review of the particular medical-necessity judgment invoked under §3211(b)(1). Bare totals or an unexplained checkbox would not adequately supply that function. The report concerns clinical work already performed, preserves confidentiality and requires no new testing or advance permission. No identified practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately supplies the particular information. The District Court's inference that written reasons necessarily chill lawful clinical judgment does not establish invalidity at this bounded scope. Confidentiality alone is not decisive. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join the limited reporting permission and statutory construction without adopting this general justification standard.

##### Confidential reporting of an emergency judgment under §3214(a)(10)

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3214(a)(10) may require a confidential after-the-fact account of the medical judgment that an emergency excused compliance with the Act. It does not require permission or reporting before treatment, narrow the prospective good-faith emergency construction, or make professional disagreement alone a newly punishable violation.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** The Court sustains documentation of the emergency actually invoked. Thus the physician need not wait until the feared injury occurs before treating the patient and later describing the judgment. The contrary claim assumes that recording reasons itself authorizes hostile or medically unreasonable enforcement. It does not. Existing lawful offense requirements continue to control; another professional's later disagreement alone is not a new offense created by the reporting provision. The Court preserves the confidential report while leaving concrete challenges to a demand for preclearance, delayed treatment or materially different enforcement for an actual dispute.

##### Confidential reporting of the diagnostic basis under §3214(a)(11)

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3214(a)(11), incorporating §3210(a), may require reporting the actual inquiries, examinations, tests and diagnostic basis used to determine probable gestational age under the prudent-physician standard. It does not prescribe additional medically unnecessary tests; §3210(a)'s exception remains when medical emergency prevents compliance, and the separate age-result field is not newly adjudicated.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** The Court sustains the report of the diagnostic process actually used. It allows the department to understand the basis of the diagnosis without replacing the governing medical standard. The emergency-prevents-compliance exception remains operative. The Court does not reopen the unappealed underlying ascertainment requirement or age-result field, and does not authorize punishment simply because another clinician would have selected a different defensible inquiry. ##### Notice-compliance and nonnotification-reason reports

**Controlling proposition:** The notice-given and reasons-for-nonnotification components of section 3214(a)(12) are invalid and severed because they enforce or reproduce section 3209's unconstitutional disclosure condition. The independent confidential marital-status question remains, and no report or certification may restore the invalid notice obligation.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** A reporting requirement cannot preserve the very condition that the substantive holding removes. Section 3214(a)(12) asks whether a married patient notified her spouse and, if not, why notice was withheld. Those fields track compliance with section 3209 and require an explanation for exercising the protected decision without compelled disclosure. Their operation cannot be justified merely by renaming the demand medical reporting.

Danforth's permission for confidential health information does not authorize enforcement of a constitutionally invalid spousal prerequisite. The Court therefore reverses approval of the notice-given and nonnotice-reason fields and directs their removal, with dependent enforcement, in the implementing proceedings. The marital-status question is separately operative and receives a separate holding. Act 64's severability clause permits invalid words or applications to be removed while provisions that can operate independently continue. The contrary suggestion that all marital data must either stand or fall together overlooks those distinct functions. The remedy removes notice enforcement without disabling lawful confidential administration.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: confidential-reporting permission does not validate enforcement of an invalid spousal condition.
- Brockett: supports relief confined to the unconstitutional component; state severability law governs the surviving text.

##### The independently severable marital-status field

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3214(a)(12)'s confidential inquiry whether the patient was married is sustained and severed from its invalid notice-compliance and nonnotification-reason questions. The field supplies demographic information without requiring spousal disclosure or an explanation for withholding it.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** Whether a patient was married is distinct from whether she notified her spouse. The former may be collected as confidential demographic information; the latter fields track an invalid legal obligation. Danforth permits appropriately directed confidential health records, and the record accepts the adequacy of the patient-protection safeguards. No patient's name is placed in the individual procedure report, and no finding establishes public release of patient identities.

Act 64 expressly preserves words and provisions capable of operating without the invalid text. The marital-status question can operate on its own and does not need a notice duty to explain its informational function. The Court therefore sustains that question while removing the notice and reasons components. The strongest contrary argument treats all three as a single mechanism, but their statutory functions are separable. This narrow holding creates no general governmental entitlement to intimate information, and it does not resolve a future claim of inadequate confidentiality or abusive collection. Forms and enforcement must conform to the surviving field rather than reconstruct the invalid notice requirement.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Brockett: supports component-limited relief, implemented through Act 64 and Pennsylvania severability law.

##### Public facility and affiliation reports

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3207(b)'s compulsory public-release condition is unconstitutional as applied to the plaintiff facilities and their covered reports. Confidential collection and otherwise lawful official access and public-expenditure disclosure remain. The cause is remanded to identify the protected reports and conform the injunction.

**Authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would sustain public release. The 5–4 holding supplies plaintiff-specific relief, not automatic statewide facial invalidation.

**Controlling explanation:** Collection and public release are distinct. Section 3207(b)'s public-release condition uses receipt of State-appropriated funds during the twelve calendar months **before the inspection request**. Section 3214(f)'s condition uses the twelve calendar months **before filing the quarterly report**. The latter preserves confidential treatment after a declaration of no qualifying funds unless contrary evidence establishes receipt. The former limits access to nonfunded-facility reports to specified officials performing their official duties.

The District Court found a supported risk of increased harassment and withdrawal from participation in medical assistance. Existing publicity does not make additional disclosure consequence-free. We accept those findings as sufficient to establish meaningful interference for the plaintiff providers, without assuming that institutional reports publicly identify individual patients or that every additional disclosure necessarily creates a substantial obstacle.

Public accountability is a permissible objective. But the conditions expose affiliation information and totals for all abortions upon receipt of qualifying funds, including funds for other services. The State must explain the fit of those additional disclosures, not merely invoke taxpayers' interest in expenditures.

Existing public expenditure records, disclosures tied to funded services, and confidential examination of underlying records provide concrete alternatives. Confidential examination alone is not identical to public accountability; public reporting of actual expenditures and funded services addresses that distinct objective. On this record, the State has not adequately justified requiring these plaintiff providers to expose the broader reports despite the demonstrated interference.

We therefore reverse approval of these public-release conditions **as applied to the plaintiff providers and their covered reports**. That relief is narrower than invalidating every possible institutional disclosure. The District Court shall identify the protected reports and conform the injunction; any extension to additional providers requires an adequate evidentiary and procedural basis.

Confidential collection, lawful official access, and ordinary disclosure of public expenditures are preserved. No general immunity from financial accountability is created. Statistical use of trimester categories does not restore the constitutional trimester timetable. [District Court findings and reporting analysis, 744 F. Supp. 1323](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/744/1323/1797783/); [Court of Appeals analysis, 947 F.2d 682](https://openjurist.org/947/f2d/682/planned-parenthood-of-southeastern-pennsylvania-v-casey).

For §3207(b), the challenged additional exposure includes affiliated and commonly controlled entities. The operative funding lookback is the twelve calendar months before a request to inspect or copy. The Court does not invalidate the separate collection or updating duties and does not create an exemption from otherwise lawful disclosure of actual public expenditures.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Danforth: confidential health administration remains distinct from public release.
- Thornburgh: actual disclosure burdens matter; institutional affiliation information is not equated with patient identities.
- Brockett: relief is confined to the proven applications, preserving severable collection and lawful accountability.

##### Independent speech challenge to medical consent information

**Controlling proposition:** The First Amendment does not forbid requiring the accurate, material medical facts in section 3205(a)(1) as part of informed consent to this treatment. This is a bounded consent rule, not a holding that paid medical speech is categorically commercial or that any truthful statement can be compelled.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: 8 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** A physician's speech does not lose constitutional protection because the physician is paid. Riley prevents that shortcut and requires examination of what the government actually compels. Here the required statements explain the proposed procedure, its material risks and alternatives, probable gestational age and the medical risks of childbirth. Their connection to the patient's authorization of treatment supplies the relevant justification.

Danforth permits informed consent, and the First Amendment does not require the State to permit treatment without the accurate material information that makes consent meaningful. The rule requires factual disclosure, not personal acceptance of the State's beliefs. Wooley's protection against compelled ideological affirmation therefore does not invalidate these particular medical commands. The Court preserves the statutory health defense and the limits of accuracy and materiality. It neither accepts a supposed commercial-speech concession nor creates general professional-speech immunity. A false, irrelevant or ideological script, and the separately invalidated exclusive-delivery and timing requirements, would require their own analysis.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Riley v. National Federation of the Blind, 487 U.S. 781 (1988): payment alone does not remove speech protection.
- Danforth: supplies the legitimate informed-consent connection.
- Wooley v. Maynard, 430 U.S. 705 (1977): distinguished because factual treatment information does not require ideological affirmation.

Stone concurs in the judgment only on this independent First Amendment ground. His agreement supplies no additional rationale join.

##### Independent speech challenge to the three ancillary notices

**Controlling proposition:** The First Amendment does not facially invalidate the concise truthful notices of available materials, possible assistance and paternal liability at the scopes separately sustained. None requires ideological assent or endorsement of a listed agency; the notice of materials remains distinct from chosen receipt.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: 7 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** The Court examines each command rather than calling the whole scheme commercial speech. Riley protects speech even when compensation is involved, and Wooley protects against forced ideological affirmation. Those principles do not convert the particular facts here into compelled belief. Availability is a fact distinct from an agency's advice; assistance is described as possible, not guaranteed; paternal liability is a legal responsibility, not a promise of payment. The paternal-support provision states: “In the case of rape, this information may be omitted.” No police report is required. The separate disclosure-health defense remains operative.

The notice does not itself require acceptance of the available materials. Clinicians may correct the information or criticize a listed agency. These limited functions bear a concrete connection to informed choice. The contrary argument rightly identifies a constitutional problem with compulsory ideology or deceptive information, but it does not establish that these bounded commands require either. The Court does not hold that truth alone permits any compelled communication. Concrete falsehood, coercive distribution or materially different enforcement remains open to challenge. Blackmun does not reach these three independent speech grounds after invalidating the mandatory ancillary commands on due process grounds.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Riley: forecloses the compensation-based shortcut; each command is examined independently.
- Wooley: protects against compelled ideological assent, absent from the sustained commands.
- Danforth: supplies the informed-choice connection.

##### Independent speech challenge to chosen materials and lawful receipt certification

**Controlling proposition:** The First Amendment does not invalidate state publication and genuinely chosen receipt of objective materials, or a certificate confined to receipt of lawful information rather than ideological assent. This principle does not supply a vote sustaining a contested underlying notice, timing or delivery obligation.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: 8 direct rationale joins including the author. 

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes information a patient chooses to receive from speech the State forces her to endorse. Section 3208 permits publication within its objective and accurate statutory terms. Section 3205(a)(3) supplies a copy only if the woman chooses to review it. Neither provision requires acceptance of a belief, and a clinician remains free to correct or criticize the material. Wooley's protection against compelled ideological affirmation therefore does not invalidate those limited functions.

The written certificate similarly records receipt rather than assent. Danforth permits documentation of informed consent, while Riley prevents treating compensation as an automatic answer to a speech objection. The relevant justification is the actual consent function, not the speaker's paid status. The Court sustains the basic documentation principle without converting any Justice's objection to an underlying duty into approval of that duty. The distinct votes on ancillary, timing and delivery links remain controlling for those applications. Concrete deceptive distribution, forced reading or a certificate demanding personal agreement would present a different question.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Wooley: distinguished at voluntary receipt and factual certification scope; ideological assent remains protected.
- Danforth: applied to consent documentation.
- Riley: paid status supplies no categorical exception to speech protection.

Stone concurs in the judgment only on this independent First Amendment ground. His agreement supplies no additional rationale join.

The minors' decisions retain their actual independent-authorization safeguards. Marks supplies no additional synthesis because five directly join the complete framework.

##### Stone — Opinion of the Court, and separately, concurring in the judgment

Chief Justice Stone delivers the Court's complete six-step standard, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter. His position preserves the protected decision while permitting supported regulation. A substantial obstacle is independently forbidden; a remaining severe intrusion demands compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring; a meaningful lesser intrusion requires substantiated justification and fit. The last protection addresses real interference before it forecloses care. It does not presume every inconvenience unconstitutional or give courts general authority to select their preferred health policy.

Stone's agreement to remove the trimester timetable depends on adoption of the entire operative substitute. That condition is satisfied by the five direct joins to the framework, not by adding votes from Justices who would abandon Roe. The previability standard remains constant while medical evidence may support stage-specific regulation. Viability remains individualized, and necessary life-and-health care remains timely at every stage. Moral disapproval cannot itself justify obstruction; accurate information, competent practice and supported administration remain permissible.

The framework produces concrete results. Unnecessary exclusive physician delivery and the unsupported fixed interval fail even without deciding that every affected patient faces a substantial obstacle. Indiscriminate identification based on referral alone fails the State's substantiation obligation. The public-release record supports relief for the plaintiff facilities, with the broader scope tied to actual proof and lawful procedure. Spousal notice independently fails because it exposes the protected decision to effective control. Each invalid obligation carries its dependent certification and enforcement with it.

Stone sustains accurate clinical content, the qualified ancillary notices, chosen materials, the actual one-parent structure and usable independent bypass, and bounded confidential clinical reports. The paternal-support rape omission carries no reporting condition. He preserves professional judgment without conferring immunity from misconduct or granting medical associations a legislative veto. He joins the denial of special materials and attendance proceedings on the established record.

Stone concurs in Souter's separate independent speech and incorporated-parental-content judgments without joining those rationales. His narrower judgment agreement is not counted as a rationale join. His complete constitutional framework is nevertheless the Court's governing standard because five Justices directly adopt it.

##### Blackmun — Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part

Justice Blackmun joins the complete six-step framework and its integrated replacement of the trimester timetable. His joinder includes the categorical substantial-obstacle prohibition, the residual severe-interference rule, the meaningful-lesser-interference rule, its proof allocation, clinical safeguards and remedial limits. It is a direct joinder to the Court's governing standard, not merely support for isolated safeguards or a concurrence in the result.

Blackmun continues to prefer strict scrutiny for every nontrivial interference with the protected decision. He nonetheless accepts the Court's complete rule as an adequate constitutional protection: it forbids preclusive obstacles outright, preserves exacting review of severe interference and requires actual justification before lesser restrictions accumulate into barriers. Unlike removal of the timetable without a replacement, this holding establishes operative protection throughout previability. The State cannot obtain approval merely by showing that some patients overcome the restriction.

He joins invalidation of fixed delay, unnecessary physician exclusivity, blanket referral-source identification, spousal notice and its report fields. He also joins the plaintiff-specific public-release relief. Although he would favor broader disclosure protection where the evidence supports it, the Court's limitation of relief to the proven applications does not warrant withholding his join. Confidential collection and the particular medical-basis reports remain permissible under their bounded clinical and confidentiality safeguards.

Blackmun dissents from the mandatory availability, assistance and paternal-support notices and their certification and parental-content dependencies. In his view, the State's selection of these childbirth-favoring messages intrudes into the decision without adequate justification. His objection concerns compulsory delivery, not truthful State publication or genuinely chosen receipt. He accepts the rape omission's actual terms and does not reach the additional independent ancillary-speech grounds after finding the compulsory commands invalid on liberty grounds. He joins the other bounded consent, emergency, materials and parental-structure judgments stated in the tables.

##### Stevens — Opinion concurring

Justice Stevens joins the complete framework and its applications. His concern is both the severity of a restriction and whether the restriction has a sufficient connection to a permissible objective. A right is insufficiently protected if a State may impose avoidable barriers whenever the last provider remains open or some patients manage to comply. Conversely, a demonstrated burden does not make a useful safety or consent requirement automatically invalid.

The Court's definitions make that inquiry administrable. Challengers must identify the duty, affected population and concrete causal burden. The comparison removes the challenged obligation while retaining actual circumstances; it does not measure every law against ideal access. Courts examine identified practicable alternatives rather than demanding perfect legislation. The State may rely on experience and reasonable prediction, but must answer the obligation's additional burden rather than repeating an abstract interest.

Stevens agrees to removal of the timetable because the complete substitute is adopted in the same holding. He joins Akron-based invalidation of fixed delay and routine physician exclusivity and the separate lesser-burden grounds supporting those results. He sustains concise accurate notices at the Court's limited scope, distinguishing voluntary informed persuasion from an obstacle imposed to deter exercise of the right.

He joins the referral-source invalidation because the documented deterrence requires an answer beyond the possibility that a referring person sometimes performs a clinical duty. He also joins the limited public-release relief. Public accountability remains legitimate, but the State has not adequately justified the additional breadth of the plaintiff facilities' disclosures after identified narrower means are considered. That conclusion does not treat institutional totals as patient records or require invalidation for every facility. He joins the remaining component judgments and the independent speech opinions at the scopes stated.

##### O'Connor — Concurrence explaining the complete standard

Justice O'Connor joins the entire six-step standard and the integrated replacement of the trimester timetable. The initial assessment of interference remains independent of the State's asserted benefits. A court must first determine what the law actually does. It may not describe a serious barrier as slight merely because the State invokes an important objective.

She accepts the prohibition on substantial obstacles and the separate exacting review of other severe interference. Their sequence prevents a court from using strict-scrutiny terminology to excuse an already forbidden obstacle. Necessary clinical regulation remains possible where its actual medical justification, tailoring and available care support it.

She also accepts the defined lesser-burden substantiation rule. Its trigger is concrete, attributable, nontrivial interference, rather than the mere existence of abortion regulation. It requires substantiation and fit, not strict scrutiny for every inconvenience. The State need not commission a study for every requirement, prove scientific certainty or disprove imaginary alternatives. It must respond to a concrete, practicable and substantially less burdensome means that would adequately accomplish its purpose. Those limitations make the rule compatible with genuine legislative responsibility while preventing unexplained accumulated restrictions.

Applying that standard, she joins invalidation of this fixed interval and routine physician exclusivity. The actual additional visits, costs and scheduling consequences trigger review; the State's general appeals to reflection and medical responsibility do not adequately answer the identified alternatives. Her conclusion does not rest on a determination that all informed-consent rules or all gestationally relevant safeguards are invalid. She joins the referral-source and limited public-release holdings for the same requirement-specific reasons.

O'Connor joins denial of special parental-attendance relief because no additional enacted or authoritative visit command has been established. She does not join the four-Justice alternative that would sustain the assumed operation. She sustains the actual one-parent and bypass structure and the Court's other bounded permissions. Spousal notice fails under the controlling liberty analysis. She additionally retains her narrow equality and family-autonomy grounds; no majority adopts those independent grounds. Her agreement with White on the intermediate-scrutiny tier for the asserted sex classification does not extend to his contrary application.

##### Kennedy — Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part

Justice Kennedy joins preservation of the protected previability decision, individualized viability and necessary life-and-health protection. He also agrees that the independent trimester timetable should end. Those conclusions do not lead him to join the Court's complete general scrutiny sequence or the State-substantiation obligation for every demonstrated meaningful lesser burden.

He would permit reasonable regulation that respects the protected choice and the relevant clinical boundaries without requiring the State to answer a less burdensome alternative whenever a nontrivial additional demand is shown. In his view, the new allocation gives courts too broad a role in evaluating ordinary medical and administrative arrangements. His concern is the general governing rule, not a denial that actual burdens and exceptions matter.

Kennedy therefore dissents from invalidation of the fixed interval, routine physician-delivery requirement, confidential referring-source field and the two public-release conditions. ##### Souter — Opinion concurring

Justice Souter joins the complete six-step framework, the integrated timetable replacement and its applications. The Court has identified the particular implementation being displaced and the protections taking its place. It preserves the underlying liberty, fixes the sequence of review, assigns proof according to the issue being decided and retains direct precedent against the unjustified waiting and speaker conditions. That supplies an adequate legal basis for changing the timetable without weakening the practical right.

The distinction between State justification and judicial policymaking is central to his joinder. Challengers must establish actual interference; the State may rely on experience and reasonable prediction; alternatives must be concrete and practically available. A court must identify why an explanation is insufficient rather than merely prefer a different policy. ### United States v. Dixon, No. 91-1231

Merits disposition, 1993-06-28

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued 1992-12-02; decided 1993-06-28. On writ of certiorari to District of Columbia Court of Appeals, 598 A.2d 724 (1991).

**Question:** Whether the Double Jeopardy Clause bars substantive criminal prosecutions based on conduct already tried in nonsummary criminal-contempt proceedings, including the different counts against Dixon and Foster and the continued force of Grady v. Corbin.

**Material record:** Dixon’s release order prohibited criminal offenses. After an evidentiary hearing, he was convicted of contempt for possession of cocaine with intent to distribute and received 180 days; the later prosecution concerns that same drug offense. Foster’s protection order prohibited assault and threats against his estranged wife. Her counsel prosecuted a three-day contempt trial. Foster was convicted for two assaults and acquitted of the three threats relevant here. The later indictment charges simple assault for the first assault (I), those three threats (II, III, IV), and assault with intent to kill for the second assault (V). The Government did not prosecute the first Foster proceeding, but it was a court’s criminal-contempt prosecution with punitive sanctions.

**Relief sought:** The United States seeks reversal permitting the substantive prosecutions. Both respondents seek affirmance of the bars to renewed prosecution.

All nine Justices—Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas—participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

Affirmed as to Dixon and Foster I, 6–3; reversed and remanded as to Foster II–V, 5–4. Grady is retained, 5–4. The contempt judgments remain undisturbed.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Nonsummary punitive contempt triggers double-jeopardy protection

**Controlling proposition:** A completed nonsummary criminal-contempt prosecution places the accused in criminal jeopardy; private initiation and an order’s protective purpose do not categorically remove that protection. Whether a later prosecution is for the same offense remains a separate inquiry; coercive civil sanctions, summary courtroom contempt and separate sovereigns are not decided.

**Authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that punishment for violating a court order can implicate the protection against renewed criminal prosecution. Bloom recognizes criminal contempt as a crime for procedural protection, while Gompers distinguishes a fixed punitive sanction from a civil sanction avoidable through compliance. Foster underwent criminal adjudication, including acquittals and imprisonment, under the court’s authority. The fact that his wife’s counsel initiated enforcement does not make that completed punitive trial a private civil remedy. The Government’s institutional distinction identifies why an order matters, but cannot eliminate the constitutional character of the punishment already imposed. Young recognizes the judiciary’s authority to enforce its orders; that authority does not itself answer the separate identity-of-offenses question. The Court does not impair prospective enforcement of protective orders or decide every civil or summary contempt setting. The later counts must therefore be examined under the applicable double-jeopardy rules, with their different outcomes and rationales stated separately.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bloom v. Illinois, 391 U.S.194: criminal character of punitive contempt applied.
- Gompers v. Bucks Stove & Range Co., 221 U.S.418: punitive/coercive distinction applied.
- Young v. United States ex rel. Vuitton, 481 U.S.787: independent judicial enforcement authority retained, not a categorical exception from criminal protection.

##### Previously prosecuted necessary offenses cannot be tried again

**Controlling proposition:** When a criminal-contempt adjudication necessarily prosecutes the substantive offense forbidden by the order, the Government cannot prosecute that same substantive offense again. Dixon’s drug offense and Foster I’s November assault were necessarily prosecuted in the respective contempts and are barred; this shared proposition does not supply a common analysis of Foster II–V.

**Authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Government cannot retry the substantive offenses already necessarily established in these contempts. Brown protects against successive prosecution for greater and included offenses, and Harris prevents an abstract description of a greater offense from concealing the substantive crime necessarily prosecuted within it. Dixon’s order required compliance with the criminal law, and his contempt adjudication established the same possession with intent to distribute later charged. Foster’s November assault similarly supplied the offense prosecuted and punished under his protective order. The existence and knowledge of the order do not erase the previously prosecuted substantive crime. The opposing abstract-elements view gives insufficient effect to the necessary content of these particular charges. The six Justices supporting this rule differ on the broader comparison for additional elements and on Grady’s application, but agree directly on this limited protection. No other contempt conviction is vacated, and the Court does not resolve the remaining counts by treating every act in a single episode as one offense.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Brown v. Ohio, 432 U.S.161: successive greater/included-offense protection applied.
- Harris v. Oklahoma, 433 U.S.682: necessary substantive offense cannot be erased by an abstract description of the first charge.
- Blockburger v. United States, 284 U.S.299: elements inquiry retained; disagreement over its broader application separately recorded.

##### Grady remains governing successive-prosecution law

**Controlling proposition:** Grady is not overruled: beyond Blockburger’s elements protection, a later prosecution is barred when establishing an essential element requires proof of conduct constituting an offense for which the defendant has already been prosecuted. Mere reuse of evidence or a common episode does not suffice, and Felix’s independently punishable conspiracy holding remains; no five-Justice rationale determines Grady’s application to Foster II–V in this fractured decision.

**Authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court retains the distinction between another prosecution of already prosecuted criminal conduct and the reuse of evidence about that conduct. Grady states that distinction, while Felix applies it to a separate conspiracy agreement and rejects a same-evidence rule. The Government’s concern about administering the conduct inquiry does not establish that this recent protection has become unworkable. Brown’s concern with finality after trial and the successive-prosecution line examined in Vitale support maintaining protection beyond a purely formal comparison of statutory language. The contrary proposal would discard Grady rather than merely clarify Felix; five Justices decline that change. Retention does not make all charges arising from one episode inseparable. Blackmun’s distinct view of contempt as vindication of judicial authority prevents a majority from applying this retained rule to bar Foster’s remaining counts, while four other Justices would overrule it. That fractured application must not be transformed into either an overruling or a new universally controlling contempt exception. Ashe remains separately available where an acquittal necessarily resolved an issue.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S.508: expressly retained; no overruling by this decision.
- United States v. Felix, March 25, 1992: conspiracy and evidentiary-overlap distinctions preserved, not recast as an overruling.
- Illinois v. Vitale, 447 U.S.410: successive-prosecution concern beyond formal elements supports retention.
- Brown v. Ohio, 432 U.S.161: finality and greater/included-offense protection retained.
- Ashe v. Swenson, 397 U.S.436: independent issue-preclusion rule preserved for the remanded threat charges.

#### Current-Law Effect

Effective June 28, nonsummary criminal contempt receives criminal-jeopardy protection, and Dixon/Foster I are barred 6–3. Grady survives by five votes. Foster II–V may proceed under a 5–4 judgment, subject to Ashe where applicable, but no majority adopts either the four-Justice Blockburger-only/overruling view or Blackmun’s special judicial-order distinction. These are incompatible rationales, not logical subsets; no Marks synthesis creates a general contempt exception or overrules Grady. Later courts retain the existing rule and the case-specific judgment without inventing a five-vote test.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Scalia, joined by Kennedy, compares the offenses incorporated by the orders for Dixon and Foster I, but considers Foster II–IV’s specified threats and V’s intent to kill additional elements absent from the contempt charges. Each contempt also requires an order and its known violation. They would overrule Grady, preserve Ashe’s independent protection and allow those additional charges. O’Connor, joined by Thomas, instead compares the abstract contempt elements to every substantive offense and would allow all later prosecutions; they agree with Scalia/Kennedy on overruling Grady but not on the incorporated-offense bars. Blackmun would permit all counts for a different reason: Young’s judicial-order enforcement serves interests distinct from the community’s ordinary criminal law. He retains Grady and objects to its abandonment for ordinary successive substantive prosecutions. His special contempt ground is not a logical subset of a Blockburger-only rule.

White, joined by Stevens and Souter in his additional discussion, would compare the substantive offenses actually prosecuted and bar every renewed charge. He regards adding intent to kill as no answer to the finality of an already tried assault and emphasizes acquittal finality on the threats. Souter, joined by Stevens in his further discussion, reads Nielsen, Harris and Vitale as a continuing protection against serial prosecution of the same criminal conduct, distinguishes multiple punishment from repeated trials, and opposes the II–V judgment. Only his limited Grady-retention section has the five named Court joins. Stone separately applies retained Grady count by count: Dixon’s drug conduct, Foster’s November assault, all three acquitted threats and the May assault are precisely the conduct the later charges would require proving. He treats Felix as a separate-agreement case and does not impose compulsory joinder of every charge from an episode. He would affirm all bars and therefore would not need an additional Ashe determination. That last procedural preference does not eliminate the Ashe inquiry preserved by the actual remand.

### Bray v. Alexandria Women’s Health Clinic, No. 90-985

Merits decision, January 13, 1993

#### Chronology and Posture

506 U.S. 263. Originally argued October 16, 1991; reargued October 6, 1992. On certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, which affirmed in National Organization for Women v. Operation Rescue, 914 F.2d 582 (1990); district findings and conclusions at 726 F. Supp. 1483 (E.D. Va. 1989).

The questions concern private clinic-blockade liability under the deprivation clause of 42 U.S.C. §1985(3), including class-based invidious purpose and interference with an independently protected private right, and consequences for federal fees and independently adjudicated state-law claims. Organizers seek reversal; clinics and organizations defend the relief and its state foundation.

The District Court found planned physical obstruction, risks to women needing timely treatment, substantial interstate clientele, and Virginia trespass and public-nuisance violations. It did not adjudicate the pleaded privacy theory or find a purpose to hinder state protection; the hindrance clause was not adjudicated below. The federal fee-and-cost award was $27,687.55. Business-interference claims were dismissed without prejudice, and damages were not awarded because no amount was established. These are lower-court findings and procedural actions.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participated at reargument and decision. Thomas was not seated for the original October 16, 1991 argument; the October 6, 1992 reargument permits his participation. No recusal is established.

#### Judgment

The Court reverses deprivation-clause liability, vacates the §1988 fee-and-cost award, and remands for the injunction’s independent state-law support. Six Justices reject the deprivation theory; five support the complete mandate because Souter would additionally direct consideration of hindrance. No hindrance-clause merits judgment is entered.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Private conspiracy requires purposeful interference with a privately protected right

**Controlling proposition:** A purely private conspiracy under the deprivation clause of §1985(3) must aim at interfering with a right protected against private as well as official encroachment; incidental effects are insufficient. Clinic obstruction applied to interstate and local patients alike does not establish that interference with interstate travel was a conscious objective merely because substantial numbers of patients crossed state lines.

**Authority:** Scalia Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Kennedy and Thomas: five direct supporters. Souter separately agrees with rejection of this theory without a formal join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory purpose requirement must be proved for the particular right invoked. Griffin recognizes that interstate travel may receive protection against private conspiracies, but it does not convert every injury suffered during such travel into a federal civil-rights violation. Scott requires the conspiracy to aim at interference with an independently protected right. Guest similarly distinguishes a robbery of an interstate traveler from a conspiracy directed at travel itself. Here the District Court found that substantial numbers of patients crossed state lines. That finding establishes clientele, not the object of the conspiracy. Respondents conceded that the obstruction did not distinguish interstate patients from local patients, and the demonstrated object was stopping abortions at the clinics. The serious consequences of the blockades cannot supply the missing statutory element. The Court rejects this claim without deciding that an interstate traveler loses protection upon reaching a destination, or that every nondiscriminatory local obstruction can never interfere with interstate passage.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Griffin v. Breckenridge, 403 U.S. 88 — applied; coverage of private conspiracies retains purposeful, invidiously discriminatory deprivation requirements.
- United Brotherhood of Carpenters & Joiners v. Scott, 463 U.S. 825 — applied; a private conspiracy must target a privately protected right.
- United States v. Guest, 383 U.S. 745 — applied by analogy; injury to a traveler differs from purposeful interference with interstate travel.

##### Constitutional abortion liberty does not itself supply the private-right element

**Controlling proposition:** Constitutional protection of abortion choice against governmental interference does not itself create a right against the purely private obstruction proved here. Without necessary state involvement or another right protected against private encroachment, the deprivation clause cannot sustain this theory merely because the conspiracy deliberately seeks to prevent abortions.

**Authority:** Scalia Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Kennedy and Thomas: five direct supporters of this distinct sufficient ground addressing the alternative privacy theory. Souter separately accepts Scott’s private-right limitation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional source and scope of a right remain separate from the statutory remedy invoked to enforce it. Scott rejected the use of the deprivation clause to protect First Amendment rights against a wholly private conspiracy when the underlying guarantee constrained governmental action. The same distinction governs here. Roe and Casey preserve the protected abortion decision and limit governmental interference; Casey’s complete framework governs enacted duties and their effects on access, purpose, clinical judgment and relief. It does not create a private-action cause of action or change §1985(3)’s elements. The organizers’ deliberate opposition to abortions therefore identifies the object of their conduct without supplying the required independently protected private right. Unlawful blockades remain subject to applicable state law. The Court neither retreats from Casey nor equates unlawful obstruction with protected persuasion. Whether a properly presented conspiracy to hinder state protection meets the separate statutory clause is left unresolved.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Scott, 463 U.S. 825 — applied; the underlying constitutional guarantee’s state/private distinction remains.
- Roe v. Wade, 410 U.S. 113 — preserved as carried forward and modified by Casey; no direct private-obstruction cause follows.
- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992 — preserved; its six-step state-regulation framework does not change §1985(3)’s elements.
- Great American Federal Savings & Loan Assn. v. Novotny, 442 U.S. 366 — applied; §1985(3) does not itself create the substantive right.

##### Federal merits failure does not retroactively destroy jurisdiction

**Controlling proposition:** These §1985(3) claims were not wholly insubstantial and frivolous, so their failure on the merits does not remove original federal jurisdiction. Power over the related state claims remains subject to the governing pendent-jurisdiction principles rather than automatic denial because of today’s federal merits ruling.

**Authority:** Scalia Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Kennedy and Thomas: five direct supporters. This does not establish entitlement to any particular injunction.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an unsuccessful federal claim and a jurisdictionally nonexistent claim are different things. Bell v. Hood permits the federal court to decide a substantial asserted federal right even when the plaintiff ultimately fails to establish a cause of action. The clinics’ theory relied on Griffin’s recognition of private conspiracies and on interstate travel; the dispute over its reach was sufficiently substantial to invoke federal jurisdiction. The Court’s rejection of that theory does not retrospectively make the litigation frivolous. Gibbs therefore remains the source of authority for the related state claims in this action, which began before §1367’s enactment. Those claims were actually adjudicated, and petitioners cannot obtain dismissal of the entire action simply by prevailing on the federal merits. Whether the remaining state judgments and the proper exercise of pendent jurisdiction support the injunction’s particular scope requires further consideration below. The Court neither establishes a new jurisdictional basis nor commands retention of every unsuccessful federal case’s state claims.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bell v. Hood, 327 U.S. 678 — applied; substantial federal jurisdiction differs from failure on the merits.
- United Mine Workers v. Gibbs, 383 U.S. 715 — preserved and applied to pendent jurisdiction in this pre-§1367 action.

##### Federal fees fall while independent state relief requires examination

**Controlling proposition:** The existing §1988 fee-and-cost award cannot stand on the rejected §1985(3) liability and is vacated. The injunction returns for examination of its state trespass/public-nuisance foundations, jurisdiction and lawful scope; federal merits failure alone neither sustains it nor requires its dissolution.

**Authority:** Scalia Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Kennedy and Thomas: five direct supporters. Souter agrees to remove the present federal basis but would additionally direct consideration of hindrance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that relief must rest on the legal ground that supports it. The fee-and-cost award rested on success under §1985(3), and that adjudicated basis fails today. Farrar recognizes prevailing-party eligibility when a plaintiff has an enforceable federal nominal-damages judgment, while leaving reasonable amount distinct. These plaintiffs retain no successful deprivation-clause judgment; Farrar’s amount inquiry cannot preserve this award. The result is not a determination that all state-law success in civil-rights litigation can never support fees under any circumstances. The injunction presents a separate issue because the District Court also entered state trespass and public-nuisance judgments. Gibbs prevents treating the federal merits loss as an automatic jurisdictional eraser, but it does not establish that every term of this injunction follows from state law. The lower court must identify the independent authority and permissible scope of continued relief. No replacement fee, new damages award, broadened injunction or predicted result on remand is ordered.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Farrar v. Hobby, December 14, 1992 — distinguished; enforceable federal nominal relief differs from reversal of the only adjudicated federal liability basis.
- Gibbs, 383 U.S. 715 — applied; pendent adjudication and support for particular relief remain distinct.

##### The unadjudicated hindrance theory is not resolved here

**Controlling proposition:** The Court does not adjudicate liability under §1985(3)’s separate clause concerning conspiracies to prevent or hinder constituted state authorities from giving or securing equal protection. That theory was not adjudicated below or fairly included in the granted deprivation-clause questions, and no finding establishes the required purpose to hinder state protection; the remand does not direct liability under that theory.

**Authority:** Scalia Part V, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Kennedy and Thomas: five direct supporters control scope and mandate. Ultimate substantive requirements of the separate clause remain undecided.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a distinct, undeveloped statutory theory cannot be converted into an adjudicated basis for relief on this review. The lower courts resolved deprivation-clause travel liability and state claims, not a purpose to prevent or hinder state protection. The District Court’s account of demonstrators outnumbering police describes effects and enforcement capacity; it does not itself supply the omitted finding of conspiratorial purpose. Respondents sought an opportunity to develop the theory, while petitioners disputed its pleading and availability. The question-presented limits preserved in Yee and Kamen’s distinction between a claim and additional arguments do not permit resolution of that separate claim here. The Court does not infer that the clause incorporates every requirement of the deprivation clause, nor that it dispenses with them. A later effort to pursue a properly pleaded and supported theory remains subject to ordinary procedural and substantive requirements. No such claim succeeds or fails on its merits today.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Yee v. City of Escondido, April 1, 1992 — applied; a distinct claim outside the grant differs from additional argument on a presented claim.
- Kamen v. Kemper Financial Services, Inc., 500 U.S. 90 — applied at that scope; additional legal arguments do not erase claim limits.

The full scope of Griffin’s nonracial class category and whether these blockades show invidious sex-based animus remain unresolved. Scalia Part III has four supporters and no controlling alternative holding. No Marks synthesis is needed because five direct joins support each holding. The Court does not decide whether §1985(3)’s text independently authorizes injunctive relief on a successful federal claim.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

No precedent is overruled. Griffin/Scott’s statutory limits control; Guest distinguishes travel interference from an injury to a traveler. Roe and Casey remain the constitutional abortion law without a private-action extension. Bell/Gibbs preserve jurisdiction despite merits failure. Farrar does not sustain fees on the rejected federal theory. The four-Justice class-animus and broader travel grounds do not alter operative law.

Effective January 13, 1993, this private-blockade record fails the deprivation clause’s targeted-private-right requirement. Constitutional abortion liberty does not itself supply that element. Federal liability and its fee basis fail; original jurisdiction survives and independent state relief requires examination. The Court does not decide whether women are a qualifying class or whether opposition to abortion proves or excludes sex-based animus. No controlling hindrance construction, categorical intrastate-obstruction rule or new constitutional abortion standard is adopted.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Scalia Part III, joined by White, Kennedy and Thomas; plurality only.** These four would additionally find no class-based invidious animus here. A group defined merely by the activity the alleged tortfeasor opposes is insufficient; opposition to abortion differs from targeting women because of sex. They leave open whether women can be a qualifying class. Their purpose analysis uses Griffin, Scott and Feeney’s distinction between action because of a group’s disadvantage and action despite its effect. They also favor a broader exclusion of purely local obstruction applied without discrimination against interstate travelers. Neither additional ground has five votes.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment, no joins.** The independent private-right defect suffices. Opposition to abortion alone neither proves nor disproves sex discrimination; Griffin’s full class boundary need not be decided. He joins jurisdiction, fees, state remand and hindrance nonreach, but does not endorse the plurality’s categorical local-obstruction formulation. Those reservations prevent attribution of the additional grounds to the Court.

**Kennedy, concurring, no joins.** Kennedy joins Scalia throughout. Limited federal coverage does not remove state responsibility to enforce ordinary trespass and obstruction laws. Respect for separate federal and state authority cannot justify tolerating serious unlawful conduct. This observation adds no enforcement order or remand direction.

**Souter, concurring in the judgment in part and dissenting in part, no joins.** Souter accepts Griffin/Scott as settled statutory constructions and rejects the present deprivation theory and its fee basis. He would consider hindrance separately. A purpose to impede constituted state authorities supplies an additional limiting element and, in his view, need not carry the deprivation clause’s entire class-animus and privately protected-right restrictions. Overwhelmed police warrant further examination, not automatic liability. He disputes exclusion of that opportunity from affirmative remand directions. His view establishes no purpose finding and no successful federal claim.

**Stevens, joined by Blackmun, dissenting.** They would affirm federal relief. Deliberate exclusion from abortion services targets a choice available only to women, and organized blockades impair actual access by interstate patients. Griffin and travel protection reach this purposeful deprivation; equal obstruction of local patients is not a complete answer. Stevens also reads hindrance to reach organized interference with state protection. They would uphold relief or at least allow development of that theory. Neither their interpretation nor the asserted inference of purpose controls.

**O’Connor, joined by Blackmun, dissenting.** They regard organized blockades as deliberate denial of equal legal protection to women seeking lawful medical care. O’Connor’s position, consistent with her Scott dissent join, treats collective coercion preventing a protected class from enjoying its rights as the statute’s concern. They reject characterization of the exclusion as incidental disadvantage and would uphold federal relief; the distinct hindrance provision reinforces their position. This does not convert Casey’s governmental-regulation framework into a freestanding constitutional prohibition on private acts.
