## Standards and Tests selection: Serious future health risks in prison conditions

### Serious future health risks in prison conditions

**Governing question:** What instructions govern serious future health risks in prison conditions?

**Current rule:** A prisoner may establish an Eighth Amendment violation through deliberate indifference to involuntary exposure posing unreasonable risk of serious future harm, without waiting for existing disease. The prisoner must prove objective gravity, including seriousness, likelihood and contemporary society's unwillingness to tolerate the risk, and the separate subjective culpability requirement under Wilson. Current conditions govern prospective relief; no later Farmer formulation or automatic relief from exposure alone enters.

## Standards and Tests selection: Physical injury in a nontrivial malicious custodial beating

### Physical injury in a nontrivial malicious custodial beating

**Governing question:** Whether significant physical injury is an additional necessary element where a restrained convicted prisoner suffers a nontrivial malicious beating satisfying *Whitley*.

**Current rule:** A nontrivial beating inflicted maliciously and without disciplinary justification on a restrained convicted prisoner violates the Eighth Amendment despite limited resulting physical injury when the established conduct satisfies *Whitley*'s demanding malicious-and-sadistic inquiry. Injury remains relevant evidence and bears on compensation; accidental harm or merely technical battery is insufficient. This rule identifies a sufficient constitutional violation and does not impose a universal mental-state standard for all prison-force claims.

**Authority by component:**

- Rejection of a significant-injury floor at the adopted narrow scope: [Hudson v. McMillian — Limited injury does not defeat this established nontrivial malicious beating](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Hudson_v_McMillian_merits_1992-02-25.md), February 25, 1992; Part A of O'Connor's opinion joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Kennedy, and Souter. The proposed universal *Whitley* extension has only four votes and is noncontrolling.

**Present operation effective:** *Hudson*, February 25, 1992.

**Open question:** A comprehensive mental-state rule for other force settings, psychological injury alone, and a general definition of punishment covering isolated unauthorized assaults remain unresolved. Medical-care, confinement-condition, personal-defense, and compensation rules remain distinct.

## Standards and Tests selection: Successive prosecutions, evidentiary overlap and criminal contempt

### Successive prosecutions, evidentiary overlap and criminal contempt

**Governing question:** When evidentiary overlap, conspiracy or completed criminal contempt bars a successive prosecution.

**Current rule:** Evidentiary use of an uncharged crime does not itself place the defendant in jeopardy for that crime. A conspiracy and its substantive objects remain distinct offenses; prosecuting the substantive crime does not bar prosecution of the agreement merely because acts help prove both. Grady's essential-conduct rule remains operative and does not displace the established agreement/substantive-offense distinction or become a same-evidence or same-transaction rule. Ordinary evidentiary restrictions retain their separate force. Beyond Blockburger, Grady bars a later prosecution when establishing an essential element requires proof of conduct constituting an offense already prosecuted; evidence reuse or a common episode alone does not suffice. Completed nonsummary criminal contempt places the accused in criminal jeopardy despite private initiation or a protective purpose. When contempt necessarily prosecutes a substantive offense forbidden by the order, the Government cannot prosecute that same offense again. Coercive civil sanctions, summary courtroom contempt and separate sovereigns remain outside Dixon. Foster II-V may proceed under its case-specific judgment, but no controlling rationale governs those counts: the Blockburger-only/overruling and special judicial-order rationales are incompatible, not logical subsets. Grady survives without an invented general contempt exception.

## Standards and Tests selection: Punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review

### Punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review

**Governing question:** What instructions govern punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review?

**Current rule:** Forfeitures to the sovereign under §881(a)(4) and (a)(7), and punitive criminal RICO forfeiture, are subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. Civil labeling, in rem form and concurrent remedial aims do not remove the covered statutory forfeitures' partly punitive character. Coverage and lawful conviction or statutory connection do not decide excessiveness. The court must separately assess the exaction in relation to its offenses; the property-offense connection may matter but review is not limited to instrumentality alone. No comprehensive formula, ratio, mandatory list of factors, incorporation holding or automatic return follows. Austin leaves truly innocent ownership's constitutional protection unresolved; Alexander's independent expressive-burden inquiry remains distinct.

## Standards and Tests selection: Immediate appeal of conclusive Eleventh Amendment immunity denials

### Immediate appeal of conclusive Eleventh Amendment immunity denials

**Governing question:** What instructions govern immediate appeal of conclusive Eleventh Amendment immunity denials?

**Current rule:** A State or asserted arm of a State may immediately appeal under §1291 a conclusive denial of Eleventh Amendment immunity from federal suit when the issue is separate from the merits and the protection would be effectively lost by awaiting final judgment. A dispute over arm-of-State status does not itself require postponement until trial. Appealability establishes neither immunity nor entity status, waiver or abrogation; prospective officer-relief rules retain their separate limits.

## Standards and Tests selection: Interlocutory review of district-court bankruptcy appellate orders

### Interlocutory review of district-court bankruptcy appellate orders

**Governing question:** Whether 28 U.S.C. §158(d) excludes otherwise available interlocutory appellate review under §1292 of an order entered by a district court in its bankruptcy appellate capacity.

**Current rule:** Section 158(d) does not preclude §1292 review of district-court bankruptcy appellate orders. Review remains subject to the particular §1292 route's requirements; under §1292(b), these include a certified controlling legal question, substantial ground for difference of opinion, material advancement of termination, timely application and circuit permission. The construction creates no automatic appeal and does not extend district-court language to bankruptcy appellate panels.

## Standards and Tests selection: No contingency enhancement under the environmental fee provisions

### No contingency enhancement under the environmental fee provisions

**Governing question:** Enhancement above a properly determined lodestar for the risk of nonpayment when counsel's compensation depends on success.

**Current rule:** A reasonable fee under 42 U.S.C. §6972(e) and 33 U.S.C. §1365(d) may not be enhanced to compensate contingency risk. A market-access rationale supplies no exception. The ordinary reasonable-hours-and-rate lodestar, applicant's ordinary burden and prohibition against double counting remain in force.

**Authority by component:**

- Categorical contingency prohibition: [City of Burlington v. Dague, “Contingency does not justify an enhanced statutory fee”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/City_of_Burlington_v_Dague_merits_1992-06-24.md), June 24, 1992; Scalia, White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. The record adopts White's previously noncontrolling Delaware Valley II approach for these statutes.

**Present operation effective:** *Dague*, June 24, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Delay adjustments, correction of an unreasonable underlying rate, differently worded compensation schemes and common-fund fees are not decided. The ruling adjudicates these two provisions; it does not enlarge Ardestani's administrative-eligibility rule or supply a federal-immunity premise.

## Standards and Tests selection: Common-law delay compensation on assumed federal program debt

### Common-law delay compensation on assumed federal program debt

**Governing question:** What instructions govern common-law delay compensation on assumed federal program debt?

**Current rule:** A State's matured contractual assumption of federal food-stamp reimbursement obligations carries applicable federal common-law compensation for delayed payment. The decision leaves the lawful rate, accrual period and any applicable qualification for determination under the underlying obligation; it adopts no formula or new exemption. State-debtor status alone supplies no free-ranging equitable balance. Sections 3701(c) and 3717 exclude state agencies from the Debt Collection Act's mandatory mechanism without extinguishing that independent common-law obligation. Statutory minimum rates, collection fees and penalties do not thereby apply, and unilaterally imposed penalties remain distinct.

## Standards and Tests selection: Instructions concerning innocence of other offenses at capital sentencing

### Instructions concerning innocence of other offenses at capital sentencing

**Governing question:** What instructions govern instructions concerning innocence of other offenses at capital sentencing?

**Current rule:** Even assuming an innocence presumption concerning other offenses applies at capital sentencing, an instruction does not automatically follow. The circumstances must create a genuine danger of attributing other criminal conduct on a basis other than lawful evidence. A silent criminal-history record, undiminished reasonable-doubt instructions on aggravation and no circumstance creating that danger do not require the instruction. The general existence and scope of the assumed presumption remain undecided.

## Standards and Tests selection: Effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation

### Effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation

**Governing question:** What instructions govern effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation?

**Current rule:** When record-supported mitigation has material culpability significance that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the charge, evidence and trial as a whole prevent that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Admission and partial relevance to future danger are insufficient if a truthful finding of deliberateness and danger prevents giving effect to youth's independent mitigating force. Disturbed upbringing need not involve a uniquely severe permanent handicap or lack any relation to future danger. The sentencer need not give favorable weight, and mere possibility of a restrictive reading is insufficient. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated this functional requirement before Graham's September 10, 1984 finality; no new Teague exception follows. Adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence can provide correction without disturbing guilt. No general positive-character rule follows from Graham's separate fractured component.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions

### Commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions

**Governing question:** What instructions govern commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions?

**Current rule:** For truthful, nonmisleading commercial speech about lawful activity, the government must establish real harms, material advancement and a reasonable fit between its chosen restriction and its interests. Where commercial and newspaper racks cause the same physical harms, the commercial category requires justification tied to those harms; benign objectives alone do not make content-selected regulation content neutral. Incremental regulation is not invalid merely because its reduction is small, but alternatives bear on fit without a least-restrictive-means or all-sources-at-once requirement. In professional solicitation the government must demonstrate advancement in the relevant professional setting, without an invariable empirical-study requirement. Deceptive or coercive solicitation remains regulable. Central Hudson and Fox remain the framework; no general equality of commercial and noncommercial speech or categorical vice exception follows.

**Authority by component:**

## Standards and Tests selection: Private enforcement of Adoption Assistance Act reasonable-efforts and reporting clauses

### Private enforcement of Adoption Assistance Act reasonable-efforts and reporting clauses

**Governing question:** Private enforcement of 42 U.S.C. §671(a)(15)'s reasonable-efforts clause and §671(a)(9)'s reporting clause through §1983 or an action implied directly under the Act.

**Current rule:** Read within the Act's plan and administrative-enforcement structure, §671(a)(15) creates no individual right enforceable under §1983 to compel the asserted reasonable efforts to prevent removal or reunify families. An approved plan's commitment, §671(a)(3)'s statewide-operation requirement and caseworker deficiencies do not supply that missing right. Nor does §671(a)(15) imply a separate private action: beneficial purpose does not establish congressional intent to create the requested remedy. Section 671(a)(9) requires a plan to provide reporting to the appropriate court or law-enforcement agency when a state agency has reason to believe a child's home or institution is unsuitable because of neglect, abuse or exploitation and the child's care is paid for in whole or in part under Title IV-E or IV-B. That administrative direction does not create the asserted private §1983 right or an implied action to obtain caseworker relief. The §1983-right and implied-action inquiries remain distinct; failure to imply an action does not bar an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy.

**Authority by component:**
- No individual §671(a)(15) right: [Suter v. Artist M.](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Suter_v_Artist_M_merits_1992-03-25.md), March 25, 1992, “Section 671(a)(15) supplies no individual reasonable-efforts right enforceable under §1983,” Part II.
- No separate implied (15) action: the same record, “Section 671(a)(15) supplies no separate implied action,” Part III.
- Reporting trigger and absence of asserted §1983 right: the same record, “The asserted §671(a)(9) reporting claim is not enforceable through §1983,” Part IV-A.
- No implied reporting action: the same record, “The asserted §671(a)(9) claim supplies no separate implied action,” Part IV-B. White for O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas supplies six controlling joins for each component.

**Present operation effective:** Suter, March 25, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** No general rule makes state-plan provisions unenforceable. Administrative funding review does not necessarily foreclose an otherwise existing §1983 right, and the State's administrative obligations remain. Appropriate relief presupposes a cognizable action and cannot create the missing right or action.

**Open question:** Private enforceability of §671(a)(16), read with §675(1) and §675(5)(B), remains undecided, as do the preserved claim's merits and proper relief.

**Holdings navigation:** [Suter v. Artist M., 503 U.S. 347 (1992) — Child-welfare statutory enforcement](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#suter-v-artist-m-503-us-347-1992-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: Member enforcement of interunion contracts under §301

### Member enforcement of interunion contracts under §301

**Governing question:** Whether an individual union member may sue for violation of a qualifying international-local union constitution.

**Current rule:** LMRA §301 permits an individual member who is a beneficiary of a qualifying contract between labor organizations to sue for its violation. An international constitution binding the international and its local remains an interunion contract when a member invokes it. The plaintiff must satisfy ordinary contract predicates; the statute does not federalize every internal union dispute or classify standalone local bylaws as covered contracts.

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal rules applied to parties govern still-open direct review

### Federal rules applied to parties govern still-open direct review

**Governing question:** What instructions govern federal rules applied to parties govern still-open direct review?

**Current rule:** When this Court applies a federal rule to the parties before it, that rule governs all still-open direct-review cases concerning events within its substantive scope, whether before or after announcement. Selective prospectivity cannot withhold the rule from another such case. Chevron Oil is displaced to that extent. Final judgments are not reopened; genuine remedial rules remain distinct, and every conceivable prospective disposition is not decided.

## Standards and Tests selection: Indian-country residence, source and state member taxation

#### Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, 503 U.S. 249 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1791.
**Decided:** March 9, 1992.

##### Whether §158(d) precludes otherwise available §1292 review of district-court bankruptcy appellate orders

**Holding:** Section 158(d) does not preclude court-of-appeals review under §1292 of a district court’s order entered in its bankruptcy appellate capacity. Review remains subject to the particular §1292 route’s requirements, including certification and circuit permission where §1292(b) applies; the provision does not grant an automatic appeal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion, joined by Stone, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; five Justices adopt the text/coexistence rationale. White, Blackmun, Stevens and O’Connor agree in judgment on their separate stated reasoning.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The applicable §1292 requirements remain, including certification of a controlling legal question, substantial ground for difference of opinion, material advancement of termination, timely application and circuit permission under §1292(b). No jury entitlement or extension of district-court language to bankruptcy appellate panels is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The jurisdictional dismissal is reversed and remanded to the Second Circuit to address the remaining statutory requirements and whether to permit the appeal.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wood v. United States, 16 Pet. 342 — applied: overlapping statutes remain effective absent positive conflict.
- United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, 489 U.S. 235 — applied: the enacted bankruptcy-related text governs.
- Rubin v. United States, 449 U.S. 424 — applied: unambiguous statutory words control the inquiry.
- United States v. Goldenberg, 168 U.S. 95 — applied: courts give effect to legislative text rather than assumed omissions.

#### Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71, 502 U.S. 93 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-967.  
**Decided:** December 4, 1991.

##### Whether lost hiring-hall referrals support legal damages carrying a Seventh Amendment jury right

**Holding:** A union member's claim for wages and benefits lost because hiring-hall referrals allegedly were withheld is a claim for compensatory legal relief carrying a Seventh Amendment jury right. Accompanying equitable requests do not eliminate the jury right on the legal issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; all eight participants.

**Material application:** Wooddell seeks replacement of lost earnings, not return of identified funds held by the union or backpay incidental to reinstatement in a former job.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Jury entitlement decides the factfinder, not retaliation, liability, or recoverable amount. Equitable relief remains separately adjudicated, with legal and equitable issues sequenced consistently with the Seventh Amendment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The jury-denial component is reversed and remanded to permit a jury on the LMRDA legal damages claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chauffeurs, Teamsters & Helpers Local 391 v. Terry supplies the historical-analogue inquiry and makes the legal or equitable character of relief the more important consideration.

For the member-beneficiary enforcement holding in Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71, decided December 4, 1991, No. 90-967, see Labor and Employment — Union-constitution enforcement.

#### City of Burlington v. Dague, 505 U.S. 557 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-810.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

##### Whether contingency risk permits enhancement under the two environmental fee statutes

**Holding:** A reasonable attorney's fee under 42 U.S.C. §6972(e) and 33 U.S.C. §1365(d) may not be enhanced above a properly determined lodestar to compensate for the risk of nonpayment when compensation depends on success.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the categorical rule. The dissenters' market-based alternatives supply no controlling exception.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated under the two reviewed fee provisions; it is not a prohibition of every possible fee adjustment.

**Material application:** Reasonable hours and rates produced a $198,027.50 lodestar. The $49,506.87 contingency addition, reported as 25%, cannot stand even though the district court found substantial difficulty obtaining counsel without possible enhancement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court uses reasonable-fee principles shared with similarly worded prevailing-party statutes but adjudicates this award under the two identified provisions. Differently worded compensation schemes, delay adjustments, unreasonable underlying rates and common-fund fees are not decided. Fee eligibility, hours and rates, expenses, state-law damages and the environmental injunction are outside the grant.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the judgment insofar as it sustains the $49,506.87 addition and remove that amount, preserving the $198,027.50 lodestar and unreviewed relief. No market-evidence remand or replacement multiplier is authorized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hensley v. Eckerhart, 461 U.S. 424, supplies the lodestar and avoidance of extensive collateral fee litigation. Blum v. Stenson, 465 U.S. 886, supplies the strong lodestar presumption, applicant's burden and nonduplication rule; its reserved contingency question is answered. Pennsylvania v. Delaware Valley Citizens' Council for Clean Air, 478 U.S. 546, supplies lodestar primacy without deciding delay or deficient rates. White's previously noncontrolling categorical position in Pennsylvania v. Delaware Valley Citizens' Council for Clean Air, 483 U.S. 711, is adopted for these statutes; the competing limited and broader permission theories remain noncontrolling. Blanchard v. Bergeron, 489 U.S. 87, establishes that the private contingent agreement does not determine the statutory fee. Ardestani v. INS, December 10, 1991, remains distinct on administrative fee eligibility; Department of Energy v. Ohio, April 21, 1992, supplies no federal-immunity premise for this municipal award.

#### Puerto Rico Aqueduct & Sewer Authority v. Metcalf & Eddy, Inc., 506 U.S. 139 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1010.

**Decided:** January 12, 1993.

##### Immediate appeal from a qualifying denial of Eleventh Amendment immunity

**Holding:** A State or entity claiming to be an arm of a State may immediately appeal under §1291 a conclusive denial of Eleventh Amendment immunity from federal suit when the immunity issue is separate from the merits and the protection would be effectively lost by waiting for final judgment. Appealability does not establish that the claimant possesses immunity, and a dispute over arm-of-State status does not by itself require postponing review until trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: seven votes for this rule and its controlling explanation. Stone-Zsela supplies an eighth vote for reversal and the narrower procedural rule in his judgment concurrence. No fractured-decision analysis is necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp., 337 U.S. 541 (1949), and Coopers & Lybrand v. Livesay, 437 U.S. 463 (1978): applied; the three collateral-order requirements remain mandatory.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511 (1985): its immunity-from-suit rationale is applied to asserted Eleventh Amendment immunity; this does not extend substantive immunity to new entities.
- Edelman v. Jordan, 415 U.S. 651 (1974), and In re Ayers, 123 U.S. 443 (1887): relied on for the existing protection from federal suit and its federalism basis, without enlarging the classes of immune defendants.
- Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123 (1908), Green v. Mansour, 474 U.S. 64 (1985), and Cory v. White, 457 U.S. 85 (1982): preserved as distinct rules concerning prospective official relief, past-violation declarations, and suits against the State or agency itself.
- Van Cauwenberghe v. Biard, 486 U.S. 517 (1988), and Catlin v. United States, 324 U.S. 229 (1945): distinguished; their ordinary forum-jurisdiction finality rules remain.
- Mt. Healthy City Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274 (1977): retained; entity status remains a distinct inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Entitlement to immunity, arm-of-State status, waiver, abrogation, Puerto Rico's unchallenged treatment as a State and contract remedies remain undecided. Ex parte Young's qualifying prospective official relief remains distinct from immunity of a State or agency itself and declarations of past violations.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the First Circuit appellate-jurisdiction dismissal and remand for the immunity inquiry; no declaration that PRASA is immune or dismissal of the contract action.

#### Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-794.
**Decided:** June 18, 1993.

##### An applied federal rule governs still-open direct review

**Holding:** When this Court applies a federal rule to the parties before it, that rule governs all still-open direct-review cases concerning events within its substantive scope, whether those events predate or postdate announcement. A State cannot use selective prospectivity to withhold the rule; Chevron Oil is displaced to the extent it permits that practice, without reopening final judgments or deciding every conceivable prospective disposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, An applied federal rule governs still-open direct review portion; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Davis, 489 U.S.803 — nondiscrimination rule applied to pending claims.
- Griffith, 479 U.S.314 — equal direct-review application extended to this civil setting.
- Beam, 501 U.S.529 — its rejection of selective application clarified through a direct majority rule.
- Chevron Oil, 404 U.S.97 — displaced only insofar as it permits selective prospectivity after application to the original litigants.

##### Retroactivity and the lawful tax remedy are separate

**Holding:** The state courts must apply Davis and determine the correction state law and federal due process require. If the State compelled payment before an adequate opportunity to contest the tax, meaningful backward-looking relief is required; lawful refunds, retroactive nondiscriminatory assessments or a combination may suffice, while a meaningful prepayment opportunity presents a different remedial setting.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, Retroactivity and the lawful tax remedy are separate portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Final judgments are not reopened; pure prospectivity where application to the original litigants is reserved is not decided. Genuine procedural and remedial rules cannot reintroduce selective prospectivity. Virginia’s refund premise is not an independent state ground insulating the federal error.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for application of Davis and lawful correction under state law and due process; no fixed refund amount, appropriation or assessment of absent taxpayers without lawful process.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McKesson, 496 U.S.18 — pay-first systems require meaningful backward-looking relief; alternatives preserved.
- Davis, 489 U.S.803 — correction of unequal taxation distinguished from a particular refund formula.
- Beam, 501 U.S.529 — procedural and remedial questions kept distinct from temporal governing law.

#### United States v. Felix, 503 U.S. 378 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1599.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

##### Whether prior evidentiary use places a defendant in jeopardy for distinct uncharged substantive offenses

**Holding:** The use of evidence of an uncharged crime in one trial does not itself place the defendant in jeopardy for that crime. The later Oklahoma substantive charges concern different acts from the Missouri attempt and are not barred merely because evidence of those acts helped prove intent in Missouri.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of White's opinion of the Court, supported by all nine Justices; all nine Justices adopt the distinct-prosecution rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Missouri attempt concerned different acts from the earlier Oklahoma offenses. Ordinary objections to the admissibility of particular evidence remain.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment reversing substantive counts 2–6 and conspiracy count 1 is reversed, restoring the prosecution’s judgments against the double-jeopardy objection, subject to preserved unresolved appellate issues. The separate disposition of travel counts 9–10 is undisturbed; no automatic new trial or evidentiary ruling is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Dowling v. United States, 493 U.S. 342 — applied: evidentiary use does not itself constitute prosecution for the described offense.
- Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S. 508 — applied and confined to essential-conduct analysis; its express rejection of a same-evidence test remains operative.

##### Whether overlapping substantive acts bar prosecution of the distinct conspiracy agreement

**Holding:** A conspiracy and its substantive objects are distinct offenses; prosecution of the substantive crime does not bar prosecution of the agreement merely because the same acts help prove both. The overlapping Missouri conduct therefore does not bar this Oklahoma conspiracy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of White's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt this rationale. Stevens and Blackmun concur only in the conspiracy judgment on a narrower ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Grady remains in force; no same-transaction requirement or new evidentiary exemption is adopted. A materially different single-incident successive prosecution remains for its own case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment reversing substantive counts 2–6 and conspiracy count 1 is reversed, restoring the prosecution’s judgments against the double-jeopardy objection, subject to preserved unresolved appellate issues. The separate disposition of travel counts 9–10 is undisturbed; no automatic new trial or evidentiary ruling is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bayer, 331 U.S. 532 — applied: the agreement is distinct from its execution.
- Pinkerton v. United States, 328 U.S. 640 — applied: substantive crime and conspiracy remain separately punishable offenses.
- Iannelli v. United States, 420 U.S. 770 — applied: conspiracy’s independent character survives overlapping proof.
- Garrett v. United States, 471 U.S. 773 — applied by analogy to the treatment of multilayered criminal conduct.
- Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S. 508 — clarified without overruling: it does not abrogate the established conspiracy/substantive-offense distinction.

#### Estelle v. McGuire, 502 U.S. 62 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1074.  
**Decided:** December 4, 1991.

##### Whether admission of prior nonaccidental-injury evidence violates due process when the earlier abuser is unidentified

**Holding:** On federal habeas review, evidence of a child's prior nonaccidental injuries may be relevant to prove that the charged death was intentional even when the identity of the person who caused the earlier injuries is unknown. Admission for that nonidentity purpose does not itself violate due process, and a defendant's choice not to contest accident does not remove the State's burden to prove intentional killing.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, joined on this proposition by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; all eight participants.

**Material application:** The repeated injuries supported the inference of nonaccidental harm without themselves identifying McGuire as the abuser. The evidence-alone theory could not support habeas relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** State-law admissibility alone is not reviewed. The Court does not decide a general constitutional prohibition of propensity evidence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lewis v. Jeffers and Pulley v. Harris exclude habeas relief for state-law error alone. Lisenba and Dowling preserve the fundamental-unfairness boundary. In re Winship supplies the prosecution's continuing burden to prove every element.

##### Whether the prior-act instruction created a reasonable likelihood that jurors bypassed elemental proof

**Holding:** An ambiguous instruction violates due process only when, read with the charge and trial as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood the jury applied it in a way that relieved the State of proving an element. This instruction did not create that likelihood because it conditioned any inference on the jury's finding that McGuire committed the prior acts and expressly prohibited a bad-character inference.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion joined at this level by Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; five of eight participants. Stone, Stevens, and O'Connor join only the distinct prior-injury-evidence proposition.

**Material application:** The complete charge neither directed a finding that McGuire caused the earlier injuries nor shifted the prosecution's burden on identity or any other element.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not approve every prior-act instruction or decide a general propensity-evidence rule. Because the controlling majority finds no constitutional error, it does not reach Teague, Rose, or Chapman or require harmless-error review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit judgment is reversed. The habeas writ remains denied; no acquittal, retrial, or release is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cupp supplies whole-charge review, and Boyde supplies the reasonable-likelihood standard. Francis and Sandstrom are distinguished and preserved for mandatory presumptions or shifts in elemental persuasion.

#### Graham v. Collins, 506 U.S. 461 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7580.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality

**Holding:** Teague does not bar Graham's claim that Texas's submitted sentencing questions prevented effective use of his youth and unstable background as material mitigation independent of deliberateness or future danger. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated that functional requirement before his conviction became final on September 10, 1984; no new procedural-rule exception is created.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this prefinality-law holding, its application to youth and background, and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288, and Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied; the relevant result must be dictated at finality, not merely supported by a later plausible extension.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — preserved as qualified approval; it does not immunize every application of the special issues.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied as the pre-1984 effective-mitigation rule.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied as recognition of that existing rule, not as law inserted retroactively into 1984.
- Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992 — applied at its existing Teague scope; disagreement alone does not establish novelty.
- Franklin v. Lynaugh, 487 U.S. 164, and Saffle v. Parks, 494 U.S. 484 — distinguished; evidence whose mitigating use fits the questions, and unrestricted sympathy, differ from material culpability mitigation excluded by them.

##### Youth requires an effective route for its independent mitigating force

**Holding:** When the record supports a material mitigating judgment about culpability that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the submitted mechanism prevents that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Graham's youth at seventeen has that independent significance notwithstanding deliberate conduct or predicted future danger, and the three submitted special issues provide no adequate route for it here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground for sentencing relief and its conditional remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied to youth's independent culpability significance; jurors may assign weight but cannot be legally prevented from giving it effect.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — its conditional framework remains; this defective application does not establish facial invalidity.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied beyond a permanent-handicap limitation; a mitigating trait need not have only aggravating relevance to future danger to require an effective vehicle.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 — applied as the reasonable-likelihood method of assessing instructional operation, not as the source of a new substantive rule at 1984 finality.

##### The submitted background evidence also requires effective consideration

**Independent alternative holding:** A capital sentencing system may not prevent a jury from acting on the material culpability significance it finds in a defendant's disturbed upbringing merely because that evidence also bears on future dangerousness or lacks a uniquely severe permanent handicap. The evidence of Graham's mother's recurring mental illness and hospitalizations and his shifting family care supports such a mitigating judgment, which the submitted special issues cannot adequately express; this independently requires the same sentencing remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Parts III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independent background-evidence ground and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 — applied to relevant background proffered as a basis for less than death.
- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied; disturbed upbringing cannot be excluded from mitigating effect as a matter of law.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied; neither a uniquely severe permanent handicap nor complete absence of relevance to the special issues is an exclusive trigger.

##### Positive character as an independent ground

**Authority of the judgment:** The separate contention that Graham's household assistance, religious observance and financial support of his children independently require additional sentencing relief is rejected by all nine Justices, on different grounds. Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter regard its asserted independent culpability significance as insubstantial on this record and its future-behavior use as available under the second issue; White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would bar the requested mitigation relief under Teague. Stone-Zsela joins the denial of this independent component in the judgment only. These grounds do not yield a common general rule or a Marks subset; the youth and background holdings independently require sentencing relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule about positive character evidence or its value apart from the asserted culpability and future-danger uses is decided. The jury remains free to hear constitutionally relevant evidence at the new sentencing proceeding. Neither denial of this independent ground nor the sentencing remedy disturbs the murder conviction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere admission or some future-danger relevance is insufficient when supported culpability mitigation cannot receive effect. The ruling requires neither favorable weight nor a categorical fourth question, finds no psychiatric disability, and imposes no juvenile-execution prohibition or facial invalidity of the scheme. Texas’s later statute is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse sentencing relief denial on youth and background and remand for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate sentencing or a lawful noncapital replacement. Preserve the conviction; no automatic acquittal or immediate release.

#### Delo v. Lashley, 507 U.S. 272 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-409.

**Decided:** March 8, 1993.

##### An evidentiary predicate for the requested mitigating circumstance

**Holding:** The Constitution does not require a capital sentencer to receive an instruction on a particular mitigating circumstance when no evidence supports that circumstance; Missouri may require some supporting evidence for its no-significant-prior-criminal-history instruction while leaving the sentencer free to consider and give effect to mitigation actually presented. ##### The proposed innocence instruction does not sustain this judgment

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming that a presumption of innocence of other offenses applies at capital sentencing, its existence does not automatically require an instruction; the relevant circumstances must create a genuine danger that jurors will attribute other criminal conduct to the defendant on a basis other than lawful evidence. **Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices expressly adopt this alternative response. Stone-Zsela and Souter do not join or reach it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Silence does not prove a no-significant-history mitigating fact. No actual history is found from material the jury did not hear; youth is a different mitigating circumstance. The alternative innocence-instruction holding assumes the protection without deciding its existence or full scope. Strickland merits and the independently rejected defaulted counsel claim are not reopened.

#### United States v. Dixon, 509 U.S. 688 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1231.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

##### Nonsummary punitive contempt triggers double-jeopardy protection

**Holding:** A completed nonsummary criminal-contempt prosecution places the accused in criminal jeopardy; private initiation and an order’s protective purpose do not categorically remove that protection. Whether a later prosecution is for the same offense remains a separate inquiry; coercive civil sanctions, summary courtroom contempt and separate sovereigns are not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bloom v. Illinois, 391 U.S.194: criminal character of punitive contempt applied.
- Gompers v. Bucks Stove & Range Co., 221 U.S.418: punitive/coercive distinction applied.
- Young v. United States ex rel. Vuitton, 481 U.S.787: independent judicial enforcement authority retained, not a categorical exception from criminal protection.

##### Previously prosecuted necessary offenses cannot be tried again

**Holding:** When a criminal-contempt adjudication necessarily prosecutes the substantive offense forbidden by the order, the Government cannot prosecute that same substantive offense again. Dixon’s drug offense and Foster I’s November assault were necessarily prosecuted in the respective contempts and are barred; this shared proposition does not supply a common analysis of Foster II–V.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brown v. Ohio, 432 U.S.161: successive greater/included-offense protection applied.
- Harris v. Oklahoma, 433 U.S.682: necessary substantive offense cannot be erased by an abstract description of the first charge.
- Blockburger v. United States, 284 U.S.299: elements inquiry retained; disagreement over its broader application separately recorded.

##### Grady remains governing successive-prosecution law

**Holding:** Grady is not overruled: beyond Blockburger’s elements protection, a later prosecution is barred when establishing an essential element requires proof of conduct constituting an offense for which the defendant has already been prosecuted. Mere reuse of evidence or a common episode does not suffice, and Felix’s independently punishable conspiracy holding remains; no five-Justice rationale determines Grady’s application to Foster II–V in this fractured decision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Grady survives; evidentiary overlap and a common episode do not alone establish a bar. Summary contempt, coercive civil sanctions and separate sovereigns are not decided. Foster II–V have no controlling rationale, as stated separately below.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the bar for Dixon’s drug charge and Foster I (6–3), leaving the contempt judgments intact. Reverse the bar for Foster II–V and remand (5–4); threat charges remain subject to a properly established Ashe bar from actual acquittals and other defenses. Foster V may proceed without authorizing a barred simple-assault conviction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S.508: expressly retained; no overruling by this decision.
- United States v. Felix, March 25, 1992: conspiracy and evidentiary-overlap distinctions preserved, not recast as an overruling.
- Illinois v. Vitale, 447 U.S.410: successive-prosecution concern beyond formal elements supports retention.
- Brown v. Ohio, 432 U.S.161: finality and greater/included-offense protection retained.
- Ashe v. Swenson, 397 U.S.436: independent issue-preclusion rule preserved for the remanded threat charges.

##### Whether Foster II–V may proceed under a common double-jeopardy rationale

**Authority of the judgment:** Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reverse the bars to Foster II–V and remand, 5–4. No controlling rationale exists under Marks: the four-Justice elements-only rationale would overrule Grady, while Blackmun retains Grady but treats judicial-order enforcement differently. Those incompatible grounds are not logical subsets. The judgment permits these counts without creating a general contempt exception or displacing the separately majority-supported retention of Grady.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Foster II–IV remain subject to any actual-acquittal issue preclusion established under Ashe and other preserved defenses. Foster V’s distinct assault-with-intent-to-kill charge may proceed; the judgment does not authorize a barred simple-assault conviction or disturb the contempt dispositions.

#### Austin v. United States, 509 U.S. 602 (1993)

**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

##### These statutory forfeitures are covered fines

**Holding:** Forfeiture to the United States under 21 U.S.C.§§ 881(a)(4) and(a)(7) constitutes payment in kind to the sovereign at least partly as punishment and is subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. Its civil designation, proceeding against property, and concurrent remedial aims do not remove coverage; coverage does not establish excessiveness or import all criminal-procedure safeguards.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

- Browning-Ferris v. Kelco Disposal, 492 U.S.257: government prosecution/receipt distinguishes this fine from private punitive award.
- United States v. Halper, 490 U.S.435: partly punitive function recognized by analogy; separate Double Jeopardy analysis not imported.
- One 1958 Plymouth Sedan v. Pennsylvania, 380 U.S.693: lawful property is not contraband merely because used in crime.
- One Lot Emerald Cut Stones v. United States, 409 U.S.232, and United States v. One Assortment of 89 Firearms, 465 U.S.354: remedial customs/contraband characterizations distinguished, not abolished.
- Buena Vista, February 24, 1993: statutory owner defenses remain provision-specific; no § 881(a)(7) or constitutional innocent owner holding imported.

##### Historical in rem form does not negate punishment

**Holding:** The historical treatment of statutory in rem forfeiture includes punishment and does not establish categorical exemption from the Excessive Fines Clause for these forfeitures. The traditionally limited common-law innocent-owner defense does not prove that forfeiture was solely remedial; the constitutionality of forfeiting a truly innocent owner’s property remains unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Peisch v. Ware, 4 Cranch 347: punitive understanding and stranger-removal qualification applied.
- The Palmyra, 12 Wheat 1: no prior personal conviction required, not an absence of punishment holding.
- J.W. Goldsmith, Jr.-Grant Co. v. United States, 254 U.S.505, and Calero-Toledo v. Pearson Yacht, 416 U.S.663: punitive/deterrent features recognized; truly innocent owner question reserved.

##### Remand leaves excessiveness distinct from statutory connection

**Holding:** The lower court must evaluate whether Austin’s actual forfeiture is excessive; statutory facilitation and coverage as punishment do not themselves decide that question. The Court adopts no comprehensive excessiveness test and does not limit the remand to instrumentality alone: the property’s connection to the offense may matter, and other relevant considerations may also be examined.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Coverage does not decide excessiveness, incorporate all criminal safeguards or establish state incorporation. Truly innocent ownership, taxation and solely compensatory recovery remain distinct; neither a comprehensive formula nor instrumentality alone is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for an excessiveness inquiry on a developed record. No automatic property return, value finding, ratio or fixed cap; the lower court’s concern was not a completed excessiveness determination.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Browning-Ferris, 492 U.S.257: fine coverage principle applied without inventing a comprehensive proportionality formula.
- Calero-Toledo, 416 U.S.663: statutory/historical connection not converted into a final excessiveness answer.

#### Hudson v. McMillian, 503 U.S. 1 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6531.
**Decided:** February 25, 1992.

##### Whether limited injury defeats the established nontrivial malicious beating of a restrained prisoner

**Holding:** A nontrivial beating inflicted maliciously and without disciplinary justification on a restrained convicted prisoner violates the Eighth Amendment even when the resulting physical injuries are limited, where the established conduct satisfies even *Whitley*’s demanding malicious-and-sadistic inquiry. Injury remains relevant evidence and bears on compensation; accidental harm or a merely technical battery is not enough.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of O’Connor’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices adopt this rule and its application. Blackmun’s concurrence in the judgment is not counted as a join. O’Connor’s Part B, joined by White, Kennedy and Souter, has only four votes for extending *Whitley* to every prison excessive-force claim. No *Marks* aggregation supplies that universal rule.

**Material application:** Hudson was handcuffed and shackled; the undisturbed findings established unnecessary repeated blows and supervisory condonation. Dental and facial injuries confirmed that the force was not trivial. The findings satisfied even *Whitley*’s heightened culpability inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No comprehensive mental-state standard for every force setting, psychological-injury-only rule, immunity decision, or exhaustive rule for unusual forms of cruelty is adopted. Medical-care, confinement-conditions, legitimate-discipline and compensation rules remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand to the Fifth Circuit for further disposition consistent with the undisturbed findings and without its significant-injury bar. No increase above the existing $800 award, immunity ruling or prison-management injunction is ordered.

**Material judgment:** Reversal and remand, 7–2: Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Whitley v. Albers*, 475 U.S. 312, is applied as a sufficient ground on these findings; its disturbance rule is not extended universally. *Wilson v. Seiter*, 501 U.S. 294, is distinguished: its objective-deprivation requirement does not become a significant-injury floor for this beating. *Estelle v. Gamble*, 429 U.S. 97, supplies the prohibition of unnecessary and wanton pain while retaining the distinct serious-medical-need and deliberate-indifference requirements.

##### Whether an assumed isolated, unauthorized assault sustains affirmance on this record

**Holding:** The undisturbed finding of supervisory condonation and the limits of the granted question prevent affirmance on an assumed characterization of this beating as an isolated, unauthorized assault.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part C of O’Connor’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; six-Justice case-specific rejection of the alternative ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court announces no general definition of punishment or general rule governing isolated, unauthorized conduct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The alternative characterization supplies no ground for preserving the Fifth Circuit’s judgment; the remand must respect the intact supervisory-condonation finding.

#### Forsyth County v. Nationalist Movement, 505 U.S. 123 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-538.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

##### Whether the County's demonstration-fee mechanism is facially invalid for unbounded discretion or audience-hostility pricing

**Holding:** A licensing rule directed at protected demonstrations is facially invalid when neither its text, authoritative construction, nor established administration supplies narrow, objective, definite standards controlling whether and how much officials charge. A challenger need not prove discriminatory administration of its own application, and a maximum fee does not supply the missing standards.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela separately concurs in the judgment on compatible grounds and does not formally join that opinion.

**Material application:** The administrator may select whether to charge, which time and support costs to include and whether to discount them without a binding method or explanation requirement. A $1,000 cap does not constrain those choices, and the finding that the particular $100 assessment reflected administrative work does not cure the facial defect.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Different fees alone do not prove invalidity. Authoritative narrowing construction and established administration may supply constraints; presumed benign discretion does not. Concrete indigency, fixed neutral fees, particular content-independent costs and municipal funding duties remain undecided; separate City and School Board claims are outside this review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm facial relief against enforcement of the County fee mechanism. The County may enact lawful neutral coordination and sufficiently bounded cost rules. No damages, refund amount, fee schedule or mandatory municipal spending formula is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941): retained and distinguished; its authoritative nondiscrimination construction supports flexible lawful fees, not unbounded discretion.
- Shuttlesworth v. Birmingham, 394 U.S. 147 (1969): applied; expressive licensing requires narrow, objective, definite standards.
- Niemotko v. Maryland, 340 U.S. 268 (1951): applied; officials licensing protected expression must act under definite standards.
- City of Lakewood v. Plain Dealer Publishing Co., 486 U.S. 750 (1988): applied; the risk inherent in unbounded expressive licensing supports facial review despite one unobjectionable application.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989): applied as to authoritative constructions and actual administration; a binding narrowing construction matters, a presumed benign discretion does not.

**Independent alternative holding:** A permit fee that varies with anticipated hostile reaction to a demonstration is content based and violates the First Amendment when justified by recovering the costs of protecting speakers from that reaction. Neither calling those expenses a secondary effect nor imposing a small numerical ceiling makes the charge constitutional.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter: an independently sufficient five-Justice holding. Stone-Zsela's separate concurrence agrees with the rule without a formal join.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** Audience-hostility pricing independently sustains the same facial protection against enforcement. It does not find the actual $100 assessment retaliatory, fix police expenditures or forbid genuinely content-independent administrative charges. The following precedent treatment applies to this alternative ground:

- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989): applied; the justification must be independent of the message.
- Boos v. Barry, 485 U.S. 312 (1988): applied at its supported content-classification level; listeners' hostile responses are not neutral secondary effects.
- Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941): distinguished and retained; coordination costs do not include a message-dependent hostility premium.
- Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991 simulated decision: consistent application of the prohibition on unjustified content-based financial burdens; its separate remedial limits are unchanged.
- Terminiello v. Chicago, 337 U.S. 1 (1949): applied in its protection against suppressing speech because of hostile response; no new police-protection entitlement is inferred.

##### Whether the First Amendment imposes a universal nominal-fee ceiling or makes a small fee necessarily valid

**Holding:** The First Amendment does not make a demonstration fee invalid merely because it exceeds a nominal amount, or valid merely because it is small. Cox's allowance for lawful, nondiscriminatory regulatory cost recovery survives; a fee's lawful purpose, content neutrality, and bounded administration govern the defects presented here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter, independently controls both rejections of numerical shortcuts. White's dissent, joined by Scalia and Thomas, and Stone's separate concurrence also reject a universal nominal ceiling; no judgment-only position is converted into a full-opinion join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No permissible dollar amount or comprehensive schedule of recoverable costs is fixed. A fee exceeding a nominal amount must still have a lawful purpose, neutral criteria and bounded administration.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court rejects the court of appeals' amount-only reasoning while affirming the facial relief on the independently sufficient discretion and hostility grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941): retained; flexible, nondiscriminatory regulatory fees are not limited to nominal amounts.
- Murdock v. Pennsylvania, 319 U.S. 105 (1943): distinguished; its nominal-fee reference does not establish an amount-only rule or displace Cox.

#### Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, 507 U.S. 410 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1200.

**Decided:** March 24, 1993.

##### The selected commercial category lacks the required fit

**Holding:** When truthful, nonmisleading commercial publications concerning lawful activity and newspapers cause the same asserted physical harms through their newsracks, the City must justify why its selected commercial category reasonably fits its safety and aesthetic interests. Cincinnati has not done so: removing respondents' sixty-two racks while retaining approximately 1,500–2,000 physically similar newspaper racks violates the First Amendment; a small reduction is not invalid merely for being small, and no least-restrictive-means or all-sources-at-once requirement follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter: seven, throughout.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S.557 (1980): applies the existing commercial-speech test to the accepted classification.
- Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S.469 (1989): applies reasonable proportional fit and government burden; preserves rejection of least restrictive means.
- Metromedia, Inc. v. San Diego, 453 U.S.490 (1981): distinguishes the billboard treatment; does not permit an unrelated content distinction without the required fit.

##### The selective prohibition is not content neutral

**Holding:** A newsrack prohibition determined by whether its publication is a commercial handbill is content based when the asserted safety and aesthetic harms do not justify that distinction without reference to the publication's content. Cincinnati cannot sustain this selective ban as a content-neutral time, place and manner restriction merely because its objective is benign; otherwise lawful neutral regulation of newsracks remains available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter: seven. This rejects the City's independent time, place and manner defense.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S.781 (1989): applies justification without reference to content; a benign objective alone does not validate the selective line.
- Clark v. Community for Creative Non-Violence, 468 U.S.288 (1984): preserves valid content-neutral time, place and manner regulation.
- Renton v. Playtime Theatres, Inc., 475 U.S.41 (1986): distinguishes different secondary effects; no such difference justifies this rack classification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Central Hudson and Fox remain governing. Incremental regulation remains possible; alternatives inform fit but do not impose least restrictive means. No strict-scrutiny rule for all truthful commercial speech, all-sources-at-once duty or commercial/noncommercial equality rule is adopted. Other lawful neutral rack controls remain available.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit and the injunction against the selective commercial-handbill newsrack prohibition.

#### Edenfield v. Fane, 507 U.S. 761 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1594.
**Decided:** April 26, 1993.

##### Real advancement must be established in the relevant professional setting

**Holding:** When truthful, nonmisleading solicitation concerns lawful CPA services, the State must demonstrate that the harms supporting its restriction are real and that the restriction alleviates them to a material degree. Florida’s unsupported predictions about direct solicitation of business clients do not satisfy that burden; the ruling neither requires empirical studies in every case nor immunizes deceptive or coercive solicitation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No strict scrutiny, universal empirical-studies requirement, or least-restrictive-means rule is adopted. Fraud, coercion and materially different professional settings remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the judgment and injunction against the challenged CPA business-solicitation prohibition; no damages or new licensing rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S.557: applies the real-advancement burden.
- Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S.469: retains reasonable fit without least restrictive means.
- Ohralik v. Ohio State Bar Assn., 436 U.S.447: distinguished by professional setting, vulnerability and pressure.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S.781: alternative time/place/manner characterization cannot cure deficient advancement.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993, : consistent application; no expansion to strict scrutiny.

#### United States v. Edge Broadcasting Co., 509 U.S. 418 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-486.
**Decided:** June 25, 1993.

##### Meaningful reduction and reasonable fit in a border broadcaster’s application

**Holding:** Assuming truthful advertising of lawful activity, the licensing-State lottery restriction satisfies Central Hudson as applied to Edge because it meaningfully reduces lottery promotion reaching North Carolina residents and reasonably accommodates the different policies of lottery and nonlottery States. Complete exclusion of cross-border advertising is unnecessary, but the actual advancement and reasonable-fit requirements remain; no categorical exception for vice advertising or bar on as-applied challenges follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The first Central Hudson inquiry is assumed in the station’s favor. Merely marginal advancement, a categorical vice exception, a bar on as-applied review and the greater-power/lesser-power theory are not adopted. The restriction’s unopposed construction excludes noncommercial lottery information.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand, removing the as-applied First Amendment basis for the injunction; genuinely unresolved preserved equality or other alternative claims remain. No facial approval of every application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission,447 U.S.557: four-part scrutiny applied, with the first inquiry assumed in Edge’s favor.
- Board of Trustees v. Fox,492 U.S.469: reasonable fit applied; no least-restrictive-means requirement.

#### Alexander v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1526.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

For the distinct Excessive Fines question in *Alexander v. United States*, June 28, 1993, No. 91-1526, see Criminal Procedure — Excessive fines and criminal RICO forfeiture.

##### Whether this postconviction RICO forfeiture constitutes a traditional prior restraint

**Holding:** A postconviction RICO forfeiture of identified assets linked to adjudicated racketeering does not constitute a traditional prior restraint merely because it impairs the owner's future ability to publish, where it neither forbids future publication nor requires permission to publish. That classification does not dispose of an independent First Amendment challenge to removal of protected works.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas; six Justices adopt this proposition. Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy do not join the classification. The independent First Amendment question has a different coalition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The classification neither licenses a future-publication ban nor permits seizure of protected stock on probable cause alone. It does not resolve the independent challenge to removal of lawful works or immunize publishing assets from neutral sanctions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The traditional-prior-restraint claim is rejected, 6–3; that rejection does not sustain the entire forfeiture. Approval of the challenged forfeiture is vacated for the independent First and Eighth Amendment examinations described in the other question blocks. The convictions remain undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Near v. Minnesota, 283 U.S.697, and Vance v. Universal Amusement Co., 445 U.S.308: distinguished as restraints on future expression; their protection remains in force.
- Fort Wayne Books, Inc. v. Indiana, 489 U.S.46: its pretrial protection is preserved; the present adjudicated RICO case differs, and its reserved postconviction question is not treated as settled.
- Arcara v. Cloud Books, Inc., 478 U.S.697: applied to distinguish an ordinary sanction's incidental consequences from regulation implicating expression; no universal asset exemption follows.

##### Whether valid convictions and the absence of a traditional prior restraint suffice to sustain forfeiture of lawful expressive inventory

**Holding:** Where obscenity predicates produce forfeiture of an expressive enterprise and removal of lawful inventory, the judgment sustaining that forfeiture cannot rest solely on the existence of valid convictions and the absence of a traditional prior restraint. The judgment must be vacated for an independent First Amendment examination of the challenged expressive burden, distinguishing the judicial forfeiture from subsequent administrative destruction and confining correction to a reviewable injury and available relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy; five Justices expressly adopt this limited rule and remand. Their separate positions do not establish a majority rule either categorically immunizing expressive inventory or adopting a comprehensive scrutiny framework for every postconviction forfeiture.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court leaves the ultimate constitutional treatment of the distinct assets unresolved. Neither categorical immunity for expressive inventory nor a comprehensive scrutiny framework commands a majority. Judicial forfeiture, subsequent administrative destruction, statutory nexus, causation, reviewable injury and available relief remain distinct. The Court neither adopts a content-selection rationale from R.A.V. nor enlarges its entered overbreadth rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Eighth Circuit judgment insofar as it sustains the challenged forfeiture without independent First Amendment examination, and remand. The lower court must address statutory nexus and reviewable injury attributable to the judgment, distinguishing subsequent administrative destruction. Relief remains confined to what this appeal can supply: no return of destroyed stock, new damages cause of action or mandatory business restoration is ordered. The convictions remain undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Arcara, 478 U.S.697: its nonexpressive-conduct rationale does not dispense with examination of this expression-triggered sanction; ordinary neutral sanctions remain enforceable.
- Fort Wayne Books, 489 U.S.46: the reservation concerning postconviction forfeiture is respected; its pretrial safeguard remains unchanged.
- Marcus v. Search Warrant, 367 U.S.717, and A Quantity of Copies of Books v. Kansas, 378 U.S.205: their protection against indiscriminate removal of expressive works supplies the need for distinct examination, without mechanically converting their pretrial remedies into this mandate.
- Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991, simulated decision: content-selective financial burdens remain subject to its rule; lawful publishing does not immunize assets from neutral forfeiture.
- Wisconsin v. Mitchell, June 11, 1993: its allowance for punishment of independently criminal conduct and relevant motive is preserved; the battery enhancement does not settle removal of protected inventory.

#### County of Yakima v. Confederated Tribes and Bands of the Yakima Indian Nation, 502 U.S. 251 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-408 and 90-577.
**Decided:** January 14, 1992.

##### Whether state ad valorem taxation may reach reservation land patented in fee under the General Allotment Act

**Holding:** The General Allotment Act's established fee-land rule and the Burke Act proviso authorize state ad valorem taxation of reservation land validly patented in fee under that Act, including qualifying land later held by the Nation. The tax follows the land, but patent source and independently applicable federal protection must be determined parcel by parcel.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; these eight Justices support the ad valorem holding. Blackmun dissents on this component.

**Material application:** The stipulated record did not establish every parcel's patent source. The Indian Reorganization Act did not automatically restore every existing fee parcel to tax-protected status, and the specific land-tax authority did not create general personal taxing jurisdiction over reservation Indians.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns qualifying General Allotment Act fee land and preserves independently applicable federal protections. It does not rest on alienability alone or authorize a governmental-impact inquiry under Brendale.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is affirmed and the case remanded with modified instructions for parcel-specific patent-source analysis and relief consistent with the two distinct tax holdings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Goudy is reaffirmed for taxation of qualifying General Allotment Act fee land. Blackfeet supplies the requirement of unmistakably clear congressional authorization. Mancari requires compatible enactments to operate together. Moe's rejection of general personal taxing jurisdiction is preserved. Brendale's fractured analysis of tribal regulation of nonmember land supplies no governmental-impact test for this statutory tax question.

##### Whether permission to tax the land also authorizes a sales excise imposed on an Indian owner

**Holding:** Congressional permission to tax qualifying fee land ad valorem does not unmistakably authorize a tax imposed on an Indian owner's sale of that land. Securing the transactional tax with a lien does not change its legal incidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion states this proposition for all nine Justices; all nine Justices support the sales-excise holding.

**Material application:** The annual property levy is triggered by ownership and burdens the realty; the sales excise is triggered by a transaction. A collection lien cannot enlarge Congress's permission by transforming an unauthorized transactional charge into a land tax.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sales excise remains prohibited, and remand relief must respect that prohibition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Blackfeet requires authorization clear at the level of the tax actually imposed. Capoeman's allotment-context discussion of taxation after fee patent does not supply blanket permission for every tax related to land.

#### Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Sac and Fox Nation, 508 U.S. 114 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-259.
**Decided:** May 17, 1993.

##### Member income protection depends on Indian-country residence and source

**Holding:** Absent express congressional authorization, a State may not tax a tribal member’s income earned in Indian country when the member resides there; tribal employment alone does not establish that protection. The residence inquiry includes §1151 reservations, dependent Indian communities and allotments whose Indian titles have not been extinguished, including rights-of-way, rather than only an undiminished formal reservation.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McClanahan v. Arizona State Tax Commission, 411 U.S.164: applied with both residence and income-source predicates.
- Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe, 498 U.S.505: applied against a reservation-label-only geography.
- County of Yakima v. Confederated Tribes, January 14, 1992: distinguished; limited land-tax authorization supplies no income-tax authority.

##### The challenged vehicle levies cannot be sustained merely as off-country use charges

**Holding:** For tribal members residing in Indian country, the challenged vehicle excise and registration levies fall within the prohibition on state property taxation absent congressional authorization; collecting the same forbidden charges from later purchasers does not save them. Their application to the established residents must be reconsidered on remand; the Court does not decide a genuinely apportioned off-country road-use charge or every tax connected to a vehicle sale.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Tribal employment or registration alone does not establish protection. Outside-Indian-country self-government claims, genuinely apportioned off-country road-use charges and nonmember taxation are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate in the reviewed respects and remand for actual member residence, income source and the vehicle levies. Qualifying allotments or other Indian country may resolve residence without deciding every reservation boundary; no blanket member exemption or refund amount.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Moe v. Confederated Salish and Kootenai Tribes, 425 U.S.463: applied to member vehicle property taxation.
- Washington v. Confederated Tribes of the Colville Indian Reservation, 447 U.S.134: applied to the substance of the vehicle levies; genuinely tailored off-country use charges remain distinct.

##### Whether an individual member-beneficiary may enforce a qualifying interunion constitution under section 301

**Holding:** Section 301 permits an individual union member who is a beneficiary of a qualifying contract between labor organizations to sue for its alleged violation. **Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; all eight participants.

#### United States v. Texas, 507 U.S. 529 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1729.

**Decided:** April 5, 1993.

##### Compensation for delayed payment of the assumed debt

**Holding:** Texas’s matured obligation to reimburse these food-stamp losses carries the applicable federal common-law obligation to compensate the United States for delayed payment. Its contractual assumption of the program’s reimbursement terms distinguishes this debt from a unilaterally imposed penalty; interest is governed by that obligation’s lawful rate, period and recognized qualifications, not free-ranging balancing merely because the debtor is a State.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Royal Indemnity, 313 U.S. 289 — applied: interest compensates delay in payment of matured contractual obligations.
- West Virginia, 479 U.S. 305 — applied to this state debt; generalized equitable balancing is not authorized.
- Rodgers, 332 U.S. 371 — distinguished: this assumed reimbursement debt is not the unilateral penalty considered there.
- Pennhurst, 451 U.S. 1 — distinguished: compensation for delay on an assumed debt is not a newly invented program condition; clear notice of program obligations remains required.

##### State exclusion from the statutory mechanism does not extinguish common law

**Holding:** Sections 3701(c) and 3717 exclude state agencies from the Debt Collection Act’s mandatory interest mechanism, but do not extinguish the independently applicable common-law obligation on this debt. That conclusion does not apply the statutory minimum rate, collection fees or penalty charges to Texas and does not depend on treating West Virginia’s 1987 holding as settled law in 1982.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Isbrandtsen, 343 U.S. 779, and Astoria, 501 U.S. 104 — applied: retain applicable common law absent contrary statutory purpose.
- Milwaukee, 451 U.S. 304, and Higginbotham, 436 U.S. 618 — distinguished: no statutory resolution extinguishes this obligation.
- West Virginia, 479 U.S. 305 — its reserved Debt Collection Act question is answered; no false 1982 chronology attributed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rate, accrual period and application of any legally available qualification are left for the common-law inquiry on remand; the Court fixes none of them. The holding does not create common law whenever federal funds are involved or apply §3717’s minimum rate, fees or penalties. Principal liability and refusal to waive reimbursement are outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for the common-law prejudgment-interest determination on this assumed matured reimbursement debt. No interest amount or generalized balancing permission is supplied.

#### General Motors Corp. v. Romein, 503 U.S. 181 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1390.
**Decided:** March 9, 1992.

##### Whether Michigan impaired a contractual obligation by requiring repayment of workers’ compensation offsets

**Holding:** A Contract Clause challenge requires an identified contractual obligation that the challenged law impairs; employment regulation does not become an immutable contractual term merely because it governs the relationship. Michigan’s repayment law impairs no offset promise or protected contractual enforcement rule in these employers’ agreements, so substantial-impairment balancing is unnecessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's unanimous opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine Justices adopt this threshold holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The agreements predated the offset statute and contained no offset promise; no contractual enforcement rule was removed. The Court does not reach substantial-impairment balancing or deny protection to genuinely impaired employment contracts.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Michigan Supreme Court’s judgment is affirmed on both constitutional claims. The repayment obligation remains enforceable; no additional benefit award or contract reformation is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Energy Reserves Group v. Kansas Power & Light, 459 U.S. 400 — applied: identify the contractual obligation before measuring impairment.
- Allied Structural Steel v. Spannaus, 438 U.S. 234 — distinguished: the identified contractual pension obligations there do not create an offset promise here.
- Anderson v. Brand, 303 U.S. 95 — applied: federal review independently determines whether the asserted contractual obligation exists.
- United States Trust Co. v. New Jersey, 431 U.S. 1 — applied with its boundary: contractual enforceability is protected, but every workplace regulation is not an immutable contract term.
- Home Building & Loan Association v. Blaisdell, 290 U.S. 398 — preserved: law may inform actual contractual obligations without freezing all public regulation.
- Von Hoffman v. City of Quincy, 4 Wall. 535 — distinguished: withdrawal of contractual enforcement support is absent.
- McCracken v. Hayward, 2 How. 608 — distinguished: this law does not remove a remedy enforcing the employers’ contracts.

##### Whether the retroactive repayment requirement satisfies due process

**Holding:** Retroactive economic legislation satisfies due process when its retroactive operation rationally advances a legitimate legislative purpose. Requiring repayment of these offsets rationally restores Michigan’s compensation compromise and equalizes affected employers, notwithstanding their reliance on the earlier coordination rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion, supported by all nine Justices; all nine Justices adopt the due-process holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Retroactivity itself requires rational justification. Reliance does not automatically defeat this restoration and equalization measure; the Court does not validate retroactive legislation categorically.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Michigan Supreme Court’s judgment is affirmed on both constitutional claims. The repayment obligation remains enforceable; no additional benefit award or contract reformation is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pension Benefit Guaranty Corp. v. R.A. Gray & Co., 467 U.S. 717 — applied: retroactivity itself must rationally serve a legitimate purpose.
- Usery v. Turner Elkhorn Mining Co., 428 U.S. 1 — applied: reasonable retrospective economic burdens can survive reliance objections.
- United States v. Sperry Corp., 493 U.S. 52 — applied: rational allocation of comparable financial burdens is legitimate.
