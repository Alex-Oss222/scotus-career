**[Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, 507 U.S. 410 (1993) — The selected commercial category lacks the required fit](inherited/HOLDINGS.md#the-selected-commercial-category-lacks-the-required-fit)** — Effective: March 24, 1993.

When truthful, nonmisleading commercial publications concerning lawful activity and newspapers cause the same asserted physical harms through their newsracks, the City must justify why its selected commercial category reasonably fits its safety and aesthetic interests. Cincinnati has not done so: removing respondents' sixty-two racks while retaining approximately 1,500–2,000 physically similar newspaper racks violates the First Amendment; a small reduction is not invalid merely for being small, and no least-restrictive-means or all-sources-at-once requirement follows.

**[Forsyth County v. A licensing rule directed at protected demonstrations is facially invalid when neither its text, authoritative construction, nor established administration supplies narrow, objective, definite standards controlling whether and how much officials charge. A challenger need not prove discriminatory administration of its own application, and a maximum fee does not supply the missing standards.

Neither calling those expenses a secondary effect nor imposing a small numerical ceiling makes the charge constitutional.

**Unresolved boundary:** Different fees alone do not prove invalidity. Authoritative narrowing construction and established administration may supply constraints; presumed benign discretion does not. Concrete indigency, fixed neutral fees, particular content-independent costs and municipal funding duties remain undecided; separate City and School Board claims are outside this review.
