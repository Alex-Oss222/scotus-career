## Standards and Tests selection: Chapter 7 strip-down of allowed real-property liens under §506(d)

### Chapter 7 strip-down of allowed real-property liens under §506(d)

**Governing question:** Whether §506(d) reduces an allowed, lien-backed real-property debt to its current judicially determined collateral value in the Chapter 7 setting decided in *Dewsnup*.

**Current rule:** When the claim is allowed under §502 and secured by the real-property lien, §506(d) does not strip that lien down merely because §506(a) values the collateral below the allowed debt. Section 506(a) continues to value secured and unsecured amounts for treatment of the claim; that valuation does not automatically avoid the corresponding lien under §506(d). The rule distinguishes personal discharge from lien survival and does not immunize liens from an independently applicable express avoidance provision.

**Authority by component:**

- Limited Chapter 7 construction and continued valuation function: [Dewsnup v. Timm — Allowed real-property liens in the presented Chapter 7 setting](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Dewsnup_v_Timm_merits_1992-01-15.md), January 15, 1992; Blackmun's opinion joined by White, Stevens, O'Connor, and Kennedy.

**Present operation effective:** *Dewsnup*, January 15, 1992.

**Open question:** Other statutory settings, reorganization restrictions, express statutory exceptions, and estate-administration issues are not resolved by this construction. It does not adopt an abandonment rationale.

**Holdings navigation:** [Dewsnup v. Timm, 502 U.S. 410 (1992) — Liens and claim valuation](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#dewsnup-v-timm-502-us-410-1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Principal-residence mortgage rights in Chapter 13

### Principal-residence mortgage rights in Chapter 13

**Governing question:** What instructions govern principal-residence mortgage rights in Chapter 13?

**Current rule:** When an allowed claim secured only by the debtor's principal-residence real property retains a secured portion, §1322(b)(2) protects the holder's mortgage rights as a whole despite §506(a) valuation and bifurcation. The debtor cannot modify the deficiency portion by altering protected repayment or enforcement rights. Independent restrictions and §1322(b)(5) remain: cure within a reasonable time and maintenance during the case are permitted when the last claim payment falls after the final plan payment, not general rewriting. The stay remains distinct. Wholly unsecured liens and classification of ancillary deed provisions are undecided; Dewsnup's Chapter 7 rule remains separate.

**Authority by component:**

- Valuation does not remove the protected rights of this mortgage holder: [Nobelman v. American Savings Bank — Valuation does not remove the protected rights of this mortgage holder](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/nobelman_merits_1993-06-01.md), 1993-06-01. Thomas’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-06-01; Nobelman v. American Savings Bank, for the components identified above.

## Standards and Tests selection: Making a firearm before final assembly

### Making a firearm before final assembly

**Governing question:** Whether the National Firearms Act's definition of making invariably requires final mechanical assembly.

**Current rule:** The references to manufacturing and otherwise producing in §5845(i) are not confined to the final mechanical act of assembling parts. A universal actual-assembly prerequisite is therefore impermissible. This negative construction supplies no general liability rule for every pure-parts kit, incomplete collection, sham arrangement or genuine lawful alternative configuration.

**Authority by component:**
- Rejection of universal final assembly: [United States v. Thompson/Center Arms Co., “Making is not invariably postponed until final assembly”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/United_States_v_Thompson_Center_Arms_Co_merits_1992-06-08.md), June 8, 1992; expressly shared proposition of Souter, O'Connor, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Stone-Zsela (seven), independent of their disagreement about the actual package. No complete opinion commands five joins.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Thompson/Center Arms Co., June 8, 1992.

**Open question:** The broader boundary among parts combinations, intent to assemble and separate criminal offenses remains unresolved. The case-specific dual-use package refund and its application of residual lenity supply no general kit exemption or new lenity method.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Thompson/Center Arms Co., 504 U.S. 505 (1992) — National Firearms Act making tax](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-thompsoncenter-arms-co-504-us-505-1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Collateral regularity of prior counseled pleas used for enhancement

### Collateral regularity of prior counseled pleas used for enhancement

**Governing question:** What instructions govern collateral regularity of prior counseled pleas used for enhancement?

**Current rule:** For collateral challenges to final counseled convictions used for sentence enhancement, due process permits Kentucky's procedure requiring the Commonwealth to prove the judgment and the defendant to produce evidence of infringement or procedural irregularity. After rebuttal, Kentucky requires the Commonwealth affirmatively to establish constitutional validity. Missing old transcripts alone, without alleged official misconduct, do not rebut regularity. Due process does not require clear-and-convincing extrarecord proof of plea validity in that setting. This is permission for that allocation, not a universal collateral proof quantum; direct-review waiver requirements and the exclusion of invalid uncounseled convictions survive.

## Standards and Tests selection: Nonemployee organizers' access under a nondiscriminatory exclusion policy

### Nonemployee organizers' access under a nondiscriminatory exclusion policy

**Governing question:** When the Board may balance a nonemployee organizer's need for entry against the employer's property rights.

**Current rule:** Nonemployee organizers must demonstrate that employees are practically beyond reasonable offsite communication before balancing may begin. Greater convenience or improved organizing success does not establish that threshold. The Board may assess actual obstacles through its expertise, but it may not replace the controlling statutory inaccessibility requirement with universal balancing that treats alternatives as merely another factor. The inquiry requires neither organizing success nor exhaustion of every imaginable contact technique. Employee solicitation, discriminatory exclusion, and lawful public-property advocacy remain governed by their distinct rules; genuine isolation by living or working location remains within the inaccessibility inquiry.

**Authority by component:**

- Antecedent inaccessibility, communication-versus-success distinction, and rejection of universal *Jean Country* balancing: [Lechmere v. NLRB — Nonemployee access requires demonstrated inaccessibility before balancing](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Lechmere_v_NLRB_merits_1992-01-27.md), January 27, 1992; Stone's opinion joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** *Lechmere*, January 27, 1992.

## Standards and Tests selection: An agency interpretation necessarily embodied in its decision

### An agency interpretation necessarily embodied in its decision

**Governing question:** Whether an agency's decision contains its own sufficient statutory ground when later counsel states that ground in more explicit words.

**Current rule:** A reviewing court may recognize an interpretation necessarily embodied in the agency's own decision even when the agency did not use the exact words later employed by counsel. The actual decision must disclose that operative premise. This permits recognition of the agency's ground, not judicial invention of a new ground, missing findings or a substitute policy; appellate rationalizations cannot sustain a rationale absent from the agency decision.

**Authority by component:**
- Discernible-agency-ground boundary: [National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp.](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/National_Railroad_Passenger_Corp_v_Boston_and_Maine_Corp_merits_1992-03-25.md), March 25, 1992, holding 2, “The agency's discernible ground”; Kennedy for Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Souter, applying Chenery while preserving State Farm.

**Present operation effective:** National Railroad Passenger Corp., March 25, 1992.

## Standards and Tests selection: Reviewability of the Secretary's completed census determination

### Reviewability of the Secretary's completed census determination

**Governing question:** APA review of a completed population count followed by presidential transmission of apportionment figures.

**Current rule:** The Secretary's completed census determination is final agency action under §704 despite the President's later statutory transmission of the resulting apportionment. The census statutes neither preclude review nor commit counting entirely to unreviewable discretion: the obligation to count the proper population supplies law to apply, while leaving substantial methodological choice. General supervision and possible correction do not make this completed decision tentative; the prescribed transmission differs from a statutorily independent presidential policy judgment.

**Authority by component:**

- Finality and reviewability: [Franklin v. Massachusetts, “The Secretary's completed census determination is reviewable”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Franklin_v_Massachusetts_merits_1992-06-26.md), June 26, 1992; Stone's Part IV joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter.

**Present operation effective:** *Franklin*, June 26, 1992.

**Open question:** The President's APA status and susceptibility to coercive judicial relief are left undecided. This rule does not turn remaining agency discretion into entitlement to a selected outcome.

**Holdings navigation:** [Franklin v. Massachusetts, 505 U.S. 788 (1992) — Census determinations and judicial review](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#franklin-v-massachusetts-505-us-788-1992-2).

## Standards and Tests selection: Unearmarked allocation and organizational policy exceptions

### Unearmarked allocation and organizational policy exceptions

**Governing question:** What instructions govern unearmarked allocation and organizational policy exceptions?

**Current rule:** Distribution of a lump-sum appropriation among authorized statutory purposes is committed to agency discretion under §701(a)(2) when Congress supplies neither a binding earmark nor another meaningful allocation standard. Legislative-history references and the general trust relationship do not supply a duty to continue a particular program. Enacted boundaries and independent constitutional claims remain. An organizational policy reallocation changing service availability, rather than imposing a new binding eligibility rule, falls within §553(b)(A)'s organization and general-policy exceptions even assuming it is a rule. The separate §552 publication question remains open.

## Standards and Tests selection: Separate due-process and Commerce Clause connections for remote use-tax collection

### Separate due-process and Commerce Clause connections for remote use-tax collection

**Governing question:** A State's imposition of sales-or-use-tax collection duties on a remote seller.

**Current rule:** Purposeful and continuous solicitation of a State's consumers may supply due process's minimum connection without physical presence when the collection obligation is rationally related to benefits received through the market. Bellas Hess's indispensable physical-presence requirement for due process no longer governs this collection duty. The separate Commerce Clause rule remains: a seller whose customer connections are limited to mail or common-carrier commerce without the requisite local presence cannot be required to collect sales or use tax merely because purposeful market access satisfies due process. Complete Auto's substantial-nexus requirement remains distinct from its other requirements. Congress may alter dormant-Commerce constraints through valid legislation but cannot authorize a due-process violation.

**Authority by component:**
- Revised due-process connection: [Quill Corp. v. North Dakota, “1. Purposeful contacts can satisfy due process without physical presence”](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Quill_Corp_v_North_Dakota_merits_1992-05-26.md), May 26, 1992; Stevens Parts I–III, supported by all eight Associate Justices including the author; Stone does not reach it.
- Surviving specific Commerce collection boundary: Quill, “2. The specific Commerce collection precedent remains controlling,” May 26, 1992; express common narrower ground of Stevens, Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Stone (eight), with the record's stated common-ground authority rather than the four-Justice Part IV policy discussion.

**Present operation effective:** Quill Corp. v. North Dakota, May 26, 1992, separating the revised due-process rule from the surviving specific Commerce collection rule.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not govern every state tax, immunize consumers from use tax, set numerical sales or property thresholds, or establish a general software-nexus rule. The particular software application does not supply an additional reusable property rule. Potential retroactive effects of overruling the Commerce rule were excluded from the grant.

**Holdings navigation:** [Quill Corp. v. North Dakota, 504 U.S. 298 (1992) — State-tax collection and interstate commerce](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#quill-corp-v-north-dakota-504-us-298-1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal rules applied to parties govern still-open direct review

### Federal rules applied to parties govern still-open direct review

**Governing question:** What instructions govern federal rules applied to parties govern still-open direct review?

**Current rule:** When this Court applies a federal rule to the parties before it, that rule governs all still-open direct-review cases concerning events within its substantive scope, whether before or after announcement. Selective prospectivity cannot withhold the rule from another such case. Chevron Oil is displaced to that extent. Final judgments are not reopened; genuine remedial rules remain distinct, and every conceivable prospective disposition is not decided.

## Standards and Tests selection: Collateral Rule 11 sanctions despite absence of merits jurisdiction

### Collateral Rule 11 sanctions despite absence of merits jurisdiction

**Governing question:** Whether an erroneous assertion of subject-matter jurisdiction defeats a Rule 11 sanction for completed litigation misconduct.

**Current rule:** A district court may impose and preserve a valid sanction under the then-effective Rule 11 for litigation misconduct before it even when its assertion of subject-matter jurisdiction over the action is later held erroneous. The sanction must address an independently authorized procedural violation collateral to the merits. It does not validate the jurisdictional error, restore the underlying merits judgment, or authorize another collateral order without that order's own lawful source and genuine independence.

**Authority by component:**

- Rule-based authority, collateral character, and Article III limit: [Willy v. Coastal Corp. — This collateral Rule 11 sanction survives the jurisdictional ruling](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Willy_v_Coastal_Corp_merits_1992-03-03.md), March 3, 1992; Stone's unanimous opinion, extending *Cooter & Gell* from voluntary dismissal to the presented jurisdictional defect.

**Present operation effective:** *Willy*, March 3, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Coercive discovery serving an unauthorized merits action does not acquire this completed sanction's independence. General inherent sanctioning power is not decided.

**Holdings navigation:** [Willy v. Coastal Corp., 503 U.S. 131 (1992) — Collateral procedural authority](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#willy-v-coastal-corp-503-us-131-1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Downstream water-quality standards in federal discharge permitting

### Downstream water-quality standards in federal discharge permitting

**Governing question:** Whether and how EPA may apply downstream state standards in an interstate federal discharge permit, including where receiving waters are already degraded.

**Current rule:** The Clean Water Act authorizes EPA under §402(a) and its valid regulations to require compliance with applicable downstream state water-quality standards; limits on the downstream State's own direct authority do not restrict that federal delegation. The Act does not categorically forbid a new discharge merely because effluent will reach already noncompliant waters irrespective of whether the discharge causes a detectable violation under applicable permit standards. For the federal interstate permit setting decided in *Arkansas*, federally approved downstream standards incorporated by valid regulation operate as federal permitting law, and EPA's reasonable, consistently held construction receives substantial deference. EPA permissibly construed Oklahoma's no-degradation requirement to prohibit an actual detectable or measurable violation, rather than every infinitesimal theoretical effect. This construction preserves enforceable standards and supplies no general tolerance for pollution labeled trivial.

**Authority by component:**

- Delegated downstream-protection authority: [Arkansas v. Oklahoma — EPA may require downstream standards in this federal permit](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Arkansas_v_Oklahoma_merits_1992-02-26.md), February 26, 1992.
- Rejection of an already-degraded-waters categorical bar: [Arkansas — Existing degradation is not a categorical statutory prohibition](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Arkansas_v_Oklahoma_merits_1992-02-26.md), February 26, 1992.
- Incorporated standards and permissible detectable-violation construction: [Arkansas — Reasonable construction of the incorporated downstream standards](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Arkansas_v_Oklahoma_merits_1992-02-26.md), February 26, 1992. Stevens's unanimous opinion controls these components.
- Distinct administrative proof and unchanged judicial-review standards needed to apply that construction: [Arkansas — Supported findings govern review of this permit](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Arkansas_v_Oklahoma_merits_1992-02-26.md), February 26, 1992; the same unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** *Arkansas v. Oklahoma*, February 26, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** The no-detectable-violation showing in this permitting proceeding uses an administrative preponderance burden; reviewing courts apply whole-record substantial-evidence review rather than reweighing merely permissible alternative findings. Those standards retain their distinct functions. Existing degradation matters where the actual governing standards make it relevant; detectable violations, unreliable findings, and failure to apply valid standards remain reviewable.

**Open question:** The Court does not hold that Congress requires identical compliance in every federal or state permitting setting, construe state law for unrelated purposes, or decide independent source-state remedies.

**Holdings navigation:** [Arkansas v. Oklahoma, 503 U.S. 91 (1992) — Federal permit interpretation and review](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#arkansas-v-oklahoma-503-us-91-1992); [Arkansas v. Oklahoma, 503 U.S. 91 (1992) — Interstate water-pollution permits](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#arkansas-v-oklahoma-503-us-91-1992-1).

## Additional verbatim Holdings selections

##### Whether Alabama may impose an additional disposal charge on equivalent hazardous waste because it originated outside the State

**Holding:** A State may not impose an additional disposal charge on out-of-state hazardous waste that it does not impose on equivalent in-state waste unless it establishes a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve. Alabama's $72-per-ton additional charge fails because the record supplies no origin-linked difference in the relevant risks or costs and adequate evenhanded means remain available; commercial hazardous-waste disposal receives Commerce Clause protection despite the material's dangers.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine adopt the rule, application and remedial limits. Stone's additional compensatory-tax observation is not part of the Court's holding.

**Material application:** The accepted long-term disposal, transportation, monitoring and capacity concerns do not establish that an equivalent imported ton is more dangerous or costly than a domestic ton. Evenhanded charges, total-volume limits and direct safety regulation address those mechanisms; these examples expose the missing justification rather than require a replacement program. Allowing the same waste upon payment of an extra fee does not establish a quarantine justification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The grant concerns the additional fee alone. The base fee, independent volume cap, other permitting provisions, state constitutional claims and other federal challenges are not adjudicated. The State did not press its fair-share or compensatory-tax theory; amici's congressional-authorization theory was neither decided below nor briefed or argued by the parties. No unlimited right to dispose of hazardous waste, new proof quantum, perfect-certainty requirement or least-imaginable-alternative rule is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Alabama Supreme Court's judgment sustaining the additional fee and remand for appropriate state relief from the discriminatory exaction, consistently with *McKesson* where its coerced-payment and postpayment-review prerequisites apply. Neither those prerequisites nor an automatic refund entitlement or amount is determined. The independent base-fee, cap and other rulings outside the grant remain undisturbed; no replacement fee, operating permit or compulsory acceptance requirement issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Philadelphia v. New Jersey*, 437 U.S. 617, applies Commerce protection and nondiscrimination to hazardous-waste transactions. *Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission*, 432 U.S. 333, preserves the State's purpose-and-alternatives burden. *Maine v. Taylor*, 477 U.S. 131, is distinguished because this record lacks distinctive imported risks and unavailable adequate alternatives. *New Energy Co. of Indiana v. Limbach*, 486 U.S. 269, applies to discriminatory charges defended on environmental grounds; complete exclusion is unnecessary. *Wyoming v. Oklahoma*, January 22, 1992, supplies the express-geographic-discrimination rule without extending its other holdings. *Pike v. Bruce Church, Inc.*, 397 U.S. 137, governs a separate evenhanded-regulation branch. *McKesson Corp. v. Division of Alcoholic Beverages and Tobacco*, 496 U.S. 18, supplies the conditional state-remedy principles without fixing a refund.

##### Whether taxing foreign-subsidiary dividends while deducting equivalent domestic-subsidiary dividends discriminates against foreign commerce

**Holding:** A State discriminates against foreign commerce when it includes dividends reflecting a foreign subsidiary’s foreign operations while deducting equivalent domestic dividends, even if the favored class includes corporations in every other State. For the relevant subsidiaries without independent Iowa operations, Iowa’s distinction is unconstitutional unless adequately justified; a taxpayer need not reorganize through a domestic intermediary to escape it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined by White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The relevant comparison is between subsidiaries without independent Iowa operations. A domestic subsidiary doing business in Iowa supplies a distinct taxable connection. Kraft's accepted unitary relationship and Iowa's general authority to apportion connected income do not answer the independent nondiscrimination question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision creates no blanket immunity for foreign dividends, changes no accepted unitary or operational tax-base boundary, and does not decide the independent international multiple-taxation, national-voice or equal-protection theories.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Iowa judgment is reversed and remanded for a constitutionally adequate nondiscriminatory result under applicable due-process remedial requirements. The Court fixes no refund amount, dictates no comprehensive tax-code rewrite and leaves calculations for lawful proceedings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Complete Auto Transit, Inc. v. Brady, 430 U.S. 274 — applied; nondiscrimination remains independent of accepted nexus and apportionment.
- Japan Line, Ltd. v. County of Los Angeles, 441 U.S. 434 — applied for foreign-commerce interests extending beyond local favoritism; no separate multiple-taxation or national-voice ruling.
- Halliburton Oil Well Cementing Co. v. Reily, 373 U.S. 64 — applied to select the most similarly situated comparison and reject compelled reorganization.
- Amerada Hess Corp. v. Director, Division of Taxation, 490 U.S. 66 — distinguished; differences in business nature do not authorize location-based discrimination.
- Westinghouse Electric Corp. v. Tully, 466 U.S. 388 — applied against tax disadvantages tied to business location and avoidance through reorganization.

##### Whether county-origin restrictions at private landfills escape Commerce Clause scrutiny because they also exclude other intrastate counties

**Holding:** A State's prohibition on a private landfill's acceptance of outside-county waste unless a county plan affirmatively authorizes it discriminates against interstate commerce even when it also excludes other counties within that State and some counties admit imports. The restriction cannot stand unless the State establishes a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve; Michigan has not done so for these Waste Import Restrictions, and planning waste services does not convert regulation of a private facility into market participation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt the same rule, application and limits. Blackmun's proposed evidentiary remand supplies no controlling join.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the restrictions on privately owned and operated facilities.

**Material application:** The challenged veto gives local waste access that equivalent outside waste lacks. Neutral quantity limits, appropriate charges and facility standards can address the planning concerns; respondents identify no distinct imported hazard or inadequacy of those alternatives and neither identify further necessary proof nor request an evidentiary remand. The alternatives illustrate the failed justification, rather than require a replacement program. No statewide total exclusion or disadvantage to every outside supplier is required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Only the 1988 Waste Import Restrictions in §§299.413a and 299.430(2), as applied to private landfills, are invalidated. The earlier planning statute, public-facility practices, neutral health, safety and capacity rules, different waste-risk classifications, unconditional operation and genuinely applicable congressional authorization remain outside the decision. No hazardous-waste question arises. Distinct due-process, taking and ownership-transfer licensing matters are not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit's judgment sustaining dismissal and remand through that court for appropriately tailored declaratory and prospective relief consistent with invalidation. Independent neutral planning and permitting remain; the judgment requires neither unconditional permit issuance, acceptance of particular waste nor operation beyond neutral capacity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Philadelphia v. New Jersey*, 437 U.S. 617, applies to origin-only exclusion at private waste facilities. *Brimmer v. Rebman*, 138 U.S. 78, and *Dean Milk Co. v. Madison*, 340 U.S. 349, prevent a substate boundary and exclusion of some intrastate commerce from curing geographic discrimination. *New Energy Co. of Indiana v. Limbach*, 486 U.S. 269, and *Wyoming v. Oklahoma*, January 22, 1992, distinguish the existence of discrimination from its extent. *Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission*, 432 U.S. 333, preserves the purpose-and-alternatives burden. *Maine v. Taylor*, 477 U.S. 131, is distinguished for lack of a distinctive imported hazard and unavailable adequate alternatives. *Hughes v. Alexandria Scrap Corp.*, 426 U.S. 794, and *Reeves, Inc. v. Stake*, 447 U.S. 429, preserve market participation while leaving private-facility regulation outside it. *Pike v. Bruce Church, Inc.*, 397 U.S. 137, remains the separate rule for evenhanded regulation.

##### Whether §318(b)(6)(A) changes governing timber law or directs results under unchanged law

**Holding:** Section 318(b)(6)(A) substitutes compliance with §318(b)(3) and (b)(5) for satisfaction of the requirements under MBTA, NEPA, NFMA, FLPMA and OCLA identified in the pending timber disputes, while leaving actual compliance for courts to decide. That express substantive amendment is effective although enacted in an appropriations statute and does not violate Article III on the ground decided below.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's unanimous opinion of the Court, supported by all nine Justices; all nine Justices adopt the changed-law holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the Northwest Timber Compromise’s covered sales and fiscal-year 1990 framework, with continued application to covered sales contracts; it does not find any sale compliant. The sixteen enjoined Forest Service sales sustain the controversy; the apparently moot BLM branch supplies no separate merits disposition. Klein’s full reach, the unraised challenge to narrowly targeted legislation, and final-judgment reopening remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit’s judgment is reversed and remanded for judicial application of the new statutory criteria to the still-live sales. No timber sale is automatically approved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Klein, 13 Wall. 128 — distinguished on the ground decided below; its full constitutional reach is not resolved.
- Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co., 18 How. 421 — consistent with adjudication under changed law; no broad targeted-legislation holding is drawn from it.
- Simpson v. United States, 435 U.S. 6 — applied: specific provisions qualify general ones.
- United States v. Will, 449 U.S. 200 — applied: clear substantive amendments may appear in appropriations legislation.
- TVA v. Hill, 437 U.S. 153 — distinguished: the amendment here is express rather than an inferred repeal.
- NLRB v. Jones & Laughlin Steel Corp., 301 U.S. 1 — preserved: a possible constitutionally valid construction reinforces, but does not replace, the enacted meaning.
