## Standards and Tests selection: Collateral Rule 11 sanctions despite absence of merits jurisdiction

### Collateral Rule 11 sanctions despite absence of merits jurisdiction

**Governing question:** Whether an erroneous assertion of subject-matter jurisdiction defeats a Rule 11 sanction for completed litigation misconduct.

**Current rule:** A district court may impose and preserve a valid sanction under the then-effective Rule 11 for litigation misconduct before it even when its assertion of subject-matter jurisdiction over the action is later held erroneous. The sanction must address an independently authorized procedural violation collateral to the merits. It does not validate the jurisdictional error, restore the underlying merits judgment, or authorize another collateral order without that order's own lawful source and genuine independence.

**Authority by component:**

- Rule-based authority, collateral character, and Article III limit: [Willy v. Coastal Corp. — This collateral Rule 11 sanction survives the jurisdictional ruling](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Willy_v_Coastal_Corp_merits_1992-03-03.md), March 3, 1992; Stone's unanimous opinion, extending *Cooter & Gell* from voluntary dismissal to the presented jurisdictional defect.

**Present operation effective:** *Willy*, March 3, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Coercive discovery serving an unauthorized merits action does not acquire this completed sanction's independence. General inherent sanctioning power is not decided.

**Holdings navigation:** [Willy v. Coastal Corp., 503 U.S. 131 (1992) — Collateral procedural authority](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#willy-v-coastal-corp-503-us-131-1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination

### Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination

**Governing question:** Whether remedial silence or Spending Clause notice bars compensatory damages in the established Title IX private action.

**Current rule:** When a cognizable federal statutory cause of action exists, courts may award appropriate relief absent clear congressional direction limiting it; recognizing relief does not itself create a new cause of action. Title IX's established private action permits compensatory damages for proved intentional discrimination, subject to proof of recipient responsibility, causation, and compensable loss. The Spending Clause notice principle does not categorically bar that compensation for intentional breach of Title IX's express nondiscrimination condition.

**Authority by component:**

- Appropriate relief in an existing action and availability of Title IX compensation: [Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools — Compensatory relief in the established Title IX action](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Franklin_v_Gwinnett_County_Public_Schools_merits_1992-02-26.md), February 26, 1992.
- Rejection of the categorical funding-notice bar: [Franklin — Notice does not bar damages for the pleaded intentional violation](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1991/records/Franklin_v_Gwinnett_County_Public_Schools_merits_1992-02-26.md), February 26, 1992. Both holdings rest on White's opinion joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; Scalia and Thomas supply judgment votes only on a narrower statutory ground.

**Present operation effective:** *Franklin*, February 26, 1992.

**Open question:** The decision supplies no final recipient-attribution rule, punitive remedy, liability for unintended discriminatory effects, or resolution of genuinely ambiguous funding conditions.

**Holdings navigation:** [Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992) — Statutory discrimination remedies](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/HOLDINGS.md#franklin-v-gwinnett-county-public-schools-503-us-60-1992).

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal rules applied to parties govern still-open direct review

### Federal rules applied to parties govern still-open direct review

**Governing question:** What instructions govern federal rules applied to parties govern still-open direct review?

**Current rule:** When this Court applies a federal rule to the parties before it, that rule governs all still-open direct-review cases concerning events within its substantive scope, whether before or after announcement. Selective prospectivity cannot withhold the rule from another such case. Chevron Oil is displaced to that extent. Final judgments are not reopened; genuine remedial rules remain distinct, and every conceivable prospective disposition is not decided.

## Standards and Tests selection: Miranda habeas review and fair litigation of independent coercion claims

### Miranda habeas review and fair litigation of independent coercion claims

**Governing question:** What instructions govern miranda habeas review and fair litigation of independent coercion claims?

**Current rule:** A full and fair state opportunity does not itself bar federal habeas review of a claim that a conviction rests on statements obtained in violation of Miranda; Powell's Fourth Amendment exclusion restriction does not extend to that claim. Ordinary habeas gates, merits and harmlessness remain. A court may not grant relief on an independent postwarning involuntariness theory neither pleaded nor tried by express or implied consent when the State lacked an opportunity to present relevant evidence. Litigation of custody and warnings alone is not consent; later presentation remains subject to pleading, exhaustion, retroactivity and other applicable requirements.

**Authority by component:**

Williams — An unpleaded, unconsented coercion theory requires fair litigation](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/withrow_merits_1993-04-21.md), 1993-04-21. ## Standards and Tests selection: Proprietary public purchases and labor preemption

### Proprietary public purchases and labor preemption

**Governing question:** What instructions govern proprietary public purchases and labor preemption?

**Current rule:** Garmon and Machinists preemption do not bar a project-specific contracting condition when the State acts for its proprietary interests as owner and purchaser, analogous private conduct is lawful, and Congress has not expressly or impliedly displaced the proprietary conduct. Public ownership or a procurement label alone is insufficient; purchasing cannot regulate unrelated conduct or impose general labor policy. The holding creates no general NLRA-employer status for a State. Distinct §§8(e) and 8(f) limits remain, including §8(f)'s preservation of employee elections under §§9(c) and 9(e).

**Authority by component:**

- Genuine proprietary project purchases are not state labor regulation: [Building & Construction Trades Council v. Associated Builders & Contractors (Boston Harbor) — Genuine proprietary project purchases are not state labor regulation](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/building-construction-trades-v-associated-builders-boston-harbor-merits-1993-03-08.md), 1993-03-08. Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: unanimous controlling authority in both dockets.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-03-08; Building & Construction Trades Council v. Associated Builders & Contractors (Boston Harbor), for the components identified above.

## Standards and Tests selection: Unreasonable-rate counterclaims, recoupment and primary jurisdiction

### Unreasonable-rate counterclaims, recoupment and primary jurisdiction

**Governing question:** What instructions govern unreasonable-rate counterclaims, recoupment and primary jurisdiction?

**Current rule:** A shipper may assert §11705(b)(3) unreasonable-rate damages as a counterclaim in an undercharge action on the same shipments; Rule 8(c) permits correcting a defense designation when justice requires. Under §11706(g), accrual occurs upon delivery or tender, without prior payment. A negotiated rate alone supplies no common-law defense to the filed tariff. The two-year affirmative limitation in §11706(c)(2) does not bar same-transaction recoupment up to the carrier's asserted liability, but supplies no extension for unrelated claims or excess affirmative recovery. The court must allow a reasonable opportunity to obtain the ICC's rate determination. It retains jurisdiction and ordinarily stays proceedings; dismissal without prejudice is permitted only if it would not unfairly disadvantage the parties. Neither payment nor a completed ICC ruling is required to file.

**Authority by component:**

- The statutory claim may be asserted before payment: [Reiter v. Cooper — The statutory claim may be asserted before payment](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/Reiter_v_Cooper_merits_1993-03-08.md), 1993-03-08. Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this statutory rule and application.
- Same-transaction recoupment survives the affirmative limitation: [Reiter v. Cooper — Same-transaction recoupment survives the affirmative limitation](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/Reiter_v_Cooper_merits_1993-03-08.md), 1993-03-08. Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices support this independent limitation ruling.
- Primary jurisdiction does not withdraw judicial jurisdiction: [Reiter v. Cooper — Primary jurisdiction does not withdraw judicial jurisdiction](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1992/records/Reiter_v_Cooper_merits_1993-03-08.md), 1993-03-08. ## 3. Standing Practices

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). It ordinarily issues without opinion; a brief per curiam and noted dissents are available. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. An application not on the Term's inventory remains subject to ordinary Circuit Justice practice. Source: OT1991 opening practice as carried through the validated OT1992 records and final OT1992 Term Continuity Note. The practice continues unchanged into OT1993.

##### No. 92-5584: an arguable current petition is not defeated by filing history

**Holding:** Rule 39.8 permits denial of leave to file without prepayment only upon a finding that the particular petition is frivolous or malicious; earlier unsuccessful filings cannot substitute for that finding. No. 92-5584's distinct general-rule and prospective statutory arguments clear the nonfrivolousness threshold on this record, so the Court grants leave without deciding that the arguments warrant certiorari or would succeed on the merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices; all nine participating Justices adopt this filing-specific application and its limits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zatko v. California, 502 U.S. 16 (November 4, 1991): applied as the existing discretionary, particular-submission rule; its extreme-abuse finding does not establish the frivolousness of a different current petition.
- District of Columbia Court of Appeals v. Feldman, 460 U.S. 462 (1983): its general-rule/individual-judgment distinction supplies an arguable legal question here; the Court does not decide which side of that distinction the complete complaint occupies.
- Denton v. Hernandez, 504 U.S. 25 (May 4, 1992): its distinction between arguability and clearly baseless factual allegations informs this limited assessment; its statutory review and remedial rules remain unchanged and are not imported wholesale into Rule 39.

## Reading copy: state/HOLDINGS.md#whether-alabama-may-impose-an-additional-disposal-charge-on-equivalent-hazardous-waste-because-it-originated-outside-the-state

##### Whether Alabama may impose an additional disposal charge on equivalent hazardous waste because it originated outside the State

**Holding:** A State may not impose an additional disposal charge on out-of-state hazardous waste that it does not impose on equivalent in-state waste unless it establishes a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve. Alabama's $72-per-ton additional charge fails because the record supplies no origin-linked difference in the relevant risks or costs and adequate evenhanded means remain available; commercial hazardous-waste disposal receives Commerce Clause protection despite the material's dangers.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine adopt the rule, application and remedial limits. Stone's additional compensatory-tax observation is not part of the Court's holding.

**Material application:** The accepted long-term disposal, transportation, monitoring and capacity concerns do not establish that an equivalent imported ton is more dangerous or costly than a domestic ton. Evenhanded charges, total-volume limits and direct safety regulation address those mechanisms; these examples expose the missing justification rather than require a replacement program. Allowing the same waste upon payment of an extra fee does not establish a quarantine justification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The grant concerns the additional fee alone. The base fee, independent volume cap, other permitting provisions, state constitutional claims and other federal challenges are not adjudicated. The State did not press its fair-share or compensatory-tax theory; amici's congressional-authorization theory was neither decided below nor briefed or argued by the parties. No unlimited right to dispose of hazardous waste, new proof quantum, perfect-certainty requirement or least-imaginable-alternative rule is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Alabama Supreme Court's judgment sustaining the additional fee and remand for appropriate state relief from the discriminatory exaction, consistently with *McKesson* where its coerced-payment and postpayment-review prerequisites apply. Neither those prerequisites nor an automatic refund entitlement or amount is determined. The independent base-fee, cap and other rulings outside the grant remain undisturbed; no replacement fee, operating permit or compulsory acceptance requirement issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Philadelphia v. New Jersey*, 437 U.S. 617, applies Commerce protection and nondiscrimination to hazardous-waste transactions. *Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission*, 432 U.S. 333, preserves the State's purpose-and-alternatives burden. *Maine v. Taylor*, 477 U.S. 131, is distinguished because this record lacks distinctive imported risks and unavailable adequate alternatives. *New Energy Co. of Indiana v. Limbach*, 486 U.S. 269, applies to discriminatory charges defended on environmental grounds; complete exclusion is unnecessary. *Wyoming v. Oklahoma*, January 22, 1992, supplies the express-geographic-discrimination rule without extending its other holdings. *Pike v. Bruce Church, Inc.*, 397 U.S. 137, governs a separate evenhanded-regulation branch. *McKesson Corp. v. Division of Alcoholic Beverages and Tobacco*, 496 U.S. 18, supplies the conditional state-remedy principles without fixing a refund.

## Reading copy: state/HOLDINGS.md#whether-county-origin-restrictions-at-private-landfills-escape-commerce-clause-scrutiny-because-they-also-exclude-other-intrastate-counties

##### Whether county-origin restrictions at private landfills escape Commerce Clause scrutiny because they also exclude other intrastate counties

**Holding:** A State's prohibition on a private landfill's acceptance of outside-county waste unless a county plan affirmatively authorizes it discriminates against interstate commerce even when it also excludes other counties within that State and some counties admit imports. The restriction cannot stand unless the State establishes a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve; Michigan has not done so for these Waste Import Restrictions, and planning waste services does not convert regulation of a private facility into market participation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt the same rule, application and limits. Blackmun's proposed evidentiary remand supplies no controlling join.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the restrictions on privately owned and operated facilities.

**Material application:** The challenged veto gives local waste access that equivalent outside waste lacks. Neutral quantity limits, appropriate charges and facility standards can address the planning concerns; respondents identify no distinct imported hazard or inadequacy of those alternatives and neither identify further necessary proof nor request an evidentiary remand. The alternatives illustrate the failed justification, rather than require a replacement program. No statewide total exclusion or disadvantage to every outside supplier is required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Only the 1988 Waste Import Restrictions in §§299.413a and 299.430(2), as applied to private landfills, are invalidated. The earlier planning statute, public-facility practices, neutral health, safety and capacity rules, different waste-risk classifications, unconditional operation and genuinely applicable congressional authorization remain outside the decision. No hazardous-waste question arises. Distinct due-process, taking and ownership-transfer licensing matters are not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit's judgment sustaining dismissal and remand through that court for appropriately tailored declaratory and prospective relief consistent with invalidation. Independent neutral planning and permitting remain; the judgment requires neither unconditional permit issuance, acceptance of particular waste nor operation beyond neutral capacity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Philadelphia v. New Jersey*, 437 U.S. 617, applies to origin-only exclusion at private waste facilities. *Brimmer v. Rebman*, 138 U.S. 78, and *Dean Milk Co. v. Madison*, 340 U.S. 349, prevent a substate boundary and exclusion of some intrastate commerce from curing geographic discrimination. *New Energy Co. of Indiana v. Limbach*, 486 U.S. 269, and *Wyoming v. Oklahoma*, January 22, 1992, distinguish the existence of discrimination from its extent. *Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission*, 432 U.S. 333, preserves the purpose-and-alternatives burden. *Maine v. Taylor*, 477 U.S. 131, is distinguished for lack of a distinctive imported hazard and unavailable adequate alternatives. *Hughes v. Alexandria Scrap Corp.*, 426 U.S. 794, and *Reeves, Inc. v. Stake*, 447 U.S. 429, preserve market participation while leaving private-facility regulation outside it. *Pike v. Bruce Church, Inc.*, 397 U.S. 137, remains the separate rule for evenhanded regulation.

## Reading copy: state/HOLDINGS.md#whether-extreme-repetitive-frivolous-filing-permits-denial-of-pending-rule-398-fee-waiver-motions

##### Whether extreme repetitive frivolous filing permits denial of pending Rule 39.8 fee-waiver motions

**Holding:** When the Court is satisfied that pending submissions are frivolous and the applicants' repeated frivolous filings constitute extreme abuse of its process, Rule 39.8 permits denial of in-forma-pauperis status, meaning permission to file without paying fees. Denial may require payment of the docketing fee and compliance with ordinary petition form rules by a stated deadline; it does not decide petition merits or impose a broader prospective filing ban.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam opinion joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; five of the eight participating Justices.

**Material application:** The present submissions were found frivolous; the applicants' extraordinary filing histories supported the finding of continuing abuse.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling is discretionary. It adopts no numerical threshold, does not make earlier denials alone conclusive, and decides no underlying claim or constitutional entitlement to unrestricted free filing.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The seventeen pending fee-waiver motions are denied, with an opportunity to pay docketing fees and submit petitions in the required form by November 25, 1991. Ordinary screening remains available upon compliance.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** In re McDonald and In re Sindram are applied as authority for tailored protection of judicial process; neither is extended to a categorical prospective ban.
