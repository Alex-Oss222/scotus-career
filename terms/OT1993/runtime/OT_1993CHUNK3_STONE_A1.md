#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Review Posture and Requested Relief:** Louisiana Supreme Court, 595 So.2d 615 (1992), reversed dismissal under forum non conveniens and held that Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure Article 123(C) barred that dismissal. The lower courts had treated federal maritime practice as controlling. American Dredging seeks reinstatement of the dismissal in favor of another forum. Miller seeks retention of his state-court Jones Act and maritime action. [P13](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/443/)

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Louisiana Supreme Court’s judgment.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Allow the state action to proceed; enter no liability award and no finding on the underlying injury claims.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: State forum procedure and maritime uniformity

**Entering Law:** Southern Pacific Co. v. Jensen, 244 U.S. 205 (1917), restricts state rules that materially prejudice characteristic features of general maritime law or interfere with its proper harmony and uniformity. It does not automatically displace every state procedure. Gulf Oil Corp. v. Gilbert, 330 U.S. 501 (1947), and Piper Aircraft Co. v. Reyno, 454 U.S. 235 (1981), describe federal forum non conveniens, including an available adequate alternative forum and private/public convenience considerations, ordinarily reviewed for abuse of discretion. Missouri ex rel. Southern R.R. v. Mayfield, 340 U.S. 1 (1950), and Bainbridge v. Merchants & Miners Transportation Co., 287 U.S. 278 (1932), recognize state procedural authority in federal maritime-related litigation. The applicable Jones Act and §1333 supply the source framework. [P13](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/443/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain affirmance, explaining uniformity rather than relying solely on the procedural label.

The saving-to-suitors clause preserves a meaningful role for competent state in personam forums. Forum non conveniens is an ordinary forum-management doctrine, not a maritime liability entitlement. *Mayfield* and *Bainbridge* support state procedural room; *Jensen* still prohibits material impairment of characteristic maritime features and necessary uniformity. [Record, 510 U.S. 443](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/443/).

This is **a permissible boundary clarification**, consistent with Stone's federalism and resistance to free-floating preemption. It does not overrule *Jensen*. Stone should nevertheless confront the strongest objection: forum selection affects expense, evidence, settlement pressure, and potentially practical outcomes, especially when Louisiana has little connection to the injury. “Procedure” alone cannot immunize a rule from maritime preemption. The stronger answer is that optional dismissal does not define primary maritime obligations, and Congress preserved diverse state forums despite ordinary procedural differences.

Courts should distinguish jurisdiction, venue, choice of substantive maritime law, and discretionary forum refusal. A state can retain an action in a competent forum without acquiring federal in rem jurisdiction or changing the duty and damages rules. A nominal procedural rule designed to defeat a federal maritime right could fail the *Jensen* inquiry even if described as docket management.

The administrability advantage is that courts can identify what legal entitlement the state rule changes rather than compare every practical litigation consequence. The cost is continued forum shopping and unequal litigation expense. Stone should accept that cost as part of the statutory allocation unless a concrete interference with maritime law is established. Affirmance permits proceedings; it decides no liability, damages, or general right to sue anywhere.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Miller, a seaman, was injured while working on the tug M/V John R. in the Delaware River. Relevant parties, treatment, and events had substantial connections to Pennsylvania, New York, and Mississippi rather than Louisiana. He sued in Louisiana for Jones Act and related maritime relief. The present issue assumes a competent state in personam forum and concerns its discretionary refusal to hear the action, not a adjudicated tort judgment. 28 U.S.C. §1333(1) preserves other remedies under the saving-to-suitors clause while reserving exclusive federal in rem admiralty jurisdiction. Venue, personal jurisdiction, merits choice of law, and forum non conveniens are distinct. This matter does not decide whether Louisiana may change the substantive maritime duty or remedy. [P13](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/443/)

**Source Support:** 510 U.S. 445-464: maritime claim, state procedural law, the saving-to-suitors clause, and earlier uniformity cases. [P13](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/443/)

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment rejecting recusal on this record, while rejecting the categorical judicial-source exclusion.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the convictions undisturbed on the recusal issue. Order no reassignment, retrial, or additional recusal procedure.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Objective appearance and the source of asserted bias

**Entering Law:** Section 455(a) requires disqualification when impartiality might reasonably be questioned; §455(b)(1) addresses personal bias or prejudice. United States v. Grinnell Corp., 384 U.S. 563, 583 (1966), discusses an extrajudicial source in the personal-bias context. Berger v. United States, 255 U.S. 22 (1921), illustrates disqualifying prejudice, and Liljeberg v. Health Services Acquisition Corp., 486 U.S. 847 (1988), enforces §455(a)’s objective protection and source-specific remedial inquiry. Ordinary judicial familiarity and rulings do not alone establish disqualifying partiality. The statutory text does not say an outside source is a mandatory element. [P14](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/540/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain affirmance under the ordinary objective appearance standard; reject both an outside-source prerequisite and a requirement that fair judgment be impossible.

Section 455(a) asks whether impartiality might reasonably be questioned. Section 455(b)(1), §144, and *Grinnell*'s personal-bias discussion do not erase that broader statutory wording. Stone can **clarify the role of source** without abandoning the established distinction between bias and ordinary judicial experience. [Case record](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/540/); [contemporaneous critique of an impossibility threshold](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/92-6921.ZC.html).

This strongly fits Stone's concern with real adjudicative neutrality and his resistance to inventing elements. Outside information is neither necessary nor sufficient: a judge can express disqualifying prejudgment in court, while a general scholarly view held outside court may not justify recusal. Simulated *Gibson* does not turn every unfavorable judicial interaction into an appearance violation.

Application must use an informed observer aware of the complete proceedings. Examine the substance, repetition, target, context, and apparent closure to contrary evidence of the comments. Firm control, impatience, or an adverse ruling ordinarily says little about impermissible partiality. A declaration that a defendant's group is inherently dishonest, or that no contrary evidence will matter, has different significance even if uttered during trial. The current incidents, assessed together, support affirmance if they remain ordinary criticism and administration.

The strongest objection is strategic recusal motions and appellate relitigation of routine rulings. Contextual specificity and ordinary review constraints address that problem without raising an appearance statute to actual impossibility. A court must explain why a reasonable observer would or would not question impartiality; “all comments occurred in court” is inadequate. No retrial or reassignment follows where the correct standard yields no violation, and the constitutional structural-error inquiry remains separate.

**Egalitarian application:** An impartial tribunal protects defendants, prosecutors, victims, and the public. Strong criticism or ordinary adverse rulings do not alone establish disqualification, but courtroom conduct may give an informed reasonable observer a basis to question impartiality without an external source or proof that fair judgment is impossible. Apply §455(a)’s objective appearance standard to the actual conduct and context, preserving both an effective recusal remedy and protection against tactical judge selection.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** At a 1991 criminal trial arising from protest activity, petitioners sought recusal based on rulings and comments during and after Bourgeois’s 1983 trial before the same judge and later admonishments of counsel and defendants in the current trial. The claimed conduct involved impatience, trial administration, evidentiary rulings, and criticism. No outside relationship, financial interest, or extrajudicial information was established. The record must be examined cumulatively rather than assuming every adverse ruling proves bias. The statutory objective appearance question is distinct from actual-bias recusal under §455(b)(1), affidavit procedure under §144, appellate correction of legal error, and the constitutional impartial-tribunal floor. The question does not warrant declaring every unfavorable ruling structurally unconstitutional. Statutory interpretation is reviewed independently; ordinary case-specific recusal rulings receive the review appropriate to discretion under that correct legal rule. [P14](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/540/)
