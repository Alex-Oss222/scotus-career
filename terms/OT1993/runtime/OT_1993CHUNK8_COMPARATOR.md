## 85. Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Gottshall

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case Name:** Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Gottshall

**Citation or Docket:** 512 U.S. 532 (1994); No. 92-1956, including Carlisle under Rule 12.2

**Historical Event Date:** 1994-06-24

Comparator only. Introduce after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. This material is not simulated precedent and does not control the unapproved Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Both judgments reversed and remanded, 6-3. Gottshall’s zone-of-danger eligibility remained for consideration; judgment for Conrail was required in Carlisle. [P38](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/532/)

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Thomas wrote for Rehnquist, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter. Souter concurred separately. Ginsburg dissented, joined by Blackmun and Stevens. [P38](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/532/)

**Historical Holding:** FELA permits negligent-emotional-distress claims within the common-law zone of danger. Ordinary work-stress claims such as Carlisle’s fall outside that boundary; the Gottshall record required further consideration. [P38](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/532/)

**Historical Authority:** FELA; Buell, Urie, Rogers, and common-law injury limits; 512 U.S. 532-572. [P38](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/532/)

**Historical Sources:** 512 U.S. 535-541 for the separate records; 541-558 for the proposed common-law boundary and historical applications; 558-572 for separate writings. [P38](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/532/)

## 87. Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC

**Historical Decision Date:** 1994-06-27

Comparator only. Introduce after provisional non-Stone modeling has been frozen; not simulated precedent and not a proposed Court result.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded, 5–4 on the judgment, with differing joins by section. [R48](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/622/)

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Kennedy wrote Part I for all nine; Parts II-A and II-B for eight, excluding Stevens; Parts II-C, II-D, and III-A for Rehnquist, Blackmun, Stevens, and Souter; and Part III-B for Rehnquist, Blackmun, and Souter. **Historical Authority:** O’Brien, Ward, Tornillo, Red Lion, and cable/broadcast distinctions. No later merits resolution of the remand is imported into this comparator or the entering law. [R48](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/622/)

Comparator only. Introduce after provisional non-Stone modeling has been frozen; not simulated precedent and not a proposed Court result.

## 89. Williamson v. United States

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case Name:** Williamson v. United States

**Citation or Docket:** 512 U.S. 594 (1994); No. 93-5256

**Historical Event Date:** 1994-06-27

Comparator only. Introduce after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. This material is not simulated precedent and does not control the unapproved Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded, 9-0 in the judgment, with divided reasoning and application. [P40](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/594/)

**Historical Opinion Structure:** O’Connor wrote Parts I, II-A, and II-B for Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, and Ginsburg; only Scalia joined Part II-C. Scalia concurred separately. Ginsburg, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, and Souter, concurred in part and the judgment. Kennedy, joined by Rehnquist and Thomas, concurred in the judgment with a broader narrative approach. [P40](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/594/)

**Historical Holding:** A six-Justice majority required the particular statement to be self-inculpatory in context; a generally inculpatory narrative does not automatically admit its other portions. The Court did not decide the confrontation issue or a universal corroboration rule for inculpatory statements. [P40](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/594/)

**Historical Authority:** Rules 104 and 804; entering confrontation authorities discussed but not displaced; 512 U.S. 594-621. [P40](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/594/)

**Historical Sources:** 512 U.S. 596-599 for record; 599-604 for the majority rule; 604-621 for the different applications and reserved issues. [P40](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/594/)

## 90. McFarland v. Scott

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case Name:** McFarland v. Scott

**Citation or Docket:** 512 U.S. 849 (1994); No. 93-6497

**Historical Decision Date:** 1994-06-30

Comparator only. Introduce after provisional non-Stone modeling has been frozen; not simulated precedent and not a proposed Court result.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed; 6–3 on prepetition counsel, 5–4 on authority to stay before a formal petition. [R50](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/849/)

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Blackmun wrote for Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg. O’Connor agreed on counsel but dissented on the stay power. Thomas dissented with Rehnquist and Scalia. [R50](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/849/)

**Historical Authority:** The then-operative §848(q), §§2251 and 2283, Habeas Rules 2 and 4, and Barefoot. [R50](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/849/)

**Historical Decision Date:** 1994-06-30

Comparator only. Introduce after provisional non-Stone modeling has been frozen; not simulated precedent and not a proposed Court result.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 5–4 in the judgment. [R51](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/874/)

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Kennedy wrote for Rehnquist and, except Part II-B, O’Connor; O’Connor wrote separately. Thomas, with Scalia, concurred in the judgment on a broader rejection of dilution claims. Blackmun dissented with Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg; Ginsburg wrote separately, and Stevens wrote a separate opinion joined by those same three. **Historical Authority:** Gingles, Chisom, Growe, §2, and the distinct §5 precedents; fractured rationales must not be treated as a single majority statutory theory. [R51](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/874/)

## 92. Johnson v. De Grandy

**Case Name:** Johnson v. De Grandy

**Citation or Docket:** 512 U.S. 997 (1994); No. 92-519, consolidated with Nos. 92-593 and 92-767

**Historical Decision Date:** 1994-06-30

Comparator only. Introduce after provisional non-Stone modeling has been frozen; not simulated precedent and not a proposed Court result.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed in part and reversed in part, 7–2. [R52](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/997/)

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Souter wrote for Rehnquist, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, and Ginsburg, with Kennedy joining except Parts III-B-2, III-B-4, and IV. O’Connor concurred; Kennedy concurred in part and in the judgment. Thomas dissented with Scalia. [R52](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/997/)

**Historical Holding:** Preclusion defenses failed. **Historical Authority:** Gingles, §2’s totality and no-proportional-entitlement language, Allen, Marrese, Rooker, and Feldman. The contemporaneous Holder opinions were not a uniform majority abandonment of dilution doctrine. [R52](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/997/)

## 93. Madsen v. Women’s Health Center, Inc.

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case Name:** Madsen v. Women’s Health Center, Inc.

**Citation or Docket:** 512 U.S. 753 (1994); No. 93-880

**Historical Decision Date:** 1994-06-30

Comparator only. Introduce after provisional non-Stone modeling has been frozen; not simulated precedent and not a proposed Court result.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed in part and reversed in part, with a five-Justice principal opinion and differing positions on individual components rather than one uniform vote on every provision. [R53](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/753/)

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Rehnquist wrote for Blackmun, O’Connor, Souter, and Ginsburg; Stevens joined Parts I, II, III-E, and IV and wrote separately. Souter concurred. Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, concurred in the judgment in part and dissented in part. Stevens disputed the reach to several components outside the petition questions. [R53](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/753/)

**Historical Holding:** The Court adopted a more demanding injunction-specific tailoring formulation; sustained the public-access 36-foot zone and limited noise restrictions; invalidated the private-land extension, visible-images ban, 300-foot approach rule, and residential zone; and rejected the named parties’ concert challenge. The historical ruling reached more component-specific tailoring issues than the proposed Stone position. [R53](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/753/)

**Historical Authority:** Ward, Frisby, Carroll, Claiborne, Yamasaki, and Regal Knitwear. The special injunction formulation was announced in this case and must not be listed as predecision law. [R53](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/753/)

**Historical Sources:** [R53](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/753/); United States Reports citation and date above. Opinion alignments are historical only.

Comparator only. Introduce after provisional non-Stone modeling has been frozen; not simulated precedent and not a proposed Court result.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Both judgments affirmed, 8–1. [R55](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/967/)

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Kennedy wrote for Rehnquist, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, and Thomas. Scalia and Souter concurred separately. Stevens, joined by Ginsburg, concurred in the judgment. Blackmun dissented. [R55](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/967/)

[R55](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/967/)

**Historical Authority:** Gregg, Woodson, Lockett, Eddings, Zant, Lowenfield, Ramos, and capital-vagueness precedents. Separate challenges and judgments retain their own docket identity. [R55](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/967/)
