## Tax, law and disputed paths

The ordinary 4.225% statewide sales tax and matched use tax are unchallenged. Section 144.748 adds 1.5% to the interstate use-tax burden and incorporates the exemption for goods subject to Missouri sales tax under §144.615(2). Local sales taxes range from none in at least one county through the supplied 0.5–3.5% range in more than 1,000 subdivisions. The stipulated figures show an excess use burden in 53.5% of jurisdictions, while more than 93% of sales dollars occur in jurisdictions with higher sales taxes. A share of jurisdictions is not a share of transactions; no taxpayer-specific amount follows from either statistic.

Actual Oregon Waste requires an identified domestic burden, rough approximation without excess and substantially equivalent taxable events. The income/disposal mismatch there is not this case's defect: complementary sale and use events are undisputedly equivalent here. The question is the proper comparison for measuring the burden. Quill's separate nexus doctrines do not authorize a discriminatory rate. Fort Gratiot and Carbone preserve nondiscrimination despite substate lines, but do not themselves compute this tax.

**Statewide-equivalence path:** Missouri would affirm because aggregate interstate use taxes are less than total local sales taxes and local delegation explains the variations. Henneford, Gregg Dyeing and General American Tank Car permit examination of complementary burdens rather than isolated labels. The strongest objection is that a buyer in a low-tax locality pays more solely because the purchase is interstate, and other purchasers' heavier local burdens never fall on the competing local transaction. A state-level statistical balance cannot identify an equal domestic burden for that disadvantaged transaction. A lack of discriminatory purpose does not cure the unequal treatment. Delegating local sales rates remains lawful, but does not remove the State's responsibility for the discriminatory difference created by its own additional use tax.

**Local comparative-burden path:** Reverse the state court's statewide-average justification and remand. Compare the interstate use burden with the sales burden on the comparable purchase in the locality where the goods are used. To the extent the additional 1.5% exceeds that locality's corresponding sales tax, the claimed compensatory justification fails. Henneford allows genuine equality; Halliburton, Armco and Maryland v. Louisiana require the relevant actual counterpart; Boston Stock Exchange and the current substate-discrimination cases prevent favorable treatment elsewhere from extinguishing the injury. The strongest objection is administrative complexity and the fact that aggregate imports may receive favorable treatment. Neither changes the relevant comparison, and no judicial choice of Missouri's replacement tax system is required.

All eight commitments adopt the local-comparison path after considering the contested aggregate alternative. The conclusion does not invalidate every application of a use tax, automatically invalidate the whole statute or settle state-law severability. It leaves the ordinary 4.225% levies intact. The Court does not prescribe a replacement rate, force local tax increases, create an exemption absent from Missouri law or calculate relief for unproved purchases.

**Retrospective remedy fork:** Petitioners request refunds as well as prospective and statewide relief. McKesson requires meaningful backward-looking relief if the State compelled payment before an adequate opportunity to contest the discriminatory tax; legitimate remedial options may include refunds, lawful additional assessments or another adequate correction. Where meaningful predeprivation contest procedures existed, ordinary state remedial requirements may differ. The packet does not establish which premise obtains. Remand must determine the actual procedures and affected taxes before choosing relief; no automatic full or partial refund is awarded and no failure to use an unproved procedure is found.

## Individual commitments

| Justice | Commitment and individually supported boundary |
|---|---|
| Blackmun | Reverse the statewide-average judgment and remand on the local-comparison ground. His Chemical Waste join forbids an inadequately justified origin exaction. His Oregon Waste dissent's strongest contrary analogy treated a comprehensive environmental program and its contributions as the relevant whole; this ordinary complementary business-tax setting has identified equivalent transactions and a direct, geographically variable excess. No conservation-cost or public-service premise supports the statewide offset. He retains McKesson's prerequisites and does not order an assumed refund. |
| Stevens | Reverse and remand. His Kraft and Fort Gratiot opinions identify the properly comparable commerce and reject substate discrimination defenses; his Oregon Waste join requires actual compensation. Aggregate amounts cannot erase the difference paid by the relevant transaction. His rule does not require statewide invalidation or disturb the ordinary matched taxes. |
| O'Connor | Reverse and remand. Her Oregon Waste and Fort Gratiot joins govern express differential treatment. Her Carbone separate position distinguished a single local service arrangement from geographic discrimination; this tax directly discriminates when the comparable local purchase bears less. She joins conditional remedial instructions, not a predetermined refund or severability choice. |
| Scalia | Reverse and remand. His Kraft, Fort Gratiot and Oregon Waste joins support the identified domestic counterpart and equivalent-event requirements. His Quill position preserves a distinct precedent concerning remote collection, not an excess-rate defense. The aggregate argument fails for the relevant comparable transaction. No broader tax or nexus holding is joined. |
| Kennedy | Reverse and remand. His Oregon Waste join and Carbone opinion reject denial of discrimination merely because local arrangements or excluded local businesses complicate the picture. His Kraft join requires the appropriate comparison. He preserves valid complementary taxation while rejecting a surcharge above the actual local counterpart, and leaves fact-dependent remedies below. |
| Souter | Reverse and remand. His Oregon Waste, Kraft and Fort Gratiot joins require an actual nondiscriminatory comparison. His Carbone dissent's municipal-service distinction is the strongest contrary institutional analogy, but this record concerns an ordinary State-imposed tax difference, not a contracted service whose character is disputed. Statewide revenue totals do not eliminate the local inequality. |
| Thomas | Reverse and remand. His Oregon Waste opinion separates identification, equivalence of events and nonexcess compensation. Sale/use equivalence here resolves one component for Missouri but not the excess-burden component. His Fort Gratiot and Kraft joins foreclose treating a substate classification or favorable transactions elsewhere as sufficient. No universal ban on use taxes or immediate refund order. |
| Ginsburg | Reverse and remand. Her Oregon Waste and Carbone joins preserve nondiscrimination and exact remedial limits. The identified local sales levy is the actual counterpart; another locality's collections cannot satisfy it. The missing Missouri predeprivation procedures require a conditional McKesson remand rather than invented taxpayer relief. |

**Frozen result:** All eight Associates reverse the judgment sustaining the additional tax on a statewide-average justification and remand for locality-specific application and lawful relief. Each adopts the local-comparison proposition and the stated limits; the remedial reservation is common, not a separate vote against relief. No Stone vote, final Court count, author, chosen replacement tax or quantified refund is supplied. Historical reconciliation remains required.
