## Added entering-law authority

[Disclosure of known direct-negation evidence and supervisory dismissal](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#disclosure-of-known-direct-negation-evidence-and-supervisory-dismissal), present operation effective United States v. Williams, May 4, 1992, supplies two separate controlling components. A federal prosecutor must disclose personally known, substantial evidence directly negating the charged offense when omission would materially distort the grand-jury presentation; the duty requires no investigation for unknown favorable evidence and no presentation of every defense. The separate sufficient ground for supervisory dismissal without prejudice additionally requires evidence plainly precluding probable cause and a violation substantially influencing the indictment or leaving grave doubt that it was free of that influence. Plain preclusion of probable cause is not part of the disclosure trigger, and the identified dismissal class is sufficient rather than exclusive.

Costello's limits on ordinary evidentiary-competence or sufficiency challenges to an otherwise lawful presentation survive. The case leaves state-grand-jury duties, constitutional disclosure, immunity and fabrication claims unresolved. It does not transplant Brady into the grand jury. The underlying accounting evidence was outside the authority-only grant and was not found by the controlling writing to satisfy every condition.

The [Williams public render](../../OT1991/output/OT_1991CHUNK8.md#united-states-v-williams-no-90-1972) records Stevens, Blackmun, O'Connor and Thomas supporting the confined direct-negation duty and sufficient dismissal class; Scalia, Kennedy and Souter oppose creating this duty through supervisory authority. All must apply the controlling law regardless of those separate views. Ginsburg was not on that Court. These are public prior positions, not private audit material.

Medina's June 22, 1992 controlling propositions remain California-specific approval of the defendant's preponderance competence burden and equivalent presumption under an adequate hearing. Its principal opinion's categorical exclusion of Mathews from criminal process had four supporters and is not a controlling exclusive due-process method. No Albright formulation may borrow that unadopted exclusivity as law.

## Effect on the Albright paths

Williams defeats any categorical claim that judges have no authority to scrutinize materially misleading charging presentations. None of the frozen Albright rows may be rendered as that proposition. Williams also reinforces the distinction between a violated duty and its remedy: a prosecutor's obligation, dismissal in the criminal proceeding, a §1983 constitutional right, and personal damages liability are separate matters. Albright involves an Illinois information and preliminary hearing, not a federal grand-jury presentation, and presents a substantive-due-process damages theory. Extending Williams to that distinct constitutional cause would require a new legal step, rather than simple application of its controlling rule.

For Path B, the affirmative-abuse analogy is strengthened: knowingly distorting a charging presentation is different from an ordinary dispute about evidence sufficiency. Nevertheless, the Williams holding itself reserves the constitutional source and state setting. For Path A, the absence of an automatic §1983 substantive claim remains a permissible narrower position, but cannot be defended by universal charging immunity, a universal ban on judicial oversight, or a categorical exclusion of all due-process methods beyond a historical criminal-procedure test. The actual unpresented Fourth Amendment theory, independent procedural protections, and the personal defenses remain reserved.
