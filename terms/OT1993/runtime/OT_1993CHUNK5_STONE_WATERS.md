#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand the Seventh Circuit’s judgment, while leaving the grant of summary judgment for the officials set aside because actual motive remains materially disputed.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require further proceedings on what motivated the discharge and whether the speech relied upon was protected under Pickering and Connick. A reasonably formed and genuinely held account may be used, but a reasonable investigation does not establish that the employer actually relied on it. Leave qualified immunity to its proper event-date analysis; award neither damages nor reinstatement on this record. [R29](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/661/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Mistaken factual premises and retaliation

**Entering Law:** Pickering v. Board of Education, 391 U.S. 563 (1968), and Connick v. Myers, 461 U.S. 138 (1983), supply public-concern analysis and employer/employee balancing. Mt. Healthy City Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), supplies protected-speech causation and the same-decision defense. Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972), distinguishes a property-based procedural claim. These authorities do not conclusively resolve the effect of an employer’s erroneous account of speech. #### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain remand and the distinction between reasonable mistake and retaliatory pretext; expressly acknowledge the new constitutional factual-premise rule.

*Pickering* and *Connick* accommodate public employment and speech but do not conclusively decide whose account controls when the words are disputed. Stone can permit discipline on a genuinely held, objectively reasonable account formed through an inquiry appropriate to the circumstances, while preserving *Mt. Healthy* causation and the same-decision defense. [Record and competing formulations, 511 U.S. 661](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/661/).

This is **a meaningful doctrinal development**, not merely factfinding under an unchanged test. Its legal basis is the public-employer balance: requiring perfect historical accuracy risks making ordinary personnel decisions constitutionally perilous; allowing any asserted belief would let mistaken labels extinguish protected criticism. The inquiry safeguard must be part of justifying the speech-based action, not a freestanding employment-hearing entitlement under *Roth*.

Application should examine report reliability, known contradictions, access to obvious witnesses, opportunity to clarify when practicable, urgency, and the harm reasonably feared. These guide objective reasonableness rather than form a mandatory interview checklist. Repeated interviews of one interested accuser may be less reliable than one neutral firsthand account. Reasonably resolving conflicting reports differs from ignoring a readily available exculpatory witness because the manager dislikes the employee's views.

Separate two factual questions: was the account reasonably and genuinely believed, and did it actually motivate the firing? The interviews may support the former; evidence of hostility to patient-care criticism leaves the latter disputed. A good investigation cannot cleanse retaliation. Nor is management embarrassment automatically operational disruption under *Pickering*.

The strongest criticisms run both ways: reasonable mistake makes the innocent speaker bear error, while constitutional inquiry review invites judicial micromanagement. Stone's middle course has those costs and needs focused reasons, ordinary proof, and separate treatment of individual immunity. Remand without reinstating summary judgment, awarding relief, importing *Garcetti*, or applying his preferred replacement for immunity as if adopted.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Churchill, a public-hospital nurse, spoke with a prospective departmental transferee during a work break. Reports described disparagement of management, unwillingness to cooperate, and discouragement of transfer. Churchill and other witnesses described concerns about patient care and cross-training. Managers interviewed the reporting employees repeatedly; the final decisionmaker also heard Churchill during the grievance process. Evidence of earlier criticism and management hostility raises a separate dispute over the actual reason for discharge. Neither version of the conversation is an appellate finding of historical truth. [R29](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/661/)

The claim arises under §1983 and the First Amendment. An at-will employment relationship supplies no assumed property entitlement. The hospital-policy/custom argument was not included in the petition question; it must not become a new merits holding. Individual qualified immunity is distinct from the factual and constitutional speech inquiry. [R29](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/661/)
