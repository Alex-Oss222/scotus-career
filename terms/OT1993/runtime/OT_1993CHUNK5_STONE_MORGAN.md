#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Changed law versus revision of a completed federal judgment

**Entering Law:** Article III vests judicial power in the federal courts. United States v. Klein, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.) 128 (1872), limits legislative prescription of judicial results, while Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, 503 U.S. 429 (1992), permits Congress to change governing law in pending litigation. Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co., 59 U.S. (18 How.) 421 (1856), distinguishes prospective injunctive operation after a legal change. McCullough v. Virginia, 172 U.S. 102 (1898), addresses completed adjudications. Lampf, 501 U.S. 350 (1991), and James B. Beam, 501 U.S. 529 (1991), supply the limitations and judicial-retroactivity background. Chase Securities Corp. v. Donaldson, 325 U.S. 304 (1945), permits some revival of time-barred claims under due process; it does not by itself decide the Article III status of a final federal judgment. [P28](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/93-609.ZPC.html) [P28.1](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/997/39/382064/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain Stone's proposed Article III finality limit, candidly recognizing the issue is open at this date.

Section 27A(b) expressly directs reinstatement, so ordinary antiretroactivity construction cannot avoid the constitutional question. The judgment here became final and unappealed before enactment. Congress's power to change governing law in pending cases under simulated *Robertson* does not itself authorize revision of a completed federal judgment. [Case record, 511 U.S. 658](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/658/); [underlying Fifth Circuit decision, 997 F.2d 39](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/997/39/382064/).

This would be **a meaningful structural doctrinal development**, grounded in Article III's allocation of conclusive judicial adjudication, *Klein*, *McCullough*, and the distinction between completed judgments and prospective equitable operation illustrated by *Wheeling Bridge*. It cannot be presented as application of later *Plaut*.

The strongest counterargument is that Congress changed a generally applicable limitations rule, reviving claims rather than directing findings or revising an adjudicated fraud merits question. *Chase Securities* supports legislative revival against a due-process objection. Stone's response should be that a final limitations dismissal is still a judicial resolution; otherwise Congress could reopen any completed case by changing its governing rule. The structural limit does not depend on a defendant's moral entitlement to avoid a fraud claim.

Application turns on finality before enactment, availability of direct review, the character of the judicial disposition, and what reopening would alter. Pending appeals differ; continuing prospective injunctions differ; ordinary judicial reopening under valid procedures differs. Avoid turning the rule into a claim that Congress can never alter a legal regime affecting a judgment's future operation.

The cost is preserving dismissals generated by a sudden intervening limitations decision even when Congress sought to correct unfairness. Concrete finality and the constitutional allocation justify the line. Deny reopening in this matter without deciding §27A(a), underlying fraud, or general severability. The historical disposition's institutional circumstances do not supply a reason for Stone's own conclusion.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Pacific Mutual filed in March 1991 over 1987 securities transactions. Existing Fifth Circuit law supplied a four-year limitations period. Lampf and James B. Beam, both decided June 20, 1991, led to dismissal with prejudice in August 1991 under the federal one-year/three-year rule. Pacific Mutual did not appeal. Congress enacted §27A on December 19, 1991; subsection (b) required reinstatement, on a timely motion, of specified actions commenced before June 20 and dismissed after June 19 as time-barred if timely under the former law. Pacific Mutual moved on January 31, 1992. The posture is reopening a completed dismissal, not merely changing law during an unresolved appeal. Article III finality is the presented merits limit on congressional authority, not a reason to deny jurisdiction to review the reinstatement judgment. Section 27A’s temporal command is express, so an ordinary presumption against retroactivity does not resolve its constitutional validity. The underlying fraud allegations and the validity of every other provision of §27A are unnecessary to the question. O’Connor does not participate; no reason for that nonparticipation is supplied.
