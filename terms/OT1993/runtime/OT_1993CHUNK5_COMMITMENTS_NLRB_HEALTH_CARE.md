## Components and current-law limits

Section 2(11) requires authority, in the employer's interest, to hire, transfer, suspend, lay off, recall, promote, discharge, assign, reward or discipline employees; responsibly direct them; adjust their grievances; or effectively recommend such action. Exercising the relevant authority must involve independent judgment rather than merely routine or clerical action. Each condition does separate work. Section 2(3)'s supervisor exclusion and §2(12)'s professional definition must both remain meaningful. Neither professional status nor a nursing title establishes all the supervisor conditions. These employees are licensed practical nurses, classified as technical employees, whose duties were said to be virtually identical to registered nurses'; the model does not redescribe them as registered professionals.

The employer points to nurses' senior presence for about three quarters of operating time and their staffing, assignment, monitoring, evaluation, counseling and reporting functions. The ALJ found limited direction time, established assignments, aides' own assignment choices, administrators always on call for nonroutine matters, and the nurses' primary care duties. Neither set of facts can be silently omitted. The Board's operative employer-interest construction, not a new judicial independent-judgment finding, is under review. The Board did not separately present the latter as an alternative basis for sustaining its order. Boston & Maine permits recognition of an agency ground actually embodied in its decision; Chenery forbids an invented substitute.

Lechmere enforces the statutory limit settled there but does not decide this definition. ABF Freight protects delegated remedial choice within statutory limits, not an unrestricted power to define coverage. John Hancock and City of Chicago retain Chevron's inquiry into Congress's actual boundary before deference to a permissible agency choice. They do not erase deference in unresolved statutory settings. The 1974 committee history may bear on context but does not amend §2(11). Counsel's complete oral explanation denied that only nurses were covered by the idea, acknowledged that other professions had not been described in identical terms, and connected the approach to minor supervisory employees. The model does not turn the middle statement into a concession of an unprecedented nurse-only exception.

## Enhanced alternative paths

**Textual employer-interest path:** Affirm the refusal of enforcement. Patient care is the nursing home's business; direction serving it is not categorically outside the employer's interest merely because professional or technical norms inform the employee. Congress expressly included responsible direction and assignment, not only formal hiring and discharge authority. Professional coverage survives because the listed authority and independent-judgment conditions remain necessary. Packard's context and its subsequent legislative change prevent reading the phrase as preserving all professional-direction personnel. The strongest contrary argument is that an ordinary meaning of employer interest would treat nearly all professional collaboration as supervision and defeat §2(12). The response confines the ruling to the third condition; ordinary collaboration does not necessarily establish supervisory authority or the required independent judgment. Yeshiva's discussion of incidental professional direction does not supply a categorical rewriting of this statutory term. No separate independent-judgment remand is available merely to save a rejected agency ground the Board has not separately presented.

**Permissible contextual Board path:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit's legal rejection and remand consistently with the Board's permissible interpretation. Reading the supervisor exclusion alongside professional and technical coverage permits the Board to distinguish direction incident to delivering the employee's own skilled service from authority exercised as management's representative. Yeshiva's discussion of ordinary professional duties and patient-care direction, the Act's coverage provisions, and Curtin Matheson and Fall River's treatment of the Board's delegated interpretation support that boundary. The strongest objection is the plain factual truth that patient care advances the nursing home's business. The answer is that the disputed phrase locates the employee's supervisory role within the statutory distinction; it need not encompass every instruction that incidentally benefits the employer. This does not create universal nurse immunity: real personnel or managerial authority remains within the exclusion, and the Board must connect its classification to the actual duties.

**Remedy and narrower path:** On the first path, affirm rejection of the Board's order on the presented classification ground; no new universal nurse classification or constitutional labor holding follows. On the second, reverse the legal ground refusing enforcement and remand for implementation consistent with the upheld interpretation. The Board's order survives this supervisor objection, but no damages, backpay amount or new factual finding is invented. A third path rejecting the employer-interest construction yet sustaining the agency on independent judgment is not adopted: the required separately presented agency basis is not supplied. A different case with that basis preserved could present a different remand question.
