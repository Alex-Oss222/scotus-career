## Reconciled propositions

**All eight:** A seller need not act with the purpose of achieving a customer's drug use. Section857(a)(1) requires knowing use of interstate conveyance as part of a scheme to sell items the defendant knows are likely to be used with illegal drugs. Specific knowledge of the statutory legal label is unnecessary; customers' likely use can establish the case without identifying an actual eventual use by a particular buyer. The supplied instruction sufficiently conveyed the required knowledge. This is instruction adequacy, not a harmless-error ruling excusing omission. The statute is not vague as applied to this operation; the separate unrelated contentions remain unreviewed for the distinct preservation reasons.513–527.

**Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg:** The design branch concerns objective design, and the primary-intended-use branch generally concerns likely use by customers in the actual commercial circumstances. Display, instructions, promotion and other §857(e) factors can make a subset of ordinarily dual-use goods covered without treating every object in the class as covered.521n.11. Mere notice that an individual customer plans to use a box of paper clips as roach clips is not enough under that general-customer rule. The seller's private desire alone does not replace the classification inquiry. The five preserve all enumerated factors as permissive, the independent authorization exemption, and the full ordinary-lawful-course/traditional-tobacco-use exemption.

**Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas:** Join the common sufficient likely-use/knowledge ground and judgment, but refuse a rule making objective customers-in-general use the exclusive meaning of the intended-use branch. A seller's intended drug use can itself qualify an otherwise lawful article; instructions and manner of display can evidence it. This is a claimed **additional sufficient** basis, not a demand that every prosecution prove seller purpose.527–529. Scalia's historical footnote also suggests known buyer intent may qualify, but expressly says the seller-intent question is all needed there; no broader buyer-intent holding is adopted by this reconciliation. A separate writing may explain that textual disagreement without resolving every hypothetical sale. Legislative-history material is unnecessary to these three's rationale.
