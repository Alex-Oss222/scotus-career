#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Utah Supreme Court’s judgment on the reservation-boundary issue and remand for disposition of the state prosecution consistently with the absence of the asserted territorial jurisdiction.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The conviction cannot stand on the proposition that opening alone removed the location from Indian country. Remand for the state court to set aside the judgment resting on that jurisdictional premise. Do not adjudicate fee titles, immunize the conduct from another sovereign’s lawful prosecution, or create a new allocation of jurisdiction. [R09](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/399/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Reservation diminishment and criminal territory

**Entering Law:** 18 U.S.C. §1151 defines Indian country without making fee ownership dispositive. Solem v. Bartlett, 465 U.S. 463 (1984), requires congressional intent, gives statutory language primary weight, and considers contemporaneous history and subsequent treatment with their assigned, unequal significance. Seymour v. Superintendent, 368 U.S. 351 (1962), and Mattz v. Arnett, 412 U.S. 481 (1973), distinguish opening from disestablishment; DeCoteau, 420 U.S. 425 (1975), and Rosebud Sioux Tribe, 430 U.S. 584 (1977), illustrate sufficient cession evidence. Ambiguity is construed for retained Indian rights; no indispensable verbal formula or payment form has been established. [R09](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/399/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain Stone's proposed boundary ruling as a credible application of Solem, but strengthen the statutory counterargument.

Only Congress can diminish a reservation; opening land and changing ownership do not necessarily change Indian-country status. Stone's insistence on clear congressional intent fits *Solem*, *Seymour*, and *Mattz* and his source-based sovereignty commitment. Neither tribal membership alone nor subsequent demographics decides jurisdiction. [Statutory sequence and majority reasoning](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/92-6281.ZO.html); [contemporaneous dissent's competing reading](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/92-6281.ZD.html).

Nevertheless, the 1902 public-domain restoration language is substantial evidence of diminishment, not just ordinary opening language. The 1903–1904 references to that Act, the 1905 continuation of sale-proceeds arrangements, and absence of an express general repeal support continuity. Stone must explain why the 1905 opening mechanism and omission of restoration language leave the territorial intent insufficiently clear when the enactments are read together. Avoid asserting that 1905 repealed everything or that only cession-plus-fixed-payment language can diminish a reservation.

This is **a disputed application of the existing clear-intent framework**, not a required overruling. Stone can give later administrative practice less weight without deleting a factor *Solem* recognizes. If he would categorically exclude such evidence, that would be a doctrinal change requiring separate justification.

Lower courts should identify the relevant enactments, the particular tract, and whether Congress altered boundaries or merely disposition. A fee patent within retained boundaries does not itself eliminate Indian country under §1151. The difficult boundary is public-domain language carried forward indirectly. Acknowledge the ambiguity and resolve it through the governing Indian-law canon, not a free-floating presumption against States. Set aside the conviction resting on the rejected territorial premise; do not disturb titles or decide every sovereign's criminal authority.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Hagen, an Indian defendant, was prosecuted for distribution of a controlled substance in Myton, within the original reservation and on lands opened to settlement in 1905. The statutory sequence includes the May 27, 1902 Act, 32 Stat. 263, its public-domain language and allotment arrangements; the 1903 and 1904 extensions; and the March 3, 1905 Act, 33 Stat. 1069, prescribing opening under homestead and townsite laws and retaining the earlier scheme’s direction for sale proceeds. Congress did not enact an express general repeal of the 1902 Act. Whether these provisions changed reservation boundaries, rather than ownership and access, is disputed. [R09](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/399/)

The federal territorial issue was decided by the highest state court and is reviewable under 28 U.S.C. §1257. The separate collateral-estoppel theory arising from Ute Indian Tribe litigation was not in the petition and was expressly disavowed by Hagen; it is not an alternative basis supplied by this packet. No private-title adjudication is presented. [R09](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/399/)

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the agency damages judgment because no direct agency constitutional damages cause of action is available on this theory.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject the FTCA-exclusivity objection to this federal-only claim, recognize the statutory waiver, but direct judgment rejecting the implied agency cause. Leave the unreviewed individual-official disposition undisturbed. No decision that Meyer received constitutionally sufficient process is necessary. [R10](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/471/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Waiver and FTCA exclusivity

**Entering Law:** FSLIC’s former 12 U.S.C. §1725(c)(4) authorizes suit; the relevant successor structure is supplied by FIRREA. FHA v. Burr, 309 U.S. 242 (1940), and Loeffler v. Frank, 486 U.S. 549 (1988), broadly construe sue-and-be-sued clauses within their terms. FTCA jurisdiction, 28 U.S.C. §1346(b), requires liability analogous to that of a private person under state law; §2679(a) makes the FTCA exclusive for claims cognizable under §1346(b). [R10](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/471/)

##### Issue 2: Agency cause of action

**Entering Law:** Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 403 U.S. 388 (1971), Davis v. Passman, 442 U.S. 228 (1979), and Carlson v. Green, 446 U.S. 14 (1980), concern personal federal-officer remedies. Bush v. Lucas, 462 U.S. 367 (1983), and Schweiker v. Chilicky, 487 U.S. 412 (1988), address limits on implication. A waiver removes a defense to suit; it is not itself a substantive damages cause. [R10](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/471/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain rejection of agency constitutional damages while recognizing the particular statutory waiver.

The FTCA's private-person, state-law analogue excludes a purely federal constitutional cause from §1346(b); that exclusion cannot simultaneously make the claim exclusively an FTCA action. The broad FSLIC sue-and-be-sued clause therefore receives its own effect under *Burr* and *Loeffler*. Waiver, however, does not create substantive liability. [Record and statutory provisions, 510 U.S. 471](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/471/).

Stone's preference for an enacted public compensation defendant supports a legislative solution but does not supply one. *Bivens*, *Davis*, and *Carlson* concern officer liability; *Bush* and *Chilicky* make expansion a serious remedial choice. Direct agency liability would change the defendant, public fiscal exposure, and relationship to the enacted remedial system. Declining that **meaningful extension** is coherent with Master §§4 and 19 and Remedies. It should not be justified by later categorical restrictions on *Bivens* that did not yet exist.

The strongest counterargument is that *Carlson* treats damages as an effective constitutional safeguard, while officer immunity can leave an established injury uncompensated. Agency liability could improve compensation without erasing officer deterrence. Stone should concede those advantages; explain why they do not answer who authorized this distinct public obligation. Do not claim agency liability necessarily eliminates deterrence or that every remedial gap is constitutionally insignificant.

In application, courts must identify the operative waiver, the claim's source, and the authorized defendant separately. An enacted damages right with a matching waiver would differ. The individual official's immunity judgment neither creates agency liability nor establishes that process was adequate. Reverse the agency award without deciding the employment entitlement or promising recovery through unspecified alternative law.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** After a thrift was placed in FSLIC receivership, Meyer was discharged from his senior management employment. He claimed a property interest and lack of constitutionally adequate process. A jury returned damages against the agency, while the individual official, Pattullo, obtained qualified immunity. The latter judgment does not establish that an agency damages remedy exists. [R10](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/471/)

Resolve sovereign immunity and then the existence of a cause of action before the underlying due-process merits. A federal constitutional claim is not made a state-law tort merely by being pleaded as damages. The FSLIC’s statutory succession and the operative, subsequently repealed sue-and-be-sued provision must be used for this claim, rather than an invented universal federal waiver. [R10](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/471/)

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand for de novo qualified-immunity review considering all relevant preexisting authority.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The lower courts must apply the proper universe of law to the established or properly assumed facts. Leave ultimate liability and immunity open; no damages award, new factfinding, or mandatory merits-first sequence is imposed by this disposition. [R12](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/510/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Legal sources in qualified-immunity review

**Entering Law:** Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800 (1982), and Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635 (1987), govern clearly established rights and objective notice. Davis v. Scherer, 468 U.S. 183 (1984), requires the relevant federal right to be clearly established, not merely a violation of unrelated law. Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573 (1980), supplies the home-arrest baseline; Al-Azzawy bears on an officer-commanded exit. [R12](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/510/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain reversal without using this case to replace qualified immunity.

The preserved legal claim does not lose applicable precedent because counsel failed to cite it below. De novo legal review requires considering the law existing when the officers acted. Distinguish a new legal authority from an unpreserved claim or new factual record. This **narrow correction** fits Stone's own simulated *Hunter* opinion, which makes immunity a legal question under ordinary summary-judgment constraints. [Record, 510 U.S. 510](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/510/).

There is a genuine broader tension: the current Master would replace the ordinary clearly-established-law defense with a limited defense of reasonable reliance on identified authoritative law. *Hunter* still applied objective reasonable-officer immunity, and *Harlow/Anderson* remain entering law. Stone should acknowledge that his preferred replacement is a change from both the governing rule and the earlier framework he used. *Elder* presents the universe of legal authorities, not the continued validity of qualified immunity. Correcting the citation restriction does not enact that replacement.

On remand, consider *Payton* and *Al-Azzawy* against the supported facts of an officer-commanded exit from the home. The relevant distinction is coercive removal versus a genuinely voluntary appearance, together with the authority, exigency, and other facts the governing doctrine recognizes. An analogous circuit case is relevant, not an automatic victory.

The counterargument is unfair appellate surprise. Courts can allow supplemental briefing while retaining legal responsibility for the correct rule; they need not pretend controlling law is evidence forfeited at trial. Reserve ultimate immunity and liability, avoid finding disputed facts, and do not require a new merits-first sequence. This preserves Stone's broader proposal for a case that actually puts its costs and reliance consequences in issue.

**Egalitarian application:** Equal treatment requires applying relevant law even when counsel overlooked a citation, while giving officers an opportunity to address that authority and preserving supported factual defenses. A person's ability to retain especially capable counsel should not decide the content of governing law. That corrective principle does not itself abolish qualified immunity.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Officers surrounded Elder’s home and ordered him outside before arresting him without a warrant. In the civil action the district court granted qualified immunity. On appeal, the pertinent circuit authority included United States v. Al-Azzawy, 784 F.2d 890 (9th Cir. 1985), which had not been cited below. The challenged appellate rule treated precedent like a forfeitable evidentiary fact. [R12](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/510/)

The underlying Fourth Amendment theory was presented; the dispute concerns additional legal authority, not a new factual claim. Legal review is de novo, but what was clearly established remains keyed to the time of the officers’ conduct. This proceeding does not itself resolve disputed arrest facts or ultimate immunity. [R12](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/510/)
