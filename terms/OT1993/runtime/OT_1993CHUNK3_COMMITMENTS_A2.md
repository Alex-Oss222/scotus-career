## 26. Fogerty v. Fantasy, Inc.

### Reach and components

All eight reach the §505 question in the supplied merits posture. The questions are (1) plaintiff/defendant equality in the fee standard, (2) whether prevailing status makes recovery automatic, and (3) the disposition of the existing denial. The infringement defense verdict, the rescission and related counterclaims, and the correctness of the district court's no-bad-faith finding are not reopened. No fee amount is fixed. The independent positions do not perform a separate grant or silently admit a historical petition disposition.

### Common operative propositions available for joining

1. **Evenhanded discretion:** Under §505, a prevailing copyright defendant is eligible for a reasonable fee under the same discretionary standard as a prevailing copyright plaintiff. Bad faith or frivolousness may be relevant to an individual application but cannot be a special prerequisite imposed only on the defendant. The inquiry must implement the Copyright Act's purposes, including the public value of both meritorious enforcement and successful defenses defining the lawful boundary of protection.
2. **No automatic award:** Prevailing status makes a party eligible; it does not require the court to award fees. Section 505's permissive wording preserves judicial discretion and the requirement of reasonableness, subject to other express limitations in the title. An ordinary automatic prevailing-party rule would remove discretion the statute retains. Equality does not command equal amounts irrespective of success, conduct or circumstances.
3. **Remedy:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's affirmance of the §505 fee denial and remand for a fresh, evenhanded discretionary determination. Do not direct an award, declare the suit frivolous, find bad faith, reopen infringement, or alter the unrelated counterclaim judgments. The district court may again deny fees if its reasoned application of the correct standard supports that result.

This is not an exhaustive factor code. Frivolousness, motivation, objective factual or legal weakness, and a supported need for compensation or deterrence can be considered insofar as they serve the Act and are applied evenhandedly; no one factor is an indispensable prerequisite or controlling weight. Neither Dague's contingency rule for its two environmental statutes nor Farrar's civil-rights amount analysis is silently extended to decide a different §505 issue. This record does not reach federal-party liability, taxable-cost categories, §412 or any other independently limiting provision.

## 27. Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc.

### Reach and operative components

All eight reach the copyright question in the supplied merits posture. Fair use is an affirmative defense. The defendants seek summary judgment and retain the burden of establishing that defense on a record that permits judgment as a matter of law. An invalid legal presumption does not shift that burden to the owner or prove the defense. Unappealed state-claim preemption and the denied Lanham amendment remain outside review. The disputed release/request sequence remains unresolved and supports no finding of deceit or bad faith.

The following five component conclusions define the exact scope available for joining. They are independent modeling results, not quotations of a same-case Supreme Court report.

1. **Purpose and character:** Section 107 permits commercial parody to qualify as fair use. The court must consider what the new work does with the original, including whether it criticizes or comments on the original through a recognizable change in expression or message. Sale for profit remains relevant but cannot supply a conclusive rule or an across-the-board presumption of unfairness that displaces the work's actual purpose. A claim of parody is not enough: criticism of the original must reasonably be apparent from the work in context. Satire addressing other subjects lacks parody's particular justification for borrowing this original; that does not create a categorical statutory ban on satire or decide its distinct fair-use showing. Neither obtaining permission nor being refused permission decides fair use.
2. **Nature of the original:** The published song is creative expression within copyright's protection. That fact counts in the statutory inquiry; it does not foreclose parody, which ordinarily must refer to a recognizable expressive work. The unpublished-work amendment to §107 is current law but does no dispositive work here. No rule strips music of protection or treats publication as permission to copy.
3. **Amount and substantiality:** Assess both how much was taken and the importance of what was taken in relation to the original as a whole, in light of the parody's purpose. Taking a recognizable opening or core can be justified to identify the object of criticism; its importance alone does not end the inquiry. Further borrowing must have a reasonable relation to the critical purpose. The described lyrical departure and opening reference support a parody inquiry, but the recurring bass riff and the extent of musical borrowing require evaluation under that standard. Do not hold that every repetition is necessary, fix a quantitative allowance, or conclusively resolve a contested sampling allegation.
4. **Market effect:** Examine substitution for the original and for legitimate potential derivatives, including the effects of widespread comparable uses. Injury caused by effective criticism is different from usurping an expressive market. The owner cannot establish cognizable substitution merely by pointing to foregone permission for the critical parody itself. Conversely, a new critical work may still take material that substitutes for an ordinary licensed derivative; a reasonably supported nonparody rap-adaptation market remains relevant. Commercial sale alone does not establish the latter injury or make it presumed for every parody. The different-audience evidence concerning the original does not prove the absence of every derivative-market injury.
5. **Judgment and remedy:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit's adverse fair-use judgment and remand for application of the integrated statutory inquiry without its categorical commerciality and harm treatment. Do not automatically reinstate the defendants' summary judgment. The extent of justified musical borrowing and the unresolved derivative-market evidence prevent this record from establishing a final fair-use result here. The appellate court may resolve matters on an adequate record or permit needed further proceedings; no infringement, damages, royalty payment or injunction is directed.

The analysis weighs the four factors together and does not replace them with a transformation-only rule. Sony's time-shifting result, Harper & Row's result protecting the first-serialization market, and Feist's originality rule remain intact. Their reasoning is applied within their proper contexts. No First Amendment immunity from copyright, separate artistic-merit test, compulsory license or special permission requirement is adopted.
