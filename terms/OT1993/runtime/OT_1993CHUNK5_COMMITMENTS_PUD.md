## Statutory components and factual limits

The applicants concede construction-related dredged/fill and tailrace discharges, establishing the §401(a)(1) trigger for this project. That does not establish that every diversion is a discharge. The trigger covers a federally licensed activity that may result in a discharge into navigable waters. Certification comes from the originating State or appropriate interstate authority, or EPA where that authority lacks power, and addresses the applicable listed federal provisions. Where the described effluent/other limitations and standards are absent, the State so certifies, without that certification satisfying §511(c). All certification applications require public-notice procedures; hearings are provided only to the extent the authority deems appropriate. Failure or refusal to act within a reasonable period capped at one year waives certification. A denial instead prohibits issuance; no license issues until certification or lawful waiver. No present denial, waiver or timing breach is established.

Section 401(d) requires the certification to state effluent and other limitations and monitoring necessary to assure the applicant's compliance with its specified federal limitations and standards and any other appropriate State-law requirement stated in the certification. Lawful certification terms become federal-license conditions. Whether this necessary-compliance language extends beyond the triggering discharge is the disputed construction, not a concession entailed by the trigger.

Section 303(c) makes designated uses and criteria based on those uses components of state water-quality standards, subject to its review, submission and approval procedure. Its separate toxic-pollutant numerical-criteria requirement and biological-assessment alternative have their own conditions; no new toxic-criteria dispute is decided. Section 131.3(b) says criteria generally protect the designated use, not that meeting a numerical subset always satisfies every standard. Criteria may be narrative. Section 121.2(a)(3) speaks of reasonable assurance concerning the activity; subsection (a)(4) separately speaks of conditions with respect to its discharge. Both phrases must be faced.

Washington's Class AA standard protects the listed uses, including fish migration, rearing, spawning and harvesting, and also has numerical and narrative criteria. The State's existing-use protection and its distinct long-term-irreparable-harm provision are not merged. Federal §131.12 protects existing instream uses and the quality necessary for them. Its separate permission to lower higher-quality water requires the stated intergovernmental coordination, public participation and continuing planning, important economic/social-development finding, full existing-use protection and applicable point/nonpoint controls. Its outstanding-resource and thermal provisions remain separate; federal land or the Class AA label does not establish every outstanding-resource designation.

The later source completion establishes §131.10(g)(4)'s limited use-removal avenue: a designated use that is not an existing use may be removed or subdivided on the required infeasibility demonstration concerning a hydrologic modification and inability feasibly to restore or operate it to attain the use. Existing uses and attainable uses retain their separate protections and procedural requirements. This does not command every dam to achieve every possible use, and no use-removal predicate is found here. Section 131.11's scientifically supported protective criteria, most-sensitive-use rule and narrative/biomonitoring supplementation are preserved; its numerical “should” formulation is not rewritten as a universal “shall.”

The project diverts water around 1.2 miles. Natural seasonal flows are 149–738 cubic feet per second, proposed residual flows 65–155, and required residual flows 100–200. The state superior court and highest court treat the condition as protecting/preserving uses; the administrative appeals board's enhancement characterization is not the operative state judgment. This Court reviews federal authorization, not an optimal-flow calculation. The certification separately requires a water-right permit before construction; it does not confer one. Sections 101(g) and 510(2) preserve state allocation rights/jurisdiction, and §510(1) preserves stricter pollution control subject to its non-less-stringent qualification. FERC has issued no conflicting license.

## Enhanced alternatives

**Applicant/activity-compliance path:** Affirm. Once this conceded discharge triggers certification, §401(d) authorizes necessary conditions on the certified activity to secure compliance with applicable water-quality requirements, including the approved designated fish uses. The applicant language and other appropriate State-law requirements supply the reach; §303's use-and-criteria structure and EPA's activity-assurance regulation reinforce it. A minimum flow can preserve biological uses even absent an alleged numerical-criteria violation. The strongest objection is that §401(a) and §121.2(a)(4) repeatedly identify discharges, so one incidental discharge could become a general state project veto. The answer confines the conditions to necessary compliance with applicable water-quality requirements and treats the discharge as a real statutory trigger, not authorization for unrelated regulation. No general construction of every possible “other appropriate” law is necessary.

**Discharge-confined path:** Reverse the condition's federal authorization and remand. Read (d)'s certification language within (a)'s limited certification of the discharge; applicant wording identifies the regulated person without changing the subject matter. Section 121.2(a)(4) supports that relationship. The strongest contrary text is (d)'s reference to the applicant and other appropriate requirements plus (a)(3)'s activity assurance. On this path those terms enlarge types of lawful discharge conditions, not all effects of an entire hydroelectric project. The minimum residual flow addresses diversion's upstream reduction, not a shown water-quality violation caused by a triggering discharge. This construction preserves genuine discharge-linked certification while leaving other federal and state powers to their own law.

**Narrow discharge-linked alternative:** The United States argues this condition may survive even if conditioning power is discharge-linked because the withdrawal/return operation and protection of the receiving water are connected. The affirming commitments regard that argument as potentially supportive but do not adopt it as an independently sufficient holding: the applicant-compliance construction resolves the case without setting a universal nexus test. The reversing commitments require a concrete link to the discharge and do not accept the mere existence of construction and tailrace discharges as proof that a condition on residual flow satisfies it. Neither side may invent scientific findings or a discharge-caused impairment absent from the packet.

**Allocation and federal-license component:** The affirming path rejects a categorical separation of water quantity from water quality: maintaining an adopted aquatic use can require enough water for that use. It neither allocates ownership nor orders issuance of the separate water-right permit. First Iowa and California v. FERC concern the federal licensing scheme and conflicting independent state requirements; Congress expressly incorporated lawful §401 conditions, and no actual conflicting FERC condition exists. The reversing path treats that statutory licensing structure as a reason to construe §401 narrowly, not a finding of a concrete conflict already adjudicated by FERC. No path grants a federal license or directs construction.

Actual Arkansas concerns §402 and its particular standards, proof and review burdens, which are not transplanted. Health Care supports permissible interpretation within a scheme, not a mandatory rule for every agency; its contrary statutory reading remains a published dissent. City of Chicago and John Hancock require respecting actual textual bounds. Dalton is selection-specific preclusion and supplies no barrier to this state certification review.
