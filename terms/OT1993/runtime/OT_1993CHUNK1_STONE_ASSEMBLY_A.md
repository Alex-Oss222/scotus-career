#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit and remand for application of the proper hostile-environment standard.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the case for reconsideration of the existing record and such proceedings as the trial court lawfully requires. Do not direct a liability finding, constructive-discharge finding, or damages award.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Hostile-environment liability

**Entering Law:** Title VII, 42 U.S.C. §2000e-2(a)(1); Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson, 477 U.S. 57, 64–67 (1986), recognizes discrimination affecting terms or conditions of employment through sufficiently severe or pervasive harassment. The Sixth Circuit’s Rabidue formulation required serious psychological effects; other circuits disagreed. Meritor supplies the inherited national rule, not a mandatory injury element. [R01](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/17/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain reversal and remand.

The proposal genuinely implements *Meritor*, 477 U.S. 57, and Title VII's protection of employment conditions. Requiring serious psychological injury would make resilience defeat a statutory claim and confuse the existence of an abusive environment with a particular consequence. Stone's equality commitments support this application, but the statutory source—not his vulnerability roster—does the legal work. The simulated *Hicks* allocation of discrimination proof neither establishes liability nor changes the hostile-environment inquiry. [Case record and statutory analysis, 510 U.S. 17](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/17/).

This is **a clarification within an existing framework**, not replacement of it. Require objectively severe or pervasive sex-based harassment and the employee's actual experience of the workplace as abusive. Frequency, humiliation, threats, relative authority, repetition after complaint, and interference with work inform the combined judgment; neither treatment, lost productivity, nor a diagnosis is indispensable. The president's resumed degrading conduct after Harris complained makes the trial court's injury premise consequential. Remand is appropriate because the appellate court should not decide the ultimate factual application itself.

The boundary is offensive conduct that remains below the statutory threshold. Stone should acknowledge that rejecting an injury element leaves a contextual standard with imperfect predictability. Explain why conduct changes working conditions, rather than merely labeling it degrading. Assess context from the position of a reasonable employee subjected to the conduct; do not replace objective review with either the perpetrator's expectations or every complainant's subjective sensitivity. No new sex-specific reasonable-person test, constructive-discharge finding, or damages award is necessary. This protects substantive equality while respecting ordinary proof and remedial limits.

**User-directed case emphasis:** The user's specified emphasis is meaningful access to employment before clinically measurable injury. Severity, frequency, humiliation, work interference, and the objective and subjective components supply observable legal criteria. Employers retain protection against liability for merely unpleasant conditions; a resilient employee does not lose protection by avoiding a diagnosis.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Manager Teresa Harris experienced repeated sex-based insults and sexual innuendo by company president Charles Hardy from 1985 to 1987. She complained; Hardy apologized and promised to stop, then resumed. She quit in October 1987. The trial court found the conduct offensive to Harris and a reasonable woman, but treated serious effects on psychological well-being and injury as important prerequisites, calling the case close. No quid pro quo claim is presented. [R01](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/17/)

A final federal employment judgment is reviewable under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). The preserved question is the liability standard, not a fresh award of damages or appellate factfinding. Forklift concedes that serious psychological harm is not itself required, but disputes whether the error affected the judgment. [R01](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/17/)

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the reimbursement judgment and the district court’s ordinary equitable authority over reasonable amounts. Do not require approval of Trident as a public placement or order general financing of private education.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Parental placement and equitable reimbursement

**Entering Law:** IDEA, then 20 U.S.C. §§1401(a)(18), 1412, 1415(e)(2); Board of Education v. Rowley, 458 U.S. 176 (1982), governs appropriate public education. School Committee of Burlington v. Department of Education, 471 U.S. 359, 369–374 (1985), authorizes equitable reimbursement for an appropriate parental placement when the public offer violates the Act, while parents act at financial risk. [R02](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/7/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain affirmance.

*Burlington*, 471 U.S. 359, already locates reimbursement in IDEA's power to grant appropriate relief. Public-program approval and supervision requirements cannot automatically be transplanted into the remedy for a district's failure to provide an appropriate education. Otherwise the district's own unmet obligations would disable the alternative placement remedy. This is a persuasive **statutory clarification**, grounded in the distinction between public duties and parental corrective action, rather than an enlargement of constitutional disability rights. [Record and relevant provisions, 510 U.S. 7](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/7/).

Stone's concern with meaningful access aligns with the result; his restraint is equally important. The inadequacy of the public IEP and the appropriateness of Trident are settled for this review. Reopen neither finding. The simulated *Zobrest* antecedent-ground remand does not settle this question or authorize religious-school financing generally.

A lower court should ask whether the public offer violated IDEA, whether the parental placement appropriately addressed this child's educational needs, and what reimbursement equity permits. Actual progress is evidence of appropriateness, not a guarantee that any successful private school must be reimbursed. Cost, parental conduct, and a placement's relevant deficiencies remain available considerations under the applicable law. The hard case is a beneficial but unnecessarily expensive placement: eligibility survives absent state approval, while reasonable reimbursement can remain limited.

The district's strongest objection is that reimbursement circumvents state educational standards. Stone should answer that parents bear the risk of proving appropriateness and obtaining equitable relief; the remedy does not certify the school for public placements or eliminate safeguards relevant to the child's education. The proposal needs no new test beyond that distinction.

Trident lacked state approval and some requirements of the statutory public-education definition. As the case reaches this Court, the inadequacy of the public IEP and the otherwise appropriate character of Trident’s education are settled, not open factual questions. [R02](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/7/)

Review under §1254(1) concerns the categorical eligibility objection. The parents used IDEA’s administrative and judicial route. Neither a new finding of public-school inadequacy nor a constitutional funding question is necessary. [R02](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/7/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Party status and question scope

**Entering Law:** Section 1254(1); Automobile Workers v. Scofield, 382 U.S. 205 (1965), permits review of an intervention denial. Rule 14.1(a) limits review to stated questions and subsidiary questions fairly included. Yee v. Escondido, 503 U.S. 519, 534-538 (1992), distinguishes genuinely included issues from merely related questions and recognizes exceptional consideration of unpresented issues. The rule is prudential, unlike the statutory party requirement; jurisdictional objections must still be noticed. [P03](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/27/)

##### Issue 2: Vacatur after settlement

**Entering Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2106 supplies appellate power to vacate as just under the circumstances. United States v. Munsingwear, Inc., 340 U.S. 36 (1950), addresses mootness preventing review; Karcher v. May, 484 U.S. 72 (1987), distinguishes voluntary abandonment. The circuits disagree over routine vacatur following settlement. The equitable source permits attention to responsibility for mootness, third-party consequences, finality, and settlement, but no later U.S. Bancorp rule may be imported into this 1993 event. [P03](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/27/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain dismissal as improvidently granted, but make the antecedent-question explanation more exact.

Stone's Master §3 permits deciding necessary antecedents; that does not make every separate adverse order a question fairly included in the petition. Review of intervention requires examination of Izumi's party status and litigation relationship. Review of consensual vacatur asks what an appellate court should do at settling parties' request. The latter does not automatically preserve the former. *Scofield* supplies a route to challenge intervention; Rule 14.1 and simulated *Yee* require proper presentation. [Record and competing reasoning, 510 U.S. 27](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/27/).

The genuine counterargument is that intervention is logically indispensable, discussed in the petition, and fully briefed. Stone should acknowledge that Rule 14.1 is prudential, and an exception is legally possible. Dismissal is a judgment about orderly review and a fact-dependent omitted issue, not proof that the Court lacks all power to notice it. An antecedent legal premise within a preserved claim differs from securing review of a separate denial necessary to become entitled to press that claim. That distinction reconciles this disposition with Stone's willingness to decide necessary antecedents elsewhere.

There is legitimate **unsettled equitable room** to develop settlement-vacatur law under §2106, *Munsingwear*, and *Karcher*. Voluntary settlement, public investment in judgments, third-party effects, and responsibility for lost review are pertinent. But Stone should not announce such a rule after declining the only route to review here. Preserving the judgment below is not approval of its vacatur policy. Do not use the later *Bancorp* rule as entering law or imply that collateral financial injury alone gives a nonparty appellate standing.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Philips sued Izumi and Windmere over electric razors; Windmere asserted an antitrust counterclaim. After further proceedings, Windmere won an antitrust judgment exceeding $89 million and defeated unfair-competition claims. Philips and Windmere settled for $57 million and jointly sought vacatur; settlement was not conditioned on vacatur. Izumi indemnified distributors and sought to preserve the judgment’s possible collateral-estoppel value in related litigation involving Sears. The Federal Circuit found that financial support and collateral-estoppel interests did not make Izumi a party or justify intervention. The petition’s stated question addressed routine vacatur; its body discussed intervention, and the merits brief added an express intervention question. 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) permits a party to seek review. A rejected applicant may seek review of denial of intervention, but the scope of this petition and Supreme Court Rule 14.1(a) must be resolved before reviewing vacatur. Article III interest, appellate party status, and question preservation are separate. An antecedent issue is not automatically fairly included merely because deciding it would enable a broader merits ruling. [P03](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/27/)
