## 19. Northwest Airlines, Inc. v. County of Kent

### Comparator and alternative constructions

The official comparator, 510 U.S. 355, is an affirmance. Ginsburg wrote for Rehnquist, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Thomas dissented; Blackmun did not participate. Thomas would reject AHTA coverage and remand the independent Commerce Clause claim. These positions are evidence whose premises must be examined.

The original clean freeze omitted the substantial construction under which section 1513(a)'s enumerated prohibited levy bases delimit the Act and subsection (b)'s saving clause does not independently regulate every airport charge. The targeted clean review cured that omission and tested dismissal of the constitutional claim before the fee trial. Those corrections required reconsidering the initial rows. They do not themselves supply an in-world reason to depart from positions historically taken on the same statute, charge formulas and procedural record.

Both statutory paths are substantial. N-R gives effect to subsection (b)'s separately named, expressly qualified reasonable rental, landing and other airport-service charges from aircraft operators. Evansville supplies a suitable measure of that condition here without claiming Congress adopted every constitutional implication of Evansville. N-C instead treats subsection (a) as the operative prohibition and the negative saving clause as insufficient to create an independent prohibition. On that view, charges based on weight, occupied area and capital cost do not use the prohibited bases. Aloha's other-tax analysis supports N-C but does not itself decide the additional airport-fee language.

Actual Itel does not resolve this contest. Its treaty holding identifies the event on which a levy is imposed; that importation prohibition lacks the AHTA's specific reasonable-airport-fee language. Neither its method nor the charge formulas command N-C. N-R may treat these airport-user fees as covered subject to the express qualification without treating every expense passed through to travelers as a prohibited head charge.

## 24. Caspari v. Bohlen

### Record and substantive alternatives

Finality occurred January 2, 1986. The class-A robbery range was ten to thirty years or life regardless of status. Persistent-offender status changed sentencing authority and removed the ordinary constraint supplied by the jury's advisory sentence; it did not increase that range. Both sentencings produced three consecutive fifteen-year terms. The first judge imposed persistent-offender sentences despite total failure of the required prior-conviction proof. Appellate insufficiency, not an express first-judge acquittal or reversal for trial error, supplies the asserted acquittal equivalent; an invitation to supplement produced no proof. The State proved prior convictions at the second hearing.

The status procedure's protections are substantial: pleaded essential facts, proof beyond reasonable doubt, findings, confrontation, cross-examination and defense evidence. Bullington's trial-like capital adjudication, Rumsey's protection despite judicial legal error, and the insufficiency rule support the claim. The sentencer consequence is real despite an unchanged maximum. Neither identical sentences nor an unchanged range alone disposes of the claim.

The contrary Teague path survives functional comparison. Bullington and Rumsey resolved the State's entitlement to death after capital acquittal-like adjudications. DiFrancesco and the prefinality sentencing authorities left room to distinguish this noncapital decisionmaker determination. The issue is whether extension was required at finality, not whether the analogy is persuasive. Prefinality lower-court division corroborates legal openness only after substantive comparison; disagreement alone does not prove novelty. Denying collateral relief need not decide the constitutional merits or whether Missouri's exact procedure is sufficiently trial-like for eventual merits protection.

It does not eliminate dictated-at-finality analysis or the distinction between a capital punishment entitlement and noncapital sentencer classification. Actual Gilmore confirms that a noncapital extension may remain new after Graham. After this targeted review, Blackmun's narrower initial ground is the stronger inference: reach the fairly included preserved habeas limitation, find a genuinely open extension at 1986 finality, and leave prospective merits support unadjudicated.
