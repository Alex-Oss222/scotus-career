## Corrected enhanced comparison

**Affirmance path.** Section 10(c) gives the Board remedial authority to repair an unfair labor practice and effectuate the Act's public purposes. Its express for-cause bar does not make later hearing misconduct an automatic disqualification. On the actual record supplied, the Board accepted the adverse falsehood finding, distinguished that lie from the employer's actual reason for the retaliatory discharge, and gave its rationale for restoring employment and backpay following that violation. Those stated reasons permit judicial evaluation of the remedy. No conclusion depends on supposing that the Board separately debated, weighed or rejected a perjury sanction.

The order rationally repairs the established interference with protected activity. The known lie does not by itself make reinstatement or restoration of lost pay unrelated to that purpose. Phelps Dodge and Virginia Electric entrust that relationship primarily to the Board, subject to statutory limits and review for arbitrariness. The Court may regard the lie as serious without holding that the only permissible remedy is forfeiture. Precision Instrument's integrity concern supplies a reason the Board could consider withholding relief, but it does not establish that every administrative reinstatement decision must contain an express, separately labeled unclean-hands analysis. No reviewed statutory factor or established categorical rule requires that additional step here. Ordinary sanctions remain available on their own legal bases.

**Case-specific vacatur path.** A court could remand if the Board's actual reasons ignored a legally required consideration, materially misunderstood the misconduct, made a remedy unrelated to the Act's purposes, or otherwise failed to permit review of its remedial judgment. The absence of an express hearing-sanction discussion is relevant to that inquiry; it is not foreclosed by a preservation rule. The strongest case for remand is that accepting testimony known to be deliberately false while awarding equitable relief may undermine the adjudicatory process, so a discharge-only explanation may leave a material remedial objection unanswered.

The affirming answer is that this record supplies both awareness of the lie and an intelligible public remedial objective. The requested extra explanation would enforce a new mandatory treatment of hearing dishonesty rather than identify a defect in the statutory connection the Board actually gave. The lie's seriousness makes forfeiture permissible, not compulsory, and it does not establish unfitness for reinstatement or erase the employer's unlawful purpose. The Board's choice remains within the range justified by its stated labor-remedial rationale. This is a case-specific assessment, not a finding that an express sanction balance occurred, not approval of every unexplained agency order, and not categorical insulation of future misconduct decisions. No additional agency finding is supplied by inference.
