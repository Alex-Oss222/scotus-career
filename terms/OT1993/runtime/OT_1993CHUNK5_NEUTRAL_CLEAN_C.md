**Question Presented:** When a public employer disciplines an employee for reported speech, does the First Amendment inquiry use what was actually said, what the employer believed was said, or facts reasonably found after an appropriate inquiry?

**Question Granted / Review Scope:** The issue stated above is reconstructed from the primary record. A separately authenticated petition question and in-world grant order were not furnished; no broader question or verbatim grant wording is represented.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Seventh Circuit, 977 F.2d 1114 (1992), reversing summary judgment for hospital officials and requiring the factfinder to determine the actual speech. [R29](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/661/)

**Relief Requested:** The hospital officials seek reversal of the employee-favorable appellate judgment; Churchill seeks further proceedings on her speech-retaliation claim. [R29](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/661/)

**Material Record:** Churchill, a public-hospital nurse, spoke with a prospective departmental transferee during a work break. Reports described disparagement of management, unwillingness to cooperate, and discouragement of transfer. Churchill and other witnesses described concerns about patient care and cross-training. Managers interviewed the reporting employees repeatedly; the final decisionmaker also heard Churchill during the grievance process. Evidence of earlier criticism and management hostility raises a separate dispute over the actual reason for discharge. Neither version of the conversation is an appellate finding of historical truth. [R29](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/661/)

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The claim arises under §1983 and the First Amendment. An at-will employment relationship supplies no assumed property entitlement. The hospital-policy/custom argument was not included in the petition question; it must not become a new merits holding. Individual qualified immunity is distinct from the factual and constitutional speech inquiry. [R29](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/661/)

##### Issue 1: Mistaken factual premises and retaliation

**Controlling Simulated Law / Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the case-specific inherited-law rules and limits above to this issue. Other eligible authorities below remain effective only to the extent consistent with those simulated holdings and any actual earlier effective OT1993 ruling.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Pickering v. Board of Education, 391 U.S. 563 (1968), and Connick v. Myers, 461 U.S. 138 (1983), supply public-concern analysis and employer/employee balancing. Mt. Healthy City Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), supplies protected-speech causation and the same-decision defense. Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972), distinguishes a property-based procedural claim. These authorities do not conclusively resolve the effect of an employer’s erroneous account of speech. [R29](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/661/)

**Open Point:** Whether, and with what safeguards, a reasonable factual mistake can supply the speech premise without immunizing pretext. No later official-duties categorical rule governs.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The officials emphasize operational discipline and the impracticality of requiring a manager to predict a jury’s later credibility findings. The Solicitor General supports reversal. Their strongest account rests on actual reliance on the reported disruptive remarks, not a license to discharge for protected patient-care criticism. [R29](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/661/)

The Seventh Circuit focused on the speech actually uttered. Churchill’s Supreme Court argument accepts reasonable employer factfinding in principle but disputes the adequacy and good faith of this inquiry. She separately identifies evidence that the reports were a pretext for hostility to protected criticism. Reasonable investigation and actual retaliatory motive are different questions. [R29](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/661/)

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

An actual-speech-only rule supports retaining the appellate inquiry; a genuine-belief-only rule offers employers broader protection; an objectively reasonable and genuinely held belief rule rejects both extremes. Even under the latter two approaches, material evidence of pretext may prevent summary judgment. The Court can remand motive and immunity without resolving ungranted municipal policy questions. [R29](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/661/)

**Question Presented:** Whether Clean Water Act §401 permits a State to condition certification of a hydroelectric project on minimum stream flows needed to protect designated uses, when the condition is not tied solely to the particular discharges triggering certification.

**Question Granted / Review Scope:** The issue stated above is reconstructed from the primary record. A separately authenticated petition question and in-world grant order were not furnished; no broader question or verbatim grant wording is represented.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Washington Supreme Court, 121 Wash.2d 179, 849 P.2d 646 (1993), sustaining the State’s authority to impose the minimum-flow condition. [R30](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/700/)

**Relief Requested:** The project applicants seek reversal of the condition’s legal authorization; the Department seeks affirmance. [R30](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/700/)

**Material Record:** The proposed Elkhorn project would divert water around a 1.2-mile reach of the Dosewallips River. Washington classified the river as Class AA and protected salmonid uses through approved water-quality standards and an antidegradation policy. Applicants proposed residual flows of 65–155 cubic feet per second, seasonally; the certification required 100–200. The administrative appeals board characterized the condition as fishery enhancement, but the superior court found protection and preservation, and the state supreme court sustained the condition. FERC had not acted on the license application. [R30](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/700/)

#### INHERITED SIMULATED LAW

Arkansas v. Oklahoma recognizes permit conditions grounded in the relevant Clean Water Act authority and applicable water-quality standards. Its §402 treatment does not automatically decide the reach of state §401 conditions; assess the distinct activity/discharge language.

**[Downstream water-quality standards in federal discharge permitting](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#downstream-water-quality-standards-in-federal-discharge-permitting)** — Effective: *Arkansas v. Oklahoma*, February 26, 1992.

The Clean Water Act authorizes EPA under §402(a) and its valid regulations to require compliance with applicable downstream state water-quality standards; limits on the downstream State's own direct authority do not restrict that federal delegation. The Act does not categorically forbid a new discharge merely because effluent will reach already noncompliant waters irrespective of whether the discharge causes a detectable violation under applicable permit standards. For the federal interstate permit setting decided in *Arkansas*, federally approved downstream standards incorporated by valid regulation operate as federal permitting law, and EPA's reasonable, consistently held construction receives substantial deference. EPA permissibly construed Oklahoma's no-degradation requirement to prohibit an actual detectable or measurable violation, rather than every infinitesimal theoretical effect. This construction preserves enforceable standards and supplies no general tolerance for pollution labeled trivial.

**Unresolved boundary:** The Court does not hold that Congress requires identical compliance in every federal or state permitting setting, construe state law for unrelated purposes, or decide independent source-state remedies.

##### Issue 1: Certification scope and designated uses

**Controlling Simulated Law / Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the case-specific inherited-law rules and limits above to this issue. Other eligible authorities below remain effective only to the extent consistent with those simulated holdings and any actual earlier effective OT1993 ruling.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** 33 U.S.C. §§1341(a), 1341(d), 1313, and 1370 distinguish the discharge trigger from limitations assuring the applicant’s compliance. Section 303 water-quality standards include designated uses and criteria. Event-date 40 C.F.R. §§121.2(a)(3), 131.12 support activity compliance and antidegradation. Chevron, 467 U.S. 837 (1984), remains inherited interpretive law. [R30](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/700/)

**Open Point:** Whether §401(d) reaches project-wide flow conditions that enforce approved uses rather than only effluent restrictions on the triggering discharge.

##### Issue 2: Water allocation and federal licensing

**Controlling Simulated Law / Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the case-specific inherited-law rules and limits above to this issue. Other eligible authorities below remain effective only to the extent consistent with those simulated holdings and any actual earlier effective OT1993 ruling.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** CWA §§101(g), 510(2), 33 U.S.C. §§1251(g), 1370(2), preserve state water allocation. First Iowa Hydro-Electric Cooperative v. FPC, 328 U.S. 152 (1946), and California v. FERC, 495 U.S. 490 (1990), govern federal licensing conflicts; their operation must be reconciled with Congress’s express §401 certification requirement. [R30](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/700/)

**Open Point:** Whether those provisions remove the condition here, absent a conflicting FERC license and without adjudicating ownership of allocated water.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Applicants read §401(a)’s discharge language as limiting the whole certification power and stress FERC’s comprehensive role. They distinguish quantity from quality and argue that a flow condition effectively allocates water. They concede the certification trigger but not unlimited state conditioning. [R30](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/700/)

The State, supported by the Solicitor General, distinguishes the trigger from §401(d)’s reference to applicant compliance. Protecting designated fish uses can require water sufficient to sustain them. The state judgment supplies the state-law predicate; federal authority still limits conditions to the statutory water-quality function. [R30](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/700/)

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A discharge-only construction supports setting aside this condition; an applicant-compliance construction permits it when tied to approved water-quality uses. The Court can affirm the condition’s statutory authority without choosing an optimal flow, licensing the project, or deciding a future concrete FERC conflict. [R30](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/700/)

## 60. Nichols v. United States

### SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Nichols v. United States

**Citation or Docket:** 511 U.S. 738 (1994); No. 92-8556

**October Term:** 1993

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; preparation for decision on the questions under review. The public record supplies an actual Supreme Court review channel, not an invented additional grant. This packet does not itself enter a simulated order.

**Question Presented:** Whether an uncounseled misdemeanor conviction that validly resulted only in a fine may increase the imprisonment range for a later federal felony under the Sentencing Guidelines.

**Question Granted / Review Scope:** The issue stated above is reconstructed from the primary record. A separately authenticated petition question and in-world grant order were not furnished; no broader question or verbatim grant wording is represented.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit, 979 F.2d 402 (1992), affirming the enhanced sentence by limiting Baldasar to conversion of a subsequent misdemeanor into a felony. [R31](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/738/)

**Relief Requested:** Nichols seeks resentencing without the disputed criminal-history point; the United States seeks affirmance and alternatively challenges the ruling that counsel was not validly waived. [R31](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/738/)

**Material Record:** Nichols’s 1983 DUI conviction resulted in a $250 fine and no incarceration. The federal court found that it was uncounseled and that the record did not establish waiver. After his later federal drug plea, the DUI added a criminal-history point, changing the Guidelines range from 168–210 to 188–235 months. The court imposed 235 months. The statutory ceiling was life, so the disputed increase concerns the Guidelines range, not the statutory maximum. [R31](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/738/)

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Nichols challenges the later use of a conviction valid under Scott, not necessarily its original validity. The government preserves an alternative contention that the district court used the wrong burden or legal standard on waiver, invoking Johnson v. Zerbst and Parke v. Raley. That contention becomes material if the Court rejects the broad enhancement rule. The separate asserted duty to warn of future enhancement is not necessary if the Sixth Amendment ground suffices. [R31](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/738/)

#### INHERITED SIMULATED LAW

Parke concerns prior counseled pleas and regularity, not use of a valid uncounseled fine-only conviction to increase later imprisonment. Preserve Scott and Baldasar and the actual effective Custis rule within their distinct scopes.

**[Collateral regularity of prior counseled pleas used for enhancement](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#collateral-regularity-of-prior-counseled-pleas-used-for-enhancement)** — Effective: 1992-12-01; Parke v. Raley, for the components identified above.

For collateral challenges to final counseled convictions used for sentence enhancement, due process permits Kentucky's procedure requiring the Commonwealth to prove the judgment and the defendant to produce evidence of infringement or procedural irregularity. After rebuttal, Kentucky requires the Commonwealth affirmatively to establish constitutional validity. Missing old transcripts alone, without alleged official misconduct, do not rebut regularity. Due process does not require clear-and-convincing extrarecord proof of plea validity in that setting. This is permission for that allocation, not a universal collateral proof quantum; direct-review waiver requirements and the exclusion of invalid uncounseled convictions survive.

##### Issue 1: Counsel, valid fine convictions, and later imprisonment

**Controlling Simulated Law / Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the case-specific inherited-law rules and limits above to this issue. Other eligible authorities below remain effective only to the extent consistent with those simulated holdings and any actual earlier effective OT1993 ruling.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Scott v. Illinois, 440 U.S. 367 (1979), permits an uncounseled misdemeanor conviction when no imprisonment is imposed. Baldasar v. Illinois, 446 U.S. 222 (1980), barred the particular use of such a conviction to convert a later misdemeanor into an imprisonable felony, but produced no single majority rationale. Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 (1977), governs treatment of fractured reasoning. Argersinger, 407 U.S. 25 (1972); Burgett, 389 U.S. 109 (1967); and Tucker, 404 U.S. 443 (1972), frame counsel and sentencing safeguards. McMillan, 477 U.S. 79 (1986), recognizes different sentencing proof requirements. [R31](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/738/)

**Open Point:** Whether Baldasar should be applied, extended, limited, or overruled in the Guidelines setting, and whether the waiver determination survives review.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Nichols argues that the absence of a felony-conversion label cannot justify additional imprisonment resting automatically on an uncounseled adjudication. The reliance and liberty concerns underlying Baldasar apply to the added point. [R31](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/738/)

The government argues that the earlier conviction was valid, the later sentence punishes a new crime, and sentencing may consider conduct never reduced to a conviction. It invokes Baldasar’s fractured reasoning and preserves its waiver objection. Nichols’s answer must distinguish adjudicated conviction points from independently established conduct, not claim that all historical misconduct is inadmissible. [R31](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/738/)

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm by limiting or overruling Baldasar; or extend its protection to the Guidelines use and remand the preserved waiver issue and any necessary resentencing. The Court cannot simply assume a conclusively invalid waiver when the government’s legal objection was left unresolved. A rule concerning later use need not invalidate the fine conviction or establish a universal warning requirement. [R31](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/738/)
