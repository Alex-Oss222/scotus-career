#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment invalidating the challenged residential-sign prohibition.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve relief against enforcement of the prohibition on Gilleo’s residential expression. Leave the City free to adopt valid, appropriately tailored regulation of size, placement, safety, and other physical effects. Do not eliminate otherwise valid signs by deleting exemptions as a supposed cure. [R35](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/43/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Residential expression and adequate alternatives

**Entering Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments; Linmark Associates v. Willingboro, 431 U.S. 85 (1977); Metromedia v. San Diego, 453 U.S. 490 (1981), with its fractured reasoning; Members of City Council v. Taxpayers for Vincent, 466 U.S. 789 (1984); and Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989). Content-neutral regulation must satisfy the applicable tailoring and alternative-channel requirements; legitimate control of physical sign effects does not erase protected expression. [R35](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/43/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain invalidation of the residential prohibition under even the City's preferred content-neutral characterization.

This directly fits Stone's insistence that alternative speech channels be real in audience, price, location, and expressive effect. A sign at the speaker's home identifies both the speaker and a continuing relationship to the neighborhood. Newspaper purchases and off-premises canvassing need not be interchangeable substitutes. Linmark, Ward, and the distinction between privately owned residential property and public visual space in Vincent support the analysis without a new scrutiny tier.

The City nevertheless has genuine aesthetic and safety interests; the home is not a constitutional immunity from size, illumination, placement, or proliferation rules. Nor does every prohibited medium become indispensable merely because its users prefer it. The stronger basis is the breadth of the elimination, the inexpensive and distinctive opportunity lost, and the available regulation of physical effects. Discovery Network's attention to actual fit reinforces that reasoning. Stone should avoid converting Ward into a general least-restrictive-means rule: evidence of obvious, substantially less speech-burdening physical controls shows why this near-total prohibition lacks adequate fit, not why any imaginable alternative invalidates regulation.

The replacement ordinance is the operative law, so repeal does not moot Gilleo's own claim. Lower courts can examine the physical restriction, alternatives' realistic reach, and effects of exemptions without first resolving every content classification. Invalidating political signs while allowing comparable sale signs also weakens the asserted necessity, even if the Court reserves whether that difference independently triggers stricter review. Deleting all exceptions would expand suppression and would not answer the medium-elimination defect. Leave genuinely tailored controls open; an oversized illuminated installation and this small window message need not receive the same result.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** After Gilleo displayed antiwar signs and sought a variance, litigation produced a preliminary injunction. Ladue repealed its former ordinance and adopted a replacement expressly reaching window signs, with ten exemptions including property-sale and identification signs. Gilleo amended her complaint to challenge the replacement. Her small window message remained prohibited. Ladue asserted aesthetics, safety, and avoiding proliferation. [R35](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/43/)

The operative challenge is to the replacement ordinance, so repeal of the first does not eliminate the controversy. Gilleo asserts her own residential speech, not a general right to place signs on public property. The Court can test the comprehensive prohibition while assuming, without deciding, that the exemptions are content neutral. [R35](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/43/)

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand the Ninth Circuit’s federal-common-law ruling.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require consideration of California imputation, estoppel, duty, and other preserved state-law questions. Do not direct judgment for the law firm or declare the FDIC’s claims meritorious. Resolve FIRREA timing only if a later necessary issue actually turns on it. [R36](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/79/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: State causes and exceptional federal common law

**Entering Law:** Erie Railroad v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64 (1938); United States v. Kimbell Foods, 440 U.S. 715 (1979); Boyle v. United Technologies, 487 U.S. 500 (1988); and Wallis v. Pan American Petroleum, 384 U.S. 63 (1966), restrict special federal rules absent an identified federal interest and significant conflict. FIRREA, including 12 U.S.C. §1821(d)(2)(A)(i), defines succession to institutional rights and supplies specific federal exceptions. [R36](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/79/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain state-law treatment and remand; avoid suggesting that Erie abolishes all special federal rules.

Stone's remedial commitments cannot turn a federal receiver's wish for greater recovery into a federal malpractice cause of action. FIRREA's succession provision and specific exceptions fit the ordinary proposition that the receiver takes the institution's state-law rights subject to their legal incidents. Boyle, Wallis, and Kimbell Foods also require a more particular federal conflict than financial exposure of a federal fund.

The competing argument is practical: attributing fraudulent officers' knowledge to an innocent receiver can reduce recoveries for victims and impose national insurance costs according to divergent state rules. Stone can acknowledge that consequence while rejecting the proposed cure's lack of a limiting principle. Virtually every unsuccessful federal recovery could then justify a new defense rule. California's own adverse-interest and related doctrines may already distinguish fraud against the institution from fraud on its behalf. Those questions belong to the state cause and require adjudication, not presumptions favoring either lawyers or receiver.

A court should first identify the claim, an applicable federal statutory exception, and a concrete incompatibility with federal operation. Mere federal jurisdiction or federal-party status is insufficient. A separately demonstrated statutory conflict could produce another result; no categorical denial of federal common-law authority is needed. The pre-FIRREA framework supplies the same answer on the presented issue, permitting Stone to avoid unnecessary retroactivity resolution without using FIRREA as retroactively controlling law. This is consistent with the simulated United States v. Texas, which preserved an established federal interest rule in its own setting, and with Meyer’s separation of jurisdiction, waiver, and cause of action. Remand the state defenses and professional-duty questions rather than direct judgment for the firm.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** The institution’s controlling officers engaged in financial fraud. The law firm represented it in two real-estate syndications and is accused of professional failures in that work. The receiver refunded investors and sued on California tort theories. The firm asserted absence of duty, imputation of the officers’ knowledge, and estoppel. The district court gave no detailed explanation for summary judgment. FSLIC became conservator in 1986 and receiver in 1988; FIRREA later substituted FDIC. The imputation issue is presented on stipulated or uncontroverted facts, not a completed malpractice trial. [R36](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/79/)

The causes of action are state-created, notwithstanding federal receiver status and federal jurisdiction. The receiver does not sue here on a new federal statutory tort. The availability of adverse-interest or other state-law exceptions and the firm’s actual liability must remain open for remand. FIRREA’s temporal reach need not be decided if the pre-FIRREA common-law analysis yields the same result. [R36](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/79/)

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand the judgment sustaining the reprimand as to both CPA and CFP designations.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Set aside the censure based on this record. Leave the Board authority to regulate actual deception and impose properly justified, proportionate disclosures in a case that supports them. Do not withdraw its licensing jurisdiction or approve every form of credential advertising. [R38](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/136/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Truthful professional credentials

**Entering Law:** Central Hudson, 447 U.S. 557 (1980); In re R.M.J., 455 U.S. 191 (1982); Zauderer, 471 U.S. 626 (1985); Peel v. Attorney Registration and Disciplinary Commission, 496 U.S. 91 (1990); and Edenfield v. Fane, 507 U.S. 761 (1993), distinguish misleading speech from truthful commercial information and require a supported interest and appropriate fit. Potential misunderstanding does not automatically establish inherent deception. [R38](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/136/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain reversal of the reprimand, distinguishing false credentials from justified explanatory disclosures.

The proposal follows Stone's commercial-speech commitments and his acceptance of record-sensitive review in simulated Edenfield and Discovery Network. An active CPA credential does not become false because the speaker contests the Board's legal authority. Authorized use of a real private CFP certification likewise is not inherently deceptive simply because a consumer might confuse its source with governmental certification.

Peel's divided reasoning should not be treated as a unanimous rule against regulation of certification claims. Central Hudson, R.M.J., and Zauderer still allow restrictions on actually or inherently misleading representations and proportionate factual disclosures addressing a supported risk. The government's required showing is part of that received framework, not Stone's creation of a universal heightened evidentiary burden. Consumer-protection judgments may draw on experience and reasonable inferences; the defect here is inadequate support and fit for the particular reprimand and demanded explanation.

In application, verify whether the license is active, who conferred the specialty credential, what expertise it certifies, and what the actual advertisement communicates. An expired credential, invented certifier, or express claim of state endorsement presents a different case. A short source-identification disclosure may be supportable where a sprawling disclaimer effectively prevents use of the credential. The difficulty is distinguishing a plausible inference of misunderstanding from ungrounded speculation; courts should identify the asserted deception and explain why the proposed disclosure addresses it. Set aside this sanction without denying the conceded licensing jurisdiction or conferring permanent immunity on every future use of the same initials.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Ibanez was a Florida lawyer, held an active Florida CPA license, and was authorized by a private certifying body to use CFP. She placed the credentials on stationery, business cards, and a directory listing. The Board treated the CPA use as misleading about regulatory submission and the CFP use as misleading about state certification or inadequately explained. The credentials themselves were not shown false or revoked. The Board’s evidence of deception and justification for its prescribed disclaimer was limited. [R38](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/136/)

Ibanez no longer contests the Board’s jurisdiction over her. That concession is not a concession that truthful speech may be sanctioned. CPA and CFP rest on different sources of certification and require distinct analysis. The case concerns this reprimand and record, not every disclaimer or professional advertising rule. [R38](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/136/)
