##### Issue 1: State forum procedure and maritime uniformity

**Controlling Simulated Law / Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the case-specific inherited-law rules and limits above to this issue. Other eligible authorities below remain effective only to the extent consistent with those simulated holdings and any actual earlier effective OT1993 ruling.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Southern Pacific Co. v. Jensen, 244 U.S. 205 (1917), restricts state rules that materially prejudice characteristic features of general maritime law or interfere with its proper harmony and uniformity. It does not automatically displace every state procedure. Gulf Oil Corp. v. Gilbert, 330 U.S. 501 (1947), and Piper Aircraft Co. v. Reyno, 454 U.S. 235 (1981), describe federal forum non conveniens, including an available adequate alternative forum and private/public convenience considerations, ordinarily reviewed for abuse of discretion. Missouri ex rel. Southern R.R. v. Mayfield, 340 U.S. 1 (1950), and Bainbridge v. Merchants & Miners Transportation Co., 287 U.S. 278 (1932), recognize state procedural authority in federal maritime-related litigation. The applicable Jones Act and §1333 supply the source framework. [P13](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/443/)

**Open Point:** Whether optional forum dismissal is a characteristic maritime feature or sufficiently affects substantive maritime uniformity to preempt Louisiana’s contrary rule.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The employer argues that forum shopping and divergent retention rules undermine the national maritime system. Miller answers that the saving clause contemplates competent state forums using their ordinary procedural rules and that no uniform substantive maritime entitlement to dismissal exists. The SG supports Miller. Louisiana’s choice does not require a finding that Louisiana is the most convenient forum, and federal inconvenience does not itself show an unconstitutional or preempted state rule. [P13](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/443/)

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm state retention if the rule is procedural and does not impair the protected maritime features; reverse if a genuinely uniform maritime entitlement governs; or reserve independent jurisdictional objections for their proper posture. Retention is not a judgment for Miller on negligence, unseaworthiness, wages, maintenance, or cure. [P13](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/443/)
