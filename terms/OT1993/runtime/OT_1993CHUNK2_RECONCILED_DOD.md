## Operative rule, qualifications and remedy

The labor statute's section7114(b)(4) duty applies only to the extent disclosure is not prohibited by law. For these covered personal records, the Privacy Act supplies a prohibition unless written request or prior written consent of the subject, or an applicable enumerated exception, permits disclosure. The invoked section552a(b)(2) exception turns on disclosure required by FOIA; it does not import every labor-law public interest into FOIA's privacy balance.

On this record the home-address privacy interest outweighs the absent or negligible demonstrated public-understanding contribution under Exemption6's own standard. FOIA therefore does not require release. **Exemption6 permits withholding; the Privacy Act supplies the operative prohibition** on compelling this release through the asserted exception. The conclusion does not decide section552a(b)(3) routine use or written consent, does not establish that the unions can never obtain the addresses, and does not govern every other employment record.

The Fifth Circuit judgment enforcing both orders is reversed. No independent inquiry into conceded necessity, employee motives or actual harassment is ordered; no damages or constitutional privacy remedy issues. Ordinary lower-court implementation does not command adjudication or approval of an unpresented routine-use notice. No route to release under a different exception is foreclosed by this holding alone. Historical general language about prohibition must be qualified accordingly, as Souter requires.
