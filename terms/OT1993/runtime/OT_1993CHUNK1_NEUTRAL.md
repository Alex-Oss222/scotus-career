#### INHERITED SIMULATED LAW

Zatko, Martin and Demos govern the Rule 39 inquiry. Determine frivolousness or malice separately for each current petition; prior denials alone cannot establish it. Martin and Demos declined prospective restrictions on their records and did not enact a categorical ban or a universal warning prerequisite. **[Zatko v. California, 502 U.S. 16 (1991) — Whether extreme repetitive frivolous filing permits denial of pending Rule 39.8 fee-waiver motions](inherited/HOLDINGS.md#whether-extreme-repetitive-frivolous-filing-permits-denial-of-pending-rule-398-fee-waiver-motions)** — Effective: November 4, 1991.

When the Court is satisfied that pending submissions are frivolous and the applicants' repeated frivolous filings constitute extreme abuse of its process, Rule 39.8 permits denial of in-forma-pauperis status, meaning permission to file without paying fees. Denial may require payment of the docketing fee and compliance with ordinary petition form rules by a stated deadline; it does not decide petition merits or impose a broader prospective filing ban.

**Unresolved boundary:** The ruling is discretionary. It adopts no numerical threshold, does not make earlier denials alone conclusive, and decides no underlying claim or constitutional entitlement to unrestricted free filing.

**[Martin v. District of Columbia Court of Appeals and Martin v. McDermott, 506 U.S. 1 (1992) — No. 92-5584: an arguable current petition is not defeated by filing history](inherited/HOLDINGS.md#no-92-5584-an-arguable-current-petition-is-not-defeated-by-filing-history)** — Effective: November 2, 1992.

Rule 39.8 permits denial of leave to file without prepayment only upon a finding that the particular petition is frivolous or malicious; earlier unsuccessful filings cannot substitute for that finding. No. 92-5584's distinct general-rule and prospective statutory arguments clear the nonfrivolousness threshold on this record, so the Court grants leave without deciding that the arguments warrant certiorari or would succeed on the merits.

**[Martin v. District of Columbia Court of Appeals and Martin v. McDermott, 506 U.S. 1 (1992) — No prospective filing restriction on this record](inherited/HOLDINGS.md#no-prospective-filing-restriction-on-this-record)** — Effective: November 2, 1992.

This case-specific refusal decides neither that a prior warning is invariably required nor that prospective restrictions are categorically unlawful, and imposes no condition on future civil or criminal filings.

The two present defects in the companion operate together; neither professional affiliation, filing volume nor a state characterization alone establishes frivolousness. No universal warning prerequisite or categorical prohibition of prospective restrictions is adopted.

**[Demos v. Storrie, 507 U.S. 290 (1993) — The official-interference allegation is not defeated by filing history](inherited/HOLDINGS.md#the-official-interference-allegation-is-not-defeated-by-filing-history)** — Effective: March 8, 1993.
