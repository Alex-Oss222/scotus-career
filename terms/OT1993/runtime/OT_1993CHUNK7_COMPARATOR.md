## 73. Simmons v. South Carolina

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case Name:** Simmons v. South Carolina

**Citation or Docket:** 512 U.S. 154 (1994); No. 92-9059

**Historical Decision Date:** 1994-06-17

Comparator only. Introduce after provisional non-Stone modeling has been frozen; not simulated precedent and not a proposed Court result.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 7–2. [R40](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/154/)

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Blackmun announced the judgment in an opinion joined by Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg. Souter, joined by Stevens, and Ginsburg separately concurred. O’Connor, joined by Rehnquist and Kennedy, concurred in the judgment on the State-raised-danger and life-without-parole ground. Scalia dissented, joined by Thomas. [R40](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/154/)

**Historical Holding:** Due process required an opportunity to inform the jury of parole ineligibility when the prosecution invoked future dangerousness and life without parole was the alternative. The ordinary-meaning instruction was inadequate. [R40](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/154/)

**Historical Authority:** Gardner, Skipper, Ramos, and South Carolina’s actual sentencing alternatives; the plurality and narrower concurrence must remain distinct. [R40](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/154/)

**Historical Sources:** [R40](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/154/); United States Reports citation and date above. Opinion alignments are historical only.

## 80. Dolan v. City of Tigard

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case Name:** Dolan v. City of Tigard

**Citation or Docket:** 512 U.S. 374 (1994); No. 93-518

**Historical Decision Date:** 1994-06-24

Comparator only. Introduce after provisional non-Stone modeling has been frozen; not simulated precedent and not a proposed Court result.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 5–4. [R43](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/374/)

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Rehnquist wrote for O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas. Stevens dissented with Blackmun and Ginsburg; Souter dissented separately. [R43](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/374/)

**Historical Holding:** After finding essential nexus, the Court required rough proportionality, with an individualized governmental determination that the dedication relates in nature and extent to development impacts; no exact mathematical calculation is necessary. **Historical Authority:** Nollan and the unconstitutional-conditions application of the Takings Clause; the nature-and-extent requirement was articulated in this decision, not inherited from a simulated predecessor. [R43](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/374/)

## 82. Heck v. Humphrey

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case Name:** Heck v. Humphrey

**Citation or Docket:** 512 U.S. 477 (1994); No. 93-6188

**Historical Decision Date:** 1994-06-24

Comparator only. Introduce after provisional non-Stone modeling has been frozen; not simulated precedent and not a proposed Court result.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 9–0 in the judgment. [R45](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/477/)

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Scalia wrote for Rehnquist, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg; Thomas concurred. Souter, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, and O’Connor, concurred in the judgment with a narrower account of the habeas overlap. [R45](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/477/)

**Historical Holding:** The majority required favorable invalidation before §1983 damages for unconstitutional conviction or imprisonment, or other conduct whose unlawfulness would necessarily invalidate the conviction or sentence; it rejected exhaustion as the correct characterization and deferred accrual. The concurrence disputed extending the limitation outside the habeas overlap. [R45](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/477/)

**Historical Authority:** Preiser, Wolff, §1983, §2254, and the malicious-prosecution analogy; the favorable-invalidation formulation is the historical comparator’s rule, not preexisting simulated law. [R45](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/477/)

**Historical Sources:** [R45](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/477/); United States Reports citation and date above. Opinion alignments are historical only.
