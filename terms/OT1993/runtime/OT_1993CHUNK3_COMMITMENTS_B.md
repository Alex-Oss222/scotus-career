## 29. Victor v. Nebraska / Sandoval v. California

**Event:** Merits review, March 22, 1994; argument January 18, 1994. Dockets 92-8894 and 92-9049 remain distinct. Research cutoff immediately before March 22.

**Reachability:** All eight reach the instruction question in both dockets. Nebraska decided Victor's instruction claim on its merits notwithstanding his earlier omissions; other barred postconviction issues do not bar this claim. Sandoval is on direct review. Neither case is a federal habeas proceeding and neither supplies an occasion to apply Teague mechanically as a jurisdictional bar. No opinion reaches unrelated guilt, sentencing, ineffectiveness or sufficiency claims.

### Governing ground and alternatives

Winship requires proof beyond reasonable doubt of every element. Actual simulated Estelle requires a reasonable likelihood, judged from the whole charge and trial, that jurors applied an ambiguous instruction to relieve the prosecution of that burden. Boyde rejects a merely possible adverse reading without requiring proof that the impermissible reading was more likely than not. Holland permits whole-charge review without one mandatory verbal formula. Cage invalidates a charge whose combined descriptions lower the burden; it is not a list of prohibited words detached from context. Actual Sullivan, unanimously joined by the seven returning non-Stone Justices, requires reversal without harmless-error substitution **if** the entire proof standard is constitutionally vitiated. Ginsburg accepts that controlling law. Strength of guilt evidence supplies no alternative affirmance after that trigger.

**Affirmance path:** Sandoval's abiding-conviction requirement expressly follows consideration of all evidence; other instructions confine factfinding to evidence and exclude prejudice and sentiment. The moral-evidence phrase distinguishes human proof from mathematical demonstration and does not invite conviction from moral disapproval. Victor additionally states that a prudent person would hesitate in a grave transaction, confines the conclusion to full and impartial consideration of evidence, preserves the prosecution's unshifting burden, and permits strong probabilities **only when** they exclude every reasonable doubt. Its contrast between actual/substantial doubt and imagination can refer to real existence rather than a heightened quantum. The strongest objection is that ordinary jurors may understand moral certainty and substantial doubt differently from lawyers. That concern is substantial, but the affirmance path finds no reasonable likelihood of the forbidden reading after the express qualifications are retained.

**Reversal path:** Victor's definition affirmatively describes the requisite doubt as substantial, combines it with strong probabilities and possible mistake, and may communicate that a real yet less substantial doubt is insufficient. Cage shows that repeating the proper burden elsewhere does not invariably cure conflicting explanations. The strongest answer is Victor's express contrast with fanciful doubt and its hesitation/abiding-conviction language. The reversal path concludes that these do not remove the reasonable likelihood created by the interacting formulations; Sullivan then forecloses harmlessness. Sandoval lacks the substantial-doubt and strong-probabilities language and therefore does not necessarily follow Victor. No Justice who reverses Victor is thereby committed to reversing Sandoval.

## 30. United States v. Granderson

**Event:** Merits review, March 22, 1994; argument January 10, 1994; No. 92-1662. Research cutoff immediately before March 22. All eight reach the legal sentencing issue; the possession finding and mandatory revocation are undisturbed. Twenty months' prison time, one-third of sixty months' probation, is the Government's requested restoration. The undisputed initial Guidelines range was zero to six months and the eleven months already served exceed that ceiling. The offense statute and Guidelines ceiling must not be conflated.

### Separate statutory components and alternative paths

**Range-based path:** The drug proviso requires revocation and a custodial sanction, but the ordinary revocation scheme identifies the previously available imprisonment range as the relevant prison measure. Its upper endpoint supplies a two-month mandatory minimum here, and the same initially available range supplies a six-month maximum. The twenty-month floor proposed by the Government converts unlike sanctions without express conversion language; §3583(g), in contrast, expressly orders imprisonment measured by the supervised-release term. Section 3565's “notwithstanding” clause supplies mandatory revocation and a minimum but does not clearly authorize a new maximum detached from initially available imprisonment. The strongest objection is that “original sentence” ordinarily means the sentence actually imposed and the selected maximum was never pronounced as this defendant's sentence. Reading the surrounding scheme narrows that uncertainty; actual Ratzlaf's residual-lenity principle, Bass and Bifulco resolve the remaining penal uncertainty against the larger prison range. Actual R. L. C. supports recognizing legally binding Guidelines limits, but does not itself determine this proviso's denominator.

**Actual-probation-term path:** Sections 3551 and 3561 expressly classify probation as a sentence; “original sentence” naturally identifies the sixty months actually imposed. Mandatory revocation prevents ordinary continuation of probation; “notwithstanding” permits the new floor to exceed the initially applicable Guidelines maximum. The corresponding supervised-release enactment confirms a policy of mandatory custody for possession. The strongest objections are the absent express conversion language, the unlike character of probation and imprisonment, and the statutory asymmetry between drug and firearm violations. This path treats those as policy consequences of the enacted distinction rather than residual ambiguity; Chapman permits Congress to adopt severe or uneven punishment without a court rewriting text for proportionality. It supports restoring this twenty-month term without deciding every conceivable maximum or a constitutional proportionality claim that is not presented.

**Narrower prison-authority path:** The Government cannot obtain twenty months' imprisonment from a proviso that does not expressly convert probation into prison. Ordinary revocation authorizes no more than initially available custody here. That establishes the six-month ceiling and release without choosing a mandatory two-month floor or deciding whether the proviso permits a new probation sentence. The strongest objection is that the Court should provide a complete construction and that revocation makes further probation an implausible reading. The narrower path need not resolve that question to deny the Government's request; Granderson has already served more than every custodial term available under its reading. The alternative-to-imprisonment theory was not preserved below, so this path supplies no affirmative order of renewed probation.

Actual Ratzlaf does not predetermine which reading leaves genuine ambiguity. Its six-Justice alternative expressly rejects using disagreement alone as the trigger. Neither dissenting from Ratzlaf nor joining it excuses examination of the words of this different statute. Bifulco's distinction among types of punishment remains relevant. Williams prevents an appellate court from inventing a departure basis, and Stinson does not give commentary power to rewrite contrary statute. No new departure, new criminal drug conviction, or later amendment is assumed.

## 31. Powell v. Nevada

**Event:** Merits review, March 30, 1994; argument February 22, 1994; No. 92-8841. Research cutoff immediately before March 30.

**Common commitment:** All eight vacate the judgment and remand for application of McLaughlin and proper consideration of any remaining barriers to relief. All eight reject the sole premise that the prosecution's pre-McLaughlin commencement prevents the rule from applying on still-open direct review. None holds that delayed probable-cause review automatically invalidates the conviction, requires suppression, establishes harmlessness, or releases Powell.

### Alternative paths and common limits

**Remand path:** Griffith applies new criminal-procedure rules to cases pending on direct review; actual Harper reinforces equal application while keeping remedy distinct. Nevada's dependence on disruption and reliance cannot replace that finality rule. The four-day interval exceeds McLaughlin's ordinary 48-hour period. The government may still establish a bona fide emergency or extraordinary circumstance; weekends and ordinary consolidation are insufficient. Even within 48 hours, an individual can prove unreasonable investigative, malicious or purposeless delay. These rules do not require a personal appearance whenever a valid neutral determination is otherwise furnished.

The strongest objection to remand is that the Court reviews judgments, and Nevada's waiver discussion or Harris may independently sustain this one. The lower court, however, discussed waiver of state arraignment/appearance requirements upon voluntary Miranda waiver. It did not distinctly adjudicate all federal prolonged-detention and exclusion questions under an applicable McLaughlin rule. Harris concerns evidence following unlawful home entry where ongoing custody was otherwise lawful; it does not conclusively answer the product-of-unlawful-delay question. First-instance resolution avoids inventing waiver, causation, justification or harmlessness facts.

**Alternative affirmance path:** A preserved independent ground could sustain a judgment despite its incorrect nonretroactivity reasoning, and Gerstein does not render every later conviction void. Voluntary prearrest admissions, a lawfully obtained later statement, a valid waiver, or proper harmlessness findings might matter. Its strongest objection is the absence of a completed governing federal determination of those predicates. On this packet, the path does not justify final affirmance without assuming a missing legal or factual premise. Remand remains bounded: it permits these defenses rather than deciding them against Nevada.

## 32. Oregon Waste Systems, Inc. v. Department of Environmental Quality

**Event:** Merits review, April 4, 1994; argument January 18, 1994; Nos. 93-70 and 93-108 in the reported proceeding. Research cutoff immediately before April 4. All eight reach the concrete fee challenge on review of the Oregon Supreme Court. Private facilities are involved; the governmental market-participant exception does not follow from environmental planning.

### Governing components and alternative paths

**Invalidation path:** The charge expressly distinguishes waste by state of origin. Actual Chemical Waste and Fort Gratiot require a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve; Pike's incidental-burden branch does not replace that rule. A compensatory charge must identify an intrastate burden for which the interstate charge compensates, establish an equivalent burden rather than an excess, and concern substantially equivalent taxable events. Armco and Maryland reject combining unlike events merely because local businesses also pay taxes. General income taxation and disposal of waste are not substantially equivalent events. Scarce landfill capacity is depleted by equivalent domestic waste too. Evenhanded charges, volume limits and direct regulation remain possible; government does not gain a private landfill owner's market-participant status.

The strongest objection is the statute's express limitation to otherwise-unpaid governmental costs and the facial-review posture that excluded detailed factfinding. The legal defect, however, is not merely a factual allegation that $2.25 is excessive. The local charge is lower because of origin and no legally adequate equivalent domestic burden or distinctive origin-linked cost has been identified. A promise that an origin-based fee will cover costs does not make it evenhanded. Actual Northwest Airlines separately requires nondiscrimination even where fees match allocated costs. Judicial review's limited forum cannot transform the statutory cost command into a finding satisfying this distinct requirement.

**Justification/evidentiary-remand path:** A State can recover genuine otherwise-unpaid costs and need not supply nonresidents services free of charge. The charge might reflect a sufficiently defined regulatory cost borne differently within the comprehensive system, rather than commercial protection. The strongest objection is the concededly geographic trigger and the inability of general taxes to satisfy Armco's equivalent-events rule. This path therefore cannot simply affirm on the ground that any rational cost nexus defeats facial discrimination. Its narrower surviving form vacates the lower court's categorical approval and permits an opportunity to establish a constitutionally sufficient justification in an appropriate factual forum before conclusive invalidation. It does not deem general income taxes a valid compensatory equivalent or shift the burden away from Oregon.
