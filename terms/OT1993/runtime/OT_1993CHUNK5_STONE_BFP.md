#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the categorical summary-judgment ruling and remand for application of §548’s substantive value requirement.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit adjudication of reasonably equivalent value and all remaining statutory conditions. Do not declare the sale avoided on BFP’s alleged appraisal alone, disturb final titles beyond this timely statutory proceeding, or prescribe a fixed recovery before the applicable avoidance and recovery provisions are satisfied. #### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Constructive fraudulent transfer and foreclosure

**Entering Law:** Event-date 11 U.S.C. §548(a)(2) permits avoidance of qualifying transfers for less than reasonably equivalent value, subject to insolvency-related conditions and the statutory period. Sections 101(54) and 548, as amended in 1984, include foreclosure and involuntary transfers. Section 1107 gives the debtor in possession pertinent trustee powers. Butner v. United States, 440 U.S. 48 (1979), respects state property interests unless federal bankruptcy law supplies a different consequence. Competing circuit approaches include Durrett and Bundles, rather than a settled Supreme Court rule. [R25](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/531/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain rejection of conclusive equivalence, but supply a substantially more usable valuation account.

Section 548(a)(2), together with the express inclusion of foreclosure transfers, supports an independent federal value comparison. State procedural compliance is evidence of value, not necessarily the federal element itself. *Butner* respects state property rights unless federal bankruptcy law supplies a different consequence; simulated *Dewsnup* and *Nobelman* do not exempt foreclosure prices from this statute. [Statutory issue and competing approach, 511 U.S. 531](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/92-1370.ZD.html).

The proposal resolves **an open Supreme Court question with substantial title consequences**. Its central insight fits Stone's nonconversion principle. But “actual forced-sale context” is not yet an administrable standard. If context means whatever the particular lawful auction happened to yield, it reproduces the rule he rejects. If it means ordinary voluntary-market value, it ignores foreclosure conditions.

Stone should require an evidence-based comparison between the value transferred and the total legally relevant consideration, accounting for the property's condition, enforceable interests and liens, legally realistic sale constraints, available purchasers, marketing and notice actually provided, and reliable comparable distressed transactions. These are valuation evidence, not new mandatory elements or federal foreclosure procedures. BFP bears its statutory avoidance burden; a conventional appraisal above price is insufficient without explaining why that appraisal represents realizable value in a comparable forced-sale setting. Conversely, compliance with minimal notice procedures cannot make a grossly depressed exchange equivalent by definition.

The alleged $725,000-plus value and $433,000 price justify inquiry only with competent evidence and the other §548 predicates. No fixed percentage follows. A property exposed to a thin market after proper marketing differs from one sold at an artificial discount despite readily available buyers; ordinary market uncertainty should not become hindsight valuation.

The strongest counterargument is destabilization of titles, increased lender costs, and lower bids caused by avoidance risk. Those are concrete reliance interests under Master §16. Stone should address the statute's limited reach, insolvency conditions, proof burden, and applicable transferee protections without inventing a conclusive safe harbor or extra heightened burden. Even then his alternative is less predictable than the conclusive rule. The text's substantive comparison can justify that cost, but not pretending it disappears. Reverse the categorical ground and remand; do not avoid the sale or choose recovery before proof.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** A California home subject to a deed of trust was sold at foreclosure for $433,000. BFP alleged value exceeding $725,000 and, after bankruptcy, sought avoidance as debtor in possession. The sale was treated as noncollusive and compliant with state foreclosure procedure. The alleged higher value is not a judicial finding of federal statutory inadequacy. [R25](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/531/)

The claim invokes the bankruptcy avoidance power through the debtor in possession, not a constitutional taking or proof of actual fraud. Transfer coverage, insolvency and other §548 conditions, value, and authorized recovery remain separate statutory requirements. A properly conducted foreclosure and an allegation of low price do not resolve all of them. [R25](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/531/)
