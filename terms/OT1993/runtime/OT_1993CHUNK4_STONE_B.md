#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Objective custody trigger

**Entering Law:** Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966); Beckwith v. United States, 425 U.S. 341 (1976); Oregon v. Mathiason, 429 U.S. 492 (1977); California v. Beheler, 463 U.S. 1121 (1983); Berkemer v. McCarty, 468 U.S. 420 (1984). Custody turns on formal arrest or restraint comparable to formal arrest assessed from objective circumstances and the reasonable person in the interviewee’s position. [R22](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/318/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain reversal and remand under an objective custody standard.

*Beckwith*, *Mathiason*, *Beheler*, and *Berkemer* distinguish arrest-equivalent restraint from investigative focus. A suspect cannot experience an officer's undisclosed mental classification. Conversely, a person treated as a witness can be restrained as fully as a suspect. The proposed correction fits Stone's emphasis on actual operation while avoiding an unadministrable inquiry into private thought. [Interview record, 511 U.S. 318](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/318/).

This is **clarification within Miranda**, not replacement of custody doctrine. Simulated *Withrow* keeps warning and coercion claims distinct; it does not settle this custody application. Officers' communicated beliefs can matter through what their words and behavior convey about freedom, but becoming an acknowledged suspect is not itself the trigger.

Lower courts should examine how Stansbury arrived, the interview's setting and duration, number and positioning of officers, control of movement, instructions about leaving, and the interrogation's expressed terms. No fixed checklist or assumption that all station interviews are custodial is needed. An accusatory question during a voluntary interview can differ from a polite interview backed by blocked exits and commands.

The strongest counterargument is that focus often predicts coercive circumstances. It can explain why officers acted as they did, but their observable actions—not the private suspicion independently—affect the reasonable person's situation. This gives officers and courts a more stable standard without treating the interviewee's undisclosed fears as controlling either.

Remand for the complete objective application and statement-specific consequences. The existing error does not establish custody throughout the interview, suppression of every statement, or an involuntary confession. Keep any Fourth Amendment restraint issue separate rather than equating every seizure with Miranda custody.

**Egalitarian application:** The custody inquiry protects a reasonable person’s ability to understand and exercise the Miranda safeguards while giving officers an objective rule. Evaluate communicated words, location, restraints, duration, and other relevant circumstances; an officer’s undisclosed suspicions neither create nor negate custody. Remand for that assessment without assuming guilt, suppressing an otherwise admissible statement automatically, or treating professional descriptions as conclusive.

The cost is compliance expense and possible disruption to municipal resource recovery. #### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Federal custody trigger and presentment safe harbor

**Entering Law:** 18 U.S.C. §3501(c) protects a voluntary confession made within six hours following arrest or other detention from exclusion solely for delay in federal presentment, with a transportation-related extension where delay is reasonable. Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 5(a) and McNabb v. United States, 318 U.S. 332 (1943), and Mallory v. United States, 354 U.S. 449 (1957), concern federal presentment. Anderson v. United States, 318 U.S. 350 (1943), addresses improper state/federal collaboration. Custody by “any” officer can trigger the federal rule when it is custody for a federal offense; officer title and the possibility of a federal charge are not substitutes for the actual detention basis. [P21](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/350/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain reversal of the state-arrest trigger, preserving the anti-evasion boundary.

Section 3501(c) measures delay in performing a federal presentment obligation. Genuine detention on state charges does not by itself start that obligation, even when federal officers learn of a possible federal offense. The word “any” officer can cover an officer holding someone for a federal offense; it does not remove the relationship to federal presentment. [Record and preserved collaboration issue, 511 U.S. 350](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/350/).

This is **a permissible statutory clarification** consistent with Stone's source separation, but the formal label of custody cannot be conclusive. *Anderson v. United States*, 318 U.S. 350, supplies an established basis for examining improper intergovernmental arrangements. Stone's accountability commitment requires preserving that doctrine, rather than adding an abstract warning with no workable application.

Courts should examine who requested and controlled continued detention, whether a genuine state basis persisted, the timing of federal interrogation, agreements to defer federal arrest, and evidence that state custody was used to bypass federal duties. Ordinary cooperation, shared evidence, or later dismissal of state charges alone is insufficient. A federal request to hold a person after the state basis ended would be materially different. The supplied record establishes routine cooperation rather than such an arrangement.

The strongest objection is that the government can postpone the federal arrest strategically. Source-bound scrutiny of actual detention addresses that risk without converting every concurrent investigation into federal custody. *Powell*'s probable-cause timing and *Stansbury*'s Miranda custody are independent questions, even if later adopted in the simulation.

Remove this §3501(c) basis for reversal and remand for any unresolved preserved issues. Do not decide that all voluntary statements beyond six hours are admissible or inadmissible, decide §3501's general constitutional status, or import later *Dickerson/Corley* doctrine.

**Egalitarian application:** Separate governments may investigate their own offenses, but labels cannot conceal federal control of a detention used to evade presentment. Assess who directed and prolonged the hold and why. Genuine cooperation is permissible; an engineered state hold that defeats the applicable federal safeguard requires its own source-grounded response.

The question does not require deciding whether every confession outside six hours must be suppressed. [P21](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/350/)
