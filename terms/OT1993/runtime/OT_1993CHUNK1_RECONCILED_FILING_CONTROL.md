## Independent component and recordability

The approved neutral packets expressly present prospective filing control as a separate institutional question referred to the full Court. A separately filed sanctions motion by an opposing party is neither supplied nor needed for the Court to resolve that question about its own docket. The Court can affirmatively decline a new prospective condition on the established record while leaving the present fee motions unadjudicated. The existence of the referred question distinguishes this bounded action from merely turning the operator's research stop into an order.

The supported ground is substantive and narrow: the established filing history does not show that treatment of particular filings under existing rules is inadequate. That conclusion does not require a finding that any pending petition is nonfrivolous or that either applicant is financially eligible. Nor does it require a finding that a prior warning failed. Missing current papers prevent their individual adjudication; they do not supply an affirmative finding of abuse or a reason to impose a condition on unidentified future papers. The Court may choose the narrower available treatment on this record without deciding the ultimate scope of its power on another record.

An order may therefore state that no new specified prospective condition is imposed and that it resolves only that question. Current fee motions are **unadjudicated**, a description of the order's bounded scope, not an order granting, denying, staying, continuing, holding, or directing further consideration of those motions. No payment deadline, correction deadline, certiorari disposition, writ disposition, or demand for new submissions follows. The exact missing-source blockers belong in operator-facing state and audit material; they are not a fictional judicial finding that the Court lacks its own papers.
