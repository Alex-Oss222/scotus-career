#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Temporal reach of the §1981 amendment

**Entering Law:** Patterson v. McLean Credit Union, 491 U.S. 164 (1989), controls preamendment §1981: contract formation and enforcement, not every postformation employment action. Section 101 of the 1991 Act expands the defined contractual activities, including termination. Bradley and Bowen supply the inherited temporal authorities until superseded or clarified by an actual effective Landgraf ruling. [R21](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/298/)

##### Issue 2: Preserved contract-enforcement claim

**Entering Law:** The Sixth Circuit construed the allegations as including interference with equal contractual enforcement, a claim potentially within Patterson itself. That remand and the separation from the reviewed retrospective amendment question are part of the procedural posture. [R21](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/298/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain prospective treatment of §101 while preserving the old-law contract-enforcement remand.

The proposed result follows Stone's recommended *Landgraf* method, but no actual same-day simulated decision is supplied. This review evaluates the method and its application; it does not manufacture a dependency holding.

*Patterson*, 491 U.S. 164, remains the preamendment construction of §1981. Section 101 expands covered contractual activity, including termination. Congress's correction of a judicial interpretation does not alone declare the amendment retroactive. The distinction between judicial interpretation and legislative change supports applying the earlier construction to earlier conduct unless a valid ground for revising *Patterson* itself is properly presented. [Record and retained claim, 511 U.S. 298](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/298/).

The tension with Stone's equality commitments is substantive: workers alleging discriminatory treatment do not receive the full new remedy. But calling the amendment restorative does not answer enacted temporal reach. Stone should confront the strongest argument—that Congress understood itself to restore §1981's original breadth—and explain why purpose does not replace a sufficiently clear retrospective instruction. This is **section-specific application of a newly clarified temporal rule**, not a necessary reconsideration of *Patterson* on an ungranted issue.

Application requires sorting the actual pleaded conduct. A discriminatory termination newly covered by the amendment differs from interference with equal contractual enforcement potentially covered even under *Patterson*. The Sixth Circuit already preserved the latter theory for a jury; affirmance on temporal reach must not erase it. No discrimination finding follows from its survival, and the distinct Title VII result remains untouched.

The difficult boundary is a termination accomplished through denial of contractual procedures. Courts should identify the legal right allegedly impaired rather than classify all employment events by the final adverse outcome. Preserve that factual and legal work on remand instead of using antiretroactivity as a blanket dismissal device.

**User-directed case emphasis:** Retain the distinction between a legislative expansion and judicial interpretation of existing law. Prospective strengthening of civil-rights remedies does not itself authorize new retrospective liability. Preserve the actual old-law contract-enforcement proceedings rather than using the temporal holding to erase a claim that the earlier statute already supported.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Rivers and Davison, garage mechanics, were discharged in 1986 after disputes over notice for disciplinary hearings. They alleged racially discriminatory discharge and retaliation for insisting on contractual procedures afforded white employees. Patterson issued before trial. The district court dismissed the §1981 claims and separately found for the employer on Title VII. The Sixth Circuit revived the narrower contract-enforcement theory and declined retrospective application of §101. [R21](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/298/)

Only the temporal reach of §101 is under review. The limited jury-trial remand under the old statute remains part of the judgment unless independently altered through a lawful review question. Patterson’s validity is not automatically reopened because Congress later amended the statute. [R21](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/298/)
