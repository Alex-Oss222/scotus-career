## 13. Weiss v. United States

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case Name:** Weiss v. United States

**Citation or Docket:** 510 U.S. 163 (1994); No. 92-1482, including Hernandez under Supreme Court Rule 12.2

**Historical Event Date:** 1994-01-19

Comparator only. Introduce after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. This material is not simulated precedent and does not control the unapproved Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Both judgments affirmed, 9-0 in result. [P07](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/163/)

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Rehnquist wrote for seven on the full opinion; Scalia and Thomas joined Parts I and II-A and concurred in part and in the judgment. **Historical Authority:** Appointments Clause; UCMJ; Shoemaker and Middendorf. 510 U.S. 169-181; separate writings at 182-199. [P07](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/163/)

**Historical Sources:** 510 U.S. 165-168 for judgments and structure; 169-181 for the relevant statutory provisions, inherited authorities, and arguments. [P07](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/163/)

## 15. Albright v. Oliver

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case Name:** Albright v. Oliver

**Citation or Docket:** 510 U.S. 266 (1994); No. 92-833

**Historical Decision Date:** 1994-01-24

Comparator only. Introduce after provisional non-Stone modeling has been frozen; not simulated precedent and not a proposed Court result.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 7–2 in judgment, with no single majority rationale.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Rehnquist’s plurality was joined by O’Connor, Scalia, and Ginsburg. Scalia and Ginsburg separately concurred. Kennedy, joined by Thomas, concurred in the judgment; Souter concurred in the judgment separately. Stevens dissented, joined by Blackmun.

**Historical Holding:** The substantive-due-process claim failed. The plurality favored Fourth Amendment analysis, Kennedy relied on the nature of the due-process claim and state remedies, and Souter found no distinct substantial liberty burden requiring substantive due process. The Court did not decide a Fourth Amendment claim.

**Historical Authority:** Graham, Gerstein, and differing applications of due-process principles. Do not treat the plurality’s full reasoning as a nine-Justice holding; 510 U.S. 271–291.

**Historical Sources:** [R07](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/266/); United States Reports citation and date above. Opinion alignments are historical only.

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case Name:** National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler

**Citation or Docket:** 510 U.S. 249 (1994); No. 92-780

**Historical Decision Date:** 1994-01-24

Comparator only. Introduce after provisional non-Stone modeling has been frozen; not simulated precedent and not a proposed Court result.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, 9–0.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Rehnquist wrote for a unanimous Court. Souter concurred, joined by Kennedy, emphasizing First Amendment questions reserved for later adjudication.
