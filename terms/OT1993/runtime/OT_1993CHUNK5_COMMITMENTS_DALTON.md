## Actual law, record and review components

Actual Franklin holds the Secretary's completed census determination reviewable notwithstanding later prescribed presidential transmission; it expressly leaves the President's own APA status and coercive relief undecided and distinguishes an independent presidential policy decision. Its rule, including those limits, controls. The historical contrary rule is not available. Actual Lincoln concerns an unearmarked lump-sum allocation lacking meaningful standards and preserves enacted boundaries and independent constitutional claims. Actual Thunder Basin permits fairly discernible channeling into meaningful administrative and judicial review; it does not turn any detailed scheme into a bar on all review. Section 701(a)(1) statutory preclusion, §701(a)(2) committed discretion and §704 finality remain distinct inquiries.

The 1990 closure Act supplies a particular coordinated mechanism: Secretary's criteria after notice and comment and recommendations to Congress and an independent eight-member Commission; Commission public hearings and a recommended package; presidential approval or disapproval of that package in its entirety; revision after initial disapproval and termination after the second or failure to approve by the statutory deadline; approval/certification to Congress; and a joint disapproval opportunity lasting 45 days or until earlier adjournment. Otherwise the Secretary must implement all approved closures. The sequence includes April 15 in 1991/March 15 in later rounds, July 1 Commission report, July 15 presidential decision, August 15 revised report and September 1 approval cutoff. Closure must begin within two years and finish within six. The expedited congressional procedure is part of this structure.

Section 2905(c) separately excludes NEPA application before presidential submission, distinguishes environmental review concerning subsequent disposal and relocation from base selection, and provides a sixty-day period for covered review. This specific treatment informs the selection scheme but does not itself dispose of a general APA claim by verbal analogy.

Plaintiffs allege improper criteria, late-disclosed information and closed meetings. No present merits finding establishes a violation or harmlessness. Their suit, though filed two days before presidential submission, now seeks to stop the Secretary's implementation of the approved Philadelphia selection. No independently preserved access/publication enforcement order is established. The President is not a defendant. No claim that he inserted a base outside the Commission package is presented. The employee/union due-process count concerning continued shipyard operation was rejected and is not brought up. Military subject matter alone is not a political-question disposition.

## Enhanced paths

**Selection-preclusion path:** Reverse and remand for dismissal of the presented statutory challenge to implementation of the approved selection. Block permits preclusion fairly discernible from a statute's full design despite the presumption of review recognized in Michigan Academy. Congress required a single package, independent Commission scrutiny, presidential all-or-nothing judgment, rapid congressional disapproval and mandatory implementation. Allowing a court at implementation to remove or undo a base selection for antecedent procedural objections would reopen that completed package through a route the scheme does not provide. The confined NEPA channel confirms that Congress distinguished later implementation matters from selection. This is a scheme-specific construction under §701(a)(1), not a claim that all procedure is discretionary or every defense to closure is barred.

The strongest objection is that mandatory procedures supply law to apply and would lose judicial protection, while Thunder Basin's alternate judicial-review route is absent. This path acknowledges that difference. Thunder Basin is not sufficient by itself; the distinctive political package mechanism and limited review provision supply the additional basis. No genuinely independent constitutional claim is presented, and calling an ordinary asserted statutory violation unconstitutional does not create one. The ruling does not decide a materially different timely challenge to a separate completed procedural obligation or a presidential action beyond the approved statutory package.

**Procedure-review path:** Affirm the Third Circuit's allowance of bounded review, or remand for it, because reviewable implementing officials must comply with mandatory antecedent procedures and the Act supplies no express universal APA exclusion. Franklin prevents using the President's later involvement as automatic immunity, and Michigan Academy's presumption plus the absence of another judicial route favor enforcement of law. The strongest objection is that the requested relief attacks a completed selection rather than an independent ongoing procedural duty and disrupts the statutorily indivisible package. The participating commitments below reject this path on that precise remedial and statutory premise; they do not reject judicial review whenever an executive action concerns defense policy.

**Independent finality path:** The Secretary and Commission recommendations do not themselves close a base; the President has genuine approval/disapproval discretion and a statutory revision/termination choice before legal implementation duties arise. This differs from Franklin's completed census count followed by prescribed transmission. The strongest objection is that the Secretary's implementation now has final consequences and the suit names that official, not the President. A proper finality analysis cannot simply ignore that action or declare the President outside the APA by importing history. Four commitments below would additionally treat review of the recommendations through this implementation attack as unavailable on the particular sequence; others reserve that broader characterization and rely solely on preclusion. No commitment decides the President's general APA status or liability for coercive relief.
