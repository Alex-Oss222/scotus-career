#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the judgment sustaining the Sarfaty death sentence and remand for application of Stone's limited responsibility rule and the applicable constitutional harmless-error inquiry. Require a new penalty proceeding if the error is not shown harmless under that standard; leave guilt untouched.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** On remand, require the State to establish constitutional harmlessness under the governing direct-review standard; four eligibility aggravators alone do not answer the effect on selection. If that showing fails, hold a new penalty hearing without the irrelevant separate death judgment. Preserve independently admissible prior-conduct evidence and the guilt adjudication.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Sentencer responsibility and reliability

**Entering Law:** Caldwell v. Mississippi, 472 U.S. 320 (1985), read with Darden v. Wainwright, 477 U.S. 168 (1986), Dugger v. Adams, 489 U.S. 401 (1989), and Sawyer v. Smith, 497 U.S. 227 (1990), reaches misleading descriptions of the jury’s sentencing responsibility. Lockett, 438 U.S. 586 (1978), Woodson, 428 U.S. 280 (1976), and Zant, 462 U.S. 862 (1983), govern individualized capital sentencing. Donnelly v. DeChristoforo, 416 U.S. 637 (1974), supplies the fundamental-unfairness standard; Estelle v. McGuire, 502 U.S. 62 (1991), distinguishes state evidence error from federal error. [R37](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/1/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** defend a limited constitutional extension, but revise the automatic new-penalty-hearing remedy.

Stone's concern that a capital sentencer genuinely own its decision supports excluding an irrelevant prior death sentence while permitting independently relevant evidence of the underlying crime. The conviction describes conduct or criminal history; another jury's death choice adds institutional authority to the sentencing judgment itself. The distinction fits individualized selection and does not depend on innocence of the earlier crime.

However, Caldwell as narrowed by Dugger and Sawyer addresses misleading descriptions of legal responsibility. A true judgment, accompanied by correct responsibility instructions and notice of an appeal, is not straightforwardly controlled. Stone should expressly extend the principle to state-presented information that, in context, substantially invites abdication of the current choice, grounding the extension in Caldwell's responsibility rationale and capital reliability. Alternatively, a due-process holding would require actual fundamental unfairness under Donnelly, not merely irrelevant evidence under state law. Labeling it an “application” of the existing narrow rule would conceal the doctrinal cost.

The rule needs more than a speculative possibility of influence. Examine prominence, the prosecutor's use, whether the sentence was avoidably included in proof of conviction, and whether a specific instruction told jurors to disregard the other death decision in making their own. General instructions may be inadequate here, but do not hold that no explicit cure could suffice. The later reversal and retrial of the Thompson case cannot be attributed to these jurors.

Most importantly, establish the constitutional error and then address the appropriate prejudice inquiry. Four aggravators establish eligibility, not harmlessness concerning selection; they do not by themselves cure the problem. Equally, that distinction does not make the defect automatically structural. Stone should require a reasoned, record-based conclusion that the applicable harmless-error standard cannot be met, or remand for that inquiry, before mandating a new penalty hearing. This better preserves his own separation of violation, prejudice, and remedy while allowing the protective extension he favors.

**Egalitarian application:** A capital jury must make this defendant's sentencing choice without treating another jury's death judgment as an authoritative substitute. The State may prove relevant criminal history and seek lawful punishment, and victims' interests do not require jurors to decide under a distorted sense of responsibility. Relief must address the demonstrated constitutional error under the applicable prejudice rule.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** At the Sarfaty penalty hearing, the State introduced the Thompson murder judgment, which disclosed both a prior conviction and death sentence. Defense counsel objected to disclosure of the sentence independently of the conviction. Jurors were told an appeal was planned and instructed on their own responsibility and four aggravating circumstances; they found all four. The Thompson conviction was later reversed for improper joinder, followed by another conviction and death sentence on retrial. Those later events must not be attributed to the Sarfaty jury’s knowledge. [R37](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/1/)

This is direct review of the limited penalty-phase federal question. Oklahoma’s ruling on relevance alone establishes no constitutional violation. The Thompson conviction’s reversal and the evidence of the underlying crime are distinct from the challenged disclosure of another jury’s death sentence. No federal habeas retroactivity bar should be imported. [R37](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/1/)
