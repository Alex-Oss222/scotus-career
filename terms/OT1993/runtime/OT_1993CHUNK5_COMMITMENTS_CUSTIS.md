## Statutory and factual components

Section 924(e)(1) covers a person violating §922(g) who has three previous convictions by the specified courts for violent felonies, serious drug offenses, or both, committed on occasions different from one another. It imposes imprisonment of at least fifteen years and a fine of no more than $25,000, bars suspension of sentence and probation for that §922(g) conviction, and bars parole for the enhanced sentence. None of the classification or separate-occasion predicates is newly litigated here. Section 924(e)(2)(C)'s inclusion of qualifying juvenile delinquency is not an additional claim in this case.

Section 921(a)(20) excludes the identified business-regulation offenses and state misdemeanors punishable by two years or less. Conviction status follows the law of the jurisdiction in which the proceedings occurred. The specified expungement, set-aside, pardon and civil-rights-restoration events remove convictions subject to the exact firearms qualification: an express restriction on shipping, transporting, possessing or receiving firearms in the pardon, expungement or restoration prevents the statutory exclusion. Beecham supplies the convicting-sovereign rule, not a collateral-adjudication channel. The source copy's omission of transporting in its Beecham summary is not used to shorten the actual statute; its erroneous isolated Granderson date likewise is not repeated.

The 235-month sentence used the 1985 Pennsylvania robbery, 1985 Maryland burglary and 1989 Maryland attempted-burglary convictions. Only the Maryland convictions are challenged. The first challenge concerns deficient advice about voluntary intoxication and an unknowing plea. The second concerns advice about rights in a stipulated-facts proceeding tantamount to a plea and alleged failure to recognize that the facts showed attempted breaking and entering rather than attempted burglary. These are allegations, not invalidity findings, and neither asserts a complete denial of counsel. The district court's initial assessment of the merits does not replace the later channel ground on which the review proceeds. Custis still had state-custody status permitting him to seek original-conviction review at the relevant sentencing time.

Section 851(c)'s express written constitutional challenge, particularized allegations, defendant's preponderance burden and waiver absent good cause exist in another enhancement scheme. They are comparative statutory evidence, not ACCA procedure. Parke approved a particular rebuttable regularity and proof allocation for final counseled pleas; it expressly did not decide whether other systems must permit collateral attacks. Blackmun's judgment-only concurrence and the Court's separate ground remain distinct. Lewis was written by Blackmun and joined by Stevens; no Stevens dissent exists. It distinguishes conviction-based firearms disability from reliance on unreliable convictions to support guilt or greater punishment, without deciding this ACCA channel.

## Enhanced two-path analysis

**Limited sentencing-channel path:** Affirm the Fourth Circuit's refusal to hear the present plea and ineffectiveness claims. ACCA uses the fact of qualifying convictions and §921 specifies effects of convictions already removed; it contains no broad collateral-review procedure. Congress's explicit procedure in §851 reinforces the inference against importing one. Gideon, Burgett and Tucker preserve the special objection to a conviction obtained through complete deprivation of appointed counsel without a valid waiver; that foundational defect remains cognizable at enhancement. A claim of counsel's deficient performance or an involuntary counseled plea involves a different factual and procedural inquiry. The strongest contrary argument is that a sentence relying on any unconstitutional predicate becomes constitutionally tainted and Lewis expressly distinguished reliability-based punishment. The response is allocation of the attack, not approval of invalid predicates: original-conviction review remained available, and a successful challenge may provide a basis to seek appropriate relief regarding the federal sentence. No outcome of such a later application is predetermined.

On this path, the Constitution does not require immediate adjudication of these counseled-plea claims in the new federal sentencing forum on this record. Johnson v. Zerbst and Gideon explain the exceptional counsel absence; Parke and Timmreck establish why final judgments are not treated like unproved original charges. Thunder Basin is only an analogue: an alternative route must actually exist and remain meaningful, not merely be imagined. Ratzlaf and Granderson require residual lenity if genuine ambiguity remains; they do not turn every dispute about forum allocation into ambiguity. The statute's structure and available review path resolve the present inquiry without expanding the exception.

**Broader validity-review path:** Reverse and remand for adjudication of the constitutional validity challenges under lawful collateral standards. Burgett and Tucker make actual predicate reliability material when punishment is increased; Lewis's disability analysis expressly distinguished that use. ACCA does not expressly prohibit such challenges, and §851's detailed regulation of another scheme does not necessarily withdraw the preexisting constitutional inquiry from this one. Parke shows a way to respect final judgments without making their validity categorically unreviewable: require a concrete challenge and preserve appropriate regularity and proof principles. The strongest contrary arguments are Congress's express provisions elsewhere, stale-record burdens and review in the convicting jurisdiction. The reply permits orderly burden and preservation rules rather than importing §851 wholesale or demanding the Government disprove invalidity in every case. Constitutional avoidance reinforces the construction without requiring adjudication that these predicates are invalid; residual lenity may reinforce it if actual statutory ambiguity remains after ordinary tools.

**Remedies and reservations:** Affirmance sustains the 235-month enhancement against the channel challenge only and makes no new finding that the state proceedings were constitutionally flawless. Reversal removes the categorical refusal and returns the claims for a proper validity determination; it does not order acquittal, automatic resentencing without the predicates, or a lesser fixed sentence. A later successful state or federal collateral attack may support an application regarding the enhancement, but timeliness, custody, exhaustion, reopening and ultimate relief follow their own law. Total unavailability of every earlier review channel is not established and is reserved by both paths.
