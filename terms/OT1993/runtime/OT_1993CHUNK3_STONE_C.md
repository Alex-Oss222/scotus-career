#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Review Posture and Requested Relief:** Ninth Circuit, 982 F.2d 386 (1992), reversed summary judgment that had barred Brown’s Arizona and Wisconsin antitrust claims by the earlier federal settlement. The prior MDL certification and settlement were final, and their Rule 23 propriety was not open to relitigation in this proceeding. Ticor and the other insurers seek restoration of claim preclusion. Brown and the other absent consumers seek to pursue damages claims they contend could not constitutionally be extinguished without an opt-out opportunity. [P16](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/117/)

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Dismiss the writ as improvidently granted.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the Ninth Circuit’s judgment undisturbed, without an affirmative Supreme Court merits endorsement. No damages award, settlement rewrite, or categorical future class-certification direction.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Vehicle, finality, and the constitutional opt-out question

**Entering Law:** Rules 23(b)(1)(A) and (b)(2) concern incompatible adjudications and classwide injunctive or declaratory relief; Rule 23(b)(3) and Rule 23(c)(2) provide an opt-out mechanism for the relevant damages class. Phillips Petroleum Co. v. Shutts, 472 U.S. 797, 811-812 and n.3 (1985), protects notice, participation, adequate representation, and opt-out rights in the state damages-class setting addressed there, expressly reserving other class forms. Hansberry v. Lee, 311 U.S. 32 (1940), limits binding absent parties without adequate representation. Claim preclusion and constitutional validity are distinct. New York City Transit Authority v. Beazer, 440 U.S. 568 (1979), supports reaching available nonconstitutional grounds first, but does not create an unavailable Rule 23 collateral attack. [P16](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/117/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain a prudential dismissal, while recognizing that deciding the constitutional question is legally possible.

The collateral preclusion dispute is live, and finality fixes the prior Rule 23 certification premise. Stone cannot use constitutional avoidance to invent a Rule 23 collateral attack that the posture forecloses. But discretionary review need not resolve a broad constitutional issue in a vehicle that prevents examination of the statutory premise on which it depends. [Record and grant limitation, 511 U.S. 117](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/117/).

This is **case-selection restraint**, not a jurisdictional necessity. It fits Master §3 if Stone states why a future case presenting certification and due process together would be materially better. The strongest objection is that absent members now face a real loss and the question was deliberately framed around a final judgment; waiting does not make that injury disappear. Stone should concede this cost and avoid describing the issue as hypothetical.

*Shutts* establishes important protections in its state damages-class setting while reserving other class forms. Extending a categorical opt-out requirement to all federal mandatory classes with monetary consequences would be a **meaningful development**, particularly for inseparable relief or legitimate limited funds. *Hansberry* independently protects adequate representation, but the supplied record does not allow inventing an inadequacy finding.

For any later merits rule, distinguish individual money claims released for classwide benefits, indivisible injunctive relief, and genuinely limited assets. Notice, participation, representation, conflicts, and the practical value of an exit are material; “some money is involved” is too crude. Here Stone should leave the Ninth Circuit judgment operative without adopting its full theory, awarding damages, rewriting the settlement, or using later *Amchem/Ortiz* law. That makes the decisional limit clear rather than hiding a merits preference in avoidance language.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** An earlier thirteen-state title-insurance antitrust class was certified under Rules 23(b)(1)(A) and (b)(2). Its settlement extinguished monetary claims in exchange for injunctive relief, additional insurance coverage, and litigation costs, not an ordinary individual cash distribution. Arizona and Wisconsin had objected to certification and the lack of opt-out rights. Brown later filed a separate damages action. The lower courts found adequate representation; the Ninth Circuit nonetheless recognized an opt-out due-process objection. The present question assumes proper certification because the prior determination is final; it does not independently establish that the Rules actually authorize every such class. A live preclusion dispute exists, but the limited question forecloses the usual prior consideration of whether the Federal Rules permit mandatory certification of these claims. A prudential dismissal is different from a jurisdictional declaration that there is no case. Do not silently reopen final Rule 23 certification or infer lack of adequate representation contrary to the record. [P16](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/117/)

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand because Alabama may not defend peremptories on sex-based presumptions about juror partiality.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the lower court to apply the constitutional restriction and Batson-type inquiry to the challenged strikes. Do not adjudicate paternity in the Supreme Court or prescribe a quota. [R18](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/127/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Sex classification in jury selection

**Entering Law:** Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79 (1986), provides the staged inquiry into discriminatory peremptories. Powers v. Ohio, 499 U.S. 400 (1991), Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete, 500 U.S. 614 (1991), and Georgia v. McCollum, 505 U.S. 42 (1992), address standing and attribution across jury settings. Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190 (1976), and Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718 (1982), require an important objective substantially served by a sex classification. Taylor v. Louisiana, 419 U.S. 522 (1975), concerns the distinct fair-cross-section guarantee. [R18](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/127/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain extension of the peremptory-strike prohibition to sex through intermediate scrutiny.

*Craig* and *Hogan* already reject sex stereotypes unsupported by an important objective and substantial relationship. *Batson*, *Powers*, *Edmonson*, and simulated *McCollum* supply a workable selection and juror-rights framework. Exclusion from civic adjudication based on presumed male or female views fits Stone's equal-citizenship commitment directly. [Selection record, 511 U.S. 127](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/127/).

This is **a justified doctrinal extension**, not a claim that *Batson* already decided sex. Stone should identify the changed coverage and preserve the existing inference, explanation, and ultimate-purpose stages. The State's use of nine strikes against men is relevant evidence; the resulting all-female jury is not itself the violation. No proportional jury entitlement or added ultimate burden shift follows.

The strongest objection is that peremptories traditionally rely on imperfect predictions and sex might correlate with views in a paternity case. The answer is that intermediate scrutiny does not let the State substitute a broad group prediction for individual civic qualification when voir dire and sex-neutral case-specific reasons are available. Do not silently convert the standard to strict scrutiny or treat every statistical average as unconstitutional in every regulatory setting.

Application requires assessing the reasons actually offered and whether they fit comparable retained jurors. A juror's expressed view about disputed paternity evidence may justify a strike; an assumption that men will favor the alleged father does not. Occupation, family experience, and demeanor can be legitimate or pretextual, so courts must examine context without inventing explanations. This leaves some pretext litigation and imperfect enforcement, a cost preferable to categorical permission for explicit sex exclusions. Remand for the proper inquiry and a new trial if required; do not decide paternity or abolish peremptories.

**User-directed case emphasis:** Population-level correlations do not establish a particular juror's viewpoint or justify this intentional sex classification. Evidence must address the government's actual jury-selection task and a legally sufficient objective, not simply document aggregate tendencies. Retain sex's own heightened scrutiny and individual civic equality rather than importing Stone's separate racial-repair framework.

The trial court rejected the sex-discrimination objection as legally unavailable rather than requiring the ordinary inquiry into permissible explanations. The number of women on the final jury is not itself the constitutional injury. [R18](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/127/)

The State exercised the challenged strikes, so state action is direct. The defendant objected, and the federal legal claim was decided below. Powers and Edmonson supply the background for juror-rights and litigant-standing analysis. This is an equal-protection challenge to selection, not a demand for a proportionally balanced petit jury. [R18](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/127/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Scope of the private securities action

**Entering Law:** 15 U.S.C. §78j(b) prohibits use of manipulative or deceptive devices in connection with securities transactions; 17 C.F.R. §240.10b-5 implements it. Superintendent of Insurance v. Bankers Life, 404 U.S. 6 (1971), recognizes the private primary action. Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder, 425 U.S. 185 (1976), and Herman & MacLean v. Huddleston, 459 U.S. 375 (1983), reserve aiding and abetting. Santa Fe Industries v. Green, 430 U.S. 462 (1977), requires the statutory deception/manipulation predicate. Musick, Peeler & Garrett, 508 U.S. 286 (1993), concerns development of the recognized implied remedy. [R19](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/164/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain rejection of the pleaded secondary-liability theory, but describe it as a substantial displacement of entrenched lower-court law.

Section 10(b) regulates manipulative or deceptive conduct. “Indirectly” can reach a person's indirect commission of that conduct without necessarily creating a civil action against everyone assisting another's violation. *Hochfelder* and *Huddleston* reserved aiding and abetting; no controlling Supreme Court holding must be overruled. Nevertheless, longstanding circuit acceptance and investor enforcement expectations deserve more than a sentence about congressional silence. [Question and competing analyses, 511 U.S. 164](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/511/164).

Stone joined the simulated *Musick, Peeler* contribution holding. The principled distinction is between allocating a common liability among persons already within the received action and adding liability for conduct that does not itself satisfy the statutory prohibition. That distinction is defensible, but not automatic: opponents can argue that accessory responsibility is an equally established incident of an implied action. Stone should address common-law accessory principles, the close express statutory analogues, and the requirements—especially reliance—that secondary liability risks bypassing.

His source-based remedial restraint supports the proposal only if those arguments leave no sufficient statutory footing. It is **open Supreme Court doctrine with major practical change**, not mere housekeeping. The cost is less private redress against knowing facilitators and altered incentives for professional gatekeepers. Regulatory usefulness cannot supply the omitted cause, but these costs must be acknowledged.

Application must distinguish assistance from the bank's own deception on the law then available. Delaying an appraisal after warnings may show culpability, but culpability alone is not a primary deceptive act. Conversely, banks and professionals are not exempt as classes when their own conduct satisfies the prohibition. Do not import later *Janus/Stoneridge* limits, resolve all SEC powers, or invent a primary claim absent from the pleadings. Apply the ruling to this pending secondary claim; final judgments stay final.

The issuer defaulted before review was completed. The Court’s review encompasses the existence and scope of the private secondary action, an issue previously reserved in Supreme Court decisions. Lower-court acceptance is significant authority and reliance context, but not a Supreme Court holding on the reserved question. Decide the cause’s reach before weighing recklessness. No later securities amendments or later definitions of primary liability apply. [R19](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/164/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Settlement credit in comparative-fault admiralty

**Entering Law:** United States v. Reliable Transfer Co., 421 U.S. 397 (1975), replaces equal division with proportionate fault when fault can reasonably be allocated. Edmonds v. Compagnie Generale Transatlantique, 443 U.S. 256 (1979), preserves the relevant joint-and-several liability principle in its statutory setting and did not decide settlement credit. Entering maritime authorities divide among pro tanto credit with contribution against the settler, pro tanto credit with settlement protection, and proportionate-share reduction. The former may require a good-faith settlement inquiry; the latter assigns settlement bargain risk to plaintiff and settler and requires allocation of responsibility. No Supreme Court rule entering this term resolves the choice. [P17](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/202/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain proportionate-share settlement credit as an expressly adopted maritime common-law rule.

Unlike an implied statutory cause, this is a recognized field of judicial maritime development. *Reliable Transfer*, 421 U.S. 397, supports allocation by fault but does not itself choose settlement credit. *Edmonds* protects the relevant joint-liability arrangement and must not be silently overruled. [Settlement, verdict, and competing methods, 511 U.S. 202](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/202/).

Stone has a legal basis to **resolve an open framework choice**: subtract the settling tortfeasors' proportionate responsibility, leaving plaintiff and settlers with the bargain's upside or shortfall and protecting the settlement against ordinary contribution for that share. This better links the nonsettler's exposure to adjudicated responsibility and avoids satellite litigation about the settlement's financial adequacy. It is not compelled simply because proportionality sounds fair.

The strongest objections are potential plaintiff overrecovery, difficulty trying an absent settler's fault, and effects on settlement incentives. A dollar credit avoids some overrecovery but makes the nonsettler's bill depend on another's bargain and may require contribution or good-faith hearings. Stone should prefer the proportionate approach for those operational reasons while preserving ordinary controls on fraudulent or invalid settlements.

Application requires a reliable fault allocation including relevant absent settling actors, with parties able to present evidence. Do not treat settlement dollars as evidence of fault. Here River Don's independently found 38% produces $798,000; the combined 30% for McDermott and settlers need not be artificially divided to resolve its share. Preserve AmClyde's unreviewed contractual limitation and avoid reallocating that share to River Don. This ruling does not abolish joint-and-several liability for unrelated insolvency or nonsettlement problems. *Boca Grande* can use it only as a recommendation or after an actual effective simulated decision.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** A crane hook failed during an offshore platform lift. Potential causes included operation, crane design, the hook, and supporting slings. McDermott settled with three sling defendants for $1 million. The jury found $2.1 million in loss and allocated 32 percent to AmClyde, 38 percent to River Don, and 30 percent jointly to McDermott and the settling defendants. The district court awarded $672,000 and $798,000 against the nonsettlers. The Fifth Circuit applied the separate contract limit for AmClyde and reduced River Don’s liability to $470,000 by subtracting the $1 million settlement from $1.47 million. No finding divides the combined 30 percent between McDermott and the settlers. General maritime law supplies the liability-allocation issue and an authorized field of federal common-law development. Do not reopen the unreviewed contractual limitation or enlarge McDermott’s requested recovery. A settlement allocation is not a constitutional damages test. Joint-and-several liability, contribution, comparative fault, and settlement credit perform different functions. [P17](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/202/)
