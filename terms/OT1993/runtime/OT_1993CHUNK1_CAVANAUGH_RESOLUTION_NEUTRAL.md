##### Issue 1: Prospective channel and retroactive punishment

**Controlling Simulated Law / Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the case-specific inherited-law rules and limits above to this issue. Other eligible authorities below remain effective only to the extent consistent with those simulated holdings and any actual earlier effective OT1993 ruling.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** U.S. Const. art. I, §10; Weaver v. Graham, 450 U.S. 24 (1981), evaluates retrospective laws disadvantaging an offender by increasing punishment; Miller v. Florida, 482 U.S. 423 (1987), applies that protection to changed sentencing rules. Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37 (1990), confines the Clause to its punishment-related categories rather than all procedural disadvantage. Dobbert v. **Open Point:** Whether loss of every alternate consideration opportunity changes punishment or is only permissible procedural administration; whether §1983 can restore the opportunity without dictating release.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Fourth Circuit held that the retrospective two-year rule materially disadvantaged Roller and that a process-only claim fit §1983. Officials argue that unchanged eligibility and wholly discretionary parole make the amendment procedural, and that habeas and exhaustion are necessary because parole affects custody. Roller answers that the removed hearing can postpone a release the Board might otherwise authorize and that he asks only for consideration. The State’s separate damages victories are conceded for this review. [P04](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/92-1510.ZPC.html) [P04.1](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/984/120/356289/)
