## Reassessment of the two paths

Path A rejects the claimed automatic forfeiture and affirms the enforcement judgment at the limited reviewed scope. Section 10(c) authorizes public remedial action after an unfair labor practice; its for-cause prohibition concerns the actual reason for suspension or discharge. Phelps Dodge and Virginia Electric leave the Board a choice within those statutory purposes. An employee's hearing dishonesty may justify withholding relief, but the statute and cited cases do not make it an automatic disqualification. The Board's knowledge of the falsehood prevents treating the award as premised on an unchallenged truthful account, but knowledge alone is not represented as express deliberation over a distinct sanction.

Path B would vacate and demand express agency consideration of hearing dishonesty. Its strongest point is that judicial deference cannot substitute a reviewing court's reasons for an agency's unexplained choice. That point remains valid for an appropriate preserved administrative-reasoning challenge. Here the petition's categorical forfeiture theory cannot be transformed into a new duty to issue an express ruling on a separate sanction. There is no reviewed finding that an otherwise mandatory statutory factor went unconsidered; no enacted rule establishes hearing misconduct as such a mandatory independent factor. Rejecting the categorical claim therefore leaves the existing enforcement judgment in place without adjudicating a broader abuse-of-discretion claim. An opinion may preserve reasoned agency action and judicial review, but cannot state that an express perjury-remedy balance occurred.

This conclusion does not require a rule that the Board never must explain its treatment of misconduct. Nor does it declare the Board's silence satisfactory in every posture. It resolves only the automatic-disqualification question actually presented. General judicial concern with truthful adjudication, reflected in Precision Instrument, remains insufficient to rewrite the administrative remedial statute; ordinary authorized sanctions remain separate.
