## Conclusion and exact new common ground

**The proposed bounded formulation supports a full join by all eight non-Stone Justices.** Ginsburg's join is a supported expansion relative to the previously reconciled opinion that affirmatively endorsed the core-purpose limit as compelled by FOIA's original text. Her judgment is unchanged. The new common opinion instead follows Reporters Committee as retained statutory precedent and expressly leaves the disputed original-text justification undecided. Her separate criticism remains available and is not withdrawn by the join.

The common ground is: section7114(b)(4) incorporates the Privacy Act without modification; section552a(b)(2) asks what FOIA requires; retained Reporters Committee governs the ordinary third-party public-interest inquiry; actual Ray requires a concrete comparison; the additional home addresses supply no demonstrated transparency contribution sufficient to outweigh the home-location privacy interest on this record. Exemption6 permits withholding, while the Privacy Act supplies the operative prohibition under the invoked route. The decision reverses both enforcement components without deciding every address request, every Privacy Act exception, or whether FOIA's original words inevitably required Reporters Committee's limitation.

## Why Ginsburg's recorded objection is resolved

The earlier reconciliation did not hold that Ginsburg can never join an opinion applying Reporters Committee. It identified her precise objection to affirmatively endorsing the precedent's underlying core-purpose restriction as unambiguously dictated by FOIA text. Her1989 Treasury concurrence,884 F.2d1446,1457-1461, accepted the statutory chain and result while urging reconsideration of the policy/textual interaction; she joined that court's opinion and judgment notwithstanding the reservation. The current official comparator supplies further evidence that her sufficient ground is statutory-precedent adherence and that her criticism concerns the stronger original-text claim.

The proposal removes that stronger claim from the common opinion. It expressly preserves Congress's recognition of bargaining value, refuses to inflate ordinary-address privacy into rap-sheet sensitivity, and grounds the relevant-interest limit in retained precedent. This is a concrete legal formulation change, not a changed record, a presumed change of mind, or a desired coalition. Under Engine section8 it cures the previously recorded join barrier at the exact point identified. Ginsburg can adopt the operative rule and this record's application because the precedent governs, while explaining separately that she remains unconvinced the restriction was the textually inevitable original construction. Those positions are compatible.

The **minimum cure** is substantive: the Court must not elsewhere restore a claim that FOIA itself plainly and inevitably excludes every congressionally recognized public interest beyond direct government illumination. It must retain the precedent-based ground and actual Ray limits. Merely adding respectful words about unions while keeping the rejected original-text premise would not suffice. Her common join also does not announce that the disparity with private-sector unions was Congress's affirmative labor-policy choice.

## Exact support and remaining limits

All eight non-Stone Justices support both reversals and the common explanation at the level stated. The new join is **Ginsburg's full support for this revised precedent-based explanation**, not her endorsement of an inevitable textual core-purpose limit or surrender of her published criticism. A separate Ginsburg concurrence may state that distinction without becoming judgment-only. The author and Stone's participation remain outside this audit.

Actual Ray permits a demonstrated derivative investigative benefit to remain legally open and requires no antecedent proof of wrongdoing. This opinion finds no sufficient demonstrated benefit here; it neither declares all indirect public benefits irrelevant nor affirmatively adopts a general derivative-investigation test. Scalia/Kennedy retain their narrower individual view. Written request or consent must mean the request or consent of the individual to whom the record pertains under the Privacy Act; the unions' request alone is not that exception. Other routes are neither guaranteed nor foreclosed.
