#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand the judgment granting summary judgment to the officials.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Reconsider the Rule 56(f) request and evaluate each defendant’s knowledge, response, causal role, and capacity. Preserve the separate individual-immunity inquiry and deny any assumption of automatic damages or a blanket placement injunction. [R32](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/825/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Serious risk and culpable disregard

**Entering Law:** Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97 (1976); Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294 (1991); and Helling v. McKinney, 509 U.S. 25 (1993), require a sufficiently serious condition and culpable indifference, with prospective protection possible before injury. Hudson v. Palmer, 468 U.S. 517 (1984), recognizes custodial safety duties. The precise subjective/objective recklessness formulation divides lower courts. [R32](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/825/)

##### Issue 2: Discovery, capacities, and prospective relief

**Entering Law:** Bivens, 403 U.S. 388 (1971), and Carlson v. Green, 446 U.S. 14 (1980), are inherited sources for the presented individual-officer damages channel. Rule 56(f), in event-date numbering, addresses needed discovery. Helling permits examination of conditions and conduct persisting during litigation for prospective relief. No later Prison Litigation Reform Act requirements apply. [R32](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/825/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain vacatur and remand; make the knowledge inference effective without converting it into constructive knowledge.

Stone's protection of persons whom government has made dependent supports a serious custodial duty. It does not authorize dispensing with Wilson's culpability requirement. His acceptance of the simulated Helling rule also supports intervention against serious future danger before another assault. The proposed subjective recklessness formulation is a legitimate resolution of an open question within that framework, rather than a necessary consequence of the word “indifference.” Civil recklessness supplies a competing, more protective formulation; adopting it despite Wilson would require considerably more explanation.

The official must actually apprehend a substantial risk, though not necessarily the precise attacker, time, or mechanism of injury. Obviousness, prior segregation, reports, communications, and experience can prove that apprehension. A disclaimer of knowledge is evidence to assess, not an immunity from inference. Conversely, vulnerability associated with transgender status and the eventual assault do not conclusively establish what every defendant knew. The district court could not make Farmer's silence dispositive while foreclosing discovery of other knowledge. This distinction is supported by the arguments and factual record collected in the [historical opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/92-7247.ZO.html), used here as a source rather than an already effective simulated holding.

Lower courts should identify each defendant's information, authority, and response. A reasonable protective response defeats deliberate indifference even if unsuccessful; inexplicable disregard of repeatedly documented danger may support knowledge and culpability. Deliberate avoidance cannot become a reward for refusing to investigate a risk already perceived, but ordinary failure to discover a risk remains insufficient. Prospective relief uses current knowledge and danger, which litigation itself may illuminate; damages cannot use later notice to manufacture earlier liability. Preserve capacities, causation, individual immunity, and the existing Bivens/Carlson channel separately. A transfer or tailored protection may be justified; a categorical ban on all penitentiary confinement requires facts this record does not supply.

**Egalitarian application:** Custody creates a duty to address known serious risks that the prisoner cannot freely escape. Officials are entitled to judgment based on their actual knowledge, responsibility, and reasonable protective options, without liability merely because harm occurred. Those limits cannot make discovery or circumstantial proof of awareness unavailable to a vulnerable prisoner.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Farmer, a transgender federal prisoner, alleges assault after transfer from FCI-Oxford to USP-Terre Haute and placement in the general male population. Officials knew personal characteristics asserted to create vulnerability; the history included protective segregation, but responsibility, knowledge, and the relevant institutional conditions remain contested. The exact 1989 security classifications are not established merely by the facilities’ general labels. Farmer made no advance express safety complaint. The district court denied a Rule 56(f) request for additional discovery and treated that silence as decisive against actual knowledge. [R32](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/825/)

The complaint invokes Bivens and Carlson against officials in specified capacities. Some defendants are official-capacity only; damages cannot be silently imposed against those defendants or the Treasury. Other officials face personal-capacity claims. Qualified immunity was raised but not the basis of the merits ruling reviewed. Prospective relief requires a current threat and appropriate responsible officials; the requested blanket ban on all penitentiary placement is not automatically justified. [R32](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/825/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Tax or successive punishment

**Entering Law:** The Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments, Benton v. Maryland, 395 U.S. 784 (1969), and North Carolina v. Pearce, 395 U.S. 711 (1969), govern successive punishment. United States v. Halper, 490 U.S. 435 (1989), remains inherited law for civil penalties; United States v. Ward, 448 U.S. 242 (1980), addresses civil/criminal characterization. A. Magnano Co. v. Hamilton, 292 U.S. 40 (1934), recognizes that a nominal tax can operate as a penalty. Montana’s 1987 Act and implementing return rules supply the actual exaction. [R33](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/767/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain disallowance of the surviving assessment, candidly treating the tax classification as a doctrinal extension.

Stone can apply the constitutional prohibition against successive punishment to an exaction that functions as punishment despite its revenue label. But ordinary taxes can deter, exceed market value, and tax illegal activity. Halper's remedial accounting for civil penalties supplies neither a universal tax-cost ceiling nor an automatic answer here. Ward and the traditional distinction between taxes and penalties require a reasoned explanation of why this assessment crosses the boundary.

The strongest ground is the combination of an arrest-linked collection mechanism, criminally conditioned liability, confiscated and destroyed contraband, exceptional rate, and a separate proceeding after criminal punishment. The State's strongest response is that taxable possession occurred before confiscation: subsequent destruction ordinarily does not erase accrued tax. Stone should answer that no single feature controls; the scheme's connection to criminal apprehension and its operation together distinguish it from an ordinary excise on possession or income. This is a significant new application, vulnerable to uncertain lines, rather than mere relabeling under settled tax law. The [historical decision and competing arguments](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/93-144.ZO.html) document those features.

Administration should focus on statutory triggers, ordinary collection opportunities, tax base, and the proceeding's relationship to criminal punishment. No eight-to-one cutoff, ban on deterrent taxes, or demand that all taxation reimburse costs is defensible. A confidential tax collectible independently of arrest presents a materially different case. Retain Halper as entering law; neither later repudiation of it nor the Excessive Fines test can silently substitute for double-jeopardy analysis. The simulated Dixon retained Grady, unlike historical Dixon, but that does not settle these respondents' different pleas. Montana's unpreserved same-offense argument is not jurisdictional merely because called “standing.” Limit the disposition to the preserved $181,000 claim; do not hold possession and conspiracy universally identical.

**Egalitarian application:** The State can tax unlawful economic activity and protect the public, while the same person's exposure to successive punishment remains constitutionally constrained. Assess what this scheme actually does rather than accepting either the tax label or the severity of its rate as conclusive. Fair administration includes respecting preserved issues and the differences among the respondents' pleas.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Following the raid and criminal proceedings, Montana assessed a drug tax collected through a return obligation triggered by arrest. The drugs had been confiscated and destroyed. Most of the roughly $895,000 original assessment was rejected under state law; the federal issue concerns the surviving $181,000 assessment on harvested marijuana. The rate was $100 per ounce, producing a charge eight times market value for part of the material. All six family members entered criminal pleas, but only Richard Kurth was convicted of possession; the others pleaded to conspiracy, with possession charges withdrawn. [R33](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/767/)

The bankruptcy proof of claim supplies the actual controversy. Montana’s new argument that only Richard had a same-offense objection was not raised below; its label as standing does not make the unresolved merits/plea issue an established jurisdictional defect. No decision should silently equate conspiracy and possession or adjudicate withdrawn charges. The reviewed assessment is not the entire original claim. [R33](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/767/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Final decisions and collateral orders

**Entering Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1291; Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp., 337 U.S. 541 (1949); Coopers & Lybrand v. Livesay, 437 U.S. 463 (1978); and Lauro Lines v. Chasser, 490 U.S. 495 (1989), require a conclusive, sufficiently important separate issue effectively unreviewable after final judgment. Abney, 431 U.S. 651 (1977), and Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511 (1985), identify particular public-law immunities. Section 1292(b) provides a distinct discretionary route. [R34](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/863/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain dismissal of the interlocutory appeal.

Stone's insistence that jurisdiction come from law supports refusing to let private drafting convert every settlement into an immediately appealable immunity. Cohen, Coopers & Lybrand, and Lauro Lines permit a categorical distinction between protection of a public interest in avoiding trial and an ordinary contractual defense. The simulated Puerto Rico Aqueduct involved sovereign immunity and therefore does not commit him to treating the two alike.

There is a genuine cost: a settlement may purchase peace, and the expense and disclosure of renewed litigation cannot always be undone by final review or damages. Stone should acknowledge that “later review is possible” is not itself a complete answer. Section 1291 tolerates irretrievable litigation burdens unless the category satisfies the exception's importance and effective-unreviewability requirements. A party's insistence that its particular release is exceptionally clear cannot determine appellate jurisdiction. Otherwise the jurisdictional inquiry would require deciding the very contract controversy it is supposed to precede.

Lower courts should classify the right asserted, rather than measure the particular lawsuit's inconvenience. Constitutional or statutory trial immunities remain subject to their own precedents; ordinary releases and contractual promises take final review, potentially available §1292(b) certification, and ordinary protective procedures. Do not declare every conceivable private right categorically incapable of collateral review. The district court's power to reopen its dismissal is also distinct from power to enforce a freestanding settlement under the proposed Kokkonen approach. Stone should neither approve reopening on the merits nor use a possibly erroneous reopening order to invent appellate jurisdiction. This is a narrow application of received finality law, not a revision of his commitment to meaningful remedies.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Desktop sued over Digital’s use of a trade name. The parties reached a confidential settlement on March 25, 1992, and Desktop dismissed the suit. Months later it alleged material misrepresentation during negotiations. The district court vacated the dismissal and allowed the dispute to proceed; Digital unsuccessfully sought reconsideration and a stay, then appealed. The truth of the fraud allegations and correctness of reopening are not established by the appellate jurisdictional ruling. [R34](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/863/)

Section 1291 finality and its collateral-order exception are the sole immediate-review question. A claimed contractual trial immunity is not a statutory jurisdictional grant. The district court’s underlying authority and the merits of settlement rescission cannot be reached through an appeal that lacks jurisdiction. [R34](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/863/)

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Review Posture and Requested Relief:** Ninth Circuit, 984 F.2d 1025 (1993), reversed a district court ruling allowing all three categories as response costs. The Supreme Court review concerns these separable cost components, not a new determination of all cleanup liability. Key Tronic seeks recovery of its legal expenditures from the Air Force and other responsible parties. The United States seeks rejection of fees not authorized by CERCLA. [P30](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/809/)

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm in part, reverse in part, and remand the Ninth Circuit’s judgment.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny the litigation and EPA-negotiation categories; permit recovery of proven necessary identification costs subject to the statutory requirements and appropriate allocation. Do not set an unsupported dollar amount.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Litigation and settlement fees

**Entering Law:** CERCLA §107(a)(4)(B), 42 U.S.C. §9607(a)(4)(B), covers necessary response costs consistent with the national contingency plan. Section 101(25) defines response to include enforcement activities; §113 supplies contribution provisions, and §120 addresses federal facilities. Alyeska Pipeline Service Co. v. Wilderness Society, 421 U.S. 240 (1975), and Runyon v. McCrary, 427 U.S. 160 (1976), require a sufficient statutory basis to depart from the American rule. SARA expressly authorizes fees in other provisions, but the language invoked here does not expressly make private cost-allocation litigation a fee-shifting action. [P30](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/809/)

##### Issue 2: Nonlitigation identification work

**Entering Law:** Section 107(a)(4)(B)’s necessity and national-contingency-plan requirements govern work undertaken as part of a response. The claimant must establish what work was performed, its response function, necessity, and the recoverable amount. The American rule governs the costs of litigating, not every professional activity a lawyer might perform. [P30](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/809/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain the differentiated cost disposition, with functional allocation of mixed work.

Stone's environmental commitments favor effective cleanup but do not enlarge CERCLA's monetary authorization. Alyeska's American-rule background and SARA's express fee provisions elsewhere support denying fees for this private cost-allocation litigation. The competing textual argument is substantial: “response” includes enforcement activities, and a private cost-recovery suit enforces CERCLA. Stone should explain why that definition does not sufficiently authorize shifting litigation expenses, rather than declare that only the exact words “attorney's fees” could ever do so.

The distinction between litigation and identification costs fits his source-sensitive method. Work discovering another responsible party can materially serve response activity independently of the suit. Its recoverability should turn on necessity, consistency with the national contingency plan, and actual function, not on whether a lawyer rather than an investigator performed it. Here identification of the Air Force supplied a cleanup participant; that is more than a lawyer locating an opponent merely to allocate the client's liability.

The difficult boundary is settlement preparation that also produces useful technical information. Deny the claimed EPA-negotiation expenses insofar as they principally protected Key Tronic's liability position; do not create a categorical exclusion for a separable sampling study simply because used in negotiations. Require task descriptions, allocation of mixed invoices, proof of the response connection, and exclusion of duplicative recovery. No invented dollar award is appropriate. This clarification is administrable, though classification will produce disputes; that is preferable to both an occupational exclusion and unrestricted recovery of every useful legal expense. Fogerty and the simulated Dague concern their own fee statutes and do not supply missing CERCLA authorization.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Key Tronic was one of several parties responsible for contaminating a landfill. It settled with the EPA and then sought recovery from other responsible parties. Its professionals performed three different functions: identifying other polluters, negotiating the EPA settlement that fixed Key Tronic’s own liability, and prosecuting this cost-recovery litigation. The identification work uncovered the Air Force’s disposal activity and materially advanced the cleanup and the identification of a solvent participant. A lawyer’s invoice is not by itself proof that every activity is a litigation fee or a necessary cleanup cost. Separate an authorized cost-recovery or contribution cause of action, federal defendant amenability under CERCLA’s federal-facility provisions, and the particular cost recoverable. No immunity obstacle to the litigated cost categories is identified, but usefulness alone creates no waiver or fee award. No later CERCLA restriction or expansion of private actions is imported. [P30](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/809/)
