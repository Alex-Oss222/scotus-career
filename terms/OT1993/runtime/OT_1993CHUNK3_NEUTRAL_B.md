#### INHERITED SIMULATED LAW

Sullivan makes a constitutionally vitiated reasonable-doubt standard structural error. First decide whether each whole charge has that defect; harmless-error review cannot cure an instruction once Sullivan's trigger is met.

**[A vitiated jury reasonable-doubt standard is structural error](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#a-vitiated-jury-reasonable-doubt-standard-is-structural-error)** — Effective: 1993-06-01; Sullivan v. Louisiana, for the components identified above.

An unconstitutional reasonable-doubt instruction vitiating the governing standard for all factual findings leaves no constitutionally valid jury verdict of guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. The defect requires reversal without harmless-error assessment; an appellate hypothetical verdict cannot replace the jury determination. Other instructional mistakes are not thereby classified as structural, and collateral-review gates remain separate.

**[Estelle v. McGuire, 502 U.S. 62 (1991) — Whether the prior-act instruction created a reasonable likelihood that jurors bypassed elemental proof](inherited/HOLDINGS.md#whether-the-prior-act-instruction-created-a-reasonable-likelihood-that-jurors-bypassed-elemental-proof)** — Effective: December 4, 1991.

An ambiguous instruction violates due process only when, read with the charge and trial as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood the jury applied it in a way that relieved the State of proving an element. This instruction did not create that likelihood because it conditioned any inference on the jury's finding that McGuire committed the prior acts and expressly prohibited a bad-character inference.

**Unresolved boundary:** The decision does not approve every prior-act instruction or decide a general propensity-evidence rule. Because the controlling majority finds no constitutional error, it does not reach Teague, Rose, or Chapman or require harmless-error review.

**[Adult Guidelines ceiling for juvenile detention](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#adult-guidelines-ceiling-for-juvenile-detention)** — Effective: R. L. C., March 24, 1992.

**[Review of Guidelines departures resting on invalid grounds](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#review-of-guidelines-departures-resting-on-invalid-grounds)** — Effective: Williams, March 9, 1992.

A departure based on a factor forbidden by the Commission, or already fully considered in the applicable range, is an incorrect Guidelines application under §3742(f)(1). An express prohibitory policy statement is authoritative guidance in applying the relevant Guideline; the arrest record itself cannot supply a departure ground. Once the challenger establishes reliance on an invalid factor, remand is required unless the party defending the sentence persuades the reviewing court, from the whole record, that the district court would have imposed the same sentence without it. Even if that error was harmless, §3742(f)(2) independently requires a reasonable departure extent justified by valid grounds the sentencing court actually identified. That the court could lawfully have imposed the sentence does not establish either actual causal harmlessness or permission for an appellate court to invent departure grounds.

**[Chemical Waste Management, Inc. v. Hunt, 504 U.S. 334 (1992) — Whether Alabama may impose an additional disposal charge on equivalent hazardous waste because it originated outside the State](inherited/HOLDINGS.md#whether-alabama-may-impose-an-additional-disposal-charge-on-equivalent-hazardous-waste-because-it-originated-outside-the-state)** — Effective: June 1, 1992.

A State may not impose an additional disposal charge on out-of-state hazardous waste that it does not impose on equivalent in-state waste unless it establishes a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve. Alabama's $72-per-ton additional charge fails because the record supplies no origin-linked difference in the relevant risks or costs and adequate evenhanded means remain available; commercial hazardous-waste disposal receives Commerce Clause protection despite the material's dangers.

**Unresolved boundary:** The grant concerns the additional fee alone. The base fee, independent volume cap, other permitting provisions, state constitutional claims and other federal challenges are not adjudicated. The State did not press its fair-share or compensatory-tax theory; amici's congressional-authorization theory was neither decided below nor briefed or argued by the parties. No unlimited right to dispose of hazardous waste, new proof quantum, perfect-certainty requirement or least-imaginable-alternative rule is created.

**[Fort Gratiot Sanitary Landfill, Inc. v. Michigan Department of Natural Resources, 504 U.S. 353 (1992) — Whether county-origin restrictions at private landfills escape Commerce Clause scrutiny because they also exclude other intrastate counties](inherited/HOLDINGS.md#whether-county-origin-restrictions-at-private-landfills-escape-commerce-clause-scrutiny-because-they-also-exclude-other-intrastate-counties)** — Effective: June 1, 1992.

A State's prohibition on a private landfill's acceptance of outside-county waste unless a county plan affirmatively authorizes it discriminates against interstate commerce even when it also excludes other counties within that State and some counties admit imports. The restriction cannot stand unless the State establishes a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve; Michigan has not done so for these Waste Import Restrictions, and planning waste services does not convert regulation of a private facility into market participation.

**Unresolved boundary:** Only the 1988 Waste Import Restrictions in §§299.413a and 299.430(2), as applied to private landfills, are invalidated. The earlier planning statute, public-facility practices, neutral health, safety and capacity rules, different waste-risk classifications, unconditional operation and genuinely applicable congressional authorization remain outside the decision. No hazardous-waste question arises. Distinct due-process, taking and ownership-transfer licensing matters are not reopened.

**Open Point:** Whether Oregon identifies an equivalent intrastate burden on substantially equivalent events, rather than invoking general taxes or origin alone.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The state court accepted the fee’s asserted nexus to actual public costs. Oregon argues that residents already support waste programs and that the differential prevents them from subsidizing outsiders’ consumption of scarce landfill capacity. It does not establish that an out-of-state ton itself creates greater costs. [R17](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/93/)

Petitioners emphasize the express geographic classification, the smaller corresponding domestic charge, and the nonequivalence of earning income and disposing of waste. They concede that interstate businesses must pay their fair share and that evenhanded environmental regulation remains permissible. A compensatory justification is a defense to discrimination, not a reason to deny that discrimination exists. [R17](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/93/)

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If no equivalent intrastate burden or origin-independent extra cost is shown, reverse and remand for relief from the discriminatory differential. If a legally adequate compensatory relationship is established, uphold that limited justification. No path requires a constitutional right to free waste disposal or invalidation of all Oregon environmental charges. Any repayment issue remains subject to the proper remedial source and record.
