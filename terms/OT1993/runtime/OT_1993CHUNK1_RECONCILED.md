## 3. Harris v. Forklift Systems — November 9, 1993

The trial court's close-case determination materially used the erroneous injury standard. Forklift's alternative claim that independent findings sustain judgment does not remove that dependence. ## 7. United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property — December 13, 1993

**Pre-seizure process.** Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg affirm the hearing ruling. Absent demonstrated exigency, the Government must give notice and a meaningful opportunity to be heard before taking real property for civil forfeiture. This rule covers real property generally, not only owner-occupied homes. Under Mathews, control and rents are substantial property interests; an adversarial opportunity can expose defenses not answered by probable cause or the prior conviction; immovable land can ordinarily be secured without immediate seizure. To establish exigency the Government must show that less restrictive measures such as a lis pendens, restraining order, or bond would not prevent sale, destruction, or continued unlawful use. No such showing is established here. Criminal forfeiture is reserved.

O'Connor, Scalia, and Thomas would reverse the pre-seizure ruling, but do not share every ground. O'Connor accepts Mathews as a proper framework and considers the warrant, conviction, continued occupancy, and public enforcement interests sufficient on these facts; she also reads Calero-Toledo and the forfeiture tradition as supporting delayed process. Scalia relies principally on historically established sovereign forfeiture procedures and the protection afforded by the warrant, with Gerstein and the traditional in rem cases supporting sufficiency in this context. This is not a proposition that the Fourth Amendment automatically displaces every due-process claim; Soldal remains binding. Thomas's sufficient ground is expressly confined to the conviction, warrant, and absence of physical dispossession on this record. He does not treat the historical fiction as an unlimited justification for the breadth of section 881(a)(7). The last two limitations preserve the frozen narrower positions rather than inventing a collective dissent rationale.
