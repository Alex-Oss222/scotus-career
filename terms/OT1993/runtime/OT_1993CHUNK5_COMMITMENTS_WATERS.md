## Components and preserved record

The case concerns an at-will public nurse's First Amendment claim on defendants' summary-judgment motion. Lack of tenure does not extinguish protection against retaliation for protected speech. Property-based entitlement to a hearing is a different question. The Seventh Circuit required ascertainment of the actual disputed speech before the Pickering/Connick balance; it expressly rejected a separate First Amendment pretermination-hearing entitlement. A reversal of its actual-speech rule therefore cannot be justified by attributing an unadopted hearing rule to it.

There are at least three distinct inquiries: what speech and context are used in applying the legal protection rule; whether the employer genuinely acted on that account rather than protected criticism; and, for individual damages, whether the violated rule was clearly established at the time. The reasonableness of a disruption prediction also differs from the reasonableness of an inquiry into what was said.

Ballew reported a long conversation criticizing Waters and obstetrics management and discouraging Perkins-Graham's transfer. Perkins-Graham described adverse comments about Waters, the earlier evaluation, management and Davis. Churchill instead describes patient-care objections to cross-training and inadequate staffing, says she defended Waters and encouraged transfer, and admits criticizing Davis's staffing policies. Koch and Welty corroborate only the portions each heard. Neither account is a finding. Private delivery and personal dissatisfaction do not automatically remove a genuine patient-care issue from public concern; nor does mentioning public hospital administration automatically protect every personal grievance or disruptive manner.

Before discharge Waters reconfirmed Ballew's account; Waters and Davis interviewed Perkins-Graham. They did not interview Churchill, Koch or Welty. During the grievance Hopper heard Churchill, reviewed the supervisors' written reports and had Ballew reinterviewed. Those acts are established, but their description does not establish every surrounding fact bearing on reliability or good faith. Churchill alleges Ballew's bias and retaliatory management hostility; the packet does not establish what each decisionmaker knew of bias. Earlier staffing criticism, changing evaluations and the cesarean-section dispute may support competing inferences about motive. There is no finding that those inferences are true or that the employer would actually have discharged Churchill without protected criticism.

Pickering supplies context-sensitive balancing, not an all-purpose factual-error rule. Connick makes public concern a legal question assessed from content, form and context; it gives substantial weight to reasonable disruption judgments where the actual working relationship needs trust, while reserving a stronger justification burden for more significant public speech. Givhan rejects a categorical exclusion of privately delivered speech and preserves manner, time and place as balancing factors. Mt. Healthy puts the initial protected-substantial-or-motivating-factor showing on the employee and permits the employer to prove by a preponderance the same decision absent protected conduct. Merely identifying a possible lawful reason is not that proof.

## Enhanced lawful alternatives

**Actual-speech path:** Affirm the appellate reversal of summary judgment and remand to establish material disputed speech and context, then apply the legal balance and causation rule. Protection attaches to the citizen employee's actual expression; a mistaken report should not convert protected criticism into a lawful ground for discharge. The strongest objection is that constitutional damages could follow an unavoidable, reasonable factual mistake and deter management even when an employer acts responsibly. On this path context-sensitive protection, the causation defense, individual immunity and ordinary remedies address that burden without altering what speech the First Amendment protects. This path creates no property right, mandatory interview or trial-like investigation, and does not decide personal liability on this record.

**Reasonably ascertained-account path:** Vacate the appellate judgment insofar as it makes the ultimately proven words invariably controlling, and remand. An employer may evaluate speech on a genuinely held account reached through an objectively reasonable inquiry suited to the employment setting. It must reasonably ascertain the material speech and context when their character matters to First Amendment protection; the standard is not judicial factfinding, formal evidence rules, compulsory interviews of every witness, or guaranteed accuracy. The strongest objections are the actual-speech path's risk of lost protection through hearsay and the genuine-belief path's objection that this creates constitutional procedure without a property right. The response ties factual care to the substantive protection for speech, while permitting ordinary responsible management methods and reasonable disruption forecasts. Reasonable inquiry is not a defense to knowingly retaliating for protected speech.

The record presents significant inquiry evidence in the supervisors' confirmation and second-witness interview and Hopper's subsequent hearing of Churchill. Omission of a particular interview alone does not establish constitutional inadequacy. Conversely, the bounded source does not establish all circumstances needed to adjudicate reasonableness as a matter of law, including the relevance and known reliability of conflicting accounts. The four commitments adopting this path leave application to remand with the correct standard, taking the summary-judgment posture seriously. A post-discharge grievance's adequacy and significance must be evaluated for the challenged action and actor; a later interview does not automatically erase an earlier violation. No conclusion that this investigation necessarily failed is frozen.

**Genuine-ground path:** Vacate the appellate actual-speech rule and remand for legal protection and actual causal ground. A public employer does not retaliate against protected expression by honestly discharging an employee for reported speech that the Constitution permits it to discipline; the First Amendment does not independently impose a reasonable-investigation duty. The strongest objection is that this may reward avoidable ignorance and turn an untested report into a means of silencing criticism. On this path actual belief and motive remain contested facts, selective investigation can be evidence of pretext, and protected speech that actually motivates discharge remains subject to Mt. Healthy. Honest belief is not a defense if the employer's believed account itself describes protected speech under the legal balance. Nor does a permissible reason establish that it was the actual reason.

**Common remand boundary:** None of the three paths reinstates the district court's categorical equation of private conversation with unprotected speech. Nor does any path direct a verdict for Churchill, hold every reported statement unprotected, or find all described disruption sufficient. The court must apply the legal balance to the relevant account and full context, separating actual duties, working relationships and supported disruption from managerial sensitivity. Each path preserves the distinct allegation that earlier or contemporaneous patient-care criticism, rather than the reported personal attack, actually caused dismissal.

## Immunity, reach and remedy

All eight preserve independent consideration of the individual defendants' damages immunity on remand. Actual Elder requires considering all relevant date-eligible authority, rather than only authorities presented below, and supplies no mandatory merits-first order or automatic trial rule. Harlow predates the conduct. Rankin (June 24, 1987) and Anderson (June 25, 1987) postdate both challenged employment actions. Their later doctrinal relevance does not establish earlier notice. Any new reasonable-inquiry rule stated in 1994 cannot itself establish that rule's clarity in January or February 1987. The actual-speech proponents likewise do not equate a general right to discuss patient care with an already clearly established answer to every reasonable-factual-error question.

This reservation does not erase clearly established protection against actual retaliation for protected speech: motive and what the decisionmaker believed remain material to the right at the appropriate level of specificity. The bounded record does not justify separate final immunity judgments for Waters, Davis or Hopper, whose actions and knowledge must be considered individually. Municipal liability is not derivative qualified immunity; the policy/custom question was omitted from the petition and is not reached. The separately dismissed expressive-association claim is not reopened. No damages sum, reinstatement order, factual credibility determination or final protected-speech verdict follows from these commitments.
