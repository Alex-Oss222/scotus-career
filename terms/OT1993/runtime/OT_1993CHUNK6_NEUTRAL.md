#### INHERITED SIMULATED LAW

Helling recognizes unreasonable future risks and Wilson's separate subjective requirement; Hudson concerns malicious force. Neither supplies the later Farmer definition of deliberate indifference. The precise awareness/inference standard remains this case's question.

**[Serious future health risks in prison conditions](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#serious-future-health-risks-in-prison-conditions)** — Effective: 1993-06-18; Helling v. McKinney, for the components identified above.

A prisoner may establish an Eighth Amendment violation through deliberate indifference to involuntary exposure posing unreasonable risk of serious future harm, without waiting for existing disease. The prisoner must prove objective gravity, including seriousness, likelihood and contemporary society's unwillingness to tolerate the risk, and the separate subjective culpability requirement under Wilson. Current conditions govern prospective relief; no later Farmer formulation or automatic relief from exposure alone enters.

**[Physical injury in a nontrivial malicious custodial beating](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#physical-injury-in-a-nontrivial-malicious-custodial-beating)** — Effective: *Hudson*, February 25, 1992.

A nontrivial beating inflicted maliciously and without disciplinary justification on a restrained convicted prisoner violates the Eighth Amendment despite limited resulting physical injury when the established conduct satisfies *Whitley*'s demanding malicious-and-sadistic inquiry. Injury remains relevant evidence and bears on compensation; accidental harm or merely technical battery is insufficient. This rule identifies a sufficient constitutional violation and does not impose a universal mental-state standard for all prison-force claims.

**Unresolved boundary:** A comprehensive mental-state rule for other force settings, psychological injury alone, and a general definition of punishment covering isolated unauthorized assaults remain unresolved. Medical-care, confinement-condition, personal-defense, and compensation rules remain distinct.

##### Issue 1: Scope of the turnover warning duty

**Controlling Simulated Law / Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the case-specific inherited-law rules and limits above to this issue. Other eligible authorities below remain effective only to the extent consistent with those simulated holdings and any actual earlier effective OT1993 ruling.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** LHWCA §5(b), 33 U.S.C. §905(b), permits an injured longshoreman’s negligence claim against a vessel while retaining the employer’s statutory compensation structure.
