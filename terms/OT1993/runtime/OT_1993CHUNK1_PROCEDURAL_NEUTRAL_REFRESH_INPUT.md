##### Issue 1: Prospective channel and retroactive punishment

**Controlling Simulated Law / Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the case-specific inherited-law rules and limits above to this issue. Other eligible authorities below remain effective only to the extent consistent with those simulated holdings and any actual earlier effective OT1993 ruling.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** U.S. Const. art. I, §10; Weaver v. #### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Tennessee Supreme Court, State v. Middlebrooks, 840 S.W.2d 317 (1992), affirmed the felony-murder and aggravated-kidnapping convictions but set aside the death sentence and ordered resentencing. It rested its aggravator ruling on both the Eighth Amendment and Article I, §16 of the Tennessee Constitution. [P05](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/124/) [P05.1](https://law.justia.com/cases/tennessee/supreme-court/1992/840-s-w-2d-317-2.html)

Middlebrooks seeks to preserve the new sentencing hearing; his guilt judgments and other rejected claims are not reopened by the State’s petition. [P05](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/124/) [P05.1](https://law.justia.com/cases/tennessee/supreme-court/1992/840-s-w-2d-317-2.html)

**Material Record:** The jury convicted Middlebrooks of felony murder and aggravated kidnapping, but acquitted on premeditated murder and other charged offenses. It found two aggravators: murder involving torture and murder committed during a felony. The state supreme court concluded that Tennessee’s broad felony-murder definition did not itself narrow death eligibility and that using the same felony again failed to narrow. It held that Article I, §16 independently prohibited the duplication, distinguished Louisiana’s narrower statute in Lowenfield, and could not find the extra aggravator harmless beyond a reasonable doubt. The State remained free to seek death at a lawful resentencing. [P05](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/124/) [P05.1](https://law.justia.com/cases/tennessee/supreme-court/1992/840-s-w-2d-317-2.html)

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Section 1257 requires a reviewable final state judgment and a federal question capable of affecting it. Michigan v. Long, 463 U.S. 1032 (1983), governs federal/state-ground ambiguity; an adequate and independent state ground defeats Supreme Court review even if federal reasoning is discussed. Resentencing also raises finality under Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn, 420 U.S. 469 (1975). The independent-ground question may dispose of review without deciding which Cox exception would otherwise apply. [P05](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/124/) [P05.1](https://law.justia.com/cases/tennessee/supreme-court/1992/840-s-w-2d-317-2.html)

#### INHERITED SIMULATED LAW

Address the independent-state-ground threshold before the federal narrowing question. Arave concerns facial eligibility language and does not establish that duplication of felony murder is valid on this record.

**[Sochor v. Florida, 504 U.S. 527 (1992) — Whether Florida's express failure-to-object ground forecloses review of the jury-heinousness instruction despite alternative merits discussion](inherited/HOLDINGS.md#whether-floridas-express-failure-to-object-ground-forecloses-review-of-the-jury-heinousness-instruction-despite-alternative-merits-discussion)** — Effective: June 8, 1992.

For the judicial aggravator, general advisory recommendation and sentence-correction holdings in *Sochor v. Florida*, June 8, 1992, No. 91-5843, see Criminal Procedure — Capital sentencing.

The Court cannot review a federal challenge to a penalty instruction when the state judgment clearly rests on an adequate independent failure-to-object ground, even if the state court also rejects the claim on its merits. Florida's express preservation ruling forecloses this jury-heinousness challenge; no sufficient basis to reject its adequacy or independence is established.

**Unresolved boundary:** The Court does not decide the instruction's constitutional merits or foreclose a supported challenge to a different procedural ground. This direct-review holding creates no habeas exception.

**[Facial capital-aggravator formulation and application review](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#facial-capital-aggravator-formulation-and-application-review)** — Effective: 1993-03-30; Arave v. Creech, for the components identified above.

Idaho's utter-disregard aggravator is facially sufficient only as authoritatively narrowed to the cold-blooded, pitiless slayer who kills without feeling or sympathy, guiding and genuinely narrowing its broad first-degree-murder class. Unqualified statutory words are not approved. A facial challenge can test consistency and sufficiency of the formulation; inconsistent application of a valid formulation is distinct. Under Jeffers, constitutional application review asks whether no reasonable sentencer could find the aggravator. Facial validity establishes no particular defendant's eligibility and supplies no cure for a separate weighing error.

**[Invalid aggravators in capital weighing and correction of sentencing error](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#invalid-aggravators-in-capital-weighing-and-correction-of-sentencing-error)** — Effective: *Espinosa*, June 29, 1992, extending the rule to the recommendation's legally required influence. The earlier direct-weighing rule remains supplied by *Stringer*, March 9, 1992.

Remaining valid aggravators do not alone cure a constitutional weighing error, although an invalid weighing factor does not automatically negate death eligibility if valid eligibility findings remain. The state judicial system must provide genuine reweighing without the invalid factor where state law authorizes it, or constitutional harmless-error review proving beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not affect the sentence. Bare factor counting, proportionality review or a harmless-error label supplies neither cure; no particular verbal formula is indispensable if the substantive finding is actually made. A jury's inadequately narrowed aggravating instruction can infect the final death sentence when the judge must give the resulting recommendation great weight, even if the judge directly finds only valid aggravators. Correction must address that transmitted influence. If no lawful cure is available, a new sentencing proceeding is required. Neither automatic life imprisonment nor disturbance of guilt follows.

##### Issue 2: Death eligibility and sentence consequence

**Controlling Simulated Law / Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the case-specific inherited-law rules and limits above to this issue. Other eligible authorities below remain effective only to the extent consistent with those simulated holdings and any actual earlier effective OT1993 ruling.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Furman, Gregg, Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983), and Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231 (1988), require genuinely narrowed eligibility; Lowenfield permits the narrowing to occur through the offense definition rather than at sentencing and does not prohibit every overlap.
