## Statute and complete boundaries

The charge is former §857(a)(1)'s use of the Postal Service or another interstate conveyance as part of a scheme to sell drug paraphernalia, and §371 conspiracy; the 1990 successor §863 is not substituted. Section 857(b)'s maximum is three years and $100,000, not Acty's aggregate sentence on distinct counts. Section 857(d) defines equipment, products and material primarily intended or designed for manufacturing, compounding, converting, concealing, producing, processing, preparing, injecting, ingesting, inhaling or otherwise introducing a controlled substance whose possession is unlawful under the Controlled Substances Act. Its examples include the listed pipes and water pipes, carburetion devices and masks, roach clips, miniature spoons, chamber/carburetor/electric/air-driven pipes, chillums, bongs, ice pipes/chillers, wired cigarette papers and cocaine-freebase kits. The complete source text governs the detailed examples; they are not a finding that every object bearing one ordinary name is criminal merchandise regardless of context.

Section 857(e) permits, in addition to other logically relevant evidence, consideration of instructions, accompanying descriptive material, advertising, sale display, legitimate related supply business, direct or circumstantial sales-ratio evidence, legitimate community uses and expert testimony. None of those eight considerations is individually mandatory. Section 857(f)(1) independently exempts persons authorized by local, state or federal law to manufacture, possess or distribute the items. Section 857(f)(2) separately exempts an item imported, exported, transported or sold through mail or other means in the normal lawful course of business and traditionally intended for tobacco use, including a pipe, paper or accessory. The 1988 change from primarily to traditionally belongs only to that tobacco exception. No seller obtains immunity for an entire enterprise merely because some ordinary merchandise or some tobacco products are sold there.

Staples now requires factual knowledge of the characteristics selecting its particular machinegun, without legal or registration knowledge. Its contextual rule supports separating objective item classification from culpability here; it does not import a gun-specific result. Ginsburg/O'Connor's narrower factual-knowledge concurrence and Stevens/Blackmun's dangerous-device dissent are public positions, not new law. Ratzlaf's express-willfulness and illegality holding is different, while its and Granderson's residual-lenity rules remain operative after ordinary interpretation.

## Alternatives and disposition components

**Objective classification plus knowledge:** Affirm the reviewed convictions and reject the as-applied vagueness claim. Designed for use concerns objective design; primarily intended concerns the item's likely use in the relevant circumstances rather than the seller's personally desired purpose. The source's lawful-use evidence and sale context remain relevant. The prosecution must separately establish that the defendant knowingly conducted the prohibited business and knew the items were likely to be used with illegal drugs; it need not prove knowledge of the legal label drug paraphernalia, the precise statute, or a particular buyer's identity or actual later use. Gypsum and Bailey distinguish knowledge of probable use from a purpose to bring it about; Hamling distinguishes factual character from legal classification. Staples reinforces factual culpability. This construction preserves innocence protection without turning the item definition into a second subjective-purpose element.

**Subjective-purpose alternative:** Petitioners would require their own purpose that purchasers use the articles unlawfully. The strongest support is the word intended and the risk to legitimate sellers of dual-use goods. The strongest objection is that this confuses the object's primary intended use or design with a distinct seller mental state and diminishes the statutory objective evidence, examples and tobacco exception. The objective-plus-knowledge construction answers the innocence concern while preserving both definition alternatives. After ordinary interpretation it leaves no residual ambiguity requiring the added purpose element. A strict-liability reading is likewise rejected: objective classification alone cannot establish knowing criminal participation.

**Instruction and vagueness:** The actual charge required knowing interstate-conveyance participation, covered articles, and knowledge of their nature, character and use. It did not omit the culpability required by the adopted construction; a subjective-purpose instruction was unnecessary. This is not a harmless-error ruling excusing an omitted necessary finding. The bongs, roach clips, drug-named kits, advertising and business presentation place these petitioners within discernible coverage. Hoffman Estates requires attention to their actual application; Kolender and Grayned preserve notice and enforcement constraints. The list, objective evidence, exceptions and knowledge requirement sufficiently constrain this application. Ordinary merchants selling only scales, mirrors or razor blades present different facts, and no protected-expression premise authorizes facial overbreadth adjudication here.

**Other counts:** Do not reach the cocaine aiding instruction-presumption argument, which was neither raised nor decided below; the laundering, investment and proceeds issues omitted from the certiorari petition; or the petition's cocaine-sufficiency question not developed in the merits brief. These separate preservation limitations do not create merits holdings. No change to the aggregate sentence or unrelated convictions follows from affirmance on the questions reviewed.
