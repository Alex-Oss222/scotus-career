**Relief Requested:** Fogerty seeks reversal of the fee denial and argues for equal treatment, including an alternative claim for ordinarily automatic prevailing-party recovery. Fantasy seeks affirmance of the dual standard. [R13](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/517/)

**Material Record:** Fogerty prevailed against Fantasy’s copyright claim that a later composition copied an earlier work whose copyright Fantasy owned. The infringement judgment is not being retried. The district court denied fees because the action was not frivolous or brought in bad faith, applying a more restrictive standard to a successful defendant. #### INHERITED SIMULATED LAW

Dague and Farrar supply bounded fee principles under other statutes. Neither imports a plaintiff/defendant asymmetry or an automatic award into Copyright Act §505.

**[No contingency enhancement under the environmental fee provisions](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#no-contingency-enhancement-under-the-environmental-fee-provisions)** — Effective: *Dague*, June 24, 1992.

A reasonable fee under 42 U.S.C. §6972(e) and 33 U.S.C. §1365(d) may not be enhanced to compensate contingency risk. A market-access rationale supplies no exception. The ordinary reasonable-hours-and-rate lodestar, applicant's ordinary burden and prohibition against double counting remain in force.

**[Nominal civil-rights relief and reasonable fees](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#nominal-civil-rights-relief-and-reasonable-fees)** — Effective: 1992-12-14; Farrar v. Hobby, for the components identified above.

An enforceable nominal-damages judgment directly benefiting the plaintiff by changing the fee defendant's legal obligation establishes prevailing-party eligibility under §1988. Magnitude bears on amount, not that eligibility. Reasonable fees require an explained Hensley assessment of actual success and unrelated unsuccessful work, permitting reduction to zero without mechanical proportionality to damages or a presumption of substantial fees. Correcting an appellate eligibility error does not itself fix the amount or automatically reinstate a prior award.

##### Issue 1: Evenhanded discretionary fee shifting

**Controlling Simulated Law / Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the case-specific inherited-law rules and limits above to this issue. Other eligible authorities below remain effective only to the extent consistent with those simulated holdings and any actual earlier effective OT1993 ruling.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Section 505 permits the court, in its discretion, to award reasonable attorney’s fees to the prevailing party. The American Rule and Alyeska Pipeline, 421 U.S. 240 (1975), establish the need for authorization. Christiansburg Garment, 434 U.S. 412 (1978), adopts an asymmetric standard under a different civil-rights statute and rationale; Hensley, 461 U.S. 424 (1983), concerns reasonable fee administration. Copyright protects authorship while preserving lawful public use and defenses. [R13](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/517/)

**Open Point:** Whether the text and copyright scheme support asymmetric treatment or automatic recovery.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Ninth Circuit and Fantasy rely on encouraging meritorious infringement suits and analogize to civil-rights fee doctrine. Fogerty argues that successful defenses also clarify copyright boundaries and that the same statutory term cannot justify routine plaintiff awards but defendant awards only for misconduct. [R13](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/517/)

Fogerty’s automatic-fee alternative faces the statute’s discretionary language and the American Rule. Equal eligibility does not require identical amounts or prevent courts from considering litigation conduct, objective weakness, or deterrence on a case-specific basis. [R13](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/517/)

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Rejecting asymmetry supports reversal and a fresh discretionary fee determination, not a directed award. Adopting automatic fee shifting would eliminate discretion the text expressly retains. Upholding asymmetry would affirm the legal standard without revisiting infringement. [R13](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/517/)

**Question Presented:** How the four statutory fair-use factors apply to a commercial parody, including commercial purpose, the amount copied for identification and criticism, and proof of market harm.

**Question Granted / Review Scope:** The issue stated above is reconstructed from the primary record. A separately authenticated petition question and in-world grant order were not furnished; no broader question or verbatim grant wording is represented.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit, 972 F.2d 1429 (1992), reversing summary judgment that had found 2 Live Crew’s version a fair use. [R14](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/)

**Relief Requested:** Campbell and the other defendants seek reversal of the Sixth Circuit’s treatment of commercial parody; Acuff-Rose seeks to preserve its infringement claim. Whether defendants have established entitlement to summary judgment is distinct from whether the appellate legal presumptions were sound. [R14](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/)

**Material Record:** 2 Live Crew released a parody of the copyrighted composition Oh, Pretty Woman after requesting permission and being refused. The work copied identifiable musical and lyrical features and then departed from them. The district court found fair use; the appellate court treated commerciality as presumptively unfair, emphasized copying the work’s heart, and presumed market harm. The summary-judgment record did not sufficiently address the potentially distinct market for a nonparody rap derivative. [R14](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/)

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Fair use is an affirmative defense on which defendants bear the burden. The proceeding reviews summary judgment, so an erroneous legal presumption does not allow the Court to invent missing market evidence or conclusively resolve disputed questions. No permission requirement is silently added to §107. [R14](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/)

##### Issue 1: Statutory fair use

**Controlling Simulated Law / Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the case-specific inherited-law rules and limits above to this issue. Other eligible authorities below remain effective only to the extent consistent with those simulated holdings and any actual earlier effective OT1993 ruling.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** 17 U.S.C. §107 requires consideration of purpose and character, including commerciality; the copyrighted work’s nature; amount and substantiality used; and effect on the potential market or value. Sony Corp. v. Universal City Studios, 464 U.S. 417 (1984), and Harper & Row v. Nation Enterprises, 471 U.S. 539 (1985), supply prior applications. Each factor belongs to an integrated inquiry, not a categorical commercial-use prohibition. [R14](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/)

**Open Point:** The proper weight of transformative parody, justified borrowing, and market presumptions when the secondary work criticizes the original.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The defendants argue that recognizable borrowing is necessary for parody and that commentary replacing the original’s message serves a different purpose. They distinguish harm from criticism, which copyright does not protect against, from substitutive market injury. Commercial sale remains relevant and parody does not license unlimited copying. [R14](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/)

Acuff-Rose argues that defendants exploited the original’s valuable musical core and could harm licensed derivative markets. The Sixth Circuit’s reasoning favors that position through commercial and market presumptions. A copyright holder cannot establish cognizable harm merely by pointing to a market for suppressing criticism or by withholding permission, but a supported nonparody derivative-market concern remains material. [R14](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/)

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Rejecting categorical presumptions warrants reversal and remand for the integrated §107 inquiry. The Court could conclude a portion of borrowing necessary to conjure the original while leaving the extent of repetition and derivative-market evidence unresolved. A blanket parody safe harbor and automatic reinstatement of summary judgment are not consequences of correcting the legal standard. [R14](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/)
