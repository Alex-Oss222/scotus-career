#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Review Posture and Requested Relief:** Third Circuit, 995 F.2d 404 (1993), reversed the district court’s dismissal to permit review of alleged procedural violations concerning selection of the Philadelphia Naval Shipyard for closure. The district court had treated the challenged selection as unreviewable. The federal defendants seek reversal and dismissal of the challenge to implementation of the approved closure selection. Respondents seek an injunction against the Secretary’s implementation based on alleged defects in the selection process. [P25](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/462/)

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Third Circuit’s judgment permitting this challenge to the approved shipyard-closure selection.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for dismissal of the claim seeking to block implementation on the alleged selection-process defects. Preserve only legally distinct claims and the Act’s express implementation-review provisions; award no advisory injunction.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Review channel and scope of statutory preclusion

**Entering Law:** APA §§701(a), 702, and 704 distinguish reviewable final agency action, preclusion by statute, and action committed to agency discretion. Simulated Franklin v. Massachusetts (June 26, 1992) holds the completed census determination final and reviewable despite the President’s later transmission; it leaves the President’s own APA status open and distinguishes a genuinely independent presidential decision. Apply that simulated holding to this different statutory structure; the historical contrary finality rule is not entering law. Bowen v. Michigan Academy of Family Physicians, 476 U.S. 667 (1986), supplies a strong presumption of review; Block v. Community Nutrition Institute, 467 U.S. 340 (1984), permits preclusion discerned from statutory structure. The 1990 Act’s §§2903-2905 and 2908 prescribe rigid package-approval deadlines, temporary Commission review, mandatory implementation, congressional review, and a separately confined NEPA implementation-review channel. Youngstown, 343 U.S. 579 (1952), does not turn every alleged statutory violation into a constitutional claim. [P25](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/462/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain the narrow statutory-preclusion disposition only with the scheme-specific explanation intact.

The supplied simulated *Franklin* held the completed census determination reviewable despite later presidential transmission and left the President's APA status open. Historical *Franklin* cannot replace it. The independent presidential package decision here is materially different, but Stone's proposal can avoid deciding a broad final-agency-action rule by resolving this requested relief under §701(a)(1). [Closure scheme and claim, 511 U.S. 462](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/462/).

This is **a substantial, contestable application of implied preclusion**, not something “national defense” or presidential involvement automatically establishes. The integrated deadlines, temporary Commission, all-or-nothing approval, congressional disapproval opportunity, mandatory implementation, and expressly confined implementation review together support the conclusion. Each alone is insufficient under the strong review presumption in *Michigan Academy*.

Stone's accountability and independent-law commitments create real tension: procedural duties can be underenforced when their breach cannot undo an approved closure. The strongest contrary reading is that judicial enforcement of Congress's own procedures complements rather than supplants the package design. Stone should explain why the relief sought would reopen final selection in a way the enacted architecture forecloses, while not using that consequence to erase independently enforceable duties at every earlier stage.

Application must identify the timing, duty, defendant, and requested relief. A postapproval demand to remove a selected base differs from timely access to a mandatory public meeting or a claim that the President added an unauthorized base. Preserve genuine constitutional and substantive ultra vires questions without assuming each has a cause or remedy. Calling an ordinary statutory error unconstitutional adds nothing.

Reverse the order allowing this selection challenge, preserve the express NEPA implementation channel, and avoid declaring all antecedent conduct unreviewable. This accepts a limited statutory constraint on review, not a general retreat from Stone's opposition to executive self-expansion.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** The Defense Base Closure and Realignment Act of 1990 assigns recommendations to the Secretary and Commission, an all-or-nothing approval decision to the President, and a time-limited opportunity for congressional disapproval. The shipyard was included in the approved package. Respondents allege failures in the antecedent statutory procedures, not that the President added a base absent from the Commission’s list. They did not plead a separate constitutional claim against the President or name him as a defendant. No court has found the asserted selection-process violations in a merits judgment under review. Separate APA final agency action, statutory preclusion, and a genuinely constitutional or ultra vires claim. Calling an alleged violation of the Act unconstitutional does not supply a new cause of action. These respondents seek to undo an approved base selection, not timely enforcement of a still-operative meeting-access or publication requirement. Military subject matter alone does not establish a political question. [P25](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/462/)
