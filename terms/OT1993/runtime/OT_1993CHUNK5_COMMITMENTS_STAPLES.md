## Entering law and component boundaries

Section 5861(d) prohibits receiving or possessing a firearm not registered to the possessor. Section 5845(a)(6) includes a machinegun, and §5845(b)'s pertinent definition includes a weapon that shoots, is designed to shoot, or can be readily restored to shoot automatically more than one shot, without manual reloading, through a single trigger function. The ten-year maximum under §5871 matters to statutory culpability; Staples's actual probation and fine do not replace the statutory consequence. The case concerns the complete rifle and knowledge of its automatic-fire characteristics. It does not settle every frame, receiver, or parts-combination alternative.

Three distinct matters must remain distinct: intentional possession of the object; knowledge of the physical characteristic placing it in the regulated class; and knowledge of nonregistration, the registration duty, or illegality. Neither lawful path below requires proof that the accused read or knew the statute. Ratzlaf's willfulness holding is confined to the charged financial provisions; its residual-lenity ground and Granderson's residual penal-construction ground operate after ordinary interpretation. Thompson/Center concerns making, not the knowledge component here.

The AR-15's ability to fire automatically is supported by the agents' testing. Staples's contrary account of his experience is testimony, not an accepted finding of his knowledge or ignorance. The removed stop and substituted components may support circumstantial proof; the model makes no ultimate evidentiary finding. The trial instruction expressly made knowledge of the regulated characteristics unnecessary. The preserved statutory instruction question is reachable; no constitutional gun-possession claim or due-process invalidity claim is decided.

## Enhanced comparison of lawful paths

**Characteristics-knowledge path:** Reverse and remand because the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that Staples knew the characteristics making this rifle a machinegun. Morissette and Gypsum preserve factual culpability despite statutory silence; Liparota cautions against sweeping apparently innocent conduct into serious criminal liability. Freed does not settle this different knowledge question: it involved knowing possession of grenades and rejected knowledge of nonregistration. A normal semiautomatic rifle is an object commonly possessed lawfully, so knowing possession of a gun alone does not establish awareness of the physical feature selecting the narrow statutory class. The strongest objection is that the statute is a regulatory prohibition involving dangerous weapons and contains no express mental-state word. Balint and Freed make that objection lawful, but the rifle's ordinary lawful identity and the substantial penalty support retaining knowledge of the distinguishing fact. The narrower rule requires factual knowledge only, permits circumstantial proof, and creates neither a general firearm exemption nor a constitutional rule for all serious offenses.

**Dangerous-device path:** Affirm because knowing, voluntary possession of an object recognized as a dangerous weapon gives the notice appropriate to this regulatory offense; proof of every characteristic triggering registration is unnecessary. Balint, International Minerals, and Freed permit knowledge of the dangerous class rather than each regulatory fact. This path does not punish a person who reasonably thinks the object is a toy or an innocuous case. Its strongest objection is that it exposes an ordinary rifle owner to ten years because of a characteristic the owner may not know. The affirmative answer is statutory: Congress selected the prohibited weapons objectively within a broader class whose known dangerous character alerts possessors to regulation; neither potential punishment nor lawful ownership elsewhere independently supplies an element. The path must still preserve knowing possession and cannot presume that Staples knew the gun's automatic capability. Ratzlaf remains distinct because its express willfulness provision and deliberate structuring question are absent here.

**Remedy consequence:** On the characteristics-knowledge path, reverse the reviewed criminal judgment and remand for proceedings under the correct element. Do not order acquittal, declare evidence insufficient, assume harmlessness, or decide an unpresented count consequence. On the dangerous-device path, affirm on the preserved instruction question. Neither path resolves a search claim or Second Amendment issue.
