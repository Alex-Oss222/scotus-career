#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Remedial discretion and misconduct in the hearing

**Entering Law:** 29 U.S.C. §160(c) authorizes affirmative action, including reinstatement with or without backpay, that effectuates the NLRA’s policies, but bars reinstatement or backpay where an employee was suspended or discharged for cause. Phelps Dodge Corp. v. NLRB, 313 U.S. 177 (1941), and Virginia Electric & Power Co. v. NLRB, 319 U.S. 533 (1943), recognize broad, judicially reviewable Board remedial discretion. Review asks whether the agency acted arbitrarily or outside the statutory purpose, not whether a court would select the same remedy. The for-cause proviso is a substantive limit, not an automatic penalty for every later false statement. Ordinary perjury sanctions remain independently available. [P09](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/317/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain enforcement of the Board's order.

False testimony is serious, but §10(c)'s remedial authority serves the statutory labor policy as well as the employee. *Phelps Dodge* and *Virginia Electric* support agency remedial discretion bounded by the Act. A judicial clean-hands rule that automatically extinguishes relief would add a restriction Congress did not prescribe. [Record, 510 U.S. 317](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/317/).

Stone's opposition to knowing official falsification does not compel the opposite result. Removing a defense from an official whose lie causes an actionable deprivation is a different legal question from using a discharged employee's later lie to erase an already established employer violation. That distinction follows source, causation, and remedy, not different moral standards for favored parties. Simulated *Lechmere* limits agency authority through the statutory construction actually decided there; it does not resolve this remedy.

This is **ordinary application of delegated remedial discretion**. The Board must recognize the misconduct and explain its remedial choice sufficiently for review. It need not treat every lie as legally dispositive, and courts cannot impose a new sanctions formula. Perjury enforcement remains available independently.

Application turns on what the falsehood concerns, whether it undermines the violation finding or a remedial predicate, and whether the chosen relief still effectuates the Act. A lie concealing inability to perform the reinstated job could materially affect relief. Here the hearing falsehood does not reopen the settled unlawful-discharge issue or the distinct original-excuse issue outside review. The institutional cost is potential underdeterrence of litigation dishonesty; the lawful response is genuine discretionary consideration and authorized sanctions, not an automatic windfall for the employer or automatic employee entitlement.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Michael Manso, a casual dockworker in Albuquerque, had repeatedly obtained relief from earlier disciplinary actions through the union. The Board found that ABF used tardiness as a pretext to discharge him for protected activity. Manso falsely attributed a late arrival to automobile trouble and repeated that account under oath before the administrative law judge. The Board knew of the falsehood but did not treat it as a categorical disqualification. The preserved Supreme Court question concerns the hearing testimony, not a fresh determination whether the discharge was unlawful or whether the original excuse independently established a lawful discharge. Review the actual question accepted for merits review. The legality of the discharge and the Board’s remedial authority notwithstanding the earlier false excuse are not reopened. A statutory for-cause limitation differs from an equitable disqualification based on later misconduct. [P09](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/317/)
