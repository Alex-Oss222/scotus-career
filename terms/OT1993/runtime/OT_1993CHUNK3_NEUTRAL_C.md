##### Issue 1: Settlement credit in comparative-fault admiralty

**Controlling Simulated Law / Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the case-specific inherited-law rules and limits above to this issue. Other eligible authorities below remain effective only to the extent consistent with those simulated holdings and any actual earlier effective OT1993 ruling.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** United States v. Reliable Transfer Co., 421 U.S. 397 (1975), replaces equal division with proportionate fault when fault can reasonably be allocated. Edmonds v. Compagnie Generale Transatlantique, 443 U.S. 256 (1979), preserves the relevant joint-and-several liability principle in its statutory setting and did not decide settlement credit. Entering maritime authorities divide among pro tanto credit with contribution against the settler, pro tanto credit with settlement protection, and proportionate-share reduction. The former may require a good-faith settlement inquiry; the latter assigns settlement bargain risk to plaintiff and settler and requires allocation of responsibility. No Supreme Court rule entering this term resolves the choice. [P17](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/202/)

**Open Point:** Which common-law allocation best fits comparative fault without confusing settlement proceeds with the litigating defendant’s adjudicated responsibility.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

McDermott argues that Reliable Transfer requires River Don to pay its found share and that settlements should remain final. Respondents invoke the one-satisfaction principle and contend that allowing settlement proceeds plus a full proportionate judgment can exceed the loss. The SG supports reversal. The response to the latter concern must recognize that settlement estimates can be higher or lower than later adjudicated responsibility; it is not a factual finding that the settlement represented precisely the loss assigned to its parties.
