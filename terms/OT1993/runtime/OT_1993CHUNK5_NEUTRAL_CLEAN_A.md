#### INHERITED SIMULATED LAW

Dewsnup and Nobelman preserve their Chapter 7 and Chapter 13 lien rules. Neither resolves §548 reasonably equivalent value or establishes that every compliant foreclosure price controls fraudulent-transfer review.

**[Chapter 7 strip-down of allowed real-property liens under §506(d)](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#chapter-7-strip-down-of-allowed-real-property-liens-under-506d)** — Effective: *Dewsnup*, January 15, 1992.

When the claim is allowed under §502 and secured by the real-property lien, §506(d) does not strip that lien down merely because §506(a) values the collateral below the allowed debt. Section 506(a) continues to value secured and unsecured amounts for treatment of the claim; that valuation does not automatically avoid the corresponding lien under §506(d). The rule distinguishes personal discharge from lien survival and does not immunize liens from an independently applicable express avoidance provision.

**Unresolved boundary:** Other statutory settings, reorganization restrictions, express statutory exceptions, and estate-administration issues are not resolved by this construction. It does not adopt an abandonment rationale.

**[Principal-residence mortgage rights in Chapter 13](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#principal-residence-mortgage-rights-in-chapter-13)** — Effective: 1993-06-01; Nobelman v. American Savings Bank, for the components identified above.

When an allowed claim secured only by the debtor's principal-residence real property retains a secured portion, §1322(b)(2) protects the holder's mortgage rights as a whole despite §506(a) valuation and bifurcation. The debtor cannot modify the deficiency portion by altering protected repayment or enforcement rights. Independent restrictions and §1322(b)(5) remain: cure within a reasonable time and maintenance during the case are permitted when the last claim payment falls after the final plan payment, not general rewriting. The stay remains distinct. Wholly unsecured liens and classification of ancillary deed provisions are undecided; Dewsnup's Chapter 7 rule remains separate.

**Open Point:** Whether the employer-interest phrase permits the Board’s categorical patient-care exclusion while preserving the Act’s protection of professionals through the remaining statutory distinctions.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Board, supported by the Solicitor General and labor/professional amici, argues that Congress protected professionals despite incidental direction of coworkers and excluded genuine management with divided loyalties. Its contextual reading seeks to prevent the supervisor exception from swallowing professional coverage. [R28](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/571/)

The employer and the Sixth Circuit argue that patient care is the nursing home’s business; treating it as unrelated to the employer creates a false dichotomy and nullifies listed responsible-direction functions. That argument does not establish that any professional instruction satisfies independent judgment and supervisory authority in every case. [R28](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/571/)

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Sustain the Board’s contextual construction as permissible and reverse, or reject its categorical employer-interest rationale and affirm the judgment refusing enforcement. The Court can leave the other statutory requirements meaningful without deciding their full content or declaring all nurses excluded. [R28](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/571/)
