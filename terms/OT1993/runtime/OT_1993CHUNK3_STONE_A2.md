#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand for an evenhanded exercise of §505 discretion.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Reconsider Fogerty’s fee application without a special frivolousness-or-bad-faith prerequisite for defendants. Do not direct an award or amount, and do not reopen the infringement verdict. [R13](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/517/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Evenhanded discretionary fee shifting

**Entering Law:** Section 505 permits the court, in its discretion, to award reasonable attorney’s fees to the prevailing party. The American Rule and Alyeska Pipeline, 421 U.S. 240 (1975), establish the need for authorization. Christiansburg Garment, 434 U.S. 412 (1978), adopts an asymmetric standard under a different civil-rights statute and rationale; Hensley, 461 U.S. 424 (1983), concerns reasonable fee administration. Copyright protects authorship while preserving lawful public use and defenses. [R13](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/517/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain evenhanded discretionary fees and remand.

Section 505 names the prevailing party without prescribing different eligibility rules for plaintiffs and defendants. Successful defenses help maintain copyright's lawful boundary, while successful infringement suits enforce it. A defendant-only frivolousness requirement therefore needs justification the text and copyright scheme do not supply. [Record and fee provision, 510 U.S. 517](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/517/).

This fits Stone's source-specific method, but not a universal preference for symmetrical treatment. *Christiansburg*'s asymmetry reflects a different enforcement setting and concern about chilling civil-rights claims. Simulated *Dague* and *Farrar* likewise retain their statutory contexts. Equal words in different statutes do not require identical fee regimes where the schemes and precedent differ. Stone is **clarifying an open copyright question**, not displacing those decisions.

Lower courts may consider frivolousness, motivation, objective unreasonableness, and compensation or deterrence in service of copyright's purposes. These are reasons guiding discretion, not cumulative elements or a scoring system. A reasonable losing claim can still leave a prevailing defendant eligible; a prevailing plaintiff is not automatically entitled. Do not import later presumptive weight for objective reasonableness.

The strongest counterargument is that fee exposure will discourage authors with uncertain but legitimate claims. That is a reason for measured discretion, equally attentive to defendants who must resist overclaimed rights, rather than a categorical eligibility barrier. The hard boundary is a close case with modest stakes but substantial defense costs. The court should explain why an award would advance the statutory policy and determine a reasonable amount, rather than assume the winner deserves full reimbursement. Remand only the fee application; preserve the infringement verdict.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Fogerty prevailed against Fantasy’s copyright claim that a later composition copied an earlier work whose copyright Fantasy owned. The infringement judgment is not being retried. The district court denied fees because the action was not frivolous or brought in bad faith, applying a more restrictive standard to a successful defendant. [R13](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/517/)

Only fees under the Copyright Act are at issue. A prevailing-party status determination does not itself fix an amount or eliminate statutory discretion. No constitutional speech holding or substantive copyright merits determination is needed. [R13](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/517/)

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit and remand for fair-use analysis under the integrated statutory factors, without directing judgment of noninfringement.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The lower court must evaluate the amount taken in relation to the parodic purpose and the market evidence, including the nonparody derivative market not adequately developed. Defendants retain their affirmative-defense burden. No final royalty, damages, or injunction determination is made. [R14](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Statutory fair use

**Entering Law:** 17 U.S.C. §107 requires consideration of purpose and character, including commerciality; the copyrighted work’s nature; amount and substantiality used; and effect on the potential market or value. Sony Corp. v. Universal City Studios, 464 U.S. 417 (1984), and Harper & Row v. Nation Enterprises, 471 U.S. 539 (1985), supply prior applications. Each factor belongs to an integrated inquiry, not a categorical commercial-use prohibition. [R14](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain reversal and remand under the four statutory factors.

Commerciality is relevant under §107, but does not alone resolve whether a work's critical purpose and use of the original are fair. A parody may need recognizable borrowing; the owner cannot veto criticism simply by refusing a license. This is genuine alignment with Stone's expressive-liberty commitments through the statute Congress enacted, not an invented First Amendment immunity. [Record and factors, 510 U.S. 569](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/).

The proposed account of transformation is **a defensible development within §107**. It explains purpose and character; it neither displaces commerciality nor absorbs amount and market effect. *Sony* and *Harper & Row* must be distinguished by the kind of use and substitution involved rather than dismissed as obsolete.

In application, ask what the secondary work comments on, why the borrowed features are necessary for recognition or criticism, how much repetition serves that purpose, and whether the use substitutes in a cognizable market. Loss of demand because criticism persuades listeners is different from displacement by a competing substitute. The record's possible nonparody rap-derivative market requires attention; label alone cannot eliminate it. The defendants retain the affirmative-defense burden on summary judgment.

The boundary is satire borrowing a song to comment on something else: it lacks parody's particular explanation for taking this original but can still argue fair use on the actual factors. The strongest objection is that “transformative” can become an aesthetic license for judicial preferences. Require an intelligible account of purpose and the relationship between taking and use, without judging artistic merit. Do not grant final noninfringement on an incomplete market record or use later fair-use law to predetermine this case.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** 2 Live Crew released a parody of the copyrighted composition Oh, Pretty Woman after requesting permission and being refused. The work copied identifiable musical and lyrical features and then departed from them. The district court found fair use; the appellate court treated commerciality as presumptively unfair, emphasized copying the work’s heart, and presumed market harm. The summary-judgment record did not sufficiently address the potentially distinct market for a nonparody rap derivative. [R14](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/)

Fair use is an affirmative defense on which defendants bear the burden. The proceeding reviews summary judgment, so an erroneous legal presumption does not allow the Court to invent missing market evidence or conclusively resolve disputed questions. No permission requirement is silently added to §107. [R14](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/)
