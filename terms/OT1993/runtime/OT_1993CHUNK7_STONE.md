#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment sustaining the death sentence and remand for a new penalty proceeding. The State's dangerousness argument, the barred accurate rebuttal, and the jury's explicit parole question defeat a finding that the omission was harmless beyond a reasonable doubt on this record; do not adopt automatic reversal for every parole-instruction omission.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Allow an accurate explanation of legal parole ineligibility and an authoritative answer to a jury question about it. The record-specific failure of harmlessness warrants a new penalty proceeding; do not promise immunity from clemency or require identical relief for materially different omissions.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Meaningful rebuttal at capital sentencing

**Entering Law:** Gardner v. Florida, 430 U.S. 349 (1977), and Skipper v. South Carolina, 476 U.S. 1 (1986), protect an opportunity to answer material sentencing information. California v. Ramos, 463 U.S. 992 (1983), recognizes state latitude over truthful sentencing information and does not by itself decide concealment of legal ineligibility. The Fourteenth Amendment and the applicable South Carolina parole statute supply the question’s sources. [R40](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/154/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain reversal on a narrow due-process rebuttal ground; explain why the error warrants a new sentencing proceeding.

Gardner and Skipper give Stone a stronger existing foundation here than Caldwell alone supplies in Romano. The State invoked future dangerousness while the defense could not explain the actual legal alternative to execution. This concerns the intelligibility of the present choice, not another jury's appraisal. Stone's insistence on effective participation in capital sentencing therefore supports different treatment of these two kinds of information.

Ramos leaves States latitude over truthful sentencing information; it does not conclusively authorize withholding a legal fact necessary to answer the State's own argument. The competing concern is that discussion of release possibilities can distract juries with speculation and changes in law. A categorical legal bar to parole is distinguishable from a prediction about release discretion. The instruction to give life its ordinary meaning is especially inadequate after the jury's express question, since ordinary understanding may include parole. The [historical opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/92-9059.ZO.html) supplies the contemporaneous record and competing statutory and constitutional arguments.

The trial court should establish parole ineligibility under actual state law and ensure the jury receives an accurate explanation, whether through an accepted defense presentation or instruction; an express jury question about the legal alternative warrants an authoritative answer. No script or guarantee against clemency is required. The State can still prove danger to inmates or guards. Claims based only on uncertain release predictions, or cases with no future-dangerousness argument, remain outside this narrow holding.

For relief, the jury's question and the blocked rebuttal strongly support finding an effect on selection; eligibility aggravators do not establish harmlessness. But Stone should articulate why the State cannot show harmlessness on the whole record rather than announce automatic reversal for every omitted parole explanation. That preserves his distinction between a substantive protection and the showing needed for its remedy.

**Egalitarian application:** The State may argue danger supported by the evidence, including danger in prison, while the defense may answer with the actual legal effect of life without parole. Jurors need an accurate choice, not reassurance beyond the law or speculation about inevitable release. This protects reliable sentencing for everyone without treating the defense's statutory fact as an improper appeal for mercy.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** The State urged future dangerousness at sentencing. Simmons offered evidence bearing on his likely conduct in prison and sought to explain that state law made him parole ineligible. The court barred that explanation. The jury expressly asked about parole; it was told not to consider parole and to give life imprisonment its ordinary meaning. It then imposed death. Legal parole ineligibility, not a prediction about discretionary release, is the central premise. [R40](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/154/)

The claim is preserved through the requested instruction and the jury’s question on direct review. The actual state-law sentencing alternatives must be fixed before choosing a constitutional rule. A possible commutation or speculative later legal change is distinct from ordinary parole eligibility. Due process can resolve the case without deciding a broader Eighth Amendment information rule. [R40](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/154/)

Class-wide technical adjustments can be permissible; significance alone does not invalidate them. The boundary is not perfectly mechanical, but it is more constrained than judicial impressions of major policy. If traditional tools genuinely leave this elimination within delegated discretion, Stone must confront Chevron rather than hide a contrary regime in “plain meaning.” On balance the structural argument supports the proposed result. Preserve lawful FCC adjustments and congressional revision; do not decide unpresented consequences for past tariff omissions or dictate the replacement regulatory program.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Integrated tax and local subsidy

**Entering Law:** Baldwin v. G.A.F. Seelig, 294 U.S. 511 (1935); Bacchus Imports v. Dias, 468 U.S. 263 (1984); and New Energy Co. of Indiana v. Limbach, 486 U.S. 269 (1988), constrain tariff-like local preference. Pike, 397 U.S. 137 (1970), governs genuinely evenhanded incidental burdens. Market-participation doctrine does not automatically cover a compulsory assessment imposed on others. [R42](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/186/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain invalidation of the linked assessment and local distribution.

This fits Stone's rejection of disguised origin preferences and his distinction between public services and protection of private local producers. A facially uniform dealer assessment can operate as a tariff when its dedicated proceeds support only the competing in-state production. Dealers and farmers being different legal persons is relevant to incidence, but cannot require courts to disregard a single program's economic and legal operation.

The strongest defense is that States ordinarily may collect nondiscriminatory taxes and spend on local needs. Not every benefit funded by general revenue becomes discrimination against the taxed outsiders. Stone should therefore make the linkage do actual work: the same commodity market is assessed, proceeds are dedicated to local producers competing in that market, and the program's operation compensates the local side for a burden imposed across the interstate supply chain. It is unnecessary to prove that each dollar of assessment is passed through to a particular farmer, but the competitive connection must be supported rather than assumed from a shared treasury.

Baldwin, Bacchus, and New Energy provide the basis; Pike's balancing cannot save a mechanism found discriminatory merely because local farms face hardship. Maintaining a local industry against interstate competition is the preference to be justified, not an independent justification. A genuine nondiscriminatory safety or supply concern would require scrutiny of reasonably available alternatives. The proposed Oregon Waste, Carbone, and Lohman approaches reinforce this analysis if adopted, but are not entering holdings by virtue of their preparation.

Leave general appropriations, genuinely separate subsidies, and lawful market participation open. A compulsory charge imposed on other market actors is not automatically market participation. Invalidation also does not determine severability or the entitlement and source for refunds. This is a justified anti-evasion application, with a bounded integration inquiry needed to prevent it from swallowing ordinary state budgets.

**User-directed case emphasis:** Analyze the linked assessment and distribution as one practical operation. Formal neutrality at the collection stage cannot conceal exclusive relief for local competitors through the same fund. Preserve ordinary evenhanded taxation and independently lawful general-revenue subsidies; sharing a treasury alone does not combine every tax and appropriation into a discriminatory scheme.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** The order imposes an assessment on dealers’ milk sales to Massachusetts retailers regardless of milk origin. Most assessed milk originates outside the State, while the proceeds are reserved to Massachusetts dairy farmers. The program addresses distress in the local dairy industry. The legal payors and subsidy recipients occupy different stages of the same market. The assessment and distribution are linked features of one program, not an unrelated general tax and appropriation. [R42](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/186/)

The assessed dealers have a direct burden and challenge the order actually applied to their interstate supply business. The issue is the integrated program’s market effect, not whether the dealer and farmer are formally the same class. No supplied federal authorization exempts this discriminatory mechanism from commerce review. [R42](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/186/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain both state judgments through an express extension of Lingle's independence principle to the RLA.

A state claim that makes contractual just cause its substantive standard is different. Mere consultation, factual overlap, and parallel remedies do not establish interpretive dependence.

This is a meaningful but well-supported extension across statutory regimes, not an assertion that §301 and the RLA are interchangeable in every respect. The proposed Livadas reasoning supports the source distinction if adopted, but cannot itself settle the RLA issue in advance. Preserve the separate dismissal of the CBA claim, actual arbitration duties, ordinary defenses, and limits against duplicate recovery. #### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: APA allocation and statutory exceptions

**Entering Law:** APA §7(c), now 5 U.S.C. §556(d), places the burden of proof on the proponent of a rule or order except as otherwise provided by statute. LHWCA §19(d), 33 U.S.C. §919(d), incorporates APA adjudication; §23(a), §923(a), relaxes common-law and technical evidentiary rules but does not expressly reverse persuasion. The Black Lung Benefits Act incorporates the relevant procedures through 30 U.S.C. §932(a), including its regulatory-exception language; 20 C.F.R. §718.3(c) states a reasonable-doubt policy. Whether that language delegates displacement of §556(d), and whether this regulation exercises any such authority, are distinct issues. Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981), treats the APA as requiring preponderance rather than clear-and-convincing proof. NLRB v. Transportation Management Corp., 462 U.S. 393, 404 n.7 (1983), says the APA phrase governs production only; that contrary statement must be confronted, not omitted. Statutory presumptions retain their specifically enacted effects. [P35](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/267/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain the persuasion allocation, but correct the proposed account of possible statutory exceptions.

Stone cannot use his residual preference for protecting vulnerable claimants to decide an evidentiary tie where enacted law assigns the risk of nonpersuasion. The APA's enactment-era legal usage, its procedural-uniformity purpose, and Steadman's treatment of proof support persuasion rather than production alone. Do not convert damages pleadings into habeas, require generic state-remedy exhaustion, or decide misconduct and immunity prematurely. #### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Ambiguous invocation after waiver

**Entering Law:** Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966); Edwards v. Arizona, 451 U.S. 477 (1981); Smith v. Illinois, 469 U.S. 91 (1984); Connecticut v. Barrett, 479 U.S. 523 (1987); and McNeil v. Wisconsin, 501 U.S. 171 (1991). A clear request for counsel stops police-initiated custodial interrogation under Edwards. Smith prevents later answers from undoing an initially clear invocation but leaves genuinely ambiguous initial references distinct. [R46](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/452/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain affirmance and articulate the clarification rule as a permissible new resolution of the open postwaiver question.

Miranda, Edwards, Barrett, and Smith support protecting an actual choice to seek counsel while leaving genuine ambiguity distinct from a clear invocation. They do not already compel Stone's middle rule. A suspect's initial waiver has continuing significance, but need not license officers to exploit uncertainty about whether that choice has changed.

The proposed requirement better fits Stone's concern for effective counsel than a rule demanding confident legal phrasing. It also avoids treating every mention of counsel as an irrevocable invocation. The strongest competing rule permits continued questioning until an objectively clear request, offering apparent simplicity and preserving the initial waiver. Stone should answer that assessing clarity is unavoidable under either rule; a short neutral clarification reduces the stakes of that judgment. The [contemporaneous opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/452/) show that this was a live choice, not a position foreclosed by a controlling Supreme Court holding.

Make the trigger a reasonable understanding, in context, that the statement may be a present request for counsel. A remark that a relative is a lawyer is not enough. “Maybe I should talk to a lawyer” ordinarily is enough to pause substantive questioning and ask what the suspect wants. A clear initial request cannot be undone by later answers elicited in violation of Edwards; Smith forbids that maneuver. Clarification must not demand justification for counsel, promise benefits for continuing, or repeatedly pressure a hesitant suspect. If neutral clarification leaves a reasonable present request unresolved, officers should not resume substantive interrogation until the choice is clarified; otherwise the pause has no protective content.

Davis received clarification, voluntarily denied requesting counsel, received renewed warnings, and later obtained cessation upon a clear request. Affirmance therefore fits this rule. The need to decide the broader ambiguity question should be candidly explained; affirmance alone does not establish a compulsory clarification duty. Reserve prewaiver conduct and the unpressed §3501 theory, and do not confuse this interrogation safeguard with attachment of the Sixth Amendment.

**Egalitarian application:** A suspect need not speak like a lawyer to indicate a possible desire for one, and officers need a practical way to distinguish a request from an unrelated mention. A neutral clarification pause protects both concerns. It cannot become pressure to abandon counsel or a means of using later answers to erase an initially clear invocation.

[R46](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/452/)
