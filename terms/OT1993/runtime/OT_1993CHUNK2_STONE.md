#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Assignment versus a new federal office

**Entering Law:** Article II, §2, cl. 2 governs appointment of officers. Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1 (1976), identifies officer functions; Shoemaker v. United States, 147 U.S. 282, 300-301 (1893), permits germane additional duties without a new appointment in the circumstances there. UCMJ Articles 26 and 66, 10 U.S.C. §§826 and 866, use detailing and assignment for commissioned officers meeting judicial qualifications. The text, actual duties, and military office determine whether a new appointment is required; mere convenience cannot substitute for appointment to a genuinely distinct office. [P07](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/163/)

##### Issue 2: Judicial independence without fixed terms

**Entering Law:** Fifth Amendment due process protects an impartial tribunal. Congress’s Article I military authority and Middendorf v. Henry, 425 U.S. 25, 43-44 (1976), require substantial respect for the military justice structure and ask whether the factors favoring an additional protection are extraordinarily weighty against Congress’s balance. UCMJ Articles 26 and 37 restrict command influence and use of judicial performance in specified military evaluations. Tumey and Ward establish that a constitutionally intolerable institutional incentive cannot be excused merely by a claim of good intentions. Mathews and Medina offer competing procedural analogies, not a settled command that military judges must have a particular tenure. [P07](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/163/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain affirmance on both institutional objections.

Existing commissioned appointments matter, but “Congress called it detailing” cannot end the Appointments Clause inquiry. Under *Shoemaker*, 147 U.S. 282, military judging must be germane to the office already held rather than a distinct office assigned by an unauthorized appointing official. Congress's design, military officers' established responsibilities in administering discipline, judicial qualifications, and the absence of a separately created office requiring fresh appointment support the proposed conclusion. This is **application with a bounded germaneness judgment**, not a general power to move any commissioned officer into any federal office. [Record and UCMJ provisions, 510 U.S. 163](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/163/).

On impartiality, Stone can respect *Middendorf*'s military-specific framework while insisting that safeguards actually address command dependence. UCMJ restrictions on influence, assignment, evaluations, and appellate supervision provide a reason beyond tradition to reject a facial fixed-term requirement. Simulated *Gibson* and *Concrete Pipe* do not require identical safeguards in differently structured tribunals.

Qualify the supplement's reliance on the absence of actual pressure. A structural due-process claim need not prove that a particular judge succumbed; *Tumey* and *Ward* concern constitutionally unacceptable incentives. The answer is that this structure, considered as a whole, has not been shown to create that risk. Courts must examine control over reassignment and promotion, prohibited evaluation practices, and credible retaliation evidence. A midtrial reassignment prompted by an unwelcome ruling presents a different case from ordinary rotation.

Fixed terms might be wise, but selecting their duration would be judicial institutional design without a demonstrated constitutional necessity. Affirmance preserves both lawful military discretion and a channel for actual command-influence or structural-bias claims.

**Egalitarian application:** Military needs and the accused's entitlement to an impartial tribunal each receive legal weight. Practical safeguards must constrain command influence rather than merely promise independence. Respect for service discipline neither answers a proved bias risk nor requires importing every civilian institution into military justice.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Both petitioners were Marines convicted on guilty pleas. Weiss pleaded guilty to larceny and received confinement, partial pay forfeiture, and a bad-conduct discharge. Hernandez pleaded guilty to drug offenses and conspiracy; the convening authority reduced his confinement from twenty-five to twenty years. The challenged judges were already commissioned military officers appointed through the constitutionally prescribed process. Judge Advocates General detailed or assigned them to judicial duties without a fresh presidential appointment or fixed tenure. UCMJ provisions regulate qualifications, assignment, performance reports, and unlawful command influence. No adjudicated instance of actual command pressure or personal bias in these cases is supplied. 28 U.S.C. §1259 supplies review of the military appellate judgments in this posture. The preserved institutional challenges concern adjudicative authority and independence; a guilty plea is not treated here as an automatic waiver of those presented structural objections. Appointments and due process are separate questions. Congress’s military authority does not itself satisfy the Appointments Clause. [P07](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/163/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Nonretroactivity presentation

**Entering Law:** Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288 (1989), and subsequent preterm cases restrict new procedural rules on collateral review. Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37, 41 (1990), recognizes that nonretroactivity is not jurisdictional. Where the State has not properly invoked it, the court must identify whether to exercise discretion rather than assume an unwaivable jurisdictional bar. [P08](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/222/)

##### Issue 2: Successive jeopardy and necessarily determined intent

**Entering Law:** The Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments, Ashe v. Swenson, 397 U.S. 436, 443-444 (1970), and Green v. United States, 355 U.S. 184 (1957), protect acquittals and ultimate facts necessarily resolved for the defendant. Ashe requires examination of the pleadings, evidence, charge, and other relevant record to determine whether a rational jury could have grounded its verdict on another issue. Bullington v. Missouri, 451 U.S. 430 (1981), and Arizona v. Rumsey, 467 U.S. 203 (1984), concern acquittal-like capital sentencing decisions; Stroud v. United States, 251 U.S. 15 (1919), and DiFrancesco, 449 U.S. 117 (1980), preclude treating every sentencing phase as a second prosecution. These authorities do not authorize disregarding an actual acquittal. [P08](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/222/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain denial of these habeas grounds, provided the verdict instructions support the identified alternative explanation.

Stone's protection of acquittals does not mean every blank verdict form is an acquittal. *Ashe*, 397 U.S. 436, requires identifying an ultimate fact necessarily decided for the defendant. If jurors understood the counts as alternative forms of one murder conviction, selecting felony murder did not necessarily reject intent. That is failure to establish preclusion, not an appellate finding that intent existed. [Record, 510 U.S. 222](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/222/).

The real tension is with Stone's present commitment to jury findings of facts authorizing greater punishment. A challenge to judicial aggravator findings would implicate that commitment, but it is distinct from the preserved double-jeopardy and issue-preclusion questions. In 1994, *Apprendi* and *Ring* are unavailable, and the existing capital cases do not make his preferred jury rule governing law. Stone can reserve that issue without renouncing the principle.

The original sentencing phase is not automatically a second prosecution. Simulated *Dixon*'s retention of *Grady* does not change that predicate. *Bullington* and *Rumsey* protect actual acquittal-like capital determinations; neither supplies a favorable jury finding absent from this record.

Declining the late *Teague* argument is a permissible case-specific procedural choice, not a general waiver rule. The State omitted it at meaningful earlier stages. Application should examine charge, forms, trial argument, and verdict practice; an express acquittal or instructions requiring separate findings could change the result. The strongest objection is that ambiguity lets the sentencing judge negate the jury's restraint. Stone should answer from this record and burden, while reserving—not dismissing—the independent jury-authority concern.

**Egalitarian application:** Respect the jury's actual determination and the State's authority to seek a lawful sentence. A blank form cannot automatically become either an acquittal or affirmative proof of intent. The full instructions and record determine the significance of the verdict, with constitutional uncertainty allocated under the governing rule rather than sympathy for either side.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** The prosecution submitted multiple murder counts concerning one killing, including a knowing-killing count and felony murder during rape. The jury returned guilty on the felony-murder count and left other forms blank. The trial judge found the statutory intentional-killing-during-rape aggravator and imposed death. The instructions and verdict practice leave a dispute whether jurors understood they could or should return multiple murder verdicts and what they necessarily decided about intent. Indiana courts and the Seventh Circuit rejected the claimed implied acquittal. The State did not invoke Teague in the lower courts or its brief opposing certiorari. Pre-AEDPA §2254 review; the State’s late Teague contention is nonjurisdictional and requires a separate discretionary decision whether to reach it. Ordinary capital sentencing and an actual second jeopardy are not interchangeable. The defendant bears the burden of showing that the jury necessarily resolved the ultimate fact in his favor. A blank verdict form is evidence to interpret with the instructions, not automatically an acquittal. [P08](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/222/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Source of the asserted federal right

**Entering Law:** 42 U.S.C. §1983; Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause; Fourth Amendment. Graham v. Connor, 490 U.S. 386, 395 (1989), directs analysis under the specific constitutional provision governing the challenged conduct rather than generalized substantive due process. Gerstein v. Pugh, 420 U.S. 103 (1975), distinguishes restraints on liberty from judicial supervision of charging. Paul v. Davis, 424 U.S. 693 (1976), rejects reputation alone as a constitutional deprivation. [R07](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/266/)

##### Issue 2: State remedies and relief channel

**Entering Law:** Parratt v. Taylor, 451 U.S. 527 (1981), and Hudson v. Palmer, 468 U.S. 517 (1984), concern procedural due process for random unauthorized acts; they are not a universal exhaustion rule for §1983 or a defense to every substantive constitutional violation. [R07](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/266/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain narrow dismissal of the substantive-due-process theory.

Stone's insistence on accountable prosecution does not make §1983 a federal malicious-prosecution tort. *Graham*, 490 U.S. 386, directs analysis of constitutionally specified restraints under their own provision; *Gerstein* distinguishes restraint from the decision to prosecute. The petition does not preserve a Fourth Amendment theory. [Claim and posture, 510 U.S. 266](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/266/).

The proposal is defensible **source identification and restraint**, but must not overstate *Graham* as foreclosing all overlapping guarantees. Simulated *Soldal* allows independently applicable protections, and a procedural hearing right does different work from a search or seizure prohibition. What fails here is the asserted freestanding substantive right to prosecution only on probable cause, not every procedural-due-process or fabrication claim involving criminal proceedings.

Do not adopt the lower court's broad requirement of incarceration, employment loss, or another “palpable consequence.” Surrender, bond, travel restrictions, and other restraints may matter under an appropriately presented claim. Likewise, the existence of a state tort action is not a universal defense to §1983. *Parratt/Hudson* concern a particular procedural-due-process setting, not general exhaustion.

The difficult boundary is deliberate fabrication causing an actionable deprivation after judicial process. That would require identification of the right, causation, and functional immunity. Stone's Master proposal withdrawing absolute immunity for knowing material falsification would substantially revise contrary law where it applies; this case does not require deciding it. Affirm dismissal of this theory without manufacturing factual findings, promising permission to relitigate, or suggesting that wrongful charging has no constitutional constraints. The cost—possible remedial incompleteness—is real but cannot supply an unpresented cause.

**Egalitarian application:** A wrongful accusation can impose real burdens before incarceration, and those burdens deserve attention under a properly presented constitutional claim. Officials also need defined legal duties and applicable defenses. Narrow rejection of this substantive-due-process theory must not become a declaration that malicious prosecution is harmless or that every abuse has an available remedy.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Albright surrendered on a warrant for selling a substance represented as an illegal drug, posted bond, and was bound over after Oliver’s testimony. The criminal charge was later dismissed because it did not state an offense under Illinois law. Albright pleaded a substantive-due-process theory of prosecution without probable cause. Assertions of baseless prosecution are allegations at the dismissal stage, not findings of officer liability. [R07](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/266/)

The petition does not present a Fourth Amendment claim; procedural due process is not the constitutional theory pressed. Section 1983 supplies a vehicle only for an established federal right. Illinois malicious-prosecution remedies and function-specific immunity may matter to distinct theories, but cannot be assumed to resolve every constitutional claim. [R07](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/266/)

##### Issue 1: Standing and RICO coverage

**Entering Law:** 18 U.S.C. §§1961, 1962(c)–(d), 1964(c), and the incorporated Hobbs Act, §1951. United States v. Turkette, 452 U.S. 576 (1981), recognizes illicit enterprises; Sedima, S.P.R.L. v. ##### Issue 2: Speech limits and predicate offenses

**Entering Law:** The First Amendment and NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co., 458 U.S. 886 (1982), protect lawful advocacy and limit association-based liability. RICO requires actual statutory predicates, causation, and a pattern; political purpose neither proves nor negates those elements. [R08](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/249/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain reversal of the economic-motive dismissal.

Sections 1961–1964 do not make profit an element of an enterprise or its predicate acts. *Turkette* and *Sedima* give substantial support for refusing that extra-textual restriction. Political motivation does not exclude conduct otherwise within enacted prohibitions. This fits Stone's general-language methodology and his separation of private conduct, constitutional rights, and statutory enforcement. Simulated *Bray* does not immunize the same conduct from a different statute. [Pleading record and statutory question, 510 U.S. 249](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/249/).

This is **clarification of open statutory coverage**. Preserve *Holmes*'s direct causation and *Reves*'s operation-or-management requirements. The clinics allege their own business and property losses; that supports their claim at the then-applicable pleading stage without resolving every plaintiff's standing or every RICO element.

The key application limit is that obstruction, coercion, and extortion are not interchangeable. Courts must determine the actual predicate, including the Hobbs Act's obtaining requirement where that offense is asserted, the defendant's participation, pattern, and causation. A lawful boycott that reduces revenue remains different from threats satisfying an actual criminal predicate. Mere membership or ideological agreement cannot carry damages liability for others' unlawful acts under *Claiborne Hardware*.

The strongest objection is the chilling reach of treble-damages litigation against protest organizations. Stone should answer through statutory elements, individual attribution, and First Amendment safeguards rather than inventing an economic-motive exemption. Nor should his abortion-access commitment dilute those protections. Remand reinstates claims; it supplies no liability finding, treble award, or private injunctive authority. Later rounds of *Scheidler* cannot be used as law already settled in January 1994.

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** The complaint alleges that PLAN and associated antiabortion activists used force, threats, and obstruction in a nationwide effort to shut clinics, causing business and property injury. It characterizes conduct as Hobbs Act extortion and alleges an association-in-fact enterprise. These are pleading allegations, not adjudicated crimes, findings that property was obtained, or a verdict of racketeering liability. [R08](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/249/)

The clinics’ allegations of injury to their own business or property supply the concrete and statutory injury question at this stage. Pleading review uses the then-governing Hishon/Conley standard, not later pleading doctrine. The economic-motive question does not resolve whether the alleged acts satisfy every Hobbs Act element. [R08](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/249/)
