#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Review channel and scope of the judgment

**Entering Law:** The Anti-Head Tax Act, former 49 U.S.C. App. §1513(a), restricts taxes on air travelers and their carriage; §1513(b) preserves reasonable airport charges. Federal jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. §1331 is distinct from an implied action. The cross-petition rule limits affirmative alteration of the judgment, although alternative grounds may support affirmance. The Airport and Airway Improvement Act is a different enactment with its own enforcement design. [P11](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/355/)

##### Issue 2: Reasonableness and interstate treatment of airport fees

**Entering Law:** Evansville-Vanderburgh Airport Authority v. Delta Airlines, 405 U.S. 707 (1972), evaluates user fees by a fair approximation of use, whether the charge is excessive in relation to benefits, and discrimination against interstate commerce. American Trucking Assns. v. Scheiner, 483 U.S. 266 (1987), addresses discriminatory transportation taxes. These are user-fee and commerce rules, not ordinary equal-protection scrutiny. AHTA’s reasonableness language presents the statutory question whether that framework is an appropriate measure here. The challenger must identify the material excess or discriminatory operation; perfect accounting and identical treatment of functionally different users are not required. [P11](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/355/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain affirmance and expressly qualify Stone's categorical sequence for causes of action.

Federal-question jurisdiction and the existence of an implied private action are distinct. A court with jurisdiction can assume the claimant has the asserted action and reject the claim without establishing a new remedy. The airport's lack of a cross-petition also prevents using the argument to eliminate the separate rescue-cost remand. [Scope-of-review reasoning, 510 U.S. 355](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/92-97.ZO.html).

This exposes a **real but limited tension** with Master §§3 and Courts, which place the cause of action before merits adjudication. Stone should refine that formulation: no affirmative relief without an established cause; no assumed jurisdiction; but an assumed nonjurisdictional cause can support a narrower denial where procedural limits and decisional economy justify it. Otherwise his own strict sequence would require deciding an unnecessary implied-right question. This is a methodological clarification, not a new AHTA cause of action.

Using *Evansville*, 405 U.S. 707, to construe reasonable airport charges is a source-grounded statutory development. Examine fair approximation of use, excess relative to benefits, and discrimination against interstate commerce. Different recovery from concessionaires or general aviation is not automatically unlawful. The airlines must show a defect in their charges, not merely an opportunity for different accounting.

A reasonable reserve can finance predictable capital needs; an unexplained accumulation disconnected from airport costs could be excessive. Indirect benefit to concessionaires does not make them users of every airfield facility. The hard cases involve cross-subsidies that conceal discrimination or shift unrelated costs to interstate carriers. Stone should preserve those inquiries without demanding exact allocation or ignoring the already identified rescue-cost defect. Affirm only the reviewed components and preserve that remand.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** The Kent County airport allocated airfield and terminal costs to commercial airlines, recovered only part of general aviation’s allocated costs, and received concession revenues exceeding concession costs. Airlines objected to the reserve surplus, the exclusion of concessionaires from airfield charges, and general aviation’s lower contribution. They did not establish that their own fees exceeded the costs fairly allocated to the facilities they used. The separate crash, fire, and rescue allocation remand was not challenged by airport cross-petition. Federal-question jurisdiction exists independently of whether the statute implies a private remedy. Do not call an assumption about the statutory action “hypothetical jurisdiction.” A respondent may defend a judgment on an alternative ground but may not enlarge its rights or diminish the adversary’s rights without the necessary cross-review. The unappealed crash, fire, and rescue ruling limits the airport’s requested change. AAIA and AHTA must not be merged. [P11](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/355/)
