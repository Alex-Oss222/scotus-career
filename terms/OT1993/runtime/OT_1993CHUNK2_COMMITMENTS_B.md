## 19. Northwest Airlines, Inc. v. County of Kent, Michigan — January 24, 1994

**Participation and reach.** Argued November 29, 1993; No.92-97. Blackmun does not participate; the supplied record gives no reason. Seven non-Stone Justices participate. The Sixth Circuit judgment, 955 F.2d1054, is reviewable on the airlines' preserved AHTA and Commerce Clause challenges. Section1331 supplies federal-question jurisdiction independently of whether the AHTA supplies a private cause of action. The airport may defend the favorable judgment on an alternative no-private-action ground, but its failure to cross-petition prevents enlargement of its rights by undoing the crash/fire/rescue remand. The airlines did not appeal the rejection of their separate AAIA cause of action. The unappealed parking correction and the favorable crash/fire/rescue remand remain intact.

**Components and operative commitments.** N1: assume, without deciding, the asserted AHTA cause for purposes of rejecting these appealed claims on their merits; make no private-action holding, no primary-jurisdiction holding, and no hypothetical-jurisdiction rationale. N2: affirm the appealed fees under §1513(b)'s reasonable-fee exception, applying the Evansville measure of fair approximation of use, no excess relative to governmental benefits supplied, and no discrimination against interstate commerce. N3: reject the Commerce Clause challenge on the established record. N4: affirm within the petition's scope while preserving the parking correction and crash/fire/rescue allocation remand; no ruling on unappealed AAIA rights, no refund of the affirmed charges, and no new revenue-diversion finding.

The three parts of the fee measure remain distinct. Market rents for concessions need not duplicate the airlines' cost allocation because concessions and aircraft operators use different facilities in different ways. Nor does general aviation's failure to pay its allocated airfield costs establish that the airlines' charges exceed the cost of the facilities furnished to them: the trial court found break-even charges to the airlines apart from the corrected parking calculation, and concession revenues covered the general-aviation shortfall. The carriers did not prove that general aviation was chiefly intrastate. Reserve accumulation is not, by itself, an excessive carrier fee or a diversion to nonairport purposes. The court of appeals' carrying-charge analysis limited the charge to recovery of capital cost in present-value terms; this commitment does not authorize unlimited profit or double recovery. The separate crash/fire/rescue remand corrects charging airlines alone for a service also benefiting general aviation.

**Two lawful paths.** The airlines' strongest path would treat unequal cost recovery and reserves as evidence of cross-subsidy, require a more exact allocation, and remand for a revised fee judgment. The airport's path applies a fair approximation, respecting the actual break-even findings and the distinction between discrimination by interstate character and differential charges by user class. The second path supplies the judgment on this record. The rejected path remains available where proof shows an unreasonable allocation, excessive user charges, or interstate discrimination; it is not barred by a rule that every airport-wide expenditure is automatically chargeable to every user. The operative AHTA text, the courts' findings, and Evansville supply the rule; no deference is attributed to a DOT fee standard absent from this record.

## 20. Hagen v. Utah — February 23, 1994

**Participation and reach.** All nine participate; eight non-Stone positions follow. Argued November2,1993; No.92-6281. Utah's final judgment, 858 P.2d925, presents the federal territorial basis for state criminal jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C.1257 and 18 U.S.C.1151. Hagen's Indian status is no longer disputed on review. Myton is in the area opened in1905. The distinct estoppel/preclusion theory was omitted from the petition and expressly disavowed; no Justice's commitment reaches it. Izumi's actual question-presented rule reinforces that limit without converting merits disagreement about reservation status into a jurisdictional defect in this Court.

**Enactments that constrain both paths.** The1902 Act made allotment, initially dependent on majority adult-male consent, precede restoration of unallotted land to the public domain; proceeds first reimbursed federal advances and then benefited the Indians. Its separately specified $70,064.48 concerned other claims and was not an outright purchase of all surplus land. The June1902 resolution required sufficient common grazing land before opening and separately removed the payment's consent condition. The1903 Act authorized allotment without consent after June1,1903, limited the grazing tract, and postponed opening under the1902 program. The1904 Act again postponed opening and funded surveys. The1905 Act postponed opening, expressly repealed the south-of-Strawberry-River grazing restriction, prescribed homestead and townsite entry, excluded other forms of entry for the first60 days, and authorized cash disposal of remaining lands after five years in parcels not exceeding640 acres. It retained the earlier proceeds arrangement but did not repeat public-domain language. The presidential proclamation recited that earlier language while its operative terms implemented the1905 entry restrictions. Neither the proclamation nor Inspector McLaughlin could independently diminish a reservation. Later practice and population are evidence, not boundary legislation.

**Two lawful paths.** H-A affirms because the1902 public-domain instruction remained effective through extensions and the1905 implementation provisions. The omission of repetition and the revision of entry methods did not impliedly repeal that instruction; no particular cession formula or lump-sum payment is indispensable under Solem and Rosebud. H-B reverses because the1905 rewrite, changed text and retained tribal proceeds leave substantial uncertainty whether Congress ultimately authorized only settlement rather than loss of reservation status; Solem requires resolving that uncertainty for the Indians. H-B distinguishes Rosebud's continuity from a clearly established cession purpose rather than claiming that consent and fixed payment are invariably mandatory. Both paths reject demographic change as an independent source of diminishment.

**Components.** H1 is the construction of the opening enactments and reservation boundary as applied to Myton. H2 is the resulting state jurisdiction over this prosecution and disposition of the Utah judgment. H3 is the refusal to decide the disavowed preclusion issue. An H-A vote affirms H2 because Myton is outside the reservation on this record; an H-B vote reverses H2 and remands for proceedings consistent with the absence of that asserted state territorial jurisdiction. Neither path decides all trust/allotment questions, the entire territory's parcel status, unrelated tribal rights, or jurisdiction over every person and offense.

## 21. FDIC v. Meyer — February 23, 1994

**Participation, record and scope.** All nine participate; No.92-741, argued October4,1993. The Ninth Circuit affirmed the $130,000 award against FSLIC and the individual judgment protecting Pattullo by qualified immunity. Only the agency award is before the Court; review of the individual ruling was denied. FDIC is the statutory successor, but former12 U.S.C.1725(c)(4), governing FSLIC's power to sue and be sued in a court of competent jurisdiction, supplies the relevant waiver. The dismissed contractual claim and receivership process are not findings that Meyer obtained an alternative remedy. The due-process claim is the only surviving agency claim, and it asserts a federal constitutional duty even though California law allegedly supplied the underlying employment interest.

**Components.** M1: the Fifth Amendment tort is not cognizable under §1346(b), whose private-person liability under the law of the place means state-law liability; thus §2679(a) does not displace the agency's sue-and-be-sued waiver for this claim. M2: the broad sue-and-be-sued provision waives immunity for this kind of suit; none of the established Burr/Loeffler limitations—statutory incompatibility, grave interference with a governmental function, or a plainly restricted congressional purpose—is established merely because the asserted duty is constitutional. M3: that waiver does not itself create a substantive damages action; decline to extend Bivens directly to the agency. M4: reverse the judgment upholding the agency award and remand for disposition of the claim consistently with the absence of that cause; leave the individual judgment and the underlying due-process merits unaltered and undecided respectively.

The M1–M3 sequence matters. A claim can survive an immunity defense yet fail for want of a cause of action. Section2679(a)'s cognizable language cannot be replaced with a general category of everything sounding in tort. Section2679(b)(1), considered in United States v. Smith, concerns suits against employees and contains different language; its treatment of an FTCA exception does not decide this agency clause. The sue-and-be-sued waiver's breadth does not mean every constitutional injury necessarily has an agency damages remedy. Conversely, the rejection of an implied constitutional cause does not erase the waiver or bar claims with a valid statutory or other substantive source.

**Two lawful paths.** M-A would allow an agency constitutional damages action because the waiver removes immunity, ordinary remedial principles favor effective redress, and an individual qualified-immunity defense may otherwise leave a proved injury uncompensated. M-B distinguishes waiver from the source of liability and declines this new class of Bivens defendant: a direct agency remedy materially changes the individual-officer deterrent structure and the balance of immunity, and commits federal funds on a scale suited to Congress's remedial choices. M-B is the commitment. It does not depend on a finding that the abandoned contract route made Meyer whole or that the Social Security scheme in Schweiker governed his employment. Its legal limit is an implied Bivens action against the federal agency; neither all judicial remedies nor every individual Bivens action is rejected.

## 23. Elder v. Holloway — February 23, 1994

Elder's appeal is about the sources a reviewing court may consult in deciding the preserved legal question, not permission to add a new constitutional claim or new evidentiary facts. The material constitutional notice date is April27,1987, not the appeal date.

**Components.** E1: the clearly-established-law issue is a question of law reviewed with all relevant date-eligible authority, including applicable circuit authority not cited below. E-B supplies the commitment. Nor does considering Al-Azzawy determine its weight: the lower court must evaluate whether its holding or reasoning clearly established the particular right in1987, alongside other then-existing law and the full supported record.

## 24. Caspari v. Bohlen — February 23, 1994

**Actual intervening authority and reach.** All nine participate; No.92-1500, argued December6,1993. The Eighth Circuit granted habeas relief from renewed persistent-offender proceedings after an initial failure of proof. The convictions became final January2,1986. The State preserved Teague below, in its petition, and in merits briefing. Actual Schiro, effective January19,1994, permits declining a late nonjurisdictional Teague defense, preserves capital acquittal protection, and leaves Teague's rule and exceptions unchanged. Its late-defense predicate is absent here. Actual Izumi governs the question presented, but Teague here is a preserved threshold legal limitation on the very habeas relief sought, not a separately unreviewed judgment such as denial of intervention. The petition's body presented the limitation; no Justice treats every logically antecedent question as automatically included.

**Record refinement essential to every commitment.** First-degree robbery was a class-A felony, with ten to thirty years or life authorized under §558.011.1(1). Persistent-offender status under §558.016.3 required two prior felonies at different times, proved beyond a reasonable doubt under §558.021. For these class-A convictions the status did **not** raise the authorized sentencing range. It changed sentencing authority from the jury's advisory sentence, which ordinarily limited the judge's upward choice, to the judge. At both sentencings the punishment was three consecutive fifteen-year terms. The initial reversal followed failure to prove the required prior convictions; at the second hearing the State proved four. The challenge is to the second proof proceeding, not to a conviction for innocent conduct or to a punishment Missouri could never impose for these crimes.

**Components.** C1: consider the preserved Teague issue before the constitutional merits. C2: the claimed prohibition on renewed noncapital persistent-offender proof was not dictated by precedent when the convictions became final January2,1986 and would therefore be a new rule on collateral review. C3: neither contemporary exception applies: the proposed rule neither places conduct or a class of punishment beyond state authority nor establishes a bedrock procedure essential to fundamental fairness and accuracy; it concerns the finality of one sentencing-status determination and the State's second opportunity to prove prior convictions. C4: reverse the Eighth Circuit's grant and remand consistently with the nonretroactivity bar, without deciding whether double jeopardy applies to such proceedings on direct review or prospectively, without overruling Bullington, and without disturbing the robbery convictions on another ground.

**Two lawful paths.** C-A treats Bullington's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt, trial-like sentencing adjudication as a general acquittal principle already extending to this proceeding, with Rumsey showing that a judge rather than jury does not defeat the protection. It would reject the Teague bar and address the merits, potentially granting relief. C-B distinguishes the capital entitlement to death, the binary sentencing structure and acquittal-like finding protected in those cases from this noncapital determination of the sentencing decisionmaker within an unchanged authorized range. DiFrancesco left ordinary sentencing with less finality, and the prefinality division among courts confirms the legal question's genuine openness, though disagreement alone is not a mechanical new-rule test. C-B is sufficient to dispose of this habeas case. Graham's actual rule against treating every application as new remains intact; this is a materially different extension, not just application of a general right to a new factual example. Gilmore's actual capital/noncapital distinction supports that conclusion.
