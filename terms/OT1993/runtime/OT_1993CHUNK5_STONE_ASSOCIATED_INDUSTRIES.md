#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Review Posture and Requested Relief:** Missouri Supreme Court, 857 S.W.2d 182 (1993), affirmed summary judgment sustaining the additional use tax. The court compared statewide aggregate sales-tax and use-tax burdens rather than requiring equality within each taxing locality. Associated Industries and Alumax seek reversal of the judgment upholding the excess tax burden. Missouri seeks to retain the statewide-average compensatory-tax justification. [P27](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/641/)

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Missouri Supreme Court’s judgment and remand.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require local, comparable-burden analysis and appropriate relief for unlawful excess collection; retain lawful equal applications and the unchallenged ordinary taxes.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Transaction-level discrimination and compensatory taxation

**Entering Law:** The Commerce Clause’s nondiscrimination rule is established by Philadelphia v. New Jersey, 437 U.S. 617 (1978), New Energy Co. v. Limbach, 486 U.S. 269 (1988), and Armco Inc. v. Hardesty, 467 U.S. 638 (1984). Henneford v. Silas Mason Co., 300 U.S. 577 (1937), permits an equivalent use tax compensating a sales tax; Gregg Dyeing Co. v. Query, 286 U.S. 472 (1932), examines the actual complementary burdens. The State must identify the intrastate levy it claims to offset, establish a comparable taxable incident, and show equivalence rather than an excess burden on the interstate transaction. General American Tank Car Corp. v. Day, 270 U.S. 367 (1926), does not create an unrestricted averaging defense. Fort Gratiot Sanitary Landfill v. Michigan DNR, 504 U.S. 353 (1992), rejects the assumption that discrimination disappears because a distinction is local rather than statewide. [P27](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/641/)

##### Issue 2: Relief from discriminatory collection

**Entering Law:** McKesson Corp. v. Division of Alcoholic Beverages, 496 U.S. 18 (1990), requires meaningful backward-looking relief when a State compels payment before a challenge to an unlawful discriminatory tax. The required remedy depends on available predeprivation procedures, the affected transactions, and lawful means of correcting the inequality; a violation does not automatically establish every requested refund amount. [P27](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/641/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain rejection of statewide averaging and tailor relief to the excess.

The relevant comparison is the interstate and intrastate transaction in the same local market. A 1.5% additional use tax burdens interstate purchases more where the corresponding local sales tax is lower; higher sales taxes elsewhere do not compensate this buyer. *Henneford*, *Armco*, and the inherited discrimination cases support that transaction-specific inquiry. [Stipulated rates and structure, 511 U.S. 641](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/641/).

This is **application and clarification of compensatory-tax doctrine**, not a special equality tier. Stone's acceptance of reasonable economic classifications does not relax the independent interstate-commerce prohibition. Nor does *Quill*'s nexus analysis answer whether a connected tax discriminates.

Application identifies the corresponding local tax, the comparable taxable incident, and the actual combined burden. The uncontested equal statewide taxes remain outside the issue. A locality with no sales tax cannot justify the added use tax by pointing to residents of a different locality paying more. Conversely, this reasoning does not invalidate an equal or lower interstate burden merely because some other applications discriminate.

The strongest objection is administrative complexity and aggregate fairness in a system with many local rates. That cost is real, but matching local burdens is intelligible; a state cannot select the comparison level that conceals the discrimination on the transaction before the court. No numerical threshold of affected jurisdictions is needed.

Remedy remains separate. *McKesson* and the inherited backward-looking tax-relief rules require meaningful correction where the relevant conditions apply, not an automatic refund figure for every taxpayer. Determine payment, preservation, available prepayment procedures, and lawful equalizing measures. Remand without designing Missouri's tax system or letting administrative inconvenience preserve an established discriminatory excess.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** The ordinary statewide sales and use taxes, each 4.225%, are not challenged. Missouri separately charges 1.5% on out-of-state purchases while local sales taxes vary, including localities with none. The stipulated figures showed the extra use tax exceeded the local sales tax in 53.5% of local taxing jurisdictions, although most sales volume occurred where local sales tax was higher. Out-of-state members must collect the use tax; Alumax pays it on purchases used in Missouri. Those other localities do not change what a particular interstate transaction pays in its own locality. The claimed injury concerns a tax actually paid or collected, not abstract objection to state policy. Keep a facial classification, the compensatory justification, and the scope of relief distinct. Identifying invalid excess in some localities does not itself establish that collection must end where local and interstate burdens are equal. [P27](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/641/)
