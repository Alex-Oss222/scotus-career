**[Chemical Waste Management, Inc. v. Hunt, 504 U.S. 334 (1992) — Whether Alabama may impose an additional disposal charge on equivalent hazardous waste because it originated outside the State](inherited/HOLDINGS.md#whether-alabama-may-impose-an-additional-disposal-charge-on-equivalent-hazardous-waste-because-it-originated-outside-the-state)** — Effective: June 1, 1992.

A State may not impose an additional disposal charge on out-of-state hazardous waste that it does not impose on equivalent in-state waste unless it establishes a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve. Alabama's $72-per-ton additional charge fails because the record supplies no origin-linked difference in the relevant risks or costs and adequate evenhanded means remain available; commercial hazardous-waste disposal receives Commerce Clause protection despite the material's dangers.

**Unresolved boundary:** The grant concerns the additional fee alone. The base fee, independent volume cap, other permitting provisions, state constitutional claims and other federal challenges are not adjudicated. The State did not press its fair-share or compensatory-tax theory; amici's congressional-authorization theory was neither decided below nor briefed or argued by the parties. No unlimited right to dispose of hazardous waste, new proof quantum, perfect-certainty requirement or least-imaginable-alternative rule is created.

**[Fort Gratiot Sanitary Landfill, Inc. v. Michigan Department of Natural Resources, 504 U.S. 353 (1992) — Whether county-origin restrictions at private landfills escape Commerce Clause scrutiny because they also exclude other intrastate counties](inherited/HOLDINGS.md#whether-county-origin-restrictions-at-private-landfills-escape-commerce-clause-scrutiny-because-they-also-exclude-other-intrastate-counties)** — Effective: June 1, 1992.

A State's prohibition on a private landfill's acceptance of outside-county waste unless a county plan affirmatively authorizes it discriminates against interstate commerce even when it also excludes other counties within that State and some counties admit imports. The restriction cannot stand unless the State establishes a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve; Michigan has not done so for these Waste Import Restrictions, and planning waste services does not convert regulation of a private facility into market participation.

**Unresolved boundary:** Only the 1988 Waste Import Restrictions in §§299.413a and 299.430(2), as applied to private landfills, are invalidated. The earlier planning statute, public-facility practices, neutral health, safety and capacity rules, different waste-risk classifications, unconditional operation and genuinely applicable congressional authorization remain outside the decision. No hazardous-waste question arises. Distinct due-process, taking and ownership-transfer licensing matters are not reopened.

**[Proprietary public purchases and labor preemption](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#proprietary-public-purchases-and-labor-preemption)** — Effective: 1993-03-08; Building & Construction Trades Council v. Associated Builders & Contractors (Boston Harbor), for the components identified above.

Garmon and Machinists preemption do not bar a project-specific contracting condition when the State acts for its proprietary interests as owner and purchaser, analogous private conduct is lawful, and Congress has not expressly or impliedly displaced the proprietary conduct. Public ownership or a procurement label alone is insufficient; purchasing cannot regulate unrelated conduct or impose general labor policy. The holding creates no general NLRA-employer status for a State. Distinct §§8(e) and 8(f) limits remain, including §8(f)'s preservation of employee elections under §§9(c) and 9(e).

**Open Point:** Whether favoring one private local facility discriminates despite burdening other local competitors, and whether financing or health objectives justify that exclusion.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Town and the courts below stress uniform routing of local waste, lawful local responsibility for disposal, and financing a facility built for public purposes. The designated station eventually becomes publicly owned. The Town argues that neither origin nor destination formally controls the routing command. [R23](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/383/)

Carbone identifies the foreclosed market for processing services: every competitor, including out-of-state firms, is excluded in favor of one local private operator. Burdening other local processors does not eliminate that preference. Nondiscriminatory safety rules and ordinary public financing are identified alternatives; Carbone does not claim exemption from all waste regulation. [R23](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/383/)

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Treating the exclusive private preference as discrimination requires examination of nondiscriminatory alternatives and can support reversal. Treating it as an evenhanded local service arrangement invokes Pike and its burden analysis. The Court can decide the actual private-operation arrangement without announcing that every publicly operated collection system is unconstitutional. [R23](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/383/)

**[Collateral Rule 11 sanctions despite absence of merits jurisdiction](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#collateral-rule-11-sanctions-despite-absence-of-merits-jurisdiction)** — Effective: *Willy*, March 3, 1992.

##### Issue 1: Postdismissal jurisdiction

**Controlling Simulated Law / Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the case-specific inherited-law rules and limits above to this issue. Other eligible authorities below remain effective only to the extent consistent with those simulated holdings and any actual earlier effective OT1993 ruling.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Article III and statutory jurisdiction, including 28 U.S.C. §1332, limit the court’s authority. Rule 41(a)(1)(ii), in its event-date numbering, governs stipulated dismissal. Ancillary jurisdiction allows appropriate management and vindication of the court’s own proceedings and disposition of sufficiently related matters, but requires a legal basis; Julian v. Central Trust, 193 U.S. 93 (1904), concerns enforcement tied to court orders. Rule 60(b) addresses relief from judgment, not automatic jurisdiction over a settlement contract. [R24](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/375/)

**Open Point:** Whether judicial awareness or approval of settlement alone makes its performance an obligation of the dismissal order.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Guardian and the lower courts invoke efficient settlement enforcement and the court’s involvement in the agreement. Their theory treats the settlement’s role in producing dismissal as sufficient to preserve federal authority. [R24](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/375/)

Kokkonen distinguishes the former claims from the new contract dispute and notes the omission of retention or incorporation. He does not deny that a court can enforce its own order when settlement obligations are made part of it, or that an independent jurisdictional basis could support a new action. [R24](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/375/)

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Absent retention, incorporation, or an independent jurisdictional basis, reverse the enforcement judgment without reaching breach. A valid order making compliance part of dismissal would present a different ancillary-enforcement basis. Judicial economy cannot itself sustain adjudication after the jurisdictional basis has ended. [R24](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/375/)

**Controlling Simulated Law / Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the case-specific inherited-law rules and limits above to this issue. Other eligible authorities below remain effective only to the extent consistent with those simulated holdings and any actual earlier effective OT1993 ruling.

**[Unreasonable-rate counterclaims, recoupment and primary jurisdiction](inherited/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#unreasonable-rate-counterclaims-recoupment-and-primary-jurisdiction)** — Effective: 1993-03-08; Reiter v. Cooper, for the components identified above.

A shipper may assert §11705(b)(3) unreasonable-rate damages as a counterclaim in an undercharge action on the same shipments; Rule 8(c) permits correcting a defense designation when justice requires. Under §11706(g), accrual occurs upon delivery or tender, without prior payment. A negotiated rate alone supplies no common-law defense to the filed tariff. The two-year affirmative limitation in §11706(c)(2) does not bar same-transaction recoupment up to the carrier's asserted liability, but supplies no extension for unrelated claims or excess affirmative recovery. The court must allow a reasonable opportunity to obtain the ICC's rate determination. It retains jurisdiction and ordinarily stays proceedings; dismissal without prejudice is permitted only if it would not unfairly disadvantage the parties. Neither payment nor a completed ICC ruling is required to file.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** The Interstate Commerce Act’s then-applicable filing and rate provisions, including 49 U.S.C. §§10761 and 10762, and ICC tariff-participation regulations require a complete ascertainable filed rate. Maislin Industries, U.S., Inc. v. Primary Steel, Inc., 497 U.S. 116 (1990), enforces effective filed rates notwithstanding negotiated discounts; it does not create missing tariff content. ICC v. American Trucking Assns., 467 U.S. 354 (1984), addresses the agency’s authority concerning tariff defects. Berwind-White Coal Mining Co. v. Chicago & Erie R.R., 235 U.S. 371 (1914), permits treatment of merely technical defects where adequate public notice remains. A missing authorized distance component differs from a harmless form error. [P24](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/431/)

**Open Point:** Whether cancellation makes the incorporated mileage component unavailable and therefore defeats the undercharge claim without displacing Maislin.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The carrier invokes Maislin and argues that the guide remains factually available and nonpayment is merely technical, or that retroactive invalidation exceeds ICC authority. Kmart and the SG answer that an incorporated essential component must be legally operative, that published cancellation preceded the transactions, and that the filed-rate doctrine presupposes an effective rate. The distinction is not between sympathetic and unsympathetic bankrupt carriers but between valid and incomplete tariffs. [P24](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/431/)

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm absence of an effective tariff; enforce the tariff if the alleged defect is legally only technical; or seek appropriate agency determination of a genuinely unresolved regulatory issue. Do not conflate a negotiated-rate equitable defense with the prerequisite that an enforceable filed rate exist. [P24](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/431/)
