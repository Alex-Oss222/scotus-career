#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm dismissal of the substantive-due-process claim on the narrow source-of-right ground, without adopting the Seventh Circuit’s entire palpable-consequence rationale.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** No damages or merits remand on an unpreserved Fourth Amendment claim. The judgment resolves the claim presented; any different claim remains subject to the ordinary procedural rules, not an affirmative authorization to relitigate.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Source of the asserted federal right

**Entering Law:** 42 U.S.C. §1983; Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause; Fourth Amendment. Graham v. Connor, 490 U.S. 386, 395 (1989), directs analysis under the specific constitutional provision governing the challenged conduct rather than generalized substantive due process. Gerstein v. Pugh, 420 U.S. 103 (1975), distinguishes restraints on liberty from judicial supervision of charging. Paul v. Davis, 424 U.S. 693 (1976), rejects reputation alone as a constitutional deprivation. [R07](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/266/)

##### Issue 2: State remedies and relief channel

**Entering Law:** Parratt v. Taylor, 451 U.S. 527 (1981), and Hudson v. Palmer, 468 U.S. 517 (1984), concern procedural due process for random unauthorized acts; they are not a universal exhaustion rule for §1983 or a defense to every substantive constitutional violation. [R07](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/266/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain narrow dismissal of the substantive-due-process theory.

Stone's insistence on accountable prosecution does not make §1983 a federal malicious-prosecution tort. *Graham*, 490 U.S. 386, directs analysis of constitutionally specified restraints under their own provision; *Gerstein* distinguishes restraint from the decision to prosecute. The petition does not preserve a Fourth Amendment theory. [Claim and posture, 510 U.S. 266](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/266/).

The proposal is defensible **source identification and restraint**, but must not overstate *Graham* as foreclosing all overlapping guarantees. Simulated *Soldal* allows independently applicable protections, and a procedural hearing right does different work from a search or seizure prohibition. What fails here is the asserted freestanding substantive right to prosecution only on probable cause, not every procedural-due-process or fabrication claim involving criminal proceedings.

Do not adopt the lower court's broad requirement of incarceration, employment loss, or another “palpable consequence.” Surrender, bond, travel restrictions, and other restraints may matter under an appropriately presented claim. Likewise, the existence of a state tort action is not a universal defense to §1983. *Parratt/Hudson* concern a particular procedural-due-process setting, not general exhaustion.

The difficult boundary is deliberate fabrication causing an actionable deprivation after judicial process. That would require identification of the right, causation, and functional immunity. Stone's Master proposal withdrawing absolute immunity for knowing material falsification would substantially revise contrary law where it applies; this case does not require deciding it. Affirm dismissal of this theory without manufacturing factual findings, promising permission to relitigate, or suggesting that wrongful charging has no constitutional constraints. The cost—possible remedial incompleteness—is real but cannot supply an unpresented cause.

**Egalitarian application:** A wrongful accusation can impose real burdens before incarceration, and those burdens deserve attention under a properly presented constitutional claim. Officials also need defined legal duties and applicable defenses. Narrow rejection of this substantive-due-process theory must not become a declaration that malicious prosecution is harmless or that every abuse has an available remedy.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Albright surrendered on a warrant for selling a substance represented as an illegal drug, posted bond, and was bound over after Oliver’s testimony. The criminal charge was later dismissed because it did not state an offense under Illinois law. Albright pleaded a substantive-due-process theory of prosecution without probable cause. Assertions of baseless prosecution are allegations at the dismissal stage, not findings of officer liability. [R07](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/266/)

The petition does not present a Fourth Amendment claim; procedural due process is not the constitutional theory pressed. Section 1983 supplies a vehicle only for an established federal right. Illinois malicious-prosecution remedies and function-specific immunity may matter to distinct theories, but cannot be assumed to resolve every constitutional claim. [R07](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/266/)

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Reinstate the affected claims without entering liability, treble damages, or an injunction. Require the lower courts to address remaining elements, individual responsibility, First Amendment limits, and the statutory basis of each requested remedy.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Standing and RICO coverage

**Entering Law:** 18 U.S.C. §§1961, 1962(c)–(d), 1964(c), and the incorporated Hobbs Act, §1951. United States v. Turkette, 452 U.S. 576 (1981), recognizes illicit enterprises; Sedima, S.P.R.L. v. Imrex Co., 473 U.S. 479 (1985), rejects extra-textual RICO restrictions. Hishon v. King & Spalding, 467 U.S. 69 (1984), governs the stated pleading posture. [R08](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/249/)

##### Issue 2: Speech limits and predicate offenses

**Entering Law:** The First Amendment and NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co., 458 U.S. 886 (1982), protect lawful advocacy and limit association-based liability. RICO requires actual statutory predicates, causation, and a pattern; political purpose neither proves nor negates those elements. [R08](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/249/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain reversal of the economic-motive dismissal.

Sections 1961–1964 do not make profit an element of an enterprise or its predicate acts. *Turkette* and *Sedima* give substantial support for refusing that extra-textual restriction. Political motivation does not exclude conduct otherwise within enacted prohibitions. This fits Stone's general-language methodology and his separation of private conduct, constitutional rights, and statutory enforcement. Simulated *Bray* does not immunize the same conduct from a different statute. [Pleading record and statutory question, 510 U.S. 249](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/249/).

This is **clarification of open statutory coverage**. Preserve *Holmes*'s direct causation and *Reves*'s operation-or-management requirements. The clinics allege their own business and property losses; that supports their claim at the then-applicable pleading stage without resolving every plaintiff's standing or every RICO element.

The key application limit is that obstruction, coercion, and extortion are not interchangeable. Courts must determine the actual predicate, including the Hobbs Act's obtaining requirement where that offense is asserted, the defendant's participation, pattern, and causation. A lawful boycott that reduces revenue remains different from threats satisfying an actual criminal predicate. Mere membership or ideological agreement cannot carry damages liability for others' unlawful acts under *Claiborne Hardware*.

The strongest objection is the chilling reach of treble-damages litigation against protest organizations. Stone should answer through statutory elements, individual attribution, and First Amendment safeguards rather than inventing an economic-motive exemption. Nor should his abortion-access commitment dilute those protections. Remand reinstates claims; it supplies no liability finding, treble award, or private injunctive authority. Later rounds of *Scheidler* cannot be used as law already settled in January 1994.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** The complaint alleges that PLAN and associated antiabortion activists used force, threats, and obstruction in a nationwide effort to shut clinics, causing business and property injury. It characterizes conduct as Hobbs Act extortion and alleges an association-in-fact enterprise. These are pleading allegations, not adjudicated crimes, findings that property was obtained, or a verdict of racketeering liability. [R08](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/249/)

The clinics’ allegations of injury to their own business or property supply the concrete and statutory injury question at this stage. Pleading review uses the then-governing Hishon/Conley standard, not later pleading doctrine. The economic-motive question does not resolve whether the alleged acts satisfy every Hobbs Act element. [R08](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/249/)

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Review Posture and Requested Relief:** Ninth Circuit, 961 F.2d 813 (1992), reversed the District of Oregon and required relief for railcar owners. The district court had treated the statutory catchall as applicable but found no forbidden discrimination; the court of appeals treated the exemptions as prohibited differential treatment. Oregon seeks reversal of the exemption requirement. The railcar owners seek equal access to exemptions or corresponding relief from the challenged property taxation. [P10](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/332/)

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit’s judgment and remand.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the requirement that Oregon extend ordinary exemptions solely under §11503(b)(4). Leave any properly preserved claim under the specific assessment or rate provisions, or a genuinely distinct targeted-tax theory, to its lawful procedural posture.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Scope of the property-tax antidiscrimination provisions

**Entering Law:** The then-current 49 U.S.C. §11503(b)(1)-(3) separately regulates assessment ratios, collection of discriminatory assessments, and rates. Section 11503(a)(4) defines the relevant commercial-and-industrial comparison property by property subject to a tax levy, excluding specified agricultural and timber uses. Section (b)(4) prohibits another tax discriminating against a rail carrier. The statute must be read as a whole: a catchall cannot simply eliminate the comparison limitations in adjacent provisions. Burlington Northern R.R. v. Oklahoma Tax Commission, 481 U.S. 454 (1987), addresses the statutory assessment remedy, not every exemption. State taxing authority survives to the extent Congress has not displaced it. [P10](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/332/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain reversal, while reserving a genuinely targeted tax.

Section 11503's specific property-assessment and rate provisions compare rail property with taxable commercial and industrial property. Reading the “another tax” catchall to require extension of every exemption would undo that specified comparison. This supports Stone's proposed construction within the enacted structure and respects residual state taxing choices. [Provisions and record, 510 U.S. 332](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/332/).

The conclusion is **a defensible resolution of statutory ambiguity**, not an unavoidable semantic truth. The railroads' strongest argument is that exemptions can produce the very discriminatory burden the catchall was designed to prevent. Stone should answer that the adjacent provisions deliberately omit exempt property from their ordinary comparisons, while leaving open schemes that effectively single out rail carriers. He should not claim that exemptions can never discriminate or that a property-tax label defeats the catchall.

For lower courts, separate an assessment ratio, rate difference, ordinary category exemption, and an effectively carrier-specific levy. Exempting several substantial classes while taxing a broad range of businesses differs from exempting almost everything except railroads. The latter boundary is fact-sensitive: who actually pays, how the categories operate, and whether the tax has become a targeted instrument matter. Do not invent a percentage of taxable property at which the exception triggers.

Stone's equality theory supplies no independent demand that unlike commercial property receive identical exemptions. There is no listed classification or demonstrated animus. This case therefore illustrates genuine alignment with ordinary economic-regulation restraint. Remand removes the erroneous categorical exemption rule; it does not award Oregon immunity from preserved assessment, rate, or properly presented targeted-tax claims.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Oregon taxed railroad cars while exempting several categories of other business personal property. The dispute is about exemptions, rather than a higher assessment ratio or rate imposed on rail transportation property compared with other taxable commercial and industrial property. Nothing in the reported record establishes a scheme in which railroads alone, or as part of a narrowly targeted isolated group, bear all meaningful property taxation. The claim arises under the Railroad Revitalization and Regulatory Reform Act’s express federal judicial remedy. Its comparison class and exemption treatment are statutory issues, reviewed independently; an economic disparity is not automatically a constitutional classification claim. Do not decide a railroad-only tax that this record does not present. [P10](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/332/)
