#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Postdismissal jurisdiction

**Entering Law:** Article III and statutory jurisdiction, including 28 U.S.C. §1332, limit the court’s authority. Rule 41(a)(1)(ii), in its event-date numbering, governs stipulated dismissal. Ancillary jurisdiction allows appropriate management and vindication of the court’s own proceedings and disposition of sufficiently related matters, but requires a legal basis; Julian v. Central Trust, 193 U.S. 93 (1904), concerns enforcement tied to court orders. Rule 60(b) addresses relief from judgment, not automatic jurisdiction over a settlement contract. [R24](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/375/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain dismissal of the enforcement motion for lack of the asserted jurisdiction.

Original diversity jurisdiction does not permanently federalize every contract generated by litigation. After an unconditional Rule 41(a)(1)(ii) dismissal, a later settlement breach requires an independent jurisdictional basis or a valid relationship to enforcement of the court's own order. Awareness and approval of the agreement are insufficient. [Dismissal record, 511 U.S. 375](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/375/).

This is **a bounded clarification of ancillary jurisdiction**, strongly consistent with Stone's source-bound adjudication. Simulated *Willy* permits certain collateral sanctions for completed litigation misconduct; enforcing a new contractual obligation performs a different function. Judicial convenience and encouragement of settlements are reasons for careful drafting, not an Article III or statutory grant.

The operative distinction must remain legally accurate. Parties cannot create federal jurisdiction by consent alone. Within the court's lawful dismissal authority and at the proper time, incorporation of settlement obligations or an express retention arrangement can make compliance part of the judicial disposition. That is different from appending retention after jurisdiction has ended or deeming every recited agreement incorporated.

Courts should examine the actual dismissal terms, the requested relief, and any independent basis for a new action. A breach of an incorporated obligation may violate an order; breach of a separate agreement requires the ordinary contract forum. Rule 60(b) reopening is another question, not a synonym for contract enforcement.

The counterargument is duplicated litigation and loss of the original judge's familiarity. Stone should accept that practical cost where the parties chose an unconditional dismissal. Reverse without deciding breach, retroactively rewriting the order, or assuming that diversity necessarily exists for the new dispute. A competent state court or a properly supported new federal action remains available under its own requirements.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** A state-law agency-contract suit was removed on diversity grounds. The parties settled during trial and recited their agreement before the judge. They then executed a Rule 41(a)(1)(ii) stipulated dismissal with prejudice. The dismissal order did not incorporate the settlement or reserve jurisdiction. A later disagreement concerned performance of settlement obligations, and Guardian moved to enforce them in the original case. [R24](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/375/)

Subject-matter jurisdiction must be decided before settlement breach. Original diversity jurisdiction does not by itself establish jurisdiction over a new postdismissal contract controversy. No independent basis was established for the enforcement motion. A motion to reopen the original judgment is distinct from the enforcement request actually presented. [R24](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/375/)

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

The district courts had accepted the state restoration theory. Jones remains within this listed canonical matter, not a separate brief. Beecham and Jones seek recognition of state-law civil-rights restoration against their federal-conviction predicates. The United States seeks affirmance of both appellate judgments. [P23](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/368/)

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Fourth Circuit judgments in both included matters.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject state restoration as the asserted removal of the federal predicate. Preserve the remaining proceedings and any separate, properly presented federal restoration ground according to their actual posture.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Choice of law for the conviction exemption

**Entering Law:** Section 922(g) bars possession by persons with qualifying convictions. Section 921(a)(20) directs that conviction status be determined under the law of the jurisdiction where proceedings occurred and excludes convictions expunged, set aside, pardoned, or followed by restoration of civil rights, subject to its firearms reservation. Its scope follows the Firearms Owners’ Protection Act text, not an assumption that state law always controls a federal conviction. Chapman v. United States, 500 U.S. 453 (1991), permits lenity only after genuine unresolved penal ambiguity. [P23](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/368/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain affirmance on the convicting-sovereign rule.

Section 921(a)(20)'s adjoining provisions determine which convictions count and when their legal consequences have been removed. Reading the jurisdiction-of-proceedings direction to govern restoration as well as the initial conviction yields a coherent sovereign-specific rule. State restoration does not remove the federal consequence of a federal conviction merely because civil rights are often regulated by States. [Text and included cases, 511 U.S. 368](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/368/).

This is **ordinary statutory construction**, with a real textual counterargument: the restoration sentence does not repeat the choice-of-law phrase, and state law may control many affected civil rights. Stone should explain why the integrated definition and neighboring pardon/set-aside mechanisms overcome that reading. Do not simply assert federal supremacy as if it supplied missing statutory text.

Stone's fair-notice and lenity commitments do not require choosing the defendant's reading whenever two readings can be proposed. If context resolves the issue, lenity never activates. Conversely, difficulty obtaining federal restoration cannot be dismissed as irrelevant hardship: it is the practical cost of the chosen reading, but does not create a judicial restoration process. His broader arms views are not presented by this statutory exemption question.

Application begins with the sovereign of conviction, then asks whether that sovereign's applicable law supplies expungement, set-aside, pardon, or restoration and any continuing firearms reservation. A state conviction restored by its own State is different from the federal convictions here. Do not assume any particular federal mechanism was available to these defendants or decide its scope unnecessarily. Preserve separately presented grounds while rejecting reliance on state restoration alone in both included matters.

**Egalitarian application:** The defendant deserves an intelligible account of which sovereign's restoration changes federal firearm status, and the public retains the protection Congress actually enacted. Read the coordinated provisions in their legal setting rather than demanding knowledge of a hidden judicial preference or allowing unrelated state action to erase a federal conviction by analogy alone.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Both petitioners had prior federal convictions and later faced §922(g) firearms prosecutions. They relied on state restoration of civil rights. The question does not involve a state conviction restored by its own sovereign, a federal pardon actually granted, or a finding that every federal restorative mechanism was available to these petitioners. The issue is statutory treatment of a prior conviction, not a facial Second Amendment challenge or the validity of the underlying convictions. Keep statutory coverage, restoration, and any continued express firearms restriction distinct. A Court may not create a restoration procedure simply because one route is unavailable. [P23](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/368/)

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny the asserted tariff undercharges. No order rewriting the tariff or distributing the bankruptcy estate.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Effective tariff prerequisite and regulatory authority

**Entering Law:** The Interstate Commerce Act’s then-applicable filing and rate provisions, including 49 U.S.C. §§10761 and 10762, and ICC tariff-participation regulations require a complete ascertainable filed rate. Maislin Industries, U.S., Inc. v. Primary Steel, Inc., 497 U.S. 116 (1990), enforces effective filed rates notwithstanding negotiated discounts; it does not create missing tariff content. ICC v. American Trucking Assns., 467 U.S. 354 (1984), addresses the agency’s authority concerning tariff defects. Berwind-White Coal Mining Co. v. Chicago & Erie R.R., 235 U.S. 371 (1914), permits treatment of merely technical defects where adequate public notice remains. A missing authorized distance component differs from a harmless form error. [P24](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/431/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain denial of the undercharges, anchored in the tariff's legal incompleteness.

*Maislin*, 497 U.S. 116, enforces a valid filed rate despite a negotiated discount; it does not manufacture an effective mileage component after the carrier's authorized participation was canceled. The cancellation preceded these shipments under already operative ICC rules. Bankruptcy changes administration of the claim, not its legal content. [Tariff and participation record, 511 U.S. 431](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/431/).

This is **a narrow distinction**, not an equitable exception to filed rates. Stone should be precise about why the defect is substantive. The relevant distances may remain physically discoverable in the guide, but the filed arrangement no longer authorizes this carrier's incorporation. “Impossible to calculate” would overstate the problem; legally effective public tariff completeness is the issue. *Berwind-White*'s tolerance for technical defects remains where legal notice and effectiveness survive.

The strongest counterargument is that a private publisher's participation fee should not erase publicly filed rate obligations, allowing shippers a windfall. Stone must ground cancellation's effect in valid ICC authority and the actual incorporated tariff terms, not the publisher's unilateral policy alone. *American Trucking* informs that authority; simulated *Reiter*'s counterclaim and referral rules do not settle it.

Lower courts should verify the incorporated schedules, authorized participation, published cancellation, and shipment dates. A minor clerical irregularity with a complete effective tariff differs from a missing legally incorporated essential component. No negotiated-rate fairness inquiry is needed once the asserted filed rate fails its own prerequisites. Affirm on that basis without rewriting a tariff, creating a general carrier-bankruptcy exception, or adjudicating the estate's unrelated claims.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** The carrier’s tariff supplied monetary rates but incorporated a Household Goods Carriers’ Bureau mileage guide for the distance component. That guide required current participation, listed in another filed tariff. The Bureau published cancellation after the carrier failed to pay participation fees. The shipments to Kmart occurred after cancellation, at negotiated prices. Bankruptcy followed. The dispute is not a shipper’s equitable request to pay less than an otherwise valid, complete filed tariff. Bankruptcy changes who administers the claim, not whether an effective tariff existed. Tariff validity and the reasonableness of an otherwise effective rate are distinct. The relevant ICC regulations were already operative before shipment; a later judicial recognition of their effect is not automatically retroactive agency rulemaking. [P24](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/431/)
