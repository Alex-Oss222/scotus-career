#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Review Posture and Requested Relief:** Eighth Circuit, 981 F.2d 991 (1992), affirmed summary judgment for the estate in a refund action. It treated the Treasury regulation as inapplicable, state law as controlling, and taxation as impermissibly retroactive. The United States seeks reversal of the refund judgment. The estate seeks to preserve the tax-free effect of the disclaimer. [P19](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/224/)

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Eighth Circuit’s judgment.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject the asserted basis for the refund and return the case for implementation consistent with taxation of the 1979 transfer. No reassessment of unrelated estate items.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Federal gift transfer and state disclaimer fiction

**Entering Law:** 26 U.S.C. §§2501(a)(1) and 2511(a) broadly reach gratuitous property transfers. Treasury Regulation §25.2511-1(c)(2), in the applicable version, affords limited disclaimer treatment requiring local-law effectiveness and action within a reasonable time after knowledge of the transfer. Jewett v. Commissioner, 455 U.S. 305 (1982), measures that time from knowledge of creation of the interest, not later possession. State-law relation back does not itself control federal gift taxation. Revenue Act of 1932 §501(b) protects transfers made on or before enactment; it does not automatically exempt every later disposition of an earlier-created interest. [P19](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/224/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain reversal of the refund judgment.

State law determines the property interest and effective disclaimer; federal law determines whether the 1979 disposition is a taxable gift. The trust's 1917 origin does not make every later gratuitous transfer pre-1932. *Jewett*, 455 U.S. 305, also makes first knowledge, rather than eventual possession, central to the applicable reasonable-time disclaimer inquiry. [Dates and tax provisions, 511 U.S. 224](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/224/).

This fits Stone's source separation and protection of concrete reliance: the correct reliance question concerns the legal consequences of the later act, not whether an old asset can never be subject to later tax law. It is **application of existing federal tax principles**, not an exception to his presumption against retroactive liability.

The proposed alternative reasoning is lawful if each route is independently sufficient. If the regulation applies, decades after actual knowledge is not a reasonable-time disclaimer on these facts. If the regulation does not reach the pre-Act interest, the estate still needs a basis for removing the later transfer from §§2501 and 2511; state relation back alone is not that basis. Explain why the broad statutory transfer language reaches the value directed to the children, rather than treating regulatory inapplicability itself as proof of taxation.

The counterargument is that the state disclaimer means Irvine never owned the transferred interest. Federal taxation need not adopt that relation-back characterization of a real disposition. A prompt refusal before acceptance presents a different case; Stone should not announce an arbitrary deadline or tax every disclaimer categorically. Reverse the asserted refund ground while leaving Minnesota property law intact. Use the applicable historical regulation, not later qualified-disclaimer provisions.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** A grandfather created the trust in 1917. Irvine knew of her contingent interest at least when she reached age twenty-one in 1931. In 1979, following termination of the trust, she disclaimed five-sixteenths of her interest, causing each of her five children to receive one-sixteenth of her distributed share. Minnesota law recognized the disclaimer. Federal tax and interest were paid, and her estate brought this refund action. No factual basis makes the forty-seven-year-plus delay a prompt disclaimer following first knowledge. State law identifies the property interest and effectiveness of the disclaimer; federal law decides the tax consequence. Separate the date the interest was created from the date the challenged gratuitous transfer occurred. The pre-1977 interest governs which regulatory version is relevant; the later statutory qualified-disclaimer provisions are not silently substituted. [P19](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/224/)

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand the judgment enforcing the discriminatory exclusive-routing requirement.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Set aside enforcement of the challenged flow-control command against Carbone. Leave neutral safety rules, lawful fees, and independently valid public financing available. Do not redesign the Town’s contract, guarantee a competitor’s profits, or decide the validity of a materially different genuinely public system. [R23](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/383/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Exclusive local processing and discrimination

**Entering Law:** Philadelphia v. New Jersey, 437 U.S. 617 (1978); Dean Milk Co. v. Madison, 340 U.S. 349 (1951); Fort Gratiot, 504 U.S. 353 (1992); and Chemical Waste, 504 U.S. 334 (1992), constrain discriminatory local reservation of commerce. Pike, 397 U.S. 137 (1970), supplies the incidental-burden alternative if the ordinance is genuinely evenhanded. Market participation under Hughes v. Alexandria Scrap, 426 U.S. 794 (1976), and South-Central Timber, 467 U.S. 82 (1984), is distinct from coercive regulation of others’ transactions. [R23](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/383/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain rejection of the mandatory routing to this private operator, while explaining why the present arrangement is private favoritism rather than genuine public provision.

*Dean Milk* and the inherited waste cases support scrutiny of a local processing reservation even where other local competitors are excluded too. The ordinance regulates transactions beyond the Town's own purchases; a financing contract does not itself create market-participant immunity. [Contract and ordinance, 511 U.S. 383](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/383/).

Stone's Master permits genuinely public service organization. The five-year transfer arrangement and eventual one-dollar public acquisition therefore deserve analysis, not dismissal as irrelevant future facts. Examine current ownership, control, revenue entitlement, risk, and the reach of coercive exclusivity. Here the guaranteed waste stream protects a particular private operator's current processing market. Future public ownership does not alone transform that present commercial reservation into government purchasing.

This is **a defensible extension/application of discrimination doctrine**, not a rule that every franchise or public monopoly is invalid. If the arrangement is instead characterized as evenhanded public-service financing, *Pike* presents a stronger competing route; Stone must explain why the private market reservation warrants the more demanding inquiry. Do not borrow the later public-facility rule from *United Haulers*.

Health standards, inspection, and nondiscriminatory financing can serve the stated objectives without the same exclusion. The Town's strongest objection is that reliable throughput is needed to finance safe waste services. That is a genuine institutional concern, but a contractual revenue promise cannot by itself justify discriminatory regulation.

Lower courts should distinguish a government buying disposal for its own waste from compelling everyone to buy a favored private service. Set aside the challenged enforcement, leaving neutral requirements and lawful public financing available. Neither competitor profitability nor invalidation of the financing contract follows automatically.

**User-directed case emphasis:** The decisive distinction is coercive routing to a selected private enterprise, not the bare fact of municipal economic involvement. Preserve Stone's room for genuine public waste provision that treats competing private actors evenhandedly under the law then available. This private arrangement cannot establish that every later public routing system is unconstitutional.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** The Town contracted for a private operator to construct and operate a transfer station for five years, after which the Town would acquire it for one dollar. A minimum-waste guarantee supported financing and a tipping fee higher than competing disposal costs. The ordinance required local waste, including already-sorted nonrecyclable residue from Carbone’s facility, to pass through the designated station. Carbone instead shipped residue to out-of-state destinations. The Town sued to enforce the route. [R23](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/383/)

The enforcement judgment burdens Carbone’s actual operations and interstate disposal transactions. The challenged service is processing and disposal, not simply physical ownership of garbage. The station’s future transfer to public ownership does not establish that it is presently a government-operated service. No unlisted companion receives a separate module. [R23](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/383/)
