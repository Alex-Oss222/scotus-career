#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand because the jury was not required to find knowledge of the characteristics making the rifle a statutory machinegun.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Further proceedings must use the correct mens rea requirement and respect ordinary sufficiency and retrial constraints. Do not direct acquittal on an unmade finding that Staples lacked knowledge, and do not invalidate the registration law. [R26](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/600/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Mens rea for statutory weapon characteristics

**Entering Law:** 26 U.S.C. §§5845(a)–(b), 5861(d), and 5871 define the regulated firearm and penalty. Morissette v. United States, 342 U.S. 246 (1952), and United States v. United States Gypsum, 438 U.S. 422 (1978), supply the mens rea presumption. United States v. Freed, 401 U.S. 601 (1971), concerns unregistered grenades and the knowledge necessary there; United States v. Balint, 258 U.S. 250 (1922), addresses public-welfare offenses. [R26](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/600/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain the knowledge-of-characteristics requirement and remand.

*Morissette* and *Gypsum* support a culpability presumption despite silence. The difference between an ordinarily possessed semiautomatic rifle and a weapon with automatic capability, together with the serious penalty, supports requiring knowledge of the facts making this a statutory machinegun. *Freed* involved grenades and does not necessarily resolve a concealed weapon characteristic. [Record and statutory definition, 511 U.S. 600](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/600/).

This is **a defensible mens rea clarification**, not a Second Amendment ruling or rejection of all public-welfare offenses. Stone's support for gun regulation and factual culpability are compatible. Knowledge that registration is legally required remains different from knowledge of automatic capability; any effective *Ratzlaf* ruling concerns a different willfulness provision.

The strongest objection is that firearms are dangerous, closely regulated objects whose possessors should investigate unusual characteristics. That supports care obligations but does not by itself establish that Congress imposed this felony without knowledge of the condition. Stone should avoid declaring all gun possession innocent or making the penalty alone dispositive. The combined statutory context matters.

Application permits circumstantial proof through firing behavior, modification work, communications, training, or observation of the mechanism. A defendant who knowingly modified the rifle cannot avoid proof by denying knowledge of the legal term machinegun. A latent malfunction producing automatic fire without notice presents the opposite case. Whether Staples's testimony is credible belongs to the factfinder.

Reverse the deficient instructional judgment with ordinary sufficiency and retrial rules preserved. Do not equate an omitted element with an acquittal or import later error-review law. Other National Firearms Act devices may present different notice and public-welfare considerations; the opinion should identify this factual-knowledge boundary rather than announce universal knowledge of illegality.

**Egalitarian application:** Public safety supports regulation of dangerous weapons; it does not eliminate the need to determine Congress's chosen culpability requirement. A possessor's actual knowledge of automatic capability may be proved circumstantially. Fair warning protects an unsuspecting possessor without shielding someone who knows the material characteristic but disputes its statutory label.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Officers seized an AR-15 rifle that could fire automatically. Staples testified that it had never done so while he possessed it and that he did not know it had that capability. He requested an instruction requiring proof of that knowledge. The jury was not required to find it. The case does not involve a preserved challenge to the search or a Second Amendment claim. [R26](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/600/)

The statutory instruction issue is preserved. Knowledge of physical characteristics, knowledge of registration status, and knowledge of illegality are distinct. The credibility of Staples’s denial is for the lawful factfinder, not presumed by this packet. [R26](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/600/)
