## Provisional result and its exact scope

All eight non-Stone Justices support ending federal review because the Tennessee judgment rests on an adequate and independent state constitutional ground. Their primary formal disposition is **dismissal of the writ for want of jurisdiction**, leaving the state judgment undisturbed. They also accept a narrower **dismissal as improvidently granted**, if the Court elects to terminate this vehicle without a reasoned jurisdictional holding. That acceptable alternative is expressly limited to the same nonmerits result; it does not include affirmance, merits reversal, vacatur for reconsideration under Lowenfield, a clarification remand, or reinstatement of death.

The sufficient common ground is the original Tennessee constitutional ruling, confirmed by the Tennessee Supreme Court's published explanation in Howell. A correction of the state court's federal analysis would not remove its state prohibition or its existing determination that Middlebrooks requires resentencing. No Justice's acceptance of this ground is an Eighth Amendment vote. Howell is not used as a way to invent a new state ground after federal review began.

These are provisional simulation inferences supported by the identified law and sources, not historical votes, factual reports of private views, or a public conference poll. ## Justice-specific commitments

### Blackmun

**Threshold and disposition:** Dismiss for want of jurisdiction; accepts the narrower nonprecedential DIG. No federal narrowing, harmlessness, or Cox determination. The Tennessee resentencing judgment remains in force; guilt and the possibility of seeking death on lawful resentencing are untouched.

**Personal support:** His separate opinion in *Michigan v. Long*, 463 U.S. 1032, 1053 (1983), accepted jurisdiction on that particular record but refused the new presumption because it increased the danger of advisory opinions. That is direct support for examining what this state judgment actually rests on. His simulated Sochor position would have reached the Florida instructional claim, but that disagreement concerned whether the asserted preservation basis actually prevented review, not a power to displace an established independent state constitutional judgment.

**Adversarial test and join boundary:** The strongest path toward reach is that a later footnote cannot itself revise the judgment under review. Here the original opinion already announces the state ground; Howell confirms rather than creates it. Join a narrow explanation of that fact and the resulting absence of federal authority. Refuse a general presumption against jurisdiction in every mixed opinion, an endorsement of the federal duplication analysis, or an invented categorical prohibition of capital punishment. A minimal nonmerits dismissal removes all such barriers.

### Stevens

**Threshold and disposition:** Dismiss for want of jurisdiction; accepts the narrower nonprecedential DIG. No merits or finality holding; preserve the state sentence reversal and resentencing.

**Personal support:** His *Long* dissent, 463 U.S. at 1065–1072, opposed presumptive review where state-law protection could independently sustain the judgment. It expressly distinguished correcting judgments from revising opinions and rejected federal uniformity alone as a reason to intervene. The original state-law text plus Howell presents stronger grounds for nonreview than the ambiguity he would have resolved against jurisdiction in Long. His simulated Sochor merits-reach position is preserved as a different assessment of that procedural record.

**Adversarial test and join boundary:** The State's strongest institutional argument is that leaving federal reasoning uncorrected affects uniform federal law. His own Long explanation answers it: the asserted federal error must affect the judgment. He joins only a disposition leaving Tennessee free to supply its own protection. No agreement to Tennessee's Eighth Amendment reasoning or to Reid's broader felony-murder restrictions is needed. He would accept omission of a general discussion of the Long presumption if a short dismissal fully states the nonmerits result.

### O'Connor

**Threshold and disposition:** Dismiss for want of jurisdiction; accepts a case-specific DIG without a new jurisdictional holding. Do not reach federal narrowing, sentence harmlessness, or Cox finality.

**Personal support:** She authored *Long*, including its express recognition that a bona fide alternative independent state ground defeats review, 463 U.S. at 1040–1042. She also authored *Coleman v. Thompson*, 501 U.S. 722, 729–732, 735–740 (1991), distinguishing the jurisdictional bar on direct review from habeas rules. She joined simulated Sochor Part II-A's independent-ground holding. Those positions make a presumption of federal dependence inappropriate once the actual state-law independence is established.

**Adversarial test and join boundary:** Long's reference to the face of the judgment or opinion supplies the strongest objection to placing dispositive weight on a subsequent case. The narrow answer rests on the original express state holding and uses Howell as authoritative confirmation. She will not join a rule that any later statement automatically defeats review, nor a rule permitting merits review merely because federal law influenced the state court. Preserve Long's conditional presumption and state precisely why its condition is absent here. No sentence-correction holding is needed.

### Scalia

**Threshold and disposition:** Dismiss for want of jurisdiction; accepts the narrower DIG. No vote approving or rejecting duplication under the Eighth Amendment and no federal harmless-error ruling.

**Personal support:** His join in O'Connor's *Coleman* opinion includes the direct-review distinction and the principle that an adequate substantive or procedural state ground removes power to issue an advisory federal ruling. His join in simulated Sochor Part II-A applies that limit notwithstanding alternative merits discussion. His disagreement with Sochor's separate constitutional weighing-error premise, recorded in the entering slice, remains intact and is not silently withdrawn by leaving the Tennessee judgment untouched.

**Adversarial test and join boundary:** The strongest alternative is that federal narrowing analysis supplied the state court's sole legal premise, despite state constitutional citations. Howell expressly contradicts that characterization, and the original text separately reasons from Tennessee law. He joins a narrow jurisdictional disposition; he refuses dicta that the Eighth Amendment invalidates the duplicate factor or that the surviving torture aggravator constitutionally requires resentencing. A recital of the state court's existing remedy must remain a description, not federal endorsement. A bare DIG avoids those disputed propositions as well.

### Kennedy

**Threshold and disposition:** Dismiss for want of jurisdiction; accepts the narrower nonmerits DIG. Do not decide duplication, harmlessness, or an unnecessary Cox question.

**Personal support:** His join in *Coleman*, 501 U.S. at 729–732, supports the direct-review restriction. His joins in simulated Sochor's state-ground and actual-cure holdings distinguish a barred claim from a reachable sentencing error. His simulated Stringer opinion supplies the requirement of adequate correction once a constitutional weighing error is properly before the Court; it does not create jurisdiction to correct an independent state judgment.

**Adversarial test and join boundary:** The available merits path invokes Lowenfield and the continuing valid aggravator. Those considerations would matter only if correcting federal law could change this judgment. Howell and the original opinion show why it cannot. He joins a narrowly stated jurisdictional explanation and the consequence that Tennessee's resentencing order stands. He will not join an inference that every duplicative factor is federally invalid or that a valid factor automatically cures all weighing error. Omission of the unreachable merits analysis resolves both barriers without changing his prior published positions.

### Souter

**Threshold and disposition:** Dismiss for want of jurisdiction; accepts a DIG confined to ending this vehicle. Federal narrowing, harmlessness, and Cox remain unadjudicated.

**Personal support:** He joined *Coleman* and authored simulated Sochor Part II-A. That holding recognizes that alternative federal discussion does not itself destroy a sufficient independent state basis. Sochor's distinct sentence-correction holdings apply only to claims the Court can reach. Here the state court's express statement and clarification are stronger evidence of substantive independence than an inference drawn solely from citations.

**Adversarial test and join boundary:** The strongest objection is an antecedent-federal-premise theory: a state rule may borrow its content so completely that it would change with federal law. Howell specifically says the federal inquiry asked whether duplication **also** violated the Eighth Amendment. He therefore supports nonreview on this record, while refusing a general equation between any state constitutional citation and independence. The explanation must connect the state prohibition to the same resentencing judgment. A terse jurisdictional dismissal or narrower DIG satisfies that boundary without creating a new capital-sentencing rule.

### Thomas

**Threshold and disposition:** Dismiss for want of jurisdiction; accepts the narrower DIG. No merits, harmlessness, or Cox vote. No federal order reinstating death or disturbing guilt.

**Personal support:** His join in simulated Sochor Part II-A directly supports nonreview of an independent state disposition. His pre-divergence dissent in *Doe v. Sullivan*, 938 F.2d 1370 (D.C. Cir. 1991), separately insisted that federal jurisdiction must persist throughout litigation and expressly withheld a merits view when he found no live case. Doe concerns mootness, not state grounds, and is used only for that threshold/merits separation. His simulated Sochor acceptance of a sufficient existing cure in that record does not establish harmlessness in this one.

**Adversarial test and join boundary:** A theoretical federal merits question is insufficient when the State cannot obtain a changed judgment. The strongest contrary position would identify an operative federal dependency in the state ground; neither the original opinion nor Howell supplies one after the express clarification. He joins a narrow statement of the jurisdictional defect and leaves all Eighth Amendment questions open. He refuses any assumed federal obligation to resentence based on Howell's distinct harmless-error analysis. The minimal revision is to describe the Tennessee remedy as continuing by its own force.

### Ginsburg

**Threshold and disposition:** Dismiss for want of jurisdiction; accepts the narrower nonprecedential DIG. No decision on federal narrowing, sentence harmlessness, or Cox finality; preserve the state judgment's own operation.

**Personal support and limit:** Her opinion in *Spann v. Colonial Village, Inc.*, 899 F.2d 24, 27–30 (D.C. Cir. 1990), resolves jurisdiction before the merits and requires concrete injury likely to be redressed by a favorable decision, rejecting abstract concerns as a substitute. It is a standing case, not a decision about independent state grounds; it is corroborating methodological support only. No pre-event Ginsburg opinion on this exact state-ground question was established in this clean review. Her concrete commitment rests on binding Long, Coleman, and simulated Sochor, together with the now explicit state-law ground, rather than an invented prior capital or state-ground position.

**Adversarial test and join boundary:** The State has a concrete desire to restore the sentence, but correcting federal reasoning cannot supply that relief while the independent state prohibition remains. The strongest route toward further proceedings would be a real uncertainty about that independence. Howell and the original holding resolve it without deciding a contested Eighth Amendment standard. She joins a case-specific jurisdictional explanation, or a narrower DIG, but no categorical rule treating lower-court federal citations as irrelevant and no purported judgment about the merits of a claim the Court cannot reach.
