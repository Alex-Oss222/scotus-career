#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit on the requirement of pre-seizure process, reverse its statutory-delay basis for possible dismissal, and remand.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Allow the timely forfeiture case to proceed under lawful procedures. Remand for the lower courts to determine the source-authorized consequences of the unconstitutional interim seizure, including any properly available restoration or accounting; do not order dismissal of forfeiture or an unsupported sovereign damages award.

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Pre-seizure process

**Entering Law:** Fifth Amendment Due Process Clause; Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319 (1976); Fuentes v. Shevin, 407 U.S. 67 (1972); Connecticut v. Doehr, 501 U.S. 1 (1991). Calero-Toledo v. Pearson Yacht Leasing Co., 416 U.S. 663 (1974), permits seizure without prior hearing in a setting involving movable property and exceptional governmental interests. Soldal v. Cook County, 506 U.S. 56 (1992), prevents treating one constitutional protection as automatically displacing another. [R03](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/43/)

##### Issue 2: Statutory timing and consequence

**Entering Law:** 19 U.S.C. §§1602–1604 impose prompt administrative duties; §1621 supplies a five-year limitations period. United States v. Montalvo-Murillo, 495 U.S. 711 (1990), and Brock v. Pierce County, 476 U.S. 253 (1986), distinguish mandatory timing language from an unstated forfeiture of governmental authority. [R03](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/43/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain the split disposition and separate the remedial questions.

The Fourth Amendment warrant does not answer whether due process permits taking rents and control before an adversarial hearing. *Mathews*, *Doehr*, and *Fuentes* permit Stone to weigh the substantial property interest, ex parte error risk, and availability of preservation measures. *Calero-Toledo*'s concern with movable property supplies a **narrow, legally grounded distinction**. Simulated *Soldal*, *Buena Vista*, and *Austin* reinforce the separation of seizure, ownership defenses, and punishment; none itself resolves this hearing question. [Record and relevant authorities, 510 U.S. 43](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/43/).

Land's immovability matters, but does not alone eliminate dissipation: an owner can waste it, divert rents, or transfer interests. Stone should require an actual explanation why notice, a lis pendens, bond, or protective order cannot secure the legitimate objective. Emergency seizure remains available on demonstrated necessity with appropriate later process. Government bears the justification for bypassing the ordinary hearing; it need not prove the entire forfeiture case before every preservation measure. The long delay and absence of exigency make this seizure particularly difficult to defend.

On statutory timing, retain refusal to invent dismissal for violation of §§1602–1604 when the action satisfies §1621. *Brock* and *Montalvo-Murillo* separate official duties from an unstated loss of power. That conclusion does not render promptness optional: independently authorized relief for unconstitutional delay remains possible.

Restoring unlawfully diverted benefits or accounting for possession requires its own remedial basis; an unconstitutional interim seizure does not necessarily invalidate the eventual forfeiture or waive sovereign immunity. This is a strong example of Stone protecting property and governmental enforcement simultaneously through distinct legal rules.

**Egalitarian application:** Equal regard protects owners against dispossession without an effective hearing and the public against concealment or continued unlawful use. The statutory forfeiture purpose does not erase the separate due-process question. Require ordinary notice and opportunity to be heard for real property, while permitting the government to establish genuine exigency and use supported, less intrusive measures to preserve the property.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Police searched Good’s Hawaii home on January 31, 1985, finding marijuana and related items. He pleaded guilty to a state drug offense. Federal forfeiture proceedings began August 8, 1989; an ex parte warrant followed on August 18 and seizure on August 21. Good then rented the house to tenants. The Government left them in place but took control of occupancy and redirected $900 monthly rent to the Marshal. No exigency was established. [R03](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/43/)

The in rem action rests on 21 U.S.C. §881(a)(7), with §881(d) incorporating customs procedures; appellate review is under §1254(1). Fourth Amendment compliance and Fifth Amendment process are distinct questions. The forfeiture merits, excessive-fines limits, and the appropriate remedy for an unlawful interim seizure must not be conflated. [R03](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/43/)

#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Plan assets and insurance exclusion

**Entering Law:** ERISA §§3(21)(A), 401(b)(2), 404 and 514, 29 U.S.C. §§1002(21)(A), 1101(b)(2), 1104, 1144. The exclusion extends only to the extent a policy provides benefits whose amount the insurer guarantees. SEC v. United Benefit Life Insurance Co., 387 U.S. 202 (1967), distinguishes insured investment features from a genuine insurance guarantee. [R04](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/86/)

##### Issue 2: State insurance law and administrative interpretation

**Entering Law:** McCarran-Ferguson Act, 15 U.S.C. §1012(b); ERISA’s preemption and insurance-saving clauses. Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. NRDC, 467 U.S. 837 (1984), remains inherited law. A 1975 Labor Department interpretive bulletin addressed prohibited transactions; the Department’s later litigating position favors broader general-account exclusion. [R04](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/86/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain affirmance, with careful treatment of mixed guarantees and deference.

ERISA §401(b)(2)'s qualified exclusion permits separating guaranteed benefits from funds still exposed to investment risk borne by the plan. Stone's source-based treatment of private power is well served: fiduciary duties arise from statutory assets and functions, not the insurer's size or the equities of a pension dispute. Simulated *Mertens* concerns remedies and *Fabe* a different insurance-law conflict. Neither decides fiduciary status. [Contract and statutory record, 510 U.S. 86](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/86/).

The proposed component-by-component reading is **ordinary statutory interpretation**, albeit contested. Examine guaranteed benefit amounts, conversion terms, discretion over free funds, and who bears investment losses. A minimum guarantee does not necessarily exempt all upside-sensitive funds; conversely, imperfect liquidity does not itself prove that an insurer holds plan assets. Courts must identify the component and fiduciary function, then separately decide breach and loss. This avoids turning all general-account insurance into ERISA trust property.

The strongest counterargument concerns administrability and insurers' reliance on pooled general accounts and agency guidance. Commingling makes attribution difficult, but does not erase the statutory phrase limiting the exclusion. Stone should engage the actual guarantees and guidance, not simply characterize the position as changing. Under entering *Chevron* law, inconsistency is not an automatic disqualification from deference. If the interpretation conflicts with the best-supported unambiguous statutory boundary, it fails there; otherwise the applicable deference analysis must be completed.

McCarran-Ferguson does not categorically displace ERISA's express insurance provisions. Preserve compatible state regulation and adjudicate any concrete conflict separately. No finding of fiduciary breach or monetary entitlement follows from affirming status.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** The retirement plan deposits funds with Hancock under a participating group annuity contract. Some amounts purchase guaranteed annuities; the disputed free funds remain in Hancock’s general account, fluctuate with investment experience, and lack a fixed guaranteed return or contractually fixed conversion protection sufficient to transfer the investment risk. Harris alleged inability to access funds and imprudent contract administration. Fiduciary status, not proven breach or damages, is the immediate question. [R04](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/86/)

The statutory fiduciary-status issue is preserved and reviewable under §1254(1). A partial remand below does not eliminate Supreme Court review of a federal appellate decision under that provision. The 1977 contract amendment and disputed risk allocation must be treated as contract features, not assumed new findings. [R04](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/86/)
