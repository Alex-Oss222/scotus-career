#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain the Teague disposition, reserving the direct-review constitutional question.

The relevant landscape is January 2, 1986, when the conviction and sentence became final. *Bullington* and *Rumsey* concerned acquittal-like capital proceedings; *DiFrancesco* resisted equating ordinary sentencing with acquittal. Extending the bar to this noncapital recidivist rehearing was plausible, but not dictated. The State consistently asserted nonretroactivity, unlike the posture proposed in *Schiro*. [Finality and procedural history, 510 U.S. 383](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/92-1500.ZO.html).

This is **application of Teague**, not rejection of Stone's protection against repeated opportunities to prove punishment. The simulated *Graham* result rested on already established effective-mitigation law; simulated *Gilmore*, which Stone joined, denied a new procedural rule. Neither protection of defendants in one case nor denial in another supplies a result here. The source and finality date do.

Explain the exception question rather than assuming it. A rule against reproving recidivism after insufficient evidence limits the sentencing procedure; it does not put an underlying crime or class of defendants beyond substantive punishment. Its protection against repeated litigation does not make it a watershed accuracy procedure under the then-recognized exception. Preserve that exception as it existed in 1994, without importing its later elimination.

For application, identify what facts the original adjudicator necessarily decided, whether the proceeding was capital or noncapital, and the prefinality precedent. A genuine capital acquittal within *Bullington* differs from an ordinary enhancement reversal for inadequate proof. The strongest objection is that finality already attached to the State's failed proof; that is a serious merits argument for an eligible direct-review case. It does not show the extension was already compelled in 1986. Reverse habeas relief on this ground without adopting later *Monge* or resolving other claims.

**Egalitarian application:** Finality protects reliable adjudication, victims, witnesses, and legitimate enforcement; the defendant retains the protection that law at the relevant finality date actually supplied. Identify why this claim seeks a new rule and why the then-existing exceptions do not apply rather than treating the word collateral as the end of the inquiry.
