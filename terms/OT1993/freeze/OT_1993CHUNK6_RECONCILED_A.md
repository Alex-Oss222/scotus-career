The opinion-compatible rule requires a substantial risk of serious harm and, for each defendant, awareness of facts indicating that risk, actual appreciation of it, and unreasonable disregard. Mere negligence or what an official should have known is insufficient. Knowledge may be proved by ordinary circumstantial evidence; obviousness permits, but does not compel, an inference. The official need not know the eventual attacker or victim-specific details if the known substantial danger covers the prisoner's situation. An honest failure to appreciate even an obvious danger differs from deliberate refusal to confirm strongly suspected facts or inferences; the latter may establish actual awareness, but the jury may not replace the subjective requirement with a reasonable-person standard. A reasonable protective response defeats culpable disregard even when harm occurs. These are proof and culpability qualifications of the frozen rule, not findings about any defendant.

The absence of an express complaint is not dispositive. Reconsideration applies whether additional discovery is ultimately allowed or denied. The record does not permit this Court to accept alternative transfer-official responsibility or present-risk grounds as conclusively established; respondents may develop them below. Personal-capacity damages and official-capacity prospective relief remain separate. Meyer supplies no direct agency damages action, and Elder requires the preserved immunity question to use the relevant law existing at the time of conduct; today's clarification is not retroactive legal notice in 1989. The official-capacity-only Terre Haute warden and Bureau Director cannot silently become personal damages defendants.

Prospective relief requires proof of a current substantial danger and knowing unreasonable nonresponse, persisting through the litigation and likely to continue. Either side may use later developments in the district court's proper procedural discretion. Established danger and actual notice developed during litigation may bear on future relief; a filed allegation alone does not establish the danger. Officials may show that the risk or culpable response has ceased and will not recur. Equitable relief remains discretionary and appropriately confined; no blanket ban on all penitentiary placement is directed. The report's discussion of ordinary equitable caution and adequate internal grievance procedures does not establish a new mandatory exhaustion holding in this record, and no later statutory exhaustion regime enters.

### Five-Justice opinion-compatible ground and limits

The tax label does not immunize an exaction from Double Jeopardy scrutiny. The assessed 1987 scheme combines a criminal-possession predicate, an arrest-triggered return and enforcement system, collection subordinate to criminal fines and forfeitures, an extraordinary charge, and an assessment on the confiscated taxable drugs. Together these features make the surviving exaction punishment, and its separate collection after the criminal sanctions is forbidden on the same-offense premise accepted in this litigation. A high rate, deterrent purpose, illegality, delayed collection or confiscation alone is not the test. The ground does not depend on turning presumed destruction into a proved fact.

Halper supplies examination of substance rather than label; Magnano recognizes that penalizing features may deprive a nominal tax of its revenue character. Sanchez leaves ordinary high and deterrent taxes intact; this scheme's criminal-process-dependent operation distinguishes it. Helvering preserves genuine remedial tax additions. The opinion must not turn Halper's fixed-civil-penalty accounting method into the measure of every tax, or adopt Austin's partly-punitive Excessive Fines test wholesale as the Double Jeopardy standard. It decides a later sanction by the same State for the same offense on the preserved premise, not all civil forfeitures, taxes on unlawful income, separate-sovereign proceedings, the reverse sequence of civil sanction then prosecution, or lawful cumulative sanctions imposed in one prosecution.

The addendum controls the statutory and remedy details: the marijuana charge is the greater of 10 percent assessed market value or $100 per ounce, and hashish's distinct $250 alternative is not the marijuana rule. The taxpayer return and substitute officer report have their own conditions. Collection after satisfaction of fines and forfeitures is not a finding that every assessment step occurred after sentencing. The surviving constitutional dispute concerns roughly $181,000 principal, not the original aggregate demand; neither the $45 discrepancy in reported totals nor exact remittance can be solved by this opinion. State-law rejections of plants, oil and tar remain outside review.

## Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc.

### Final components

All eight non-Stone Justices — Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg — affirm the Tenth Circuit's dismissal of the interlocutory appeal for want of jurisdiction. All support the following common formulation and limits.

Section 1291's narrow collateral-order category requires a conclusive decision resolving an important issue completely separate from the merits that is effectively unreviewable after final judgment. The asserted private settlement protection fails the importance/effective-review inquiry even assuming conclusiveness and separateness. A privately negotiated promise that can be described as freedom from trial does not carry the public importance of the recognized constitutional or official immunity categories. Trial costs and the impossibility of undoing the experience of litigation do not suffice. Appealability concerns the category, not this agreement's price, drafting, supposed clarity or alleged rescission error. The Court leaves open whether a different privately originating right could qualify and preserves express statutory interlocutory routes; it adopts no categorical rule that private rights can never receive early review.

**Independently sufficient adequate-vindication ground:** Even apart from the dispute over importance terminology, settlement rights of this category remain adequately vindicable after final judgment. Protection against ultimate liability remains reviewable; avoiding trial is ordinarily an incident of that bargain, not the distinct public injury guarded against by double-jeopardy or official-immunity rules. Ordinary contract enforcement or other lawful district-court protections may address a valid promise or improper litigation when their own requirements are met. This is not a finding that Digital wins a contract action, deserves sanctions, or has proved rescission invalid. The neutral packet expressly presented postjudgment review and left breach and reopening undecided; the frozen common rationale already accepted imperfect postjudgment vindication. The comparator confirms the independent sufficiency of this explanation rather than introducing a new fact or claim.

Germain leaves §1292(b) open on its own conditions: written district certification of a controlling legal question, substantial ground for difference of opinion, potential material advancement of termination, timely application and appellate permission. None is found satisfied here. Kokkonen's settlement-enforcement jurisdiction rule reserves Rule 60(b) reopening; it supplies neither immediate appealability nor a merits ruling on the order under review. Morgan Stanley's final-judgment rule concerns compulsory legislative reopening and expressly preserves independently lawful judicial reopening; it does not transform this private-settlement order into a collateral order. No historical Supreme Court stay is entered as an in-world action.

### Opinion-compatible grounds and boundaries

**Five-person litigation-fee ground:** The American Rule requires sufficiently explicit statutory authority for the disputed shifting of private litigation expenses; no particular verbal incantation is invariably indispensable. In CERCLA's text and structure, §107(a)(4)(B)'s necessary response costs and §101(25)'s related enforcement activities do not clearly authorize the fees of this private cost-recovery lawsuit. The existence of a private action, and the general benefit of private enforcement, do not themselves supply that incident. Congress expressly addressed fees in other CERCLA provisions while omitting an analogous authorization here and in §113. The Court need not deny the private cost-recovery cause, decide every relation between §§107 and 113, or declare that every implied cause necessarily excludes fees. It may explain the textual difference between a statutory payment obligation/private action and sufficiently explicit authorization of these litigation costs. It does not decide government fee recovery.

**Three-person broader ground:** Blackmun, Scalia and Thomas read the actual enactment as sufficient authorization. Section 101(25) expressly includes related enforcement activities within response, and the cost-recovery suit enforces the liability §107(a)(4)(B) creates for necessary response costs incurred by other persons. Legal expenses naturally form part of that enforcement. Their position retains the American Rule; it finds Congress's exception in the combined terms and does not demand the literal words attorney's fees. Express fee language elsewhere does not narrow a definition they find unambiguous. Necessity, relation to response, consistency with the national contingency plan and proper amount remain requirements; no automatic prevailing-party award follows.

**Common identification ground:** Actual professional work identifying other responsible polluters may itself perform a necessary response function distinct from litigation. The established Air Force search and consequent EPA action support that category. The lawyer's title neither excludes the work nor proves that it qualifies; equivalent investigative work could be performed by a nonlawyer. On remand, actual task, response function, necessity, plan consistency and amount must be established. The entire undifferentiated $155,500 award is not automatically restored, and merely relabeling ordinary lawsuit preparation as investigation does not bring it within the rule.

**Common negotiation ground:** The work at issue prepared and negotiated the agreement fixing Key Tronic's own liability and primarily protected its interests as a settling defendant. That function is not necessary response or enforcement against another liable party, even if it incidentally assisted EPA or affected cleanup arrangements. A distinct task independently qualifying as response is not excluded merely because it occurred during a negotiation, but no such additional category is found or awarded here. This is a record-based functional limit, not a prohibition on every expense associated with a settlement.

All components retain §107(a)(4)(B)'s **consistent with** the national contingency plan language. Government subsection (A)'s **not inconsistent with** formulation cannot replace it. The conceded §122(g)(5) bar for matters addressed in the Air Force settlement continues to exclude the $4.2 million consent-obligation contribution claim; it is not a universal release for the site. The separately settled response costs and unappealed prejudgment-interest award are untouched. No new federal-facility waiver, allocation, general contribution-immunity rule or cause-of-action restriction enters through the fees decision.
