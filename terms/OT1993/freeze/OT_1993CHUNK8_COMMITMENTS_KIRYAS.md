## Freshly validated issue boundaries

The final New York Court of Appeals judgment rests on federal establishment law; it expressly reserved the state constitutional question. The lower trial court's state-law ground is not an independently sustained state ground defeating Supreme Court review. The School Boards Association and officers did not retain standing in those capacities; the individual respondents proceeded as citizen taxpayers under State Finance Law §123. The district, whose statutory authority has been invalidated, seeks review of a concrete adverse judgment. ASARCO Inc. v. Kadish, 490 U.S. 605, 617–624 (1989), permits review at the request of a directly injured losing state-court party although the original state plaintiff could not have initiated the same case in a federal trial court. It does not create an unrestricted taxpayer exception. All eight non-Stone Justices would reach this appeal under the existing rule; none receives an invented separate threshold holding. Scalia's ASARCO dissent join is noted below rather than erased.

Chapter 748 makes the village's territory a separate union free school district with all corresponding powers and duties; a board of five to nine members is elected by qualified village voters for terms of no more than five years. The act takes effect on the first July 1 after becoming law. It contains no religious-membership requirement, no rabbinical appointment, no special-education-only limit, and no sunset. It creates public governmental authority, not a religious school. Current secular special education, outside professional staff, coeducational classrooms, absence of religious symbols, and service to numerous children from neighboring districts are genuine counterweights to an inference of establishment and must appear in the evaluation.

The village arose under general incorporation law. Religious residents retain the same political rights as others; religious homogeneity alone is not an unconstitutional fact. The challenged state action is the special legislative district, not the village's incorporation, private choice of neighbors, private religious schooling, or an assumed religious curriculum. The public board's general taxing, personnel, curricular and administrative powers cannot be reduced by inference to its present program. The act neither limits voters by religion nor immunizes board action from ordinary public-school law.

Wieder recognized discretion to supply a local public special-education program without creating a separate district. It did not command that program or hold that emotional distress proved a religious-doctrine violation. IDEA eligibility is not extinguished if the special district is invalid. Carter's actual November 9, 1993 reimbursement holding supplies no constitutional right to a religious governmental unit and no Establishment Clause exemption. Aguilar and Ball remain date-eligible and are not automatically reopened by the program's history. Zobrest resolved a remand on antecedent grounds and supplies no merits approval of religious-school aid in this timeline.

## Governing law and alternative-path comparison

Larkin, 459 U.S. 116, prohibits a standardless governmental licensing veto conferred on churches. Its actual direct recipients were religious institutions; elected public officials in this case require a genuine analogy, not a false statement that rabbis received statutory office. Larson, 456 U.S. 228, supplies demanding review of denominational preference. Amos, 483 U.S. 327, permits accommodation through the general religious-employer exemption in its nonprofit application; relief from interference with religious mission may be legitimate without being constitutionally required. O'Connor's separate rationale and Blackmun's concurrence require attention to the burden being lifted and distinguish accommodation from endorsement. McDaniel protects religious citizens from political disabilities, while not authorizing religious allocation of state offices.

The actual Lee decision (June 24, 1992) has independent official-school-worship and adolescent-pressure holdings. Neither is a universal funding or accommodation test. Souter's concurrence, joined by Stevens and O'Connor, opposes nonpreferential endorsement and requires a discernible burden for accommodation; Scalia's dissent, joined by Thomas and White, requires law-backed compulsion for coercion and separately distinguishes sectarian endorsement. Lamb's Chapel (June 7, 1993) protects private viewpoint access and applies Lemon to nonsponsored private use; Kennedy declined that formulation, while Scalia and Thomas preferred neutral accommodation without Lemon. Lukumi (June 11, 1993) prohibits objective religious targeting and substantially selective burdens; it does not automatically convert every accommodation into a discriminatory preference. Zobrest preserves antecedent legal inquiry and constitutional nondecision. Lemon therefore remains entering law, but independently applicable denominational-neutrality principles may dispose of this case without replacing it.

**Path A — impermissible religious allocation.** The State deliberately selected the boundaries of a known denominational community for a one-off grant of general school-government authority, rather than providing a service through a neutral mechanism open on comparable terms. The governmental organization, rather than religious instruction inside the school, supplies the constitutional concern. The strongest objection is that the boundary is an existing lawful municipality, officials are public and elected, the statute is facially geographic, and students/staff need not be Satmar. An adequate invalidation rationale must acknowledge those facts and identify the State's particular selection and the character of the power, rather than disqualifying religious voters.

**Path B — permissible secular accommodation.** A public district with secular instruction and normal accountability addresses a specific educational problem. Amos permits alleviation beyond constitutionally compelled exemptions, and McDaniel prevents a religious community's exclusion from ordinary public institutions. The strongest objection is that a permanent grant of general taxing and governing authority exceeds the immediate special-education need and lacks a neutral eligibility structure explaining which other communities may receive the same power. Sustaining the district cannot rest on a nonexistent free-exercise obligation, a factual finding of doctrinal impossibility, or an assumption that every future board act will remain the same.

**Path C — affirmance limited to equal treatment and deliberate governmental classification.** The narrow sufficient concern is the State's use of a religious community as the unit for exceptional allocation of public authority without a neutral governing explanation; no rule makes all special statutes or religiously homogeneous municipalities invalid. This path leaves general-law districting, ordinary political participation and lawful secular services available. Its strongest objection is that the absence of comparable requests does not establish an actual denial to another denomination. The answer cannot invent such a denial: it must explain how the enacted allocation itself makes denomination do governmental work, including why its structure supplies no adequate neutral basis or limit.

**Path D — resolve by broadly abandoning Lemon.** The petition's second question supplies an adversarial request, but a new comprehensive test is not necessary to decide the actual allocation. The alternative presents scope and briefing limits and risks determining school worship, aid, and accommodation categories not presented here. Even Justices who reject reliance on Lemon can decide the statute on their narrower neutrality/accommodation ground without pretending that a new Court rule has obtained the requisite support. The freeze therefore records doctrinal preferences without generating a comprehensive replacement holding.

## Individual commitments

### Blackmun

**Threshold and judgment:** Reach the federal question and affirm the invalidation of Chapter 748. Preserve statutory education obligations and lawful local public services; do not order termination of an identified child's services, invalidate the village, or prescribe a new district.

**Sufficient ground:** A State may not confer exceptional general governmental authority on a denominational community as such merely because the resulting government currently runs a secular school. The particular selection and breadth distinguish permissible aid to children's education from official advancement of a religious community's autonomous governmental organization. The rule does not exclude religious voters or require proof of sectarian teaching.

**Own eligible authority:** His Larkin join addresses transfer of governmental discretion; his Amos concurrence adopts essentially O'Connor's accommodation account with a reserved for-profit boundary. His Lee Court opinion makes official sponsorship independently significant, and his Lamb's Chapel join distinguishes private equal access from governmental advancement. His ASARCO Court join and Brennan concurrence join support reaching the present appeal without unnecessarily deciding the original taxpayers' hypothetical federal standing.

**Competing path and response:** The program's secular and inclusive facts and the parents' real difficulty make the accommodation argument substantial. They do not explain why a permanent general district, rather than lawful local services recognized in Wieder, is an accommodation necessary to lift a governmental religious burden. The absence of a religious office qualification does not immunize the legislature's own religious allocation. He would not join an assertion that ordinary residence in a religiously homogeneous village is itself establishment.

**Join barriers, narrow position, Lemon and remedy:** Can join Path C, with the actual statutory powers and legitimate educational need retained. Will not join categorical hostility to accommodation or an assertion that all unique legislation is invalid. Would retain applicable Lemon principles, but need not reach comprehensive reconsideration here; rejects adopting the petition's replacement. Affirmance leaves Chapter 748 invalid while educational authorities remain free to provide lawful services. No reopening Aguilar/Ball or constitutional decision in Zobrest.

### Stevens

**Threshold and judgment:** Reach and affirm. The district's general grant cannot be sustained merely because current classes comply with secular public-school requirements. No broader remedy against the community or its schools.

**Sufficient ground:** The particular state selection of a religiously defined community for autonomous school government advances that community's separation through state authority. Neutral public provision of appropriate education remains permissible. A legislature may respond to distinctive needs, but it may not make religious identity the criterion for granting governmental power when the same educational objective can be pursued without that assignment.

**Own eligible authority:** His Larkin join resists a governmental role for religious identity; his Amos Court join permits general accommodation and therefore prevents a categorical rule against every religious benefit. His Lee joins include both independently controlling grounds and Souter's broader neutrality concurrence. His Lamb's Chapel join distinguishes private voluntary speech from official sponsorship. His ASARCO Court join allows review of the injured appellant's concrete state judgment, and his unanimous Carter join protects appropriate IDEA remedies without constitutionalizing parental preference.

**Competing path and response:** The best defense relies on a lawful municipality, elected lay officials, secular professional instruction and children from other districts. Those facts preclude treating the school as a church, but do not remove the State's deliberate governmental organization around the particular sect. His Lee continuity gives official identification independent significance, while Amos's general exemption lacks this denominational allocation. He does not infer a factual denial to another sect or require children to prove psychological injury from religious teaching.

**Join barriers, narrower position, Lemon and remedy:** Can join a neutrality/denominational-allocation ground without a comprehensive Lemon decision. May emphasize the government's endorsement of separation, but will not require a general ban on culturally sensitive special education. Would reject a replacement that permits governmental preference merely because its immediate activity is secular. Relief is affirmance of the judgment against Chapter 748; lawful accommodation of children remains open, and no new mandatory placement is ordered.

### O'Connor

**Threshold and judgment:** Reach and affirm on a bounded equal-treatment ground. Religious citizens may form and govern public communities, and special legislation is not invalid merely for being special.

**Sufficient ground:** Chapter 748 selects a particular denominational community for general public power without a neutral structure that explains and constrains that selection on equal terms. This is distinct from generally available public benefits or a general accommodation relieving interference with religious exercise. The equality defect concerns how the State chose the recipient, not a demand that every governmental line cross religious communities.

**Own eligible authority:** Her Larkin join distinguishes participation in government from discretionary power granted on a religious basis. Her Amos concurrence requires examination of the burden relieved and the objective distinction between accommodation and endorsement. Her Lee joins include Souter's discernible-burden/neutrality account; her Lamb's Chapel join allows equal private access under Lemon without government sponsorship. Her Carter Court opinion protects statutory access while retaining the specific limits of that remedial statute.

**Competing path and response:** Equal political citizenship and secular instruction are powerful reasons not to invalidate the village or deny services. The real educational difficulty justifies accommodation in principle. Yet the act grants ongoing general powers, not simply relief from an identified obstacle to religious exercise, and does not state neutral criteria that would make this exceptional delegation an equal benefit. The claim fails on that narrower governmental selection rather than assumed future religious abuse.

**Join barriers, narrower position, Lemon and remedy:** Will not join automatic equation of the elected board with a church or a rule requiring every accommodation to arise under a generally applicable statute. Nor will she treat absence of a previously rejected comparator as dispositive proof of equality. Can join Path C if those limits are explicit. She favors context-sensitive application of establishment principles and would not adopt a universal replacement here; leaving Lemon unreconsidered does not convert its formula into a mandatory analytical sequence for every category. Affirmance preserves lawful secular educational arrangements without requiring a particular one.

### Scalia

**Threshold and judgment:** Reach under the existing ASARCO rule and reverse the federal establishment judgment. His join in the ASARCO dissent remains a prior objection to that jurisdictional rationale, but it does not itself overrule governing law or support silently treating the present concrete adverse judgment as unreviewable. No independent request or developed basis to reopen ASARCO is supplied.

**Sufficient ground:** A generally lawful village's religious homogeneity does not disqualify it from a public school district created to address secular education. Chapter 748 gives power to elected public officials under ordinary educational duties, not to clergy as such, and imposes neither religion-based eligibility nor devotional instruction. Accommodating the actual educational difficulty may be lawful although free exercise does not require this precise remedy.

**Own eligible authority:** His Amos Court join permits noncompulsory accommodation and distinguishes it from denominational discrimination. His Lee dissent, joined there by Thomas, emphasizes the limits of coercion and retains a separate sectarian-establishment concern. His Lamb's Chapel concurrence with Thomas protects neutral accommodation without relying on Lemon. His Lukumi joins enforce objective neutrality and resist religious disabilities, making the State's actual legal classification rather than the residents' beliefs central.

**Competing path and response:** Path A accurately identifies broad permanent public authority and a Legislature acting with knowledge of the religious community. The missing step is proof that a geographic public district is a religious institution or a discriminatory grant merely because residents share faith. Larkin transferred veto power to religious institutions; this board is publicly elected, subject to public duties and open without a faith test. Lack of a comparable application cannot establish that another community would be treated differently. Secular need and public administration supply a valid ground even though district creation was not compelled.

**Join barriers, narrower position, Lemon and remedy:** Will not join a rule allowing clergy to wield public vetoes, religious voter qualifications, or favoritism among otherwise similarly situated groups. Accepts a narrow result limited to this secular public program and the absence of statutory religious exclusion. Rejects reliance on Lemon as the ground of decision, but the present source/scope record does not authorize attributing a vote for a fully formulated replacement test or for overruling every Lemon-derived holding. Would reverse the federal judgment and remand; the reserved state constitutional question remains for the state courts, so he does not declare the act immune from all state-law challenge.

### Kennedy

**Threshold and judgment:** Reach and affirm. The federal constitutional objection is the State's deliberate religious classification in allocating governmental authority, not merely the community's private association or its public school's curriculum.

**Sufficient ground:** Government may not deliberately use denominational membership as the organizing criterion for conferring a special governmental unit. Formal reference to village boundaries does not cure a record-supported choice of those boundaries precisely to allocate authority to the religious community. Ordinary geographic government, including religiously homogeneous government produced by private choices under neutral law, remains permissible. The rule must establish the State's act rather than treating demographic concentration as sufficient.

**Own eligible authority:** His Lee concurrence adopted a concrete pressure ground rather than the broader sponsorship-only theory. His Lamb's Chapel concurrence rejects unnecessary Lemon/endorsement reliance. His Lukumi opinion and joins enforce objective scrutiny of text, operation and religious classification. His ASARCO Court opinion supports reaching review of the directly injured state-court loser. These writings support an independent neutrality limit without claiming that all perceived endorsement is unconstitutional.

**Competing path and response:** The district is public, lay-elected and secular, and the village's original incorporation is not attacked. McDaniel's equal-citizenship principle therefore bars invalidation based simply on who lives there. Nonetheless the special legislative allocation uses the known religious community as the recipient of general governmental authority, exceeding neutral protection of equal access to secular education. The operative inference must remain grounded in the particular enactment and circumstances; a bare expectation that religious citizens will vote together would be insufficient.

**Join barriers, narrower position, Lemon and remedy:** Will not join a broad Larkin equation treating elected religious citizens as a church delegation merely because they are religious, or a holding that absence of a universally available district-creation procedure alone invalidates every special act. Can join a direct deliberate-religious-classification holding with ordinary geographic government expressly preserved. Does not join Lemon or endorsement as necessary ground and declines a comprehensive replacement decision here. Affirmance affects Chapter 748, not ordinary incorporation or secular services. No coercion finding or new district boundaries are invented.

### Souter

**Threshold and judgment:** Reach and affirm. The exceptional allocation of public power to this denominational community fails neutrality notwithstanding the secular program.

**Sufficient ground:** A State must not identify a religious community for an exclusive, discretionary grant of general governmental authority without a neutral allocation principle. The result does not turn on clergy holding office or on the school teaching religion. Larkin's concern with the boundary between civil power and religious identity and Larson's denominational neutrality explain why the choice of recipient is independently material. Equal political participation under general law remains protected.

**Own eligible authority:** His Lee concurrence, joined by Stevens and O'Connor, treats coercion as not universally necessary and accommodation as relief of a discernible burden. He joined both controlling Lee grounds and the establishment portion of Lamb's Chapel; the latter distinguishes equal private permission from state advancement. His Lukumi strict-scrutiny joins preserve the need to connect religious distinctions to their precise lawful justification without converting history-of-enactment reasoning into universal law. He did not participate in Larkin or Amos; no direct historical join is attributed.

**Competing path and response:** Public elections, secular classrooms, outside personnel and children from other districts establish that the school is not conducting devotional instruction. Amos nevertheless concerned a general exemption addressing governmental interference in religious mission. Here the educational difficulty does not explain a permanent grant of all union-free-district powers on a particular denominational boundary. Wieder supplies a lawful local-services alternative without commanding it. The State may accommodate but must do so on terms that do not assign special civil authority by denomination.

**Join barriers, narrower position, Lemon and remedy:** The narrow neutrality ground suffices without announcing a comprehensive Lemon replacement. Will not join a rule against every religious accommodation or all special legislation, and will not assert that another denomination has actually been refused a district. Religious residents remain eligible for public office and neutral districting. Affirmance leaves education duties and lawful local arrangements in place; the Court supplies neither a new aid entitlement nor a holding overruling Aguilar or Ball.

### Thomas

**Threshold and judgment:** Reach under the governing threshold law and reverse the federal establishment judgment, remanding for remaining state-law proceedings. He receives no invented ASARCO participation or Ginsburg-era lower-court position.

**Sufficient ground:** The act creates a public district defined by a preexisting lawful municipality, with elections and ordinary public educational duties. The record of secular service and absence of religious qualifications do not establish that the State has made a church its governing authority or established religious exercise. Religious association cannot itself disqualify citizens from public institutions. Accommodation of secular access needs need not be constitutionally compelled to be permissible.

**Own eligible authority:** His Lee dissent join distinguishes lawful ceremonial accommodation from law-backed compulsion and preserves objections to genuine sectarian establishment. His Lamb's Chapel concurrence join with Scalia relies on viewpoint equality and neutral accommodation while refusing Lemon. His Lukumi joins protect against objective religious burdens. These positions support assessing statutory authority and actual governmental religious requirements, not treating the residents' identity as dispositive. His Carter join preserves the children's education remedies independently.

**Competing path and response:** The Legislature plainly knew the community's religious identity and granted broad power. That circumstance would matter if it granted religious officials authority as such or created a discriminatory religious qualification. The act instead authorizes a regulated public institution. The narrow invalidation path risks converting a safeguard against religious preference into a disability for a community whose members privately share a religion; the source packet supplies no actual exclusion of another faith or sectarian teaching.

**Join barriers, narrower position, Lemon and remedy:** Will not infer a complete originalist/federalism replacement of establishment law from the absence of supplied prior authority; no later Thomas doctrine enters. Will not approve religious voter qualifications, governmental worship or clergy vetoes. Rejects Lemon reliance in this case but supplies no comprehensive overruling vote without authenticated scope and a developed replacement question. The narrower lawful sustaining position remains this secular, public, nondisqualifying arrangement. Reverse on federal law and leave the reserved state constitutional issue to the state judiciary.

### Ginsburg

**Threshold and judgment:** Reach and affirm on religious neutrality in public authority allocation. No verified predecision Ginsburg establishment opinion is supplied; the row relies on governing law, the particular record, and limited actual public continuity rather than invented personal precedent.

**Sufficient ground:** The special act grants general governmental powers to the deliberately selected denominational community without an adequate neutral structure for the allocation. The relevant discrimination lies in the State's exceptional selection, not religious residents' entitlement to vote, educate their children, or live together. A secular program and equal access by individual students do not alone answer that structural question.

**Own eligible continuity and governing authority:** Her Carter join protects access to an appropriate statutory education and preserves the distinction between a remedy and general constitutional funding rights. Her Ladue join similarly respects concrete constitutional limits despite a legitimate public purpose, though it is not establishment precedent. Larkin, Larson, Amos, the actual Lee/Lamb's Chapel holdings and their carefully bounded accommodation distinctions provide the substantive establishment rules. No pre-accession Supreme Court join or unverified D.C. Circuit holding is attributed to her.

**Competing path and response:** The public program's staff, pupils, curriculum and electoral accountability make the accommodation defense substantial and exclude an inference that all aid benefiting religious children is establishment. Yet the act confers general autonomous power beyond supplying an accessible classroom, while the law already permits local public services. The neutral provision of services to children is a materially narrower response than exceptional governmental organization by denomination. The decision should turn on that enacted choice rather than a prediction of future discrimination.

**Join barriers, narrower position, Lemon and remedy:** Can join Path C with explicit protection for ordinary incorporation, religious voters and appropriate services. Cannot join a categorical invalidity rule for all homogeneous districts or all special legislation. Would leave Lemon intact without deciding its universal formulation and would not adopt the petition's broad replacement. Her limited personal source record requires careful reconciliation but does not justify changing the legal inquiry into an unsupported assertion of former views. Affirmance preserves children's statutory rights and leaves lawful future arrangements to the appropriate authorities.
