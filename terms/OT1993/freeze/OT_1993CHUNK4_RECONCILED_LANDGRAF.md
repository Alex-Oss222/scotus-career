## Individual commitments

### Blackmun

**Result and scope:** reverse the refusal to make §102 compensatory damages available in this pending action; remand for the resulting claim and conditional jury election. Reserve punitive damages without expressing an opinion. Do not represent the reservation as affirmance, as agreement with a punitive holding, or as an abstention from the case's overall judgment.

**Sufficient ground:** the enacted text's immediate-effectiveness clause, read with the separate express temporal limitations in §§109(c) and 402(b), most naturally supports applying compensation in pending cases. Section 109(c) governs its extraterritorial amendments; §402(b) requires both a pre-March 1, 1975 disparate-impact complaint and an initial decision after October 30, 1983. The clauses are separate and their combined negative inference supplies Blackmun's statutory argument, not a general holding that every new law applies to pending litigation. His independent alternative is that a remedial provision compensating injury from already forbidden discrimination does not disturb a vested entitlement to lawful conduct. His own Bradley opinion and his join in White's Kaiser dissent substantiate the neutral path; his historical writing confirms these grounds at this precise level.

**Strongest alternative and personal constraint:** his Bennett join protects substantive obligations fixed under old law; substantial added monetary liability against a private employer is the strongest objection. He distinguishes the underlying prohibition from its remedies in this open action and denies that an employer has a vested right to engage in discrimination. He does not treat the legislative history as reliable evidence of a contrary limitation. Punitive liability is a materially harder question and remains reserved, rather than resolved through this remedial rationale.

**Join barriers/minimum path:** cannot join the denial of compensation or the seven associates' conclusion that the added compensation itself triggers the presumption. Cannot join a punitive-retroactivity holding either way. May join a limited remand allowing compensation and its statutory jury election while leaving the court below to decide remaining predicates and proper trial scope. No automatic erasure of prior findings, mandatory retrial of all issues, award, or malice finding follows.

### Stevens

**Result:** affirm the judgment withholding §102 relief and the associated jury election for the pre-enactment conduct.

**Ground:** first determine whether Congress expressly prescribed temporal reach; absent such prescription determine whether the proposed application attaches new legal consequences to completed events, including impairment of existing rights, increased liability for past conduct, or new duties concerning completed transactions. If it does, apply the presumption against that reach absent clear congressional intent. An ordinary effective date and the two limited temporal exceptions do not provide that intent here. Compensation creates a new monetary liability even though the discrimination was already unlawful; punishment presents an even stronger retrospective consequence. The jury provision follows its statutory damages condition.

**Personal test and limits:** his Kaiser join supports the statutory-first inquiry. His Bennett dissent treated the change there as clarification; §102 creates remedies not already supplied by old Title VII. His Franklin join supplies no contrary premise. Bradley's litigation-fee setting can be preserved without treating every new remedy as prospective. Prospective relief, changes in jurisdiction, and genuinely procedural changes require their own analysis; even a procedural amendment does not automatically undo a completed complaint or trial. These qualifications are part of the permissible framework, not an invitation to replace statutory interpretation with an unconstrained fairness balance.

**Narrower path:** statutory text plus new liability and the conditional jury conclusion suffice. He does not require overruling Bradley or deciding the validity of an expressly retroactive punitive enactment.
