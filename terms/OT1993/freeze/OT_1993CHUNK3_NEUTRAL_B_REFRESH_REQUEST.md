## Granderson — cutoff immediately before March 22, 1994

The offense statute, 18 U.S.C. §1703(a), had a five-year maximum; the Guidelines range and statutory maximum are distinct. Independently examine whether the proviso's reference to the original sentence concerns the probation component alone or the entire initial sentence, including an originally imposed fine. Distinguish that question from whether a later fine or renewed probation is permitted and from whether any permitted prison term may exceed the initially applicable Guidelines range. Assess whether a statutory minimum measured in a different kind of punishment requires a comparison of severity, whether such comparison can be made on this record, and whether the already-served eleven months resolves this Government appeal without ordering renewed probation or a fine. Do not infer an additional requested disposition or a preserved claim from the existence of a possible legal construction.

Retain the complete alternative under which the verb sentence may permit probation rather than mandate imprisonment: §3565(a)(2)'s placement opposite subsection (a)(1), the drug proviso's notwithstanding clause, probation's statutory status as a sentence, and the expressly different prison language in §3583(g) require independent treatment. The two drug provisions were enacted together as §7303(a)(2) and (b)(2) of the Anti-Drug Abuse Act of 1988, 102 Stat. 4464. More specifically, it reports preparation within one hour of arrest and that the November 7 magistrate determination relied on the facts contained in that declaration. It reports no suggestion that the intervening delay enabled police to obtain additional evidence presented to the magistrate. These are reported record predicates, not an independent inspection of the declaration or a finding that federal causation is resolved. The neutral chronology appears at 511 U.S. 81; the additional declaration particulars appear at 511 U.S. 88–90.
