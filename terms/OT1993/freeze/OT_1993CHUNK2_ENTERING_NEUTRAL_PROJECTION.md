## Current law

### 1993-10-12 — [Day v. Day, Nos. 92-8788, 92-8792, 92-8888, 92-8905, 92-8906, 92-9018, 92-9101, and 93-5430.](../records/Day_v_Day_prospective_filing_control_1993-10-12.md#public-projection)

The Court declines to impose a new requirement that Day pay the Rule 38 docketing fee and comply with Rule 33's printed-form requirements for unidentified future noncriminal certiorari petitions because the established record does not show that treatment of particular filings under existing rules is inadequate. This record-specific refusal neither requires a prior warning in every case nor makes prospective restrictions categorically unlawful, and it does not decide any current fee motion or displace existing authority over demonstrated abuse.

Per curiam opinion of the Court, supported throughout by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine participating Justices adopt the same rule, application, and limited disposition. This reasoned filing-control decision supplies procedural authority; it is not an underlying certiorari disposition or a Rule-of-Four poll.

The governing rule remains individual treatment under existing filing rules unless a supported record warrants a broader response. This decision applies Martin and Demos to Day's prospective noncriminal-certiorari question; it establishes no new filing-count threshold, mandatory warning, constitutional right to unlimited fee-free discretionary review, or categorical prohibition of future controls. McDonald and Sindram retain their extraordinary-writ force. No criminal-filing or habeas rule changes. Financial eligibility and the frivolousness or malice of each current petition remain separate questions and are not decided here.

### 1993-11-09 — [Florence County School District Four v. Carter, No. 91-1523.](../records/Florence_County_School_District_Four_v_Carter_merits_1993-11-09.md#public-projection)

When the public offer violates IDEA and the parents' unilateral private placement provides an education otherwise appropriate for the child under the Act, §1415(e)(2) permits equitable reimbursement even if that placement lacks state approval or does not satisfy every requirement in §1401(a)(18)'s definition of a publicly provided free appropriate education. Parents act at financial risk and must establish both public inadequacy and private appropriateness; neither their choice nor the child's progress alone guarantees reimbursement, the court must consider relevant equitable circumstances and a reasonable amount, equitable considerations may support reduction or denial of relief, and full reimbursement is inappropriate if the private cost was unreasonable.

O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for this rule and affirmance. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

Effective November 9, 1993, parental reimbursement depends on a public offer that violates IDEA, a private placement otherwise appropriate under the Act, and equitable relief in an appropriate amount. Every public-program requirement is not an absolute condition of the private corrective remedy. The private education must still be appropriate. A State's lack of an approved-school list is not essential to the rule. Relevant parental conduct, expense, and educational deficiencies remain available equitable considerations under governing law; no new mandatory checklist is created. Public-placement standards are not abolished, and the decision neither certifies Trident nor establishes a constitutional funding right.

### 1993-11-09 — [Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., No. 92-1168.](../records/Harris_v_Forklift_Systems_Inc_merits_1993-11-09.md#public-projection)

Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considered in all the circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and that the employee actually perceives as abusive; serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis, and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than a merely offensive utterance, and unreasonable interference with work, but no listed consideration is indispensable; psychological effects may inform subjective abuse, while offensive conduct that does not meet the objective and subjective requirements remains insufficient.

O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

Effective November 9, 1993, the governing Title VII inquiry is severe-or-pervasive discriminatory alteration of working conditions, objectively hostile or abusive and actually perceived as such, assessed from all circumstances without an indispensable injury or work-loss factor. Psychological consequences remain possible evidence. Mere offense remains insufficient. The decision does not resolve the proposed EEOC regulations, constructive discharge, ultimate liability, damages, or a sex-specific reasonable-person formulation.

### 1993-11-30 — [Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp., No. 92-1123.](../records/Izumi_Seimitsu_Kogyo_Kabushiki_Kaisha_v_US_Philips_Corp_DIG_1993-11-30.md#public-projection)

Under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1), an unsuccessful applicant for intervention may seek review of the denial, but cannot obtain review of the parties' underlying judgment on the strength of an unreviewed intervention denial or a financial interest alone. Because Izumi's denial of intervention remains undisturbed, Izumi lacks the party status necessary to press the settlement-vacatur question in this petition; the Court does not decide whether intervention should have been allowed.

Per curiam opinion of the Court supported by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg, seven Justices; Blackmun and Stevens dissent. This stated threshold ground supports the dismissal and carries procedural authority, without a settlement-vacatur holding.

Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding a question not stated in the certiorari petition or fairly included in a stated question, subject to exceptional circumstances; a separate intervention denial is not fairly included merely because it must be overturned before the Court can reach a settlement-vacatur question. Neither discussion in the petition's body and later merits briefing nor the antecedent relationship establishes exceptional urgency or economy on this record, so the Court declines review of intervention and dismisses the writ as improvidently granted, leaving vacatur undecided.

Per curiam opinion of the Court supported by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg, seven Justices; Blackmun and Stevens dissent. The reasoned dismissal establishes this question-presentation ruling, not the merits of the judgment below.

Effective November 30, 1993, the reasoned threshold opinion establishes the two procedural propositions stated above. The statutory party requirement and prudential question-presentation rule remain distinct; the latter does not eliminate the Court's obligation to notice jurisdictional defects. The dismissal establishes no rule approving or forbidding routine settlement vacatur, no intervention entitlement on these facts, and no collateral-estoppel result in other litigation. Section 2106's equitable authority and existing vacatur precedents are left without a new merits construction.

### 1993-12-13 — [John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank, No. 92-1074](../records/John_Hancock_Mutual_Life_Insurance_Co_v_Harris_Trust_Savings_Bank_merits_1993-12-13.md#public-projection)

Under ERISA §401(b)(2), a guaranteed-benefit policy itself is a plan asset, while the insurer's underlying assets are not deemed plan assets solely because it issued the policy to the extent the policy genuinely guarantees the amount of benefits. The disputed nonguaranteed free-funds component is not excluded: its plan-asset character subjects Hancock to fiduciary obligations to the extent it exercises authority or control over management or disposition of those assets under §3(21)(A), without deciding breach, causation, loss, or remedy.

Ginsburg's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, and Souter. Six Justices adopt the component-specific construction and its application to the disputed free funds.

McCarran-Ferguson does not bar application of ERISA's express insurance-related fiduciary and guaranteed-benefit provisions to this general-account contract. ERISA's insurance-saving clause preserves compatible state insurance regulation, but does not make a conflicting state obligation controlling over ERISA itself; a concrete conflict must be assessed as such rather than treated as a categorical exemption for all insurer assets.

Ginsburg's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, and Souter. This six-Justice ground rejects the asserted state-insurance obstacle to affirmance.

The Labor Department's reading excluding general-account assets categorically cannot control because it exceeds the permissible statutory range by eliminating §401(b)(2)'s component limitation. The Court does not hold that inconsistency or presentation in litigation invariably defeats deference, and leaves the general degree of deference due agency litigation positions unresolved.

Ginsburg's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, and Souter. These six adopt the statutory-limit ground; the dissenters separately reject the categorical agency position without adopting the Court's entire investment-risk analysis.

Effective December 13, 1993, the guaranteed-benefit exclusion operates only to the extent of genuine guarantees; this contract's nonguaranteed free funds remain plan assets despite general-account placement. The policy itself and the insurer's underlying assets remain distinct. Fiduciary responsibility follows the statutory asset-and-function inquiry, while breach and remedies remain separate. Compatible state insurance regulation survives, actual conflict yields to ERISA, and the categorical agency exclusion exceeds the statutory boundary. Mertens's remedial categories and Fabe's different insurance-priority holding remain unchanged. No universal guarantee formula, rule making illiquidity alone dispositive, general denial of deference to changed agency views, or remedy entitlement is adopted.

### 1993-12-13 — [Tennessee v. Middlebrooks; No. 92-989.](../records/Tennessee_v_Middlebrooks_jurisdictional_dismissal_1993-12-13.md#public-projection)

The Court lacks jurisdiction to review a state judgment when an adequate state constitutional ground independent of federal law requires the same judgment whatever the answer to the federal question. The Tennessee Supreme Court's original state constitutional analysis in Middlebrooks, expressly confirmed by its subsequent majority opinion in Howell, independently requires this resentencing judgment, so the writ is dismissed for want of jurisdiction.

Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine Justices support the rule and its application. No combination of separate opinions is necessary.

Federal direct review cannot proceed here because a decision about the Eighth Amendment would leave the state resentencing judgment unchanged. The conclusion rests on the original independent state analysis and the Tennessee Supreme Court's express clarification together; a lawyer's assertion or the mere presence of a later footnote does not automatically defeat review.

The Tennessee rule concerns defendants convicted of first-degree murder solely on the basis of felony murder and remains a state constitutional rule. It does not become a federal prohibition of every aggravator that overlaps an offense element, nor does this decision invalidate that aggravator for every premeditated-murder conviction. Federal capital eligibility, narrowing, harmlessness, guilt, habeas review, and Cox finality remain undecided in this case.

### 1993-12-13 — [United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, No. 92-1180](../records/United_States_v_James_Daniel_Good_Real_Property_merits_1993-12-13.md#public-projection)

Before seizing real property for civil forfeiture, the Government must ordinarily give the owner notice and a meaningful opportunity to be heard; a Fourth Amendment warrant does not itself satisfy this independent Fifth Amendment requirement. To bypass prior process for exigent circumstances, the Government must show that less restrictive measures, such as notice of the pending claim recorded against the property, a restraining order, or a bond, would not prevent sale, destruction, or continued unlawful use; emergency seizure remains possible on that showing with appropriate subsequent process.

Kennedy's opinion for the Court, Pre-seizure process portion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg. These six adopt the same rule, its exigency qualification, and its application.

Noncompliance with the distinct reporting and commencement duties in 19 U.S.C. §§1602–1604 does not require dismissal of a forfeiture action filed within §1621's five-year limitations period. Section 1602 requires immediate reporting by customs agents to customs officers; §1603(b) requires prompt reporting to the United States attorney when the specified seizure or discovery requires proceedings by that office; and §1604, for recovery requiring district-court or Court of International Trade proceedings, requires those proceedings to be commenced forthwith when the prescribed probable basis for a fine, penalty, or forfeiture appears, subject to the Attorney General's preserved determination, after inquiry, that such proceedings probably cannot be sustained or that public justice does not require institution or prosecution, in which event the facts are instead reported to the Treasury Secretary for direction. These remain mandatory duties within their stated conditions, and the ruling does not extinguish an independently supported constitutional-delay claim or other source-authorized relief.

Kennedy's opinion for the Court, Statutory timing portion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this construction and reversal of the contrary ground below.

Effective December 13, 1993, civil seizure of real property ordinarily requires prior notice and a meaningful hearing, with the Government bearing the stated exigency showing. The rule includes rented property and protects control and income, not only physical occupancy. Fourth Amendment compliance does not answer the separate process question. The customs promptness duties remain mandatory but supply no dismissal sanction for an action within §1621's five-year period. The innocent-owner and relation-back rules in United States v. 92 Buena Vista Avenue and the Excessive Fines coverage in Austin v. United States remain separate and unchanged. No criminal-forfeiture rule, final ownership determination, sovereign-immunity waiver, or automatic compensation remedy is decided.

### 1994-01-10 — [Burden v. Zant; No. 92-8836.](../records/Burden_v_Zant_summary_merits_1994-01-10.md#public-projection)

A federal habeas judgment resting on deference to a district-court finding that the district court never made must be reversed when that mistake leaves the actual conflict claim unadjudicated. Absence of formal transactional immunity does not establish absence of an informal nonprosecution agreement; on remand the courts must determine whether counsel had an actual conflict that adversely affected his performance under Cuyler v. Sullivan, without presuming those predicates from joint representation alone.

The per curiam Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg; all nine participants support this narrow record-correction rule and remand. No fractured-opinion synthesis is needed.

The decision applies record integrity and the existing conflict standard without changing the constitutional merits test. An actual conflict and an adverse effect must be established; joint representation alone is insufficient. A proved Sullivan violation needs no separate Strickland outcome-prejudice showing. The statutory exception question, actual agreement, constitutional violation, and ultimate habeas relief remain for proper proceedings.

### 1994-01-11 — [Ratzlaf v. United States; No. 92-1196.](../records/Ratzlaf_v_United_States_merits_1994-01-11.md#public-projection)

For the charged offense under 31 U.S.C. §§5322(a) and 5324(3), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the structuring was unlawful, in addition to knowing the financial institution's reporting duty and acting for the purpose of evading it. Knowledge may be established through reasonable circumstantial inferences; the prosecution need not prove knowledge of a section number or that the defendant read the Code.

Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; six of nine participants adopt the rule and instructional-error disposition.

If ordinary interpretation leaves genuine ambiguity about whether §5322(a) requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful, the rule of lenity independently requires that narrower construction of this charged criminal offense. Legislative purpose and conflicting legislative history do not justify dispensing with the additional knowledge requirement when the enacted criminal language does not clearly do so.

Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; the same six Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient alternative holding.

The charged §5322(a)/§5324(3) offense now requires proof of knowledge that structuring is unlawful, which may be circumstantial. The bank's reporting obligation and the customer's antistructuring obligation remain distinct. No general constitutional ignorance-of-law defense, legal-proceeds requirement, rule for civil forfeiture, or construction of every other willfulness statute is announced. The exception in §5322(a) for §5315 and its regulations remains; it is not an exception within §5324(3).

### 1994-01-19 — [Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich; No. 92-896.](../records/Thunder_Basin_Coal_Co_v_Reich_merits_1994-01-19.md#public-projection)

The Mine Act precludes original district-court review of this anticipatory challenge to miners' representation and posting requirements because Congress's intent to channel the dispute through the independent Commission and then a court of appeals is fairly discernible from the Act's language, structure, and purpose, and these claims can receive meaningful review there. The claim's relation to the statutory enforcement process, relevant agency expertise, and actual availability of judicial review inform that inquiry; they are not three newly mandatory elements governing every administrative statute.

Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, statutory-channel portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg; all nine participants adopt this proposition at the stated level of generality.

The operator has not shown that compliance with these representation and posting requirements, or their contest through the Mine Act's review process, imposes a constitutionally intolerable obstacle to meaningful judicial review. This conclusion depends on the actual absence of demonstrated irreversible injury, review before contested penalties become payable, expedited proceedings, and the statute's bounded opportunities for temporary relief; it creates neither immunity from lawful penalties nor a rule that delayed payment alone always makes review adequate.

Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, meaningful-review and due-process portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; seven participants adopt this ground. Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment on a distinct constitutional formulation and are not counted as joining this explanation.

From this decision, this anticipatory Mine Act representation/posting dispute must use the Commission and court-of-appeals route. The holding clarifies implied statutory allocation on these facts, without excluding every constitutional or collateral administrative challenge. The Commission's possible inability to decide a constitutional question finally does not eliminate the appellate court's ability to decide it. Actual claim connection and meaningful review remain necessary to the stated result, with the precise qualifications set out above.

### 1993-11-09 — [Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., No. 92-1168.](../records/Harris_v_Forklift_Systems_Inc_merits_1993-11-09.md#public-projection)

Scalia concurs and joins the Court's opinion in full. He emphasizes that the contextual standard cannot mechanically determine close cases. Making work impairment an indispensable element would provide a sharper boundary, but the statutory protection of employment conditions supplies no basis for that restriction. The inquiry is discriminatory alteration of working conditions, not proof of measurable lost output or a separate psychological injury. His reservation about predictability does not withdraw any part of his join.

Ginsburg concurs and joins the Court's opinion in full. Her equality explanation asks whether sex-based conduct imposes disadvantageous working conditions. Difficulty doing the job may demonstrate that alteration without a decline in tangible productivity. That emphasis remains subject to the Court's requirements of objective hostility and the employee's actual perception of abuse; it neither substitutes subjective sensitivity for the objective inquiry nor turns measurable work loss into a required element. These concurrences explain the rule's limits and are noncontrolling additional explanations.

### 1993-11-30 — [Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp., No. 92-1123.](../records/Izumi_Seimitsu_Kogyo_Kabushiki_Kaisha_v_US_Philips_Corp_DIG_1993-11-30.md#public-projection)

Stevens dissents, joined by Blackmun. They would exercise the prudential exception to reach intervention, reverse the denial and the routine settlement-vacatur disposition, and remand for an actual equitable assessment. The intervention argument was disclosed in the petition and addressed adversarially; in their view, dismissal after plenary review gives the omitted question heading more weight than the notice and economy purposes of Rule 14.1(a) warrant. *Yee* preserves exceptional consideration, while *Scofield* supplies the route to review the denial. Izumi's indemnity exposure and the settling parties' newly adverse request support the limited right to intervene to oppose erasure. The fact that an applicant was not already a party does not itself answer whether it should be allowed to become one.

On vacatur, the dissent reads §2106 as requiring an actual equitable judgment that accounts for public and third-party interests. *Munsingwear* addresses loss of appellate review through mootness, while *Karcher* distinguishes voluntary abandonment; voluntary settlement alone does not automatically justify erasing a judgment. *Cardinal Chemical* supplies a limited analogy against an automatic practice disguised as discretion, rather than a decision about settlement itself. Judgments represent public work and can affect persons outside the bargain. The dissent would require those interests to be assessed before vacatur, while preserving lawful case-specific relief. It does not declare settlement vacatur categorically unavailable, give every nonparty an intervention right, or command permanent preservation of these judgments. Its proposed intervention and vacatur rules receive two votes and do not govern the Court's disposition.

### 1993-12-13 — [John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank, No. 92-1074](../records/John_Hancock_Mutual_Life_Insurance_Co_v_Harris_Trust_Savings_Bank_merits_1993-12-13.md#public-projection)

Thomas dissents, joined by O'Connor and Kennedy. They would remand for consideration of the 1977 amendment under their reading of a policy that provides for guaranteed benefits. That phrase can cover arrangements making provision for future guaranteed payments before every benefit amount has vested; it need not require the insurer to guarantee an aggregate investment return at every stage. But the statutory limitation still excludes only qualifying components. They therefore reject a blanket exemption for all general-account assets. The amendment ended automatic new guarantees and allowed nonguaranteed payments, so its actual operation requires assessment under their construction. Their position is a contract-specific remand, not a finding that every free fund is exempt. They also reject the agency's categorical exclusion and claimed entitlement to Chevron deference. That common negative conclusion does not make them join the Court's different guarantee-and-risk analysis. No member of either side decides fiduciary breach or the amount or form of relief.

### 1993-12-13 — [United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, No. 92-1180](../records/United_States_v_James_Daniel_Good_Real_Property_merits_1993-12-13.md#public-projection)

O'Connor concurs in the statutory-timing ruling and dissents from the hearing ruling. Scalia concurs in the statutory-timing ruling and dissents from the hearing ruling. His ground is the sufficiency of historically established sovereign forfeiture procedures and the warrant in this setting, informed by Gerstein's distinction between preliminary seizure procedures and a later adversarial determination. Thomas concurs in the statutory-timing ruling and dissents from the hearing ruling on the narrower combination of the conviction, warrant, and absence of physical dispossession in this record. ### 1994-01-11 — [Ratzlaf v. United States; No. 92-1196.](../records/Ratzlaf_v_United_States_merits_1994-01-11.md#public-projection)

Justice Blackmun, joined by Justices O'Connor and Thomas, dissents. They would affirm because knowing the bank's reporting duty and deliberately structuring to evade it supplies willfulness in this statutory context. The general penalty provision need not add a different element to every underlying offense: some substantive provisions already define conduct sufficiently culpable to satisfy it. Cheek's special treatment of tax duties and Liparota's concern with apparently innocent conduct do not require an additional legal-knowledge element for this deliberate evasion offense. Their position preserves the Government's burdens to prove actual knowledge of the reporting requirement and the evasion purpose; it does not punish inadvertent banking activity or genuinely separate ordinary transactions merely because each falls below the reporting threshold. They read the statute as clear on that point and therefore find no residual ambiguity for lenity to resolve. This interpretation is noncontrolling.

### 1994-01-19 — [Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich; No. 92-896.](../records/Thunder_Basin_Coal_Co_v_Reich_merits_1994-01-19.md#public-projection)

Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, concurs in part and in the judgment. They join the statutory text, structure, and claim-channel analysis. They do not rely on legislative history and do not join the Court's formulation of the separate constitutional objection. In their view, a district court retains §1331 jurisdiction for the limited question whether the exclusion of original review is itself unconstitutional and therefore ineffective. That question cannot be rejected on the merits while every prerequisite to entertaining it is reserved; they find no such obstacle here.

They also decline to assume that compliance causes only insignificant harm. If the operator is right on its underlying claim, entry by union officials and disclosure of business information can have unrecoverable consequences. But that premise still does not require original pre-enforcement district-court review. The operator may comply and obtain review, or refuse and obtain judicial review before noncompliance penalties are imposed in the constitutionally material sense. Ordinary unrecoverable compliance costs therefore do not themselves establish a due-process violation. Ex parte Young addressed a different dilemma: compliance foreclosed review, while resistance exposed the challenger to severe penalties that practically prevented access. That foreclosure is absent here. Their distinct constitutional formulation is noncontrolling; their partial join does not turn it into the Court's seven-Justice explanation or add two votes to it.
