## Statute and record

26 U.S.C. §5861(d): “It shall be unlawful for any person ... to receive or possess a firearm which is not registered to him in the National Firearms Registration and Transfer Record.” The omitted introductory portion is the common introduction to a list; no exception to subsection (d) is omitted. Section 5845(a)(6) includes a machinegun within the defined term firearm. Section 5845(b)'s operative clause covers a weapon which shoots, is designed to shoot, or can be readily restored to shoot, automatically more than one shot, without manual reloading, by a single function of the trigger. The report's quotation elides the “is designed to shoot” alternative; no modeling may treat that alternative as absent. Section 5871 permits a fine up to $10,000 or imprisonment up to ten years, or both. These are the National Firearms Act provisions governing the prosecution; no subsequent gun-control enactment is supplied.

Staples possessed an AR-15, ordinarily a semiautomatic civilian rifle. The metal stop preventing selection of automatic fire had been filed away; his rifle contained an M-16 selector switch, hammer, disconnector, and trigger. BATF agents testified that testing produced multiple shots with one trigger pull. Nonregistration was undisputed. Staples testified the gun had never fired automatically while he possessed it, and that even semiautomatic operation sometimes failed, requiring manual ejection and chambering. The legal question concerns knowledge of the physical characteristics that make the weapon a statutory firearm, not knowledge of registration law.

He requested an instruction requiring proof beyond a reasonable doubt that he knew the gun would fire fully automatically. The court instead charged that voluntary and intentional possession was required, but the Government need not prove knowledge of every characteristic subjecting the weapon to regulation; it sufficed that he knew he was dealing with a dangerous device of a type that would alert one to the likelihood of regulation. The final sentence specified that knowledge the weapon was a firearm within the statute was unnecessary. The parties treated “suggested” in the transcript as a mistranscription of “which subjects it.” The indictment charged knowing receipt and possession of two unregistered machineguns; the jury convicted as to one. Sentence: five years' probation and a $5,000 fine. The Tenth Circuit relied on United States v. Mittleider, 835 F.2d 769 (1987), to hold that knowledge of the physical properties was not required.

## Competing submissions and eligible authorities

The Government's public-welfare position requires knowing possession of an item recognized as a dangerous weapon likely to be regulated; it does not demand conviction of someone who reasonably thought the object was a toy or a violin case. Its brief invokes Balint and Freed and contends that proof of the additional knowledge would frustrate enforcement. Its formulation is not a concession that only guns readily convertible to automatic fire count as the relevant category. Staples invokes the presumption of factual knowledge, lawful gun ownership, severe potential punishment, and the distinction between knowing possession of a gun and knowing possession of a machinegun. Circumstantial evidence can establish the required knowledge if that requirement governs; the legal issue does not determine what inferences this jury permissibly could have drawn. Staples does not present a constitutional due-process attack on the statute itself.

Ginsburg and Thomas sat on the panel issuing United States v. Harris, 959 F.2d 246, 259–261 (D.C. Cir. 1992), requiring knowledge of the gun characteristics under §5861(d). ## Exact limits

The report does not supply a factfinding that Staples lacked knowledge or that he necessarily knew. A knowledge rule does not entail a requirement to prove knowledge of nonregistration, knowledge of the statute, or a purpose to violate it. The merits issue does not settle a harmless-error inquiry or guarantee acquittal on remand. The full underlying trial record has not been independently acquired.
