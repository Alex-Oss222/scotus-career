## Operative rule, alternative, and remedy

In a general maritime tort action governed by proportionate-share settlement credit, a nonsettling defendant may not recover contribution from a settling defendant merely because the settlement dollars are less than the settler's equitable share. The reduction of the plaintiff's claim supplies the nonsettler's protection; contribution is neither necessary nor permitted to undo that bargain. Reliable allocation of fault remains necessary, but the settlement need not equal the settler's eventual percentage of total damages.

The strongest alternative was contribution to prevent excessive payment under Great Lakes' different credit mechanism. It fails because effective McDermott already prevents the excessive allocation through equitable-share reduction. Combining proportionate credit with contribution would give incompatible protections. This is an application of current law, not a new selection among settlement systems.

Vacatur and remand do not reinstate every district-court rationale, calculate damages or credit, or resolve exoneration, limitation, a separate indemnity right, settlement fraud or invalidity, or a nonsettlement shortfall. The unpassed alternative record contention remains unnecessary to this legal disposition. No new factual supplement is required for this bounded decision.
