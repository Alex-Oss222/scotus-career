The reconciled common affirmance proposition is that federal maritime law does not require this competent state court to apply the federal forum non conveniens rule to this domestic Jones Act and accompanying in personam maritime action. Mayfield's local-policy treatment of FELA forum convenience carries through the Jones Act's incorporation, while Bainbridge preserves state-court venue rules. Harmonizing general maritime law with that legislation avoids dividing a seaman's combined claims between inconsistent forum rules. This proposition does not approve Louisiana jurisdiction in every case, choose the most convenient forum, displace substantive maritime rights, or resolve a discrimination or Commerce Clause challenge that is not presented. Mayfield's own antidiscrimination qualifications remain intact; they do not become a new adjudicated claim here.

Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, and Ginsburg additionally support application of Jensen's characteristic-feature and uniformity limitations: forum non conveniens is a general rule of forum administration, and its discretionary, fact-sensitive operation does not supply a uniform substantive maritime entitlement to dismissal. Reliance on the procedural character is functional, not a universal exemption for anything labeled procedural. Burdens integral to a maritime right and substantive defenses remain different. These five supply support for that rationale without Stevens. No view is expressed on a dispute involving foreign parties; the actual parties' domestic status bounds the holding. Federal courts retain their own applicable forum rules.

The following clarification applies the frozen standard to the lower-court record already supplied and read by clean neutral A2; it uses no new source fact or new legal ground:

1. **Purpose and character:** Commercial parody can qualify, and the profit motive creates no dispositive rule or hard presumption. The reported work reasonably permits perception of criticism directed at the original's romantic presentation; this threshold does not certify artistic merit, final fairness, or every assertion of parody. Kennedy can join that limited conclusion while remaining doubtful about the strength of the claimed critical relationship and insisting that later courts test it rather than accept a label. A claim to parody's borrowing justification must target the original at least in part. Satire is not categorically excluded from fair use, but lacks that particular justification merely from being satire. A request for or refusal of permission is not a reason to deny an otherwise fair use.
2. **Nature of the original:** The published creative song lies within copyright's core. That fact remains part of the inquiry but contributes little to separating fair from unfair parody because recognizable expressive works are its ordinary objects. It is neither a categorical obstacle nor an exemption from assessing the second factor. The 1992 unpublished-work amendment is effective but does not decide this published-work dispute.
3. **Amount and substantiality, split by material:** Taking the original's recognizable opening and core is not excessive merely because it reaches the original's most valuable part. As to the lyrics, the short identifying reference followed by markedly different critical words does not take more than the parody reasonably needs; the copying is not excessive in relation to that parodic purpose. The broader repeated musical borrowing is not conclusively resolved. Its amount must be assessed against purpose, transformation, and potential substitution. No fixed quantitative allowance, finding that all repetitions are necessary, or established sampling violation follows.
4. **Market:** The defense bears its affirmative-defense burden. Commerciality does not create the rejected market-harm presumption for this transformative use. Consider substitution for the original and legitimate potential derivatives, including widespread comparable conduct. Harm from criticism is different from displacement; a hypothetical license to the critical attack itself is not a cognizable lost derivative market. A nonparody rap derivative may be a proper market to examine. The defense evidence about the original's audience does not resolve it, and this record establishes neither actual derivative harm nor its absence. No universal rule about markets for noncritical satire is adopted.
5. **Remedy:** Reverse and remand without reinstating summary judgment for the defense. The unresolved musical-taking inquiry and missing evidence about a potentially material derivative market require further consideration under the integrated factors. No final infringement or fair-use judgment, royalty obligation, damages, or injunction is directed. No independent constitutional speech immunity is adopted.
