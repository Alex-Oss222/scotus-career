## 69. Livadas v. Bradshaw

### Components and retained legal scope

**L1 — independent state claim and §301.** The wage-payment penalty claim does not require interpreting disputed CBA terms. Consulting the undisputed wage rate to calculate a possible penalty does not convert it into a federal contract dispute or preempt it under §301. *Lingle*, 486 U.S. 399, supplies independence from contract interpretation; *Allis-Chalmers*, *Lueck*, and *Lingle* do not immunize every claim bearing a state-law label. A genuinely contract-dependent claim remains different. The nonnegotiable character of this right matters but does not establish a universal exemption from preemption.

**L2 — interference with protected bargaining.** California's challenged policy withholds an otherwise available enforcement benefit solely because the employee is covered by a CBA containing arbitration terms, although her present claim requires no contract interpretation. That condition burdens the exercise of the NLRA-protected right to bargain collectively, including carrying bargaining through to an agreement providing for arbitration. The NLRA does not compel the employer to agree, guarantee a particular agreement, or generally require a State to supply wage enforcement. The vice is this policy's burden on protected activity. A private suit does not cure exclusion from the offered state channel. *Fort Halifax* permits independent minimum labor standards and does not make any bargaining-based exclusion lawful merely because some statutory standards permit genuine negotiated alternatives. Actual effects matter; neither good motives nor a mistaken belief about §301 saves this policy.

**L3 — the enacted §1983 channel.** The claim identifies an individual federal labor right, sufficiently definite to enforce, and state interference with that right. It does not rest on supremacy alone, every NLRA preemption rule, or an implied damages action created without statutory authorization. *Golden State*, 493 U.S. 103, supplies the separate inquiries into the right and congressional foreclosure. The NLRA's machinery for private labor disputes does not foreclose this §1983 action against governmental interference. The right to complete protected collective bargaining is inherent in the Act's concrete protection of that process; it need not be recast as an express statutory guarantee that the parties reach agreement.

**L4 — judgment and remedy.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit and remand for proceedings consistent with L1–L3. The defendant is the official-capacity successor, not a personally liable official established by this record. This does not automatically reinstate a money award, decide Eleventh Amendment objections, or decide personal qualified immunity. Lower courts retain unresolved questions about appropriate relief and the underlying penalty. No automatic entitlement to the three days claimed follows from prevailing on the federal policy challenge.

The statutory distinctions remain indispensable. Section 201 requires earned, unpaid wages on discharge. Section 203 addresses willful failure to pay, continues wages until payment or suit subject to its thirty-day limit, and does not make every late payment penal without willfulness. Section 219's restriction on private waiver and its permission for more frequent, greater, or earlier payment are distinct from §229's rule allowing an individual wage suit despite private arbitration, and §229's exception for interpretation or application of a CBA with an arbitration agreement. The specialized provisions concerning executive, administrative, or professional pay periods and CBA vacation-pay terms are not silently transferred into §§201 or 203. The Commissioner's broader administrative refusal is not itself a command written into §229. Contract §18.8 permits the relevant direct wage claims through arbitration or an authorized court or agency when no contract interpretation is required; the Court does not decide every possible contract waiver. Administrative proceedings under §§98 and 98.1, discretionary court representation under §98.3 with its financial-inability and valid-enforceable-claim conditions, and the employee's own §218 suit with §218.5 fee consequences are distinct channels, not interchangeable guarantees of identical access.

The governing simulated *Suter* remains exactly bounded. Section 671(a)(15), read in the plan and administrative framework, supplies no asserted individual reasonable-efforts right and no separate implied action. Section 671(a)(9)'s triggered reporting duty supplies neither the asserted §1983 right nor the separate implied action. The §1983-right and implied-action inquiries are distinct. There is no categorical rule against enforcement of state-plan provisions and no categorical rule that funding review forecloses an otherwise existing §1983 right. Section 671(a)(16), read with §§675(1) and 675(5)(B), remains unresolved. *Livadas* does not revise those holdings. No later *Blessing* framework or other post-cutoff authority is imported.

## 70. United States v. Carlton

### Components and retained legal scope

**C1 — enacted temporal reach.** Congress expressly made the 1987 amendment operate as if included in the 1986 enactment. That resolves statutory temporal direction, subject to constitutional review; it does not itself decide constitutionality. *Landgraf* and *Rivers* preserve this distinction. The original provision did not contain the decedent-ownership condition later added. The executor's eligibility under the original text and satisfaction of the other conditions are not treated as statutory mistakes merely because Congress corrected the provision.

**C2 — principal due-process ground.** For Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg, the retroactive amendment rationally serves a legitimate corrective purpose: Congress promptly restricted an unintended expansion of the ESOP deduction and the associated revenue loss. *R. A. Gray* and *Romein* supply the rational legislative purpose test for retroactive economic legislation. The corrective aim, prompt response, and modest period support this particular application. The older new-tax decisions, including *Untermyer*, do not establish a vested entitlement to every deduction or invalidate this amendment of an existing tax regime. This is not a rule that every law called curative is constitutional.

**C3 — reliance and duration under that ground.** The executor actually relied, bought stock after the decedent's death, sold it to the ESOP at a loss, and lacked notice of the amendment when acting. These facts are not denied. They do not by themselves establish a constitutional violation. Congress's period, extending slightly more than a year from the 1986 enactment to the December 1987 amendment, is modest in the circumstances. The principal ground establishes no categorical one-year ceiling and no unlimited permission for very long retroactivity. The taxes in *Irvine* involved a later taxable transfer and do not substitute for this genuine retroactivity inquiry; *Morgan Stanley* involved legislative reopening of a final judicial judgment and supplies no such predicate here.

**C4 — judgment and bounded implementation.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit and remand. The added decedent-ownership condition defeats the claimed deduction on the stipulated facts; the decedent did not own the securities immediately before death. The amendment's allocation condition is distinct and is not an independently adjudicated factual failure in this case. The other deduction conditions were stipulated satisfied. No new refund amount, penalty, valuation, final-judgment reopening, or unpursued Contract Clause or Takings ruling follows.

The original 50-percent deduction, proceeds and return-deadline requirements, qualifying securities and ESOP categories, exclusions, verified §4979A consent, and statutory time limitations remain distinct conditions. The amendment requires both qualifying decedent ownership immediately before death and allocation to participants or the specified future-allocation arrangements involving an exempt loan or qualifying transfer. The substitution qualification for a bona fide business purpose such as a merger concerns allocation; it does not excuse decedent ownership. The present decision cannot be summarized as a generic ban on every estate's stock sale to an ESOP, or as a determination that these shares failed an allocation requirement. The neutral packet reports an immaterial discrepancy in the printed deduction figure between sources; this freeze neither resolves it nor relies on a new amount.

### Corrected grounds requiring distinct treatment

**O'Connor's ground (OC).** She accepts legitimate revenue-raising purpose and rational review, but does not make speculation about the earlier Congress's intent do the work assigned to corrective purpose by the principal rationale. Duration independently matters to her constitutional assessment. Taxpayers commonly face a limited period of retroactive adjustment, and the short period here is permissible. Her warning that retroactivity reaching farther than the year preceding the legislative session would raise serious constitutional questions is a caution about a different case, not a categorical numerical invalidity rule. Reliance is substantial and does not disappear; the permissible short period defeats this challenge. She joins the judgment, not the complete C2–C3 principal explanation.

**Scalia and Thomas's ground (ST).** They reject the claimed substantive economic protection under the Due Process Clause. The Clause supplies process rather than a substantive restriction of the kind asserted here; no adequate procedural deficiency is presented. Their public *TXO* position already states that distinction. They do not uphold the law by joining the principal rational-purpose/modest-period constitutional method. Indeed, accepting that method's premise would make the Government's retroactive defeat of deliberate reliance troubling in their view; describing the period as short does not answer the particular reliance injury. Their judgment agreement therefore cannot be counted as agreement with C2–C3 or as two additional votes for a “not harsh and oppressive” application. They accept the enacted temporal direction and the judgment consequence on their own constitutional ground.

## 71. Department of Taxation and Finance v. Milhelm Attea & Bros.

### Components and retained legal scope

**M1 — federal trader statutes and taxable nonmember trade.** The Indian Trader statutes do not categorically displace reasonable state requirements directed to collecting taxes validly imposed on nonexempt cigarette consumers. *Warren Trading Post* preserves its restriction on a direct tax on federally protected reservation trade; its broad language does not establish immunity from every otherwise permissible collection requirement. *Moe*, *Colville*, and *Potawatomi* permit reasonably necessary collection burdens while preserving exempt trade and the distinct bar against suing an immune tribe. The federal authority to appoint and regulate Indian traders is not disregarded.

**M2 — documentation and collection structure.** Certificates, invoice and record requirements, monthly reports, state-agent and stamping requirements for nonexempt sales, and advance collection through the distribution chain do not establish facial preemption merely because they burden a federally licensed trader. Their legitimate operation concerns state collection of valid taxes, not a second state judgment of fitness to hold the federal trader appointment. Whether a particular execution becomes unreasonable remains different from the present facial attack.

**M3 — quotas and exemptions.** Limiting tax-free deliveries to probable demand by exempt purchasers may reasonably prevent diversion to taxable trade, provided exempt purchases remain adequately available. The regulations include tribal and information-based mechanisms to estimate demand and distribute it; no demonstrated inadequate quota is before the Court. Prior approval and the quantity mechanism are sustained against the present facial objections, not approved regardless of delay, inaccurate calculation, tribal rights, or future administration. Stamped cigarettes have no corresponding quantity ceiling.

**M4 — judgment and remedy.** Reverse the New York Court of Appeals' facial judgment on both its categorical preemption theory and its alternative excessive-burden ground, and remand. Neither a particular quota's correctness, an implemented denial of exempt cigarettes, enforcement against a tribe, nor a finding that a particular retailer evaded taxes is adjudicated. The Seneca treaty argument was not addressed below and is not decided here. The earlier real-world GVR is historical procedural background, not an unrecorded simulated adjudication.

The protected exemption is not “all Indian purchases”: it concerns qualifying enrolled members of New York Indian nations or tribes purchasing within qualified reservations for their own consumption there, not resale. Untaxed wholesale deliveries require the proper exemption certificate, intended exempt purchasers, and reservation delivery. The wholesaler's initial certification and subsequent invoice procedure are distinct from a consumer's initial certificate and later written identity procedure. A tribe may enter the §336.7(c)(1) agreement and administer delivery approval; that possibility is not a command to surrender state or tribal authority. Otherwise, probable demand uses evidence submitted by a regulating tribe or statewide per-capita consumption and enrollment where the tribe does not regulate. Territory allocation similarly uses tribal consultation or Department information. Approval still requires valid orders and reasonable quantities, with the applicable monthly coupon mechanism. Withholding approval for relevant violations and cancellation for noncompliance remain tied to lawful collection; no discretion to destroy the exemption is authorized by this judgment. Unlimited stamped supply, documentation, payment, and exemption channels must be read together.

The record's statewide sales disparities support concern about diversion, not a finding that every retailer or respondent evaded tax. The lower report's §335 and later report's §336 numbering is not used to invent a substantive amendment. No extra same-case-report factual detail, including an additional characterization of certification practices, is made a new indispensable premise. The frozen facial posture and supplied safeguards suffice.

## 72. Howlett v. Birkdale Shipping Co.

### Components and retained legal scope

**H1 — the statutory allocation.** Section 905(b) permits negligence liability under its conditions, not an unseaworthiness warranty or automatic liability for injury aboard ship. **H2 — turnover warning and inspection.** A vessel must warn of hazards it knows, or should know through exercise of reasonable care, that are likely to be encountered by the unloading stevedore and are neither known nor obvious to, nor reasonably anticipated by, a competent stevedore performing the work. For a cargo-stow hazard, this does not impose a general duty to supervise the foreign or prior loading stevedore or inspect the completed stow to discover dangerous conditions. Without actual knowledge, warning depends on a duty of reasonable care that would require discovery; the rejected general-inspection theory cannot supply that duty. Contract, positive law, or custom can affect the ordinary allocation when established; no new such predicate is found here. **H3 — actual knowledge and the summary-judgment record.** Actual knowledge remains material on the theories presented. Supplying plastic sheeting alone does not conclusively establish vessel knowledge that it was dangerously placed. Visibility to crew during loading is not the same as visibility to the stevedore during unloading. The latter question uses the competent-stevedore standard, not merely the plaintiff's subjective lack of awareness. Evidence that the plaintiff's own witness could see the plastic from above during unloading is adverse evidence to be addressed, not suppressed. Crew role and knowledge attribution cannot be assumed from the fact that somebody aboard might have seen material. Neither actual knowledge nor the latent-hazard condition is finally found.

Summary judgment can remain appropriate if the actual record, assessed under the right standards, does not establish a triable claim.
