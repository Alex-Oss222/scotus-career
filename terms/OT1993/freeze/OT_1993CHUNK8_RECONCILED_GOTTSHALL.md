## Reconciled operative positions

**Five-Justice physical-danger position:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas recognize a FELA negligent-emotional-injury claim when the employee sustains physical impact through employer negligence or is placed in immediate risk of physical harm by that negligence and suffers emotional injury from fear of harm to himself. Duty, breach, legally cognizable injury and FELA causation remain distinct. Seriousness, medical genuineness, foreseeability and the employment relationship do not alone replace this boundary. An impact-only restriction is rejected, as is bystander recovery outside the employee's zone of danger. This is a resolution of an issue Buell reserved, not a holding already supplied by Buell or Hawaiian Airlines.

For Gottshall, reverse the Third Circuit judgment and remand for that court to apply the zone-of-danger rule in the first instance; no finding here resolves his exposure, causation or ultimate liability. For Carlisle, reverse and remand with instructions to enter judgment for Conrail because the work-stress claim presented falls outside the boundary. This is a legal exclusion, not a finding that the trial lacked ordinary negligence instructions or competent evidence of serious illness. The remand does not permit a retrial of the same legally excluded claim.

**Three-Justice ordinary-negligence position:** Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg would affirm both judgments. Serious, genuine psychiatric injury directly sustained in negligently unsafe railway employment may be compensable without immediate physical danger. The employee must prove unreasonable working conditions, reasonable foreseeability, serious genuine injury and the causal contribution FELA requires. The actual medical evidence and physical manifestations suffice to address genuineness in these records; whether such manifestations are indispensable in every case remains open. This does not make employment stress, diagnostic labels or proof of injury alone sufficient. It does not establish a separate unlimited bystander rule. Gottshall retains trial access, not a damages award; Carlisle retains his $386,500 verdict.

## Assembly compatibility and published-position content

Five non-Stone Justices support the common zone rule and both reversals; three support both affirmances. All eight recognize that some negligent emotional injuries are compensable under FELA, but that shared general proposition does not merge their incompatible boundaries. No Marks synthesis is needed or authorized. Historical authorship must not determine assignment.

The five support an opinion preserving Buell's open question, Urie's occupational-disease coverage, Rogers's statutory causation rule and Ellis's refusal of insurer liability, while giving common-law boundaries substantial weight under Monessen. A blanket declaration that foreseeability never constrains any negligence duty would exceed this emotional-injury question. A claim that common law is necessarily dispositive despite the statute and its developments also exceeds the adopted position. Souter's evolving-federal-common-law explanation is compatible with full support for the zone rule; it may be preserved separately if it performs public legal work. It supplies no broader liability rule.

The three dissenting positions require a distinction between ordinary workplace unhappiness and serious injury from proved unreasonable working conditions. Their answer to the unlimited-liability argument is the confined employee relationship plus ordinary fault and genuine, severe injury, with medical/physical evidence on these records. The objection to Carlisle's categorical exclusion remains even accepting that the claim does not satisfy the zone rule. Their support for Gottshall does not turn allegations into findings.
