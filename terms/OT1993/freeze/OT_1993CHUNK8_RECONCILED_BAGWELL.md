## Reconciled result and authority map

All eight associates—Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg—retain the threshold decision to reach the federal classification/jury question and the judgment to reverse the Virginia Supreme Court insofar as it sustains the surviving public fines, with remand for proceedings consistent with their criminal character and the jury requirement. Each also supports the following propositions at the same operative generality:

1. These substantial, fixed, noncompensatory fines for completed out-of-court violations, payable to public beneficiaries and without a later compliance-based opportunity to avoid the assessed liability, are criminal sanctions. Advance announcement does not itself transform them into civil coercion. The complex continuing decree, dispersed violations and extensive retrospective factfinding reinforce that conclusion; complexity alone is not a universal classification test.
2. Serious criminal contempt requires the applicable criminal safeguards, including a jury. Proof beyond a reasonable doubt before a judge does not replace that independent protection. These approximately $52 million public fines are plainly serious and cannot stand after the nonjury proceedings; no exact petty-fine ceiling is fixed.
3. The criminal-classification ruling removes the basis for collecting the surviving public fines through the civil nonjury adjudications. It revives neither the initial vacated fines nor the approximately $12 million Company fines; supplies no substitute compensatory award; commands no fresh prosecution; and prejudges no future jeopardy or other defense.

**Additional procedural account:** Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support a bounded explanation of why the contempt power's necessity and procedural protections differ among immediate courtroom obstruction, conduct impeding adjudication, discrete acts susceptible to coercion, and dispersed violations of a complex continuing decree requiring extensive factfinding. Ginsburg does not join this additional account as an independent formulation of the civil/criminal boundary. That division tracks the historical Part II-B distinction, but supplies no assignment or required numbering. The seven do not adopt a free-floating reliability balance that could dispense with the criminal jury once serious criminal contempt is established, or a mechanical command that every complex injunction must be enforced criminally.

Ginsburg joins the common classification, jury rule, application, remedy and preserved powers; her withholding of the additional procedural account is not judgment-only agreement with the entire decision. Scalia supports the common propositions and bounded additional account, with his own separate historical-method explanation and express refusal to choose a comprehensive test in this extreme case. Thomas is not assigned a join in Scalia's separate writing. These distinctions require proposition-level and writing-level accounting, not a single unanimous full-opinion label. No Marks synthesis is needed for the directly shared propositions.

## Separate grounds preserved for assembly and public continuity

**Scalia's separate ground.** Scalia joins the decision on these extreme facts while declining to choose among every competing civil/criminal test. Traditional civil coercion commonly enforced an identifiable act, or cessation of an identifiable continuing act, with confinement or per-day pressure ending upon compliance. That history differs from a continuing judicial code governing numerous activities, where no single performance ends the obligations and retrospective credibility disputes multiply. The concern is the concentration of rulemaking, accusation, factfinding and punishment in the same judge without ordinary criminal safeguards. His actual Weiss historical-practice ground and Honda concurrence supply date-eligible personal support for requiring historical justification rather than a modern free-form estimate of overall fairness. His reference to the known supervisory provision illustrates that a discrete act can remain enforceable through civil coercion; it does not authorize clause-by-clause relief absent the order and allocations. Past coercive fines need not always be erased after compliance: stopping future per-day pressure differs from undoing punishment already endured. He leaves for a properly presented case the exact boundary identifying which modern injunctions sufficiently resemble the historically accepted forms. This is an additional explanation of concurrence, not a separately controlling universal test. Thomas has no established join in it.

**Ginsburg's separate ground.** Ginsburg retains the traditional Gompers/Hicks classification as the immediate basis for deciding whether criminal safeguards apply. Coercive relief benefits the complainant by inducing an act the contemnor can perform; punishment for a completed forbidden act cannot be undone merely by promising future obedience. Incidental overlap in purposes does not erase that distinction. Bagwell's theory that the fines were conditional because obedience could have prevented them would classify ordinary criminal fines as civil too. Mine Workers's suspended fine offered postimposition compliance through specified affirmative acts; these assessments offered no such route. The survival of the unpaid public fines after settlement, withdrawal of the complainant, and appointment of a public collection official also reveal vindication of public authority rather than remedial enforcement for the private complainant. If the proceedings truly retained only that civil character, the parties' settlement and joint request would call for vacatur in this setting. Her conclusion is tied to these uncalibrated public liabilities, not a general payee-only rule or resolution of every settlement effect under state law. Serious criminal classification requires the jury regardless of the substantial bench procedures provided. She joins the common historical discussion and its application and remedial limits, but does not adopt the additional Part-II-B-type necessity/factfinding account as a new boundary rule. Her Honda dissent about civil punitive-damages safeguards supplies no contrary criminal-jury position.

## Complete legal limits and remedy

- Classification is a federal constitutional inquiry into substantive relief. A civil label and a court's announced coercive aim are relevant descriptions, not dispositive. State law supplies guidance; deference appropriate to legislative civil schemes does not make a single judge's classification conclusive. No general Ward civil/criminal framework is overruled.
- Compensation for proved losses remains a civil remedy. Genuine conditional coercion can also remain civil when the ordered act is within the contemnor's ability and compliance supplies the relevant means of avoiding or ending the sanction. The present decision does not require erasure of every accrued per-day fine or invalidate a fixed fine suspended on a real compliance condition. It does not decide every discovery sanction or every simple injunction.
- Merely phrasing an order affirmatively or negatively does not answer classification. A continuing command can be expressed either way, and this decree contained both. No unrequested clause-by-clause severance is performed.
- The civil/criminal distinction is not identical to the serious/petty distinction. Serious criminal contempt requires a jury; petty criminal contempt may be tried without one but does not become civil and retains applicable criminal safeguards. The Court need not select the dollar boundary to classify roughly $52 million as serious.
- The traditional power to address direct courtroom contempt is preserved subject to its existing limits. Serious criminal punishment does not lose the jury requirement merely because the contempt was direct; summary authority is not an unrestricted exception. This case concerns indirect contempt. Discovery and other litigation-management sanctions remain in their proper categories.
- The holding does not prohibit serious fines through lawful criminal-contempt proceedings. It directs no prosecution, resolves no double-jeopardy or other defense to a later proceeding, finds no jury waiver or harmlessness, and makes no finding of actual judicial bias. A high burden of proof does not replace the jury.
- Reverse the state high court's judgment sustaining the public assessments and return for proceedings consistent with these conclusions. No reinstatement of either vacated set of fines, new compensation, exact money command, substantive-excessiveness ruling, wholesale invalidation of the dissolved injunction, special mandate date or retained Supreme Court proceeding is supplied.
