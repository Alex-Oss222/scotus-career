## Added statutory details and their bounded effect

**Basic tier:** Section 623(b)(7), added by Act §3 at 106 Stat. 1467, requires a separately available basic tier whose subscription is prerequisite to other tiers. It includes §614/§615 carriage, required franchise PEG programming, and provided broadcast signals subject to the stated satellite-outside-local-service-area exception. An operator may add other video signals or services; those additions become subject to basic-tier rate regulation. This provision is distinct from §615(h)'s lowest-priced-tier duty for compulsory noncommercial carriage. It clarifies actual distribution and potential burden; it neither proves actual programmer displacement nor establishes final fit. It leaves both original classification paths available.

**Election:** Section 325(b)(3)(B), (4)–(6), added by Act §6 at 106 Stat. 1482–1483, directs regulations requiring the initial election within one year of enactment and subsequent elections every three years, with a common election for systems serving the same geographic area. A station choosing retransmission consent as to a system cannot simultaneously demand §614 carriage from that system. Its consent rights cannot supersede another station's elected §614/§615 carriage, and the copyright compulsory license and station–programmer licensing agreements are preserved. These details confirm the original no-double-entitlement premise. They establish no particular station's actual election, negotiated payment or FCC implementation finding. Section 6's operation may be relevant background without adding an independent challenge to this limited appeal.

**Separate leased access:** Section 612(i)(4), added by Act §9(c) at 106 Stat. 1485, preserves the separate §615 obligation. The leased-access source categories do not replace noncommercial-station eligibility or silently enlarge the constitutional question. The original commitments do not depend on merging those definitions, so no change follows.

None of these additions changes commercial/noncommercial numerical duties, the conditional small-system exemption, duplicate-signal rules, payment/signal qualifications, channel-position alternatives or the stated transition provisions. No added basic-tier, election or leased-access fact determines strict versus intermediate scrutiny or cures the existing evidentiary disputes. The original reservations concerning separately content-sensitive low-power/market-adjustment criteria and current home-shopping implementation remain. The lower opinion's distinct low-power cross-claim discussion does not itself decide the different constitutional characterization of every programming-based eligibility criterion; no new component is adjudicated through this narrow refresh.
