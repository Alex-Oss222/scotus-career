## Effective premises and reconciliation findings

Actual Landgraf now supplies the governing provision-specific framework: first determine congressional temporal direction; absent sufficient direction, determine whether application would attach new legal consequences to completed events; apply the presumption against that reach where it would. Its four associate full joiners are Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, and Ginsburg. Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas use their distinct relevant-activity and textual-command method. Blackmun maintains the contrary pending-case/remedial position stated in his actual dissent. The actual Court's five-vote general framework is not converted into a seven-associate framework by a shared Rivers result.

Actual Harper separately governs judicial interpretation applied to parties and still-open direct review. It displaces the lower court's selective Chevron Oil balancing within that scope. Actual Robertson confirms that Congress may enact changed law governing identified pending disputes; it does not supply missing temporal direction in §101. The historical Rivers result rests on materially the same relevant premises. Neither the actual Landgraf framework, its individual joins, nor actual Harper/Robertson establishes a Justice-specific changed premise requiring departure.

The enacted §101 redesignates the old text as subsection (a), adds subsection (b) defining making and enforcing contracts to include making, performance, modification, termination, and enjoyment of all contractual benefits, privileges, terms, and conditions, and adds subsection (c)'s protection against nongovernmental discrimination and impairment under color of state law. The reviewed expansion concerns contractual coverage relevant to the broader discharge theory. This is not a separate contest over private-contract coverage or every consequence of subsection (c).

Section 402(a)'s effectiveness upon enactment, except as specifically otherwise provided, is not by itself backward coverage. Section 109(c) separately limits its extraterritorial amendments to later conduct. Section 402(b) excludes disparate-impact cases whose complaints were filed before March 1, 1975 and whose initial decisions were rendered after October 30, 1983; both conditions matter. Those clauses do not become a single exception or a command that every other amendment reaches every pending case. Section 3(4)'s response-to-decisions and expansion purpose identifies the substantive aim, not the missing temporal reach.

The seven affirmers conclude that §101 adds substantive coverage and legal liability beyond old §1981 as authoritatively construed. A later Congress may amend a statute it believes misconstrued and may provide sufficiently clear retrospective reach within constitutional bounds. Neither corrective purpose alone nor enactment in the form of a definition supplies that direction. Restorative purpose is relevant evidence of intent, not a separate presumption of retroactivity. Frisbie and Freeborn involved actual congressional direction to cure the identified problem; they do not establish such a presumption. Section 101 has substantial prospective operation and is not ineffective without application to earlier conduct. The broader coverage of §1981, including contracts outside employment and employers outside Title VII's size threshold, also defeats an assertion that the amendment only adjusts remedies for an unchanged Title VII duty.

Judicial interpretation and subsequent legislation remain distinct. Patterson supplies the governing old-text construction within its actual scope; disagreement with its correctness does not permit treating a later enacted definition as the meaning the old statute always had. Harper explains its application to open cases involving earlier conduct. No actual reliance on Patterson at the time of the 1986 conduct is required to identify a later amendment's expanded legal consequences. The review does not reopen Patterson's original merits, declare all postformation conduct unprotected, or independently decide every discriminatory-discharge question that Patterson itself did not adjudicate. The preserved enforcement claim demonstrates why that scope matters.

## Individual commitments and strongest alternatives

### Blackmun — reverse the temporal ruling and remand

**Sufficient grounds:** maintain his actual Landgraf reading of §402(a) with the two specific exceptions as supporting pending-case application; apply that reading to §101's targeted restoration of protection after Patterson. Independently, his Bradley approach asks whether application upsets vested rights or settled expectations. Here the discharges occurred in 1986, before Patterson, when Sixth Circuit law already recognized §1981 liability for discriminatory contract termination. He would apply §101 at least in this pending-appeal setting where it restores the contemporaneous exposure rather than creating an unforeseeable burden for this respondent. The official comparator makes this record-specific limitation explicit; the final freeze preserves it instead of extending his position automatically to all conduct between Patterson and enactment.

**Personal evidence and adversarial test:** his own Bradley opinion, Kaiser dissent join, complete Brennan Patterson dissent join, and actual Landgraf dissent give concrete support. His historical Rivers dissent expressly accepts that §101 expands conduct prohibited under Patterson, but distinguishes this pre-Patterson case because the earlier recognized duty and settled expectations align with the amended rule. His Bennett join and the new-coverage nature of §101 provide the strongest contrary path; Bowen and Bennett protect fixed legal expectations in their settings, whereas his chosen reading treats this employer's existing exposure as the decisive difference. This does not deny that some §101 conduct lies outside Title VII or earlier statutory liability.

**Join barriers and narrower route:** no join in the absence-of-direction conclusion or the denial of amended coverage here. He can acknowledge Congress's need to legislate and Harper's distinction between judicial interpretation and amendment; his disagreement is with the reach and presumption governing the enacted replacement. Minimum reversal permits broader coverage for this pre-Patterson, pending case, subject to ordinary elements, defenses, and remedies. No present liability or damages award, freestanding §1981 punitive temporal holding, or automatic reopening of all earlier issues follows. His Landgraf reservation on §102 punitive damages is not silently converted into an additional Rivers vote.

**Reconciliation:** result confirmed, with the concrete pre-Patterson/settled-expectations limitation made explicit. No historical departure.

### Stevens — affirm and remand

**Sufficient ground:** apply his actual Landgraf framework separately to §101. Expanded contractual coverage adds legal consequences to the 1986 events. The Act contains no sufficient retrospective instruction; substantive disapproval of Patterson does not supply one. Patterson remains the authoritative old-text construction within the scope relevant to the temporal question, while the old enforcement claim survives separately.

**Personal evidence and alternative:** his own Patterson separate opinion and limited joins in Brennan's opinion rejected the narrow ongoing-contract reading; that is the strongest personal basis for accepting restoration. His actual Landgraf authorship, Kaiser majority join, and actual Harper join nevertheless distinguish the merits of a statutory reading from the temporal reach of later legislation. The direct historical Rivers writing confirms that distinction notwithstanding his disagreement with Patterson. No actual precedent changes the premise.

**Join barriers/minimum route:** may join the full provision-specific explanation under Landgraf, including the rejection of a separate restorative presumption and the legislative/judicial distinction. No constitutional prohibition on clear retrospective correction; no endorsement of the lower court's entire-Act generalization or its superseded Chevron Oil method. Affirm and remand with the existing old-law jury proceedings intact, not a total dismissal or new merits judgment. Historical authorship does not itself assign his role in the assembled Court.

Section 102's new Title VII jury provision does not govern this old §1981 jury remedy. No historical departure.

No historical departure.
