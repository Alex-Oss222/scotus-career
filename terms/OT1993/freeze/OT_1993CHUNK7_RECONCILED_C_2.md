## 82. Heck v. Humphrey, No. 93-6188

### Judgment, statutory grounds and shared limits

All eight associates affirm the Seventh Circuit's judgment maintaining dismissal **without prejudice**. Their sufficient grounds reject the lower court's conversion of damages into habeas and reject general exhaustion as the condition of a §1983 action. They do not award damages, order release, determine the underlying alleged violations, decide immunity or personal responsibility, or adjudicate state-law preclusion. They accept the lower courts' characterization of the presented action as necessarily attacking the conviction; Heck did not dispute that characterization in his petition or principal merits brief. The late reply contention does not establish a separately preserved independent-injury count requiring remand.

**Shared necessary-invalidity rule for this prisoner:** When success on the claimed damages would necessarily establish the invalidity of an outstanding conviction or sentence, the claim cannot proceed unless the conviction or sentence has been reversed on direct review, expunged by executive order, declared invalid by a state tribunal authorized to do so, or called into question by a federal court's issuance of a writ of habeas corpus. Exhaustion, an unsuccessful state challenge, and an unsuccessful federal habeas petition are not qualifying invalidation. For the claim barred on that ground, accrual awaits qualifying invalidation; it is not accrual now plus the Seventh Circuit's equitable tolling. A stay is unnecessary to preserve this unaccrued claim. This does not decide accrual for an independently cognizable claim.

**Independent injuries remain distinct:** Mere connection with the same investigation or trial is insufficient. An unlawful-search claim may coexist with a valid conviction because of independent source, inevitable discovery or harmless error. Compensatory recovery still requires an actual compensable injury; conviction or imprisonment injury cannot be recovered while the necessary invalidity remains unestablished. Ordinary immunity, preclusion, causation and other defenses remain available. Possible abstention in pending parallel criminal proceedings is not a rule or remedy adopted for this completed-conviction record. No malice or absence-of-probable-cause element is added to every constitutional damages action.

**Two different sufficient rationales:** The common-law-informed ground treats favorable invalidation as a prerequisite to an action necessarily impugning an outstanding criminal judgment; its operation does not depend on whether the plaintiff remains incarcerated when suing. The narrower statutory ground imposes the prerequisite at the intersection of §1983 and the available habeas channel, using favorable termination as a means to prevent circumvention of that channel. It does not use the tort analogy to foreclose people genuinely outside that intersection. Agreement about Heck's actual claim and delayed accrual is not agreement about this difference.

The alternative immediate-damages or stay path has the substantial support of §1983's text, Patsy and the unavailability of damages in habeas. Each associate rejects that path for this action because its indispensable invalidity premise would contradict the still-operative conviction. Preclusion cannot simply be presumed, and a stay does not explain why a defeated custody challenge could be retried for damages. Their rejection rests on the specified grounds rather than on a finding that every prisoner claim belongs in habeas.

### Exact direct-join scope independent of the disputed framework

Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg each directly accept the following separable propositions at the stated level of generality; their support does not require combining incompatible rationales under Marks:

1. **Present cognizability:** For a state prisoner in Heck's position—currently in custody, with a lawful opportunity to challenge the conviction, and seeking damages whose success necessarily establishes the invalidity of that still-operative conviction—the §1983 claim is not presently cognizable unless one of the four specified invalidating events has occurred. The opportunity description identifies the case being decided; it does not announce that meaningful habeas access is an element required in every application of the rule.
2. **Invalidation and accrual:** Reversal on direct review, executive expungement, an authorized state tribunal's declaration of invalidity, or a federal habeas writ supplies qualifying invalidation. Exhaustion or loss of review does not. The damages claim described in proposition 1 accrues only on qualifying invalidation; the lower court's tolling solution and a preservation stay are unnecessary for that claim.
3. **Independent injury and remedy:** A claim whose success does not necessarily establish invalidity is outside this bar, although its merits, compensable injury and ordinary defenses must still be established. Here the accepted lower-court characterization brings the presented action within proposition 1, so dismissal without prejudice stands on cognizability, not exhaustion or conversion. No constitutional merits, preclusion, immunity, damages amount or release relief is decided.

The eight associates can directly join a severable section stating those propositions and their statutory application to Heck. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg need not abandon their broader ground to join it. Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter need not adopt that broader ground. Any explanation treating common-law tort policy as an independently sufficient universal bar, or making actual habeas availability a universal necessary element, must remain separately tested against the four opposing join barriers. The narrower four's affirmative protection of persons genuinely outside the habeas intersection is their separate position, not an eight-associate reservation. The broader four's refusal to create an exception merely because custody has ended is likewise distinct. This freezes support for exact propositions, without assigning any writing or stating a full-Court coalition.

### Reconciliation against actual entering law

Cavanaugh's opportunity-only holding leaves conviction validity and custody duration undetermined. It therefore remains intact when a damages action is barred because success necessarily determines invalidity. O'Connor's authorship and Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter's joins do not imply that they permit a contradictory invalidity judgment. Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg's broader habeas-channel objection in Cavanaugh supplies additional position evidence, not a holding that all prisoner civil claims fail.

Albright supplies no controlling rationale recognizing or rejecting a universal constitutional malicious-prosecution action. Its individual positions identify constitutional sources and injuries; a common-law analogy used to determine timing and cognizability does not create the underlying federal right. Custis's controlling ACCA forum rule and its express no-meaningful-review reservation remain fully effective **for that sentencing channel**. The reservation neither creates a §1983 habeas-availability element nor erases a Justice's contrary choice in the different damages statute. Reed separates §2254 jurisdiction, collateral entitlement and denial on a particular record. Its denial does not mean a review channel was unavailable or a conviction was invalidated. Hawaiian Airlines confirms the importance of actual legal dependence rather than factual overlap but decides the RLA, not §1983.

These actual decisions sharpen the explanation and preserve independent injuries. They do not establish a changed fact, statutory text, custody status, preserved question or prior §1983 holding sufficient to justify swapping O'Connor's and Ginsburg's historical grounds. O'Connor's own narrower statutory approach and Ginsburg's actual channel positions make the source-specific corrections below appropriate. Nor do the actual decisions supply a concrete reason to turn Scalia, Kennedy or Thomas's general favorable-invalidation approach into an agreement that current habeas availability is invariably essential.

## 83. Davis v. United States, No. 92-1949

### Judgment, factual locks and alternative rules

All eight associates affirm the Court of Military Appeals' judgment sustaining admission of the challenged November 4 statements and the military conviction. They do so without reopening the separate October 20 interview question. They accept the valid initial oral/written waiver, the credited first statement “Maybe I should talk to a lawyer,” the actual pause in offense questioning and limited clarification, the response disclaiming a request, and cessation when Davis later said that he wanted a lawyer before saying anything else. The intervening reminder was **not** repetition of all warnings or a new written waiver. The military judge resolved the disputed factual accounts; this review supplies no new credibility finding.

**Clear-request ground:** After a knowing and voluntary waiver, a suspect must communicate a request sufficiently clearly that a reasonable officer in the circumstances would understand that counsel is requested. No prescribed language or legal sophistication is required. A reference understood only as a possible desire for counsel does not activate Edwards's cessation rule. Limited clarification is permissible and often advisable but is not mandatory under this ground.

**Clarification ground:** When actual postwaiver words reasonably suggest a wish for counsel but leave the choice uncertain, officers must pause substantive interrogation and confine questioning to neutral clarification. They may not discourage counsel, predict counsel's advice or use purported clarification to badger the suspect. If the clarified choice is to proceed, substantive questioning may resume; if counsel is requested, Edwards applies. An unrelated mention of a lawyer or pure speculation about an unexpressed wish does not supply this trigger.

Both grounds permit the actual clarifying exchange. Neither permits using later answers to undo an initially clear request, as Smith forbids. A clear invocation stops police-initiated custodial questioning unless counsel is present or the suspect initiates further communication and validly waives the protection under its governing rules. The third path—treating the first remark as requiring complete termination, including a prohibition on genuine clarification—has no associate support on the credited record. Rejecting it is not approval of coercive clarification. Under neither selected ground is suppression or further remedial review necessary here.

The military Miranda/Edwards framework is accepted as applied by the lower courts and the parties. Direct Fifth Amendment operation in the military is not separately adjudicated. Sixth Amendment attachment and an invalid initial waiver are not presented. The Government did not invoke §3501 below or rely on it as its merits ground; the amici's invitation does not become a Government argument. No associate adopts §3501 as an alternative affirmance ground or resolves its military applicability, interpretation or constitutionality.

**Exact direct common ground:** Each of the eight associates directly accepts that the credited first remark presented genuine uncertainty rather than an already clear invocation that later answers could undo; that officers may pause offense questioning and use the neutral, limited clarification used here to ascertain the suspect's choice; and that, after this valid initial waiver and this clarification disclaiming a request, admission of the resulting statements supplies no ground to reverse the military judgment. All eight also accept the final clear request's cessation consequence. A severable actual-clarification/application section can receive those direct joins without a Marks inference. None of these propositions answers whether officers who instead continued offense questioning through an ambiguous counsel reference would violate a clarification duty. The mandatory-clarification four retain that affirmative duty; the clear-request four retain their refusal to impose it.
