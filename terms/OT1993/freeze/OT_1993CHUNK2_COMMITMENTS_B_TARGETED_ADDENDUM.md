## 19. Northwest Airlines — January24,1994

### The omitted statutory path and its consequences

The original freeze proceeded from §1513(b)'s reference to reasonable airport charges to an Evansville statutory inquiry without first testing whether these charges fall within the prohibition in §1513(a). That was an incomplete comparison. Aloha Airlines requires attention to the actual basis of the levy, not its name, the identity of the payer alone or ultimate economic incidence on passengers. Its holding invalidates a supposed property tax actually measured by airline gross receipts; footnote6 describes(a)'s limited enumeration and(b)'s clarification that other taxes remain available. The decision did not decide whether the separate fee language in(b) supplies a comprehensive user-fee standard. Stevens and O'Connor joined Aloha; it predates the appointments of Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg and is binding authority for them, not their personal prior writing.

The newly specified calculation bases matter: landing charges use aircraft weight, terminal rent uses occupied square footage, and the carrying charge uses depreciation of capital assets. None uses passenger count or air-transportation gross receipts as its basis. A head charge cannot escape(a) by being called a landing fee. Equally, a charge on use of airport property does not become a passenger charge merely because an airline may recover the cost through fares.

**Path N-R: reasonable airport charges are the particular statutory permission.** Section(b) separately addresses taxes other than those enumerated in(a), then reasonable rental, landing and other airport-service fees. Its specific reference to aircraft-operator fees and the adjective reasonable support applying that condition to these facility charges. Unlike ordinary property or net-income taxes, these fees pay for the very airport services enabling air carriage. Aloha's treatment of other taxes does not erase this additional fee language. This reading need not treat every passed-through business expense as an indirect passenger charge or assert that Congress automatically codified every aspect of Evansville when it forbade the head charges Evansville had permitted. Evansville instead supplies a fitting established measure of the express reasonableness condition, absent a contrary applicable agency rule. Under that measure the existing break-even findings, allocation record and lack of established interstate discrimination support the original affirmance, with both surviving corrections intact.

**Path N-C: coverage precedes a limited saving clause.** Section(a) is the operative prohibition; the negative saving language in(b) does not independently prohibit everything it does not expressly save. Aircraft-weight fees, square-foot rents and capital-cost charges do not use the enumerated prohibited bases. The reasonable-fee reference assures the continued availability of ordinary airport charges against an overbroad reading of(a); it neither authorizes unreasonable fees against other law nor establishes an independent federal regulatory code for every charge outside(a). Reasonable thus remains meaningful as the class Congress affirmatively preserved; omission from that safe class does not itself supply a prohibition absent(a) coverage. This reading gives Aloha's limited-preemption account greater structural force without treating its tax footnote as an express holding about all airport fees. It rejects the airlines' AHTA claim on statutory scope and leaves constitutional constraints intact.

**The constitutional fork is separate.** The lower courts had treated the aviation enactments as displacing the constitutional claim. Those statutes do not grant blanket immunity from the Commerce Clause. Deciding the constitutional claim now can be justified because the airlines tried the fee facts under the same Evansville measure they proposed, and the existing record does not show excess or interstate discrimination. Remand is also a substantial path: dismissal of the independent constitutional claim before trial may have constrained evidence and presentation; an absence of proof under a dismissed theory is not automatically a full opportunity to prove it. The choice must be made under each Justice's own constitutional standard. Actual Itel is relevant to that standard and to identifying the basis of a levy, but it does not decide §1513(b)'s scope.

## 24. Caspari v. Bohlen — February23,1994

### Presentation must be decided before nonretroactivity

The original neutral packet labeled its reconstructed Question Presented as asking whether Teague permits the requested relief. The targeted source clarification instead identifies a stated question about double jeopardy in repeated noncapital sentencing, with Teague discussed in the petition's body. This is a material correction to the presentation premise, not a historical outcome. The first freeze's reliance on consistent assertion and an antecedent relation therefore did not finish Rule14.1(a)'s separate fair-inclusion inquiry.

**Path C-I: fairly included subsidiary limitation.** The habeas judgment under review grants relief by applying the asserted double-jeopardy rule. Whether that rule may be applied to this final conviction can be treated as a subsidiary limit on the very relief challenged, rather than a separate claim or separately reviewable order. It concerns the legal availability of the same writ. Actual Izumi, read with actual Yee, distinguishes subsidiary arguments from related independent questions; it does not say every distinct legal test within a claim needs a separate question. Consistent assertion supplies notice and avoids forfeiture, but it is not what makes the issue fairly included under this path. The necessary classification is that this particular nonretroactivity objection regulates the availability of the relief on the presented habeas claim. No exceptional-circumstances bypass is needed if that classification is accepted.

**Path C-X: a related independent question outside the grant.** Whether successive offender-status litigation violates double jeopardy and whether a constitutional rule was dictated at January2,1986 finality are different inquiries. One can answer either without answering the other, and Teague's exceptions introduce further independent questions. The State seeks reversal on this nonjurisdictional ground despite omitting it from the stated question. Actual Izumi makes disclosure in the body, complete briefing and an antecedent relationship insufficient by themselves to overcome the presumption. Its separate intervention order is a factual distinction, but not a universal condition for the presentation rule, as actual Yee's two taking theories confirm. No exceptional urgency or economy particular to this case has been established beyond the considerations Izumi held inadequate. This path declines the defense as presented and requires a merits disposition of the double-jeopardy question.

Actual Schiro is not dispositive of this fork. It permits declining a defense first introduced at merits briefing after omissions below and in the opposition to certiorari. Missouri's consistent assertion here defeats that particular lateness analogy, but does not establish Rule14 fair inclusion. Teague remains nonjurisdictional; finality concerns do not make it compulsory irrespective of the grant.

### The downstream merits and the separate1986 inquiry

The Eighth Circuit's complete opinion adds concrete detail to the trial-like-status argument: §558.021 required allegations of the essential prior-conviction facts, evidence proving them beyond reasonable doubt, and corresponding judicial findings; the defendant had confrontation, cross-examination and evidence-presentation rights. The initial proceeding resulted in persistent-offender sentencing despite a total failure of prior-conviction proof. The state appellate court invited supplementation and received none. This was evidentiary insufficiency, not a reversal because sufficient evidence was erroneously admitted or excluded. The State's complete failure to offer its required proof does not itself show that the statutory adjudication could never carry jeopardy consequences.

The lower opinion used broad enhancement language and an August1985 finality date. Those propositions are not adopted here: the correct finality date is January2,1986, and for these class-A robberies the status changed the sentencer, **not** the authorized punishment range. The two fifteen-year sentencing results cannot establish that the second procedure was harmless if a protected acquittal interest had already attached.

**Merits protection path.** Bullington, written by Blackmun and joined by Stevens, ties its capital exception to a trial-like proceeding in which the State must prove the factual basis for a distinct sentencing entitlement. Rumsey, written by O'Connor and joined by Blackmun and Stevens, shows that a judge's adjudication and legal error do not undo an actual acquittal's finality. Coupled with the insufficiency rule applied in Bullington from Burks, those reasons support protection for this binary offender-status adjudication. The State's entitlement to remove the jury's ordinary limiting role depends on formally pleaded facts proved beyond reasonable doubt. The accused's interest in finality of that adjudication can matter even though the maximum class-A sentence stays the same. On this path the state appellate insufficiency reversal bars a second opportunity to establish that status. It does not acquit Bohlen of the robberies, prohibit ordinary lawful resentencing without that renewed status adjudication, or establish a constitutional right to jury sentencing independent of the state's chosen procedure.

**New-extension path.** Bullington and Rumsey adjudicated entitlement to death within capital systems, rather than a decisionmaker designation inside an unchanged noncapital range. Their safeguards and binary choices supply an analogy, not necessarily a dictated extension. DiFrancesco's rejection of automatic acquittal treatment for ordinary sentencing leaves room for that distinction. A court in January1986 could reasonably distinguish this proceeding by both its noncapital setting and its sentencing function; mere lower-court disagreement is not sufficient by itself. Actual Graham requires a real inquiry whether earlier principles dictated an application; actual Gilmore permits finding a new rule where a capital rule does not determine a materially different noncapital procedure. Neither actual decision lets a court use the words death is different as a substitute for comparing the proceedings.

If the new-extension path governs after Teague is reached, neither1994 exception permits relief: this claim does not remove robbery conduct, a category of persons or this authorized noncapital punishment from the State's power, and protecting finality of a prior-conviction adjudication is not a new bedrock procedure essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness of the criminal determination. Both exceptions remain valid for appropriate cases. Actual Dixon retains Grady but does not eliminate the threshold question whether this sentencing-status proceeding constitutes jeopardy in the relevant sense. Actual Schiro is current authority protecting actual capital acquittals; it is not1986 precedent and supplies no categorical noncapital answer.
