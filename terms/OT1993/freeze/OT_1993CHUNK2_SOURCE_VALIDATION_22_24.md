## Department of Defense v. FLRA, 510 U.S. 487

[Official report](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep510/usrep510487/usrep510487.pdf). No. 92-1223, argued November 8, 1993; decided February 23, 1994. Fifth Circuit, 975 F.2d 1105, reversed, 9–0 in judgment. Thomas wrote for Rehnquist, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; Souter also concurred, restricting broad language to the FOIA exception rather than all Privacy Act exceptions. Ginsburg concurred in the judgment only. Her statutory-stare-decisis rationale accepted the result under Reporters Committee while criticizing the core-purpose limitation as not compelled by FOIA's text; she contrasted the congressionally declared bargaining interest and preserved the possibility of legislative correction. She did not challenge Reporters Committee's rap-sheet result. No merits dissent and no nonparticipant.

Scope: necessity and availability of addresses were not contested; only the FOIA exception was invoked. Routine use, consent, and disclosure of other employment records remain distinct. Exemption 6 does not itself command nondisclosure; the Privacy Act supplies the prohibition where FOIA does not require release and no other applicable route is established. No statutory superiority for every labor request was announced. References to Connecticut National Bank v. Germain and Ray in the historical reasoning must be tested against their actual prior simulated versions before use.
