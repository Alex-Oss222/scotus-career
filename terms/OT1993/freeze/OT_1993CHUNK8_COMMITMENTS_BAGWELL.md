## Entering law and its limits

The controlling register selection is **Successive prosecutions, evidentiary overlap and criminal contempt**, present operation effective June 28, 1993 through Dixon; Felix's evidence/conspiracy components remain effective March 25, 1992. The actual Dixon public render, supplied verbatim in the authorized prior-public reading copy, establishes that completed nonsummary punitive contempt receives criminal-jeopardy protection despite private initiation or a protective purpose. Its eight-Justice coalition includes Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; Blackmun did not join that proposition. His separate judicial-order/community-offense distinction and his join in retaining Grady must not be conflated. The register preserves coercive civil contempt separately and does not classify this schedule or determine this jury entitlement. No historical post-divergence Dixon rule enters.

Hicks v. Feiock, 485 U.S. 624, 630–641 (1988), makes constitutional classification a federal inquiry into the substantive proceeding and relief. Labels and desired deterrence do not settle it. Fixed punishment and a genuinely conditional sanction differ; a court-payable fine can be civil when compliance with the ordered act supplies an actual means of avoiding the sanction. Suspension alone does not establish civil character. Its majority, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, required a remand to establish the actual effect of satisfying the support arrears. O'Connor's dissent, joined by Scalia, also used objective characteristics, recognized the criminal character of a fixed fine payable to the court, and defended the particular continuing private-support arrangement and its compliance conditions. Its view does not make all advance warning of punishment civil coercion.

Shillitani v. United States, 384 U.S. 364, 368–372 (1966), preserves conditional civil confinement with an unqualified release right upon testimony and holds that confinement cannot continue after the grand jury's end removes the ability to comply. It requires ordinary civil due process and does not grant unlimited coercive authority. Its future-compliance mechanism differs from liability for these completed violations. Hicks's treatment of Gompers, Penfield and Mine Workers preserves coercive and compensatory remedies without requiring every effective sanction to be criminal. The standalone Gompers and Mine Workers reports were not independently read in this context; no new proposition is attributed to them beyond the neutral handoff and Hicks's verified treatment.

Bloom v. Illinois, 391 U.S. 194, 201–211 (1968), requires a jury for serious criminal contempt, preserves the petty-offense qualification, and rejects efficiency or vindication of judicial authority as sufficient reasons to omit the jury. The criminal safeguards include notice of the charge, a fair opportunity to defend, and proof beyond a reasonable doubt; giving one does not dispense with another. Bloom does not supply a universal dollar boundary for union fines. No such boundary is needed to recognize the seriousness of these multimillion-dollar assessments. No finding of actual judicial bias is necessary, and the recusal allegation rejected below is not newly adjudicated.

The actual Kurth Ranch public projection, June 6, 1994, supplies an additional substance-over-label rule for its unusual second drug exaction, adopted by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg among the present non-Stone Justices. It expressly preserves separate contempt, Excessive Fines and civil/criminal inquiries. O'Connor's conditional Halper-accounting position, and Scalia and Thomas's rejection of an independent multiple-punishments theory for a proceeding they regarded as civil, do not answer whether the present enforcement proceedings were criminal. Bagwell involves no calculation of a tax-to-cost ratio and no adjudicated successive-prosecution question.

Weiss, January 19, 1994, is used only as Justice-specific evidence concerning structural safeguards. Kennedy, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg joined the military-context analysis preserving independent constitutional review; Scalia and Thomas relied on the historical military practice. ## Supported paths and proposed common propositions

**Criminal-classification path:** The surviving fixed public assessments punish completed out-of-court violations, cannot be purged by later compliance and are not measured compensation. Prospective announcement establishes notice and an incentive to obey but does not by itself transform that punishment into civil relief. Their extraordinary scale and the complex continuing injunction, enforced through extensive factfinding about dispersed past conduct, reinforce the need for criminal safeguards. No summary courtroom emergency supplies a competing justification. The fines therefore cannot stand after proceedings that denied a jury.

**Strongest competing civil path:** A specified prospective schedule can direct conduct more predictably and effectively than a sanction first invented after disobedience. The Union could control its exposure by compliance; the proceedings furnished notice, extensive adversarial opportunity and the criminal proof burden. Genuine civil coercion also has deterrent effects, and a rule treating all fixed monetary sums as criminal could disable useful enforcement of injunctions. This is a lawful competing classification argument, particularly in light of the private-enforcement emphasis of O'Connor's Hicks dissent.

**Resolution and necessary boundary:** That defense does not explain how these existing public judgments can coerce a performance that will eliminate or appropriately condition the liability. The previously announced price of a completed violation remains punishment when assessed; otherwise ordinary advance criminal prohibitions would satisfy the same purported civil criterion. The residual civil category remains real: compensation for proved loss and genuinely conditional coercion to secure an ordered act may proceed under civil rules with applicable due process and a real ability to comply. A compliance deadline or a coercive schedule is not made criminal merely because a sum is specified in advance. Conversely, a preannounced amount is not enough to validate this form of completed-violation liability. The present decision need not resolve every recurring per-day fine, every discovery sanction or every simple injunction. Complexity and magnitude are important features of this record, not a mechanical factor list or universal independent trigger. A categorical affirmative-command/prohibitory-command distinction is neither needed nor adopted.

**Jury/remedy alternative:** It could be argued that the judge's use of proof beyond a reasonable doubt and extensive hearings supplied all material criminal protection, making remand unnecessary. That argument fails under Bloom once the fines are classified as serious criminal contempt: the jury independently limits official adjudication of serious punitive liability. The model does not invent jury waiver, harmlessness findings or consent. Nor does it convert criminal classification into immunity from lawful enforcement. The reviewed judgment must be reversed insofar as it sustains these public fines, and the case remanded for proceedings consistent with their criminal character and jury entitlement. No order to prosecute or retry automatically follows, and no double-jeopardy or future-prosecution defense is adjudicated in advance.

The propositions all eight non-Stone Justices support at the same level of generality are:

1. For these substantial fixed noncompensatory public fines imposed for completed out-of-court violations without a later compliance-based means of avoiding the assessed liability, advance announcement alone does not make the contempt civil. They are criminal sanctions. The ongoing complex decree and retrospective adjudications reinforce that conclusion without creating a universal complexity test.
2. Serious criminal contempt requires the applicable criminal safeguards, including a jury; proof beyond a reasonable doubt before the judge is not a substitute for the jury. These multimillion-dollar fines cannot stand after the nonjury proceedings. No precise petty-fine ceiling is fixed.
3. Reverse the Virginia Supreme Court judgment to the extent it sustains the surviving public fines and remand for proceedings consistent with those conclusions. The initial vacated fines and vacated Company assessments are not reinstated. Genuine compensatory and conditional coercive civil remedies remain available in an appropriate case; no such substitute award is made here.
