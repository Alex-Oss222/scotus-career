Remaining valid aggravators do not alone cure a constitutional weighing error, although an invalid weighing factor does not automatically negate death eligibility if valid eligibility findings remain. The state judicial system must provide genuine reweighing without the invalid factor where state law authorizes it, or constitutional harmless-error review proving beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not affect the sentence. Bare factor counting, proportionality review or a harmless-error label supplies neither cure; no particular verbal formula is indispensable if the substantive finding is actually made. A jury's inadequately narrowed aggravating instruction can infect the final death sentence when the judge must give the resulting recommendation great weight, even if the judge directly finds only valid aggravators. Correction must address that transmitted influence. If no lawful cure is available, a new sentencing proceeding is required. Neither automatic life imprisonment nor disturbance of guilt follows.
