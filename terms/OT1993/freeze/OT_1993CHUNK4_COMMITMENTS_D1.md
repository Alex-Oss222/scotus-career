## Common legal and remedial boundaries

Section 3001(i) applies only to a resource-recovery facility recovering energy from mass burning of municipal solid waste which receives and burns only household waste from the specified residential sources and commercial or industrial solid waste containing no hazardous waste identified or listed under §3001; does not accept such identified or listed hazardous wastes; and whose owner or operator has established contractual requirements or other appropriate notification or inspection procedures to assure that hazardous wastes are not received or burned. Those are distinct requirements, and the permitted kinds of safeguard are alternatives. EDF's stipulated noncontest of Chicago's input-control procedures supports the procedural posture without turning the statutory commands into factual findings for other facilities.

Two viable paths remain. **Path A** treats §3001(i) as a limited facility exclusion that does not exempt generated ash from Subtitle C when the ash actually meets hazardous-waste criteria. It affirms the Seventh Circuit's judgment and allows further coverage and remedial proceedings. **Path B** reads the provision in continuity with the household-waste exclusion to protect the residue of the qualifying waste stream; it reverses the Seventh Circuit and restores the exemption-based judgment for Chicago within this claim's scope. Its minimum form can accept EPA's reasonable construction of genuine ambiguity without declaring every contrary interpretation impossible.

Every Justice rejects a rule that agency inconsistency alone eliminates Chevron deference. The current John Hancock holding forbids attributing that rule to the Court. An agency remains bounded by the enacted text. The dispute is whether omission of generation and the facility/waste distinction resolve this text or leave a range that includes EPA's reading. Legislative history, a statute's purpose, and policy choice have distinct roles; a committee report cannot enact missing language, while an actual ambiguity can leave room for administrative choice.

Neither path fixes a civil penalty, awards damages, prescribes a cleanup allocation, establishes that all municipal ash is hazardous, or decides every regulation governing an exclusively household-waste operation. Path A preserves generator obligations and downstream handling consequences required by applicable law without deciding every facility permit issue or location at which ash first becomes subject to a particular regulatory requirement. Path B is confined to facilities satisfying all §3001(i) conditions; it supplies no exemption for accepting regulated hazardous industrial inputs and no municipal immunity from independent laws.

## Blackmun

**Frozen result:** reverse under Path B; the qualifying facility's ash falls within the exclusion as reasonably construed by EPA, and the exemption-based judgment for Chicago should be restored on this claim.

**Own authority and ground:** Blackmun authored Pauley, 501 U.S. 680, which calls for deference where a technical statute entrusts a range of policy choices to an agency and cautions against allowing a mechanical negative inference to defeat the scheme's contextual meaning. He joined Stevens's Chevron opinion, including its recognition that an agency may reconsider its interpretation. The household-waste regulation's protection of treatment residues, the amendment's focus on qualifying resource-recovery facilities, and the inclusion of nonhazardous commercial material make waste-stream continuity a permissible reading. The Senate report's express reference to generation confirms the available reading without serving as an independent enactment.

**Strongest opposite path:** the statute distinguishes generation from management, and his actual John Hancock join rejects erasing a qualified statutory limitation. A facility cannot necessarily pass its protected status to a chemically different hazardous output. His chosen distinction is that John Hancock contained a component restriction the agency eliminated, whereas §3001(i)'s application to ordinary residue remains uncertain when its introductory facility language and regulatory background are read together. This is a substantive ambiguity conclusion, not deference based solely on environmental complexity.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** no join in an unconditional ash exemption or in a general rule that technical issues are always for agencies. He can rest on permissible agency interpretation without requiring a holding that the statute unambiguously demands EPA's answer. If the controlling text were revised to make generation expressly excluded from protection, the deference premise would fail; that is the concrete barrier to joining Path A's reasoning as it stands. The remedy concerns the asserted Subtitle C exemption, not a declaration that ash is chemically harmless.

## Stevens

**Frozen result:** reverse under Path B and restore Chicago's exemption-based judgment on the presented claim.

**Own authority and ground:** his Chevron opinion, 467 U.S. 837, distinguishes a congressional command from an agency's permissible reconciliation of competing statutory policies. It rejects freezing an agency's first view merely because the agency later changes it. He joined Pauley and the current John Hancock agency analysis. Read in context, §3001(i)'s protection for a resource-recovery facility handling the specified waste streams plausibly includes the inevitable residue of that recovery. Its clarification of the household-waste scheme and the contemporaneous Senate report favor continuity over a reading that protects receipt and combustion while changing the status of the residue after addition of only nonhazardous material.

**Strongest opposite path:** separate definitions and omission of generation can express a deliberate limited exemption; John Hancock requires respecting exactly that kind of statutory boundary when present. He rejects treating the omission as decisive here because the introductory facility language, the catchall management phrase, and the existing exclusion supply a broader contextual meaning. At minimum, the statutory scheme leaves room for EPA's construction; environmental risk is a policy reason to amend or administer the scheme within lawful bounds, not a warrant to foreclose a reasonable reading.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** cannot join a rationale that dismisses the statutory and regulatory history as irrelevant once one word is omitted, or treats changed agency views as categorically disqualified. A narrower concurrence could accept only that EPA's present construction falls within a permissible range, reserving whether it is the single best interpretation. No protection is supplied to an incinerator that fails any required input safeguard; no existing state-law or independent federal obligation is set aside.

## O'Connor

**Frozen result:** affirm under Path A; §3001(i) does not remove generated hazardous ash from Subtitle C merely because its producer meets the facility conditions.

**Own authority and ground:** her actual John Hancock dissent join accepts a bounded statutory exclusion and rejects an agency's categorical enlargement even while disagreeing about the proper contractual application. Her Pauley join is strong evidence for giving an agency real interpretive room when Congress delegates it. Here, however, the defined generation category and its absence from §3001(i), coupled with language about how the facility is deemed to manage waste rather than what waste is hazardous, provide a concrete statutory boundary. Enforcing it leaves the input-related facility protection operative.

**Strongest opposite path:** the 1980 regulation, clarification history, and EPA's expertise make the waste-stream reading plausible. Pauley counsels against treating every omission as conclusive. The response is statutory structure: the claimed exclusion would reach a separately regulated activity and the hazardous status of a new substance, although Congress stated an exclusion for specified facility activities. That is a boundary question before administrative policy balancing.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** no join in the lower court's categorical rejection of deference for changing views, and no claim that she joined Chevron in 1984—she did not participate in its decision. Minimum join is the generator/ash proposition as applied to this mixed-input operation, with actual hazardous character and remedies left open. She need not endorse a general rejection of committee reports or decide all possible on-site treatment and storage obligations.

## Scalia

**Frozen result:** affirm under Path A.

**Own authority and ground:** his Pauley dissent explains that technical complexity is not itself ambiguity and that careful reading may identify a single permissible meaning. His actual John Hancock join rejects an agency's conversion of a qualified exclusion into a categorical one. RCRA separately identifies generation, and §3001(i) omits it while listing management activities. The operative sentence concerns a facility's deemed activities, not a reclassification of the material it generates. A committee report using the omitted term cannot supply it to the enactment.

**Strongest opposite path:** EPA's interpretation may be reasonable if the facility language and regulatory background leave the output question unresolved; Chevron does not require the agency's view to be the court's preferred one. His answer is that the text and distinct statutory categories resolve that question before deference arises. The full safeguard conditions explain when the limited facility protection applies; they do not make every resulting material nonhazardous.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** no refusal to consider an agency merely because it changed position; the textual-range ground is sufficient. He can join the bounded ash-generation holding without policy speculation, a general attack on EPA, or an assertion that all ash is hazardous. No procedural invalidity of the memorandum is necessary. The statutory omission matters because of this structure, not as an absolute rule that any unlisted word defeats every agency construction.

## Kennedy

**Frozen result:** affirm under Path A.

**Own authority and ground:** his Bowen opinion, 488 U.S. 204, identifies statutory authorization as the limit of administrative action; it does not supply a rule against all changed interpretations. His actual John Hancock dissent join similarly rejects a categorical agency exemption while preserving a different, narrower statutory construction. Section 3001(i) protects listed activities at a qualifying facility. Removing hazardous ash generation from Subtitle C would add a distinct exemption not expressed in that limitation.

**Strongest opposite path:** the resource-recovery policy and regulatory history can support viewing the entire operation as protected; within genuine ambiguity Chevron would require respect for EPA's accommodation. He finds that the separate statutory generation category and facility-specific form keep that reading outside the permissible range. He did not participate in Pauley, and no Pauley position is attributed to him.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** a minimum join preserves Chevron and the actual John Hancock rule, rejects only the asserted expansion, and remits actual coverage and remedy. No holding that the memorandum is invalid because it is retrospective, that all administrative memoranda lack weight, or that regulation of municipal waste is categorically reserved to a particular sovereign. The City's qualifying status remains relevant to the protection the statute actually grants.

## Souter

**Frozen result:** affirm under Path A on the bounded statutory distinction.

**Own authority and ground:** he joined Blackmun's Pauley opinion, which requires attention to technical context and agency reasonableness, and actually joined Ginsburg's John Hancock opinion, which preserves the limiting words of a statutory exclusion. The latter supplies the closest present public boundary: administrative accommodation cannot remove an independently regulated category from the scheme by extending a facility exclusion to every output. Reading generation separately gives effect to the statute's distinct classifications without denying its protection to qualifying facility operations.

**Strongest opposite path:** Pauley and Chevron support a genuine ambiguity analysis rather than mechanical word counting; the household-residue rule and the Senate report materially support Chicago. His answer is that those sources explain the protection of the incoming qualifying stream but do not establish Congress's elimination of generator responsibilities for new hazardous residue. The categorical waste-stream reading requires more than choosing between permissible methods of implementing an open-ended standard.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** reject the lower court's complete denial of agency deference based on inconsistency. Minimum join leaves the degree of weight owed agency memoranda generally undecided, reaches this mixed-input facility's assertion only, and preserves ordinary factfinding on hazardous characteristics. No policy view about the desirable cost of incineration supplies his vote.

## Thomas

**Frozen result:** affirm under Path A.

**Own authority and ground:** his actual John Hancock dissent insists that the statutory words defining an exclusion control its reach, rejects an agency's blanket exemption, and seeks a remand rather than fabricating the missing application premise. Here the enacted exclusion names the facility and specified management activities; generation is separately defined and omitted. An ash exemption would convert limited facility protection into a different statutory immunity.

**Strongest opposite path:** the amendment's clarifying purpose and regulatory background support reading the listed activities as part of a single protected operation. Yet the statute's safeguards do not say that all output is excluded, and using the Senate report's generation language as a replacement would disregard the enacted boundary. Chevron controls when a lawful range exists; it does not create that range solely because the subject is technical. No personal join in pre-appointment Chevron or Pauley is invented.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** he can join the textual ash-generation holding and affirmance while declining broader environmental-policy reasoning, an all-purpose rule against agency changes, or adjudication of violations not reached below. His own John Hancock insistence on careful remedy supports leaving proof and appropriate enforcement consequences to further proceedings rather than directing a final liability judgment.

## Ginsburg

**Frozen result:** affirm under Path A.

**Own authority and ground:** her actual John Hancock opinion supplies a concrete, current statutory method: identify the object and extent of a qualified exclusion, preserve its limits, and decline an agency interpretation that makes those limits inoperative. Section 3001(i) protects a qualifying facility from being deemed to perform the listed hazardous-waste-management activities. It does not declare its generated ash excluded regardless of the ash's characteristics, and it omits the statute's distinct generation category. The statutory boundary, rather than distrust of EPA's changes, defeats the requested extension.

**Strongest opposite path:** household-waste regulatory continuity and the Senate report provide the City's best account of Congress's intended protection. The issue is whether the facility's output is already within that protection, not whether energy recovery is desirable. Her chosen reading permits the enacted facility protection to operate without changing the status of a newly generated hazardous material. It thus follows her component-specific John Hancock approach, while acknowledging that an agency would receive respect if Congress had left the relevant coverage question genuinely open.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** no categorical withdrawal of deference from a changed interpretation or a litigation position; her own current writing expressly leaves broader agency-weight questions unresolved. Minimum join is the mixed-input ash proposition, with every statutory safeguard stated and actual hazardous coverage and remedy left for the lower court. No claim about an unlocated personal ash or RCRA opinion is made.

## Remedial and handoff receipt

The Path A commitments affirm the appellate judgment rejecting Chicago's categorical ash exemption and allow the citizen action to continue subject to actual Subtitle C coverage, lawful proof, and appropriate relief. They do not direct liability or a penalty. The Path B commitments reverse that appellate judgment and restore the exemption-based judgment for Chicago within the presented claim, subject to the established qualifying-facility posture; they do not validate handling under independent laws.
