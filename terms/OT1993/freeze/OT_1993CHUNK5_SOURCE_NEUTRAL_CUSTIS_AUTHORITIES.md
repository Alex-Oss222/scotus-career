## Lewis: attribution and substantive limit

The complete official report, **Lewis v. United States, 445 U.S. 55–73 (1980)**, has been downloaded and read (19 PDF pages): https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep445/usrep445055/usrep445055.pdf . **Blackmun wrote the Court's opinion; Stevens joined it. There is no Stevens dissent.** Brennan dissented with Marshall and Powell (report p.56).

Blackmun and Stevens therefore personally supported a distinction between an outstanding conviction as the statutory trigger for a civil firearms disability enforced by criminal sanction and use of an unreliable conviction to support guilt or enhance punishment. The former statute's unqualified reference to conviction, its specified relief mechanisms, and availability of a prior challenge supported requiring the person to clear his status before obtaining a firearm (pp.60–65, 67–68). The opinion accepted Burgett, Tucker, and Loper and explained that their subsequent guilt or punishment depended on reliability of the uncounseled conviction, whereas this firearms disability turned on the fact of an extant conviction (pp.59–60, 66–67). It rejected continued disability after the predicate had actually been vacated (p.61 n.5). It did not decide ACCA sentencing or hold every collateral invalidity claim available in every forum. Its distinction supplies evidence on both sides of the present sentencing/channel question and must not be reduced to a general authorization to punish on invalid predicates.

## Public simulated Parke: seven current Justices' personal positions

The public **Parke v. Raley**, December 1, 1992, entry in `terms/OT1992/output/OT_1992CHUNK1.md` was read, including its holdings, separate position, limits, and source notes. It controls over historical Parke wherever the two differ.

**O'Connor's Court opinion was joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas** (also Stone and White). Each supported Kentucky's rebuttable presumption of regularity and initial production burden in a collateral challenge to final counseled guilty-plea convictions. Missing transcripts alone, without misconduct, did not establish constitutional invalidity. On actual rebuttal Kentucky still had to make its affirmative showing. They rejected the Sixth Circuit's compulsory clear-and-convincing quantum and distinguished direct-review Boykin. Their position therefore supports respect for finality, procedural allocation, and the difference between an unsupported attack and an actually invalid predicate. **It did not decide whether other systems must permit collateral attacks**; the question had not been raised below or in the certiorari petition. It did not authorize reliance on actually invalid or uncounseled convictions.

**Blackmun concurred in the judgment only.** He accepted that burden allocation because enhancement after conviction concerned sentencing, not proof of a separate offense. A proved final judgment could carry a rebuttable presumption, with opportunity to show irregularity and the Commonwealth's responsive affirmative proof. He reserved a separate repeat-offender crime, for which he would not let a silent record and evidentiary shift replace constitutionally required affirmative proof. He expressly preserved the distinction between finality and an actually involuntary plea or invalid uncounseled conviction. This is his actual public simulated position, not a join in the Court's whole framework.

## Current-term channel and statutory-text writings

The public projection of **Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich**, January 19, 1994, in `terms/OT1993/entering-law/OT_1993CHUNK5_NEUTRAL_CURRENT_LAW.md`, provides verified individual channel positions for all eight current non-Stone Justices. **Blackmun wrote the statutory-channel opinion; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg joined it.** They respected an implied allocation fairly discernible from the particular statute's text, structure, and purpose where the connected claim could receive meaningful review in the specified route. This is an administrative review scheme, so its premise of an actual, meaningful alternative route must be examined before analogy to ACCA; it is not a categorical rule that statutory silence excludes review.

**Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg also joined Blackmun's record-specific meaningful-review ground**, preserving practical access and reserving a scheme whose penalties or irreversible injury foreclosed review. **Scalia, joined by Thomas**, separately retained §1331 jurisdiction to decide whether exclusion of original review itself was unconstitutional, rejected legislative-history reliance, and distinguished ordinary compliance costs from practical destruction of access. Their partial join does not supply votes for the Court's distinct constitutional explanation.

The slice's **Beecham v. United States**, May 16, 1994, supplies the current construction of §921(a)(20): conviction-jurisdiction law governs the specified restoration inquiry. Its restoration rule should be used as current law, without converting it into an answer about the distinct availability of a collateral sentencing challenge.
