#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain affirmance against the presented challenges, while expressly preserving meaningful selection-stage constraints.

Stone's capital-sentencing commitments require both lawful eligibility narrowing and a genuine opportunity to give mitigating evidence effect. They do not require that every consideration at selection become a numerically weighted factual element. Woodson, Lockett, Eddings, Zant, and Lowenfield permit broad but intelligible consideration of the individual crime and offender after eligibility has been established. That reconciles this outcome with his protective positions in simulated Graham and Johnson.

“Circumstances of the crime” identifies relevant subject matter rather than a pejorative label such as an undefined heinousness aggravator doing eligibility work. Godfrey and Maynard therefore are important cautions, but not automatic commands to invalidate this instruction. The strongest objection is that broad selection factors allow almost any fact to favor death, making narrowing ineffective. Stone should answer through the actual distinction between intelligible discretion, forbidden criteria, and meaningful mitigation, not by saying eligibility ends constitutional supervision. Supported evidence, correct instructions, and review of actual impermissible use still matter.

For Tuilaepa's additional challenges, violent criminal activity is an understandable factual subject whose occurrence still must be proved under applicable law. Age is ascertainable but has contextual significance; no universal numerical weight or direction is required. Absence of youth cannot automatically become aggravation, and the possibility of an improper prosecutorial characterization does not establish a facial defect or a proved as-applied violation. A prosecutor's argument that every possible age independently warrants death would require examination on its own preserved record rather than approval through this broad holding.

Keep Proctor's different instructions and narrower challenge separate. The supplied record does not establish an independently preserved mitigation exclusion, racial consideration, or invalid eligibility scheme, and Stone should not invent one to implement a general capital-reliability concern. Affirm these judgments against these claims, with no advisory endorsement of the entire statute. Any adopted Romano or Simmons rule would regulate different distortions of selection, and would not require every selection factor to narrow eligibility again. The rule preserves discretion to decline death without abandoning judicial control over unconstitutional reasons for imposing it.

**Egalitarian application:** Individualized sentencing permits consideration of the person and offense without predetermined numerical weights. It does not permit forbidden status judgments, exclusion of mitigation, or treating every possible age as an independent reason for death. Preserve the State's lawful sentencing authority and the individual's protection against arbitrary selection through the actual instructions, evidence, and preserved claims.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Tuilaepa was convicted of a 1986 murder during a bar robbery, with the robbery special circumstance found true. Proctor was convicted of a 1982 murder with rape, burglary, and torture special circumstances. After a penalty mistrial, Proctor received a new penalty jury in another county. Both sentencing juries received §190.3 factors; the instructions differed in that Tuilaepa’s were limited to factors considered relevant and Proctor’s included the full list. The State’s eligibility narrowing under §190.2 is not itself challenged in these questions. [R55](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/967/)

Keep the two judgments and the different challenges distinct. The present objections concern selection after eligibility, not whether every homicide qualifies for death or whether race affected either particular verdict. A factor’s relevance and intelligible subject matter are different from a mandatory factual element or an assigned numerical weight. Allegations about how prosecutors might characterize any age do not alone establish a preserved, proved as-applied constitutional violation in either case. [R55](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/967/)
