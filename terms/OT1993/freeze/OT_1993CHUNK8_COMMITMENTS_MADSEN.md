## Components and common distinctions

**Scope.** Rule 14.1(a), as construed in the actual Izumi decision, creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding independent questions absent from the petition; neither merits briefing nor a related issue’s usefulness alone overcomes it. The exact first question challenges the 36-foot zone as content based, which is distinct from geographic tailoring. Seven associates read the third question’s broader challenge to restraints on peaceful expression within designated zones as fairly including that subsidiary justification inquiry; Stevens confines it to party/association coverage and declines the distinct tailoring question. Neither reading invents a new grant. Both 300-foot components are expressly presented, although the second question’s shorthand does not accurately state the two provisions’ different terms.

Paragraph (4), noise and images, is not named in those questions. Seven associates exercise an express, bounded prudential exception; Stevens declines. The exceptional combination is a continuing categorical restraint on observable expression at the very clinic whose access-zone remedy is properly before the Court, medical findings already supplying the competing justification, and separable correction of the written rule without an additional fact-dependent adverse order or new findings. The source-only supplement authenticates that petitioners argued noise/images breadth and vagueness and respondents expressly defended both through tailoring, alternatives and overbreadth, with a general vagueness defense. That limited notice-and-presentation fact supports the exception; it is not a finding that every possible subissue was fully argued, a grant, a preservation ruling, or a sufficient reason by itself. The seven do not invoke an automatic First Amendment exception. Stevens accepts exceptional-review power but would decide only the selected questions: these independent provisions are unnecessary to their resolution, unlike the intervention issue his Izumi dissent would have reached. Paragraphs (7)–(9) receive the separate narrower scope treatment below.

**Standard.** Four associates accept the injunction as content neutral as construed and require that each challenged term burden no more protected speech than necessary to serve the significant remedial interests proved here. This is a more exacting fit inquiry than ordinary Ward review of generally applicable regulations, grounded in Carroll's precision requirement and the specific wrong/remedy connection in Claiborne; it does not import every licensing safeguard or make every injunction content based. Stevens retains ordinary time/place/manner review with close attention to this adjudicated wrong and available channels. Scalia and Thomas apply strict review to this direct, selective restraint on future protected advocacy; Kennedy applies the heavy presumption and strict necessity governing a direct prior restraint without treating party selectivity alone as conclusive content discrimination. Their exact grounds are not added together as an identical proposition.

**36-foot geography.** The south public access area and road protect the two driveway approaches and traffic movement after the predecessor's no-obstruction commands failed to secure access. The east-shoulder permission, five-foot setback and no-embankment term survive as actual exceptions; the south unpaved shoulder remains usable. The designated crosswalk permits prompt crossing only, with no stopping, hesitation, picketing, congregation, demonstration or impediment to traffic. These provisions are an integrated public access arrangement. The north/west private-property extension is separate: the reported ladder/noise conduct does not establish that excluding all otherwise lawful expression across that whole area is necessary to keep the driveways open. The owners/invitees exception concerns entry and retains other restrictions when they act in concert. No constitutional right to trespass on private property is recognized by removing a restriction the government imposed on otherwise lawful presence.

**Medical noise and images.** Paragraph (4) retains both temporal qualifications: 7:30 a.m.–noon, Monday–Saturday, during surgical procedures and recovery. It concerns patients inside the clinic. The four associates sustaining its noise part read it in the lower court's medical-operation context: sound of the listed kinds reaching those patients during the protected treatment periods, whose disruption the findings establish, not a generalized power to suppress disfavored ideas or silent expression. The visual prohibition is separately invalid for seven associates. No finding establishes that every observable image disrupts medical care or that physical shielding or other narrower measures would be inadequate. Protection from the persuasive or distressing meaning of political images is not a sufficient general neutral justification. The order is not rewritten into an invented medical-volume threshold, and no particular window treatment is commanded.

**300-foot approach.** Paragraph (5) concerns physical approach to a person seeking services, not every word uttered or sign displayed within 300 feet. Initial approach requires the person's indication of interest by approaching or inquiring. Invited nonthreatening conversation and literature are allowed there but never inside the 36-foot zone. The declining listener may leave and may not be accompanied, encircled, surrounded, harassed, threatened or physically or verbally abused. Seven associates find the prior-invitation condition excessive even with those limits; Stevens sustains it for these bound respondents. No vote invalidating prior consent recognizes a right to pursue a person after refusal, obstruct passage, threaten or force literature upon anyone.

**300-foot residences.** Paragraph (6)'s broad territorial restraint and its separate obstruction commands are distinct. Its geographical ban covers approaching, congregating, picketing, patrolling, demonstration and amplification within 300 feet of specified residences. The general invitation paragraph permits residential contact only upon an invitation conveyed from a distance beforehand, excludes obtaining invitation by an uninvited knock or doorbell, and requires immediate termination/departure upon revocation. It does not replicate paragraph (5). Seven associates reject the 300-foot territorial ban; Stevens sustains it for the bound parties. Ordinary trespass, threats, obstruction of driveways or sole-access streets and properly tailored targeted-picketing/noise relief remain possible. No replacement distance is invented.

**Persons bound.** All eight reject the named petitioners' argument that an injunction is invalid simply because it binds named wrongdoers and actual actors in concert who have notice. Regal Knitwear and the Florida construction confine that reach; shared abortion opposition, presence nearby, organizational sympathy or notice alone is insufficient. Preliminary hearing statements suggesting viewpoint coverage are concerning but do not replace the entire written order and authoritative construction or finally adjudicate any independent nonparty. No Justice determines Cheffer's ultimate rights or an absent person's contempt. For the three Justices rejecting the operative speech restraints, this conclusion about permissible coverage does not sustain those restraints.


**Paragraphs (7), (8) and (9): independent merits not reached.** Both sides disputed these provisions, but none is expressly identified by the exact petition questions. Paragraph (7) covers physical abuse, grabbing, intimidation, harassment, touching, pushing, shoving, crowding and assault of persons using or working at the clinic or seeking access to the specified homes; it contains the separate invited-contact qualification. Paragraph (8) concerns harassment, intimidation, physical abuse, assault and threats against present/former clinicians, staff, employees or volunteers; it has no corresponding invited-contact text. Paragraph (9) concerns encouraging, inciting or securing others to commit any prohibited act. These differences remain operative; no exception is transferred from one paragraph to another.

All eight decline the independent facial vagueness/overbreadth merits of those provisions. The third question fairly presents whom an injunction may bind in designated zones, and that issue is decided. It does not automatically present every possible speech/conduct boundary in all-place harassment, intimidation and encouragement commands. The strongest contrary path invokes exactly the ongoing chill and adversarial arguments supporting the paragraph (4) exception. The answer is narrower and discretionary, not jurisdictional: paragraph (4) supplies a direct categorical observable-expression rule whose asserted medical justification is already before the Court, whereas deciding these additional clauses would require independent contextual constructions of materially different conduct terms and their applications, beyond what is necessary to resolve the presented zones and party-coverage questions. Their incomplete record cannot be supplemented by assuming an unreported contempt application or that respondents’ characterizations are findings. The possibility of a legal facial argument is recognized, not denied; the associates decline that further exceptional extension. No merits approval of every use of “harassing,” “intimidating,” “crowding,” “abuse” or “encouraging” is implied.

The same scope conclusion covers a claim labeled as applied that independently attacks (7)–(9)'s substantive conduct terms: respondents' competing facial/as-applied characterization does not place a new question in the petition. The named petitioners are personally bound; this is not a ruling that they lack Article III standing to contest their own restraints. Their presented challenge to named/actual-concert coverage is decided, while independent conduct-language applications remain unreviewed. No Justice treats a hypothetical protected example or a reported preliminary arrest exchange as a final contempt adjudication.

Paragraph (9) nevertheless has an unavoidable derivative limit: a command against encouraging, inciting or securing “any of the prohibited acts” cannot sustain enforcement based on an operative prohibition set aside in this judgment. Each associate’s remedy removes that dependent predicate to the extent of that associate’s reversals. This is a consequence of effective relief on reached components, not a ruling on the independent facial validity of paragraph (9), a general incitement test, or a new contempt disposition. The same no-evasion limit prevents an invalidated zone restriction from being reimposed merely by relabeling protected conduct under another paragraph. Concrete lawful obstruction, threats, assault and independently valid conduct restraints remain available, without deciding every disputed outer application.

## Proposition agreement and circulation barriers

Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg support the same content-neutral classification and heightened no-more-speech-than-necessary standard, the same qualified public access/noise affirmances and the same private-extension/images/300-foot reversals. Their agreement requires explicit rejection of viewpoint guilt, preservation of all actual exceptions, acceptance of findings without record invention, and a separate showing for each restriction. They do not adopt strict scrutiny for every neutral injunction or a general least-restrictive-means test for legislation.

Stevens supports the public-access disposition and party-coverage construction but not the heightened standard, additional merits rulings outside his reading of the petition questions or invalidation of the two 300-foot restrictions. Minimum formulations preserving his agreement must identify his scope-based leave-intact votes and his physical-approach/individual-remedy rationale; describing him as joining the broader necessity rule would not resolve an objection.

Scalia and Thomas support strict review of the selective direct speech restraints; Kennedy supports the strict prior-restraint route without declaring every defendant-specific order content based. Their shared reversals do not alone establish a unified content-classification rationale. All three accept genuine conduct-specific enforcement and require no new factual determination denying the earlier misconduct. A formulation confined to prohibiting obstruction, threatening conduct, intrusive harmful noise and established unlawful participation is a lawful narrower path; this handoff does not invent new text or promise their approval of an unseen redraft.

All eight preserve the actual-concert/notice construction and the rights of independent nonparties. The three strict-review Justices' acceptance of that construction supplies no join sustaining an operative speech prohibition. None uses Casey as a direct private-speech restriction, treats NOW as the state-law source, imports a historical author or coalition, issues relief in Cheffer, sets a replacement distance/decibel level, determines a new contempt conviction, or adjudicates the independent validity of the additional conduct clauses merely because they were briefed.

## Remedy and open limits

Every reversal position is limited to Florida's approval of the specified new restriction and carries a remand for an injunction conforming to the constitutional limits stated. The 36-foot public arrangement, noise rule and other challenged provisions require separate treatment; invalidating the private extension, images or an overbroad 300-foot condition does not automatically vacate the whole decree. The seven reversing the 300-foot provisions preserve separable lawful commands against obstruction, threats and following after refusal without deciding every disputed outer application of verbal-abuse, harassment or intimidation language. Stevens would leave those territorial provisions intact. Paragraphs (7)–(9) remain independently undecided on the stated Rule 14 grounds, despite the merits arguments; paragraph (9) cannot retain a predicate actually set aside. No unchallenged paragraph is adjudicated merely because its text supplied context.

The legal form and remedy do not depend on a new factual finding, a complete video assessment or a new grant. A subsequent proposal relying on an unverified finding about a particular person, precise street dimension, specific patient's captivity or independent nonparty's conduct must return for source clarification. The absence of those findings is a limit on the proposition, not permission to manufacture them.
