## Final legal commitments by compatible ground

### Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg — discrimination and tailored reversal

Each independently commits to reversal and remand on the ground that the ordinance reserves a local processing service to the designated private operator and excludes substitutable interstate services. The article of commerce includes processing and disposal service, not only the physical movement of garbage. Covering competing in-town processors does not eliminate the favored local private-service reservation. The number of beneficiaries does not make the exclusion nondiscriminatory. Dean Milk supplies the service-location principle; New Energy rejects a minimum-number-of-beneficiaries condition; actual Fort Gratiot and Oregon Waste preserve the distinction between governing private waste transactions and selling a government's own service.

The valid local interests in sanitary disposal, reliable capacity, and financing do not alone justify this discriminatory means. The Town must establish a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve. The actual entering rule is preserved: no new proof quantum, demand for perfect certainty, or requirement to disprove every imaginable alternative is adopted. Neutral safety standards and lawful financing measures expose the missing justification here; the opinion must not command a particular tax, bond, subsidy, fee, or replacement program. None of these Justices requires an independent Pike holding.

**Individual join boundaries retained:** Stevens requires the distinction between this compulsory local private-service preference and ordinary market-structure regulation described in Exxon. Scalia will not join a freestanding, open-ended judicial balance of economic advantages; the discrimination and adequate-alternatives inquiry suffices. Kennedy requires preserving ordinary proprietary procurement and neutral safety or capacity regulation. Thomas requires distinguishing classification, justification, and actual regulatory capacity rather than importing an unpresented compensatory-tax ruling. Ginsburg requires the same private-operation boundary without an invented finding about bidding, investor residence, or a fully public system. Each may join a narrow common statement of these grounds; none is committed to every possible broader sentence about municipal monopolies.

### O'Connor — separate Pike reversal and statutory authorization limit

O'Connor agrees that the ordinance must fall, but does not agree that selecting one municipal service contractor necessarily discriminates between geographic classes. Other local processors and outside processors bear the exclusion alike. Her actual Fort Gratiot and Oregon Waste joins condemn express geographic preferences; they do not erase her distinction here. A discrimination opinion cannot count her judgment vote as a join in its framework.

She instead treats the command as an excessive interstate burden under Pike: already processed residue must make an additional mandated local stop, at a protected price, to finance the selected operation. The serious sanitary objective can be served by neutral processing standards, and financing does not establish that this particular compulsory processing route is needed. The $11 difference is not itself an adjudicated social-cost measure and does not make every higher-priced municipal service unconstitutional. Her ground does not require finding an unavoidable conflict with a New Jersey ordinance where the record and competing historical writings do not establish such a finding.

She may state separately that the cited RCRA planning, contract, and technical-assistance provisions fail to unmistakably authorize otherwise unconstitutional flow control. Permission to remove state and local impediments to long-term contracts is not an express displacement of federal Commerce Clause constraints; an EPA-assistance provision and a committee report do not supply that displacement. Congress retains power to provide it expressly. This supports the disposition without changing the actual entering rule or converting Chemical Waste's unaddressed amicus theory into an earlier holding.

**Minimum compatible position:** a judgment-only concurrence under Pike with the authorization discussion as her separate supporting analysis. She refuses a discrimination-framework join unless the classification objection is substantively resolved; narrowing the affected facility alone does not resolve it. She is not committed to reviewing unrelated licensing, zoning, or waste exemptions.

### Souter — corrected bounded affirmance

Souter commits to affirmance of the Commerce Clause enforcement ruling. He distinguishes the public service delivered through the Town's build-and-operate contract from geographically favoring a class of independent local businesses. The Town bears the consent-decree duty to replace its landfill and a contractual tonnage-shortfall obligation; the operator implements that service, and the Town has an option to acquire the facility. The option is not described as an obligation, and present private operation is not denied. Formal market-participant immunity is unnecessary to this conclusion: the proposed classification is evenhanded municipal-service provision, followed by Pike review.

Under that review he gives weight to dependable sanitary service, financing risk, spreading costs among waste generators, and the lack of established injury to outside processing enterprises or disruption of interstate disposal on this record. A monopoly's price and loss of Carbone's local business do not alone prove a constitutionally excessive interstate burden. The actual record does contain interstate waste movement; his position does not deny Commerce Clause applicability. Nor does it establish that no conceivable outside processor could be affected. It treats the proof offered as insufficient for the constitutional conclusion requested.

**Strongest contrary argument and response:** compulsory purchase of a privately operated local service prevents out-of-state providers from serving that demand, and Fort Gratiot forbids treating government planning as proprietary conduct. He accepts the latter rule but distinguishes this single publicly guaranteed service from reserving independent private landfill capacity by origin. His historical writing made precisely that distinction while aware of current private title and optional later purchase. Actual Oregon Waste does not decide the municipal-franchise boundary. No changed simulated premise requires abandoning it.

**Join barrier:** the present reservation of a compulsory service is not, for him, sufficient to trigger the discrimination branch; and the neutral record has not established the additional interstate injury needed for Pike invalidation. A disclaimer about fully public facilities answers only breadth, not this classification or proof objection. He can join a narrower affirmance limited to this contract, financing, regulatory duty, and record. He does not announce immunity for all public-purpose private contractors or for origin-based exclusion at private landfills.

### Blackmun — bounded affirmance retained

Blackmun retains his neutral commitment to affirm on the same limited municipal-service/Pike path. His Reeves opinion supports room for public-service provision but does not prove present public ownership here. His actual Fort Gratiot dissent sought an opportunity to demonstrate planning justification; his actual Oregon Waste dissent treats comprehensive conservation and local burdens as materially relevant without assuming exact cost equality or public title. Those public positions support his selection of the municipal-service path and are consistent with his historical join in Souter's reasoning.

His own Exxon dissent remains the strongest adverse authority: excluding a service market cannot be excused merely because the physical material continues to cross state lines. His commitment therefore requires the concrete public responsibility, capital-financing guarantee, single contracted provider, and local cost allocation, rather than relying solely on unchanged shipments or a benevolent objective. He regards those features and the present proof as sufficient under Pike. If material proof were genuinely missing under a legal test he accepted, an evidentiary remand could be narrower; it is not the selected disposition here.

**Join barrier:** no conclusive invalidation based only on private title, the acquisition option, or comparative prices. He can join the bounded municipal-service/Pike affirmance without declaring every environmental program exempt, rewriting Fort Gratiot, or finding costs or future acquisition proved beyond the actual record.
