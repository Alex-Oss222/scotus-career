## Effective premise and exact question

Actual Landgraf is effective through entering-law/OT_1993CHUNK4_LANDGRAF_PUBLIC.md. Its five-Justice general framework asks first for congressional temporal direction, then whether the proposed application changes legal consequences for completed events. Its three-Justice relevant-activity concurrence reaches the §102 result separately. The ruling is provision-specific and does not answer §101 merely because both provisions belong to the same Act.

Rivers asks only whether §101's amended contractual coverage governs these 1986 events in a case pending at the Act's November 21, 1991 enactment. The employees separately retain a Sixth Circuit remand for a jury trial on a racially motivated interference-with-contract-enforcement theory under the old statute. That remand is not a second temporal claim and is not adjudicated anew here. The Supreme Court's decision on §101 must leave it intact unless a lawful additional review ground exists; none is supplied.

## Section-specific ground supporting affirmance

Section 101 does not govern the completed preenactment conduct insofar as the employees seek the new contractual coverage it adds to §1981. Congress may change statutory law, including for past events when it supplies sufficient direction within constitutional limits. But Congress's disagreement with a judicial construction does not alone make the newly enacted replacement the meaning that the old enactment always had. Patterson supplies the operative construction of old §1981 within its actual scope until legislatively displaced for the events to which the amendment applies. Actual Harper explains why the judicial construction applies to still-open cases concerning earlier conduct; actual Landgraf separately governs the reach of later legislation. No constitutional ban on retrospective corrective legislation is announced.

The new subsection (b) encompasses making, performance, modification, termination, and enjoyment of all benefits, privileges, terms and conditions of the contractual relationship. On this record, extending §1981 to the broader discharge claim adds a legal basis of liability absent under the old-text rule applied below. Section 402(a) specifies when the Act takes effect, not which prior conduct it covers. Section 402(b) excludes the distinct disparate-impact cases with complaints before March 1, 1975 and initial decisions after October 30, 1983; §109(c) separately limits its extraterritorial amendments to later conduct. Those exclusions neither merge nor unavoidably command retrospective application of §101. Section 3(4)'s response-to-decisions purpose and expansion language establish substantive aim without supplying the missing temporal direction.

Robertson shows that Congress can expressly substitute new law for identified pending disputes; it does not allow a court to infer the same specific reach from any statutory correction. Bradley remains effective in its collateral-fee setting under actual Landgraf, but does not supply new primary §1981 coverage here. The conduct's earlier date than Patterson, and the possible absence of actual reliance on Patterson, do not prevent the amendment from changing its legal consequences. The Court need not approve discrimination or recognize a right to discriminate to maintain the difference between existing prohibitions and added statutory liability.

The old contract-enforcement remand and its existing jury remedy remain. Affirmance is limited to the temporal ruling under review; it is not an endorsement of the Sixth Circuit's broad statement that the entire 1991 Act cannot apply retrospectively or of its obsolete Chevron Oil inquiry into Patterson's judicial temporal effect. No declaration that all postformation conduct was outside old §1981 is made. Patterson protects contract enforcement, and its actual Supreme Court issues did not independently decide every discriminatory discharge claim. The broader discharge construction below is the premise of this limited §101 question, not a new universal discharge rule.

He does not seek a present damages award or a finding that the employees proved discrimination.

**Own authority and two-path test:** His actual Landgraf dissent reads §402(a), §109(c) and §402(b) as supporting compensation in other pending cases. His Bradley opinion and Kaiser dissent join support present application where the change repairs relief for already wrongful conduct. His full join in Brennan's Patterson partial concurrence/dissent supplies a direct reason to regard the old narrowing as inconsistent with the contractual equality Congress sought to protect. The alternative is stronger here than in Landgraf: §101 expands a cause's substantive coverage, rather than merely providing compensation under an existing prohibition. Bennett, which he joined, and actual Landgraf's controlling framework therefore require a serious check against calling all civil-rights amendments remedial. Nevertheless, the targeted response to Patterson combined with his maintained reading of the enacted temporal provisions supplies the stronger personal basis for reversal in this pending case.

**Join barriers and narrower position:** He can acknowledge that Congress changes law through enactment and cannot erase a Supreme Court construction merely by subsequent legislative opinion. He need not deny Harper or withhold Patterson selectively; his ground is that the 1991 enactment now directs the applicable coverage. He does not join a declaration that there is no sufficient retrospective direction here. His position is a separate disagreement with the application and, where necessary, the presumption adopted in actual Landgraf; it is not the governing law lower courts may select instead. **Remedy:** Permit the broader claim to proceed, subject to ordinary elements, defenses and remedial predicates. No separate §1981 punitive temporal proposition is committed on this review question.
