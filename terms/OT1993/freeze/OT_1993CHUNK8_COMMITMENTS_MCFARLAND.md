## Fresh neutral validation and entering law

The initial neutral description of the lower judgment is incomplete without its alternative grounds. The full supplied Fifth Circuit opinion supplies all of them: absence of a pending habeas proceeding; §2283's restriction with no exception found; lack of a general constitutional state-collateral counsel entitlement; no identified substantial constitutional case for a stay; and footnote 1's conclusion that converting the motion into a habeas petition would encounter unexhausted claims. These are distinct grounds. The two statutory questions must be answered without treating that last observation as a merits rejection of an identified exhausted claim.

Section 848(q)(4)(B) entitles a defendant who is or becomes financially unable to obtain adequate representation or investigative, expert or other reasonably necessary services to one or more attorneys and those services in a postconviction proceeding under §2254 or §2255 seeking to vacate or set aside a death sentence. Paragraph (6), governing appointment after judgment, requires at least one appointed attorney with at least five years of admission to the court of appeals and at least three years handling felony appeals there. Paragraph (5)'s different pre-judgment requirement is five years of admission to the trial court and three years actually trying felony prosecutions there; it is not substituted for paragraph (6). Under paragraph (7), good cause permits another attorney whose background, knowledge or experience otherwise enables proper representation, with due consideration of the possible penalty's seriousness and the litigation's unique and complex nature.

Paragraph (8) requires each appointed attorney to represent the defendant throughout every subsequent stage of available judicial proceedings, including pretrial, trial, sentencing, new-trial motions, appeals, certiorari applications and all available postconviction process, together with stay applications and other appropriate motions and procedures, plus available competency and executive or other clemency proceedings. Replacement by similarly qualified counsel may occur on the attorney's own or the defendant's motion. This continuity language does not decide the unpresented breadth of federal representation in every state proceeding. Paragraph (9) requires the court, upon an ex parte finding that investigative, expert or other services are reasonably necessary for representation concerning guilt or sentence, to authorize their procurement and order payment under paragraph (10). Retroactive authorization and payment are permitted, not required, if timely procurement could not practicably await prior authorization. Paragraph (10) requires compensation and service fees/expenses at rates or amounts reasonably necessary for paragraphs (4)–(9), notwithstanding generally applicable criminal-case limits. No later amendment replaces these terms. None eliminates financial eligibility, a capital sentence, an identified contemplated federal habeas challenge or counsel qualifications.

Section 2251 separately permits, rather than commands, a stay by the federal justice or judge before whom a habeas proceeding is pending: before final judgment, after final judgment of discharge, or pending appeal. It reaches proceedings against the detained person in a state court or under state authority for a matter involved in the habeas proceeding. A granted stay makes covered subsequent state proceedings void; without a stay, state proceedings remain as valid as if no habeas proceeding or appeal were pending. Section 2283 separately permits an injunction expressly authorized by Congress, necessary in aid of jurisdiction, or protecting/effectuating judgments. A finding of counsel entitlement alone cannot silently do the work of all three provisions.

## Alternative statutory paths and their consequences

**Counsel path A — preparation is included.** A financially unable capital prisoner invokes the federal proceeding for this limited purpose by expressly requesting qualified counsel to prepare the contemplated first federal habeas attack. The entitlement covers the assistance needed to investigate and formulate the petition; the statute's provision of qualified counsel and necessary services is not postponed until the unassisted prisoner has already completed that work. The strongest contrary argument is that “in any post conviction proceeding under” §2254 ordinarily presupposes an already-filed application. The answer adopted by this path is that “proceeding” is broader than the merits application, and a request invoking the specific congressional appointment mechanism initiates the preparatory federal proceeding. This does not invent a constitutional right or exempt a later petition from pleading, exhaustion, default or abuse requirements.

**Counsel path B — a petition must first exist.** The statute grants assistance within a §2254/§2255 proceeding; those sections concern applications for habeas relief. A motion seeking only counsel to file a later application is not yet that proceeding. The strongest contrary argument is the practical nullification of funded investigation before a factually adequate first pleading and the resulting procedural risks. This path responds that the court cannot alter the express entry condition to improve the program: assistance after filing still has substantial work to do, and an inadequate petition's available treatment does not turn a counsel motion into a petition. No premise that a bare petition must be adequate or that counsel will necessarily be found is invented.

**Stay path A — the expressly invoked preparation proceeding qualifies under §2251.** The same habeas undertaking can be pending for the limited counsel stage and therefore can support the judge's discretionary preservation of it. Section 2251 supplies express congressional authorization under §2283; §848 alone is not an injunction power. The strongest contrary argument is that Congress did not amend §2251's older pendency language or provide merits issues against which ordinary stay standards can operate. The answer is that “proceeding” need not mean only a merits petition and that discretion at the preparation stage asks whether time is reasonably necessary to make the enacted right effective, subject to diligence and abuse limits, rather than demanding completed counseled claims as the price of counsel. Ordinary merits review rules resume at their proper stage.

**Stay path B — appointment can precede the separate stay predicate.** Even if Congress funded preparation, a habeas proceeding within §2251 must be pending through an actual application. Section 848's representation requirement concerning stay applications does not confer jurisdiction to grant them. The best objection is that execution could defeat the right before counsel can prepare a pleading. This path answers that practical need cannot replace Congress's separate jurisdictional language; other lawful state relief or a proper federal application remains possible, but neither is presumed granted or adequate here. No unbounded inherent or All Writs power is adopted.

## Component commitments and limits for reconciliation

- Prepetition statutory counsel in this qualifying October22 proceeding: Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg support; Scalia and Thomas oppose. Six to two among the non-Stone Justices.
- Prepetition §2251 stay authority, separately analyzed through its pendency requirement and §2283: Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg support; O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas oppose. Five to three among the non-Stone Justices.
- No automatic stay; no underlying merits adjudication; no determination of the separate October26 petition; no general constitutional state-collateral-counsel holding: all eight preserve these limits.
- Supporting both statutory rights means reversing the categorical counsel and stay denials and remanding for qualified appointment and lawful stay discretion. It does not itself issue a current execution stay or invalidate conviction/sentence. O'Connor supports partial reversal on counsel and affirmance on stay; Scalia and Thomas support affirmance on both. This mixed disposition must not be rendered as an undifferentiated binary judgment vote.
- The lower substantial-case alternative does not sustain a categorical rejection of the preparation-stage motion under path A, because demanding developed claims before the provided investigation frustrates that stage. It remains pertinent when the court reviews a merits petition or applies the corresponding later-stage stay standard. The lack-of-constitutional-right ground never defeats an otherwise applicable enacted right. Footnote1's unexhausted hypothetical petition remains distinct from the actual counsel motion; the later petition's exhaustion is not adjudicated here.
