## Component conclusions

Each of the eight participating Associate Justices provisionally supports dismissing the writ as improvidently granted. The sufficient ground is discretionary vehicle deterioration: an enacted, soon-operative crime-date classification rule, an intervening state high-court decision recognizing annual review, and petitioners' acknowledged obligation to provide that review materially change the setting in which a broad constitutional or remedial ruling would operate. These developments do not establish present mootness. The judgment below remains undisturbed; no Munsingwear vacatur or new merits remand follows.

For every Justice listed below, the ex post facto question, §1983-versus-habeas question, actual January 1 mootness, statutory savings-clause construction, and the parties' alternative vacatur dispute remain **not reached**. This is an express component-level nonreach position, not a hidden merits vote. The lower court's damages disposition is outside the review. There is no new Supreme Court holding on parole eligibility, hearing intervals, exhaustion, or vacatur.

## Enhanced comparison of the live procedural paths

**Retain review.** Roller may still obtain present effectual relief. The legislative change takes effect January 1, not on its June enactment date; the latest hearing-status representation confirms no 1993 hearing as of November 8. The officials still contest the constitutional rule, and an unsettled national issue does not disappear because South Carolina changed its future law. Church of Scientology's partial-relief rule is a substantial counterargument to any claim of jurisdictional mootness.

**Dismiss discretionary review.** Even allowing a live controversy, this litigation no longer presents the same continuing enforcement setting. Griffin now recognizes annual consideration, the officials acknowledge an obligation to schedule it, and the newly identified enacted rule is expected by both sides to eliminate the hearing-frequency dispute after January 1. The Court can leave the already-existing appellate remedy operative without deciding the broad merits or using an incomplete current factual account to erase the judgment. The strongest counterargument is the investment in plenary briefing and argument plus Roller's outstanding opportunity for relief. The selected position answers that objection by preserving the judgment providing relief; the justification is the materially changed vehicle, not a categorical rule against deciding soon-expiring disputes.

**Vacate for mootness now.** Rejected on the present record. Munsingwear's predicate has not been established by an effective legal change or a verified completed event. It would be improper to assume a hearing, early statutory effectiveness, or loss of every useful remedy. Moreover, Karcher preserves the distinction between ending review and a justified vacatur; the dispute over the State's responsibility for future mootness remains open.

**Hold until January 1.** Lawful but not selected. It postpones the same threshold assessment and requires an additional verified dated action; no source supplies an automatic future termination. The particular case can be concluded now through discretionary dismissal without anticipating the later facts or resolving the merits. A decision to hold instead would be a materially different procedural commitment requiring explicit reconciliation, not an editorial reformulation of these rows.
