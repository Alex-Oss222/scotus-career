## Reconciled operative positions

**Six-Justice no-error position:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg affirm. Sections 4242 and 4243 prescribe a special verdict and commitment/release procedures, not an instruction explaining consequences to a jury with no sentencing function. The Court should not require such an explanation as ordinary federal practice merely because insanity is raised and the defendant requests it. A jury decides offense and defense elements from the evidence; ordinary assumed misunderstanding about release does not displace the proper guilt-only instruction. An accurate account of possible early release can itself divert attention toward consequences.

The rule is not an absolute prohibition. A particular trial misstatement or error—for example, a prosecutor or witness telling the jury that an insanity acquittee will go free—may require an appropriate accurate corrective instruction. The response must address the actual error; neither its wording nor necessity is identical in every setting. The reported trial contains no such event. The six therefore affirm on no-error grounds without an alternative harmless-error decision. They do not adopt the Fifth Circuit's categorical denial of corrective authority.

**Two-Justice requested-explanation position:** Blackmun and Stevens would vacate the affirmance and remand. When insanity is properly raised and a defendant requests clarification, federal supervisory practice should provide a short accurate explanation of immediate commitment followed by statutory judicial review, paired with a direction to decide guilt and insanity without regard to disposition. The instruction describes the special verdict without promising any particular duration of hospitalization. The basis is a federal practice rule, not a statutory command and not an Eighth Amendment rule extended beyond capital sentencing. They retain ordinary Rule 52(a)/Kotteakos review: the Government must establish harmlessness of the preserved nonconstitutional error; a new trial follows only if the error is not harmless. The Supreme Court should not make an unseen juror finding or order acquittal or commitment.

**Statutory accuracy shared by all eight:** Immediate commitment is distinct from continuing hospitalization. The examination/report and section 4247(d) hearing must precede the release determination; the hearing occurs no later than forty days after the special verdict. Section 4243(d) requires clear-and-convincing proof for the category involving bodily injury, serious damage to another's property, or substantial risk of either; other offenses use a preponderance. The issue is release-related substantial risk due to present mental disease or defect. No determination assigns Shannon's section 922(g) charge to either category. Attorney General custody, reasonable efforts toward state acceptance, state transfer when accepted, conditional release under an approved regimen, revocation and the distinct furlough provisions retain the terms stated in the frozen neutral commitment and verbatim supplement. No fixed or indefinite hospitalization promise is permissible.

## Proposition and topology limits for assembly

Six support the no-statutory-entitlement proposition and the rejection of a general supervisory requirement, together with the accurate corrective reservation. Two support a new requested-explanation practice. All eight reject a promise of guaranteed prolonged hospitalization. Agreement on that accuracy limit does not merge the competing instruction rules. There is no fracture requiring Marks analysis and no partial majority join carried over from history. Assembly must test actual proposed language and determine authorship independently.

The merits adjudication should remain the statutory and federal-practice question presented. Simmons must be distinguished at its actual capital-sentencing scope. The frozen references to absence of a general due-process entitlement are not authority for a freestanding categorical constitutional holding covering unpresented misleading-error cases; no constitutional challenge to the Act or its commitment burdens is decided. The corrective reservation cannot become a mandatory full commitment lecture whenever insanity is raised, and the general rule cannot become an absolute ban.

The historical majority also discussed the borrowing canon and differences from the District of Columbia system. Those historical arguments are not automatically new controlling alternative holdings of this Court. The frozen common sufficient ground is the actual text's absence of a consequences-instruction command and the continuing jury-function rule. A proposed additional independent holding based on an unmodeled statutory-borrowing theory must be separately supported rather than imported from the comparator.

For the requested-explanation writing, preserve the distinction between using legislative history as context for a sound practice and giving it enacted force. The Senate report says the jury may be instructed and leaves omission at the defendant's request to the court's discretion. The dissenters' newly proposed defendant-request rule is their judicial practice choice; it must not be mislabeled the report's command. The dissent's argument that the special verdict otherwise invites a false freedom inference remains an institutional risk assessment, not a finding of this jury's thoughts. The original commitment rows supply the additional qualifications and authorities.
