## Question, facts and shared threshold commitments

All eight Justices reach the limited statutory question on the supplied certiorari channel. The limited review evidence excludes challenges to the appellate findings of cohesion and sufficient white bloc voting. None reweighs election statistics or treats a renewed attack on those findings as an alternative ground for reversal. The Eleventh Circuit found statutory liability under the totality and ordered formulation of a remedy; it did not prescribe a finished map or final number of commissioners. The district court's rejection of intentional discrimination has not been reviewed by that court of appeals.

The structural claim challenges one countywide commissioner who exercises both executive and legislative authority. The offered local alternative has five district seats **and an at-large chair**, as authorized conditionally by the 1985 act and rejected in the 1986 referendum. The five school-board districts are an existing electoral arrangement for another body, including a district with a Black electoral majority. Neither source makes a six-officer commission current law. No Justice's commitment below depends on an invented statutory division of duties within that proposed commission.

All preserve the difference between a voting practice's coverage, an administrable nondilutive comparison, proof of unequal opportunity, and an available remedy. Section 5's comparison to an existing practice does not establish a Section 2 violation. A statutory results violation requires no purpose finding, but neither voting-age percentage nor lack of a Black commissioner establishes one by itself. The proportional-representation proviso remains operative. The rejected referendum does not immunize an independently established federal violation; nor does state permission by itself prove one.

## Entering law and the material open question

Gingles supplies the causal preconditions and totality inquiry. **Section 2 fragmentation, state districting choice and influence claims**, present operation March 2, 1993, combines the actual Growe and Voinovich holdings: a compact potential majority, cohesion and defeating majority bloc voting are necessary in the ordinary fragmentation claim; totality remains independently necessary. Growe expressly extended those prerequisites to fragmentation. Voinovich distinguishes the State's independent districting authority from a federal court's power to order a remedy for an established violation. Neither identifies a norm for changing a governing body's size.

Houston Lawyers, 501 U.S. 419, 425–428, rejects categorical exclusion of elections for independent single-office holders and treats state interests in an existing structure as material to totality and remedy. Its several elected judgeships already existed; it does not order creation of additional offices. Stevens wrote it; Blackmun, O'Connor and Souter joined. Scalia dissented with Kennedy on the distinct Chisom judicial-election question. Those positions do not mechanically answer whether the number of county legislators is a practicable comparison variable.

**Voting Rights Act Section 5 coverage of reallocations of governing authority**, effective January 27, 1992, comes from Kennedy's actual Presley opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas. It requires a direct relation to voting and expressly preserves separate Section 2 claims. Stevens's public dissent, joined by Blackmun, emphasizes the electoral significance of meaningful governing authority. Both positions bear on characterization, but neither settles Section 2 body-size liability.

**Territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury**, effective June 28, 1993, supplies the actual Shaw boundary. Blackmun, Stevens and Souter joined the Court's limited rejection of the design-only claim; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas disagreed on that constitutional theory. Shaw expressly imposes no added constitutional-injury gate on Section 2. A Justice may retain a separate constitutional concern without defeating a statutory claim through a displaced rule.

## Alternative paths tested

**Contextual comparison and affirmance.** A legislature with one member remains a representative institution covered by the statute. Five district seats are a locally available, practicable comparison because the State authorized them for this county and the school board demonstrates a workable district pattern. With cohesion and bloc-voting findings outside review and the circuit's totality determination intact, retain liability and the remedy remand. The strongest objection is that practicability shows that several alternatives could work but does not establish why five district seats, rather than any other reasonable number, measures a statutory entitlement. The countervailing answer is that Section 2 asks for equal opportunity under real conditions, not a uniquely optimal governmental structure; a concrete and reasonable comparison can establish dilution without maximizing seats or guaranteeing proportional outcomes.

**No usable size comparison and reversal.** Existing offices and the choice of districts in which to elect them supply a determinate object of comparison; selecting the number of offices first requires a legislative policy judgment not supplied by the statute. Local authorization and school-board practice establish alternatives, but do not identify a nondilutive norm for this different governing body. The strongest objection is that this approach insulates severe local exclusion despite Houston Lawyers and the ordinary availability of reasonable remedial alternatives. The answer on this path is limited: those precedents prohibit categorical office exemptions but do not establish that each challenged practice admits a results comparison. Unequal access, discriminatory election rules and intentional discrimination remain examinable under their own elements; the present claim fails on the variable it asks a court to change.

**Broader removal of dilution doctrine.** A claim that Section 2 does not reach any vote dilution would require displacement of Gingles and the actual Growe/Voinovich holdings. The existing source record does not supply an independently supported necessity for that much broader step. No such overruling commitment is inferred from a narrow reversal vote. A categorical immunity for every election to a single-member office would also conflict with Houston Lawyers. A new basis of either kind needs express Justice-specific support in later authorized reconciliation, not attribution through these rows.

## Provisional proposition and remedy map

Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support the stated contextual benchmark and affirmance. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support the bounded rejection of this size comparison and reversal. This is an eight-Justice provisional division, not a Court judgment or an equal-division disposition; Stone has not entered and is not predicted.

The four affirmance positions agree only to the record-specific benchmark, preserved totality finding and existing remedy remand. They do not order five seats, six seats, the school-board map, cumulative voting or any other unexamined alternative. The four reversal positions can share the stated comparison ground without assuming that every further textual or constitutional theory is shared. On their path the circuit must address the previously unreached constitutional claim, not enter an appellate purpose finding supplied by this Court. On the affirmance path that alternative claim need not be decided.

All eight preserve the actual Growe/Voinovich doctrine and the distinction from Section 5 at this provisional stage. No Marks synthesis or new controlling rule is declared. No author's identity or final join is assigned.
