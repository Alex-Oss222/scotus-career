## Operative propositions and complete qualifications for assembly

**Counsel.** A capital prisoner who is or becomes financially unable to obtain adequate representation or investigative, expert or other reasonably necessary services may initiate the §848(q)(4)(B) postconviction proceeding for appointment by filing a motion requesting counsel for the contemplated federal §2254 or §2255 attack seeking to vacate or set aside the death sentence. A formal, legally sufficient merits application need not precede that appointment; the right includes assistance preparing it. This October 22 request qualifies. Six associates support this proposition; O'Connor's agreement with it does not imply agreement with the separate stay construction.

The statutory qualifications are preserved rather than decided away. For appointment after judgment, paragraph (6) requires at least one attorney admitted to the relevant court of appeals for five years with three years handling felony appeals there. Paragraph (5)'s different pre-judgment trial-court admission and actual felony-trial experience requirements do not replace paragraph (6). Paragraph (7) permits another attorney for good cause whose background, knowledge or experience enables proper representation, with due consideration of the possible penalty's seriousness and the litigation's unique and complex nature. Paragraph (8) requires continuing representation through subsequent available judicial stages, available postconviction process, stay applications and other appropriate motions and procedures, and available competency and clemency proceedings unless similarly qualified replacement occurs on the attorney's or defendant's motion. This case does not resolve the full breadth of representation in state exhaustion or every later state proceeding.

Under the then-operative paragraph (9), an ex parte finding that investigative, expert or other services are reasonably necessary for representation concerning guilt or sentence requires authorization and payment under paragraph (10). Retroactive authorization and payment are permitted when timely procurement could not practicably await prior approval; they are not mandatory. Paragraph (10) requires reasonably necessary compensation and service fees/expenses notwithstanding generally applicable criminal-case rate and maximum limits. These conditions do not create unconditional funding for unidentified services. Use the pre-September 1994 text, not later amendments.

**Stay authority.** Once the qualifying capital prisoner invokes this statutory appointment proceeding, a habeas proceeding is pending for §2251's limited preparatory purpose. Section 2251 expressly authorizes the stay and therefore supplies §2283's express-Act-of-Congress exception. Counsel entitlement alone is not an injunction power. Five associates support this proposition. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas retain their actual-application objection; O'Connor's counsel position must never be counted in the stay coalition.

Section 2251 permits a United States justice or judge before whom the proceeding is pending to stay a covered state proceeding before final judgment, after final judgment of discharge, or pending appeal. The state proceeding must be against the detained person in a state court or under state authority and concern a matter involved in the habeas proceeding. A granted stay makes covered subsequent state proceedings void; absent a stay, those proceedings remain valid as though no habeas proceeding or appeal were pending. Section 2283's other exceptions—necessity in aid of jurisdiction and protection or effectuation of judgments—remain distinct and are not independently found satisfied here.

**Discretion is not automatic entitlement.** The district court must afford a reasonable opportunity for appointed counsel meaningfully to investigate and present available habeas claims. A stay may be necessary to protect that opportunity. But the existence of authority does not command a stay on every motion. A prisoner who inexcusably ignores an available opportunity and flouts the process may lawfully be denied one. No such finding is made about McFarland. The Court does not infer a new execution date, issue an indefinite stay, find an underlying substantial constitutional claim, or decide final habeas relief. Barefoot's merits and successive-review principles remain effective in their settings; demanding the completed counseled claim as the price of preparatory counsel would defeat the enacted stage. No uniform new merits-stay standard displaces those principles.

**Lower alternative grounds and remedy.** The absence of a general constitutional entitlement to state collateral counsel does not answer an express federal statutory entitlement. The lower court's hypothetical treatment of the motion as an unexhausted petition is not a reason to convert it into that petition and then reject the relief actually requested. Its no-substantial-claim observation does not sustain categorical absence of preparatory stay power, although merits and later-stage stay standards retain their proper operation. Reverse the reviewed categorical rulings to the extent supported above and return for qualified appointment and lawful consideration of a necessary stay. Do not adjudicate the separate October 26 petition's sufficiency, exhaustion or dismissal; do not grant a certificate for unidentified claims. The operative statutory and procedural consequences explain each component without a general §2253 holding.
