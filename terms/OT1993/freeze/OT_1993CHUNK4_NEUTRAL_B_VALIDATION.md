## Stansbury: record and source validation

**Predecision primary text:** People v. Stansbury, 4 Cal.4th 1017, 846 P.2d 756 (March 8, 1993), especially its Miranda section, recovered directly from `https://static.case.law/cal-4th/4/cases/1017-01.json`. Local copies: `chunk4-sources/stansbury-ca.json` and `.txt`.

The suppression issue was decided on its merits below. The state court treated the mislabeled statutory motion as a nonstatutory Evidence Code section 402 motion; it rejected the suggestion that the challenged statements were admitted solely for impeachment. They entered the prosecution's case and could show consciousness of guilt. There is no established adequate independent state procedural ground barring the reviewed federal question.

The record supports these particular circumstances, without deciding their ultimate objective legal effect: four plainclothes officers arrived at about 11 p.m.; weapons were held but the opinion identifies no evidence Stansbury saw them; he was invited to the station, offered a choice to drive himself, and accepted an unrestrained front-seat ride; he was questioned in a locked interview room in the secure jail area and would have needed officer assistance to leave; the interview lasted twenty or thirty minutes; questioning was described as nonaccusatory; mention of a turquoise car changed the investigating officer's suspicion; statements after that point were suppressed. The police accounts that they would have released him if asked, and their witness/suspect characterizations, cannot establish what was communicated to him merely because the officers testified to them later.

The lower opinion explicitly lists investigative focus among its most important custody considerations and sustains the focus finding as a central part of its analysis. It also discusses objective circumstances. Thus the live choice is legal-standard correction and remand versus treating the erroneous subjective consideration as immaterial in an otherwise objective application. A final custody finding or automatic suppression is unnecessary and would require more than correction of the announced standard.

The received packet is supplemented by these source-supported details. It should not suggest the prior conviction questions or the change in suspicion mechanically establish the first instant of custody. Nor should it reduce custody to every restriction on movement or to the interviewee's own undisclosed belief.

## Alvarez-Sanchez: record, statute, and source validation

**Predecision primary text:** United States v. Alvarez-Sanchez, 975 F.2d 1396 (September 15, 1992), majority, dissent, and footnotes read in full from `https://static.case.law/f2d/975/cases/1396-01.json`. Local copies: `chunk4-sources/alvarez-ca9.json` and `.txt`.

The opinion reproduces 18 U.S.C. §3501(a)–(e), including every condition in (c). Its safe harbor protects against exclusion **solely because of delay** in bringing the defendant before an officer empowered to commit persons charged with federal or D.C. offenses, provided the trial judge finds the confession voluntary, the weight is left to the jury, and the confession is given within six hours immediately after the relevant arrest/detention. The proviso removes that time limitation where the trial judge finds delay beyond six hours reasonable considering transportation means and distance to the nearest available committing officer. That transportation proviso is not a free-standing exemption for every reasonable administrative delay. Subsection (b)'s voluntariness considerations and subsection (d)'s uncoerced statements without interrogation or arrest are separate provisions.

Local officers arrested Alvarez-Sanchez on state narcotics charges on Friday August 5, 1988, while executing a search warrant and recovered counterfeit money. The reported record gives questioning by Secret Service agents on Monday August 8 while state custody continued; after a Miranda waiver he admitted knowing possession. Federal custody followed later that afternoon, and federal presentment was Tuesday August 9. The dissent identifies 11:30 a.m. questioning, a Spanish-speaking sheriff's deputy, subsequent booking, and a full magistrate calendar; it also reports uncertainty whether the state prosecutor declined or the case was never submitted. Those details must not be conflated with a majority finding that no state prosecution ever could have occurred.

The majority aggregated state and federal custody, found no transportation reason extending the safe harbor, and regarded Monday interrogation-related delay as sufficient under either its McNabb-Mallory or Halbert/Wilson approach. Its alternative §3501(b) assessment relied in part on the same cumulative delay; it did not make a distinct constitutional coercion finding independent of that construction. Although the main text says the government did not contest that the confession was outside the safe harbor, footnotes expressly record its arguments that state/federal time aggregates only on collusion and that Rule 5(a) imposes no federal presentment duty during state custody. The court rejected those arguments under Fouche. Thus the federal-trigger question was presented and rejected below; the isolated sentence does not establish forfeiture of that question.

The lawful competing statutory path takes “any law-enforcement officer” to encompass state detention regardless of the charge and invokes avoidance concerns. The narrower federal-trigger path reads those words together with the specified federal/D.C. committing officer and Rule 5(a). Both must keep improper working arrangements distinct from ordinary notification and cooperation. The supplied neutral facts support no finding of the former. The independent issue in Anderson v. United States is preserved; the statutory result does not decide all circumstances involving a federal hold, sham state custody, or federal control without a formally announced federal arrest.

No automatic reinstatement, categorical exclusion of every confession after six hours, displacement of Miranda, constitutional voluntariness ruling, or ruling on an unpresented McLaughlin remedy follows. The precise preservation of any distinct constitutional ground is reserved for remand under the applicable rules rather than invented here.
