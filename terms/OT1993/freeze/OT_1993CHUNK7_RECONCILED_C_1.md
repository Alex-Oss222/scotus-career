## 80. Dolan v. City of Tigard, No. 93-518

### Historical verification and actual-law comparison

The official report verifies the historical reversal and remand and its five-Justice opinion: Rehnquist, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Stevens's historical dissent was joined by Blackmun and Ginsburg; Souter wrote separately. These are comparator facts only, not assignments or automatically imported simulated joins. The report's topology is at 375; the opinion's two-stage inquiry, burden, applications and mandate are at 385–396; the dissenting analyses are at 396–414.

Historically, the purpose nexus was satisfied: controlling runoff and reducing traffic are legitimate objectives linked to this expansion. The disputed next step required rough proportionality—some individualized determination that the dedication relates in both nature and extent to the development's impact, with the City bearing the justification burden. No precise mathematical calculation was required. Generalized public benefits were insufficient, but the stricter requirement that each exaction be specifically and uniquely attributable to the development was rejected. The government burden was tied to the individualized permit decision demanding identified property interests; the discussion did not adjudicate every legislative obligation or fee. See 512 U.S. 386–391 and 391 n.8.

The historical greenway application distinguished leaving the floodplain open from surrendering the right to exclude the public: the City had not justified the latter interest by its flood-control premise. The pathway application accepted increased traffic and the legitimacy of alternative transportation, but required an effort to connect and quantify the dedication's mitigation beyond the finding that it could offset some traffic. It did not require exact predictive arithmetic, invalidate every dedication, compel unconditional permit issuance or adjudicate the constitutionality of the variance process. See 383 n.4, 392–396.

Actual *Lucas* retains its complete-deprivation trigger and background-law exception; the record establishes continued commercial use here. Its ordinary proof rule is not an exaction burden. Actual *Alaska* concerns preservation of existing submerged-land boundaries against project-created changes and does not authorize surrender of existing property. Actual *Yee* preserves the difference between invited occupation and a demanded conveyance. None displaces *Nollan* or settles the required degree of connection. Current *PUD* and *Health Care* concern bounded statutory authority; they do not insulate a permit condition from a constitutional property challenge. *Carlton*'s ordinary economic due-process review does not replace the distinct Takings Clause inquiry. No changed legal, factual, procedural or remedial premise supports an associate's departure from the verified historical positions.

### Reconciled ground and remedy definitions

**Dedication ground:** For this individualized permit requiring identified property interests, the City must demonstrate an approximate, individualized relationship between the nature and extent of each dedication and the proposed development's impacts after the purpose nexus is established. Reasonable estimates are permitted; mere general benefits and an unexplained possible traffic offset are not enough. The public-access interest and pathway extent are insufficiently justified on this record. This supports reversal of the judgment sustaining both challenged dedications and remand for lawful permit treatment under that standard.

**Planning ground:** The challenger retains the burden of establishing constitutional inadequacy once the conditions' actual relationship to legitimate land-use objectives is considered. The concrete runoff and traffic findings, connected mitigation functions and comprehensive plan suffice on this record; no new government persuasion burden or exact prediction is justified. The property appropriation objection must be answered, and neither retained economic use nor ordinance origin alone immunizes a condition. This supports affirmance of both conditions. Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg can additionally agree that a concededly germane demand shown to be grossly disproportionate would require further constitutional scrutiny; Souter's narrower ground reserves a new general degree-of-fit rule.

**Shared remedy limits:** The conveyance has not occurred. Reversal means further state proceedings concerning both dedications, not a dollar award, a determination of a completed taking, or an unconditional building permit. Affirmance leaves the reviewed conditions and variance disposition in place. Neither position decides the independent 15-percent open-space rule, the constitutionality of the variance procedure, all legislative exactions, money fees or ordinary zoning.

## 81. Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg, No. 93-644

### Historical verification and actual-law comparison

The official report verifies a historical seven-to-two reversal and remand. Stevens's opinion was joined by Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; Scalia separately concurred while expressly joining it. Ginsburg dissented, joined by Rehnquist. See 512 U.S. 416–417, 435–436. These identities establish the comparator; they do not assign a simulated opinion or provide a Stone vote.

The historical controlling procedural rule invalidated Oregon's denial of judicial review of punitive amount after it removed an established safeguard without an adequate substitute. The opinion distinguished review of whether any punitive award was authorized from review of the amount imposed on a culpable defendant. It did not decide what precise review standard the Constitution demands, whether this $5 million award was itself substantively excessive, a constitutional ratio, or a mandatory remittitur procedure. See 426–435, especially 429, 430–433 and 432 n.10. The common-law history supplies a functional safeguard against arbitrary deprivation, not an absolute prohibition on procedural innovation; *Hurtado* illustrates permissible replacement protection. *International Shoe* illustrates adaptation to changed conditions, not approval of eliminating an unreplaced safeguard.

The exact substitutes matter. The statute listed seven criteria, but five were instructed: likelihood of serious harm, awareness of that likelihood, duration, attitude after notice and financial condition. Honda objected to the profitability instruction as phrased and waived the other-punishment instruction on the represented absence of previous punishment. Clear-and-convincing proof governed punitive entitlement; wealth evidence required a prima facie entitlement showing; punitive damages were optional. The $5 million limit came from the complaint, not a statutory maximum. Legal-error, instructional-error and no-evidence review remained. No verified routine power to set a pre-verdict amount ceiling or infer passion/prejudice solely from amount supplied the missing review. See 418–419 n.1, 426–429, 432–433, 438–445 and 441 n.6.

Actual *TXO* establishes only the majority-supported sufficiency of its instructed jury, trial hearing/review and state supreme court review. It supplies no controlling potential-harm formula, ratio, instruction-waiver rule or general substantive-excessiveness test. *Haslip* retains its existing force. Thus the historical Honda introduction's characterization of historical *TXO* cannot be copied as a new account of current law. The procedural conclusion is independently supported by actual *TXO* and *Haslip*. Scalia and Thomas's rejection of a substantive numerical doctrine remains compatible with the procedural ground; their actual *Carlton* position likewise distinguishes asserted substantive economic protection from a genuine procedural defect. Ginsburg had no *Haslip* or *TXO* vote. Her historical Honda whole-process assessment confirms the independently frozen application inference, rather than creating a fictitious earlier join.

### Reconciled ground and remedy definitions

**Review ground:** Oregon may innovate in procedure, but on this record it has eliminated an important traditional judicial safeguard against an arbitrary punitive amount without an adequate replacement. The actual instructions, entitlement burden, wealth gate, pleaded ceiling and retained legal-error/no-evidence review do not adequately perform the omitted amount-review function. The judgment sustaining the punitive award must therefore be reversed and the matter remanded for constitutionally adequate review and further proceedings. This does not prescribe the exact standard, require appellate reweighing, announce a numerical test, find this award excessive, remove punitive entitlement or disturb compensation.

**Whole-process ground:** The State's actual package sufficiently guides punitive choice at trial and permits judicial correction of trial/legal errors and absence of evidence of entitlement. Neither *Haslip* nor actual *TXO* made their particular postverdict procedures a universal checklist. The instructed criteria, higher culpability burden, wealth gate and other protections can therefore be assessed together as a constitutionally sufficient alternative here. This supports affirmance of the existing judgment against this procedural challenge, without representing Oregon as having an ordinary postverdict power to reduce an excessive amount. It does not decide a future refusal to entertain a properly presented federal substantive claim.
