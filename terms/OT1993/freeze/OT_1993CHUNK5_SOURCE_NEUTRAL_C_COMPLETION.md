# PUD and Nichols — source completion addendum

This separate immutable addendum supplements, without rewriting, the released PUD and Nichols neutral packets. No same-case historical judgment, vote or rationale appears. Cutoffs: May31,1994 for PUD; June6,1994 for Nichols.

## Nichols: complete official November1990 chapter recovered

The Commission's **entire14-page Chapter Four** was downloaded and read, including definitions, all exceptions, commentary and departure policy: https://www.ussc.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/guidelines-manual/1990/manual-pdf/Chapter_4.pdf . Local `tmp/ot1993_chunk5_sources/guidelines1990_ch4.pdf/.txt`. This closes the initial packet's incomplete historical-manual source limit.

The contemporaneous petition's §4A1.1 reproduction is not an exact reproduction of the1990 manual in every respect: the official1990 chapter has items(a)–(e), not the petition's added(f), and(c) says **“Add1 point for each prior sentence not included in(a) or(b), up to a total of4 points for this item.”** The earlier packet's “not counted” reflects the petition, but the official words are “not included.” No live Nichols count changes: the DUI receives one point under(c). Do not attribute the petition's entire reproduction to the1990 manual.

Section4A1.2(a)(1) includes a sentence imposed upon adjudication of guilt by guilty plea, trial **or nolo contendere**, for conduct outside the instant offense. Section4A1.2(e)(2) counts other prior sentences imposed within ten years of commencement of the instant offense; subsection(e)(3) excludes those outside the applicable periods. The1983 DUI preceding the1990 conspiracy plea is the record's identified prior; the actual offense-commencement facts govern, and no new timing dispute is invented.

Section4A1.2(c) counts felony sentences, and misdemeanor/petty sentences except its enumerated classes. Subsection(c)(1) counts the listed or similar offenses only if **probation of at least one year**, imprisonment of at least thirty days, **or** similarity to an instant offense. Its list is: careless/reckless driving; contempt; disorderly conduct/disturbing peace; driving without license or with revoked/suspended license; false information to police; fish/game violations; gambling; hindering/failure to obey police; insufficient-funds check; leaving an accident; local-ordinance violations except ones also state crimes; nonsupport; prostitution; resisting arrest; trespassing. Subsection(c)(2) never counts its listed or similar offenses: hitchhiking; juvenile-status offenses/truancy; loitering; minor traffic infractions such as speeding; public intoxication; vagrancy. These are the1990 lists/threshold, not later versions.

Application Note5 expressly states that driving while intoxicated/under the influence and similar offenses, whatever named, are counted and **are not minor traffic infractions under§4A1.2(c)**. Do not import later added “always” language or later decisions resolving other exception arguments. No such additional exception argument is presented here.

Note6 excludes sentences for convictions reversed/vacated for legal error or subsequently discovered exonerating evidence, and convictions shown previously ruled constitutionally invalid. It permits considering underlying criminal conduct under§4A1.3. The background onp4.9 confirms the quoted uncounseled-fine-only counting rule and **expressly leaves to courts whether a defendant may collaterally attack a prior conviction at sentencing**. This is not a Commission decision resolving the constitutional channel; actual Custis must retain its statutory setting.

Section4A1.3,pp4.9–4.10, is a policy statement permitting departure on reliable information where the category inadequately represents seriousness or recidivism risk. For significant overrepresentation, a downward departure may be considered; it is not required, and the reference is the range for a lower criminal-history category. Its example is two minor misdemeanors close to ten years old with no intervening criminal behavior. It forbids using a prior arrest record itself and forbids going below CategoryI's lower limit on this criminal-history-adequacy ground. These details qualify any reliance on a departure mechanism as protection against overstatement; they do not establish Nichols's entitlement or erase a constitutional objection.

## PUD: designated-use removal qualification recovered

The complete operative§131.10 and§131.11 text was checked in the official CFR reproduction, whose part source note is48Fed.Reg.51405(Nov8,1983) and which shows no later amendment note for these sections. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-1997-title40-vol12/pdf/CFR-1997-title40-vol12-part131.pdf , printedpp718–719. This later printing is used only to recover the unchanged1983 regulation, not as post-cutoff law. Relevant complete sections, not all unrelated1997 provisions, were reviewed. The official same-case report was already read in full. The original PUD packet's§131.10(g)(4) gap is now closed.

Section131.10(g) permits removal of a designated use **which is not an existing use**, or establishment of subcategories, if the State demonstrates attaining the designated use infeasible because of one of six specified conditions. Paragraph(g)(4) states: **“Dams, diversions or other types of hydrologic modifications preclude the attainment of the use, and it is not feasible to restore the water body to its original condition or to operate such modification in a way that would result in the attainment of the use.”** Both the infeasibility condition and the distinction between designated and existing uses matter. This provision cannot fairly be restated as an unconditional duty that every dam attain every possible use.

The other(g) grounds are naturally occurring pollutant concentrations; natural ephemeral/intermittent/low flows unless sufficient effluent can compensate consistently with state conservation requirements; irremediable human-caused conditions or ones more damaging to correct; specified natural physical features unrelated to water quality preventing aquatic-life protection; and controls beyond§§301(b)/306 causing substantial widespread economic/social impact. Section(h) forbids removal of existing uses unless a more protective use is added, and forbids removal of uses attainable through the statutory effluent limits plus cost-effective reasonable nonpoint best-management practices. Section(d) deems such uses attainable at minimum. Section(e) requires notice and hearing opportunity before adding/removing uses or subcategories; section(j) requires attainability analysis for omission/removal of§101(a)(2) uses or less stringent subcategories; section(k) does not require that analysis when designating uses including§101(a)(2)'s uses. Section(f)'s seasonal criteria may not prevent attaining a more protective use in another season. Existing uses and unattained aspirational uses therefore cannot be merged.

Section131.11(a)(1) requires criteria based on sound scientific rationale, with enough parameters/constituents to protect designated use; multiple-use waters require protection of the most sensitive use. Section(b) says States **should** establish numerical values from§304(a) guidance, site-specific modification or other scientifically defensible methods, and narrative/biomonitoring criteria where numerical criteria cannot be established **or to supplement numerical criteria**. Section(a)(2) separately requires data review and protective criteria for relevant toxic pollutants, with identification of a method to regulate point-source toxic discharges on water-quality-limited segments when narrative criteria are used. The statutory§303(c)(2)(B) specific toxic-numerical qualifications remain distinct and govern in their own terms. Do not turn regulatory “should” into “shall,” or erase the narrative-supplement alternative.
