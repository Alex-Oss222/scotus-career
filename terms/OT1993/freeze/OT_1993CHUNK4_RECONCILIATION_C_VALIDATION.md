O'Connor concurred only in the judgment, rejecting the discrimination classification and relying on the excessive burden under Pike. She separately rejected the asserted congressional authorization. Souter dissented, joined by Rehnquist and Blackmun, treating the arrangement as provision of a municipal service and finding no sufficient interstate burden under Pike. ## Newly complete ordinance and source qualifications

The official report's appendix, 511 U.S. 395–400, now supplies the whole Local Law No. 9; the neutral-stage limitation that the complete ordinance was unavailable is therefore resolved for assembly. The construction contract, full merits briefs, and joint appendix were not independently recovered. The official report supplies additional reported figures, including the 120,000-ton annual guarantee and estimated $1.4 million construction cost, but neither figure is essential to the reconciled commitments. Do not present them as independently verified contract terms. The lower opinion and Souter's account establish the acquisition **option**; the majority's abbreviated “would buy” description does not convert that permission to obligation.

Material ordinance qualifications that assembly must preserve if describing the corresponding provisions:

- Section 3(C) allows source-separated recyclable material to go to facilities within or outside the Town. Acceptable waste may initially go to authorized recycling centers, but unrecycled residue must go to the designated Town facility. That does not establish unconditional permission to operate any recycling center.
- Section 3(B)'s licensed-carter requirement excepts construction and demolition debris and also preserves the exceptions the chapter and rules provide. This licensing question is not independently adjudicated here.
- Acceptable waste excludes hazardous waste, pathological waste, and sludge under §1. Section 4(A), however, permits the Town Board by resolution to provide for disposal of sewer sludge generated by a municipal sewer system or the Rockland County sewer district at a facility within the Town; §4(B) preserves that exception. Do not turn the definition into an absolute holding forbidding all sludge disposal in town.
- Section 5(A)'s exception for outside acceptable waste requires disposal at a Town facility **pursuant to agreement with the Town**; its separate exception permits recyclables at specially permitted recycling centers. Section 5(B)'s import-and-dump/mixture-concealment restriction does not apply to Town composting of acceptable waste. These are different provisions and different exceptions.
- Section 8 expressly preserves remaining portions when a portion is invalidated. Reversal of the presented flow-control enforcement ground does not automatically invalidate the entire chapter, the separate one-transfer-station zoning law, or lawful independent health, permitting, dumping, or recycling rules.

O'Connor's historical discussion adds a concrete statutory-authorization answer worth preserving as her permissible separate ground: RCRA §§6941, 6943(a)(2), 6943(a)(5), and 6948(d)(3)(C) encourage environmentally sound planning, contracts, and EPA assistance; their references do not unmistakably authorize otherwise unconstitutional flow control. Section 6943(a)(5) addresses state or local legal impediments to long-term supply and operation contracts. Section 6948(d)(3)(C) authorizes EPA assistance concerning institutional obstacles, not an express delegation to localities to displace Commerce Clause limits. The quoted House Report does not itself enact the omitted constitutional authorization; 40 C.F.R. §256.42(h) encourages free and unrestricted movement across state and local boundaries. This ground supplies no broad ruling that Congress is unable to authorize flow control.

O'Connor's discussion of a possible conflict with New Jersey's return-of-residue requirement and Souter's rebuttal were both inspected. They are competing accounts and no additional adjudicated conflict finding is supplied by the frozen neutral record. Her existing Pike commitment can stand on the compelled processing and financing burden without pretending that a proven unavoidable interjurisdictional conflict is an undisputed fact. This is a bounded ground selection, not a change in judgment or general Pike framework.
