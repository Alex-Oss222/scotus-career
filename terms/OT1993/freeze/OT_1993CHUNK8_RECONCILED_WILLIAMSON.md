## Stable record and operative components

The reviewed judgment is the Eleventh Circuit's affirmance without opinion, reported at 981 F.2d 1262. The reported district ruling found unavailability, penal adversity and corroboration under Harrell; it does not establish that the judge assessed every material assertion separately. No appellate rationale is invented.

Harris was stopped with approximately nineteen kilograms of cocaine. His successive accounts admitted transportation but assigned the drugs and arrangements to Williamson; he recanted the Cuban, note and dumpster details after Walton began arranging a controlled delivery. Walton promised to report cooperation, denied promising a reward, and described Harris's fear and refusal to record or sign. The quantity, shifting account, cooperation setting and division of responsibility are the common record for the competing applications. His refusal to testify despite use immunity, an order and contempt establishes Rule 804(a)(2) unavailability. It does not establish truth or the exception's adversity condition.

The then-operative Rule 804(b)(3) requires that, when made, the statement be so contrary to the declarant's pecuniary or proprietary interest, so tend to subject the declarant to civil or criminal liability, or so tend to invalidate the declarant's claim against another that a reasonable person in that position would not have made it unless believing it true. The express second sentence requires corroborating circumstances clearly indicating trustworthiness for a statement tending to expose the declarant to criminal liability and offered to exculpate the accused. It does not expressly impose a prosecution-side corroboration requirement. Harrell, 788 F.2d at 1526–1527, and Taggart, 944 F.2d at 840–841, supply the distinct circuit inculpatory-corroboration rule applied below. Whether that rule follows nationwide from Rule 804(b)(3) remains undecided.

The trial prosecutor expressly disclaimed Rule 801(d)(2)(E), agreeing that Harris's statements were not in furtherance of the conspiracy. Neither the conspiracy charge nor the general availability of other hearsay exceptions restores that abandoned ground. The official opinion's generic reference to the then-current residual exception, Rule 804(b)(5), is no holding that its independent predicates were established in this case.

The reported willingness to join a mistrial if the trial judge found admission improper shows the prosecutor's assessment at that conditional trial stage. It does not establish a blanket surrender of all appellate harmlessness arguments. ## Exact competing rules and join limits

### Assertion-specific contextual rule — six associates

Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg require each offered assertion to be sufficiently against the declarant's interest in its actual setting. A generally inculpatory narrative cannot carry an independently neutral, noninculpatory or self-serving assertion into Rule 804(b)(3). The rule does not parse isolated words mechanically: apparently neutral details can expose the declarant's criminal role, reveal significant incriminating knowledge, locate incriminating evidence or connect the declarant to a conspiracy. Naming another participant is neither automatically sufficient nor automatically disqualifying.

The common explanation is the Rule's own reasonable-person reliability premise. An actual exposure to penal liability can support reliability; simple proximity to another assertion that supplies exposure cannot. The ordinary-language possibility that a statement means an entire account is real, but the Rule's underlying condition favors the narrower unit. The Advisory note's related-statement passage and its reference to McCormick are considered together with its express custody and favor-seeking warning. The six do not need a categorical rule about the evidentiary weight of all Advisory notes. Lee's warning about selective unreliability in accomplice accusations supports careful application; neither Lee nor Bruton creates a new categorical evidentiary exclusion here.

These six can join the general rule, its surrounding-circumstances qualification and rejection of automatic other-person exclusion at the same operative level of generality. They cannot all join one conclusion about the admissibility of the Harris statements. A statement that the general rule itself forbids every jointly inculpatory remark, or requires each sentence to recite an element of an offense, loses this coalition.

### Qualified related-statement rule — Kennedy and Thomas

Kennedy and Thomas would first identify a declaration containing a fact against penal interest. Related collateral statements may then be admitted even though they are not independently self-inculpatory, subject to two separate limits. First, a collateral statement must be excluded when it is so self-serving as to be unreliable, including blame allocation tending to reduce the declarant's charges or punishment. Second, when the circumstances make a significant motivation to obtain favorable treatment likely, as with an explicit offer of leniency in exchange for an admission, the entire declaration must be excluded. That second inquiry can disqualify the precise self-inculpatory words as well as collateral assertions. The absence of an express bargain does not automatically admit the account; custody alone does not automatically exclude it either.

Their construction rests on the ambiguity in the unit of statement, the original note's express treatment of related assertions, its reliance on the more limited McCormick approach rather than indiscriminate admission of every fact, and the familiar common-law background acknowledged in the source materials. Meaningful application of the exception is a supporting consideration, not permission to disregard its words. The line between a collateral neutral assertion and one used to obtain leniency requires contextual judicial judgment under Rule 104(a). They favor remand for that fact-sensitive assessment with appropriate discretion for the district judge.

The original provisional description of Kennedy's path must not be read to require that a collateral statement be literally inseparable from the disserving words, or itself increase penal exposure. The reconciled path requires the relation and safeguards just stated. Its strongest objection is precisely that a neutral accusation can be false without increasing the speaker's exposure; Kennedy and Thomas answer with the note, background and two exclusions, while the other six reject that answer. This is a genuine difference in the selected evidentiary rule, not a different description of the same narrow rule. The competing path and source note were already presented neutrally; this clarification adds no new factual premise or previously absent lawful alternative.
