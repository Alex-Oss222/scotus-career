## 15. Albright v. Oliver, No. 92-833

### Neutral verification and entering law

All nine seated Justices participate. Review is from dismissal under Rule 12(b)(6); the allegations of an unreliable informant, deliberate misuse of authority and baseless charge are not trial findings. Albright surrendered, posted bond, and needed permission to leave Illinois. The prosecution ended by dismissal for failure to charge an offense, not by acquittal. The complaint initially included a Fourth Amendment claim but abandoned it in the district court and did not renew it here. The presented merits theory is substantive due process; neither the separate interstate-travel theory nor a challenge to the general adequacy of Illinois procedures is presented. Municipal attribution and qualified-immunity alternatives below do not become findings against the individual-capacity merits theory under review. The state malicious-prosecution claim was dismissed without prejudice.

Inherited rules: [Voluntary public employment and substantive workplace safety duties](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#voluntary-public-employment-and-substantive-workplace-safety-duties), effective Collins, February 26, 1992, requires identification of an actual constitutional deprivation and preserves different affirmative-injury claims. [Possessory seizures and the claimant's own Fourth Amendment interest](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#possessory-seizures-and-the-claimants-own-fourth-amendment-interest), present operation effective May 3, 1993, comprises Soldal's December 8, 1992 independent-protection rule and Padilla's May 3, 1993 personal-interest rule. [Individual-capacity liability for official conduct under §1983](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#individual-capacity-liability-for-official-conduct-under-1983), effective Hafer, November 5, 1991, preserves personal defenses and does not create the substantive right. None decides constitutional malicious prosecution.

James Daniel Good, December 13, 1993, holds that a Fourth Amendment warrant does not itself satisfy the independent Fifth Amendment requirement of notice and meaningful process before civil seizure of real property, absent the specified exigency showing. Its published majority includes Kennedy, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. It preserves separately applicable protections; it neither establishes generalized substantive due process for prosecution nor makes one constitutional provision exclusive of every other. Graham v. Connor's specific-source treatment of excessive force and Gerstein's distinction between restraint and prosecution remain available at their proper scope.

### Enhanced alternatives

Path A affirms dismissal of the presented substantive theory. Arrest, compelled appearance and pretrial restraints have a specific Fourth Amendment source; reputational injury and prosecution anxiety alone do not create a freestanding substantive right to prosecution only on probable cause. The unpresented Fourth Amendment claim is reserved. A does not endorse the Seventh Circuit's categorical palpable-consequence formulation or declare that release on bond always ends a seizure. Its strongest objection is that deliberately initiating a baseless prosecution can inflict affirmative injury beyond a particular arrest, and Soldal/Good reject constitutional exclusivity. The sufficient narrow answer is that these alleged prosecution-related injuries do not establish an independent substantive guarantee; the specific seizure claim and any properly presented procedural claim remain distinct.

Path B reverses the categorical dismissal and remands for a substantive-due-process claim confined to deliberate, arbitrary misuse of criminal process against an innocent person. Its strongest obstacle is the risk of making common-law malicious prosecution automatically constitutional and evading the chosen Fourth Amendment source. B requires an affirmative, arbitrary deprivation rather than negligence, embarrassment or mere failure of prosecution, and leaves qualified immunity, causation and municipal attribution unresolved. It is not automatic damages, nor a decision that every prosecution lacking probable cause violates substantive due process.

The state-remedy route is independently limited. Parratt and Hudson concern procedural adequacy for unauthorized deprivations; an adequate state tort remedy cannot defeat an otherwise established substantive constitutional violation or impose a general §1983 exhaustion requirement. Illinois's preliminary probable-cause procedure and malicious-prosecution remedy may support a procedural answer to unauthorized misuse, but do not decide a procedural claim Albright has not presented.

## 16. National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler, No. 92-780

### Neutral verification and entering law

All nine seated Justices participate. Only Delaware Women's Health Organization and Summit Women's Health Organization asserted the RICO claims; NOW did not. No class was certified. The two clinics' allegations of threatened loss of staff and patients and direct injury to their businesses and property provide the reviewed standing basis at the pleading stage. Allegations of force and threats are not findings that respondents obtained property, committed extortion, managed an enterprise or caused recoverable loss.

The dismissal on review concerns §1962(c)'s supposed economic-motive requirement and the derivative §1962(d) dismissal. The Sherman Act ruling and §1962(a) income theory do not automatically return. Sections 1961(1), 1961(4), 1962(c), 1962(d), 1964(c) and Hobbs Act §1951(b)(2) remain distinct. Obtaining another's property is part of the stated extortion definition; the motive issue does not decide that element. The existence of requests for injunctions does not establish private RICO injunctive authority.

[Direct relation between a civil RICO violation and injury](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#direct-relation-between-a-civil-rico-violation-and-injury), effective Holmes, March 24, 1992, requires factual/proximate cause and contextual directness; it rejects an extra racketeering-injury requirement. [Direction of enterprise affairs under §1962(c)](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#direction-of-enterprise-affairs-under-1962c), effective Reves, March 3, 1993, requires some role in directing enterprise affairs, with its lower-rung boundary reserved. [Private-conspiracy deprivation claims under §1985(3)](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#private-conspiracy-deprivation-claims-under-19853), effective Bray, January 13, 1993, supplies no immunity from a different statute and no RICO economic-motive element. Turkette and Sedima govern text-based RICO construction; Claiborne Hardware protects association and advocacy against liability without unlawful attributable conduct.

### Two paths and common sufficient rule

Path A reverses the economic-motive and derivative dismissal, preserving all actual statutory elements. Section 1962(c) and the enterprise definition contain no requirement that either enterprise or predicate conduct be profit-seeking. A noneconomic association can affect interstate commerce, including through the alleged commercial injuries here. The strongest opposing point is RICO's economic vocabulary, antiracketeering purpose and exposure of protected protest to serious sanctions. Those matters require application of commerce, enterprise, pattern, predicate, causation, attribution and constitutional limits; they do not supply an omitted motive element. Path B would infer that extra element from the statute's setting or avoidance. It fails for all eight at the reviewed level: no inescapable constitutional conflict requires distorting the text, and actual speech defenses can be assessed against proved conduct. No Justice equates dissent or membership with racketeering.

## 17. ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB, No. 92-1550

### Neutral verification and applicable law

All nine seated Justices participate. The Board found the third discharge retaliatory, accepted the hearing falsehood, and nevertheless awarded reinstatement and backpay. The presented question is whether purposeful false testimony before the ALJ automatically forfeits those remedies. It does not reopen the actual discharge reason, the antiunion finding or a distinct challenge based on the original lie to the employer. Section 10(c)'s prohibition for an employee actually discharged for cause remains mandatory; the accepted finding here is that this was not the operative discharge reason.

[Nonemployee organizers' access under a nondiscriminatory exclusion policy](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#nonemployee-organizers-access-under-a-nondiscriminatory-exclusion-policy), effective Lechmere, January 27, 1992, restricts Board authority where a controlling judicial statutory construction governs. It supplies no categorical perjury forfeiture rule. Section 10(c), Phelps Dodge, 313 U.S. 177, 194 (1941), and Virginia Electric, 319 U.S. 533, 539–540 (1943), leave remedy selection to the Board within the Act's purposes and judicial review. Precision Instrument, 324 U.S. 806 (1945), supplies the competing concern for integrity of adjudication but arises in equitable judicial relief; it does not amend the Board's public-enforcement statute.

### Enhanced comparison and rows

Path A affirms: purposeful hearing falsehood can be considered and may justify withholding relief, but it does not automatically extinguish §10(c) authority. The Board knowingly considered the lie and reasonably restored the status quo after an accepted retaliatory discharge. The strongest counterargument is that reinstatement rewards perjury and degrades adjudication. A answers that the statute serves the public labor policy as well as the worker, the remedy repairs the unlawful discharge rather than compensating the false testimony, and ordinary criminal or other authorized sanctions remain distinct. Path B would apply an automatic unclean-hands bar or require vacatur for inadequate consideration. The categorical branch lacks statutory authority; the case-specific branch would require an actual failure of reasoned consideration, not mere disagreement with the remedy. Neither is established on this narrowed record. This analysis does not find criminal perjury by every discredited witness.

## 18. Department of Revenue of Oregon v. ACF Industries, No. 92-74

### Neutral verification and entering law

All nine seated Justices participate. Eight car-leasing companies challenge exemptions under §11503(b)(4), not rates or ratios under (b)(1)–(3). The district court's 31.4 percent and the Ninth Circuit's at-least-25-percent calculation are different judicial calculations; neither is a statutory cutoff. Timber's severance tax and vehicles' registration charges cannot be ignored on any merits comparison. The Ninth Circuit directed total exemption. Threshold coverage, discriminatory treatment and relief are separate questions.

The operative §11503(a)(4) comparison definition excludes agricultural/timber land and covers specified property subject to a tax levy. Subsections (b)(1), (b)(2), and (b)(3) separately govern assessment ratios, collection and rates. Subsection (b)(4) covers another tax that discriminates; the original §306(1)(d) wording and 1978 no-substantive-change direction remain relevant. No prior simulated holding resolves the exemptions question. Wrigley and Kraft retain their own statutory and constitutional tax questions; neither supplies an automatic 4-R exemption. Burlington Northern, 481 U.S. 454 (1987), construes assessment challenges and rejects an extra intentional-discrimination requirement there; it does not itself decide exemptions.

### Enhanced alternatives

Path A reverses the (b)(4) exemption judgment. The specific property-tax protections select taxable comparison property. Treating every differential exemption as a catchall violation would defeat that choice and extend the statute beyond its coordinated property-tax design. The strongest objection is that a broad residual prohibition exists precisely to catch burdens escaping the first three paragraphs and that a State can use exemptions to isolate railroads. The answer is limited: ordinary exemptions within a generally applicable property tax differ from a tax falling only on railroads or a small isolated class. The latter possibility is reserved, not declared lawful. No finding establishes such a scheme here. No general statement that (b)(4) can never reach a tax labeled a property tax is necessary.

Path B treats exemption discrimination as cognizable under the broad residual text. Exclusion of untaxed property from the ratio comparison in (b)(1) does not expressly exempt every differential exemption from (b)(4). Its strongest objection is the conflict with the specific comparison design and the risk of federal equalization of every state exemption. B answers that a claimant must still establish unjustified discriminatory treatment; real differences and substitute taxes may justify categories. Thus cognizability does not itself sustain the Ninth Circuit's total exemption or supply a new percentage test. The narrower B remedy is vacatur and remand for an actual discrimination assessment and justified relief.

This difference affects both the legal rule and remand and must not be hidden by grouping all votes as generic remand votes.
