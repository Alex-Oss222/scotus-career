## Supported propositions and reservations

1. **Private preclusion:** all eight associates support the limited §1738 proposition above. Florida's validation remains legally operative; its reservation of a later factual challenge is part of the judgment's effect, not a federal refusal to respect it. *Marrese*, 470 U.S. 373, 380–386; *Allen*, 449 U.S. 90, 94–105. Growe's respect for an operative replacement plan does not create an enlarged preclusion rule or invalidate amended new-plan pleadings.
2. **United States' original suit:** all eight support the limited Rooker/Feldman distinction. *Rooker*, 263 U.S. 413, 415–416, and *Feldman*, 460 U.S. 462, 482–488, prohibit lower-federal appellate review of a state judicial decision, not this independent enforcement suit by a nonparty. No pronouncement on every possible privity, intervention or nonparty case is adopted.
3. **Section 2 proof and maximization:** Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg support the complete rule that the Gingles predicates establish necessary causal conditions, not an automatic statutory violation. The claimant must show under the totality that the challenged arrangement leaves protected citizens less opportunity than other members of the electorate to participate and elect their chosen representatives. Feasibility of an additional majority-minority district does not itself require its creation. A sufficiently large compact minority, political cohesion and majority bloc voting ordinarily defeating its preferred candidate remain necessary for this fragmentation claim, with the established special-circumstances qualification such as an unopposed minority candidate. No discriminatory-purpose requirement is added.
4. **Proportionality:** those same six support treating the relationship between a group's population share and the share of districts affording effective opportunity to elect its preferred candidates as relevant evidence within the totality. It is not actual officeholding by persons of a particular race, a guarantee of proportional election, an entitlement to proportional districts, an affirmative defense shifting the statutory burden, or a conclusive safe harbor. The absence of proportionality likewise does not alone establish a violation. Context, district-specific discrimination and other evidence can defeat an inference of equal opportunity despite aggregate proportionality. A State cannot knowingly pack or fragment particular voters and invoke compensating opportunities elsewhere as automatic immunity.
5. **House application:** those six find the proof insufficient here, even taking the disputed feasible additional districts in the challengers' favor. Effective opportunity is already roughly proportional under the tried local frames. The discrimination and polarization evidence is serious but does not turn the difference between nine and eleven possible Hispanic districts into unequal opportunity by itself. The court's liability rationale and refusal of a ten-district remedy treated an additional feasible district as a statutory requirement. Its recitation of split-neighborhood testimony is not an express finding of different districting standards for comparable minority and nonminority neighborhoods. No universal selective-treatment prerequisite is adopted. The conclusion is the sufficiency of this record after correcting the legal standard, not a declaration that past discrimination vanished or a reversal of supported historical and voting findings.
6. **Senate application:** those six find the same totality failure for the appealed demand for an additional Hispanic district. They reject the supposed obligation to maximize Hispanic and Black majority districts separately and then choose which proved violation to leave unremedied. Three effective Hispanic districts and one Black-majority district in the seven-district area are roughly proportional to the respective VAP shares; the additional district in which Black voters can elect their preferred candidate with crossover support is also a relevant fact. It is not recast as a Black-majority district or a newly guaranteed coalition/influence entitlement. The five wholly-Dade and broader South Florida accounts remain distinct. The Senate judgment can stand on absent statutory injury rather than an excuse for nonremedy.
7. **Geography and population limits:** those six use the case actually tried. The United States' proposed statewide affirmative-defense measure does not justify deciding an untried statewide claim or disregarding the local record. No universal rule selects county, statewide, district, total population, VAP or citizen-VAP as the required frame for all cases. The first Gingles predicate and citizenship dispute are assumed favorably for this decision, not resolved. A preservation dispute need not be decided because proportionality is relevant totality evidence, not the State's proposed conclusive new defense. No general Rule 8(c) ruling follows.

Thomas and Scalia support the two procedural propositions and the judgments but **do not join propositions 3–7 as governing dilution doctrine**. Their contrary statutory construction is the sufficient merits ground described below. Agreement that there is no right to maximization or proportional representation is not a join in the six-Justice framework. No fractured rationale or Marks combination is needed or justified at this stage; the eventual Court coalitions await assembly.

## Enhanced comparisons — common lawful paths

These are concise legal conclusions and support, not deliberation transcripts. Each Justice's rows below test the same materially available alternatives with that Justice's own relevant public positions.

**Private preclusion paths.** A: give the state validation its expressly limited preclusive scope and hear preserved factual Section 2 claims. B: treat the plan-wide validation as conclusive because the private challengers could present objections in the state proceeding. B has the strong finality and state-authority interests recognized in Allen, Marrese and Growe, but it fails here because it extends the validation beyond its own reservation. No exception to §1738 need be invented. If the reservation's scope became disputed beyond the supplied facts, the state opinion would require direct targeted recovery before enlarging the holding.

**United States jurisdiction paths.** A: hear a nonparty's independent original enforcement action. B: characterize any request invalidating a court-approved plan as impermissible appellate review. B draws force from the practical effect on a state-approved plan and Feldman's application to intertwined claims, but the United States neither lost a state judicial proceeding nor asks the District Court to reverse an adjudication of its own rights. The federal suit tests the enactment's compliance with federal law. A does not erase ordinary preclusion, authorize collateral appeal by a state litigant, or require classifying every state validation function as legislative rather than judicial.

**House merits paths.** A: reverse the maximization-based liability determination and its remedy on this sufficient record, considering rough proportional opportunity within the totality and assuming the contested first predicate. B: affirm because the trial court found history, polarized voting, specific fragmentation and eleven feasible districts, or vacate and remand for a new totality assessment under a corrected standard. B's strongest support is Gingles's clear-error review and Stevens's writing, joined by Blackmun, resisting conclusive proportional-success rules. A's strongest objection is the danger of replacing local findings with an aggregate ratio. The selected A must therefore address all those findings, reject a conclusive ratio, and state that neither the supposed requirement of eleven districts nor the testimonial descriptions supplies the missing statutory showing here. It does not reverse a finding that the court never made or impose a new intent burden. If an assembly draft relies only on arithmetic, Blackmun and Stevens' joins fail; a remand or narrower correction would then need renewed testing.

**Senate paths.** A: affirm no additional relief because the full record fails to establish the claimed unequal opportunity. B: remand to cure violations the lower opinion found, rejecting its invocation of mutually exclusive remedies as an excuse. B is compelling if a violation is first established; Holder requires workable relief for a proved violation. It does not survive the corrected liability inquiry here. The court cannot infer an injury solely from separate feasible Hispanic and Black maximizations, nor treat the lack of a plan satisfying both aspirations as proof that federal rights conflict. Both groups' actual opportunities and the demonstrated crossover district matter. A leaves no new right to a crossover district, no automatic approval of race-conscious boundaries and no Senate constitutional holding.

**Broader noncoverage path.** Thomas and Scalia maintain the interpretation they published in actual Holder: Section 2 protects registration, ballot access, casting and proper counting, not group electoral weight. The strongest objection is the text as construed in Gingles, the 1982 amendment and statutory stare decisis, emphasized by Stevens's Holder concurrence and O'Connor's affirmative coverage writing. They propose rejection of contrary dilution precedents as their sufficient ground; they do not describe their view as existing majority law. Their path changes no ballot-access results test and adds no intent requirement to practices they regard as covered.
