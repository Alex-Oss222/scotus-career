## Substantive supplemental brief, printed pages 1–2 (PDF pages 3–4)

The respondent says that Howell removes dispute about whether Middlebrooks rests on an alternative adequate and independent Tennessee constitutional ground. It identifies Howell's November 10 date and quotes footnote 7 on slip-opinion page 46. It then argues that the state judgment would remain the same under article I, section 16 regardless of a contrary federal Eighth Amendment decision, and requests dismissal for lack of jurisdiction. Those statements describe the respondent's argument; they are not substituted for the attached judicial text.

## Howell judicial text

**State v. Howell, 868 S.W.2d 238 (Tenn. November 10, 1993), Anderson, J., majority footnote 7**, reproduced at slip-opinion page 46 in the filed appendix:

> The decision in Middlebrooks was required by Art. I, § 16 of the Tennessee Constitution. We reviewed federal constitutional law in our analysis to determine whether the duplication also violated the Eighth Amendment to the United States Constitution, but Middlebrooks was decided on separate and independent state constitutional grounds. See Middlebrooks, 840 S.W.2d at 346; Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. at 877, 103 S.Ct. 2742.

The surrounding majority text applies Middlebrooks to the felony-murder aggravator and undertakes harmless-error review in Howell's separate sentencing record. That different defendant's ultimate sentence and facts are not findings in Middlebrooks.

The full published majority and Reid concurrence are separately preserved from [CAP's complete reported opinion](https://static.case.law/sw2d/868/cases/0238-01.json) at `../sources/chunk1/howell_868sw2d238.json` and `.txt`. The effect of the clarification and the meaning of the whole opinion require independent legal assessment; no disposition is selected here.
