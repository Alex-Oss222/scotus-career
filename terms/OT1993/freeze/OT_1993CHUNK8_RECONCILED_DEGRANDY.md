## Supported propositions, boundaries and remedial grounds

1. **Private preclusion.** Section 1738 accords Florida's validation the scope it has under Florida law, including its express preservation of a later factual Section 2 challenge. An optional return to state court does not become an exhaustion requirement. Allen and Marrese supply ordinary preclusion principles; no Voting Rights Act exemption from preclusion or general right to relitigate a completed state adjudication is created.
2. **United States' original suit.** Rooker and Feldman bar lower-federal appellate review of a state judgment against a losing state litigant. The United States was not a party to the validation and brings its first original enforcement suit against the plan. This narrow conclusion does not decide every nonparty, privity, intervention or intertwined-claim scenario and does not authorize review of preclearance.
3. **Necessary causal conditions and ultimate burden.** Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg support the distinction between necessary Gingles conditions and sufficient proof of unequal opportunity under the statutory totality. A feasible additional compact majority district, political cohesion and ordinarily defeating majority bloc voting do not automatically establish liability. The established special-circumstances qualification, such as an unopposed minority candidate, remains; discriminatory purpose is not required. The claimants retain their statutory burden. Thomas and Scalia do not join this dilution framework.
4. **No maximization.** The same six reject an entitlement to every feasible additional majority-minority district. This rule supplies Kennedy's sufficient merits ground here. The question remains equal opportunity to participate and elect chosen representatives; neither a preferred map nor a possible additional district answers it. Thomas and Scalia reject the claim at the anterior coverage stage and are not added to a shared dilution rule.
5. **Proportionality's evidentiary role.** Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg adopt the contextual rule: the relationship between effective opportunity districts and the group's share of the relevant population is relevant within the totality; its significance varies with other evidence. It is neither a guarantee of proportional officeholding, a racial floor or ceiling, a burden-shifting affirmative defense, nor a conclusive safe harbor. Disproportionality alone likewise does not establish liability. Kennedy expressly accepts those limited propositions in his own statutory understanding; his assent may count only at that exact level, not as a whole-opinion join in the full application or every supporting rationale. Proportional district opportunity, the race of elected officials and actual electoral outcomes remain distinct. O'Connor is prepared to emphasize that relevant proportionality evidence must be considered, but no universal geographic or population denominator follows.
6. **House application — five full adherents.** Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg conclude that the tried record does not establish the asserted unequal opportunity once the maximization premise is removed. They accept the serious findings of polarization and continuing discriminatory effects. They assume the disputed first Gingles predicate favorably, consider the nine existing effective districts in the reported local frames, and address the specific neighborhood testimony. The lower court did not find that comparable minority and other neighborhoods received inconsistent districting standards. Descriptions of divided or concentrated neighborhoods, even if credited, do not establish an entitlement to the two additional districts in this record. This is not a universal selective-treatment prerequisite or intent requirement. The House liability finding is infected by an erroneous legal standard; Rule 52(a) protects supported findings, but does not protect that error. The dependent Plan 268 substitution falls without reviewing its alternative remedial merits or choosing a replacement map. Kennedy joins reversal on the narrower no-maximization ground, not this full affirmative proportionality application.
7. **Senate application — five full adherents.** Those five affirm the operative no-additional-relief judgment because the corrected totality inquiry does not establish the claimed unequal opportunity, not because a violation may stand unremedied. The seven-district area's three effective Hispanic districts, one Black-majority district and a further proven Black electoral opportunity with crossover support are assessed in context. The crossover district is not renamed a second Black-majority district or made a statutory entitlement. Both groups' histories and actual opportunities matter; their separate cohesion does not establish cohesion between them. The lower opinion's assumption that each possible additional district must be supplied cannot establish two competing statutory injuries. Kennedy joins the Senate result through rejection of that maximization premise while reserving the full application. No separate Black appeal is created by explaining the lower court's remedial dilemma.
8. **First predicate, geographic scope and preservation.** The same six may assume the first predicate without resolving the citizenship dispute, the population denominator, influence-claim cognizability or adaptation of the majority-size requirement. They use the case as actually tried. The United States' proposed statewide affirmative-defense approach does not require disregarding the local evidence or deciding an untried statewide claim. Because proportionality is part of the statutory totality, no general Rule 8(c) or waiver rule is needed. Do not announce a universal local or statewide frame.
9. **Thomas and Scalia's distinct construction.** They read Section 2's protected practices as registration, ballot access, casting and proper counting, not the allocation of electoral weight through districting. They would reject contrary dilution constructions in Gingles and the incompatible parts of Growe and Voinovich, despite their earlier joins. Actual Holder already records that position. The results test remains for covered practices; their view does not add an intent requirement to those practices. It is a proposed contrary construction, not current majority law or a narrower version of the five associates' totality rule. Vacatur and dismissal, rather than approval of the Senate plan under dilution doctrine, follows.

No Marks aggregation combines noncoverage with totality or Kennedy's reservations into a new rule. Exact controlling force, authorship and publication form await assembly. No proposed historical author has been assigned.
