## Posture and statutory scope

Argument October 13, 1993; event April 26, 1994. Sixth Circuit merits decision August 24, 1992; October 13, 1992 is denial of rehearing, not its merits date. Rivers and Davison were discharged September 26, 1986 and sued in February 1987. Patterson preceded their trial. The district court dismissed their §1981 claims, then ruled for Roadway at a Title VII bench trial. The Sixth Circuit rejected retrospective §101 coverage but reversed dismissal of the narrower racially motivated retaliation-for-contract-enforcement theory and remanded for a jury. Harvis's separate case and the union/LMRA matters are outside this review.

Section 101 redesignates the former §1981 text as subsection (a), adds subsection (b)'s definition extending making/enforcement to making, performance, modification, termination, and enjoyment of all contractual benefits, privileges, terms and conditions, and adds subsection (c)'s protection against nongovernmental discrimination and impairment under color of State law. The temporal issue concerns the expanded coverage relevant to the broader discharge claim, not a separate challenge to private-contract coverage.

Section 402(a) makes the Act effective upon enactment except as otherwise specifically provided. Section 402(b) separately excludes disparate-impact cases whose complaints were filed before March 1, 1975 AND whose initial decisions were rendered after October 30, 1983. Section 109(c)'s post-enactment limitation concerns its extraterritorial amendments. These different provisions are not conflated. Section 3(4) states a purpose of responding to recent decisions by expanding relevant civil-rights statutes. None expressly says that §101 governs preenactment conduct or all pending cases.
