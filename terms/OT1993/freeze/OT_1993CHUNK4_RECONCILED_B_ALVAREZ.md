## Result and decisive rule

All eight non-Stone Justices support **reversal of the Ninth Circuit judgment and remand for further proceedings consistent with the narrow federal-presentment rule**. A confession made while the defendant is actually held solely on state charges does not trigger §3501(c)'s federal-presentment period. The statute addresses delay in performing an obligation to present a person to an officer empowered to commit defendants charged with federal or D.C. offenses. That obligation does not arise merely because an officer knows or suspects a possible federal crime. “Any” law-enforcement officer includes state or local officers holding a person on the relevant federal basis; it does not turn all state-only custody into federal detention. Actual detention controls, not the officer's title or an untested label.

Here the case reaches the Court on the state-only-custody premise: the confession preceded the later federal arrest. Ordinary notification of the discovery of counterfeit currency does not by itself establish an improper federal-state holding arrangement. The later failure to arraign or prosecute the state charges does not retrospectively convert all earlier custody into federal detention. The district court found no evidence of collusion; that is an identified lower finding, not a new Supreme Court factual finding. The Ninth Circuit's aggregation of state-only detention into federal presentment delay was legally mistaken. Its alternative subsection (b) delay analysis did not supply an independent, correctly premised coercion basis to affirm.

Section 3501(c)'s protection is against inadmissibility **solely because of presentment delay** and requires the trial judge's voluntariness finding, leaving weight to the jury, and a confession within six hours of the relevant arrest or detention. Its proviso removes that time limitation when the judge finds the delay beyond six hours reasonable considering the means of transportation and distance to the nearest available committing officer. It is not a general exception for docket congestion or administrative convenience. Subsection (b)'s nonexclusive voluntariness factors, subsection (a)'s admissibility text, and subsection (d)'s distinct treatment of voluntary noninterrogated or noncustodial statements must not be conflated with that proviso.

## Alternatives, limits, and mandate

The strongest opposing construction relies on “any” officer and concern that state detention may evade federal presentment. Context answers the first concern: delay presupposes a relevant duty. Anderson v. United States supplies the serious working-arrangement concern. The Court does not decide §3501's effect, if any, on Anderson's rule, or define every arrangement that makes actual custody federal. It cannot treat state labels as conclusive or presume the problem imaginary. The strongest alternative affirmance ground would require an established independent legal basis; the record supplies neither a preserved Gerstein/McLaughlin claim nor an independent coercion finding sustaining this judgment.

Reserve the general relation of subsections (a), (b), and (c), especially the admissibility of a confession obtained more than six hours after a federal arrest. Do not decide §3501's entire relationship with Miranda. Reversal and remand remove the erroneous statutory-delay basis; they do not themselves order categorical admission, automatic reinstatement of conviction, a new trial, acquittal, release, or adjudication of the unpreserved Fourth Amendment alternative. No further Stansbury dependency or neutral-source blocker remains.
