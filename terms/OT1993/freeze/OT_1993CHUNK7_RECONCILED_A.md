### Exact additional instruction position of Souter and Stevens

On their independent Eighth Amendment ground, a capital defendant may demand a judicial explanation of a legal term describing the available sentencing alternatives when there is a reasonable likelihood that a juror will misunderstand it. A death sentence after refusal of that required request cannot stand. This entitlement does not depend on the State's having argued future dangerousness. Because counsel's advocacy does not generally have the authoritative force of the court's statement of law, permission for defense argument alone does not answer this separate ground. Here the requested meaning of life was fixed by state law, and the express parole inquiry made the missing legal explanation concrete. The trigger is a request and a reasonable likelihood of misunderstanding, not an automatic duty to catalogue every release contingency in every capital trial.

Their support rests on the already presented heightened-reliability/meaningful-choice path, the actual Graham, Johnson, and Romano positions, and the surviving Woodson, Gregg, Beck, and Boyde principles discussed by their historical concurrence at 512 U.S. 172–174. The historical writing is comparator evidence, not a new in-world holding of those cases. No changed premise supports reducing these Justices to a mere reservation. This correction preserves an independently sufficient position of two Justices without claiming broader support, choosing an author, or predeciding whether assembly calls for a separate writing.

### Two sufficient affirmance grounds and competing interpretation

**Modification ground:** In the context of §203, modification permits limited alteration, including meaningful changes to form, contents, timing, and place of filing, and potentially deferral or waiver in limited circumstances. It does not authorize replacing obligatory tariff filing for the whole nondominant long-distance category with a regime in which those carriers may choose not to file. The importance of the filed-tariff mechanism, the limiting verb, the universal command with its express exception, and the 120-day ceiling establish that statutory boundary. Chevron does not require acceptance of a construction outside the meaning the section can bear. This is a construction of the identified statute, not a general rule forbidding every substantial agency action or an economy-wide clear-statement doctrine.

**Independent general-order condition:** Even if the scope of modify could encompass the change, this general order's exemption of every long-distance carrier but AT&T, covering approximately forty percent of customers, does not concern special circumstances or conditions within this section's bounded delegation. The sector's breadth is evidence of the order's ordinary regulatory reach, not a fixed forty-percent cutoff or a numerical definition of special. The condition cannot be treated as satisfied merely by redescribing the broad substitute regulatory system as nondominance. This ground leaves real authority for qualifying general orders and does not decide what smaller or differently conditioned waiver would qualify.

**Competing ground of Blackmun, Stevens, and Souter:** Any requirement and the express general-order route give the Commission latitude to adapt filing to the special condition of competition and lack of market power. Nondominance can qualify by kind, not rarity. The permissive rule retains substantive rate duties, enforcement, voluntary tariffs, and reimposition; these Justices regard it as an authorized accommodation rather than replacement of the statutory objective. They reject both the restrictive modification construction and the claim that the general-order condition independently invalidates this order. The 120-day limit restricts increasing a burden; they do not read it as a complete measure of the power to relax filing. Good cause, an actual qualifying condition, and reasons belonging to the agency remain necessary.

## 75. West Lynn Creamery, Inc. v. Healy, No. 93-141

### Comparator and reconciliation of the competing rationales

The historical Court reversed, 7–2. Stevens's opinion was joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg. Scalia concurred in the judgment with Thomas; Rehnquist dissented with Blackmun. The official report's final operative sentence reverses the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court, 512 U.S. 207; it does not contain a separate bespoke refund or remedial command. The frozen shorthand reverse/remand means reversal followed by the appropriate further state proceedings, not a claim that the historical report expressly ordered a particular remand remedy.

The seven non-Stone reversal positions and Blackmun's affirmance remain supported. The actual Oregon Waste, Carbone, and Lohman decisions do not displace the relevant historical classification forks. O'Connor's Carbone Pike position and Souter's municipal-service dissent do not compel the same characterization of this program, which expressly distributes to the local producer class rather than selecting a single municipal service provider. Blackmun's prior public-program approach supports his contrary classification here but does not furnish proprietary immunity where the State buys no milk.

The broad common factual account must preserve the amended February 26 order, the uniform assessment on dealer sales to Massachusetts retailers, exclusive Massachusetts producer distributions, the 200,000-pound producer cap, the $15 combined-return ceiling, and return of surplus to dealers. The premium is one-third of the gap between $15 per hundredweight and the federal blend price, multiplied by covered sales in corresponding hundredweight units. Dealer payors and producer recipients are not the same persons. Declining production and the lower court's rejection of a demonstrated output increase remain record facts. Protection against interstate competition can be disputed without inventing an increase in milk volume or a precise incidence calculation.

Five Justices support examining the linked assessment and local-only payout as an integrated discriminatory mechanism. The State cannot use the formally even collection stage to insulate the dedicated geographic preference; preservation of a local dairy industry is not itself a sufficient justification, and the asserted supply, open-land, and emergency purposes have not been shown to lack adequate reasonable nondiscriminatory means. This is discrimination review, not ordinary Pike balancing. The conclusion concerns this assessment-and-fund program; it does not establish that every measure improving a local competitor's position is invalid.

Scalia and Thomas support a narrower tax/subsidy category: a nondiscriminatory industry tax whose revenue is segregated and paid to only local members of that industry is materially equivalent to a discriminatory tax exemption or credit. Their support must not be aggregated into a generalized principle forbidding any state measure that offsets an out-of-state cost advantage. The comparator confirms a further distinction understated by the first freeze: they would allow a domestic-industry subsidy paid from general revenues raised through nondiscriminatory taxation. That is an affirmative separate position, not merely their reservation of the question. The five broader reversal Justices need not adopt that safe harbor in deciding this fund.
