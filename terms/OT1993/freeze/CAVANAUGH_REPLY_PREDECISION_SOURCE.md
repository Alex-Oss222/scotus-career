ARGUMENT 
I. The United States Supreme Court's jurisdiction is 
unaffected by the South Carolina Supreme Court's 
decision in Griffin v. State, Op. No. 23909 (July 19, 
1993) because the South Carolina Supreme Court 
based its decision upon federal law as interpreted 
by the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth 
Circuit. 
The decision in Griffin v. State is based upon federal 
law: 
The Fourth Circuit’s analysis is compelling. It is 
difficult to determine where the difference lies 
between a review once every two years and 
once every eight years. This gray area tortures 
the ex post facto analysis between a change in the 
standards for review and a procedural change in 
timing. The Akins Court, [922 F.2d 1558 (11th 
Cir. 1991), cert. denied, 111 S.Ct. 2915 (1991)] was 
faced with a statute that provided for a pro- 
cedural change which effectively changed the 
standards for parole. We must now acknow!l- 
edge that where a procedural rule is so overly
