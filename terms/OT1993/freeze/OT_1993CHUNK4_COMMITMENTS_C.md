## Carbone: operative issue and lawful alternatives

No. 92-1402; decision event May 16, 1994; argument December 7, 1993. All eight non-Stone Justices participate. The relevant judgment is the state enforcement judgment maintained by the Second Department's August 31, 1992 affirmance of the September 16, 1991 reargument order. The proposed reversal positions concern the Commerce Clause enforcement ruling and a remand to implement that ruling; they do not automatically reopen separate environmental-review, taking, zoning or state-law conclusions.

**Discrimination path.** A town may not compel private waste handlers to purchase a designated local private facility's processing service, excluding competing interstate processing services, unless it establishes a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve. Dean Milk and Fort Gratiot establish that excluding some local competitors does not remove the interstate preference. New Energy rejects a minimum-number-of-favored-firms requirement. The ordinance governs Carbone's independent transactions rather than merely selecting the Town's own counterparty; Boston Harbor and Reeves therefore do not make the routing command proprietary purchasing. Health standards and lawful nondiscriminatory funding remain available. The actual record does not establish that processing all residue at this private facility is necessary for safe disposal. Revenue needed to satisfy the Town's own minimum-tonnage arrangement cannot alone justify excluding equivalent outside services. Reverse and remand concerning the routing requirement.

**Strongest objection to that path.** The ordinance neither charges differently by origin nor favors a class of private local competitors: it selects one operator to carry out a municipal disposal responsibility following closure of an unsafe public landfill, gives the Town substantial contractual exposure, and permits later public acquisition. Exxon warns that the Clause protects the interstate market, not particular firms or market structures. Treating every exclusive service arrangement as geographic discrimination risks deciding the reserved public-facility question without an appropriate record. A discrimination opinion must identify the actual private processing market and compulsory transactions, and expressly leave materially different public systems open.

**Pike path.** If this municipal-service arrangement is treated as evenhanded rather than origin discrimination, Pike still permits invalidation of a local-processing command whose burden on interstate commerce is clearly excessive in relation to its putative local benefits. Pike's local-packing application is relevant because the Town compels an additional local processing step before otherwise lawful interstate disposition. Safe disposal remains a legitimate interest; unsupported duplication and financing the selected facility are not automatically a sufficient benefit. This path can reverse without resolving whether every exclusive municipal franchise is discriminatory.

**Strongest objection to that path.** The record establishes a real local remediation and disposal program, not only an employment preference; the lower court found no excessive interstate burden. The $11 difference between the two reported tipping fees is not a conclusive measure of social cost. Pike cannot become a judicial requirement that a town choose the least expensive disposal arrangement or exhaust every imaginable funding alternative. A Pike disposition must rest on compelled local processing and the absence of a demonstrated connection between that mandatory detour and an additional disposal benefit, not on fee arithmetic alone.

**Affirmance path.** Treat the arrangement as evenhanded organization of a local public service and sustain it under Pike on this record. The record shows municipal responsibility, a capital-financing guarantee, equal charges regardless of origin, and substantial environmental objectives. The public objective does not prove formal market-participant immunity, but may distinguish this program from reserving local private landfills or subsidizing ordinary local sellers. No categorical exemption for government-connected private firms follows. This path leaves the enforcement judgment in place.

**Strongest objection to affirmance.** Fort Gratiot expressly refuses to convert private-facility regulation into market participation merely because it implements a waste plan. The current operator is private; later acquisition is optional. Carbone's mandatory purchase is imposed by ordinance, not by consent to the Town's contract. Dean Milk and New Energy make the exclusion of other local competitors and the small number of preferred firms poor answers to discrimination. Affirmance therefore requires a carefully limited public-service characterization; it cannot rest on an inaccurate finding of present public ownership or on a general immunity for environmental programs.

## Scope, remedies and proposition discipline

Seven non-Stone Justices provisionally support reversal on the presented Commerce Clause component; Blackmun supports affirmance. Six of the reversing Justices support the discrimination rationale; O'Connor supports the stated Pike ground only. This is a report of independent commitments, not a nine-member Court tally. Stone has not been modeled, added or inferred. No majority opinion, author, final assignment, writing join or published dissent is generated at this stage.

For reversal, return the case for relief consistent with invalidating compulsory routing through this private facility. The scope of the existing injunction must be adjusted to the Commerce Clause holding; neutral dumping, safety, permitting and valid recycling requirements are not automatically invalid. No damages, refund, replacement financing program, unconditional operating permit, forced acceptance of particular waste, or specific mandate issuance date is established. The contract's enforceability between its parties is not decided. Review does not adjudicate the distinct zoning, state environmental-review or taking rulings.

The neutral record establishes no congressional authorization displacing Commerce Clause review. No holding on all publicly owned waste systems is necessary. Financing a lawful public facility is legitimate; the contested question is whether this mandatory private-local processing route is a lawful means. A permitted tax, subsidy, neutral fee or regulation is an example of a potentially adequate alternative, not a command to adopt it and not a finding that every proposed replacement is valid.
