## Historical check and conclusion

Official report511U.S.572 identifies Kennedy's Court opinion joined by Rehnquist, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas; Ginsburg's dissent joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Souter. The judgment affirmed the Sixth Circuit. Primary source: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep511/usrep511571/usrep511571.pdf . The comparator's alignment is confirmed without relying solely on its aggregator link. **All eight independent non-Stone dispositions and their sufficient grounds remain unchanged.** No changed premise justifies replacing any of them. Historical agreement confirms the frozen analysis; it does not import historical authorship, every incidental argument, or an additional ground.

The affirmative side's common rule is confined to §2(11)'s employer-interest requirement: when a nursing home's business is patient care, supervisory authority directed to that care is not excluded from the employer's interest merely because it also serves patients or follows professional norms. A listed supervisory authority and nonroutine independent judgment remain separately necessary.511U.S.573–574,577–580. History expressly reserved the other statutory elements and rested the affirmance on the Board's presented ground, because it sought no alternative theory supporting the order. Id.582–584. This matches, rather than changes, the frozen restriction on an invented independent-judgment salvage ground.

The opposite side's common rule is that Congress's coverage of professionals/technical employees alongside exclusion of supervisors permits the Board's distinction between direction incidental to skilled service and genuine front-line management authority. It does not deny that patient care economically benefits the employer. Id.584–590,594–595. The historical dissent relied on Yeshiva's ordinary professional-duty discussion, the Board's experience across occupations, and the actual ALJ's findings about limited authority. Id.590–594,596–599. Those premises support the model's reverse-and-remand path without authorizing a universal nurse exemption or finding new facts.
