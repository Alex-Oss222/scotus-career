**Material corrections and additions:**

- The Town **may acquire**, rather than necessarily will acquire, the transfer station for $1 after five years. The lower opinion describes a contractual acquisition option; this handoff does not convert permission into a transfer obligation. The current private operation, public financing obligations and future option must remain distinct.
- In August 1989 a consent decree required closure of the Town's landfill and a remedial plan. In January 1990 the Town contracted with Clarkstown Recycling for construction and operation of the replacement transfer station. The Town guaranteed an annual quantity and a penalty for a shortfall. The reported tipping fees were $81 at the designated facility and $70 at Carbone's facility. The latter was a price Carbone could change, not an adjudicated measure of efficient disposal costs.
- The ordinance covers acceptable solid waste **within or generated within** the Town, including waste brought from outside and handled there. It is not limited to waste generated locally. Section 3 requires delivery to the designated facility or an approved recycling center; unrecycled residue at a recycling center must go to the designated facility. Source-separated recyclables may go to facilities within or outside the Town. Sludge and the described hazardous/pathological wastes outside the facility's acceptance category are excluded. No hazardous-waste holding is appropriate.
- Section 5 separately prohibits dumping imported waste on property within the Town; the reported exceptions permit disposal at the designated facility and receipt of recyclables at a specially permitted recycling center. The flow-control question does not require independent invalidation of every dumping rule.
- Section 6 allows fee resolutions; no claim or record finding says the fee varies with waste origin. The discrimination claim concerns access to processing services, not an origin-differentiated tipping charge.
- Carbone processed waste originating both inside and outside New York and shipped residue to other States. The police incident and later observations described in the opinion support an actual interstate transaction, not hypothetical standing.
- The parties stipulated to convert the Town's preliminary-injunction motion into summary judgment. The earlier federal action was discontinued after the state judgment. Its preliminary ruling is not an independent final judgment entering this Court's review.
- The lower court rejected the Commerce Clause claim under Exxon and Pike, emphasizing equal treatment by origin, no demonstrated excessive burden, and health and financing objectives. It also rejected environmental-review standing, police-power and taking/due-process challenges. No direct challenge to the zoning provision permitting one transfer station was presented; no legal-nonconforming-use claim was made; the prior negative environmental declaration was not challenged on appeal. Review is confined to the Commerce Clause flow-control question supplied in the packet. Those distinct rulings are not independently reversed or adopted.

## Entering law actually supplied

1. Chemical Waste Management v. Hunt, June 1, 1992, is unanimous controlling law on origin-based disposal charges: legitimate purpose plus inadequacy of reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives; no new proof quantum, perfect certainty or least-imaginable-alternative requirement. Its unpressed compensatory-tax theory remains unadjudicated there.
2. Fort Gratiot, June 1, 1992, is controlling by eight votes, including Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: exclusion of other intrastate counties does not cure interstate discrimination; planning does not make private-facility regulation market participation. Blackmun's published position sought an evidentiary opportunity before conclusive invalidation. Public-facility practices were reserved.
3. Oregon Waste, April 4, 1994, is effective current-term law. Thomas's opinion was joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, as well as the Chief. It requires separate classification and justification of express waste-origin discrimination, rejects general income taxation as a substantially equivalent compensatory event, and distinguishes regulation of private landfills from government ownership. It does not decide private exclusive franchises or public monopolies. Blackmun's published dissent would sustain the cost-directed charge against the facial attack without finding exact added costs proved.
4. Boston Harbor, March 8, 1993, unanimous, preserves genuine project-specific proprietary contracting, while public ownership or a purchasing label alone cannot insulate regulation of unrelated conduct. It concerns labor preemption and supplies a relevant distinction, not an independently dispositive Commerce Clause holding.
