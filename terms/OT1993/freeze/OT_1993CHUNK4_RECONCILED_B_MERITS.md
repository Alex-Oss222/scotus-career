## Stansbury v. California — final reconciled non-Stone commitments

**Disposition:** Each of Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg supports **reversal of the California Supreme Court judgment and remand**, correcting its use of uncommunicated investigative focus as an independent custody consideration. All eight support the frozen objective-custody ground and bounded remedy. No Stone vote, full-Court tally, author or final published opinion form is supplied.

**Operative rule and limit:** Miranda custody depends on formal arrest or restraint on freedom of movement of the degree associated with formal arrest, assessed from the objective interrogation circumstances as a reasonable person in the interviewee's position would understand them. An officer's uncommunicated view that the person is, or is not, a suspect has no independent role; the interviewee's own undisclosed subjective belief likewise does not control. An officer's words or conduct may make a belief relevant to the extent the communication would affect that objective understanding. Even an express statement that the person is a principal suspect is not automatically custody. Undisclosed views may be evidence bearing on the credibility of an officer's description of what occurred, but are not thereby independent custody factors.

The state court must reconsider custody on the objective circumstances. Neither custody from the outset nor lack of custody is decided. Statement-specific admissibility, derivative evidence, harmlessness and any consequent relief retain their own governing requirements. Reversal does not itself order suppression, a new trial, acquittal, release or capital resentencing.
