## Record, issue divisions, and entering law

The Fifth Circuit enforced two FLRA orders requiring home-address disclosure. The Navy Exchange matter concerns Gulfport; the Army and Air Force Exchange Service is headquartered in Dallas, while that request concerns employees at Lowry Air Force Base. Names and work stations were supplied, addresses withheld. Necessity for collective bargaining and reasonable availability are not challenged. The unions represent members and nonmembers; they already have members' addresses and others voluntarily furnished. They may contact employees at work and request addresses, but that fact does not reopen the conceded necessity determination. No finding of actual harassment or unwanted visits is supplied.

The question divides into three material components:

- **Statutory coordination:** Whether the labor statute's public interest enlarges the public-interest side of the FOIA balance reached through §552a(b)(2).
- **Exemption 6 application:** Whether the relevant privacy interest is outweighed by these disclosures' contribution to public understanding of agency operations, retaining the actual Exemption 6 wording and the agencies' burden to justify withholding.
- **Judgment and scope:** Whether the Fifth Circuit may enforce these orders on the FOIA-exception ground; what remains undecided concerning routine use, consent, bargaining necessity, different records, and other forms of relief.

The labor statute's duty to furnish requested bargaining data is limited to the extent disclosure is not prohibited by law. The covered records are within the Privacy Act's general nondisclosure provision. Section 552a(b)(2) excepts disclosures required under FOIA; §552a(b)(3) separately concerns routine use. Exemption 6 exempts personnel, medical, and similar files when their disclosure would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy. It permits withholding under FOIA; the separate Privacy Act prohibition is why the present disclosure order cannot stand if the asserted FOIA exception fails. No holding that FOIA independently forbids release is authorized.

The simulated Ray rule controls: identifiers may be withheld when their concrete privacy cost outweighs the demonstrated additional contribution to public understanding after substantive information has been released. Its confidentiality assurance was relevant, not dispositive. It leaves derivative investigation potentially relevant and requires no antecedent proof of wrongdoing. Reporters Committee remains pre-divergence authority for the public-understanding interest and ordinary requester neutrality; its Exemption 7(C) privacy threshold is not substituted for Exemption 6's more demanding terms. Its exception concerning a privilege holder's request does not apply to these third-party union requests and is not overruled. Treasury/IRS v. FLRA supplies a separate statutory principle: the FLRA's administration of the labor statute warrants appropriate deference, but does not erase enacted limits. No need to disturb the accepted necessity determination follows from that principle.

No supplied current-term public holding changes this FOIA framework. The current neutral projection's statutory and agency decisions do not independently answer it. Earlier public positions are used only within their actual scope.

## Adversarial comparison of the lawful paths

**Path favoring enforcement.** Read the statutes together in light of §7101(a)'s declared public interest and §7114(a)(1)'s obligation to represent nonmembers. The labor statute generates a restricted disclosure demand, so its statutory purpose could inform what makes an invasion unwarranted. Ordinary home addresses are less sensitive than criminal histories or Ray's identifiable interviews. The Fifth Circuit reasoned that this contextual interpretation would not give a commercial requester the union's labor-law entitlement. Ginsburg's 1989 concurrence supplies substantial support for the policy concern and recognizes the relatively modest privacy interest. A contextual construction could therefore support enforcement without denying any privacy interest.

**Strongest objection to enforcement.** That construction has to alter the cross-reference in §552a(b)(2), which asks whether FOIA requires disclosure. A right confined to requesters invoking another statute is not ordinary FOIA access. Congress's declaration that bargaining serves the public does not expressly amend the FOIA inquiry or create a new Privacy Act exception; §7114(b)(4) expressly preserves disclosure prohibitions. Ray already uses public understanding of government operations in Exemption 6. The textually narrower privacy standard does not itself add a different public-interest category. A labor-policy disagreement with the statutory outcome is not a new factual or legal premise.

**Path favoring reversal.** Apply the FOIA exception on FOIA's own terms. Disclosure of these addresses adds no demonstrated material illumination of agency conduct on the established record. Employees retain a nontrivial interest in controlling official dissemination of where they live, even if particular addresses can be found elsewhere; publication would also make later contact easier. Actual harassment need not be found to recognize disclosure's effect on personal information. Against the absent or negligible demonstrated FOIA contribution here, that privacy cost makes the invasion clearly unwarranted. Because FOIA does not require disclosure and no other Privacy Act exception is presented for decision, the Privacy Act prevents compelling it through the labor statute.

**Strongest objection to reversal.** The privacy interest is modest, Exemption 6 strongly favors disclosure, and Congress affirmatively recognized the public importance of effective bargaining. A generalized fear of nuisance mail cannot replace the actual statutory standard; nor may the Court equate addresses with highly sensitive histories, make all identifying data categorically private, require proof of wrongdoing, or decide that obtaining useful information from identified employees can never further public understanding. Reversal survives these objections only at the bounded level stated above. It does not deny the recognized bargaining value; it holds that value insufficient to establish FOIA-required disclosure through this exception.

## Individual commitments

### Blackmun

**Statutory coordination — result and sufficient ground:** Reject the Fifth Circuit's enlargement of the FOIA public-interest inquiry. His full join in the simulated Ray opinion places him with the public-understanding measure in Exemption 6. His join in Scalia's Treasury/IRS opinion, 494 U.S. 922, confirms that the labor statute's enacted limitations remain enforceable notwithstanding its bargaining purposes. The Privacy Act cross-reference supplies the decisive limit here; no rejection of necessity or of labor bargaining policy is needed.

**Privacy application — result and two-path comparison:** Withhold these addresses under Exemption 6. His Reporters Committee concurrence in the judgment, 489 U.S. 780–781, rejects automatic protection of every record in a broad class and preserves exceptional circumstances supporting disclosure. That writing gives the enforcement path its strongest Blackmun-specific objection: a government employee's address may sometimes bear on public scrutiny, and the privacy interest cannot simply be announced conclusive. His Ray join nevertheless supports reversal where the actual incremental contribution is absent or insufficient. The present record supplies no particular public-accountability use outweighing control over disclosure of a residence. The narrower record-specific ground answers the objection; an unqualified categorical exemption does not.

**Judgment, boundaries, and join conditions:** Reverse both enforcement components on the FOIA route. Join a concrete balance that preserves meaningful public contributions in a different record and does not demand proof of wrongdoing. Do not attribute to him blanket approval of Reporters Committee's categorical reasoning. His earlier Lechmere dissent with White favored administrative accommodation in the distinct private-property access setting; it supplies no power for the FLRA to replace the Privacy Act's exception. Routine use and consent remain open, and necessity remains accepted.

### Stevens

**Statutory coordination — result and sufficient ground:** Apply the ordinary FOIA inquiry and reject the additional labor-policy weight. His own Reporters Committee opinion, 489 U.S. 771–775, ties privacy balancing to illumination of government conduct and makes an ordinary third-party request independent of requester identity. His own simulated Ray opinion applies that public-interest understanding under Exemption 6. A statute-specific access claim is the strongest counterargument, but the labor statute expressly preserves other legal prohibitions and the Privacy Act refers to FOIA-required disclosure, not every socially useful disclosure.

**Privacy application — result and two-path comparison:** Find Exemption 6 satisfied here. Reporters Committee recognizes that prior or scattered availability does not necessarily eliminate an interest in controlling broader dissemination. Ray supports examining what identifiers add to already available information. Unlike Ray's interview identifiers, addresses carry no necessary implication of similarly sensitive personal facts; the interest must be assessed as the home-address interest actually presented. Enforcement could follow if the disclosure materially illuminated agency administration, but this record's established use is improved bargaining communication, not such illumination. The negligible demonstrated FOIA benefit does not outweigh even this less intense privacy invasion.

**Judgment, boundaries, and join conditions:** Reverse the enforcement judgment on the presented exception, reserving routine use and consent. His Treasury/IRS dissent, 494 U.S. 937–938, favored outright reversal under the applicable textual labor-law limits rather than disregarding them. His separate Lechmere position endorsed accommodation within the relevant access precedents, not an unrestricted policy override. He can join the common rule, provided it preserves Ray's open derivative-benefit question and states no general condition requiring proven government wrongdoing.

### O'Connor

**Statutory coordination — result and sufficient ground:** Reject enforcement based on a special collective-bargaining interest in the FOIA balance. Her full joins in Reporters Committee and the simulated Ray opinion support the ordinary public-understanding measure; her Treasury/IRS join supports giving the labor statute's explicit limits effect. The competing statutory-context reading recognizes a real congressional purpose, but does not explain why the words required under FOIA change meaning when another statute initiates the request.

**Privacy application — result and two-path comparison:** Sustain withholding on this record. Her Ray join permits a practical assessment without requiring that confidentiality alone control. The strongest enforcement argument is that ordinary addresses have less intimate content and Exemption 6 carries a stronger disclosure preference than Exemption 7(C). Those distinctions change the privacy weight, not the absence of a demonstrated public-understanding gain here. The interest in limiting official dissemination of home locations remains real even without proven harassment. She does not need a finding that union communications are harmful.

**Judgment, boundaries, and join conditions:** Reverse both enforcement components. Her Lechmere majority join supports respecting an enacted or precedential boundary while preserving separate employee rights; it does not resolve the conceded bargaining necessity question. Join the common text-and-balance rationale with no universal privacy rule, no constitutional holding, and no decision on a routine-use notice that is not presented. No independent remand to redetermine necessity is supported.

### Scalia

**Statutory coordination — result and sufficient ground:** Apply the enacted chain of cross-references without adding collective-bargaining policy to FOIA. His Treasury/IRS opinion, 494 U.S. 928–933, requires the FLRA to respect express statutory boundaries while preserving its reasonable authority within actual ambiguities. His Reporters Committee join independently supports requester neutrality. Enforcement's strongest argument is Congress's express bargaining purpose, but neither that purpose nor agency expertise changes §552a(b)(2)'s identification of FOIA as the relevant disclosure obligation.

**Privacy application — result and two-path comparison:** Find these addresses exempt. His separate simulated Ray opinion, joined by Kennedy, would not count a requester's proposed investigation as a FOIA benefit when the disclosed data themselves say nothing more about agency performance. That narrower position makes the informational deficiency sufficient here even before any broader derivative theory could matter. The opposing argument that employees could be useful informants must not be converted into a finding that these disclosures reveal governmental conduct. He also accepts Reporters Committee's distinction between some public availability and official dissemination of a compiled record. The Exemption 6 standard remains would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion, not Exemption 7(C)'s text.

**Judgment, boundaries, and join conditions:** Reverse both components on §552a(b)(2). Join the shared proposition that this record supplies no sufficient FOIA public benefit without endorsing broader derivative-benefit reasoning. No legislative-history premise is necessary. Preserve the Privacy Act/FOIA permission-prohibition distinction, routine-use reservation, and the uncontested necessity premise. His Ray reservation need not produce a separate writing unless a later proposed opinion affirmatively resolves that reserved issue against his position.

### Kennedy

**Statutory coordination — result and sufficient ground:** Reject the Fifth Circuit's special public-interest balance. His joins in Reporters Committee and Treasury/IRS establish both the ordinary FOIA public-interest rule and respect for statutory limits on the labor agency. The union-specific statutory demand is a stronger enforcement argument than requester preference alone, but the Privacy Act exception still asks whether FOIA requires release and the labor duty expressly yields to prohibitions of law.

**Privacy application — result and two-path comparison:** Sustain Exemption 6 on the present record. His join in Scalia's simulated Ray concurrence preserves the narrower position that information which itself supplies no additional knowledge of agency performance does not gain FOIA weight merely from a proposed follow-up investigation. The competing argument emphasizes the modest privacy interest and the utility of unconstrained communication with government workers. It fails here because collective bargaining utility is not the identified FOIA contribution and the record establishes no stronger transparency use. A home-address privacy interest need not depend on proven harassment.

**Judgment, boundaries, and join conditions:** Reverse the enforcement judgment in both underlying matters. A common opinion may reserve the derivative-investigation question; it may not count Kennedy as affirmatively accepting Ray's broader discussion. His Lechmere majority join supports honoring a limited access rule, but is not authority to reopen §7114 necessity. Routine use, written consent, different personnel records, and constitutional privacy remain undecided. Ordinary implementation of reversal is permissible; a new merits inquiry under §552a(b)(3) is not directed.

### Souter

**Statutory coordination — result and sufficient ground:** Apply FOIA's own public-interest measure within the Privacy Act exception. His full join in the simulated Ray opinion is a direct date-eligible FOIA commitment. The strongest contrary path reads the bargaining legislation as supplying a congressionally selected disclosure interest, rather than an administrator's policy. It still faces the express prohibition-saving language and the Privacy Act's specific reference to FOIA-required disclosure. No supplied prior Souter position supports changing that reference.

**Privacy application — result and two-path comparison:** Find this record insufficient to overcome the privacy interest. His Ray join supports comparing demonstrated incremental public understanding against the actual disclosure injury; it does not make confidentiality a prerequisite or require government wrongdoing. An address is less sensitive than Ray's linked personal interviews, which favors enforcement on the privacy side. But the comparison remains unfavorable to release when its demonstrated benefit is bargaining communication rather than public understanding. The balance can be resolved without declaring potential follow-up investigation legally irrelevant in all cases.

**Judgment, boundaries, and join conditions:** Reverse both enforcement components on the narrow statutory route. His Lechmere majority join provides a limited analogy for preserving the actual legal threshold rather than replacing it with generalized accommodation; it supplies no new rule about federal employees or §7114 necessity. Join an explanation reserving routine use and consent, retaining Exemption 6's distinct words, and avoiding a presumption that every address is always exempt. No source establishes an independent threshold obstacle requiring dismissal.

### Ginsburg

**Statutory coordination — result and sufficient ground:** Apply §552a(b)(2) according to ordinary FOIA rules, with the policy concern expressly retained. Her own Treasury concurrence, 884 F.2d 1457–1461, accepts that §7114(b)(4) incorporates the Privacy Act unmodified and that the FOIA route does not give the union superior third-party access. It also asks whether the Supreme Court or Congress should reconsider the unexpected effect on federal bargaining. The latter is the strongest affirmative argument for a different path; now sitting on this Court, she is not limited by a lower court's inability to qualify precedent. But the writing identifies a reason for reconsideration, not an alternative adopted construction. The direct cross-reference and Ray's subsequent controlling Exemption 6 framework support adherence at the presented level.

**Privacy application — result and two-path comparison:** Sustain withholding while characterizing the interest accurately as modest rather than equivalent to a rap sheet. Her concurrence emphasizes ordinary public availability, the lack of additional embarrassing information, the narrower wording of Exemption 6, and the public/private labor-law disparity. Those considerations strongly support enforcement if broader bargaining interests may enter the balance. Her same writing acknowledges, however, that the statutory cross-reference and Reporters Committee exclude that weight from the FOIA balance. On this record no sufficient distinct public-understanding contribution is supplied. The privacy cost therefore prevails without disparaging unions or finding feared contacts actually occurred.

**Judgment, boundaries, and join conditions:** Reverse both enforcement components under §552a(b)(2). Join a limited opinion that does not deny the substantial public value of bargaining, exaggerate home-address sensitivity, or decide that Congress intended the precise practical disadvantage as an affirmative labor policy. A statement preserving that concern could perform a separate-writing function if the assembled opinion otherwise obscures it; no writing or author is assigned here. Her 1989 routine-use disposition concerned a different notice and record and supplies no outcome for the unpressed §552a(b)(3) issue here. Consent and other legally available disclosure routes remain separate.

## Coalition scope and remedy to carry into reconciliation

All eight provisional commitments support these propositions at the same bounded level: the labor request does not enlarge the FOIA public-interest inquiry reached through §552a(b)(2); these addresses' demonstrated public-understanding contribution does not outweigh the concrete interest in controlling disclosure of a residence under Exemption 6; FOIA therefore does not require disclosure; the Privacy Act consequently prevents the particular compulsory disclosure sought on this route. Neither a new exemption for all employment records nor a rule that all home-address requests invariably fail has eight-Justice support established here.

Blackmun requires the record-specific qualification. Scalia and Kennedy retain their narrower derivative-benefit position. Ginsburg preserves the recognized bargaining value and her concern about the statutory interaction. The common rationale can accommodate those positions by deciding this record and leaving different demonstrated transparency uses open. No additional Justice is counted as accepting an individual's broader reasoning.

The judgment position is reversal of the Fifth Circuit judgment enforcing the two FLRA orders. It does not require a new factual inquiry into bargaining necessity, an employee-by-employee veto scheme, damages, or a constitutional privacy remedy. Any ordinary remand implements the stated statutory decision; it does not command adjudication or approval of a routine-use notice not presented here. No position assumes that unions can never obtain the information through consent or another applicable statutory route.
