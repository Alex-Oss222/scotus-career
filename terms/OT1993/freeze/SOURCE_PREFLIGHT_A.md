## Material neutral corrections or precision additions for fresh validation

1. Carlisle's ordinary-negligence record includes duty, foreseeability/notice, breach, and causation; injury genuineness is separate. The district opinion confirms those charge elements and quotes substantial limiting instructions. It does not reproduce the entire charge. Coworker illness testimony was notice evidence, not substantive proof that Conrail caused those coworkers' ailments. The court reserved pure emotional injury without physical manifestations.
2. Gottshall remains a summary-judgment record. Water was available; attribution of continued exposure to Johns's body and the conditions/breaks are contested. Roth did not reject every emotional-injury claim.
3. Shannon's lower rule barred discretion, not just mandatory entitlement. Mental illness differs from the insanity defense; experts and the concession concerning sufficient government evidence matter to harmlessness. The whole §4243 procedure and burdens must accompany an account of commitment. Committee permission is not a statutory command.
4. Williamson's appellate disposition was without opinion. District-court grounds must not be attributed as an independently stated panel analysis. The original rule's corroboration sentence expressly addresses exculpation. The trial prosecution disclaimed a conspiracy-in-furtherance theory; its conditional mistrial remark is not a blanket harmlessness concession.
5. McFarland's separate skeletal petition must not be collapsed into the counsel-motion proceeding. The Fifth Circuit had alternative grounds and an exhaustion footnote. Counsel entitlement, pending-proceeding interpretation, and discretionary stay are distinct. The 1996 version of §848(q)(9) is ineligible; the contemporaneous ex parte/shall text applies.
