## Threshold and procedural description

Cable Act §23, adding Communications Act §635(c)(1), **106 Stat. 1500**, requires the three-judge district court for this constitutional challenge to §§614–615. The separate **28 U.S.C. §1253** supplies direct review of the final merits denial of the requested permanent injunctive relief. The corrected source establishes claims for declaratory and injunctive relief, mandatory-panel treatment, adversarial dispositive proceedings and oral argument. Final dismissal of those constitutional claims with prejudice rejects the injunction on its merits. Postponement of an earlier preliminary-injunction hearing under the standstill does not erase those later adversarial merits proceedings.

Section 635(c)(2) instead covers interlocutory or final judgments, decrees or orders **holding** a challenged carriage provision unconstitutional and supplies a twenty-day appeal period for that stated class. It does not itself describe this order sustaining the provisions, and it does not expressly withdraw §1253's ordinary injunction route. The statutes can operate together. No Justice's frozen reachability commitment depended on applying the special invalidating-order trigger to an upholding order. Correcting that attribution leaves the lawful review channel intact. No appeal-filing date, timeliness finding under an unverified date, additional Supreme Court jurisdictional order, or new generally controlling jurisdictional holding is invented.

The lower court's actual final order denied plaintiffs' summary-judgment motions, granted the federal defendants' dismissal motion, dismissed the must-carry constitutional claims with prejudice, and disposed of the remaining related motions as moot. That wording governs the procedural description. References in the original reconciliation to judgment on a summary-judgment record describe the substantive evidentiary posture; they must not be converted into a claim that the federal defendants moved for or obtained an order expressly granting their summary-judgment motion. The corrected form of the adverse merits judgment does not resolve the disputes over evidence, constitutional classification or fit. A remand supporter would set aside that actual judgment; an invalidation supporter would reverse its constitutional conclusion; Stevens's primary affirmance would preserve it.

The strongest threshold objection is that the special statute expressly provides direct appeal only for invalidating orders. The separate §1253 text, mandatory-panel predicate and final merits denial of injunctive relief answer that objection on the supplied facts. No revised associate threshold position or further neutral return is necessary.

## Additional statutory cross-references

The basic-tier clarification preserves two distinct duties. Section 623(b)(7) requires a separately available basic tier, subscription to which precedes access to other tiers, containing required §§614–615 signals, franchise-required PEG programming and provided broadcast signals subject to the exception for specified satellite retransmission outside the local service area. Other programming may be added, with basic-tier rate regulation following. Section 615(h) separately locates compulsory noncommercial signals in the lowest-priced tier containing local commercial broadcasts. These terms clarify distribution without proving actual displacement, burden or constitutional fit; neither classification coalition changes.

The election clarification preserves the regulatory direction for an initial election within a year and every three years thereafter, a common election for systems serving the same geographic area, and the rule that choosing retransmission consent for a system removes that station's §614 carriage entitlement on that system. Other stations' elected §§614–615 rights, the §111 copyright license and station–programmer agreements remain protected. No station's actual choice or payment is established. This confirms the no-double-entitlement premise and leaves Stevens's prediction about use by vulnerable broadcasters a prediction, not a factual finding.

Section 612(i)(4)'s leased-access definition does not substitute for §615's station requirement. No leased-access or basic-tier constitutional challenge is added. The source refresh reports no further change to the previously supplied carriage quantities, conditional small-system exception, duplicate-signal choices, delivery/payment conditions, channel-position alternatives or transition provisions. The reserved low-power/market-adjustment questions and unverified current home-shopping administration remain reserved.
