#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

McFarland argues that requiring an unaided, factually adequate petition before counsel makes the enacted assistance unavailable at the stage when it is most needed, particularly given early dismissal and abuse-of-the-writ consequences. He reads the counsel and stay provisions together as covering preparation. [R50](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/849/)

Texas emphasizes the ordinary filing of a habeas application, the text requiring a pending proceeding for a stay, state finality, and the risk of delay without identified claims. A narrower position could recognize prepetition counsel while denying that the counsel statute independently changes §2251’s jurisdictional predicate. [R50](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/849/)

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Recognize both prepetition counsel and a pending proceeding sufficient for discretionary stay authority; recognize counsel but require an actual petition for a stay; or require the petition for both. Under the first route, the court still must exercise stay discretion rather than grant one automatically. None of these paths decides the validity of the underlying conviction or unfiled claims. [R50](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/849/)
