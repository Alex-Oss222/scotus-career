## 73. Simmons v. South Carolina, No. 92-9059

### Reach, record, and entering law

All eight non-Stone Justices participate at argument, January 18, 1994, and the June 17 decision. The whole Court has nine participants and a quorum; five would be required for a Court judgment or controlling proposition. Certiorari reviews the South Carolina Supreme Court's death-sustaining judgment, 310 S.C. 439, 427 S.E.2d 175. Each modeled Justice reaches the preserved sentencing-information questions on direct review. Neither a collateral-review Teague bar nor an adjudication of the separate guilt-stage evidentiary dispute is adopted.

The due-process rebuttal claim and Eighth Amendment mitigation/reliability claim remain distinct preserved theories. The primary proposed sufficient ground is the Fourteenth Amendment right to answer the State's future-danger case. The narrower operative proposition is: when the State puts future dangerousness in issue in a capital sentencing and the legally available noncapital alternative is life imprisonment without ordinary parole, the defendant must have an effective opportunity to inform the jury of that ineligibility, by accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction. The rule does not require a particular wording, a prediction of commutation, an assertion that release can never occur under any future law, or favorable weight to the defendant's evidence. It does not determine what must be disclosed when the prosecution has not put future danger in issue, when parole eligibility is disputed, or when ordinary parole remains available.

On the accepted record, the State's community-protection and future-danger argument put that subject in issue despite the absence of an express assertion that Simmons would be paroled. The jury's precise parole question is an objective feature of the proceeding, not a finding about actual juror thoughts. The answer directing jurors not to consider parole and to apply life's ordinary meaning did not tell them the actual legal alternative. The survey provides context but is unnecessary to, and does not prove, the constitutional conclusion.

The eligibility premise rests on §24-21-640's second-or-subsequent violent conviction following separate sentencing for a prior conviction, together with the uncontested departmental testimony. Offenses within one day or one continuous course count as one for that provision. The separate attacks and convictions are the relevant record; no new board eligibility adjudication is made. Work-release and furlough restrictions derive from the reported regulations, not this statutory sentence. Act 184's §16-1-60(B) offense-date classification condition, retroactivity exception, prospective remainder, savings terms and January 1, 1994 effective date remain distinct; nothing supplied changes the uncontested ineligibility in this case. Cavanaugh restored Roller's annual consideration under different provisions and never made every violent offender eligible for parole.

Graham's effective-mitigation rule, present operation January 25, 1993, and Johnson v. Texas's June 24, 1993 direct-review application protect a real route for supported culpability mitigation. They do not themselves establish a legal-sentencing-information rule. Romano, June 13, 1994, controls its responsibility-displacement problem and shows that accurate general instructions do not automatically answer every capital-sentencing defect. Its five-Justice holding did not adopt ordinary due process as an independently sufficient ground or prescribe a general evidence code. Its four-Justice new-hearing position is not a Court-wide mandatory-remedy rule. These limits enter every row below.

### Adversarial paths and conclusions

**Rebuttal path:** Gardner's opportunity to answer material sentencing information and Skipper's future-danger discussion support disclosure of a fixed legal constraint directly answering the State's asserted reason for death. The strongest contrary argument is that Ramos permits state control over release-related information, no false parole assertion occurred, and dangerousness can extend to prison. The answer supporting this path is limited: the State may argue danger in prison and may distinguish parole from clemency, but cannot close the entire avenue for explaining the existing noncapital alternative after invoking community danger. An instruction directing silence on the jurors' precise question does not communicate the legal fact. Ramos's approval of truthful commutation information does not require withholding truthful parole ineligibility.

**No-error path:** The State may exclude parole speculation symmetrically, accurately charge death and life, and rely on a defendant's dangerous character without making a factual assertion of early release. Under this reading, Skipper protects excluded adaptation evidence, while the actual adaptation evidence here was heard; Gardner's inaccessible adverse material and Romano's affirmative separate-death exhibit are different. The strongest contrary argument is that an accurate legal alternative is neither speculation nor ordinary character evidence, and the State itself made future danger salient. The two Justices taking this path nevertheless regard the lack of an express misleading release assertion, the retained prison-danger issue and the given instruction as insufficient to establish fundamental unfairness. They do not dispute the actual ineligibility or revive a false account of state law.

**Eighth Amendment path:** Effective individualized choice could independently require accurate information about the actual alternatives, extending the functional concerns in Lockett, Eddings, Graham, Johnson and Romano. The strongest objection is that the availability of parole is not itself a circumstance of character, record or offense, and Romano concerns displacement of responsibility by another death judgment. The due-process disposition does not require treating those authorities as already deciding this extension. The individualized positions below preserve support for an extension, reservations and rejection instead of importing an additional controlling rule from a judgment vote.

## 74. MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T, Nos. 93-356 and 93-521

### Reach, statute, and entering law

Seven non-Stone Justices participate; O'Connor takes no part, with no invented explanation. Including Stone, the Court has eight participants, a quorum and a five-vote majority threshold. Argument was March 21, 1994; event date is June 17. Both consolidated dockets concern the same rule-review judgment and receive the same modeled disposition. The judgment directly reviewed is the unpublished D.C. Circuit order setting aside the November 1992 permissive rule, 7 FCC Rcd 8072, relying on 978 F.2d 727. The earlier §208 complaint and its relief are not a separate damages component of this review.

Section 203(a) imposes filing, publication and public-inspection duties on every common carrier except connecting carriers, with the stated schedule and connecting-carrier provisions. Under (b)(1), changes require 120 days' notice, not the old ninety-day period. Subsection (b)(2) permits the Commission, in its discretion and for good cause shown, to modify any requirement made by or under this section, either in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions, but not to require a notice period longer than 120 days. Those conditions are operative limits, not surplusage. Subsection (c)'s distinct proviso permits otherwise-authorized action under the chapter; it is not a second connecting-carrier exception or unlimited freestanding exemption. The filed-rate charging, rebate and privilege restrictions remain distinct from filing and from the notice ceiling. Sections 201–202's substantive duties and §§206–208's liability and complaint machinery survive the challenged rule; they do not conclusively decide the intended role of the tariff mechanism.

The rule makes filing optional for nondominant carriers. It does not prohibit filing. That distinction must appear in any final judgment and reasoning. The earlier Ginsburg opinion, 765 F.2d 1186, rejected compulsory nonfiling and expressly reserved permissive orders, including the possible enforcement-discretion distinction; it is strong issue-specific support but no prior decision of the reserved question.

The operative inherited rules are Lechmere's preservation of binding statutory limits (January 27, 1992), Boston & Maine's discernible-agency-ground rule (March 25, 1992), and Good Samaritan's permissible-change rule (June 7, 1993). The actual Health Care & Retirement and PUD No. 1 decisions sustain bounded agency constructions grounded in their particular statutory schemes. They neither compel approval here nor enact a general major-questions rule. Chevron remains available after examining the statute with ordinary interpretive tools; importance alone supplies no separate clear-statement demand.

### Competing paths

**Invalidity path:** Making the filing obligation optional for the entire nondominant category removes its obligatory force rather than modifying its details within Congress's tariff system. Read with (a) and (c), the power to modify does not permit replacing the statutory filed-tariff mechanism with retained general rate duties and complaints. The connecting-carrier exception reinforces that Congress distinguished exempt carriers from regulated carriers. The strongest contrary argument is that “any,” general-order authority, good cause, and special conditions expressly permit a class-based adaptation; mandatory filing is itself subject to the modification provision. The answer on this path is that those terms confer real authority over the section's requirements without eliminating the limiting verb or authorizing a system in which an entire principal carrier class need never file. Neither the number of firms nor their collective market share alone supplies the legal rule.

**Validity path:** Nondominance can be a special condition, and the Commission may reasonably determine for good cause that compulsory filing harms competition without advancing protection against market power. The provision expressly extends to any requirement and general orders; retaining §§201–202 and complaints, permitting voluntary filing and reimposition, and keeping dominant carriers under tariff filing make this a bounded adjustment. The strongest objection is that optionality removes a central statutory protection rather than adjusts it. On this path, the enacted modification clause qualifies the mandatory starting point, and the Court should not read a categorical no-exemption limit into Congress's express special-condition delegation. The conclusion is permissibility within the identified conditions, not a freestanding executive power to repeal inconvenient regulation.

No modeled Justice adopts an independently sufficient construction of the unquoted full §226 or §332 enactments. They may supply contextual arguments, but no decisive later-ratification, implied-repeal, or express-prohibition inference is manufactured. No one awards AT&T damages, computes a filed-rate recovery, or supplies a new cease-and-desist command in the complaint proceeding.

## 75. West Lynn Creamery, Inc. v. Healy, No. 93-141

### Reach, mechanics, and entering law

All eight non-Stone Justices participate at the March 2, 1994 argument and June 17 decision. The whole Court has nine participants, a quorum and a five-vote majority threshold. The assessed dealers' direct obligation and enforcement exposure support reaching their challenge to the integrated order. The lower court's assumption about asserting producers' interests is not transformed into a separate Supreme Court standing holding. No modeled Justice would dismiss this challenge merely because dealers pay and producers receive distributions.

The review concerns the amended February 26, 1992 order, not its February 18 predecessor. The SJC sustained constitutionality and remanded for Superior Court proceedings. The dealers sought declaratory and injunctive relief and damages, but no federal refund sum or farmer-restitution award is established. Their license proceedings and conditional payments do not themselves fix the federal remedy.

The assessment is uniform at the collection stage: all licensed dealers selling Class I milk to Massachusetts retailers pay regardless of origin. The per-hundredweight premium is one-third of the difference between $15 and the federal blend price, multiplied by covered sales expressed in hundredweight. All premiums enter the Dairy Equalization Fund; distributions go only to Massachusetts producers in proportion to the preceding month's production. No producer receives credit for more than 200,000 pounds monthly; no payment may bring the producer's combined federal-minimum and fund return above $15 per hundredweight; surplus is returned to dealers. These qualifications limit the program but do not erase its geographic recipient line. They preclude asserting unlimited expansion incentives or unrestricted retention of surplus.

The State does not buy or sell the assessed milk. Producers and dealers are different legal actors in the same market. The estimated imported share is context, not the source of a facially different dealer tax rate. The department reported declining production after the order, and the SJC found an output-increase prediction speculative in light of caps, herd-expansion expense and duration. Those findings remain intact. The proposed invalidity ground concerns protecting local production against interstate competition, including preserving output that would otherwise decline, without inventing an inevitable increase or a dollar-for-dollar incidence finding.

Wyoming, Chemical Waste and Fort Gratiot retain their limits. Oregon Waste's actual April 4 discrimination and compensatory-tax holdings, Carbone's May 16 private-service ruling and distinct separate positions, and Lohman's May 23 locality-specific comparison govern where applicable. New Energy supplies both the prohibition of discriminatory tax advantages and the substantial distinction for ordinary direct subsidy. None has already decided every dedicated tax-and-subsidy combination. No congressional authorization for this challenged geographic preference is supplied. The federal milk system sets a minimum price, not a command for this fund or immunity from Commerce Clause review.

### Two-path comparison

**Integrated-discrimination path:** A State cannot avoid the prohibition of origin discrimination by charging dealers uniformly while tying the proceeds to exclusive support of competing local producers in the same milk market. The collection and payout are two operative parts of the challenged instrument. Baldwin's concern with insulating local producers, Bacchus's treatment of local-product preference and New Energy's discriminatory tax rule support examining that combined operation. The strongest contrary argument is that a neutral tax and a permissible local subsidy do not become unlawful merely because enacted together; dealer tax incidence and capped producer aid are not a rebate to the same person. The response is confined to the designated milk-sales fund: the funded local protection is inseparable from the interstate market assessment as the program operates, even without identical legal payors and recipients. The rule does not assume that the State may never subsidize local enterprise from general revenues.

If classified as discrimination, preservation of a local dairy industry is not itself a sufficient justification. A secure milk supply, open land and emergency relief are legitimate objectives in the abstract, but the record does not show why reasonable nondiscriminatory means cannot adequately serve them. Pike's ordinary incidental-burden balancing is not substituted for that justification requirement. No new proof quantum, perfect-certainty requirement or least-imaginable-alternative test is imposed.

**Evenhanded-tax/ordinary-subsidy path:** Dealers pay the same premium on every source, can still choose lower-price suppliers, and the capped short-term distributions address local emergency production costs without an established increase in output or clearly excessive interstate burden. New Energy's subsidy distinction and Eisenberg's local-price setting supply substantial analogies. The strongest contrary argument is that separating accounting stages overlooks a legislatively linked geographic preference financed by the milk market itself.
