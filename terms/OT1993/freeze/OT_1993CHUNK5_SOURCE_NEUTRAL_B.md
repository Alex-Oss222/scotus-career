## Dalton v. Specter

Primary report: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep511/usrep511462/usrep511462.pdf . No.93-289; argued March 2, 1994; review of 995 F.2d404 (3d Cir.1993). The governing statute is the Defense Base Closure and Realignment Act of 1990, 104 Stat.1808, §§2901–2908, as amended, note to 10 U.S.C.§2687 (1988 ed., Supp.IV).

The Act provides closure rounds in 1991, 1993, and 1995. Under §2903(b),(c), the Secretary recommends closures using selection criteria established after notice and opportunity for public comment. He sends recommendations to Congress and an independent eight-member Commission appointed by the President with Senate consent. The Commission holds public hearings, assesses the recommendations, and submits its own package to the President under §2903(d). The President must approve or disapprove the package **in its entirety**, rather than select individual bases. Disapproval returns the package for a revised report; second disapproval ends that year's closures. Approval and certification go to Congress. A joint disapproval resolution within 45 days, or earlier adjournment, prevents the closures; otherwise §2904(a),(b) directs the Secretary to close **all** installations in the approved package. Section2908 supplies expedited congressional consideration. These features may bear on statutory preclusion separately from the APA's general definitions.

The statutory timetable is concrete: Secretary by April15 in1991 and March15 in1993/1995; Commission report July1; presidential decision July15; any revised Commission report August15; presidential approval by September1 or no closures that year. Closure must begin within two years and finish within six (§2904). Section2905(c) limits NEPA's application: no application to the process up to the President's submission; environmental review for later disposal and relocation is distinguished from the base-selection decision; covered review has a 60-day filing limit. This specific limited review provision is relevant to the parties' competing construction of the statutory package, not a freestanding disposition of the APA claim.

In April1991 the Secretary recommended Philadelphia's closure. After Washington and Philadelphia hearings the Commission recommended closure or realignment of82 bases, including Philadelphia. President Bush approved in July1991; the House rejected disapproval364–60. Plaintiffs filed two days before presidential submission. They include employees/unions, members of Congress, states and state officials, and Philadelphia. Defendants are the Secretaries of Defense/Navy and Commission/members; **the President is not named**. The challenged counts allege improper criteria, omission of information until after public hearings, and closed Navy/Commission meetings. These remain allegations, not adjudicated violations.

The district court dismissed on alternative statutory-preclusion and political-question grounds. The Third Circuit allowed review of specified procedural compliance, initially971F.2d936 and after remand995F.2d404, treating compliance as reviewable constitutional restraint on implementation. Its dissent favored statutory preclusion. The third count, employee/union due process, was rejected for absence of a protected property interest in continued shipyard operation; respondents did not seek review of that ruling.

The Government invokes the structure/timetable/package approval, APA finality, executive discretion, and the difference between an actual constitutional claim and an alleged statutory violation. Respondents invoke enforceable mandatory procedures, review of implementing officials, Marbury/Youngstown, and the presumption of review. **Use this Court's actual simulated Franklin**, not the different historical report's account of Franklin. A statutory scheme-preclusion argument survives as a distinct question even if simulated Franklin supplies APA reviewability; conversely finality alone does not establish that Congress foreclosed every procedural claim. No record here supplies an out-of-package presidential addition or a timely interlocutory order enforcing procedures before submission. Full administrative record and all merits briefs were not recovered; do not decide whether the alleged violations actually occurred.

## Posters 'N' Things, Ltd. v. United States

Primary report: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep511/usrep511513/usrep511513.pdf . No.92-903; argued October5,1993; Eighth Circuit969F.2d652 affirmed the convictions, required scienter, and rejected the as-applied vagueness attack. March1990 searches of Acty's residence and the business seized bongs, pipes, roach clips, scales, diluents (mannitol/inositol), cash, records and advertisements for Coke Kits, Free Base Kits, PseudoCaine and Procaine. Posters also ran a diet-aid store and an art gallery. The outlet formerly named Forbidden Fruit was renamed World Wide Imports.

Petitioners were convicted under former21U.S.C.§857(a)(1) and conspiracy under18U.S.C.§371. Acty also faced the distinct aiding-and-abetting cocaine, drug-income investment, money-laundering and criminal-proceeds transaction convictions. Acty's aggregate sentence was108months, five years supervised release, and$150,000 fine; Posters' fine was$75,000. These totals must not be described as the §857 maximum. Section857(b) provides up to three years' imprisonment and$100,000 fine.

The offense-date §857(a) states: “It is unlawful for any person—(1) to make use of the services of the Postal Service or other interstate conveyance as part of a scheme to sell drug paraphernalia; (2) to offer for sale and transportation in interstate or foreign commerce drug paraphernalia; or (3) to import or export drug paraphernalia.” The1990 replacement §863 changed that general offense description; do not substitute its language for the charged §857(a)(1).

Section857(d), in full: “The term ‘drug paraphernalia’ means any equipment, product, or material of any kind which is primarily intended or designed for use in manufacturing, compounding, converting, concealing, producing, processing, preparing, injecting, ingesting, inhaling, or otherwise introducing into the human body a controlled substance, possession of which is unlawful under the Controlled Substances Act (title II of Public Law91-513) [21U.S.C.§§801 et seq.]. It includes items primarily intended or designed for use in ingesting, inhaling, or otherwise introducing marijuana, cocaine, hashish, hashish oil, PCP, or amphetamines into the human body, such as—(1) metal, wooden, acrylic, glass, stone, plastic, or ceramic pipes with or without screens, permanent screens, hashish heads, or punctured metal bowls; (2) water pipes; (3) carburetion tubes and devices; (4) smoking and carburetion masks; (5) roach clips: meaning objects used to hold burning material, such as a marihuana cigarette, that has become too small or too short to be held in the hand; (6) miniature spoons with level capacities of one-tenth cubic centimeter or less; (7) chamber pipes; (8) carburetor pipes; (9) electric pipes; (10) air-driven pipes; (11) chillums; (12) bongs; (13) ice pipes or chillers; (14) wired cigarette papers; or (15) cocaine freebase kits.”

Section857(e): “In determining whether an item constitutes drug paraphernalia, in addition to all other logically relevant factors, the following may be considered: (1) instructions, oral or written, provided with the item concerning its use; (2) descriptive materials accompanying the item which explain or depict its use; (3) national and local advertising concerning its use; (4) the manner in which the item is displayed for sale; (5) whether the owner, or anyone in control of the item, is a legitimate supplier of like or related items to the community, such as a licensed distributor or dealer of tobacco products; (6) direct or circumstantial evidence of the ratio of sales of the item(s) to the total sales of the business enterprise; (7) the existence and scope of legitimate uses of the item in the community; and (8) expert testimony concerning its use.” This is permission to consider listed and other logically relevant factors, not a requirement to prove each.

Section857(f): “This section shall not apply to—(1) any person authorized by local, State, or Federal law to manufacture, possess, or distribute such items; or (2) any item that, in the normal lawful course of business, is imported, exported, transported, or sold through the mail or by any other means, and traditionally intended for use with tobacco products, including any pipe, paper, or accessory.” The1988 amendment substituted “traditionally” for “primarily” in(f),102Stat.4384. Do not merge the distinct person-authorization and ordinary-lawful-business tobacco exemptions.

The instructions required knowingly using the interstate conveyance as part of the scheme, finding that the items met the statutory definition, and knowledge of their nature/character; elaboration referred to defendants' knowledge of “the nature, character, and use of the items being sold or offered for sale at the store” (App.16–35). Petitioners argue that “primarily intended” requires their subjective intent that the items be used with drugs. Government urges an objective definition with knowledge of the items' character, not knowledge of their legal classification. Their other dispute concerns sufficient notice and arbitrary enforcement as applied to this shop. Possible applications to ordinary merchants selling only dual-use scales, mirrors or razor blades are distinct from the present assortment and advertising.

Prior authorities: Hoffman Estates,455U.S.489, distinguishes manufacturer's design from a retailer's marketing and addresses scienter as mitigation of vagueness; Gypsum,438U.S.422, addresses implied criminal intent and knowledge of probable consequences; Bailey,444U.S.394, distinguishes knowledge from purpose; Hamling,418U.S.87, distinguishes factual knowledge of material's nature from knowledge of legal obscenity; Kolender,461U.S.352, and Grayned,408U.S.104, supply notice/enforcement principles. The clean context must use actual simulated Staples only after its effective event is supplied, not this report's historical account.

Preservation is component-specific: Acty's proposed instructional presumption objection on cocaine aiding/abetting was neither raised nor addressed below; money-laundering, investment and proceeds issues were omitted from the certiorari petition; the petition's cocaine-evidence-sufficiency question was not developed in the merits brief. These are record/preservation facts, not decisions by this packet. No full trial transcript was independently recovered, and the quoted instruction excerpts are not a complete transcript.

## Associated Industries of Missouri v. Lohman

Primary report: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep511/usrep511641/usrep511641.pdf . No.93-397; argued March28,1994; review of857S.W.2d182 (Mo.1993). Missouri has matched statewide4.225% sales/use levies, unchallenged here. The challenged **additional1.5% use tax** is §144.748 (Supp.1993), incorporating §144.615(2)'s exemption for goods subject to Missouri sales tax. More than1,000 subdivisions impose sales taxes0.5%–3.5%; at least one county imposes none. Do not treat this as a challenge to all use taxation or to the matched4.225% base.

The trade association represents collecting sellers; Alumax Foils is an in-state manufacturer paying use tax on out-of-state purchases. The trial court granted respondents summary judgment. The Missouri Supreme Court used statewide aggregate comparison and General American Tank Car,270U.S.367, to sustain the additional tax. Its stipulated1990 figures showed use tax exceeding local sales tax in53.5% of local jurisdictions, but more than93% of sales dollars occurring in jurisdictions with higher local sales taxes; a flat1.5% local sales tax would have reduced in-state tax by$100million. Chief Justice Robertson dissented, urging equality within each locality. Statistics are the state court's account of the stipulation; do not convert them into a tax measure applicable to any particular unproved purchase.

Respondents defend statewide equalization, decentralized local choices, absence of discriminatory purpose, and state authority to delegate taxation. Petitioners challenge locality-level disparate burdens and additionally seek invalidation/refund of the tax in its entirety because no guaranteed statewide matching tax exists. Those are separate merits/remedy propositions. Any compensatory analysis must identify the intrastate burden and substantially equivalent events; sales/use equivalence here is undisputed. Actual earlier Oregon Waste, Fort Gratiot, and C&A Carbone must be read as this Court decided them. Henneford v.Silas Mason,300U.S.577, Halliburton,373U.S.64, Maryland v.Louisiana,451U.S.725, Armco,467U.S.638, Boston Stock Exchange,429U.S.318, Gregg Dyeing,286U.S.472, and McKesson,496U.S.18, are eligible additional authorities.
