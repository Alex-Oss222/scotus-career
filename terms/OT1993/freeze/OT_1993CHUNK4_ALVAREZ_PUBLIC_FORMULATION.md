# Alvarez-Sanchez — proposed public formulation for final join audit

This is a proposed legal formulation, not an adopted opinion, a Stone position, a vote request, or an assertion of historical circulation. Review against the frozen neutral packet, refreshed commitments, comparator and final reconciliation. No private position is supplied.

The Court reverses and remands because detention solely for state charges does not itself start the federal-presentment period described in 18 U.S.C. §3501(c). That period concerns delay in performing the duty to bring the person before an officer empowered to commit defendants charged with federal or District of Columbia offenses. The phrase covering any law-enforcement officer includes state and local officers when the actual basis or arrangement of detention engages that duty; it does not make every state-only arrest federal detention. A formal federal charge or arrest is not declared indispensable in every possible custody arrangement. Actual detention and arrangements control, not a label.

The case reaches this Court on the premise that the confession preceded the later federal arrest and occurred during state-only detention. The district court reported no evidence of collusion. The Court identifies that finding as the posture below and does not review or endorse its factual correctness, which the Court of Appeals did not decide. Ordinary notice that officers discovered counterfeit currency does not, without more, establish an improper holding arrangement. The Court does not presume that improper arrangements are rare, discount their possible importance, or decide whether this record would establish such an arrangement under a properly presented challenge. Anderson v. United States remains the relevant warning against using state detention to evade federal duties; the effect, if any, of §3501 on that rule is left open.

The statutory six-hour protection concerns exclusion solely for presentment delay, requires the judge's voluntariness finding, leaves weight to the jury, and retains its transportation-and-distance proviso. The Court leaves open the general treatment of confessions obtained more than six hours after a relevant federal arrest and the general relationship among subsections (a), (b), and (c). No categorical admission or suppression rule, or ruling on §3501's entire relation to Miranda, follows.

The alternative Gerstein/McLaughlin Fourth Amendment theory was not raised in either lower court and is not decided or affirmatively reopened by this remand. No independent correctly premised coercion finding supports affirmance. The Court removes the Ninth Circuit's erroneous aggregation of state detention into federal delay and leaves further proceedings consistent with this limited rule. It does not itself direct admission, reinstate a conviction, order a new trial, grant release, or decide the constitutional legality of the state detention.

The proposed changes from the historical opinion answer three specific recorded objections: no categorical formal-arrest prerequisite; no characterization of improper arrangements as rare; and no appellate endorsement of the lower no-collusion finding. The audit must determine independently whether these material scope revisions permit Stevens to join the opinion or whether a precise remaining objection requires a separate judgment-only position. The other seven reconciled associates' limits and the Ginsburg/Blackmun post-six-hour reservation must also remain intact. Do not assume a changed join from the proposal itself.
