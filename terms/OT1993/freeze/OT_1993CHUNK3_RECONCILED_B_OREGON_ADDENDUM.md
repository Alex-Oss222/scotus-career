## Corrected Blackmun commitment

**Reach and judgment:** Reach the federal facial challenge in both reported dockets. Affirm the state judgment insofar as it rejects that challenge to the cost-directed out-of-state surcharge. Do not reopen Oregon's state-law severance of the legislative-committee approval provisions. No refund, replacement fee, mandatory acceptance of waste, or independent public-landfill adjudication follows from his position.

**Sufficient ground:** In his view, this surcharge should be assessed within Oregon's comprehensive waste-management program and its direction to recover governmental disposal costs otherwise borne through local fees, taxes and conservation efforts. A modest cost-recovery charge associated with that program need not be treated as impermissible commercial protection merely because the fee schedule expressly distinguishes origin. Sporhase supports considering the State's conservation efforts and the burdens already imposed locally; Evansville supports reasonable approximation of the service or privilege rather than exact accounting identity. His disagreement with the invalidating coalition concerns the characterization and justification of this particular comprehensive program, including its nondisposal regulatory costs, rather than a denial that waste transactions are commerce.

**Response to the central objection:** Armco and the seven-Justice position require substantially equivalent taxable events for the asserted compensatory-tax defense; the geographic fee difference is also undisputed. Blackmun nevertheless regards a comparison confined to the per-ton disposal charges as omitting the comprehensive system's local contributions and resource-preservation work. He would not treat this fee as an unrelated attempt to shelter a local seller from outside competition. This is his dissenting application of existing doctrine, not a controlling exception to Armco, a holding that general income taxation always offsets interstate taxes, or an empirical determination of a precise offset. His earlier Armco join is not erased; he regards the regulatory setting as sufficiently different to support the surcharge here.

**Cost and procedural qualifications:** ORS 459.298 requires the amount to reflect otherwise-unpaid governmental disposal costs, including the enumerated management, permitting, monitoring and closure/postclosure categories, without making the list exhaustive. The requirement remains a statutory command. The limited ORS 183.400 proceeding did not adjudicate that the $2.25 equals proved incremental costs attributable to imported waste, and Blackmun's affirmance must not be written as such a finding. His ground sustains the challenged rule against this facial constitutional attack; it does not manufacture a proportionality trial, a new numerical cost finding or a decision on an unpresented operational challenge. The original reconciliation's warning that the challenged surcharge is not the entire disposal bill also remains intact.

**User-fee and public-facility limit:** The landfills here are private. Governmental regulation does not make the State their owner or establish the market-participant exception. Blackmun's reliance on service-fee reasonableness is an analogy supporting his cost-recovery position, not a factual finding of public ownership. He does not adjudicate the separate constitutionality of charges at government-owned landfills. Northwest Airlines's distinct nondiscrimination requirement remains law; his position is that this program's treatment should not be condemned as the impermissible economic preference the seven identify.

**Join barrier and alternative remedy:** Blackmun does not join conclusive invalidation, the proposition that the particular local contributions are legally irrelevant merely because the ordinary per-ton rates differ, or the seven-Justice application of the equivalent-events rule to defeat Oregon's justification. The earlier provisional evidentiary-remand alternative remains a recorded, rejected provisional path, not his selected final remedy or an additional vote for the judgment. No circulated revision has been proposed or validated; this addendum invents no draft exchange. A later proposal changing his disposition would need an actual ground resolving a recorded objection and, if material, renewed reconciliation under Engine §8.

**Historical comparison:** The corrected disposition and sufficient justification track the position Blackmun historically joined in Oregon Waste, while retaining the accurate neutral-record limitations. No historical author, partner or section number is imported into a future opinion. Assembly may select the writing and its precise compatible scope from these grounds. The correction does not claim that historical dissent is in-world authority.
