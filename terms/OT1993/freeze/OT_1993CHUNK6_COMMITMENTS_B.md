## 65. City of Ladue v. Gilleo, No. 92-1856

Argument: 1994-02-23. Review: Eighth Circuit, 986 F.2d 1180, affirming the permanent injunction against the replacement residential-sign ordinance. The amended complaint addresses the replacement; repeal of the prior variance regime does not moot that challenge. The separate fee adjustment is not an independently presented review component. All eight reach the merits.

### Components and bounded common formulation

L1 is the substantive validity of the replacement scheme's nearly complete elimination of residential signs expressing the resident's own views. L2 is the independent content-classification route used below. L3 is the remedy and preservation of otherwise lawful sign regulation.

The ten exempt categories are municipal signs; subdivision/residence identification; road or driveway danger, direction and identification; health-inspection signs; churches, religious institutions and schools under their separate provision; identification for other nonprofits; public-transport stops; property sale, rental or exchange within its specified content/location conditions; commercial signs in commercial/industrial districts; and safety-hazard signs. Residence identification has its one-square-foot condition; the packet identifies other category-specific limits without reproducing every dimension. The separate filling-station provision does not license general household political messages. Nothing in this freeze validates an unexamined size condition or treats every exemption as identical.

**L1 common sufficient proposition:** Even assuming for decision that the City's exemptions and regulation may be treated as content neutral, Ladue cannot eliminate this distinct, important residential means of expressing personal views when the asserted alternatives do not provide adequate substitutes. Signs at one's own residence inexpensively identify the speaker, reach neighbors and passersby at that location, and communicate continuously without requiring the resident's attendance; general appeals to aesthetics, traffic and proliferation do not justify the breadth of this suppression on the established record.

This is a narrow application of the alternative-channel requirement, reinforced by the ordinance's breadth. It is not a least-restrictive-means requirement for all sign laws, a rule against every content distinction, or an entitlement to any number, size, placement, illumination or manner of display. The resident's own interests in the appearance of the property also distinguish uncontrolled posting on public fixtures. The City may pursue otherwise lawful safety, size, number and location regulation; this record does not adjudicate a new ordinance.

**L2 reservation:** The eight need not decide whether each exception is impermissibly content based, whether some distinctions can survive the appropriate scrutiny, or whether severing all exceptions would save the general ban. Assuming neutrality does not approve the classifications. The independent loss-of-medium ground sustains the relief without extending Discovery Network beyond its actual content/fit holding.

**L3 disposition:** Affirm the judgment maintaining relief against the challenged scheme. Do not reinstate the old variance procedure, impose the unpreserved proposed six-square-foot substitute, alter the separately disposed fee issue, or award additional relief.

### Adversarial comparison

Path A sustains the injunction on the loss of an important residential channel even assuming content neutrality. Vincent is the strongest contrary authority: a city may eliminate the very medium causing visual blight, and cheapness alone does not immunize it. Its precise answer is that Vincent preserved private-property signs and found no uniquely valuable channel lost; its private/public distinction and express alternative-channel inquiry materially differ here. Linmark identifies why other paid or labor-intensive channels may fail to substitute, while expressly leaving genuinely neutral sign laws open; it is support for this functional inquiry, not a prior categorical residential-sign holding.

Path B would sustain reasonable content-neutral control as an aesthetic measure, treating flags, hand-held signs, printed distribution, letters, newspaper advertisements and meetings as adequate. That path has a legitimate Vincent/Metromedia basis, and the City's public-need and natural-limitation explanations deserve consideration. It fails at this ordinance's near-total residential coverage: the listed alternatives change the audience, attribution, expense, presence or physical medium. Rectangular flags do not preserve the ordinary yard/window-sign channel, and the exemptions leave conventional household civic/religious/personal expression without it. No finding establishes that targeted physical controls are inadequate. The conclusion does not depend on holding each alternative useless or on requiring a perfect substitute.

A separate Path C would affirm by directly invalidating the content preferences. Discovery Network makes benign motive insufficient when the asserted physical harms do not explain the classification, but deciding all ten categories would require more distinctions than the sufficient medium ground. No Justice's frozen commitment requires that broader holding.

## 66. O'Melveny & Myers v. FDIC, No. 93-489

Argument: 1994-03-21. Review: Ninth Circuit, 969 F.2d 744, which reversed summary judgment for the law firm using a federal-common-law rule concerning imputation to the receiver. All eight reach the governing-law question. Federal jurisdiction is not itself a federal substantive rule of decision.

### Components and bounded common formulation

O1 concerns the source of the imputation/defense rule for the institution's California negligence and fiduciary-duty claims. O2 concerns the proposed pre-FIRREA federal-common-law exception. O3 concerns the FIRREA argument and whether temporal application must be decided. O4 concerns remand and the matters not determined.

The FSLIC became conservator in February 1986 and receiver in June 1988; the FDIC's 1989 substitution followed FIRREA. The disputed professional work occurred in December 1985. These are client claims acquired through receivership. The alleged client losses include its own fees, settlement costs, commissions and property-related losses as described in the packet. This is expressly not a claim to reimburse rescission payments that the ADSB subsidiary underwrote. Neither the institution's loss nor the law firm's breach, engagement scope, duty, causation or liability has been established by this choice-of-law decision.

**O1/O2 common sufficient proposition:** California law governs the imputation and associated defenses to these acquired California causes of action. Neither the FDIC's presence nor its interest in protecting the insurance fund establishes the specific significant conflict with federal policy necessary to create a federal rule displacing ordinary state law; the receiver does not acquire greater rights on this ground merely because the wrongdoers no longer control the institution.

Wallis requires identification of a particular conflict; Boyle preserves both a uniquely federal interest and a significant conflict with the particular state rule rather than treating the first as enough. Kimbell Foods demonstrates that even federal-program rights do not invariably demand uniform national rules, and that settled commercial expectations matter. The conclusion leaves genuine statutory displacement and established federal-common-law categories intact.

**O3 temporal limit:** The same pre-FIRREA analysis independently rejects the asserted federal displacement. It is unnecessary to decide FIRREA's temporal reach. If §1821(d)(2)(A)(i) applies as the FDIC is willing to assume, its succession to the institution's rights does not authorize this additional federal rule. Its specific limitations, repudiation, officer/director liability and agreement provisions have distinct triggers and do not supply an unstated exception to state imputation law for these claims. This conditional response is not a holding that FIRREA applies retroactively or that it universally occupies every receivership question.

**O4 disposition and remedy:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's federal-common-law judgment and remand for application of California law. Do not automatically reinstate the district court's unexplained summary judgment or direct a malpractice award. California adverse-interest/imputation rules, the actual attorney-client undertaking, duty, breach, causation, damages and properly preserved alternative defenses remain for the lower courts. The Court does not decide the assigned-investor-claim, repayment or statute-of-frauds issues by implication.

### Adversarial comparison

Path A rejects federal displacement. Its strongest contrary argument is the conjunction of a federal receiver, a national fund, dishonest controlling officers and a remedial mission benefiting innocent depositors. A federal rule could protect collections from local variations and prevent wrongdoers' knowledge from defeating recovery. But these concerns describe nearly every receivership collection action. They do not identify a federal duty requiring malpractice defendants to pay when state law denies the acquired institution a claim. California may itself decline imputation under the applicable facts; no federal override is needed merely to ensure that state law gets considered.

Path B creates a uniform federal no-imputation rule, or takes federal law as the source and adopts state rules selectively under Kimbell Foods. The decisive objection is that Kimbell involved rights generated by federal lending programs; these claims derive from a pre-existing state client relationship. Boyle involved procurement discretion and a concrete conflict with approved military specifications, while Wallis rejected displacement without the required conflict. The institution's insolvency and transfer of its state rights do not transform their source. Public Meyer distinguishes waiver/jurisdiction from the substantive cause, and American Dredging does not make abstract uniformity dispositive even within an established federal field. United States v. Texas preserved an existing federal interest rule for a debt owed to the United States; it did not federalize every acquired claim.

Path C relies exclusively on FIRREA's detailed succession regime to reject the new federal rule. That can explain why the FDIC's assumed statutory premise does not help, but an exclusive retroactive statutory ground would create an unnecessary Landgraf question. The shared pre-FIRREA analysis is independently sufficient. This preserves the genuinely different Landgraf approaches among these Justices without manufacturing a disagreement that the disposition need not resolve.

## 67. Romano v. Oklahoma, No. 92-9093

Argument: 1994-03-22. Direct review of Oklahoma's judgment sustaining the Sarfaty death sentence, 847 P.2d 368. The grant concerns the effect of revealing a separate death sentence on the sentencing jury's responsibility under the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments. Guilt, the Thompson prosecution's disposition and every other state appellate issue are not independently before the Court. All eight reach this preserved merits question; no Teague bar is applied.

### Fixed record and components

The Sarfaty jury initially found four aggravators: prior violent felony; especially heinous, atrocious or cruel murder; avoidance of arrest/prosecution; and continuing threat. It received seventeen mitigating circumstances and instructions requiring unanimous findings of at least one aggravator beyond reasonable doubt and that aggravation outweigh mitigation before death. The weighing itself was not required to be proved beyond reasonable doubt.

The contested exhibit disclosed the Thompson conviction, its death sentence, an execution command and notice of intended appeal. Defense offered to stipulate to the conviction and objected to the death sentence. The judge told this jury the other conviction was on appeal and nonfinal, and correctly charged the jury's responsibility in the Sarfaty proceeding. These facts must appear on both sides of the comparison; do not invent prosecutorial assurances of automatic execution or an appellate-court safety net.

Oklahoma held the death-sentence evidence irrelevant but rejected the constitutional claim. After the Thompson conviction was reversed for improper joinder, it removed the prior-violent-felony aggravator, reweighed the remaining three against mitigation and sustained the Sarfaty sentence. It continued to permit use of the underlying conduct for future danger because reversal was not for insufficient evidence. Thus the reviewed sentence is the three-aggravator, reweighed judgment. The separately reported reconviction is not something the Sarfaty jury knew and supplies no retroactive instruction or cure.

R1: Caldwell responsibility claim and its scope. R2: independent ordinary due-process unfairness claim. R3: remedy and effect of the later deletion/reweighing. Agreement on these distinct questions must not be manufactured from a judgment vote.

### Alternative paths and sufficient grounds

**A — affirm on existing Caldwell limits and whole-proceeding fairness:** Caldwell as limited by O'Connor's controlling concurrence and explained in Darden, Dugger and Sawyer concerns misleading the jury about its assigned role. A true report of another jury's verdict, coupled with notice of nonfinality and an accurate account of this jury's authority, does not misdescribe its legal role here. Irrelevance under state evidence law is not automatically an Eighth Amendment violation. Independently, Darden's due-process inquiry asks whether the proceeding was made fundamentally unfair, not whether evidence might have influenced a juror. The charges, nonfinality warning, lawful evidence of the underlying conduct and the actual proceeding do not establish that constitutional threshold. This position affirms without declaring the exhibit proper evidence or treating eligibility alone as harmlessness.

The strongest objection to A is that responsibility can be displaced by an exhibit's practical message even if each fact is true: another jury has already decided death, so this jury may believe its own judgment adds little. Generic correct instructions may not neutralize that particular reassurance. Victor and the public Graham/Johnson decisions forbid reliance on accurate isolated language when the operating procedure prevents the protected judgment. A's answer is the difference between a legal/instructional constraint on the current jury and speculation about the psychological effect of truthful evidence. This charge leaves every lawful sentencing option and mitigating use open; no official statement transfers the current decision to another body. The principle does not make instructions an automatic cure in every record.

**B — sentence relief for practical displacement of independent capital responsibility:** The official introduction of another jury's completed death determination can invite this jury to treat its own life-or-death choice as inconsequential. On this record the defense's offered stipulation could supply the relevant conviction without the irrelevant death determination; notice of appeal does not tell the jury to disregard the other sentence when deciding its own responsibility. Correct general responsibility instructions do not specifically answer that false practical inference. The heightened responsibility principle supports vacating the judgment sustaining death and remanding for constitutionally adequate further penalty proceedings, while preserving guilt and lawful consideration of underlying conduct.

This is an express proposed extension of Caldwell's protection to an official evidentiary disclosure producing the asserted displacement, or an application beyond the narrow literal-misdescription rule urged by A. It is not represented as already compelled by Dugger's statement about erroneous descriptions of the role assigned by state law. Blackmun's Dugger dissent expressly preserved the possibility of accurate language minimizing responsibility, and Stevens joined it; the broader responsibility principle in Caldwell supplies their earlier basis. For Souter and Ginsburg, current public functional-sentencing/reliability positions support the extension but do not themselves decide this question. The strongest objection is that the Constitution does not prohibit all prejudicial or irrelevant facts and that this jury knew its independent authority. B responds to the specific effect of a second official death determination, not a general relevance rule. No finding of actual juror thought or assurance of inevitable execution is invented.

**R2 distinction:** The affirming four additionally reject a separately sufficient Darden due-process claim at the whole-proceeding threshold. The four favoring sentence relief rely on R1's capital-responsibility protection and do not decide that the general Darden standard independently requires relief. They do not join a holding rejecting that independent theory; it is unnecessary to their disposition. Their refusal to join is not counted as four votes for the opposite due-process holding.

**R3 distinction:** The affirming four find no constitutional error and therefore do not rest on a new Supreme Court harmless-error finding. They review the modified three-aggravator judgment. The four favoring relief reject the proposition that deletion of the prior-conviction aggravator and reweighing automatically cures impairment of the jury's global responsibility; the state court did not use their responsibility rule in its reweighing. Their judgment is vacatur of the state judgment insofar as it sustains death and remand for proceedings consistent with that rule, without directing acquittal, release or automatic life imprisonment. No appellate harmlessness is conclusively found; any constitutionally permissible remedial inquiry must actually address this error rather than rely merely on three surviving aggravators. A new adequate penalty proceeding or a lawful noncapital disposition remains available as appropriate under state procedure.

## 68. Ibanez v. Florida Board of Accountancy, No. 93-639

Argument: 1994-04-19. Review: First District Court of Appeal, 621 So.2d 435, affirming the May 12, 1992 reprimand without opinion.
