## 13. Weiss v. United States

No.92-1482, including Hernandez under Rule12.2; argued November 3, 1993; decided January 19, 1994. Both military appellate judgments affirmed, **9-0 in result**. Rehnquist authored the Court opinion; Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg joined in full. Scalia and Thomas joined Parts I and II-A, and otherwise concurred in part and in the judgment. Souter and Ginsburg each wrote an unjoined concurrence. Scalia wrote a concurrence in part and in the judgment, joined by Thomas. No nonparticipant.

Already commissioned military officers did not need a second appointment for the judicial duties at issue. The Court's full reasoning includes the statutory assignment structure and germaneness; the lack of fixed terms does not violate due process in this military setting. Scalia and Thomas insist that germaneness is constitutionally necessary where assignment by the Judge Advocates General cannot constitute a new appointment, and reject open-ended balancing of independence against accountability as a substitute for history. Souter addresses inferior-officer analysis; Ginsburg emphasizes servicemembers' constitutional protections within the military framework. Historical full-opinion support is seven, not nine. Civilian appellate appointments and actual or apparent bias are not decided.

Accuracy checks: retain both appellants and distinct military convictions; do not equate a command prohibition with evidence of actual bias. The statutory and safeguard facts are detailed in the neutral handoff. First-pair reconciliation is separately frozen in [RECONCILED_A1](OT_1993CHUNK2_RECONCILED_A1.md); the narrower Scalia/Thomas grounds there result from individualized reconciliation, not automatic vote copying.

## 14. Schiro v. Farley

No.92-7549; argued November 1, 1993; decided January 19, 1994. Seventh Circuit judgment, 963 F.2d962, affirmed **7-2**. O'Connor authored for Rehnquist, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Blackmun wrote a separate dissent and joined Stevens's dissent; Stevens's dissent has Blackmun as its only join. No nonparticipant.

The Court declined the State's belated Teague argument, treated the sentencing as part of the initial prosecution, and held that Schiro did not establish a necessarily decided favorable intent issue. It did not hold that collateral estoppel can never operate between guilt and sentencing or decide that broader question. Stevens reads the blank knowing-murder count as an acquittal on this trial record; Blackmun also explains why capital sentencing can receive double-jeopardy protection. The historical reference to overruling Grady cannot replace the simulation's retained Grady rule.

Critical accuracy checks: CountI charged **knowing**, not solely intentional, murder; CountII charged murder during rape and CountIII during criminal deviate conduct. The sole guilty verdict on CountII and blank other forms are not express special findings. The ten forms included a single general not-guilty form, not three count-specific not-guilty forms. Instruction8 stated knowledge or intent; its scope across counts was disputed. Instruction to sign the verdict(s) and counsel's one-verdict presentations must not be collapsed into an express legal command to return only one verdict. The statutory death aggravator required **intentional killing during rape**, not just felony murder. The jury's recommendation against death was advisory, without a special intent finding; the judge later supplied written findings of intent and no mitigation. Teague was not raised below or in the opposition to certiorari, unlike Caspari. These are model-relevant record facts, not a predetermined estoppel resolution.

## 15. Albright v. Oliver

No.92-833; argued October 12, 1993; decided January 24, 1994. Seventh Circuit, 975 F.2d343, affirmed **7-2 in judgment**, with no Opinion of the Court adopting one complete rationale. Rehnquist announced judgment and wrote the plurality joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Ginsburg. Scalia and Ginsburg each filed an unjoined concurrence. Kennedy, joined by Thomas, concurred in the judgment. Souter separately concurred in the judgment. Stevens dissented, joined by Blackmun. No nonparticipant.

The plurality uses the Fourth Amendment as the specific constitutional source for the alleged pretrial liberty injury and rejects the substantive-due-process theory. Kennedy/Thomas address the nature of prosecution and, alternatively, adequate state remedies for random unauthorized conduct under Parratt. Souter finds no distinct substantial liberty burden outside the Fourth Amendment's ordinary coverage, while preserving a residual role for substantive due process. Stevens/Blackmun would recognize the asserted substantive due-process protection. Do not combine these rationales into a single majority rule through a head count or assume that one is a logical subset without analysis. The actual Fourth Amendment claim remains undecided.

Critical accuracy checks: Albright initially invoked the Fourth Amendment, but abandoned it in District Court and did not renew it here. The surviving presented theory is substantive due process; a broadly phrased certiorari question does not restore an abandoned claim. State procedural adequacy was not attacked. The complaint's assertions are accepted at Rule12(b)(6), not trial findings. State malicious-prosecution claims were dismissed without prejudice; neither municipal liability nor the officer's immunity supplies an adjudicated current merits holding simply from this affirmance. Appellate commentary on accrual does not establish a definitive Supreme Court limitations judgment.

## 16. National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler

No.92-780; argued December 8, 1993; decided January 24, 1994. Seventh Circuit, 968 F.2d612, reversed **9-0**. Rehnquist authored for Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Souter concurred, joined by Kennedy; there are no other separate opinions or nonparticipants.

The clinics' pleaded extortion and business/property injury suffice for standing at the pleading stage. Section1962(c) requires no economic motive for the enterprise or predicate acts, and the derivative section1962(d) dismissal cannot rest on that motive requirement. Predicate elements remain necessary. The Court does not resolve every Hobbs Act allegation, injunction entitlement or First Amendment defense. Souter/Kennedy emphasize that speech concerns can matter in construing predicates and assessing proof even though they do not create an economic-motive requirement.

Accuracy checks: NOW itself was not a RICO plaintiff; only DWHO and SWHO were. No class was certified. Dismissed Sherman Act and section1962(a) claims are not revived wholesale by the limited economic-motive ruling. DOJ's 1984 charging guidance allowed an economic **or other identifiable** goal. The constitutional-construction argument had not been raised in the Court of Appeals. No later Scheidler decisions are date-eligible law here.

## 17. ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB

No.92-1550; argued December 1, 1993; decided January 24, 1994. Tenth Circuit, 982 F.2d441, affirmed **9-0 in judgment**. Stevens authored for Rehnquist, Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Kennedy filed an unjoined concurrence. Scalia concurred in the judgment, joined by O'Connor. No nonparticipant.

The Board retains remedial discretion despite a claimant's false hearing testimony; no categorical forfeiture rule governs, and the particular reinstatement/backpay order was not an abuse. Section10(c)'s actual-for-cause discharge bar remains distinct. Kennedy emphasizes the gravity of dishonest testimony and the latitude to deny relief in an appropriate case. Scalia/O'Connor reject the Court's favorable equitable characterization of the Board's response, distinguish disbelieved testimony about motive from the established factual lie, and regard the Board's failure even to consider a sanction as barely tolerable. They nevertheless accept that the Board acted within lawful discretion and that the Court cannot compel the sanction. The actual reasons for Scalia's concurrence must be preserved rather than making all nine endorse every equitable justification in the majority.

Accuracy checks: the limited petition concerns hearing testimony, not a reopening of the established antiunion discharge or the original lie to the employer as a stand-alone defense. Board rejection of employer testimony is not a criminal perjury conviction of its witnesses. Reinstatement may be withheld for a supported discretionary reason; it is not automatically forbidden whenever testimony is false.

## 18. Department of Revenue of Oregon v. ACF Industries

No.92-74; argued November 8, 1993; decided January 24, 1994. Ninth Circuit, 961 F.2d813, reversed and remanded **8-1**. Kennedy authored for Rehnquist, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stevens dissented alone. No other separate writing or nonparticipant.

Section11503(b)(4) does not prohibit the challenged exemptions within a generally applicable ad valorem property tax. The definition of commercial and industrial property and subsections(b)(1)-(3) constrain a reading of(b)(4) that would nullify the permission for exemptions. The Court leaves open a scheme in which railroads alone, or railroads and only an isolated group, bear the tax. Stevens reads(b)(4) as a residual discrimination prohibition but does not regard every exemption as impermissible or embrace an automatic full-exemption remedy without the separate discrimination inquiry.

Accuracy checks: subsection(a)(4)'s definition is not the same text as(b)(4); do not invent an express exemptions clause in the latter. District Court31.4% and Ninth Circuit minimum25% are different calculations. Some ostensibly favored property bears substitute taxes. Threshold statutory coverage, actual discrimination, and remedy must remain separate; reversal/remand is not a universal validation of every selective rail tax.

## 19. Northwest Airlines, Inc. v. County of Kent

No.92-97; argued November 29, 1993; decided January 24, 1994. Sixth Circuit, 955 F.2d1054, affirmed **7-1**, Blackmun not participating. Ginsburg authored for Rehnquist, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Thomas dissented alone. No other separate writing.

The Court assumes an AHTA private action for this case without deciding a general implied right, because ruling it out would remove the airlines' favorable crash/fire/rescue remand although Airport filed no cross-petition. The challenged charges satisfy the adopted Evansville fair-use approximation, no-excess and nondiscrimination standard on this record. The Court does not conclusively resolve the scope of congressional authorization under the dormant Commerce Clause; the fees survive even assuming that inquiry remains. Thomas reads the Act as prohibiting fees calculated on the listed bases rather than regulating every airport user fee. He would remand for initial lower-court consideration of the dormant Commerce Clause merits; his dissent is not merely a demand for a stricter cost formula.

Accuracy checks: affirmance preserves the Sixth Circuit's favorable CFR remand; it does not restore100% airline allocation. District Court's parking break-even correction was also unappealed. No AAIA action question is before the Court. General aviation was not proven chiefly intrastate. The cost findings are lower-court findings, not statutory mandates; concessions' surplus and airlines' own break-even fees are distinct. No reason for Blackmun's nonparticipation is stated and none should be invented.

## 20. Hagen v. Utah

No.92-6281; argued November 2, 1993; decided February 23, 1994. Utah Supreme Court, 858 P.2d925, affirmed **7-2**. O'Connor authored for Rehnquist, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Blackmun dissented, joined by Souter. No other separate writing or nonparticipant.

The majority reads the1902 public-domain restoration provision as effective with the later amendments and contextual evidence, finding diminishment and state jurisdiction at Myton. Blackmun/Souter find the statutory sequence and removal of public-domain language insufficiently clear under the Indian-law canons. The disposition is not an adjudication of every portion of the larger Uintah and Ouray territory. Estoppel was neither presented in the petition nor retained in the supplemental brief.

Critical accuracy checks: the1902 consent condition, June1902 payment amendment,1903 unilateral-allotment authority,1904 postponement, and1905 entry terms must remain separate. The1905 Act expressly repealed the south-of-Strawberry grazing restriction, not expressly the public-domain wording. The legal effect of its omission is contested. The$70,064.48 payment settled separate claims and was not a fixed purchase price for the entire reservation. Administrative explanations promised continuing agency protection as well as described changed outside boundaries. Later jurisdictional exercise was substantial but not uncontested. Neither demographics nor terminology in later documents substitutes for congressional action. The neutral handoff includes both sides of those facts.

## 21. FDIC v. Meyer

No.92-741; argued October 4, 1993; decided February 23, 1994. Ninth Circuit, 944 F.2d562, reversed **9-0**. Thomas authored for Rehnquist, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. No separate writing or nonparticipant.

The claim is outside section1346(b), so section2679(a) does not make FTCA relief exclusive. FSLIC's broad sue-and-be-sued provision waives immunity for this constitutional tort claim. The separate cause-of-action inquiry nevertheless defeats recovery: Bivens does not create a direct agency damages remedy. The Court relies on the individual-officer structure and deterrence of Bivens, and independently on fiscal-policy considerations counselling against judicial creation of agency damages. It does not decide the underlying due-process merits or reopen Pattullo's qualified-immunity judgment. Sovereign immunity and a substantive cause of action are analytically distinct.

Accuracy checks: FSLIC was receiver under both federal and state appointments; the1982 employment action remains governed by the pertinent former FSLIC statute despite1989 repeal and FDIC substitution. The agency and individual judgments differ. Section2679(a), with its cognizable-under1346(b) condition, is distinct from employee exclusivity under2679(b)(1). A claim's failure of proof is different from a claim outside the1346(b) category. The supplied comparator includes Bush in an authority list, but the official Meyer opinion does **not** cite Bush; use it only if independently relevant entering law, not as a reported relied-on precedent. The precise statutory sequence is in the sanitized handoff.
