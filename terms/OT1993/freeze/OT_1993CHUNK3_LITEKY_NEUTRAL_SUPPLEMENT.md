## Combined reported allegations

### Original recusal motion: 1983 proceedings

Petitioners alleged impatience, disregard of the defense, and animosity toward Bourgeois, his codefendants, and their beliefs. The reported examples are:

1. The judge stated at the outset that the proceeding was a criminal trial rather than a political forum.
2. After Bourgeois's opening account of his protest's purposes, the judge stated that the opening should address the anticipated evidentiary showing.
3. The judge limited defense cross-examination and questioned witnesses.
4. The judge cautioned defense counsel to ask questions concerning material trial issues and cautioned witnesses to give responsive answers concerning those issues.
5. The judge admonished Bourgeois that closing argument was not an occasion to make a political speech.
6. Petitioners regarded Bourgeois's sentence as excessive. The report does not establish a separately presented sentencing error.
7. The judge interrupted a codefendant's closing argument to stop the introduction of new facts and require discussion of evidence already presented. Petitioners' counsel identified this as the most serious asserted incident at argument.

### Current prosecution and renewed motion: 1991 proceedings

The indictment charged willful destruction of United States property under 18 U.S.C. §1361, including spilling human blood on walls and objects at Fort Benning. Before trial, the district judge denied the recusal motion on the stated view that matters arising from judicial proceedings were not a proper basis for recusal.

Bourgeois's counsel proposed a defense focused on the political purposes of protesting United States involvement in El Salvador. The judge said petitioners could state their political purposes in opening and testify about them, while excluding lengthy speeches or discussion of Government policy. During opening, counsel began explaining events in El Salvador. After a prosecution objection, the judge excluded that discussion and directed counsel to address the expected evidence.

At the close of the prosecution's case, Bourgeois renewed the motion. He added the judge's public admonishment concerning that opening and unspecified admonishments of others, particularly his two pro se codefendants. The motion was denied. The convictions and the lower court's rejection of the recusal challenge are the posture for review; no separate new finding about any incident is supplied.
