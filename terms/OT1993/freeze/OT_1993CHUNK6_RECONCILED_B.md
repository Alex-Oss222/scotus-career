## 65. City of Ladue v. Gilleo — No. 92-1856

Argued February 23, 1994; decision date June 13, 1994. The reviewed Eighth Circuit judgment, 986 F.2d 1180, maintains the injunction against the replacement residential-sign ordinance. The amendment challenges that replacement; the prior ordinance's repeal does not remove this dispute. The separately handled fee adjustment is not a fresh merits component.

### Components and reconciled rules

**L1 — Residential channel.** All eight affirm because the replacement scheme virtually eliminates a distinctive, inexpensive means by which residents express their own views at their homes, without adequate alternative channels. The sufficient ground assumes content neutrality for decision. Residential signs identify the speaker, communicate to neighbors and passersby, operate continuously without the speaker's attendance, and impose little cost. Newspaper advertisements, distribution, meetings, hand-held signs, or flags do not adequately preserve that combined function. The Court need not find each substitute useless, demand an identical substitute, or deny substantial aesthetic and safety interests.

The ten exemptions are municipal signs; subdivision/residence identification; road or driveway danger, direction and identification; health-inspection signs; signs for churches, religious institutions and schools under their separate provision; identification of other nonprofit institutions; public-transport stops; property sale, rental or exchange signs subject to the owner's/agent's identification, permissible-message and placement limits; commercial signs in commercial or industrial districts; and safety-hazard signs. Residence identification has the stated one-square-foot condition. The sale/rental/exchange category does not permit attachment to trees, fences or utility poles. The separate filling-station provision does not restore ordinary household political speech. Category-specific dimensions not reproduced in the neutral packet are not adjudicated by inference.

**L2 — Classification reserved.** No Justice must decide the independent validity of every content distinction or whether severing all exceptions would cure the general prohibition. Assuming the distinctions permissible does not approve them. The ten exemptions inform the scheme's reach but are not collapsed into a single independently adjudicated content violation. O'Connor retains the normal sequence of asking whether a speech restriction is content based and then applying the corresponding scrutiny. This unusual assumption is permissible because the scheme fails even the neutral-regulation inquiry; it creates no general exemption from the usual analysis.

**L3 — Remedy and limits.** Affirm the judgment maintaining relief against the challenged replacement scheme. Do not restore the old variance procedure, adopt the unpreserved six-square-foot substitute, decide a new ordinance, or alter fees. Otherwise lawful size, number, location and safety rules remain possible. No absolute right to any physical medium or blanket least-restrictive-means test follows.

The strongest sustaining path is Vincent's recognition that signs themselves cause visual clutter and that a city need not leave the precise source of that clutter untouched. It fails here because Vincent preserved private-property signs and did not eliminate this distinctive home-based channel. Linmark supplies the functional inquiry into inadequate, costlier or less effective alternatives; its reservation about genuinely neutral regulation is preserved. The independent classification route under Discovery Network could go farther, but the necessary medium ground avoids deciding all ten categories. The historical full report, 512 U.S. 43, 48–59, confirms this limited application rather than replacing the legal inquiry.

## 66. O'Melveny & Myers v. FDIC — No. 93-489

### Components and reconciled rules

**O1 — Ordinary imputation.** California law governs imputation of the controlling officers' knowledge to the institution and the related defenses to these acquired California claims. Their alleged fraud, ownership/control, and the displacement of those officers do not themselves create a federal receiver's exemption. The Court does not choose California's substantive imputation answer.

**O2 — Independent pre-FIRREA ground.** Federal common law requires a specific significant conflict with federal policy in an appropriate federal-interest category. The FDIC's fund-protection interest, desire for more recovery and administrative uniformity do not identify that conflict here. Wallis requires the particular conflict; Boyle does not make a uniquely federal interest sufficient by itself; Kimbell Foods distinguishes federal-program rights and shows that even such rights need not have a nationally uniform rule. United States v. Texas preserved an existing federal rule concerning federal debts; it does not federalize every acquired tort claim. Meyer distinguishes waiver and a substantive cause, not a new receiver imputation privilege.

**O3 — Conditional FIRREA analysis.** If FIRREA applies, §1821(d)(2)(A)(i)'s succession language transfers the institution's rights subject to genuine enacted exceptions; it does not authorize adding the proposed anti-imputation rule. **O4 — Remedy.** Reverse and remand for consideration under California law. The strongest federal path treats protection of a nationally administered deposit-insurance program and innocent creditors as warranting an exception once the wrongdoers lose control. ## 67. Romano v. Oklahoma — No. 92-9093

Argued March 22, 1994; decision date June 13, 1994. The direct-review question concerns the effect of the Thompson death-sentence exhibit on the Sarfaty capital sentencing, not guilt, the whole Thompson prosecution, or a Teague bar.

### Components, shared premises, and competing rules

The exhibit reported the then-existing Thompson conviction and death sentence, included an execution command and notice of appeal, and was accompanied by the judge's explanation that the judgment was not final because of appeal. The defense offered to stipulate to the conviction. Correct general instructions described this jury's sentencing duty. They did not direct the jury to disregard the other death sentence. The later Thompson reversal concerned improper joinder, not insufficient evidence. The state appellate court removed the prior-violent-felony aggravator and reweighed the **three remaining aggravators**: especially heinous, atrocious or cruel murder; murder to avoid arrest/prosecution; and continuing threat. Seventeen mitigating circumstances were presented. The jury had to find at least one aggravator unanimously and beyond a reasonable doubt, and find aggravation outweighed mitigation; the packet does not impose a beyond-reasonable-doubt weighing standard. Underlying prior conduct and the other jury's punishment decision are distinct.

**R1 — Caldwell responsibility.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject the claim because the exhibit and instructions did not inaccurately describe the jury's legal role under state law. Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would vacate because official introduction of the other death determination creates an unacceptable risk that this jury will not independently undertake responsibility for choosing death. The latter four do not invent a finding of actual juror reliance or assume that execution elsewhere was certain. The challenged feature is another jury's death choice, not merely prior criminal conduct.

Caldwell, 472 U.S. 320, is not wholly a plurality: O'Connor joined Parts I, II, III, IV-B, IV-C and V, declining only Part IV-A. Her separate writing preserved accurate, nonmisleading explanation of postsentencing procedure. No Marks computation over the entire opinion is warranted. The affirming bloc relies additionally on Darden, Dugger and Sawyer's formulations concerning misdescription of the jury's role. O'Connor's historical Romano concurrence, 512 U.S. 14–15, makes inaccuracy a necessary condition of this Caldwell claim. Her join barrier against a categorical immunity for all truthful evidence concerns other constitutional grounds; it does not make her accept a broader Caldwell test.

The relief bloc's strongest legal objection to the affirmers is that Caldwell's Court-endorsed responsibility principle reaches the practical effect of the official evidence, not merely a false verbal description of law. Its own contrary-authority answer must be expressed: Darden's relevant remarks occurred at guilt rather than sentencing and tended to increase responsibility; Dugger resolved procedural default and expressly did not decide the underlying Caldwell merits; Sawyer concerned whether a rule was new under Teague. Those cases are serious contrary evidence, not ignored or silently overruled. On direct review here, the relief bloc would apply Caldwell's responsibility rationale to evidence that affirmatively tells the jury another sentencer has already selected death. General role instructions and notice of appeal do not specifically remove that message. This is a disputed application of the existing principle, not a falsely described unanimous holding of Caldwell and not an automatic consequence of the simulated mitigation decisions.

**R2 — Independent ordinary due process.** The affirming four also reject the claim under Donnelly/Darden's fundamental-unfairness standard after evaluating the proceeding as a whole. The proper instructions, evidentiary setting and lack of a demonstrated distortion do not establish a fundamentally unfair sentencing here. Speculation that the other death sentence might push either direction is not a finding of no possible prejudice and not a general rule that true evidence is always harmless. The relief four do not reach an independently sufficient ordinary-due-process ground; their silence supplies no join for rejecting it.

**R3 — Remedy.** The affirming four affirm the state appellate judgment as modified following removal of one aggravator; their position is no federal constitutional error, not a harmless-error cure by four aggravators. The relief four vacate the death sentence and remand for a constitutionally adequate new sentencing proceeding if the State continues to seek death, leaving the conviction intact and not compelling a death prosecution. Appellate reweighing of three aggravators does not cure the responsibility error they identify. No new factual determination or general guilt retrial is ordered here.

**R4 — General Eighth Amendment evidence theory.** Distinct from R1, the affirming four reject conversion of a state-law irrelevance objection into a federal constitutional evidence code. The individualized-sentencing principles associated with Woodson and Lockett do not exclude every arguably irrelevant item. The relief four need not resolve that independent proposition to grant R1 relief. R4 makes an existing issue explicit; it adds no neutral fact or new claim. The current Graham/Johnson rule protects a jury's ability to give mitigating effect; no supplied fact establishes that such effect was foreclosed here. Victor requires context-specific assessment rather than automatic cure by accurate general instructions. Nichols addresses the particular noncapital use of an uncounseled conviction with a meaningful opportunity to contest reliability/relevance; it does not control another death sentence's effect on capital responsibility.

The affirming four also distinguish two more particular objections within R4: the exhibit is not challenged as penalizing constitutionally protected conduct, and the subsequently invalid prior-conviction aggravator was actually removed before the state appellate court reweighed the remaining three. Historical Romano, 512 U.S. 10–12, discusses Zant and Johnson v. Mississippi on those respective points; the latter is distinct from the current-term mitigation authority Johnson v. Texas. The state court's treatment of the invalid aggravator does not itself answer the different responsibility claim asserted by the relief bloc. No Justice treats the Thompson conviction as still-valid aggravator evidence or mistakes the later reported reconviction for information known to the Sarfaty jury.

## 68. Ibanez v. Florida Board of Accountancy — No. 93-639

Argued April 19, 1994; decision date June 13, 1994. Review concerns the First District Court of Appeal's unreasoned affirmance, 621 So.2d 435, of the May 12, 1992 reprimand. That form supplied no appeal of right to the Florida Supreme Court. All eight reach the preserved as-applied speech dispute. Jurisdiction is now conceded; it does not establish that any particular advertisement is deceptive.

### Components, distinct provisions, and reconciled rules

**I1 — CPA.** All eight reverse the reprimand insofar as it rests on her truthful use of her active Florida CPA license. Her jurisdictional objection does not falsify the credential; no revocation or independently sustained conduct violation is assumed. Otherwise lawful professional regulation remains available. The withdrawn unlicensed-firm count cannot sustain this reprimand.

**I2 — CFP and prohibition theories.** Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg reverse the CFP ground because authorized private certification is useful, verifiable factual information and this Board record does not justify suppressing it as actually or inherently deceptive. The certifying organization is not identified in the advertisement, and CPA and CFP appear next to one another. These are real potential-confusion arguments; the seven reject treating them as sufficient proof of the actual harm and fit required for this restriction. No deceived customer is established by the anonymous mailing, and the three agency/former-agency witnesses are not consumer complaints. The hearing officer's recommended dismissal and the Board's reprimand are different acts. The certifier's described standards come from the reported amicus representation, not a newly adjudicated finding guaranteeing superior competence.

Paragraph **(g)** concerns a statement or implication of recognized specialization in public accounting unless the recognizing agency is approved by the Board. Paragraph **(i)** independently prohibits use of recognition by another entity using the term certified. Paragraph **(j)** is not an exception to (i). The seven's as-applied conclusion defeats the presented specialty/certification justification for the sanction; it does not invalidate every application of the three provisions or decide uncharged violations.

O'Connor reverses I1 but would sustain the CFP sanction.
