## Conclusion and exact objection

**No new join in the proposed conditional harmlessness remand is supported for Blackmun, Stevens, Souter or Ginsburg.** Each retains support for setting aside the existing Sarfaty death sentence and requiring a new adequate penalty proceeding if the State continues to seek death. Blackmun also retains his independently sufficient administered-system objection and preferred noncapital disposition. The four no-error affirmers remain O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; they supply no hypothetical conditional join.

The proposal properly distinguishes death eligibility from the selection of death, places the direct-review burden on the State, refuses to equate the prior deletion/reweighing with a completed responsibility-error determination, preserves admissible underlying-conduct evidence, and reserves the broader constitutional question. Those changes answer potential defects in a harmlessness formulation. They do not resolve the four relief Justices' remaining objection: permitting the existing sentence to survive a further harmlessness inquiry would reopen their conclusion about the effect of this official death determination on this jury's responsibility. A precise burden can govern an open effect question; it does not itself create a reason to reopen a question these positions resolve against retention of the sentence.

The original provisional B model left a constitutionally permissible remedial inquiry open if it actually addressed the responsibility error. ## Applicable law and the limits of the analogy

[Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep386/usrep386018/usrep386018.pdf), 21–26, permits harmless review for some constitutional trial errors and requires the beneficiary to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. It rejects a bare sufficiency-of-other-evidence inquiry and also preserves errors that cannot be treated as harmless. Chapman itself applied its standard and required a new trial rather than returning the established harmfulness question for a second attempt under a better formula. The standard's existence therefore proves neither that this responsibility error is amenable to such review nor that this record's application should remain open.

[Satterwhite v. Texas, 486 U.S. 249](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep486/usrep486249/usrep486249.pdf), 256–260, is the strongest capital-error analogy favoring the proposal. Its majority, joined by Stevens, held that the identified psychiatric-testimony error could be assessed under Chapman. It nevertheless distinguished sufficient lawful evidence from absence of contribution and concluded that the State had failed to establish harmlessness on that record. The decision does not establish that every capital error is structural or that every newly identified capital error requires an initial lower-court harmlessness remand. Blackmun joined only Part II of Marshall's separate opinion and wrote at 267–268; his objection addressed the particular Estelle v. Smith examination/testimony error and the difficulty of measuring its consequences. He did not join Part I's broader doubts about all capital harmless-error review. Those scopes matter here.

Caldwell, 472 U.S. 320, 328–341, addresses responsibility for the individualized death choice and the limits of reconstructing that choice from a record. Its conclusion that the Court could not say the responsibility-minimizing effort had no effect supported sentence vacatur. Historical Romano's responsibility dissent applies that reasoning to this record and expressly reaches a new-hearing remedy, 512 U.S. 1, 24–25; Blackmun's personal writing at 15 agrees with that analysis as well as supplying his independent objection. Those are date-eligible personal positions used as evidence, not an imported historical Court holding.

The refusal does not treat proof of a constitutional violation alone as automatic proof of harmfulness. It rests on the particular official death-commanding exhibit, its presentation as evidence despite the offered conviction stipulation, the absence of a direction to disregard the other death choice, and the failure of general role instructions and appeal information to remove the identified risk. No new finding about what a juror actually thought is invented. The recognized risk concerns selection of death, not merely proof of an aggravator. Even assuming this kind of error can be assessed under Chapman, these four positions do not support leaving this record's consequence undecided. A universal structural classification is unnecessary and is not adopted by this audit.

Actual prior simulated law must also be kept distinct from historical collateral-review doctrine. The public **Brecht** entry of April 21, 1993 retains Chapman, with the State bearing uncertainty, for the preserved Doyle claim on habeas and remands for its unresolved application. Blackmun and Souter joined that rule/remand; Stevens supported a demanding whole-record Kotteakos approach in dissent. That is affirmative evidence that Blackmun and Souter can support conditional relief for an assessable error whose effect remains unresolved. It is not a general requirement to remand rather than resolve effect, and the historical Kotteakos constitutional-habeas outcome is not governing law here. Romano is direct review; no collateral finality rationale changes its burden or authorizes a lesser standard.

The public **Sullivan** entry of June 1, 1993, and the current public **Victor/Sandoval** entry preserve structural treatment where an invalid reasonable-doubt charge leaves no constitutionally valid guilt verdict to assess. Blackmun, Stevens and Souter joined Sullivan; Ginsburg's current Victor position operates under that law. These authorities warn against an appellate substitute for the constitutionally required jury judgment, but do not by themselves classify Romano's distinct sentencing-responsibility error. They neither compel a categorical no-harmlessness rule here nor supply a reason to disregard the four Justices' actual assessment of this record.

## Separate the minimum disposition from the four-Justice remedial direction

The minimum disposition is **death-only vacatur and remand**. The direction requiring a new hearing if death remains sought is a further remedial proposition with **four non-Stone supporters**, not an established five-Justice command. If final assembly has an additional vote for vacatur/remand alone, that yields the common minimum without converting the four-Justice new-hearing direction into the Court's mandate. This audit supplies no additional vote and does not test any unexposed Justice's position.

The following formulation states the **four relief Justices' position**, subject to Blackmun's separate broader ground and preferred noncapital relief. Its new-hearing instruction must be attributed to those four unless actual assembly establishes additional support:

> Vacate the reviewed judgment insofar as it sustains the Sarfaty death sentence and remand for further proceedings. The existing death sentence cannot stand on this record. If the State continues to seek death, it must provide a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding that omits the other jury's death determination; independently admissible evidence of the underlying conduct remains available. Removing the prior-violent-felony aggravator and reweighing the three remaining aggravators does not cure the identified impairment of this jury's independent sentencing responsibility. The conviction and independent Thompson proceedings remain undisturbed. No precise noncapital sentence, acquittal or release is ordered. The case-specific disposition does not resolve the broader constitutional validity of capital punishment or declare that correcting this error answers Blackmun's separate objection.

The four Justices' agreement on that further direction is not four votes that renewed capital punishment is sufficient under Blackmun's broader theory. Their preferred remedy does not leave the original death sentence available for preservation through another harmlessness assessment, but this does not prove that the full Court has adopted that remedial limit. The State may elect a lawful noncapital disposition; permission is not a requirement that it seek death. The four/four issue-specific merits division remains unchanged. Assembly must distinguish the common death-only vacatur/remand from any fractured further directions, state the actual support for each proposition, and neither derive a fifth new-hearing vote nor infer a controlling Marks rule from this audit.
