#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Civil coercion, compensation, and criminal contempt

**Entering Law:** Gompers v. Bucks Stove & Range, 221 U.S. 418 (1911); United States v. Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258 (1947); Shillitani v. United States, 384 U.S. 364 (1966); Bloom v. Illinois, 391 U.S. 194 (1968); and Hicks v. Feiock, 485 U.S. 624 (1988). Civil contempt may compensate proven losses or coerce compliance through a genuinely avoidable/purgeable sanction. Serious criminal contempt carries criminal safeguards including a jury. [R54](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/821/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain reversal of the outstanding public fines on the criminal-procedure ground.

Stone's labor commitments neither immunize a union from sanctions nor diminish its procedural protections. Gompers, Mine Workers, Shillitani, Bloom, and Hicks require classification by operation. Announcing fixed fines before future violations does not make them civil: ordinary criminal laws also announce consequences in advance. Once imposed for completed breaches, these sanctions cannot be purged through renewed compliance and are not compensation for demonstrated loss.

The State's strongest argument is functional coercion: the announced schedule sought compliance with an ongoing decree. That purpose alone cannot distinguish it from punishment. The large remaining public fines, numerous out-of-court events, and substantial adjudication needed reinforce the criminal character. Beyond-reasonable-doubt proof supplied one safeguard without replacing the jury required for serious criminal contempt. Settlement did not moot the public assessments, and dissolution of the injunction makes their continuing punitive operation especially clear.

Do not generalize this into a ban on coercive fines that become payable over time. A genuine prospective per-day sanction avoidable through present compliance, a conditional sanction with a real opportunity to purge, and compensation measured by actual loss remain distinct. Nor should every difficult injunction become unenforceable in civil contempt. Courts should identify the sanction's beneficiary, measure, trigger, available purge, and whether it punishes a completed act rather than induces performance of an act still possible. No single dollar cutoff or complexity factor alone supplies the rule.

The simulated Dixon's treatment of criminal contempt and the proposed Kurth Ranch classification support functional analysis within their own limits; neither substitutes for contempt precedent. Reverse enforcement of approximately $52 million through these civil proceedings. Do not revive the vacated private fines or order a new conviction. Any further criminal proceedings require their own authority and consideration of all applicable defenses. This is a strong extension and clarification of received classifications, not a special labor immunity or a reason to invalidate the original injunction wholesale.

**Egalitarian application:** A union's lawful association, the affected community's safety, and a court's authority to enforce orders all deserve protection. Violent or obstructive conduct may be punished, but seriousness does not excuse the criminal safeguards appropriate to these fines. Civil coercion and compensation remain available when their actual legal predicates exist.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** During the 1989 labor dispute, an injunction prohibited violence, obstruction, and specified picketing conduct and imposed reporting and supervisory duties. The judge announced fixed sums for future violent and nonviolent breaches. Later civil hearings found more than 400 violations and imposed over $64 million; proof was beyond a reasonable doubt, but no jury was provided. After settlement the injunction was dissolved and about $12 million payable to the companies was vacated. Roughly $52 million payable to the public remained, collectible by Bagwell. No party justified those remaining sums as compensation for proven losses. [R54](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/821/)

The continuing public fines keep the case live despite settlement. The issue is the process for these sanctions, not wholesale invalidation of the original labor injunction. Advance notice and later ability to purge are different. The civil label and use of proof beyond a reasonable doubt do not answer the independent jury question. [R54](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/821/)
