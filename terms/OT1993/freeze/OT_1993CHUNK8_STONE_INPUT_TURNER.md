#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain vacatur for factual development under intermediate scrutiny, but give fuller reasons for distinguishing compelled editorial publication.

The Master's protection of the press function applies to corporate cable operators. Stone's rejection of superior civic status for artificial wealth cannot diminish a genuine editorial right, and private control of an important communications route is not state action. Tornillo and the relevant Pacific Gas & Electric reasoning create serious tension with compulsory carriage, not an analogy to dismiss merely by saying “competition.”

The legitimate distinction is a carriage rule directed at a physical distribution bottleneck and preservation of independently available broadcast service, triggered without judging the stations' individual messages. Carriage of separately identified signals also need not attribute their positions to the operator. These features support O'Brien/Ward treatment without extending Red Lion's spectrum rationale to cable. But source neutrality is not automatic content neutrality: the statutory distinction between commercial and noncommercial educational stations, Congress's references to valuable programming, and actual administration deserve examination for message preference. Stone should conclude that the operative scheme addresses distribution and access rather than selecting favored ideas, not declare those concerns legally irrelevant.

That is a substantial application across media, rather than a general right to compel private publication. Lower courts should test Congress's prediction of threatened broadcast service, cable incentives and capacity, the effects of denied carriage, and the programming actually displaced. Evidence of some dropped stations does not alone prove systemic jeopardy; spare capacity does not prove absence of burden on every affected operator. Reasonable forecasts can justify preventive action without waiting for failures. Judicial review must assess whether those forecasts have support and whether burdens are materially excessive; it need not demand scientific certainty or the least restrictive imaginable alternative.

The unresolved record prevents summary judgment for the defenders. Remand with those questions, leaving final validity and proper interim relief open. Stone's Ladue reasoning about realistic alternatives reinforces attention to actual speech effects, while MCI's limit on an agency's delegation says little about Congress's enacted choice. The cost of the intermediate approach is allowing some compelled carriage despite editorial preference; its bounded transmission rationale must prevent expansion into an editorial-balance mandate for newspapers or other media.

**User-directed case emphasis:** Congress may make supported predictions about market structure and access before the feared damage is complete. Test the connection between the particular speech burden and the identified danger without demanding certainty or treating findings as conclusive. This institutional lesson does not transplant the First Amendment's scrutiny framework into equal protection.
