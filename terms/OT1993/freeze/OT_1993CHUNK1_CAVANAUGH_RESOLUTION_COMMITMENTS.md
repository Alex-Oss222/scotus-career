## 3. Retained-review questions and exact alternative rules

### A. Section 1983 versus habeas

**Process-relief rule available for affirmance:** A state prisoner may seek prospective declaratory and injunctive relief under §1983 against responsible officials to restore the legally required frequency of discretionary parole consideration when the judgment neither orders release nor establishes an entitlement to release or a shorter period of custody. The Board retains the same lawful authority to deny parole. Preiser's exclusive-habeas rule for relief determining immediate or speedier release does not encompass this particular opportunity-only judgment. Patsy's rejection of a general §1983 exhaustion prerequisite applies once the claim is properly within §1983; it does not answer the anterior channel question by itself.

**Strongest objection:** The injury and requested remedy concern the opportunity to leave prison earlier, so the case attacks a condition of custody; pleading a hearing rather than release must not permit evasion of §2254. Chatman-Bey gives that objection concrete judicial support. **Response accepted by the affirmance side:** This judgment leaves the legality and duration of custody undetermined and does not restore a credit that itself reduces a term. Preiser distinguishes the scope of habeas availability from its exclusivity, and Wolff and Gerstein provide materially closer process-remedy examples. The constitutional reason a hearing matters does not transform the relief actually ordered into release. Greenholtz is corroborative procedural context, not a silent holding resolving the disputed channel.

**Habeas rule available for reversal of the channel ruling:** When the asserted constitutional wrong is a retrospective legal postponement of the prisoner's eligibility for consideration for release, and the requested injunction removes that temporal restriction in order to regain earlier release opportunities, the challenge goes to the terms of custody and belongs in habeas, although release is not assured. The claim is distinct from an attack on the fairness of an otherwise available hearing. Preiser's protection of Congress's more specific habeas remedy and Chatman-Bey's eligibility reasoning support this narrower rule.

**Strongest objection:** The requested declaration and annual hearing neither invalidate confinement nor compel earlier release, and Gerstein approved a required hearing without a release order. **Response accepted by the habeas side:** A claim that the legally permissible interval of confinement before renewed consideration has been increased is more than a defect in hearing procedure; timing eligibility is the substance of the challenged restraint. The distinction must be expressly confined to this temporal-eligibility claim rather than every process claim bearing on potential release. This is an extension of Preiser, not a proposition its exact holding already resolved.

### B. Ex Post Facto Clause, if the channel permits decision

**Narrow rule available for affirmance:** A State may not apply to a pre-enactment offender a later statutory requirement that removes every alternate mandatory parole-reconsideration opportunity by doubling the interval from twelve to twenty-four months after rejection, where ordinary parole depends on consideration and the record establishes no retained ordinary means of obtaining that intervening review. This binding postponement of an existing release opportunity makes the punishment more onerous notwithstanding unchanged initial eligibility, maximum sentence, and ultimate Board discretion. The rule does not require a finding that Roller would have been released at the missing hearing.

The legal predicate is the binding lost opportunity supplied by the offense-date statute and the actual calendar as administered, not merely disappointment or the word 'disadvantage.' Weaver rejects the vested-right defense and protects a legally available avenue to reduced imprisonment; Miller rejects the proposition that the same ultimate sentence could lawfully have been imposed as a complete answer to a binding adverse change. Collins preserves scrutiny of disguised increases in punishment while rejecting a free-standing prohibition of all procedural disadvantage. The new annual-review obstacle is materially different from correcting an unauthorized fine without increasing punishment in Collins and from the altered sentencing procedure addressed in Dobbert. No new balancing of prison costs against an established increase in punishment is adopted.

**Strongest objection:** Weaver reduced mandatory credits and Miller altered binding sentencing presumptions; Roller's parole grant remains wholly discretionary, the original sentence is unchanged, and the record proves no longer actual confinement. **Response accepted by the affirmance side:** The law previously required the Board to make a decision at the intermediate year and now prevents the ordinary opportunity. The Board's possible refusal on the old schedule does not make the lost legally required occasion meaningless. Lindsey's opportunity principle, applied in Weaver and Miller, prevents treating uncertainty about the old outcome as conclusive. The comparison does not assume a favorable parole decision or promise any particular release date.

**Narrow rule available for rejection of the claim:** On this record, changing the recurrent calendar from annual to biennial consideration after a denial does not establish a retrospective increase in the punishment attached to the offense, when the statutory sentence, initial eligibility, substantive suitability criteria, and wholly discretionary release decision remain unchanged. A required hearing is not itself a prescribed reduction in punishment; losing one does not alone establish the necessary change in punitive consequences under Collins. Weaver's mandatory credit reduction and Miller's binding sentencing range are distinguishable. This position rejects the per se inference from fewer occasions to greater punishment; it does not approve abolition of parole or any possible extension of the interval.

**Strongest objection:** The State admittedly eliminates a year in which a statutorily required consideration could have produced release and offers no ordinary expedited review. **Response accepted by this side:** That demonstrates a disadvantage in access to discretionary clemency, but the present record still does not establish a changed entitlement to a shorter term or a more onerous punishment rule of the kinds applied in Weaver and Miller. The State's absolute claim that the Clause can never reach parole is unnecessary and not adopted. No later 'significant risk' formula, numerical probability threshold, or universal administrative safe harbor is created.

## 4. Justice-specific conditional merits commitments

### Blackmun

**Channel and judgment:** Accept the process-relief rule and affirm the Fourth Circuit's prospective-relief judgment if review is retained. He joined Preiser's actual confinement/credit-restoration rule, so a generic opposition to exhaustion cannot explain this commitment. The decisive distinction is that no release entitlement is adjudicated. His supplied McCarthy opinion makes the actual congressional remedy and relief important; the specific-habeas rule controls within its proper scope, but does not supply a general exhaustion requirement here.

**Constitutional merits:** Adopt the narrow affirmance rule. His Weaver concurrence must be confronted, not omitted: he would have rejected the claim on a clean slate because good time removed only hope and opportunity, but accepted Lindsey and Greenfield as binding contrary authority. His subsequent full Miller join and Collins join permit application of the existing opportunity principle to a binding calendar while forbidding a general procedural-disadvantage rule. His supplied Austin opinion also refuses to let form alone settle whether a measure is punishment; it is only an analogy here.

**Alternative, barrier, minimum revision:** The strongest competing path distinguishes mandatory credits from discretionary parole and revives the skepticism expressed in his Weaver concurrence. That distinction does not overcome the fixed statutory exclusion from consideration on this record. He will not join language making any reduction in frequency automatically unconstitutional, treating every procedural right as punishment, or asserting inevitable additional incarceration. Delete those generalizations and tie the holding to a mandatory lost annual opportunity without ordinary intervening review. He can join that narrower formulation without adopting a due-process liberty interest in release.

**Remedy:** Affirm declaration and remand for an injunction only if needed to secure lawful annual consideration. No release, damages, renewed immunity adjudication, or permanent injunction against every part of the 1986 Act. January 1 developments may inform future equitable implementation without undoing the November 30 holding by assumption.

### Stevens

**Channel and judgment:** Accept §1983 and affirm. A process-only judgment supplies no determination of unlawful custody. His join in Patsy's rule, reflected in the entering-law treatment of McCarthy, supports refusing a new general exhaustion bar; Preiser's specific holding remains respected. The available remedy is the concrete opportunity, not the prisoner's ultimate aspiration for freedom.

**Constitutional merits:** Adopt the narrow affirmance rule. He joined Weaver and Miller, and his Collins concurrence rejected the majority's unnecessary repudiation of protection for substantial procedural rights. He need not reestablish that broader concurrence to decide this case: the statutory removal affects punishment's release opportunity, within Collins's surviving punitive category itself. His supplied Graham join recognizes the distinction between freedom to deny a favorable outcome and legal exclusion of the occasion for meaningful consideration; that capital holding is analogous, not controlling parole law.

**Alternative, barrier, minimum revision:** The best State argument invokes his Collins concurrence's distinction between trial/sentencing safeguards and changes to postconviction remedial machinery. Collins merely removed a windfall new trial after correcting an unauthorized fine; this law removes a recurring mandatory avenue out of confinement. He will not join the assertion that all changes in administration of an existing sentence are immune, nor require proof of the precise parole decision the Board would have made. He can join an opinion limited to this binding schedule without making his broader Collins approach a holding. He rejects a rule that expectation alone, without the offense-date legal requirement, suffices.

**Remedy:** Same narrow declaration and conditional injunction as Blackmun. Preserve the distinction between enforcing consideration and directing favorable exercise of discretion. Existing failure to schedule may justify further relief on remand, but this Court should not invent a hearing date or find contempt.

### O'Connor

**Channel and judgment:** Accept §1983 on this particular relief and affirm. Her supplied McCarthy join concerns the actual statutory gate and available remedy. It does not erase habeas exhaustion or authorize avoidance of §2254 by labels. Here an annual hearing leaves release entirely open, so the order does not accomplish the release or credit restoration sought in Preiser. The contrary comity argument has force but cannot extend the exclusive-remedy category to every process that may improve a prisoner's prospects.

**Constitutional merits:** Adopt the narrow affirmance rule. Her Miller opinion is the strongest individual authority: unchanged outer sentence limits and the possibility of the same outcome did not answer a binding adverse change; flexible discretionary guideposts were expressly distinguished from rules with legal force. Her Collins join requires locating the injury within greater punishment rather than undefined substantial personal rights. Here mandatory interval extension and the absence of ordinary interim consideration satisfy that narrow comparison.

**Alternative, barrier, minimum revision:** She gives full weight to Miller's distinction between binding sentencing standards and flexible parole guidance. The actual statute removes an occasion on which discretion must otherwise be exercised; it does not merely describe how discretion will be exercised. She will refuse a facial rule invalidating all changes to parole boards, voting requirements, standards, or hearing administration. Limit the decision to the crime-date mandatory annual schedule and this fixed two-year postponement, with no ordinary intervening review shown. She will not turn counsel's manual representation into a holding about every statutory exception; §24-21-610's medical terms remain distinct and factually inapplicable.

**Remedy:** Affirm the Fourth Circuit's requirement of declaratory relief and of injunctive relief only if necessary. A declaration may suffice; an injunction is not automatically mandatory. Preserve the district court's capacity to assess actual compliance and the later enactment at implementation.

### Scalia

**Channel and judgment:** Require habeas for the temporal-eligibility claim; vacate the prospective merits judgment and remand for dismissal of that §1983 claim without prejudice to a properly presented habeas claim. Preiser's specific statutory remedy and its refusal to allow pleading labels to evade exhaustion supply the ground. His supplied McCarthy concurrence rests on the unavailability of the sought money remedy, not broad judicial balancing; here habeas can provide the requested opportunity-related relief. Chatman-Bey is persuasive on this particular distinction, not binding on this Court.

**Constitutional merits if §1983 is accepted:** Reject the ex post facto claim and reverse the prospective-relief judgment. His Collins join supports requiring a change in the legally prescribed punishment rather than an undifferentiated loss of advantage. His Miller join is a substantial constraint: a nominally procedural legal change can increase punishment and the unchanged maximum alone is insufficient. He distinguishes Miller because its presumptive range, departure burden, and appeal protection bound the sentence, whereas the unchanged substantive parole discretion supplies no comparable promise of release. He does not adopt counsel's claim that the Clause categorically excludes every parole change.

**Alternative, barrier, minimum revision:** A bare 'no vested right, therefore no ex post facto protection' formulation is inconsistent with Weaver and cannot support his join. His available narrower position is that this recurring calendar, without alteration of sentence, initial eligibility, or release criteria and without further demonstrated punitive operation, does not alone prove an increased punishment. He could join a remand limited to materially disputed operation if the Court identifies such a record issue, but the current record presents the fixed schedule as undisputed; he does not invent an unrecorded expedited policy to sustain it. He cannot join affirmance merely by adding a sentence that release remains discretionary: that is his substantive objection, not a drafting omission.

**Remedy:** If his channel rule controls, no constitutional merits judgment and no damages revival. If the Court reaches and accepts his constitutional ground instead, reverse the prospective-relief portion and remand consistently with judgment for the officials on this federal ex post facto claim. Do not order continued biennial treatment contrary to any independently operative state duty, and do not erase Griffin.

### Kennedy

**Channel and judgment:** Accept §1983 and affirm. His McCarthy join supports matching exhaustion to the actual statutory and remedial setting. Here the requested order requires consideration while leaving its result undecided. The stronger competing argument is Preiser's concern for the State's first opportunity to resolve custody-related claims; nevertheless the order sought neither reduces a sentence nor adjudges continued confinement unlawful. He joins no general exemption for claims labelled 'procedural.'

**Constitutional merits:** Adopt the narrow affirmance rule. His Collins join confines the Clause to retroactively altered crime or punishment rules while warning that procedural labels cannot conceal increases. His supplied R. L. C. join treats a legally operative sentencing constraint as real despite discretion within the remaining range; it does not establish parole equivalence. Weaver and Miller remain governing precedent, and the compulsory removal of the intervening consideration opportunity supplies the punitive connection those cases require. This is a reasoned extension to the precise hearing restriction, not a claimed prior holding of his on parole intervals.

**Alternative, barrier, minimum revision:** The strongest opposing path characterizes reconsideration as administration of unchanged clemency discretion. It is insufficient here because the law forecloses the intermediate occasion rather than leaving the Board free to decide its usefulness in the individual case. He would refuse language equating every administrative inconvenience with punishment or presuming release in the omitted year. Confine the rule to the later binding postponement and preserve genuinely discretionary scheduling systems and the availability of fact-specific consideration in other records.

**Remedy:** Prospective declaration; injunction only to the extent necessary after review of compliance. No release direction or prediction about January 1. The State retains its unchanged discretion on suitability, and no new substantive criteria are imposed.

### Souter

**Channel and judgment:** Accept §1983 and affirm. Preiser leaves concurrent remedies outside its exclusive core; availability of habeas is not identical to exclusivity. His supplied McCarthy join requires identification of the actual congressional gate and practical remedy. Since no favorable parole outcome is legally required by a judgment here, requiring annual consideration is an appropriate prospective process remedy.

**Constitutional merits:** Adopt the narrow affirmance rule. His supplied R. L. C. opinion recognizes operative constraints beyond an abstract statutory maximum, while his supplied Graham opinion distinguishes genuine legal access to consideration from nominal admission that cannot have operative effect. Those are limited analogies; the controlling basis here is Weaver, Miller, and Collins. The legal promise is consideration every twelve months, not release; the later statute removes that obligatory opportunity and permits continued confinement without the intervening decision the law required.

**Alternative, barrier, minimum revision:** The best objection is that no record establishes what the Board would have done under an annual schedule. Miller forecloses making certainty of a favorable former outcome necessary where a legally material opportunity has been withdrawn. He will refuse a broad rule freezing every administrative feature at the crime date, as well as a rule based merely on subjective expectations. Specify the compulsory interval, the relation of consideration to possible release, and the absence of a preserved ordinary intervening opportunity. He reserves a system in which a longer scheduled interval coexists with meaningful individualized reconsideration; no formula for that different case is announced.

**Remedy:** Affirm narrowly, preserving the lower court's distinction between declaration and an injunction only if necessary. Future compliance and the effective statute are implementation questions, not invented current facts.

### Thomas

**Channel and judgment:** Require habeas for this challenge to the legally prescribed time before release consideration. Vacate the prospective judgment and remand for dismissal of the §1983 claim without prejudice to habeas. Preiser's textually grounded specific-remedy treatment supplies the legal basis. His supplied McCarthy judgment concurrence is confined to an administrative procedure that could not award damages; it supplies no general license to bypass a remedy that can address the claimed custody restriction. The precise parole-channel conclusion is an inference, not a previously located Thomas holding.

**Constitutional merits if the Court allows §1983:** Reject the claim on the narrower Collins ground stated above. His supplied Austin join recognizes that legal labels do not alone defeat punitive characterization, and his R. L. C. join respects legally operative sentencing constraints; neither supplies a categorical 'formal sentence only' theory. Even so, these precedents do not equate a recurring opportunity for wholly discretionary clemency with a mandatory credit or binding presumptive sentence. On this record the changed frequency alone is insufficient to prove that the punishment prescribed for the offense increased.

**Alternative, barrier, minimum revision:** Weaver and Miller make categorical reliance on the unchanged maximum or absence of a vested right untenable; he does not adopt it. The most difficult contrary fact is the Board's inability to consider the prisoner in the intervening year. His conclusion treats that as a calendar restriction rather than an established increase in the quantum of punishment, while reserving changes to initial eligibility, release criteria, or elimination of parole. He can join only an opinion that performs that comparison explicitly. A ruling for Roller would require acceptance of the legally lost opportunity itself as an increase in punishment; that is the substantive proposition he presently declines, not a gap curable by stylistic revision.

**Remedy:** Same two branches as Scalia, with no constitutional holding if the channel ground controls and no instruction displacing state law. No exhaustion exception is found merely from past Gunter precedent: Griffin now supplies favorable state law, and no absence or ineffectiveness of state corrective process has been established.

### Ginsburg

**Channel and judgment:** Require habeas for this statutory postponement of release consideration; vacate the prospective merits judgment and remand for dismissal of the §1983 claim without prejudice to habeas. Her own prior join is direct evidence: Chatman-Bey v. Thornburgh, 864 F.2d 804, 808–10 & nn.4–6 (D.C. Cir. 1988) (en banc), rejects the proposition that an eligibility challenge escapes habeas because success does not guarantee release. That opinion concerned a federal prisoner, mandamus/declaratory alternatives, and forum location. Those limits must be preserved; it does not mechanically decide a state §1983 case. Nevertheless, its central custody characterization fits the fixed postponement asserted here, and Preiser supplies the state-prisoner exhaustion consequence.

**Constitutional merits if the Court accepts §1983:** Adopt the narrow affirmance rule. In Warren v. United States Parole Commission, 659 F.2d 183, 197–99 (D.C. Cir. 1981), her dissent insisted that a prisoner receive an opportunity to prove rigid operation of later parole guidelines, rejected assumptions drawn from other records about flexibility, and explained that added prison time under the parole mechanism would be punishment for the original conviction. Warren did not find an ex post facto violation. Here the statute itself, the appendix, and counsel's account supply the binding interval and lack of ordinary interim consideration that Warren's undeveloped record lacked. Weaver and Miller support treating that legally excluded opportunity as material despite discretionary ultimate release.

**Alternative, barrier, minimum revision:** The strongest §1983 path is Wolff/Gerstein: this is a hearing order, with release neither asked nor ordered. Chatman-Bey itself distinguished those state cases and preserved the importance of access to a federal forum. She nevertheless regards the demanded alteration of a legal eligibility interval as materially different from requiring fair procedures at an available hearing. She will not join a broad holding that every process-only parole suit lies outside habeas, and mere deletion of release language does not answer her objection. A narrower holding confined to the fairness or conduct of an already available hearing would remove that objection but would not decide Roller's timing claim; such a revision therefore cannot silently be counted as her join in this §1983 holding.

On ex post facto merits her barrier runs the other way: no inferred administrative flexibility, no invented individualized early-review program, and no factual finding that Roller would have been released. Her narrower available affirmative holding is the fixed statutory loss of the annual consideration opportunity on this actual record. She does not freeze discretionary guidelines as law without examining their force or convert Warren's dissent into a controlling rule.

**Remedy:** On her channel ground, dismissal without prejudice preserves proper state habeas/postconviction recourse and any later available federal habeas remedy; no declaration that no constitutional injury occurred. If the Court reaches and adopts her constitutional position, she supports the same tailored declaration and conditional injunction as the affirmance group. She does not join an order granting parole or reinstating damages.
