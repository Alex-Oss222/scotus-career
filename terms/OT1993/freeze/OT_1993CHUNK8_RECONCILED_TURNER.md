## Justice-specific reconciliation

### Blackmun

**Disposition and joins.** Vacate the summary judgment and remand; adopt the principal opinion's full substantive classification, intermediate standard and III-B analysis, with the preserved statutory and footnote-6 limits. Retain his separate emphasis on substantial congressional deference. His protected-expression and judicial-review commitments do not require final invalidation.

**Historical comparison.** The original freeze is confirmed. His concurrence, 512 U.S. 669, explains that only portions of the legislative record were submitted, sufficient to oppose plaintiffs' summary judgment but inadequate to obtain defendants' summary judgment in the presence of a few unresolved material issues. He accepts the possibility that further material will support the law. This is not a finding that Congress had no evidence or that every station must prove impending bankruptcy.

**Strong contrary ground and response.** His Leathers dissent and Preferred Communications concurrence protect editorial freedom and require attention to cable's actual characteristics. Strict scrutiny remains a serious alternative. The full-power transmission rule and the independent free-service objective supply the noncontent distinction he historically accepted; Discovery Network's objection to an unexplained content choice does not remove that distinction. Conversely, predictive deference does not authorize judgment over genuine disputes. The actual June 24 Dolan dissent is compatible with deference here but does not decide this different record. No changed premise supports departure.

**Join barrier.** Do not recast his concurrence as rational-basis review, categorical acceptance of legislative findings or approval of every subsidiary carriage criterion. Minimum compatible formulation preserves substantial deference, the actual incomplete court record, and a bounded remand.

### Stevens

**Primary disposition and exact joins.** Affirm the judgment sustaining the core provisions. Join the substantive equivalents of I, II-C, II-D and III-A. Do not join II-A, II-B or III-B as complete portions. His concurrence, 512 U.S. 669–674 and notes 1–6, is affirmative evidence of these limits; historical silence is not converted into an invented objection to every sentence of the unjoined portions.

**Reconciliation correction.** The original freeze made him support remand because material evidence and fit questions remained. The comparator establishes the opposite application: he regards the existing congressional findings, bottleneck structure, incentives and large cable audience as sufficient. He accepts the general inquiry whether Congress drew reasonable inferences from substantial evidence provided Congress need not make a judicial or agency-style formal record (670–671 n.1). He rejects requiring further demonstration of serious harm to a broadcaster excluded from the cable audience. Protective action need not await industry collapse. Congress need not find every broadcaster vulnerable, and the separate retransmission-consent option may leave must-carry particularly useful to vulnerable stations; that is his prediction, not an adjudicated election outcome. Displacement forecasts can be speculative and may be affected by parties' voluntary conduct. He rejects III-B's requirement of additional proof for the facial constitutionality question.

**Conditional disposition accommodation.** He is willing to concur in vacatur and remand when otherwise no disposition commands a majority and his accommodation supplies a majority for the remand, because he substantially agrees with the intermediate-review analysis. His historical statement expressly invokes that condition and Screws, 325 U.S. 91, 134 (Rutledge, J., concurring in result). Preserve primary affirmance until assembly can test the condition against the final lawful positions. If the condition is met, record judgment concurrence and continued disagreement with III-B. Do not describe him as compelled by precedent to accommodate, import historical vote arithmetic, or count a theoretical remand preference as an already final vote. If the proposed accommodation cannot produce a majority, it does not itself settle the disposition.

**Strong contrary ground and why the correction is supported.** Preferred Communications and Discovery Network permit meaningful review and serious consideration of disputed effects. They do not establish that legislative prediction on this record is insufficient. His Leathers join, Discovery Network's allowance for incremental policy, MCI dissent's account of structural competition, and June 24 Dolan dissent's treatment of supported planning forecasts remain consistent with his historical acceptance of this evidence. Nothing in actual Ladue or the June 24 decisions displaces that premise. The original independent remand application supplies no concrete changed premise; independence alone cannot justify retaining it. This is a correction of the provisional application to the stronger comparator evidence, not a new historical departure.

**Minimum compatible formulation.** Retain case-specific content neutrality and intermediate review, acknowledge sufficient predictive support in his view, and make any remand support judgment-only under the condition. No coalition convenience can obtain his join in a claim that further evidence is constitutionally necessary.

### O'Connor

**Disposition and grounds.** Reverse. Join the principal opinion only through the equivalents of I, II-A and II-B. Adopt the separate opinion's Parts I–III: the statutory preference is content based and fails strict scrutiny; independently, even assuming neutrality, it places substantial burdens where the asserted competition/free-broadcast interests do not require them. The latter is a final tailoring objection, not merely a reason for more evidence.

**Historical comparison and refinement.** The primary strict-scrutiny commitment is confirmed. The original fallback description was too weak: 512 U.S. 682–683 says further factfinding cannot cure the excessive scope. Congress could protect threatened broadcasters or address anticompetitive behavior, but not restrict the broad category in circumstances where neither problem occurs. Her Parts I and III reject a compelled content preference while acknowledging cable monopoly and possible lawful competition, capacity-access or common-carriage measures. Those examples are reserved possibilities, not holdings that Congress must adopt them or that takings questions are resolved.

**Strong contrary ground and response.** Entire station schedules and free access support the structural account. She nonetheless treats enacted localism, educational and informational objectives, and operative local-programming criteria as evidence of a content justification; their possible severability does not erase that evidentiary function. Her own Leathers opinion distinguished general taxation from censorship risks, her Discovery Network join rejected benign-purpose neutrality, and her Ladue concurrence maintained classification-first scrutiny. Her strict ground is not dependent on R.A.V.'s unavailable historical majority. No changed premise displaces either historical sufficient ground.

**Limits.** Not every media or speaker distinction requires strict scrutiny. Localism and viewpoint variety are insufficiently compelling to impose this preference; educational/public-affairs aims fail tailoring even if their compelling status is assumed. Do not convert that assumption into a holding that they can never be compelling. No wholesale Cable Act relief or home-shopping finding follows.

### Scalia

**Disposition and joins.** Reverse on the same strict ground and independently on the O'Connor Part II intermediate-tailoring ground. Join I, II-A and II-B of the principal analysis; refuse the actual content-neutral classification and III-B remand rationale. His historical full join in O'Connor, including Part II, refines the original generic alternative into a concrete independently sufficient objection.

**Strong contrary ground and response.** Leathers allows neutral intermedia treatment, and station-wide carriage can serve transmission goals. But the enacted content objectives remain operative evidence in his view, and monopoly does not license Congress to choose favored speech on another private speaker's finite capacity. His published R.A.V. concurrence, Discovery Network join and Ladue join support that distinction. The surviving neutral regulatory possibilities in O'Connor Part III prevent a categorical speech-immunity claim. No new factual or doctrinal premise warrants divergence from the historical joins.

**Limits and minimum formulation.** Preserve his noncontrolling R.A.V. position without citing it as Court law; preserve the separate final intermediate-tailoring ground and the hypothetical character of alternate regulatory suggestions. An evidence-only remand does not resolve his legal objections.

### Kennedy

**Disposition and joins.** Vacate/remand. Adopt the principal analysis through III-B, including protected editorial choice, rejection of broadcast/rational-basis shortcuts, full-power content neutrality, substantial interests, and the incomplete-record application. All ancillary reservations remain.

**Historical comparison.** The original freeze matches. The Government must support both the likelihood of significant carriage losses and resulting serious economic deterioration, while meaningful judicial review asks for reasonable legislative inferences grounded in substantial evidence, not judicial replacement of Congress's policy or de novo reweighing. The absence of proof of actual bankruptcy is part of the observed evidentiary gap, not an indispensable condition to preventive legislation. The scope of displacement and usable capacity also matter to fit.

**Strong contrary ground and response.** Tornillo and PG&E protect editorial independence, and content references cannot be dismissed categorically. Here the station-wide rule, cable's physical gatekeeping, lack of a content-triggered penalty or expected endorsement, and preservation of free service support the different treatment. His Leathers join, Discovery Network join, Edenfield opinion and Ladue join support both the classification inquiry and meaningful justification. Actual R.A.V. does not supply his historical citation as a controlling rule, but surviving protected-speech authorities and his prior separate position sustain the relevant proposition. No change of result or operative ground is justified.

**Join barriers.** No universal station-by-station insolvency requirement, no compulsory legislative trial record, no least-restrictive-policy mandate and no final validation. The minimum acceptable remand preserves the Legislature's predictive role and the reviewing court's independent constitutional duty.

### Souter

**Disposition and joins.** Vacate/remand; adopt the full principal analysis including III-B. Treat the congressional predictive record seriously while requiring a supported link among threatened harm, the remedy and the actual expressive burden.

**Historical comparison and strongest alternative.** Confirm the original freeze. Affirmance has force because preventive economic legislation need not await a failed station, and MCI/Dolan continuity permits reasoned forecasts. Those positions do not transform this incomplete record into an adequate one. His Leathers, Discovery Network, Edenfield and Ladue joins support the distinction between an allowable structural interest and proof of its fit. Strict scrutiny has force as an editorial-space objection, but the full-power station-wide/transmission classification and lack of viewpoint selection provide the narrower lawful basis for intermediate review. No materially changed premise supports departure from the historical full join.

**Limits.** Preserve the statutory qualifications and footnote-6 reservations. Do not assert that unused capacity eliminates injury or treat the evidence of no past bankruptcy as proof of no prospective danger. No new quota, damages or final merits outcome on remand is supplied.

### Thomas

**Disposition and joins.** Reverse under the content-preference and strict-scrutiny analysis. Join the principal opinion's I, II-A and II-B, and the equivalents of O'Connor's Parts I and III. Do not join her Part II; no independently sufficient intermediate-tailoring vote is established for him.

**Historical refinement.** His original observation that the present judgment could not stand under another path must not be enlarged into a full alternative holding. The exact historical nonjoin is strong evidence against attributing the content-neutral overbreadth theory to him. He can oppose summary judgment on his strict ground without adopting either the remand plurality's evidence standard/application or O'Connor's broad intermediate invalidity rule.

**Strong contrary ground and response.** Structural bottleneck regulation remains possible, and his Discovery Network dissent join permitted incremental physical regulation using a commercial-speech distinction. The present compelled preference among protected cable speakers is different. His R.A.V. separate join, Edenfield join and Ladue join are consistent with recognizing the content injury while reserving distinct intermediate-review questions. No invented Leathers or Preferred Communications participation, later Thomas theory or unexplained vote is needed. History supplies no reason for a departure.

**Minimum compatible formulation.** Strict invalidity plus bounded relief, with no attribution of the unjoined alternative. Alternative capacity or common-carrier arrangements remain possibilities with their legal conditions, not dispositive approval.

### Ginsburg

**Disposition and joins.** Reverse. Join I, II-A and II-B of the principal opinion and O'Connor's entire separate opinion, including the independently sufficient intermediate-tailoring objection. Her own short explanation additionally emphasizes the lower dissent's content-preference and unsubstantiated-danger account, 512 U.S. 685–686.

**Historical comparison.** Her primary position is confirmed, and the complete alternative ground replaces the original weaker suggestion that the record merely prevents summary judgment. She sees no viewpoint selection, but a subject/content preference still warrants close review. An interwoven content-neutral objective does not neutralize enacted local-programming preference. If a remand nevertheless occurs and the danger evidence remains deficient, judgment should ultimately go to appellants; this conditional observation is not assent to the present remand.

**Strong contrary ground and response.** The station-wide rule and physical bottleneck give intermediate review substantial plausibility. The available predecision Ginsburg cable record is sparse, as the neutral freeze candidly states. Her actual Ladue join supports attention to lost expressive opportunities, but the same-matter full concurrence and joins now supply much stronger evidence than extrapolation from general sources. No changed simulated premise defeats those positions. MCI and Honda concern different statutory/procedural questions and provide no reason for a change here.

**Limits.** Do not turn insufficient evidence of current risk into a proved fact that no broadcaster could suffer. Do not attribute the historical Stevens R.A.V. quotation as a verified quotation from the prior simulated writing. Preserve the distinction between content and viewpoint and the possibility of lawful structural regulation.
