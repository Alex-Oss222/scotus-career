#### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Speech-neutral remedies and their tailoring

**Entering Law:** First Amendment; Ward, 491 U.S. 781 (1989); Frisby v. Schultz, 487 U.S. 474 (1988); Carroll v. Princess Anne, 393 U.S. 175 (1968); NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware, 458 U.S. 886 (1982); and Califano v. Yamasaki, 442 U.S. 682 (1979). Injunctions can address proven wrongdoing but may not suppress unrelated lawful expression; state-law access, traffic, and property protections supply the remedial interests. [R53](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/753/)

##### Issue 2: Parties, concerted action, and association

**Entering Law:** Regal Knitwear v. NLRB, 324 U.S. 9 (1945), governs the limited reach and construction of injunction language extending to persons acting with a bound party. Claiborne protects lawful association rather than liability merely from a shared viewpoint. [R53](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/753/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** sustain the 36-foot entrance and driveway buffer and targeted medical-noise restrictions; invalidate the unsupported private-land extension, broad images and residential restrictions, and the 300-foot consent-to-approach rule; remand to substitute Stone's narrower post-refusal protection.

This supersedes the earlier review's disposition that reserved several provisions and declined an injunction-specific standard. Stone now evaluates the challenged injunction component by component and adopts the closer tailoring appropriate to orders against identified parties after established misconduct.

The legal basis is the First Amendment together with Carroll's precision requirement, Claiborne's protection of lawful advocacy and association, Yamasaki's remedial limits, Ward's distinction between content-neutral and content-based burdens, and Frisby's specific residential protection. The proposed standard asks whether each restraint burdens no more speech than necessary to remedy the established conduct and serve the corresponding significant interest. This is an express development of injunction doctrine, not an inherited holding or a general rule that all injunctions receive strict scrutiny. Content- or viewpoint-based discrimination remains subject to its own more demanding rules.

The review-scope objection must be answered rather than ignored. The supplied questions support a narrower approach, as the [contemporaneous scope objection](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/93-880.ZX.html) explains. Stone treats the challenged operative provisions' tailoring as fairly included in the presented constitutional attack on this integrated injunction; alternatively, he would exercise the narrow discretion to reach those closely connected legal issues, with an opportunity for supplemental submissions if necessary. Rule 14 is prudential, but not meaningless. This choice does not authorize new factual claims or manufacture an expanded grant. The incomplete record requires acceptance of the supported findings of interference, not invention of findings about the precise effect of a new remedy.

The fixed access zone addresses repeated entrance, exit, driveway, and traffic obstruction after narrower relief failed. Targeted limits on excessive noise during treatment and recovery address actual medical interference while preserving nearby communication. Neither opposition to abortion nor patient offense at a message supplies the justification. The private-land extension without a demonstrated access or operational connection is different and should be removed. A ban on images visible from the clinic suppresses expression because it can be seen; threatening conduct remains regulable under its own law, but visual disagreement does not justify this blanket restriction. The broad residential zone exceeds the targeted-home protection recognized in Frisby. The [historical primary record](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/753/) supplies those provision-specific facts and competing arguments.

**Post-refusal rule.** Outside the fixed access zone, an enjoined person may make a peaceful initial approach, offer literature, or request conversation. Words or clear conduct communicating that further individualized contact is unwanted—such as “no,” “stop,” or “leave me alone”—trigger protection once a reasonable person would understand the refusal and the enjoined speaker has notice. Silence, disagreement with the viewpoint, or mere entry into the vicinity is not an automatic refusal. After a clear refusal, that speaker must cease following, shadowing, accompanying, repeated reapproaches, surrounding, or directing individualized speech at close range, including coordinated substitution of another participant to continue the same pursuit, and maintain at least eight feet of separation while the person proceeds toward or away from the facility within the defined clinic vicinity.

General expression remains protected: lawful stationary signs, speech to the public, offers to willing listeners, and communication from outside the separation remain available. Refusal terminates the particular unwanted encounter, not all exposure to the viewpoint. The order must not penalize an unavoidable passing encounter, a patient voluntarily approaching a stationary protester, or momentary inability to separate safely. It should require the enjoined person to stop targeted pursuit and take reasonable safe steps to comply once able, rather than permit the listener to force the speaker from an otherwise lawful stationary location. Renewed voluntary engagement can reopen communication, but the speaker may not solicit that permission through repeated unwanted approaches.

Eight feet is Stone's proposed practical separation rule, not a constitutional distance derived from text or already established by 1994 precedent. Its justification is to make termination of a close encounter observable and enforceable after conduct that defeated earlier protection. The counterargument is serious: moving personal zones can burden leafleting, be hard to measure on narrow sidewalks, and turn aversion to speech into suppression. The refusal trigger, limited parties, finite route-related geography, stationary-speech protection, and treatment of unavoidable proximity address those concerns, but do not prove the number's necessity by assertion. Remand for the state court to specify an ascertainable clinic-vicinity boundary, implement the eight-foot rule with record-supported findings on space and necessity, and consider concrete less burdensome means. If eight feet cannot be justified on that record, require a supported narrower implementation instead of imposing an unsupported numerical floor. Stone adopts the post-refusal protection and proposes this operational measure; the Supreme Court should not pretend it has found unprovided site facts. Later floating-buffer or consent-zone cases supply no entering authority.

Touching, grabbing, intentional obstruction, threats, vehicle interference, surrounding, and coordinated physical intimidation may be prohibited independently of refusal when the record and governing law support them. The rule binds identified defendants and persons legally shown to act in concert with notice, not every person sharing their views. No direct private-liability right follows simply from simulated Casey, and Bray's preservation of independent state relief does not dilute the First Amendment. Contempt requires notice and the process appropriate to the actual sanction; Bagwell remains a separate question.

This is egalitarian protection in application: patients and companions retain usable access and a way to end persistent pursuit; protesters retain an initial peaceful offer and public advocacy; clinicians retain an environment fit for treatment; and enforcement rests on observable conduct rather than ideological identity. It does not assign a general right to avoid offensive ideas in public or an unlimited right to pursue an unwilling listener. The cost and uncertainty of the new personal-separation remedy remain explicit, with geographic precision and lawful implementation essential to its defensibility.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Following violations of an earlier injunction, the trial court entered more detailed restrictions. They included a 36-foot zone at the clinic, also reaching adjacent private land; noise restrictions during specified treatment periods and a ban on images visible inside; a 300-foot zone limiting physical approaches to people seeking clinic services without an indication that communication was desired; and a 300-foot residential picketing restriction. The approach provision is not literally a ban on all speech. The court found blocked access, traffic interference, and disruptive conduct. Petitioners did not supply a complete certified evidentiary record and opposed inclusion of one in the state appeal, so the findings cannot simply be contradicted by selected material. [R53](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/753/)

The injunction rests on identified conduct, not proof that every person sharing the protesters’ view is bound. Named petitioners’ attempt to litigate hypothetical applications to absent nonparties differs from their own association claim. The question-presented limits and incomplete-record consequences are material before reviewing individual restrictions. Ordinary access protection and lawful medical services supply interests independent of an ideological disagreement over abortion. [R53](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/753/)
