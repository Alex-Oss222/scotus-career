## 76. Hawaiian Airlines, Inc. v. Norris — No. 92-2058, including Finazzo

### Scope, lower judgments and common reconciled ground

Argument was April 28, 1994; the event is June 20. There are two actions and two independent state causes: the public-policy discharge claim and the Hawaii Whistleblowers' Protection Act retaliation claim. The individual-officer action against Paul Finazzo, Howard Ogden and Hatsuo Honma is before the Court with the airline action under the supplied Rule 12.2 posture. The reviewed judgments are 74 Haw. 648, 847 P.2d 263 (airline) and 74 Haw. 235, 842 P.2d 634 (officers). The full published officers opinion reverses dismissal of Counts I and II, vacates the certified final judgment and remands. The separate later airline order was not independently recovered. The official report identifies the airline trial dismissal specifically as dismissal of the public-policy count, while its account of the officers action identifies both counts; assembly must not invent an additional certified dismissal or mandate for the airline order. The federal preemption rule addresses the two state-law causes, and affirmance is of each judgment actually brought up. The unchallenged federal dismissal of the airline contract count remains outside review.

**Common sufficient rule:** The RLA's exclusive minor-dispute machinery governs agreement-rooted claims and claims whose resolution requires interpreting the collective-bargaining agreement. It does not preempt these independent state duties where the claim turns on factual questions about discharge, the conduct and certification demand, FAA reporting, or retaliatory motive rather than disputed contract interpretation. Parallel grievance relief, evidentiary overlap, or an employer's asserted contractual justification does not alone make a state claim a minor dispute. A claim labeled statutory or nonnegotiable is not categorically immune if resolving its actual elements requires contract interpretation.

The statutory count's exception for a report the employee knows is false remains part of the stated state provision; it is not a finding that Norris knowingly made a false report. Reporting or impending reporting to a public body may be verbal or written and may concern an actual or suspected violation of Hawaii, political-subdivision or federal law or rules. The lower court's common-law protection for union employees and the statutory precedence over inferior CBA rights do not themselves defeat federal preemption. The independent source and actual interpretive dependence do the federal work. Section 184 concerns the airline adjustment boards with prescribed jurisdiction, not a transfer of these tort claims to the railroad board.

**Adversarial comparison retained:** The carrier's broad-grievance reading and Conrail's arguable-justification test would protect genuinely contractual discipline disputes from artful pleading. They do not answer whether the asserted right is contractual in the first place. Buell preserves a statutory right despite grievance overlap; Stevens's Lingle separates factual discharge and motive questions from contract construction; actual unanimous Livadas preserves that distinction without making every contract reference irrelevant. The opposite overbroad path—every state-law label defeats RLA preemption—also fails. Neither path warrants deciding whether Norris was actually fired, whether the demanded certification was unsafe or unlawful, or whether his conduct satisfied the CBA.

## 77. Director, OWCP v. Greenwich Collieries — No. 93-744, including Maher Terminals

### Questions, facts and separate legal paths

Argument was April 25, 1994; the event is June 20. Greenwich concerns Andrew Ondecko's black-lung claim and findings of equipoise on pneumoconiosis and total disability. The ten-year coal-employment presumption concerns the employment connection after the necessary predicate; it does not prove every medical element. Maher concerns Pasqualina Santoro's claims for Michael Santoro's lifetime disability and death benefits, including whether the work injury aggravated his condition and hastened death. It does not rest on a finding that the accident originally caused the cancer. The §20(a) presumption was rebutted and did not supply the ultimate evidentiary determination.

**Exact judgments below:** [Greenwich, 990 F.2d 730](https://static.case.law/f2d/990/html/0730-01.html), vacates the Board's decision and remands for the ALJ to determine whether the evidence preponderates; it relies on the regulation/Mullins/preponderance ground and expressly reserves in note 4 whether the APA's first sentence itself allocates persuasion. [Maher, 992 F.2d 1277](https://static.case.law/f2d/992/html/1277-01.html), vacates the Board's order with directions to remand to the ALJ for a preponderance determination; it uses the APA allocation ground. Both courts raise a possible failure to complete weighing. The historical Supreme Court report's description of Maher as “reversed” is shorthand, not authority to replace its actual vacatur-and-remand mandate. Neither lower judgment grants an unconditional final employer victory.

**APA incorporation is program-specific:** Section 919(d) requires §554 adjudication notwithstanding other Longshore provisions; §554 brings the relevant APA adjudicatory provisions into operation. Section 923(a)'s evidence and technical-procedure flexibility is expressly subject to the chapter and does not by itself allocate ultimate persuasion. Its separate deceased-employee declaration provision requires reception and, when corroborated by other evidence, suffices to establish the injury; no supplied claim makes that route independently establish the disputed aggravation or hastened death. Section 932(a) separately incorporates the relevant Longshore provisions with its own statutory and regulatory qualifications. A possible power to depart by regulation and the actual exercise of that power are different questions.

**Persuasion ground, retained by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg:** APA §556(d), except as otherwise provided by statute, assigns the risk of nonpersuasion to the proponent of an order. Steadman's preponderance standard and that allocation require a claimant to lose on an element left in genuine equipoise. The whole-record command and substantial-evidence review are different rules. For Longshore, §923(a) does not remove the §919(d) APA requirement. For black lung, assume without deciding that §932(a) can authorize an appropriate regulatory departure: §718.3(c)'s statement of congressional reasonable-doubt policy does not establish the claimed displacement of §556(d). This is not a holding that the Secretary can never exercise such delegated power. It also is not an independently sufficient adoption of the lower court's reading of §718.403 or Mullins. The APA ground is sufficient; those distinct grounds need not be reached by these five.

Transportation Management's note 7 expressly says the APA burden is production rather than persuasion. These five reject that general statement to the extent inconsistent with the present allocation; they do not pretend that it was absent or silently overrule the whole case. Its Wright Line holding survives: the General Counsel must persuade the Board that prohibited motivation contributed to discharge, and the employer bears persuasion on its same-decision affirmative defense. Their strongest objection is statutory stare decisis and the unchanged text. Their answer is the distinction between the unnecessary general APA sentence and the sustainable affirmative-defense holding, together with the enacted allocation and Steadman's reasoning. No general hostility to presumptions or to agency administration follows.

**Production/discretion ground, retained by Blackmun, Stevens and Souter:** Preserve Transportation Management's direct construction of the allocation sentence as a production burden. Steadman fixes the degree of persuasion, not which side bears its risk. Mullins requires actual weighing under its interim regulation and did not decide the APA alternative. Within the statutory space left by that production construction, these compensation schemes permit a claimant-favoring rule for genuine ties after complete weighing. Longshore's remedial scheme and §923(a) bear on that choice within statutory bounds; they are not categorical APA exemptions. For black lung, §932(a) and the actual reasonable-doubt regulation reinforce the permissible choice, and §718.403's burden language may reasonably be understood consistently with the production reading. These three reject the lower court's regulation/Mullins rationale as an independent categorical prohibition. They do not uphold an award based on weaker evidence or excuse incomplete weighing.

The strongest objection to that path is that the ordinary allocation and preponderance requirement place equipoise against the proponent. These three answer with the direct earlier statutory interpretation, the distinction between standard and allocation, and administration within the particular compensation schemes. Their conclusion does not confer general agency power to override an APA persuasion requirement if that requirement is adopted. Nor does it turn the APA itself over to whichever benefits agency is a party.
