## Shared reach and component boundaries

All eight Justices reach the preserved selection-factor questions on direct review of the two California Supreme Court judgments. No habeas new-rule bar enters. Both petitioners challenge factor (a), the circumstances of the offense and existence of found special circumstances. Only Tuilaepa challenges factor (b), the presence or absence of criminal activity involving force, violence, attempted force or violence, or express or implied threat, and factor (i), age at the crime. The introductory statutory command applies to factors **if relevant**. The Section 190.2 special-circumstance narrowing is outside the challenge.

The claim is not that ordinary crime circumstances, other violent acts or age are never relevant. It concerns whether the selection directions guide a jury sufficiently, including whether the State must identify propositions, direction or weights for those subjects. Eligibility, constitutional suitability of a selection factor, proof of particular acts, treatment of mitigation, the final moral assessment and correction of an actual error remain different inquiries.

Tuilaepa's relevant-factor instructions expressly prohibited mechanical counting and arbitrary weights, permitted moral and sympathetic valuation and required aggravation sufficiently substantial compared with mitigation to warrant death. The other-violent-act charge required proof beyond reasonable doubt before the acts could count as aggravation. This included misconduct in custody and during trial, not merely pre-offense misconduct. The state's construction of criminal activity concerns punishable offenses involving force or threatened force against a person. No-request and no-prejudice grounds concerning definitions of the offenses' elements are separate lower-court grounds, not a new statutory command.

Proctor's second penalty jury received the full factor list and older mandatory-language instruction, together with directions against mechanical counting and in favor of permitted mitigation, pity, sympathy and mercy. The California Supreme Court assessed the complete charge and advocacy, not the older formula alone. The defense-requested venue change and new penalty jury prove neither actual bias nor compelled death. Unrelated guilt-phase comments, absence-of-minor-accomplice-role arguments and all-factor challenges do not automatically expand the limited review.

## Actual entering capital law

**Facial capital-aggravator formulation and application review**, effective March 30, 1993 through Arave, approves Idaho's aggravator only under the authoritative cold-blooded/pitiless-slayer construction, preserves meaningful guidance and actual narrowing, and separates facial validity from application and weighing error. O'Connor wrote; Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas joined. Blackmun's dissent, joined by Stevens, demanded a coherent enforceable limiting principle. The case concerns eligibility language; a conclusion here must independently explain its relationship to selection.

**Effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation**, effective January 25, 1993 through Graham, requires a lawful route for supported mitigating significance that the authorized sentencing answers otherwise cannot express. Souter's opinion was joined on youth and background by Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor. O'Connor's June 24, 1993 Johnson v. Texas opinion applied that rule on direct review, with the same four non-Stone Justices. Admission and partial relevance do not suffice, but the sentencer need not assign favorable weight. The California directions leave a different decisional route from the constrained Texas special issues.

**Invalid aggravators in capital weighing and correction of sentencing error**, effective June 29, 1992 through Espinosa, builds on Kennedy's Stringer opinion. Remaining valid aggravators do not alone cure invalid weighing; genuine state-authorized reweighing or constitutional harmlessness beyond reasonable doubt is required, or a new sentencing proceeding if no lawful cure exists. This doctrine remains controlling whether or not these factors are found invalid. Scalia's published Espinosa dissent rejects that further weighing premise, but it did not become law. Souter's and Thomas's Stringer dissent rested on the collateral new-rule question and does not establish that they reject every direct-review selection safeguard.

The actual Victor/Sandoval decision, March 22, 1994, applies whole-charge reasonable-likelihood review without prescribed words or automatic cure by boilerplate. Romano, June 13, protects the present capital jury's responsibility against an irrelevant separate death decision substantially inviting displacement of its own choice. Simmons, June 17, protects effective rebuttal of State-raised future danger through accurate parole-ineligibility information when life without ordinary parole is the only noncapital alternative. Neither establishes that every broad sentencing subject is vague; neither permits the view that all constitutional controls end at eligibility. Their remedies remain record-specific.

Blackmun's published Romano and Simmons concurrences independently object to capital punishment as presently administered and seek lawful noncapital relief, while accepting the Court's case-specific relief as a minimum. That is an actual prior public position in this timeline, not an imported historical Callins action. Stevens and Souter's additional Simmons instruction theory was not joined by Ginsburg and did not become Court law.

## Enhanced alternative-path comparison

### Factors (a), (b), and (i): intelligible selection subjects or insufficient guidance

**Sustain the factors within constitutional limits.** Eligibility has already been established on unchallenged findings. At selection, the State may identify ordinary, relevant subjects for individualized assessment without assigning numerical weights or a universal aggravating/mitigating direction to every fact. Crime circumstances, defined violent criminal conduct and age are intelligible subjects; variation in their lawful significance is not the same as an undefined eligibility category. Boyde permits structured moral assessment and tests the full instructions for effective mitigating use. The actual Graham/Johnson rule remains enforceable because no finding of future danger compels a false answer as the only way to give age mitigating force here.

The strongest objection is that an understandable label does little to channel selection if every possible age and circumstance can be argued toward death. Stringer and Espinosa make guidance consequential beyond eligibility. The sustaining response must therefore remain bounded: no constitutional immunity for selection, no permission to rely on race or constitutionally protected status, no automatic conversion of absent mitigation into aggravation, and no exclusion of the meaningful mitigating use required by governing law. A concrete unconstitutional use remains reviewable on its own record. The present facial breadth objection supplies no such finding by itself.

**Require additional selection guidance for factors (a) and (i).** These labels name everything about the crime and one numerical characteristic without a principle explaining which uses legitimately support death. On that view the statute's legally consequential aggravation/mitigation weighing allows death selection without a reviewable boundary, even after valid eligibility narrowing. A requirement that the State guide the aggravating use of these subjects need not impose numerical weights or restrict the defendant's mitigating use.

The strongest contrary authority is Boyde's acceptance of broadly described, individualized assessment and its rejection of unfettered jury discretion as a constitutional requirement. The contrary record is also substantial: both juries were directed toward evidence and individual judgment, and Tuilaepa's was explicitly warned against counting. The invalidity response distinguishes the factor-(k) mitigation issue decided in Boyde from the independent validity of an aggravating selection direction, and treats the nonmechanical directions as insufficient to tell jurors which adverse inferences are lawful. That is a proposed extension of guidance requirements, not a claim that Arave itself decided these factors.

**Factor (b) differs.** The defined class of punishable violent or threatening conduct supplies an objective subject with a discernible culpability and danger relation. The proof instruction in Tuilaepa prevents mere suspicion from being weighed as an act. No proposed broad rule excluding all unadjudicated conduct is supported here; no disposition concerning conviction evidence or a separate death judgment follows. Objections to the elements instruction's adequacy or actual prosecutorial use would need their own preserved record. A Justice may sustain this formulation while rejecting factors (a) and (i) or independently objecting to capital punishment.

### Docket-specific relief if a defect is found

The sustaining path affirms each judgment only against its presented challenges. It does not approve every word of either charge or decide an invented as-applied claim.

The invalid-factor path requires a separate remedy. Tuilaepa's lower concurrences discuss harmlessness, but their age-specific positions do not supply majority constitutional harmless-error review of every factor-(a) defect. Proctor has a different jury, charge and state analysis. The lawful limited course is to vacate each death-sustaining judgment and remand for constitutional correction of the identified selection error: genuine reweighing if authorized by state law, harmless-error review under the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden, or a new sentencing proceeding if no lawful cure is available. No automatic acquittal or life sentence follows from this factor-specific path. A record-specific conclusion that harmlessness is impossible requires support not supplied by the bare fact that factor (a) concerns the central offense.

Blackmun's independent broader path seeks lawful noncapital relief and would not be cured by another capital hearing. His willingness to accept minimum case-specific relief does not abandon that position or turn it into a vote that such a hearing cures his broader objection.

For Proctor's factor-(a) claim, the same six would reject the challenge and the same two support relief. These are six-to-two non-Stone provisional positions, not announced Court votes.
