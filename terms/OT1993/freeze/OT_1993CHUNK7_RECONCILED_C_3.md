## Record, operative qualifications and remedy locks

1. The final Administrator decision modified PRRB decision 90-D05 on January 18, 1990. The district court's May 1, 1992 memorandum/order was entered May 4; the Third Circuit affirmed without opinion April 21, 1993. The court's four eligibility conditions and its findings about community support are rulings under review, not statutory commands. The timely Medicare review route is established; no new jurisdictional obstacle is identified.
2. The university's teaching hospital conducts an approved clinical GME program through affiliated college faculty. Before 1974 no GME reimbursement was claimed for unexplained reasons. During 1974–1983 the hospital claimed salary-related categories while the college carried other costs. The later study added nonsalary categories; a change in the underlying training activity is not established. The interpretation dispute is whether the changed allocation of historically school-borne costs is itself the redistribution described in (c), or a refinement identifying otherwise eligible hospital costs. No side may silently replace the Administrator's actual historic-cost ground with a new finding that the college undertook a different educational activity.
3. The study supported $8,806,205 in total claimed GME and physician-administrative costs; the original disputed difference was $2,861,247. Those figures are not the amount now automatically payable or the amount necessarily remaining after the faculty settlement. The Secretary stipulated below to payment of particular direct faculty salary/fringe and physician-administrative amounts not actually disallowed by the Administrator. The alternative enforcement claim was consequently not adjudicated. Every associate leaves that settlement and unadjudicated alternative claim untouched.
4. Section 1395x(v)(1)(A) authorizes reasonable-cost methods and included items, taking direct and indirect costs into account within that system. Good Samaritan precludes converting actual expenditure alone into entitlement. Section 413.85(a)(1) says allowable cost **may include** net approved-education cost, calculated under (g) and apportioned for Medicare utilization under §413.50. No rejecting position converts “may include” into an unconditional payment command.
5. The complete recodified §413.85(a)(2) exception directs payment for reporting periods beginning on or after July 1, 1985, for approved medicine, osteopathy, dentistry and podiatry residency programs to §413.86. The September 29, 1989 final rule replaced the earlier temporary limit with that exception. The reporting year reviewed here ends June 30, 1985; the exception does not itself disallow it. No later-period reimbursement amount or base-year consequence is decided.
6. Paragraph (g) deducts tuition revenue from total approved-activity costs and includes trainee stipends, teacher compensation and other direct and indirect activity costs under Medicare cost-finding principles. Section 413.17(a), except as provided in its separate paragraph (d), includes services/facilities/supplies furnished by commonly owned or controlled related organizations at their cost, capped at comparable outside prices. Neither provision makes an otherwise ineligible item allowable. The complete then-operative §413.17(d) exception remains unverified and uninvoked; no associate decides or relies on its substance.
7. Letter 78-7 requires costs allowable if incurred directly, consistent allocation among recipient components, comparable-price compliance, related-organization compliance, documented patient-care relation, reasonable allocation and reasonable amount. Its identified additional nonfaculty costs are limited to direct and space costs. It forbids duplicative additions for the hospital's own corresponding library, administrative structure, office or clerical support. Neither silence about redistribution nor permission for some costs is an unconditional promise of all overhead.
8. The final agency decision actually invokes redistribution and community support and separately disallows the identified university general/administrative costs under §§413.9, 413.85 and Letter 78-7. The agency's additional ruling is not simply a court-created alternative. Its scope and lawful application require review if the broader (c) grounds fail; its mention does not establish that it disposes of every remaining disputed dollar. An affirming associate can reserve it because redistribution is sufficient. A rejecting associate cannot order blanket payment or silently erase it.
