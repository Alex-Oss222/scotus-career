## Common operative text and limits

Section 3001(i), 42 U.S.C. §6921(i), protects a resource-recovery facility recovering energy from mass burning of municipal solid waste only if it receives and burns household waste from the specified residential sources and commercial or industrial solid waste containing no hazardous waste identified or listed under §3001; does not accept such identified or listed hazardous wastes; and its owner or operator has established contractual requirements or other appropriate notification or inspection procedures to assure that hazardous wastes are not received or burned. The input limits, nonacceptance condition, and safeguard requirement are distinct. The stated kinds of safeguard are alternatives. The statutory commands are not new findings about every plant; EDF's stipulation concerning Chicago's procedures supplies this case's qualifying posture.

The protected activities are treating, storing, disposing of, and otherwise managing hazardous wastes. Section 6903(6) separately defines hazardous-waste generation as the act or process of producing hazardous waste. Section 6903(7)'s management definition covers systematic control of collection, source separation, storage, transportation, processing, treatment, recovery, and disposal; generation is absent. The subject of the operative exclusion is the facility in those activities, not the chemical or legal status of every output.

The six affirming associates conclude that §3001(i) does not exempt hazardous ash generated by a qualifying resource-recovery facility. When ash actually meets the applicable hazardous-waste criteria, generator obligations and the legally applicable downstream requirements remain; the facility's stated protection from treatment, storage, and disposal regulation is a separate matter. A nonhazardous input does not prove nonhazardous ash, and this ruling does not establish that every ash sample is hazardous. The citizen action continues under the affirmed appellate judgment for ordinary coverage and remedial proceedings. No final penalty, cleanup allocation, breach finding, or damages award is directed.

**Scope correction from the neutral freeze:** the statutory no-ash-exemption rule does not depend on the addition of nonhazardous industrial waste. It also withholds a §3001(i) waste-stream exemption for ash produced by an exclusively household-waste resource-recovery facility. The same statutory facility/generation distinction governs. The historical report expressly reaches this legal consequence at 511 U.S. 338; no changed statute, source, review posture, or actual precedent supports making its reservation broader merely because Chicago uses mixed inputs. At the same time, the validity of the household-waste regulation as applied to resource-recovery facilities before §3001(i)'s effective date remains undecided, as does the regulatory scheme for household waste not processed by resource-recovery facilities. Those two express reservations must accompany any statement of the broader statutory consequence. Do not describe the case as invalidating the entire household-waste regulation or as adjudicating every on-site permit or point-of-generation question.

The agency's change of position supplies no categorical reason to deny deference. The six reject the asserted interpretation because it exceeds the statutory range, regardless of whether a memorandum ordinarily receives the same degree of Chevron deference as rulemaking or adjudication. That broader memorandum-weight issue remains open. Stevens and O'Connor find the statute permits the agency's interpretation and thus do not accept the six associates' ambiguity conclusion. Neither side relies on a newly invented notice-and-comment defect.
