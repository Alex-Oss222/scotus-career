# Romano — proposed public remedial formulation for separate compatibility audit

Scope: Romano v. Oklahoma, No. 92-9093; June 13, 1994. This is a proposed public formulation for the eight non-Chief Justices' compatibility assessment, not a decision or frozen change. It supplies no new fact and identifies no Chief Justice position.

## Proposed public formulation

The judgment of the Oklahoma Court of Criminal Appeals is vacated insofar as it sustains the Sarfaty death sentence, and the case is remanded for proceedings consistent with the sentencing-responsibility rule. On direct review the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the unconstitutional disclosure did not contribute to the jury's selection of death. The existence of aggravating circumstances sufficient for death eligibility does not by itself establish that showing. The state court's earlier deletion of the prior-violent-felony aggravator and reweighing of the remaining three did not apply the responsibility rule announced here and therefore supplies no completed harmless-error determination under that rule. If the State does not establish constitutional harmlessness, a new penalty proceeding is required if the State continues to seek death; the separate death judgment must be omitted, while independently admissible evidence of prior conduct remains available. Guilt and the independent Thompson proceedings remain untouched. No finding of harmlessness or automatic life sentence is made by this Court. This case-specific remedy does not adjudicate the broader constitutional validity of capital punishment or declare that correcting this evidence error answers any Justice's separate objection to its administration.

## Existing commitments and exact compatibility question

The original B model's R3 supplies case-specific vacatur/remand, rejects automatic cure by deleting an aggravator and reweighing, makes no conclusive appellate harmlessness finding, and permits a constitutionally permissible remedial inquiry only if it actually addresses the responsibility error. The base B reconciliation's R3 instead states a constitutionally adequate new sentencing proceeding if the State continues to seek death. It must not be silently read as an already frozen join in the proposal above.

- **Stevens:** The recorded objection is impairment of meaningful individual sentencing by the extraneous death determination. The minimum possible remedial accommodation would preserve that established violation, reject eligibility or generic reweighing as a cure, require an actual direct-review constitutional harmlessness showing, and order a corrected hearing if that showing fails. Does his frozen case-specific ground and date-eligible jurisprudence support accepting this bounded remand, or does he require an unconditional new hearing on this record?
- **Souter:** The recorded objection is practical impairment of sentencing responsibility despite facially correct instructions; eliminating one aggravator is not an automatic cure. The proposed formulation leaves that conclusion intact and permits only a record-based inquiry addressing the identified distortion. Is conditional further relief a compatible narrower remand, or does his commitment require a new hearing without that inquiry?
- **Ginsburg:** The recorded objection is that neither general instructions nor appeal notice withdraws the message that another jury has already made the decisive choice. The proposal does not reclassify those facts as a cure or find harmlessness. Is the State-burden remand compatible with her frozen responsibility ground and date-eligible position, or would it materially retreat from her recorded new-hearing remedy?
- **Blackmun:** The final supplement confirms support for minimum case-specific vacatur/remand while separately retaining an independently sufficient as-administered objection and preferred noncapital disposition. It expressly prohibits counting him for a declaration that another capital hearing fully answers that broader objection. The proposal preserves that reservation and does not reject his broader ground. Does his accepted minimum-relief envelope extend to this conditional case-specific remand, while his separate writing continues to seek noncapital relief regardless of case-specific harmlessness? If not, preserve the exact limit rather than count him for this proposal.

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas are frozen no-error affirmers. Their positions must not be counted as conditional votes for a harmless-error rule simply because such a rule would be narrower than automatic new-hearing relief. Confirm any hypothetical conditional join only if independently supported; none is assumed or requested as a numerical shortcut.

## Audit requirements

Use only permitted frozen neutral packets, original and supplemental commitments, original and supplemental reconciliations, historical comparator and date-eligible public sources. Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967), is the candidate direct-review standard; its applicability to the extended responsibility error must be evaluated, not assumed from a generic reference to harmlessness. If the candidate materially changes a commitment, record the Justice-specific legal basis, the actual objection resolved, the narrow agreement and any historical departure. Return a separate immutable supplement; do not rewrite an existing freeze. A refusal or unresolved issue is an acceptable substantive audit result. This proposal has no operative effect until separate reconciliation and final compatibility review.
