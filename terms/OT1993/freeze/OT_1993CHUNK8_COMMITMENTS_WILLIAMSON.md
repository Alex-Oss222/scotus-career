## Entering rules and live components

**Rule conditions and unit.** Under the then-operative Rule 804(b)(3), the statement must, when made, have been so contrary to the declarant's pecuniary/proprietary interest, so tended to impose civil/criminal liability, or so tended to invalidate a claim against another that a reasonable person in that position would not have made it unless believing it true. The live dispute is whether a generally self-inculpatory account permits accompanying collateral assertions that do not themselves satisfy that condition. Context is necessary under either lawful construction. It does not follow that each word must separately confess a crime, that the statement must be the final missing element necessary to convict, or that every mention of another participant fails.

**Application.** A custodial declarant caught with drugs can admit possession while trying to lessen his responsibility by assigning ownership, direction or greater culpability to another. On the other hand, an assertion identifying a confederate or describing a joint delivery can expose the declarant's knowing participation or conspiracy and therefore genuinely be adverse in its setting. The incriminating effect on the defendant and the penal-interest effect on the declarant are separate questions. The trial court must assess the latter when and as the words were spoken, not infer it from guilt evidence discovered elsewhere.

**Preliminary proof.** Rule104(a) places admissibility facts with the judge. Bourjaily, 483 U.S. at 175–181, supplies ordinary preponderance treatment for preliminary facts and permits examining the offered statement in that inquiry. It does not make all statements true, displace the substantive exception, or supply the in-furtherance element expressly absent here. Blackmun's Bourjaily dissent agreed with the preponderance standard while disputing the independent-evidence question; the two must not be conflated.

**Corroboration.** The text expressly requires corroborating circumstances clearly indicating trustworthiness for a statement offered to exculpate the accused. It does not expressly impose the same sentence on prosecution-inculpatory statements. The district court's Harrell-based corroboration inquiry is a separate circuit-law construction, not a quotation of the Rule. A national rule for inculpatory corroboration need not be resolved to determine the proper statement unit; neither proof corroborating the narrative nor absence of an express inculpatory sentence dispenses with the penal-interest predicate.

Harrell, 788 F.2d at 1526–1527, independently verifies the circuit's three conditions: unavailability, genuine penal adversity and corroborating circumstances indicating trustworthiness. It admitted recorded admissions made to another participant, found independent corroboration, rejected a supposed requirement that the speaker know he was recorded or speaking to someone capable of causing prosecution, and expressly did not decide the coconspirator alternative. Taggart, 944 F.2d at 840–841, applies an inculpatory corroboration requirement and separately invokes constitutional harmlessness on its own evidence. Neither circuit holding establishes that Harris's police accusations satisfy the Supreme Court's Rule construction; neither imports a harmlessness finding into Williamson. Taggart's treatment of external evidence as constitutionally sufficient cannot silently displace controlling Wright.

**Original note and enactment.** The original Advisory Committee note supports two real possibilities: it describes accomplice-implicating portions as potentially admissible related statements, but then expressly asks whether the actual statement is against interest in the circumstances and identifies custody and a desire to curry favor as reasons it may fail. It leaves confrontation separate. The House added an accomplice-confession exclusion intended to codify Bruton; the Senate removed it because constitutional evidentiary principles should not be codified and the categorical text could neglect exceptions; conference adopted that removal on the same stated basis. Thus the enacted Rule contains no categorical other-person bar. Deleting that constitutional provision does not itself decide whether each assertion must satisfy the remaining penal-interest condition. The House's elimination of hatred, ridicule or disgrace remains effective; the Senate and conference restored civil-liability and invalidated-claim language, not that eliminated category. The House also strengthened exculpatory corroboration from simple corroboration to circumstances clearly indicating trustworthiness. The 1987 note describes only technical amendments. Proposed language is not the June 1994 operative Rule.

**Confrontation.** Roberts, Lee, Wright and simulated White retain their exception-sensitive framework. Lee treats an accomplice's custodial accusation with particular suspicion and rejects the mere label “against penal interest” as adequate constitutional analysis. Wright requires particularized trustworthiness from the circumstances of making when that route is necessary; ordinary external corroboration does not itself satisfy that constitutional inquiry. Simulated White, Jan.15, 1992, rejects categorical restriction of the Clause to formal affidavits/equivalent trial substitutes and preserves the foundations of each exception. It does not deem these custodial accusations covered by its accepted spontaneous-declaration or medical-treatment exceptions. A Rule804 remand can leave this preserved question open; no later testimonial test is used.

**Harmlessness and relief.** An established preserved nonconstitutional error receives the applicable Kotteakos/Rule52(a) inquiry, with the Government bearing its harmlessness burden; an actually adjudicated constitutional trial error receives Chapman. Neither Lee nor Wright converts an evidentiary error into automatic reversal. Corroborating evidence may matter greatly at this distinct stage. The incomplete transcript prevents a present reliable finding that every challenged assertion was decisive, that every error was harmless, or that a conditional trial concession surrendered every possible harmlessness argument. Remand must preserve properly available arguments and determine their scope rather than manufacture concessions.

Simulated Salerno, June19, 1992, requires actual satisfaction of the selected hearsay exception and rejects using practical fairness to dispense with a distinct condition. Blackmun's concurrence demands actual context, and Stevens's dissent disagrees about the application of a condition rather than its existence. Simulated Olano, Apr.26, 1993, keeps waiver, forfeiture, prejudice and remedial discretion distinct; Kennedy's concurrence expressly differentiates the Government's burden following a preserved objection. The inherited Standards and Tests entries have the same operative dates and rules: “Former testimony, similar motive and the Government's evidentiary objection”; “Confrontation and firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions”; and “Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b).” None decides Rule804(b)(3)'s statement unit. No later formulation of any Rule is supplied.

## Competing operative constructions

**Assertion-specific contextual construction.** Admit under this exception only an assertion that is itself sufficiently adverse in its actual context. A broader confession's genuinely adverse passages do not carry separate neutral or self-serving accusations into the exception. An apparently neutral fact can nevertheless qualify where identifying it exposes the declarant's criminal role; context includes audience, known circumstances and incentives when spoken. The strongest contrary argument is that ordinary usage permits “statement” to mean an entire narrative, the Advisory note speaks of related statements, and surgical separation can make a truthful confession unintelligible. This construction answers that contextual reading need not atomize words; the note itself tests actual adversity and warns against favorable-treatment accusations, while the enacted reasonable-person condition cannot be satisfied merely by a general narrative label. Removal of the House's constitutional exclusion rejects an automatic accomplice bar without making all related accusations penal declarations.

**Qualified narrative construction.** A sufficiently self-inculpatory account may include closely connected collateral assertions, provided those assertions are not used to diminish the declarant's responsibility or shift blame and the requisite reliability safeguards are met. The Advisory note's related-statement discussion gives this reading direct historical support, and rejection of the House's categorical provision prevents an automatic other-person exclusion. The strongest contrary argument is that this admits a particular accusation which a reasonable declarant could lie about without increasing his own exposure, defeating the textual premise. Its proponent must therefore identify the genuinely inculpatory account and the collateral assertion's inseparable explanatory role; a confession plus an unrelated or self-exculpatory accusation never suffices. The note's custody/curry-favor warning limits, rather than automatically validates, this reading. No unconditional whole-confession rule survives either path.

**Application choice.** Remand for individualized reconsideration under the selected rule, or conclude that the described police accusations fail the penal-interest condition and remand for resulting judgment/error consequences. The latter path must not invent the exact words of unrecovered passages or erase genuinely inculpatory fragments that remain. A court may reject particular blame-shifting propositions without adopting a categorical rule against all custodial or jointly inculpatory statements.
