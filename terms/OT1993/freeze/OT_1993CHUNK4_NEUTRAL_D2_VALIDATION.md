## Kokkonen: framing and completeness

No. 93-263, argued March 1; May 16 event. Guardian removed the state contract action on diversity grounds and asserted state counterclaims. Settlement followed closing arguments and preceded jury instructions. It was recited in chambers on the record. The parties' Rule 41(a)(1)(ii) stipulation dismissed complaint and cross-complaint with prejudice; the judge signed April 13, 1992. It did not mention or incorporate the agreement and did not reserve jurisdiction to enforce it. A subsequent files-return dispute generated Guardian's May 21 motion and the August 19 order asserting inherent enforcement power. Kokkonen appealed solely on subject-matter jurisdiction. The Ninth Circuit's April 27, 1993 table disposition, 993 F.2d 883, relied on Wilkinson.

The decisive fork is independent jurisdiction over a new contract controversy versus ancillary enforcement of an existing federal obligation. Awareness/approval of the bargain is not an obligation in the dismissal order. Actual Willy preserves collateral Rule 11 process sanctions despite later discovery of absent merits jurisdiction; it does not create general power to adjudicate new substantive contract claims after dismissal. Wilkinson's underlying surveillance/FOIA litigation involved multiple court stipulations/orders governing production, including a January 26, 1984 production stipulation/order. Its broad inherent-supervisory formulation is not a substitute for identifying a retained or incorporated obligation here.

Lawful alternatives: reverse/remand for lack of the asserted ancillary basis; affirm a broad power to enforce any settlement of a case once properly pending; or examine an actual retained/incorporated obligation or independent new-action jurisdiction if established. Only the first has its necessary factual predicates here. Merely restoring the former action under Rule 60 and enforcing the bargain are distinct requests; no Rule 60 disposition is presented. A state contract action or independently supported federal action is not foreclosed. The record does not establish whether the particular breach claim independently meets diversity requirements; prior diversity of the dismissed dispute does not by itself answer that question.

## Beecham/Jones: framing and completeness

No. 93-445 includes Jones under Rule 12.2; argument March 21. Fourth Circuit 993 F.2d 1539 and 993 F.2d 1131 reversed district recognition of state restoration as removing federal predicates. Beecham's prior conviction was a 1979 federal Tennessee §922(h) conviction. Jones had a 1971 federal Ohio stolen-automobile conviction and two West Virginia state convictions, with state rights restored in 1982. The state convictions' removal is conceded; the federal predicate remains contested. Use the authenticated 1971 date, not the isolated later 1979 reference in Jones's lower opinion.

The relevant §921(a)(20) text reads: “What constitutes a conviction of such a crime shall be determined in accordance with the law of the jurisdiction in which the proceedings were held.” It then provides: “Any conviction which has been expunged, or set aside or for which a person has been pardoned or has had civil rights restored shall not be considered a conviction for purposes of this chapter, unless such pardon, expungement, or restoration of civil rights expressly provides that the person may not ship, possess, or receive firearms.” The firearms qualification must remain whenever the broader restoration exception is stated. This case chooses the sovereign's law; it does not decide which particular bundle of civil rights suffices or every qualification under a valid restoration.

Jones's indictment contained §922(g)(1) possession and §922(a)(6)/§924(a)(1)(B) false-statement counts. The Government did not argue that the latter independently survived if restoration defeated the predicate; the district disposition treated them together. The appellate remedy reinstated the indictment and remanded, not a conviction. Affirmance of that legal ruling must retain both counts' procedural status without inventing independent materiality, mens rea, or guilt findings. The two district courts used different state-law geographical theories (Beecham's prior crime's location; Jones's residence when the later offense occurred). Those theories expose the difficulty of reading the restoration sentence without its preceding sovereign choice.

The strongest opposing law is the Geyler/Edwards view described and analyzed in the full Jones opinion: States administer voting, jury service, and officeholding, while federal law may furnish no ordinary restoration route. The text nevertheless provides several alternatives without promising each one to every convicted person. The integrated reading supplies the sovereign rule without resolving actual federal pardon or §925(c) availability. Chapman and actual Granderson keep lenity residual; a circuit split or harsh practical result does not alone establish unresolvable ambiguity. Stevens's Chapman dissent and actual Granderson's majority explain why statutory purpose and genuine anomaly still deserve consideration; they do not erase the expressly supplied sovereign choice here.

## Security Services: independent framing and completeness

No. 93-284; argument February 28. Riss issued ICC RISS 501-B on August 20, 1984, effective thirty days later. It contained dollars-per-mile terms and incorporated HGB 100-A, its supplements and subsequent issues for mileage. HGB's Supplement 17 published cancellation of participation on February 19, 1985 for unpaid participation charges. Kmart's transportation contract followed April 17, 1986; the shipments occurred November 3, 1986–December 29, 1989. Bankruptcy was filed November 1989; the undercharge complaint was filed October 30, 1991. The monetary tariff remained on file. The legal question is whether its rate remained effective for these post-cancellation shipments, not whether the distance numbers physically ceased to exist.

The lower opinion supplies the tariff terms and operative regulations. Section 1312.30 requires both money and mileage components for a distance tariff and permits reference to a separate filed mileage tariff. Section 1312.27(e) requires carriers using a governing separate tariff to participate in it. Section 1312.4(d) requires an effective power of attorney/concurrence, states that absent such authority tariffs are void as a matter of law, and assigns proof to the carrier on a challenge. Sections 1312.13(c)(1), .25(a), .17, and .18 govern participant listings and supplements. The issue is the regulatory consequence of published cancellation, not a free-floating judicial power to excuse filed rates.

No direct evidence shows an effective power of attorney after cancellation. The lower court accepted, for summary-judgment purposes, an inference that one once existed. Mere nonpayment as revocation and general agency renunciation were additional lower rationales; the narrower adjudication need not adopt an automatic rule that any late private payment voids a tariff. Published cancellation of participation before the relevant shipments, under the governing tariff scheme, is the operative premise.

The strongest competing path is substantial compliance: the published monetary rates and referenced mileage numbers remained ascertainable, and private nonpayment should not erase statutory collection rights. Berwind-White and Davis protect against treating harmless tariff irregularities as destruction of a complete rate. American Trucking does not recognize an unlimited power retroactively to reject an effective tariff; it tests implied agency remedial authority by a specific statutory mandate and a direct, close relation. The substantive counterquestion is whether requiring legal participation in an indispensable incorporated tariff implements the statutory complete-public-rate requirement or merely enforces a bureau's private dues. The commitments answer that fork expressly, rather than using the packet's characterization as a conclusion.

The lower opinion also establishes limits that must survive: Kmart waived its three-year limitations defense; the carrier's alternative reasonable-compensation theory under §10701(a) was not raised below and was not entertained; contract-carriage and rate-unreasonableness defenses were not decided; no collateral-estoppel disposition was selected. An earlier guide cannot simply be substituted for the incorporated guide after cancellation, and no independently applicable former Riss tariff is identified. Actual Reiter governs counterclaims, recoupment and referral only when their predicates are presented; it does not prove a valid tariff or require referral of a pure legal effectiveness question already governed by the regulations and the ICC's Jasper Wyman interpretation.

## Intervening legislation check, without an alternative disposition

Recovered and read the complete Negotiated Rates Act of 1993, Pub. L. 103-180, 107 Stat. 2044–2053, approved December 3, 1993: `https://www.congress.gov/103/statute/STATUTE-107/STATUTE-107-Pg2044.pdf`, local `chunk4-sources/negotiated-rates-1993.pdf/.txt`. This is date-eligible enacted text, not subsequent commentary. No present commitment depends on an election, settlement percentage, agency referral, or exemption under this enactment.

Section 2(c) makes the added settlement procedures applicable to pending claims as well as the specified shipment period; §2(f) separately preserves prior final adjudications/settlements subject to its fraud/coercion qualification. Section 2(e) creates a distinct unreasonable-practice procedure for qualifying pre-September 30, 1990 negotiated rates, with carrier-operation and other predicates and ICC determination. Section 5's validation provisions concern account codes and range tariffs, not cancellation of participation in a mileage guide. Section 9 preserves specified bankruptcy/court-jurisdiction/ERISA provisions. These are not interchangeable exceptions. No current election, qualifying cessation of operations, proper notice, or finality finding is supplied here.

The effective-tariff prerequisite can be decided without adjudicating any of those new remedies, which neither supply missing participation nor compel collection of an otherwise ineffective asserted rate. If assembly elects to make the Act affirmative authority for a separate disposition, it must obtain the required bounded source admission and validate the relevant predicates. The present narrow adjudication should not state that Maislin forbids every legislatively authorized negotiated-rate defense after this enactment.
