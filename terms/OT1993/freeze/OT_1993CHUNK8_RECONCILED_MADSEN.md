## Operative provisions and preserved qualifications

The public 36-foot arrangement concerns the public access approaches and road. On the south the buffer is demarcated by the south edge of Dixie Way's paved surface; the unpaved south shoulder remains usable subject to other lawful restrictions. On the east, respondents may enter the Highway One shoulder, may not ascend the embankment, and must remain five feet from the clinic's east line. The north/west record owners and invitees are exempt from the **entry** prohibition; other restrictions still apply when they act in concert. Removing the government's private-side speech restriction recognizes no right to trespass.

The crossing is a precisely located eight-foot strip pending customary painted lines, followed by the marked crosswalk. It permits prompt passage only: no stopping, hesitation, picketing, congregation, demonstration or impediment to traffic. The four heightened-review associates sustain this arrangement as part of the tailored public access clearance; Stevens leaves it intact against the presented categorical objections; the three strict-review associates reject using it to save the broader speech exclusion. None declares that public traffic rules or a properly drawn crossing arrangement are inherently invalid. No scale or new numerical remedy is inferred from the drawing, which is expressly not to scale.

Paragraph (4) operates from 7:30 a.m. through noon, Monday–Saturday, **during surgical procedures and recovery periods**, and concerns patients **inside** the clinic. It lists singing, chanting, whistling, shouting, yelling, bullhorns, auto horns, sound amplification equipment, and other sounds or images observable to or within earshot of those patients. Both temporal qualifications remain. Noise and images are severable. Affirming noise adds no decibel threshold, volume condition, new intent requirement or judicially drafted harmful-sound exception. The four affirming associates rely on the actual medical-operation context and findings concerning intrusive sound, while rejecting any license to suppress disfavored ideas as such. The three reversing associates do not deny the medical harm or the possibility of lawful noise relief. The erroneous printed “during” sentence in respondents' brief, the brief's two-day operating assertion, and its patient-name sign allegations do not amend the order or become findings.

Paragraph (5) regulates **physical approach** to persons seeking clinic services within 300 feet. A person indicates interest by approaching or inquiring; invited nonthreatening conversation and literature are permitted there but never within the 36-foot zone. Upon refusal the person may leave; respondents may not accompany, encircle, surround, harass, threaten, or physically or verbally abuse the declining person. Invalidating the initial-invitation condition establishes no right to follow, obstruct, threaten or force communication after refusal. It does not facially approve every possible application of the disputed harassment or verbal-abuse terms.

Paragraph (6)'s territorial ban prohibits approaching, congregating, picketing, patrolling, demonstrating and amplification within 300 feet of specified staff/owner/agent residences. Its commands against blocking residences' entrances, exits or driveways, and against impeding a street providing sole access to their street, are distinct. The separate invitation paragraph allows contact upon an affirmative indication of interest; for residential contact that invitation must be conveyed **from a distance and before the contact**. An uninvited knock or doorbell does not supply it. Cancellation requires immediate termination and departure. Those provisions permit willing contact but do not restore ordinary neighborhood marching or communication captured by the radius. None of the eight associates sustains the territorial ban after reconciliation; none invalidates every distinct obstruction command or commands a substitute distance.

Paragraph (7) covers physical abuse, grabbing, intimidation, harassment, touching, pushing, shoving, crowding and assault of persons entering, leaving, working at or using the clinic, or obtaining access to specified homes, with its own invited-contact qualification. Paragraph (8) concerns harassment, intimidation, physical abuse, assault and threats against present/former clinicians, staff, employees and volunteers; it has no equivalent invited-contact text. Paragraph (9) prohibits encouraging, inciting or securing another to perform a prohibited act. Their independent substantive validity remains undecided for the scope reasons below; qualifications are not transferred among them.

### Stevens

His own *Claiborne* opinion, *Frisby* dissent, *Ward* dissent join and actual *Discovery Network*, *Ladue*, *Bray* and *Izumi* positions support a conduct-sensitive remedy while protecting persuasion. The historical writing at 778–779 is concrete evidence that he treats an injunction against identified wrongdoers differently from a legislative rule for all speakers. The remedy must be no more burdensome than necessary to give complete relief, with scope related to the violation and recurrence; it may restrain more than the exact past act when needed to avoid recurrence or eliminate its consequences. *Yamasaki*, *Professional Engineers*, *Hudson* and *Paradise* supply that remedial account. He gives the trial judge appropriate familiarity-based deference and rejects the other four's heightened injunction test. His description of paragraph (5) as reasonable time/place/manner regulation is not a whole-opinion adoption of ordinary *Ward* as the test for every injunction.

He rejects per se content discrimination and the categorical association challenge, leaves the public arrangement intact on those grounds, and declines separate private-extension, noise and images merits. The *Izumi* dissent is distinguishable for the reason already stated: the additional questions here are unnecessary to resolving those selected. He sustains paragraph (5) as a physical-approach constraint on the bound actors in the found setting, preserving distant advocacy and offered verbal or written counsel that does not become the prohibited approach, pursuit, encirclement or harassment. This is not a ban on every unsolicited word or a requirement that every member of the public obtain a listener's permission before speaking.

His frozen residential affirmance cannot survive reconciliation. His historical III-E join expressly held the findings inadequate for a 300-foot territorial ban; his own *Frisby* dissent recognized willing communication and lawful communicative purpose. The additional invitation text was already part of the same historical order, not a new timeline fact, and still excludes uninvited door-to-door initiation and general neighborhood activity. The present *Ladue* holding reinforces practical channel analysis; it supplies no reason to reverse that judgment. He therefore reverses the territorial ban while preserving lawful residential obstruction and appropriately focused protection. His paragraph (9) consequence must now remove that residential predicate. He declines the independent (7)–(9) merits, with no blanket assurance that every harassment or encouragement application is valid. No unsupported departure is retained for this Justice.
