### Common operative qualifications

Complete Auto requires substantial nexus, fair apportionment, nondiscrimination and a fair relationship to state services. The worldwide unitary base and the California share of that base are distinct: California does not tax all worldwide income. The formula for these years equally weights property, payroll and sales. An accepted unitary base does not immunize an unfair formula, discriminatory burden or foreign-commerce defect. Colgate's separate-accounting comparison does not itself account for flows of unitary value or prove gross distortion. The clear-and-cogent constitutional burden and the lower court's clear-and-convincing state statutory inquiry are not collapsed.

Section 25137 permits a taxpayer to petition, or the Board to require, a reasonable alternative when ordinary allocation and apportionment do not fairly represent California business activity. The alternatives, for all or part of the business, are separate accounting, excluding one or more factors, adding factors fairly representing in-state activity, or another method producing equitable allocation and apportionment. There is no unconditional taxpayer election. The lower court's failure-of-predicate ruling did not decide a categorical refusal by the Board to consider separate accounting.

Regulation §25137-6(e)(1) **requires consideration** of effort and expense. It **permits acceptance** of reasonable approximations in appropriate cases, including when ordinary business records cannot supply needed data. It does not require acceptance of every approximation. The optional advance determination under subsection (e)(2) is individual and limited to submitted facts, preserves later factual review, and is not a prerequisite to later variance consideration. The lower court's findings that accommodations were used and no reasonable approximation was shown to have been unreasonably rejected are application facts, not a statutory exemption from reporting.

The $5 million setup and $2 million annual literal-compliance estimates concern a broader undertaking than BBI's $900–$1,250 historical return costs. The latter are BBI-only costs, not a whole-group cost finding. The burden challenge fails for the affirming positions because of the actual regulatory accommodation and record, not because those unlike figures can be equated. Reasonableness, the duty to consider expense, judicial review and available clarification answer the presented notice/discretion challenge without denying future concrete arbitrary-application claims.

## 79. Reed v. Farley

**Identity and posture:** Argued March 28, 1994; proposed decision date June 20, 1994. Review of the Seventh Circuit's January 19, 1993 judgment, 984 F.2d 209, affirming the federal habeas denial; not direct review of the Indiana conviction. The timing claim arises under pre-AEDPA §2254 and IAD Article IV(c). The lower grounds remain separate: Indiana relied on inadequate scheduling objection; the federal district court relied on defense motions/delay; the Seventh Circuit adopted a Powell analogy and expressly recorded that respondents had not invoked Sykes default.

**Historical check:** Among the eight present associates, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg supported affirmance; Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter dissented. The official historical majority accepted Hill's statutory collateral threshold. Ginsburg/O'Connor reserved the stronger case of a state court disregarding a timely specific request; Scalia/Thomas rejected making preservation alone decisive. The historical dissent also supplied a statutory merits and dismissal conclusion, not merely an abstract right to another hearing. No historical author is assigned here.

### Statutory and record predicates retained in every row

Article IV(c) requires trial within 120 days after arrival in the receiving State. For good cause shown in open court, with the prisoner **or** counsel present, the court with jurisdiction **may** grant a necessary **or** reasonable continuance. Article VI(a) separately tolls the period whenever and for as long as the prisoner is unable to stand trial, as determined by the court with jurisdiction. Motion filing is not textually identical to inability. Article V(c), when its predicate is met, requires the appropriate court to dismiss the pending indictment, information or complaint with prejudice; a detainer based on it ceases to have force. Whether that remedy can be obtained through habeas is distinct from whether it is optional once legally available.

Arrival occurred April 27, 1983; absent qualifying extension or tolling the deadline was August 25. Trial began October 18. No precise Article IV(c) objection was made during the June 27 and August 1 scheduling discussions. Predeadline written motions nevertheless invoked IAD timing and opposed extensions, and the August 29 discharge motion specified the 120-day claim. The unresolved July 25/26 and related filing-date references do not erase those writings or establish knowing waiver. No intentional concealment is found. The September 19 defense request explains the later postponement; it cannot by itself supply a lawful basis for the earlier August 26–September 19 interval.

The reported record identifies no loss of evidence or impairment of the defense attributable to that initial overrun. Confinement, lost halfway-house status and preparation difficulties remain asserted adverse consequences, not nonexistent events. Counsel expressly disclaimed an independent Sixth Amendment violation; there is no new constitutional claim or categorical assertion that Barker always requires separately proved particularized prejudice. Actual Doggett remains intact.

Article III's request-triggered 180 days, IV(e)'s anti-shuttling rule, VI(b)'s mental-illness exclusion and the federal-receiving-State dismissal proviso do not decide this Indiana timing claim. No relevant VI(b) predicate is established. Section 5's federal enforcement direction is considered with §2254, rather than treated as a free-standing repeal of habeas limits. Neither exhaustion nor its absent/ineffective-state-process exceptions supplies an independently invoked bar here. The unpresented transfer-hearing and habitual-offender issues receive no new disposition.

### Collateral entitlement and independently sufficient grounds

**Narrow affirmance position:** Hill and Timmreck's fundamental-defect/rudimentary-fairness threshold applies to ordinary nonconstitutional statutory attacks under §2254 as well as §2255. Article V(c) supplies the trial remedy, not an express answer to every collateral gate. The lack of a specific scheduling objection, considered together with the timely but general writings, absence of established defense impairment and absence of the stronger deliberate-refusal record, permits denial here. This does not find a statutory waiver or decide the actual merits of every claimed exclusion. It expressly leaves open the case in which a court confronted with a timely specific request nevertheless refuses the statutory period. It does not decide that better preservation automatically suffices.

**Broader affirmance position:** An ordinary Article IV(c) overrun does not itself satisfy Hill merely because it was precisely invoked or deliberately disregarded. Additional prejudice must make the underlying proceeding fundamentally defective; the judge's degree of blame alone does not do so. Constitutional, jurisdictional or independently fundamental defects remain separate. A prior opportunity for full and fair litigation may also justify refusing renewed statutory litigation in Scalia and Thomas's equitable view; that additional proposition is not the basis of an uninvoked procedural-default finding and is not attributed to the other Justices.

**Reversal position, two supporting grounds:** First, the federal-forum and uniformity functions of §2254 resist uncritical transplantation of §2255's prudential screen to an expressly asserted federal statutory custody claim; Davis establishes statutory reach, not identity of every equitable consideration. Second, even assuming Hill's screen, Congress's prescribed dismissal with prejudice makes this established violation more than a formal omission without consequence. The four reversing Justices regard that second ground as independently sufficient. They do not require a Sixth Amendment violation, factual innocence or proof that another trial would reach a different verdict where the federal statute forbids continuation on these charges. Conversely, no rule permitting habeas for every minor statutory error is adopted.

### Merits and remedy commitments

For the four reversing Justices, the reported trial and appellate grounds do not establish an applicable statutory exception. The district court's attribution of significant delay to many motions is not a determination that the prisoner was unable to stand trial for a specified qualifying interval under Article VI(a), and does not become a continuance granted on the Article IV(c) conditions. The State's argument that any pending defense motion stops the clock is therefore rejected. No new finding about a particular unreported motion is needed. The reported state disposition relies on inadequate objection, which the timely writings and the statute's terms do not support as a basis for denying this right. Mauro protects substantive invocation without requiring a prescribed citation; it does not create an exception to the compact's actual periods.

Those Justices conclude that the initial unexcused overrun violated Article IV(c). The later defense request does not erase that completed violation. This does not settle whether a properly presented continuance first sought after expiration could ever satisfy the compact on another record; it refuses to invent such an order here. The State's scope objection does not require an indefinite factfinding remand where its stated alternative is legally insufficient on the reported record. The historical dissent's conclusion is corroboration, not a replacement for this statutory analysis.
