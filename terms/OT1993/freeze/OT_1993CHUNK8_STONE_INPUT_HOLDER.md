#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** affirm the Eleventh Circuit's statutory ruling and continue remedial proceedings, preserving the concrete comparator and every §2 element.

The prior review's preference for reopening liability is withdrawn. The court of appeals did address cohesion, bloc voting, and totality after correcting the district court's treatment of those matters. A new merits remand should not be manufactured from the mistaken premise that a comparator alone supplied the lower court's finding. This implements the user's specified disposition and Blackmun's approach on the actual posture.

Section 2's language and results amendment, Chisom, Gingles, and the inherited Growe/Voinovich rules leave legitimate room to challenge this arrangement. The comparator is not chosen merely because it creates a desired racial result. Georgia authorized five district-elected commissioners with an at-large chair; the county's school board uses five districts; and the trial court found a compact Black-majority district possible under that local arrangement. The attachment's five-member shorthand refers to the district-elected component; retain the actual authorized chair rather than inventing a different plan. These independent institutional roots make the alternative an available measure of opportunity rather than a judicial choice of an ideal legislature. The [contemporaneous Blackmun opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/91-2012.ZD.html) supplies the competing interpretive approach, not an already adopted simulated holding.

The strongest objection remains that size has no uniquely correct constitutional baseline, that the referendum rejected the alternative, and that a school board is not a county executive. Stone should address those points without converting them into unsupported new factual defects. Section 2 does not demand a uniquely ideal system; it requires a reasonable, workable comparison in the actual institutional setting and proof of unequal opportunity. State authorization for this very county is stronger evidence of institutional feasibility than an analogy to unrelated government. The school-board practice supports the geographic comparison without itself proving identical functions. A referendum remains relevant local choice, but cannot immunize a separately established statutory violation.

This is a meaningful extension of the reasonable-alternative inquiry to body size. It neither overrules an entering Supreme Court bar nor follows automatically from §5 coverage. A claimant in another county must identify a legally and practically grounded comparator, show suitability to the actual governmental task, and prove Gingles and totality. Population fraction, inability to elect one preferred official, or a map that can produce a seat is insufficient. Comparators may be multiple without being arbitrary, but each needs reasons independent of the desired racial tally. A proposal to fragment an indivisible executive office solely to manufacture seats presents a different problem.

Affirm liability on the reviewed record and leave the existing remand to formulate a workable, limited remedy with the responsible institutions. The authorized structure informs relief without compelling its exact adoption, a racial quota, or any candidate's victory. Do not replace that remedial inquiry with a fresh liability trial or reach the constitutional-purpose claim unless it remains necessary. De Grandy's refusal to require maximization is complementary: a valid benchmark permits analysis, not automatic additional districts. Later SCLC and Mixon applications remain future questions, with no automatic liability or backdated authority.

Equal regard extends to minority voters' effective participation and to the county's legitimate institutional choices. The legal reason for relief is the proved statutory violation, not treating one group's desired electoral outcome as superior to everyone else's. The remaining administrability cost—judicial evaluation of competing workable structures—is real, but it does not justify excluding a statutorily cognizable injury when this record provides an independently grounded comparison.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** Bleckley County’s single commissioner exercises executive and legislative powers. Black voters comprise nearly one fifth of the eligible electorate. Georgia authorized a change to five district-elected commissioners plus an at-large chair in 1985; voters rejected that change in 1986. The existing school board uses five districts. The district court found that those districts could yield a Black-majority district in a restructured commission, but rejected the remaining Gingles predicates and found no proven discriminatory purpose. It also found a substantial history and continuing effects of racial exclusion. The court of appeals found the statutory predicates and totality satisfied. [R51](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/874/)

Statutory results liability is distinct from the constitutional purpose claim. Section 5 coverage of a size change, which petitioners conceded, is not proof of a §2 violation: the provisions use different liability inquiries and comparison baselines. The case is reviewable despite the circuit’s remand for a remedy. No election result or demographic fraction alone establishes a right to a particular number of offices.
