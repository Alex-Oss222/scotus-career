## 13. Weiss v. United States, No. 92-1482, including Hernandez

### Validated neutral record and entering law

Section 1259 supplies the review channel. Both military appellate judgments are before the Court; all nine seated Justices participate. Both trial and appellate appointments, and both petitioners' judgments, require treatment. No guilty-plea waiver of the presented institutional claims is supplied. The challenged judges were commissioned officers appointed by the President with Senate confirmation. This fact does not establish the complete appointment history of every officer, resolve the appointment of a civilian appellate judge, or exempt military officers from Article II.

The actual texts distinguish Article 26 trial detailing from Article 66 appellate assignment. Article 26 qualification and performance-report limits, Article 37's prohibition on command censure respecting judicial work, Article 98's sanction, disqualification under Articles 26(c) and 66(h), cause challenges under Article 41, and the judge-identity protection in Article 16 do different work. None supplies fixed tenure. Neither petitioner alleges actual or apparent bias of his own judges. Mabe, 33 M.J. 200 (1991), and Graf, 35 M.J. 450 (1992), prohibit sentence-based ratings and retaliation by transfer or decertification; those are legal safeguards, not findings that no military judge has ever suffered pressure.

Relevant inherited rules are [Impartiality of balanced bar-dues arbitration panels](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#impartiality-of-balanced-bar-dues-arbitration-panels), effective Gibson, December 4, 1991, and [Neutral adjudication and withdrawal-liability rebuttal](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#neutral-adjudication-and-withdrawal-liability-rebuttal), effective Concrete Pipe, June 14, 1993. They forbid genuinely interested adjudication but reject automatic disqualification from institutional membership alone; their own factual settings do not settle military tenure. Medina, June 22, 1992, has no controlling categorical rejection of Mathews. Its California-specific burden conclusion cannot become an exclusive method for this case. The military-specific predivergence authority is Middendorf, 425 U.S. 25, 43–44 (1976), with Solorio, 483 U.S. 435 (1987), relevant to respect for Congress's military judgments; neither eliminates independent constitutional scrutiny.

### Enhanced comparison and operative commitments

Appointment path A treats the new judicial duties as germane military assignments under an existing constitutionally sufficient commission; no second appointment is required. Path B treats the 1968 changes and adjudicative authority as creating a distinct office requiring a new appointment. B's strongest point is Freytag's insistence that substantial adjudicative authority counts as officer authority and cannot be delegated merely for convenience. A's sufficient answer on these facts is that the actors already hold Article II appointments and military judging falls within the commissioned military system; Shoemaker, 147 U.S. 282, 300–301 (1893), permits germane additional duties. A does not rest on Judge Crawford's view that the Appointments Clause is inapplicable. No general permission to transfer any commissioned officer to an unrelated principal office is adopted.

Tenure path A upholds these judgments because fixed terms are not indispensable to impartial adjudication within the actual statutory military structure. Path B finds assignment and career control sufficiently dangerous to require a structural correction despite no actual bias allegation. B's strongest point is that Tumey and Ward do not require proof of actual bias before correcting an intolerable institutional incentive. A's answer is specific: protected judicial evaluations, forbidden interference and retaliation, qualification and disqualification rules, and independent review reduce the alleged risk; the bare absence of a fixed term is insufficient. A neither declares all military adjudication impartial nor makes actual bias indispensable to every future structural challenge. No Justice commits to judicial selection of a particular term length.

## 14. Schiro v. Farley, No. 92-7549

### Validated neutral record and entering law

Section 2254 pre-AEDPA collateral review; all nine seated Justices participate. The State raised Teague neither below nor in its opposition to review, but did raise it at merits stage. This requires an exercise of discretion; it is not a jurisdictional defect. Count I charged knowing killing, Count II murder during rape, Count III murder during criminal deviate conduct. Only Count II's guilty form was returned. There was one general not-guilty form, and the charge did not expressly require verdicts on each count. The prosecutor said one verdict would be returned; the court's final reference to verdict(s) did not repeat that statement as an express legal instruction. Instruction 8's knowing-or-intentional language did not explicitly identify all three counts. Its scope is disputed. No commitment treats a lawyer's explanation as a judicial command.

The intentional-killing-during-rape aggravator under §35-50-2-9(b)(1) required proof beyond reasonable doubt. The advisory jury recommended against death, while the judge imposed it and later supplied written intent and mitigation findings. The advisory recommendation does not disclose whether the jury rejected aggravation, found mitigation, or exercised leniency. The mental-illness evidence is not a concession of knowing or intentional killing merely because the act was conceded.

The controlling [Successive prosecutions, evidentiary overlap and criminal contempt](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#successive-prosecutions-evidentiary-overlap-and-criminal-contempt) rule is effective June 28, 1993, Dixon, retaining Grady; Felix's evidence/conspiracy components remain effective March 25, 1992. Grady's retention does not turn an ordinary initial sentencing into a second prosecution. Ashe remains separate and protects ultimate facts necessarily determined for the defendant. The [Factually unsupported lawful grounds in general verdicts and capital recommendations](../../../state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md#factually-unsupported-lawful-grounds-in-general-verdicts-and-capital-recommendations) rule is effective Sochor, June 8, 1992, with Griffin's conspiracy component effective December 3, 1991. It does not transform silence into a finding. No holding overruling Grady is available.

### Merits two-path comparison

Path A affirms because no prior acquittal-like sentencing determination or necessary jury rejection of intent is established. Bullington and Rumsey protect a completed rejection of death; the judge's first sentencing here follows the conviction within one prosecution. The ten alternative forms, single general not-guilty option, absence of an each-count instruction and counsel's one-verdict descriptions allow a rational jury to select felony murder without rejecting knowing or intentional killing. Instruction 8 supplies an additional possible understanding; the conclusion does not require finding that every juror actually applied it to felony murder. Ashe and Dowling put the burden on the claimant to establish the necessarily determined fact, and the record permits another rational account. A need not decide whether issue preclusion can ever operate between guilt and punishment phases.

The strongest objection is Green: a defendant who confronted an accusation and emerged without conviction on it ordinarily receives acquittal finality, and mental culpability was disputed. Treating every blank form as irreducibly ambiguous could destroy implied acquittal. A's answer is bounded by the multi-count, single-murder alternatives and actual instructions; Green's greater-and-included-offense verdict does not make every unused alternative form a conclusive finding about mens rea.

Path B reverses as to death because the selection of felony murder rather than the knowing-killing count finally rejected the knowing mental state; intentional killing cannot logically be found while giving that acquittal effect. Green and Ashe protect substance over form, and Bullington and Rumsey prevent capital eligibility from becoming a fresh opportunity to prove the rejected fact. Its strongest obstacle is the record's permissible one-verdict understanding and Instruction 8, plus the absence of count-specific not-guilty forms. B treats the verdict as completion of all submitted alternatives and reads the disputed mental-state evidence as supplying the material distinction; it does not rest solely on the advisory refusal to recommend death. Relief would remove death based on this aggravator while leaving the felony-murder conviction, rather than acquit Schiro of the killing.
