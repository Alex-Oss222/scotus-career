## 76. Hawaiian Airlines, Inc. v. Norris — No. 92-2058, including Finazzo

### Reach, components and rule

The preemption determination is reviewable through the supplied state-judgment channel. The officer and airline judgments both reject RLA dismissal of the independent state claims. The unchallenged federal contract dismissal is not reopened. The components are the Hawaii public-policy discharge count and the statutory whistleblower count in each action; both are reached only on federal preemption. Ultimate liability, the truth and safety implications of the demanded certification, damages and any FAA private cause remain outside the decision.

**Formal disposition source:** The validated source handoff identifies the officer judgment as 74 Haw. 235, 842 P.2d 634 (December 16, 1992), reversing dismissal of Counts I and II, vacating the certified final judgment and remanding; it identifies the later airline judgment as 74 Haw. 648, 847 P.2d 263 (1993). The petition also brings up the individual-officer action under the supplied Rule 12.2 posture. The separate airline order was not recovered, so this handoff does not invent additional mandate terms beyond the linked preemption determination and continuation of state proceedings.

**Permissible affirmative rule:** The RLA's minor-dispute machinery is exclusive for claims founded on the CBA or requiring interpretation of it; it does not displace these claims founded on independent state duties and resolvable through factual questions about conduct, the demanded certification and retaliatory motive. Overlapping evidence, a possible contract remedy, or an employer's assertion of contractual justification does not itself transform the independent claim into a minor dispute. A genuinely necessary interpretation of disputed CBA terms remains subject to the statutory machinery.

The whistleblower protection includes reports or impending reports to a public body, verbally or in writing, of a violation or suspected violation of a law or rule of Hawaii, a political subdivision or the United States, unless the employee knows the report is false. This exception is not a finding against Norris. The state source preserves the common-law count for union workers and gives the HWPA precedence where a CBA supplies inferior rights and remedies. None of those state provisions displaces a valid federal preemption rule; the federal issue remains whether these rights actually depend on contractual interpretation. Section 184 establishes airline adjustment boards of the prescribed limited jurisdiction, not the railroad board itself. Its mandatory handling process and permitted referral after adjustment fails remain intact for actual contractual minor disputes.

**Alternative paths tested:** The employer's strongest route treats §184's disjunctive reference to grievances and Conrail's arguable-justification threshold as capturing every discipline dispute. It correctly protects resolution of contractual obligations and prevents pleading labels from evading arbitration. It fails here because it moves a test for classifying disputes over the agreement into the antecedent question whether the asserted right is contractual at all. Buell rejects that collapse in the RLA setting; Lingle and actual Livadas identify the factual-versus-interpretive boundary. Conversely, a rule immunizing every claim labeled statutory or nonnegotiable would be overbroad: its elements might still require disputed contract interpretation. The stated narrower rule avoids both errors.

## 77. Director, OWCP v. Greenwich Collieries — No. 93-744, including Maher Terminals

### Entering law and distinct questions

Both APA incorporation paths are reached. Longshore §919(d) mandates §554 adjudication notwithstanding other provisions; §923(a)'s relaxed evidence and technical-procedure language does not expressly allocate ultimate persuasion. Black-lung §932(a) incorporates the relevant Longshore machinery subject to its statutory and regulatory exceptions. Whether Congress delegated an exception and whether §§718.3(c)/718.403 actually establish one are different questions.

**Formal disposition sources:** Greenwich, 990 F.2d 730 (3d Cir. March 23, 1993), vacated the Board's decision and remanded to the ALJ for a preponderance determination. Its regulation/Mullins ground and possible incomplete-weighing concern did not decide the APA first-sentence allocation issue; footnote 4 expressly reserved that question. Maher, 992 F.2d 1277 (3d Cir. April 19, 1993), vacated the Board's order with directions to remand to the ALJ for a preponderance determination, relying on the APA allocation. The modeled affirmance positions sustain these separate judgments on the grounds below, without attributing the Maher rationale to Greenwich or converting either remand into final employer entitlement.

The APA allocation begins with the exception for what is otherwise provided by statute. Section 923(a)'s evidence/procedure flexibility is itself subject to the chapter; its separate command admitting a deceased employee's injury declaration and making it sufficient if corroborated does not establish a general equipoise rule. No claim that this specific evidentiary route independently established Santoro's disputed aggravation or hastened death is supplied. Section 932(a)'s regulatory-exception language is considered with its incorporated and excluded provisions; the model does not decide power over an excluded provision or an unpresented category of benefits.

The APA's first sentence allocates a burden; its later whole-record provision, Steadman's proof quantum, statutory presumptions and substantial-evidence review are separate. Maher's §20(a) presumption was rebutted and no longer supplies the ultimate determination. Greenwich's ten-year presumption concerns coal-mine causation, not every medical element. A tribunal must actually weigh the whole record; a lawful tie rule never excuses completing the weighing.

Current John Hancock rejects an agency construction outside statutory bounds; current Health Care permits a scheme-consistent construction without deleting the statutory conditions; current PUD sustains a particular delegated water-quality interpretation. None decides APA persuasion allocation or converts deference into power to change an unambiguous cross-agency command. Actual MCI has now been checked as detailed in the refresh above: its structural boundary is controlling, but it does not decide §556(d), either compensation statute or Transportation Management's footnote.

### Competing sufficient grounds

**Persuasion path:** Burden of proof in §556(d)'s enacted framework assigns the risk of nonpersuasion to the proponent of an order. Read with Steadman's preponderance standard, a benefits claimant who has not carried persuasion cannot win merely because the evidence is tied. Transportation Management's footnote 7 states a contrary production-only proposition. Its Wright Line holding survives because the General Counsel proves the prohibited motivation and the employer is proponent of a genuine affirmative defense; the broader APA sentence must be disapproved to the extent inconsistent. This is a limited precedent treatment, not a claim the prior sentence never existed.

For Longshore, §919(d) and the APA govern, and §923(a)'s investigatory flexibility is insufficient to reverse persuasion. For black lung, even assuming §932(a) permits regulatory departures in an appropriate exercise of delegated power, §718.3(c)'s explanation of congressional reasonable-doubt policy does not clearly establish an operative equipoise allocation overriding §718.403 and the incorporated APA. The broad scope of potential exception-making need not be decided. Existing statutory presumptions retain their own force.

**Production/discretion path:** Transportation Management directly construed the allocation sentence as production-only. Steadman decides the standard for weighing, not the party losing a genuine tie; neither holding need be discarded. Mullins required weighing qualifying evidence under an interim regulation, expressly did not decide the APA alternative, and recognized true doubt as part of the agency's approach. Once reliable evidence has been weighed, a claimant-favoring tie rule is a permissible construction of these compensation systems; black-lung §932(a)'s regulatory language and §§718.3(c)/718.403 strengthen that conclusion rather than become a mandatory license to ignore the statute. Longshore's remedial scheme and §923(a) matter within the space left by the production-only APA reading. This path rejects a judicially imposed persuasion allocation, not all proof burdens.

The strongest objection to the persuasion path is its retreat from a direct unanimous statutory statement and recognized agency practice without a changed text; the strongest objection to the production path is that a standard requiring preponderance plus the proponent's burden ordinarily places equipoise against that proponent, and policy cannot override that statutory decision. The rows identify each Justice's resolution; unlike grounds are not added together.

## 78. Barclays Bank PLC and Colgate-Palmolive Co. v. Franchise Tax Board — Nos. 92-1384 and 92-1839

### Questions and common limits

The two refund controversies remain live. Barclays concerns the foreign-parent group's 1977 assessments; Colgate concerns a domestic parent and 1970–1973 assessments. Unitary status is accepted in the appellate posture; separate legal incidence, tax-base selection, apportionment and international effect remain distinct. The constitutional questions are reached only on the actual assessments, not every later water's-edge rule.

**Formal disposition sources:** In Barclays, 2 Cal.4th 708, 829 P.2d 279 (1992), rejected the one-voice challenge and remanded other issues; 10 Cal.App.4th 1742, 13 Cal.Rptr.2d 377 (November 20, 1992), then reversed the taxpayer's refund judgment and directed entry of judgment for the Board, with each side bearing its appellate costs. In Colgate, 10 Cal.App.4th 1768, 13 Cal.Rptr.2d 761 (November 20, 1992), reversed the foreign-commerce portion, affirmed the constitutional/statutory distortion rulings and denial of discovery sanctions, and awarded the Board appellate costs. The federal dispositions below address each reviewed judgment's presented tax questions; they do not decide a new sanctions question or prescribe an unsupported alteration of costs.

Under Complete Auto, the State needs nexus, fair apportionment, nondiscrimination and a fair relation to services. Actual Allied-Signal controls the connected tax base, Kraft prohibits its identified foreign-dividend discrimination, and actual Associated Industries/Oregon Waste demand a proper comparison and an actual compensating burden. None says that income from an accepted unitary group cannot be combined or that a different foreign accounting method itself proves domestic discrimination. Colgate's 70% separate-accounting disparity must be assessed with the flows of unitary value; the comparison is not conclusive proof of gross distortion.

Under Japan Line and actual Itel, international multiple taxation and impairment of national uniformity are independent requirements. Passing one does not cure failing the other. Container's domestic-parent holding controls Colgate's methodological attack absent a sufficient reason to depart. Its express foreign-parent reservation requires actual analysis for Barclays.

California's regulation requires consideration of effort and expense. It permits reasonable approximations in appropriate cases, including where ordinary business records cannot develop the needed data. It does not command their acceptance whenever requested. Advance determinations are optional, individual and limited to submitted facts; later factual review remains available, and failure to request or obtain a favorable advance determination does not bar later variance consideration. Section 25137 permits the taxpayer to petition, or the Board to require, a reasonable alternative for all or part of the business if ordinary allocation and apportionment do not fairly represent California business activity. The alternatives are separate accounting, excluding a factor or factors, adding factors fairly representing in-state activity, or another method producing equitable allocation and apportionment. There is no unconditional taxpayer election. The lower court's clear-and-cogent constitutional distortion inquiry and clear-and-convincing state statutory inquiry are distinct; its failure-of-predicate ruling did not decide a categorical Board refusal to consider separate accounting. The full literal-compliance estimates and limited BBI historical costs are not comparable measures; rejection of the burden challenge rests on the actual accommodated administration and absence of a concrete unreasonable rejection, not equating those figures.

### Alternative paths and grounds

**Affirmance of both dockets:** Accepted unitary income is fairly attributable through the three-factor approach absent the required showing of distortion; the foreign-parent record does not prove a discriminatory tax rate or unconstitutional compliance burden under the actual adjustments. ## 79. Reed v. Farley — No. 93-5418

### Reach and current-law constraints

The congressionally sanctioned IAD is federal law under Cuyler and actual Fex, notwithstanding its compact origin. Section 2254 supplies jurisdiction to consider a properly presented federal statutory custody claim; it does not make every breach a ground for a writ. The State's jurisdictional characterization does not erase settled compact status. This threshold is distinct from collateral cognizability, procedural default and the merits of an IAD violation.

**Formal disposition source:** Reed v. Clark, 984 F.2d 209 (7th Cir. January 19, 1993), affirmed the district court's September 21, 1990 habeas denial. That federal appellate judgment is the judgment to affirm or reverse. Reed v. State, 491 N.E.2d 182 (April 7, 1986), is the earlier state judgment, not the direct judgment reviewed here. The Seventh Circuit's Powell analogy, the district court's delay-exclusion reasoning and the state court's inadequate-objection rationale remain distinct.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Article IV(c) requires trial to commence within 120 days of arrival in the receiving State, but the court may grant a necessary or reasonable continuance for good cause shown in open court with the prisoner or counsel present. Article VI(a) instead tolls the period while the prisoner is unable to stand trial, as determined by the court; a defense motion is not automatically equivalent to that predicate. Article V(c) commands dismissal with prejudice of the pending indictment, information or complaint and termination of the detainer's force if the applicable trial period is violated; the collateral-review question concerns access to that remedy through habeas, not discretion to soften the statutory dismissal once properly available. Article VI(b)'s exclusion for persons adjudged mentally ill has no established predicate here. Article IV(e)'s anti-shuttling protection, Article III's request-triggered 180-day period, and the federal receiving-state dismissal proviso do not govern Indiana's presented timing claim. Section 2254's exhaustion requirement or its absence/ineffectiveness-of-state-process exceptions supply no independently invoked failure on this record; they are not substitutes for the omitted Sykes defense.

The Court may resolve collateral entitlement assuming the timing violation; it need not decide contested extensions or tolling outside the granted question. No independent Sixth Amendment violation is presented: counsel expressly disclaimed one. Actual Doggett therefore supplies neither an automatic constitutional deadline nor a replacement claim. Actual Fex concerns Article III receipt, not Article IV's arrival trigger; it preserves the actual continuance, inability-tolling and dismissal terms. Actual Keeney concerns deficient development of facts on an already presented claim, not an uninvoked state default. Actual Brecht retains Chapman for the preserved Doyle claim and does not create a universal standard for statutory claims. Actual Withrow rejects extending Powell merely from a fair state opportunity to a different trial safeguard; it requires a claim-specific explanation here. Schiro also rejects converting a late nonjurisdictional defense into a compulsory jurisdictional bar.

Reed's written IAD objections before August 25 are part of every row. He failed specifically to object at the June/early-August scheduling events; that fact is not complete silence, deliberate concealment, a knowing waiver or a Sykes default. The State did not invoke the latter. September 19 defense-requested postponement cannot retroactively establish a lawful extension of August 26–September 19. Good cause in open court with prisoner or counsel present and judicially determined inability to stand trial are different statutory qualifications. Article V(c)'s dismissal with prejudice is mandatory when an actual violation is established in a proceeding where the remedy is available; §5's enforcement direction does not expressly repeal collateral-review conditions.

### Competing paths

**Narrow affirmance:** Hill's fundamental-defect/rudimentary-fairness threshold applies to nonconstitutional statutory claims on §2254 just as to the equivalent collateral remedy in §2255. The assumed Article IV(c) breach, without established impairment of the defense or other circumstances making this conviction fundamentally unfair, is not alone a complete miscarriage of justice. Confinement, loss of halfway-house status and preparation inconvenience are adverse consequences; they do not establish the required collateral defect on this record. Timely writings defeat a waiver fiction but do not themselves make every overrun collateral error. The Court can affirm without adopting the Seventh Circuit's categorical Powell analogy, deciding actual tolling, or closing every case of deliberate disregard of a properly and specifically invoked demand.

**Reverse and remand for the statutory claim:** IAD's express mandatory dismissal protects a federal entitlement to termination, unlike a formal allocution or plea-advice omission with no shown consequence. The written objections and later motion for discharge make the statute's enforcement a live asserted right, not a technical omission discovered long afterward. Davis allows statutory claims whose legal consequence is that custody may not continue; actual Withrow and §2254 resist a categorical state-opportunity bar. Hill and Timmreck preserve aggravated cases. The correct remedy is remand to determine the actual violation and any lawful extension/tolling, followed by source-authorized habeas relief if the statute required dismissal; it is not an immediate unexamined order discharging Reed. The strongest objection is that Davis concerned conduct beyond the criminal prohibition rather than a lost procedural deadline, and treating every discharge remedy as inherently fundamental would erase Hill's limit.

**Scalia/Thomas broader collateral ground:** An ordinary IAD timing breach with no constitutional or jurisdictional defect and no impairment of fair adjudication does not become cognizable simply because the prisoner contemporaneously requested statutory compliance. Their actual Withrow equitable approach and actual Brecht dissents support limiting collateral relitigation after the supplied opportunities. They need not make preservation dispositive or posit a missing default. They would not suggest that more precise oral objections alone would convert the same nonfundamental timing violation into a habeas ground. This differs from the narrow affirmance's express reservation about aggravated circumstances; it does not erase the Court's binding Withrow holding or deny habeas for a separately established fundamental defect.
