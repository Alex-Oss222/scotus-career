## 80. Dolan v. City of Tigard, No. 93-518

### Entering law and separable components

Nollan forbids using a permit condition to obtain an easement unrelated to the purpose supporting the development restriction. Its assumption of a reasonable-relationship test for argument did not decide the necessary degree of fit. Its insistence on actual justification for demanded property coexists with ordinary land-use authority. Lucas's June 29, 1992 complete-deprivation rule and Standards entry “Complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use” concern complete loss, not this continued commercial use; its ordinary proof rule does not prescribe the dedication test. The April 21, 1992 Alaska Standards entry “Boundary-preserving conditions for artificial coastal extension” permits preservation of existing submerged-land boundaries, not surrender of existing territory. Neither resolves this case. Yee distinguishes invited occupancy from compulsory occupation; it supplies no automatic dedication rule. Penn Central retains contextual regulation analysis.

The contested components are the floodplain public-greenway dedication and adjacent 15-foot pedestrian/bicycle strip. The unchallenged 15-percent open-space requirement is distinct. Runoff findings support keeping floodplain land open, but do not themselves explain public access; the traffic estimate supports a transportation concern, but “could offset some” does not establish measured equivalence. The case involves identified conveyances demanded in an individualized permit decision under ordinances and plans. No conclusion here applies automatically to all legislative obligations, money fees or ordinary zoning.

**Path A:** Reverse and remand because, beyond purpose nexus, the City must establish an individualized reasonable proportional relationship between the character and extent of each demanded property interest and the development's effects. No mathematical equivalence or special evidentiary quantum is required. A drainage justification must explain the public interest demanded, and the pathway finding must connect its extent to traffic mitigation. Strongest objection: cumulative effects, comprehensive planning and predictive uncertainty make exact quantification impracticable. Response within this path: reasonable estimation is allowed; a general public benefit is not a substitute for identifying the developer's attributable burden.

**Path B:** Affirm under Nollan's required connection, applying a reasonable-relationship inquiry with the challenger retaining the burden of showing constitutional inadequacy. The City's site-specific drainage/traffic findings, integrated plan, modest fraction of the parcel and credit toward required open space suffice without a new government persuasion burden. Strongest objection: a public easement is property, and the record does not separately explain why drainage requires public access. Response within this path: the conditions form an integrated greenway and circulation arrangement responding to development impacts; Nollan does not require isolated valuation or proof that each feature is indispensable. This conclusion cannot rest merely on retained economic use.

## 81. Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg, No. 93-644

### Entering law and two paths

Haslip sustains its Alabama process, including instructions and meaningful trial/appellate amount review; it establishes no universal procedure or ratio. The simulated TXO June 25, 1993 holding has Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas on record-specific procedural sufficiency, including actual judicial reasonableness review; its substantive rationales do not yield a controlling ratio, potential-harm formula or universal review checklist. Souter's scoped TXO dissent addresses actual instructions, prejudice and deficient review, not the whole proportionality analysis. Ginsburg had no vote there or in Haslip. James Daniel Good applies Mathews to its deprivation and preserves functionally distinct safeguards, without determining punitive-damages review. BFP supplies a statutory value inquiry, not a constitutional punitive-amount rule.

**Path A:** Reverse the judgment insofar as it sustains the punitive award without meaningful judicial review of amount, and remand for constitutionally sufficient review. Oregon's no-evidence rule and correction of legal errors do not review an amount after entitlement is established. Its five actual factors, clear-and-convincing entitlement burden, prima-facie wealth gate and pleaded ceiling reduce risk, but do not supply an independent amount check. Hurtado permits changed forms when protection remains adequate; it does not make every substitution adequate. No court must select a particular ratio, reproduce Alabama's system or always issue a separate written opinion.

**Path B:** Affirm because due process assesses the combined safeguards, not the preservation of one common-law review device. Oregon gives materially more ex ante direction than bare common-law punishment/deterrence instructions, requires heightened proof of culpability, restricts wealth evidence, permits legal-error/no-evidence correction and limits the award to the amount pleaded. Strongest objection: these constrain entitlement and jury method but leave the amount unchecked. The response is that guidance can constrain choice at the decision stage, and Haslip/TXO did not hold postverdict excessiveness review indispensable in every different regime. The response does not invent an amount-review power Oregon lacks.

## 82. Heck v. Humphrey, No. 93-6188

### Entering law, trigger and paths

Preiser reserves habeas for release-determining attacks; Wolff permits claims consistent with the continued validity of confinement; Patsy rejects general exhaustion for a properly cognizable §1983 claim. Cavanaugh's November 30, 1993 holding permits opportunity-only parole consideration relief because success determines neither release nor conviction validity. Albright's January 24, 1994 judgment has no controlling rationale announcing a freestanding constitutional malicious-prosecution tort or universal state-remedy bar. Custis's May 23, 1994 channel rule concerns ACCA sentencing and expressly reserves no-meaningful-review settings. None directly decides damages cognizability here.

**Path A:** Affirm dismissal without prejudice on a favorable-invalidation prerequisite, not exhaustion or jurisdiction. When damages for unconstitutional conviction/imprisonment, or for other conduct, require a judgment whose success necessarily demonstrates invalidity of an outstanding conviction or sentence, the claim is not presently cognizable. Qualifying invalidation is reversal on direct review, executive expungement, a declaration of invalidity by an authorized state tribunal or issuance of federal habeas relief. Accrual of that barred damages claim awaits qualifying invalidation. Constitutional claims whose success can coexist with conviction are not categorically barred; a search violation may coexist through independent source, inevitable discovery or harmless error, but conviction injury remains unavailable while invalidity is unestablished.

**Path B:** Permit the damages-only action, or preserve it by a supported stay, because §1983's text contains no exhaustion requirement and habeas cannot give this relief. Ordinary preclusion, immunity and proof rules can control relitigation. Strongest objection: an award necessarily declaring a conviction unlawful could bypass Congress's specifically conditioned custody-review channel. A stay does not answer whether losing habeas may still be followed by contradictory damages adjudication, and ordinary preclusion cannot simply be presumed on this record.

**Narrow Path A:** Require invalidation for this in-custody prisoner whose claims, as litigated below and in his principal submissions, necessarily attack conviction, while reserving persons lacking a meaningful habeas opportunity. Statutory reconciliation, rather than wholesale incorporation of common-law malicious prosecution, is sufficient. For this claim dismissal without prejudice with deferred accrual avoids turning the prerequisite into a limitations trap. Do not add malice or absence of probable cause as elements of every constitutional damages action.

## 83. Davis v. United States, No. 92-1949

### Entering law and three paths

Edwards bars police-initiated custodial interrogation after counsel is invoked, subject to counsel's presence or suspect initiation followed by valid waiver. Smith forbids using later answers to undo an initially clear request, but reserves genuinely ambiguous initial language. Barrett respects the actual scope of an expressed request. McNeil requires an expression reasonably understood to seek counsel for custodial questioning; it did not decide this ambiguous postwaiver exchange. Withrow's April 21, 1993 Standards entry “Miranda habeas review and fair litigation of independent coercion claims” keeps Miranda and voluntariness distinct and does not prescribe invocation clarity. Stansbury's April 26, 1994 unanimous public rule makes custody objective; it does not answer invocation.

**Path A:** Affirm under an objective clear-request rule: after a valid waiver, a reference that a reasonable officer would understand only as a possible desire for counsel does not invoke Edwards. Clarification is advisable and permissible but is not a universal constitutional duty. Strongest objection: ordinary people speak tentatively under custodial pressure. Response: no legal formula is required; an ordinary unequivocal request suffices, while an assumed possible wish does not withdraw an actual waiver.

**Path B:** Affirm under mandatory limited clarification: when postwaiver words reasonably suggest a desire for counsel but are genuinely uncertain, substantive interrogation must pause for neutral clarification. Officers may ask what the suspect wants without discouraging counsel or predicting its advice; a clarified wish to proceed permits questioning, while a request triggers Edwards. Strongest objection: a vague trigger invites disputes and adds a protection beyond a clear invocation. Response: a short objective clarification resolves the ambiguity without requiring legal sophistication or ending every interview.

**Path C:** Treat the initial “Maybe I should talk to a lawyer” as sufficient invocation requiring complete cessation, then assess suppression and available relief. Strongest objection: this record establishes a valid initial waiver, genuinely equivocal first words and credited noncoercive clarification disclaiming a present request, not police use of substantive answers to erode a clear invocation. No associate selects Path C on these accepted facts.
