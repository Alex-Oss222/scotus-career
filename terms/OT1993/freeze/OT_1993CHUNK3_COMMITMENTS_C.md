## 33. Ticor Title Insurance Co. v. Brown — No. 92-1988

### Validated neutral premises and reach

Argument March 1, 1994; proposed event April 4, 1994. Review under §1254(1) of the Ninth Circuit judgment reported at 982 F.2d 386. The lower court **affirmed preclusion of further injunctive relief**, reversed the damages-preclusion ruling, and remanded. The packet's general description of reversal must not erase that mixed disposition. The challenged constitutional question concerns preclusion of absent class members' monetary claims, on a conclusive premise that the earlier Rule 23(b)(1)(A)/(b)(2) certification was proper. Certification cannot now be reopened. The record does not establish inadequate representation; the Ninth Circuit rejected that separate objection.

The 1986 settlement abandoned monetary claims in exchange for an injunction, increased existing policy coverage, additional coverage on new policies, and approved litigation expenses. This differs from an ordinary cash damages distribution. The absence of an opt-out opportunity is established. The case remains a live dispute; discretionary dismissal would leave the Ninth Circuit's judgment in place, rather than vacate it or declare absence of Article III jurisdiction. State-action immunity and the filed-tariff issues discussed below are outside this proposed decision.

### Alternative paths and strongest objections

**Dismiss the writ as improvidently granted.** The normal sequence would test whether the Rules authorize extinguishing substantial individual damages claims in this mandatory class, then reach the Constitution only if necessary. Finality has removed the first question from this case. A constitutional holding on an assumed-valid certification could therefore settle a broad question that a direct certification case might never present. Beazer supports the avoidance sequence; Shutts expressly reserved other class forms, so it does not compel a universal opt-out holding here. Hansberry's representation requirement is important but does not decide the separate opt-out issue. The strongest objection is that the binding premise is a legally real feature of a ripe, fully briefed dispute and the parties need an answer. The response supporting dismissal is a discretionary vehicle judgment, not a declaration that constitutional review is unavailable or unnecessary between these parties.

**Retain the case and resolve the constitutional preclusion question.** Final certification does not decide constitutional validity, and the present judgment directly turns on the latter. The strongest objection is that neither a general federal opt-out rule nor a general mandatory-class exception can safely be formulated without defining the permissible Rule 23 category. A retained decision would have to respect the fixed certification and adequate-representation premises and confine its scope; it could not invalidate the certification or use uncertainty to fabricate inadequate representation. Retention alone does not require a merits position before the threshold poll is settled.

**Unavailable shortcut.** No Justice may dispose of this review by declaring the prior certification invalid under Rule 23, finding inadequate representation contrary to the record, or treating the live case as an advisory request. A DIG supplies no new substantive preclusion or due-process holding.

## 34. J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B. — No. 92-1239

### Validated neutral premises and components

Argument November 2, 1993; proposed event April 19, 1994. Review under §1257 of the Alabama Court of Civil Appeals' 606 So.2d 156 judgment, after the Alabama Supreme Court denied review October 23, 1992. The State's challenged peremptories are direct governmental action; no private-actor attribution is needed. J.E.B. objected before the jury was empaneled and renewed the challenge after judgment. The courts rejected the objection because Batson did not extend to sex, not after a finding that the State had supplied sufficient neutral reasons.

The operator's sanitized official-report verification establishes a venire of 36 (12 men, 24 women), 33 after three cause excusals, including 10 men. The State used nine of ten peremptories on men; J.E.B. used all but one on women. The jury was all female. The injury asserted is discriminatory exclusion, not the numerical composition of the final jury. The paternity and child-support judgments are under review through the selection claim. Lower-court blood-sample and DNA admissibility rulings do not become additional Supreme Court questions merely because the lower opinion discusses them.

Components: (1) whether sex-based state peremptories are subject to the Equal Protection Clause; (2) the applicable justification and the distinction between group stereotype and individual reason; (3) Batson procedure and remedy. No supplied record justifies an immediate finding that every challenged strike was intentionally discriminatory or a numerical jury quota.

### Alternative paths and strongest objections

**Extend the prohibition.** Craig and Hogan require an important objective and a substantial relationship between a sex classification and that objective; Hogan applies to discrimination against men as well as women. Selecting an impartial jury is important, but the presumption that men as men will favor a paternity defendant substitutes group sex for an individual assessment. Powers protects the excluded juror's right; Batson supplies a workable inquiry; actual McCollum preserves the same purposeful-discrimination burden when jury selection falls within constitutional control. The strongest objection is that a peremptory challenge has always permitted less specific and more intuitive predictions than a challenge for cause and that some experiences correlate with sex. The selected response leaves peremptories, individual experience, answers, and actual bias available; it forbids sex itself as the predictive shortcut. A genuinely sex-neutral individual reason is not invalid solely because it is statistically more common in one sex, but a pretext or sex proxy remains impermissible.

**Keep Batson confined to race.** Peremptories traditionally permit group-based intuitions not amounting to cause; race has a distinct history in jury exclusion; intermediate scrutiny does not make all sex distinctions equivalent to racial ones. The strongest objection is the State's direct classification and Hogan's rejection of stereotypes. The dissenting response is confined to the jury-selection mechanism: trial-specific predictions by both sides differ from a statutory exclusion of a sex from the venire or citizenship. This is a disagreement about applying equal protection to peremptories, not a denial that sex discrimination generally receives heightened review. McCollum's treatment of racial strikes remains controlling and is not overruled by that dissent.

**Procedure and remedy on the extension path.** The objector must establish an initial inference of purposeful sex discrimination; the striking party then must supply a sex-neutral explanation; the trial court determines whether the objector has proved intentional discrimination, with the ultimate burden remaining on the objector. Explanation need not satisfy a for-cause challenge, and no explanation is automatically demanded for every strike. Reverse the legal ruling barring the inquiry and remand for proceedings consistent with it, including the proper inquiry and a new trial if selection cannot lawfully be sustained. The Court neither orders a particular sex composition nor adjudicates paternity or the unreviewed evidence questions itself.

## 35. Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. — No. 92-854

### Validated neutral premises and reach

Argument November 30, 1993; proposed event April 19, 1994. Review under §1254(1) of 969 F.2d 891. The supplied scope includes whether private aiding-and-abetting liability exists and, if so, scienter and substantial assistance. A separate petition or grant is not represented as verbatim verified text. The underlying Rule 10b-5 primary action remains received law; its existence is not reopened.

The lower court held that the indenture imposed no disclosure duty relevant to purchasers. Plaintiffs' surviving theory against Central Bank was **affirmative agreement to delay** an independent appraisal review until after closing, rather than passive failure to warn. The bank had received serious appraisal concerns, its own appraiser recommended review, and it first required an independent review before agreeing to defer it. Contrary evidence included explanations about improvements and comparable sales, certifications, and added collateral for the older bond issue; those facts prevent treating wrongdoing as already proved. The lower court found triable questions, not liability. The lower opinion's Pring controlling-person and secondary-liability rulings are not silently adjudicated as claims against Central Bank.

### Entering law that must remain distinct

Musick, Peeler permits contribution among persons already jointly liable within the received Rule 10b-5 action. Its actual holding expressly does not establish aiding-and-abetting liability or general professional liability. Kennedy's majority was joined by Stevens, Scalia and Souter among the sitting associates. Thomas's dissent, joined by Blackmun and O'Connor, distinguished an accepted investor action from a new action by a wrongdoer seeking reimbursement; it did not immunize professional defendants from their own established violations.

Actual Meyer unanimously distinguishes an established action's relief from creation of a distinct defendant and obligation. Its federal-fiscal and official-immunity considerations do not mechanically govern a securities suit against private participants. Actual NOW unanimously refuses an unwritten economic-motive limit in expressly enacted RICO; it neither creates §10(b) secondary liability nor relaxes individual responsibility, statutory predicates or causation. The Court must decide which description fits the secondary investor action rather than deriving the result from either analogy alone.

### Alternative paths and strongest objections

**Reject the private secondary action.** Section 10(b) requires the defendant's use or employment of a manipulative or deceptive device, directly or indirectly. Assistance to another's violation without the defendant's own statutory wrong is not the same thing. Hochfelder and Santa Fe insist on the statutory predicate; Rule 10b-5 cannot expand the statute. Express controlling-person and other liabilities elsewhere demonstrate tailored congressional choices, and 18 U.S.C. §2 does not create a private civil claim. Contribution under Musick assumes liability rather than creating it. The strongest objection is the long and broad lower-court acceptance of secondary liability and the judicial development of the received investor action. The answer is that a question expressly reserved by this Court is not settled Supreme Court law, and implied-remedy development cannot erase the conduct Congress made unlawful. Reverse the reinstatement of the bank's secondary claim without deciding the disputed mental state. A professional or trustee who independently commits a primary violation remains subject to the ordinary elements; no categorical professional immunity follows.

**Retain a confined secondary investor action.** The received antifraud action developed against a background of culpable participants' responsibility, and courts have long treated substantial assistance as within that investor remedy. Musick confirms that a received implied action has incidents requiring judicial definition. The strongest objection is the absence of aiding-and-abetting language and Congress's express liability choices. The response is that this is continued definition of culpable participation in an established investor action, not a new claimant's recourse or a new public defendant, and that indirect conduct and the antifraud context permit that reading. Lower-court consistency supplies substantial reliance and interpretive weight, not conclusive legislative ratification. Meyer and NOW still require a specific legal source and the defendant's own culpable assistance; generalized deterrence alone does not suffice.

**Mental state and record, if retained.** Confine the rule to primary fraud, affirmative substantial assistance, and at least reckless disregard of the fraud and the defendant's assisting role. Recklessness means an extreme departure from ordinary care presenting a danger known to the defendant or so obvious that the defendant must have been aware of it; ordinary negligence is insufficient. An absent disclosure duty does not categorically immunize affirmative assistance. Central Bank's argument that the transaction involved only lawful indenture powers does not decide whether its affirmative agreement culpably facilitated fraud. The strongest contrary argument is that treating a deferral as an affirmative act makes every omission actionable; the response is that the bank had actually demanded the review and then affirmatively agreed to change that requirement, with evidence of serious known deficiencies. No general duty to investigate or disclose is created. On this record the competing evidence leaves scienter and assistance for trial. Plaintiffs still must prove primary fraud, the bank's qualifying mental state and assistance, and causal injury. A record of silence alone without a duty, or ordinary services without the required culpability and assistance, remains outside the rule selected here. A universal recklessness holding for every primary or secondary setting is unnecessary.

## 36. McDermott, Inc. v. AmClyde — No. 92-1479

### Validated neutral premises and components

Argument January 11, 1994; proposed event April 20, 1994. Review under §1254(1) of 979 F.2d 1068, concerning settlement credit against River Don. General maritime law is an authorized field of federal common-law development. The contractual limit eliminating AmClyde's liability, crane-damage ruling, and other matters outside the stated question are not reopened.

The jury found $2.1 million in deck damage and responsibility of 32% AmClyde, 38% River Don, 0% Hudson, and 30% McDermott/sling defendants together. McDermott accepted the settling sling defendants' responsibility for trial; no finding separates its own share from theirs. It settled with the three sling defendants for $1 million. River Don's found share equals $798,000. The Fifth Circuit reduced recoverable additional damages to $470,000 by deducting the settlement from $1.47 million, while leaving the AmClyde contract protection intact. McDermott requests its $798,000 River Don share, not transfer of AmClyde's uncollectible share.

The lower court expressly did not decide River Don's alternative request for a new trial based on evidentiary rulings because the credit disposition made that unnecessary (979 F.2d at 1081 n.16). The selected remedy must therefore reverse the credit ruling and remand for proceedings consistent with the correct method; it must not unconditionally direct final payment regardless of that preserved issue. The combined 30% creates no arithmetic gap for calculating River Don's 38% and does not justify inventing a split. A post-trial allocation of settlement proceeds between crane and deck need not decide the selected proportionate-credit issue.

### Alternative paths and strongest objections

**Proportionate-share reduction.** Reduce the claim against nonsettlers by the settling tortfeasors' adjudicated proportionate responsibility rather than their negotiated payment. Reliable Transfer's proportionate allocation supplies the governing maritime direction. Plaintiff and settler bear their bargain's risk; a nonsettler pays its adjudicated responsibility without benefiting from a favorable settlement or bearing an unfavorable one. The strongest objection is potential aggregate recovery above the adjudicated damages, and the need to litigate absent settling parties' responsibility. The answer is that settlement prices purchase risk resolution and finality and do not purport to be exact adjudications of liability; fault allocation is already required under comparative responsibility. The method does not promise every plaintiff full recovery, because a low settlement can leave the plaintiff short. Ordinary need to allocate responsibility is preferable to a separate settlement-value trial. No general good-faith hearing or judicial approval of every settlement becomes an additional prerequisite to credit.

**Dollar credit with contribution preserved.** Subtract the settlement dollars and allow the nonsettler to pursue a settler whose payment was too low. This can protect the nonsettler's proportionate allocation but deprives the settling defendant of dependable peace and fosters further litigation. Its strongest virtue is avoiding an apparent windfall; its central defect is weakening settlement finality and shifting settlement risk back through contribution.

**Dollar credit with a settlement bar.** Subtract dollars and protect the settler from contribution, ordinarily using a good-faith inquiry to guard against collusion. This secures settlement peace but can charge a nonsettler beyond its proportionate share when the settlement is low, while awarding the nonsettler a discount when it is high. A sufficiently searching fairness hearing risks duplicating the liability trial. The selected proportional method avoids this arbitrary dependence on another party's bargain. No assumption of collusion in this actual settlement is made.

**Edmonds limitation.** Edmonds preserves joint-and-several recovery in its nonsettlement statutory setting; it did not prescribe settlement credit. The selected rule allocates the consequences of a plaintiff's voluntary settlement. It neither abolishes joint-and-several liability generally nor replaces the rules for an insolvent nonsettler. Its contribution consequence is that a nonsettler paying only its own adjudicated share has no settlement shortfall to recover from the settler; the expressly dependent Boca Grande dispute still needs its own posture and decision.
