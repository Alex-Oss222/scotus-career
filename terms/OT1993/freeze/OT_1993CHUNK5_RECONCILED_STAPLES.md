## Proposition and remedy boundaries frozen for assembly

The six reversal commitments support the same operative statutory proposition: to convict for possessing this unregistered machinegun under §5861(d), the prosecution must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the characteristics bringing the rifle within the Act's machinegun definition. Circumstantial proof may establish that knowledge. This does not require knowledge that registration was legally required, that the gun was unregistered, or that possession violated law. Freed's nonregistration rule is not overruled. The conviction and reviewed appellate judgment must be reversed and the cause remanded for proceedings consistent with the required element; no acquittal, insufficiency finding, established harmlessness result, or mandatory retrial is supplied.

A text may explain that traditional mens rea, the narrow statutory firearm class, ordinary lawful rifle possession, and substantial potential punishment distinguish knowing grenade possession in Freed and narcotics in Balint. It must not state that all gun possession is harmless, that danger is irrelevant, that every felony requires knowledge of every statutory fact, or that lenity is essential if the ordinary interpretive tools resolve the statute. The record does not warrant replacing the preserved element question with a constitutional Second Amendment or due-process invalidity question.

The two affirmance commitments join only the rival statutory construction preserving knowing possession of a dangerous weapon likely to be regulated. They do not establish a competing factual finding. Any separate writing's author, its joins, and the author of the controlling opinion remain assembly choices tested against these actual grounds. If an assembly proposal adds legal knowledge, removes statutory alternatives, resolves ultimate knowledge, or converts the narrow Ginsburg/O'Connor support into a broader explanatory join, it exceeds this handoff and requires the applicable renewed scoped process.
