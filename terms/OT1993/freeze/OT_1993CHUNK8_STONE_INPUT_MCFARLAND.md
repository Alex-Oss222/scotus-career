#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit’s judgment and remand for appointment of qualified counsel and consideration of a stay under the correct statutory authority.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Appoint counsel to prepare the federal petition and permit a stay where necessary to provide a meaningful opportunity for that work. #### C. ENTERING LAW BY ISSUE

##### Issue 1: Statutory representation before formal pleading

**Entering Law:** 21 U.S.C. §848(q)(4)(B), (7), (8), and (9), as then enacted, provide counsel qualifications, representation, and necessary services in capital collateral proceedings. Habeas Rules 2(c) and 4 require factual pleading and permit early dismissal. Murray v. Giarratano, 492 U.S. 1 (1989), does not itself create a general constitutional collateral-counsel right; this claim rests on an enacted entitlement. [R50](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/849/)

##### Issue 2: Stay authority and discretion

**Entering Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2251 authorizes a stay by the judge before whom a habeas proceeding is pending; §2283 preserves expressly authorized exceptions to its anti-injunction rule. Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880 (1983), supplies the relevant preterm capital-habeas context. [R50](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/849/)

#### D. STONE’S REVISED REASONING, APPLICATION, AND LIMITS

**Stone’s Position and Scope:** retain prepetition appointment and discretionary stay authority through two linked statutory interpretations.

This gives concrete effect to Stone's commitment to usable legal assistance without inventing a general constitutional right to collateral counsel. Section 848(q)'s qualified counsel and necessary services have substantial work before a demanding first habeas pleading is filed. Reading “proceeding” to begin with an express request for that representation gives the enacted protection a coherent function; a rule requiring an uninformed merits filing first would materially frustrate it.

The strongest objection is that courts cannot create jurisdiction merely because assistance would be useful. Stone must therefore identify §2251 separately: a motion expressly invoking counsel to pursue federal habeas starts a proceeding for that limited statutory purpose, permitting its stay authority to operate. Section 2251 also supplies the relevant express exception to §2283. This is an interpretation of interlocking provisions, not free-floating equity or an implication that every counsel entitlement carries an injunction power.

Administration should require indigence, a covered capital case, and an actual request to prepare the identified federal collateral challenge. The motion need not prematurely prove an exhausted meritorious claim, but a request unrelated to habeas does not qualify. Appointment and a stay are different decisions. The court should consider execution timing, realistic preparation needs, prior opportunities for assistance, and any demonstrated deliberate obstruction, with reasonable schedules and later review. Inability to find volunteer counsel and the actual sequence here cannot be transformed into strategic abuse by inference from lateness alone.

Reverse the denial of statutory authority, secure qualified counsel, and remand stay discretion. Exhaustion and other habeas limits continue to govern the eventual petition. Stone need not revise Giarratano's constitutional rule or derive this result from his innocence commitments in Herrera. The rule is a substantial but bounded statutory clarification, with its anti-delay boundary supplied by genuine case management rather than an automatic or indefinite stay.

**Egalitarian application:** The enacted counsel entitlement must be usable to prepare the pleading it concerns. Protect a first capital habeas applicant's meaningful opportunity while allowing courts to manage demonstrated delay or abuse through defined statutory discretion. Neither imminence of execution nor a bare assertion of dilatory motive supplies the whole analysis.

#### E. RECORD AND SOURCE SUPPORT

**Independent Record and Threshold Context:** After his Texas capital conviction and direct review, McFarland sought time and counsel for collateral review. The state trial court first postponed execution but later declined to appoint counsel or change the October 27, 1993 date. The resource center had been unable to find volunteer counsel. On October 22 he filed a federal pro se motion expressly seeking to challenge the conviction and sentence under §2254, requesting counsel under §848(q), and requesting a stay. He had not filed a formal merits petition. [R50](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/849/)

Counsel entitlement and stay jurisdiction are separate statutory questions. A right to representation does not alone create stay power; §2251 and the Anti-Injunction Act must be addressed. This is preparation for a contemplated first federal habeas petition, not adjudication of a particular exhausted merits claim. No finding of deliberate evasion or abusive delay is supplied. [R50](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/849/)
