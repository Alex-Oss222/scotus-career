## The competing lawful rules and their consequences

**Physical-danger boundary.** FELA permits recovery for negligently inflicted emotional injury where the employee sustains a physical impact or is placed in immediate risk of physical harm by the employer's negligent conduct. Duty, breach, injury and statutory causation remain separate requirements; neither physical danger without injury nor a diagnosis alone establishes liability. Immediate risk is assessed from the actual conditions, not merely the employee's fears. This construction uses the common-law zone-of-danger limit as an administrable boundary within the statutory employment relationship. It rejects a stand-alone ordinary work-stress claim and does not adopt a close-relative bystander exception. It does not decide all possible intentional-infliction claims, fear of future disease, or distress accompanying an independently compensable physical injury.

The strongest objection is that §51 says injury without an emotional/physical distinction; Urie covers cumulative occupational disease, and Buell treats common law as developing guidance. A categorical boundary may therefore exclude serious, foreseeable, medically supported harm from unsafe employment. The response supporting this path is that the statute remains a negligence statute informed by common-law limits: remedial construction and Rogers's causal-contribution rule do not eliminate the distinct legal boundary of responsibility. Physical-danger coverage is broader than an impact-only rule and fits railroad safety without an open-ended duty to insure psychological response to every workplace demand.

Under this path Gottshall's Third Circuit judgment is reversed and remanded for application of the physical-danger standard to the summary-judgment record. Severe heat and the surrounding working conditions may present an immediate-danger issue, but the Court does not decide the unresolved application, deem witnessing death sufficient by itself, or direct a defendant judgment. Carlisle's judgment is reversed and remanded with the conclusion that the work-stress injury presented is outside this boundary. His substantial evidence of negligence and genuine illness does not supply an immediate physical impact or threatened impact. The remand implements the legal bar to this claim; it does not invite a new trial under the same rejected theory.

**Bounded ordinary-negligence rule.** Serious, genuine emotional injury directly suffered by a railroad employee from negligently unsafe employment conditions is not categorically excluded because the employee was not within immediate physical danger. The plaintiff must establish the railroad's failure to use reasonable care, reasonable foreseeability of injury, a genuine serious injury and the causal contribution required by FELA. Medical and physical manifestations may establish genuineness on these records; the Court need not settle claims without such manifestations. The employer is not an insurer. Transient distress, an ordinary assignment's unwanted burdens, or fear for others alone is insufficient without the independently established elements. No universal unconscionable-abuse requirement is imported from intentional-infliction law.

The strongest objection is that severity and foreseeability do not themselves supply a stable boundary against numerous stress claims, and flexible language may allow a jury to define the duty after an injury. The response supporting this path is that the employment relationship confines claimants and the source of injury; objective foreseeability, proof of unreasonable conditions, serious injury and causation are real constraints. Carlisle was actually tried with duty, breach, causation, foreseeability and express limitations, not a charge allowing diagnosis alone to establish fault. Urie's occupational-disease treatment makes the absence of sudden physical peril a poor categorical proxy for statutory injury.

Under this path both judgments are affirmed: Gottshall may proceed to trial without any finding that Conrail was negligent; Carlisle retains the verdict sustained below. The affirmance does not approve every phrase of the Third Circuit's genuineness discussion or turn coworker notice testimony into causation proof. There is no support for ordering Carlisle a new trial merely to provide negligence instructions already given.

An impact-only exclusion is narrower than necessary to address the common-law concerns and is not adopted by any row. A direct disposition for Conrail on Gottshall's foreseeability alone would resolve contested inferences at summary judgment and is not selected. A bystander recovery rule is unnecessary to the broader employment-duty path and is not selected.

## Freeze totals and compatibility limits

Five non-Stone Justices—O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas—support the physical-danger rule and reversal of each appellate judgment. Three—Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg—support the bounded ordinary-negligence rule and affirmance of each. The Gottshall reversal entails further application proceedings; the Carlisle reversal entails implementation of the bar to the work-stress claim presented. No row supports deciding liability or a new damages amount for Gottshall.

These counts are provisional judgment/proposition commitments, not an Opinion of the Court, assignment, or prediction of the final coalition. Any proposed broader formulation must separately test the five recorded duty-boundary objections; better proof of injury or a promise to retain ordinary negligence alone does not answer those objections. Any narrower direct-exposure formulation may be assessed in reconciliation, but cannot be counted as adopted without the required supported commitment change.
