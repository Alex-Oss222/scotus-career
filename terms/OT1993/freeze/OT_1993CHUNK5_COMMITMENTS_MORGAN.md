## Necessary premises and separate components

Pacific Mutual's March 1991 action was dismissed with prejudice in August 1991 after Lampf and Beam; it did not appeal. The judgment was final and unappealable before December 19, 1991 enactment. The January 31, 1992 reinstatement motion was within sixty days. The Fifth Circuit reversed denial of reinstatement and remanded under §27A(b). Simmons/TGX's without-prejudice, relation-back and waiver facts are not this case's facts.

Section 27A(b) requires a private implied §10(b) action commenced on or before June 19, 1991, dismissed as time-barred after that date, and timely under the jurisdiction's law including retroactivity principles as that law existed on June 19, together with a plaintiff's motion within sixty days of enactment. Subsection (a)'s treatment of pending actions is distinct. The text supplies explicit temporal direction. No model participant uses a default anti-retroactivity presumption to defeat that direction. The dispute instead concerns constitutional power over this final private judgment.

Three legal components remain distinct: Congress's power to change substantive or limitation law rather than prescribe a decision under unchanged law; Article III's protection of completed adjudication; and due process respecting judgment rights and a repose defense. A legislature can change applicable law without necessarily possessing power to reopen final judgments. Conversely, describing a judgment as final identifies the constitutional question rather than mechanically answering it under a later precedent.

Robertson authorizes the identified replacement of substantive timber rules in pending litigation and expressly reserves final-judgment reopening. Harper reaches still-open direct review and preserves final judgments, but does not itself decide Congress's power. Landgraf and Rivers respect explicit temporal commands within constitutional limits and reserve this constitutional issue. The present case differs from Kokkonen's unincorporated private settlement: §27A is an express statutory reinstatement mechanism, so the missing-ancillary-jurisdiction ground cannot be copied into this dispute. Central Bank's preserved culpable-assistance claim supplies neither a reopening power nor a finding that Pacific Mutual can prove liability.

## Enhanced legal-path comparison

**Final-private-judgment path:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit's reinstatement mandate as applied to this final judgment. Article III's judicial power entails authority to end a private case conclusively when review ends. Hayburn's Case, O'Grady and McCullough support a distinction between enacted law governing adjudication and political revision of completed judgments. Robertson and Schooner Peggy concern pending adjudication; Wheeling Bridge and Fleming concern continuing prospective operation; Sioux Nation concerns the United States' treatment of its own defense and liability. The strongest objection is that Congress changed limitation law and left liability to judicial resolution, so the statute does not direct a merits result under unchanged law. The answer is that a new-law characterization avoids one Klein problem but not the different problem of reopening this terminated judicial action. This path does not deny legislative power to regulate future conduct, alter pending-case law or authorize a lawful judicial Rule 60 inquiry.

**Permissible-restoration path:** Affirm the Fifth Circuit's reinstatement/remand judgment. Congress replaced the governing limitation rule for a defined, timely moved class and left all fraud, causation, reliance, defense and remedy questions to Article III courts. Robertson supplies the distinction between changed law and dictated decision; Schor supplies attention to practical interference with judicial authority; Sioux Nation demonstrates that finality language alone does not answer every legislative-reconsideration question. The strongest objection is that this was a with-prejudice private judgment beyond appeal and the law commands reinstatement. The answer is a confined constitutional distinction: restoration of a recently extinguished statutory remedy according to the filing law on which parties acted, without a legislative finding or liability direction, is permissible changed-law adjudication. The Government-defense limitation of Sioux Nation and the pending posture of Robertson are acknowledged; neither is misdescribed as squarely deciding this private judgment.

**Due-process alternative:** For those sustaining §27A(b), Chase Securities and Turner Elkhorn support rational retroactive restoration of the earlier limitations expectation; defendants must still defeat the securities claim only under the law governing the reopened case. Danzer's extinguished-liability distinction and McCullough's judgment rights supply the strongest objection. These judges do not deem the original final judgment unreal; they conclude that this defined limitations correction, which does not create new fraud liability or direct a damages award, does not amount to arbitrary deprivation. For those reversing under Article III, a separate due-process judgment is unnecessary and reserved. No participant treats Beam or Harper as barring Congress from making an otherwise constitutional statutory change.
