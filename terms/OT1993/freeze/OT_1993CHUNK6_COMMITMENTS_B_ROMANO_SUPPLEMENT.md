# Romano v. Oklahoma — bounded Blackmun supplemental commitment

**Event:** No. 92-9093; decision modeling for June 13, 1994; direct review of the Sarfaty penalty judgment.

**Status and scope:** Frozen independent non-Stone modeling handoff under Engine §§3B and 7. This supplement addresses Blackmun alone and the newly supplied broader capital-punishment ground. **Provisional result:** Blackmun would reject continued imposition of the Sarfaty death sentence on the independently sufficient ground that capital punishment as presently administered cannot satisfy the Eighth Amendment's demands of consistency, individualized fairness and reliability. He would reverse the reviewed judgment insofar as it sustains death and remand for a lawful noncapital disposition through appropriate state proceedings, leaving the conviction and independent Thompson proceedings untouched. This is his individual ground, not entering Court law or an adjudication of a party-presented categorical claim. Removing the challenged disclosure and allowing another capital hearing would not satisfy this broader remedial position.

## Actual continuity and the changed-premise comparison

The complete public entries in *Graham* and *Johnson* establish a real difference from the historical institutional account in *Callins*. Blackmun joined effective consideration of youth and background, rejection of merely partial future-danger relevance, and remedies permitting adequate capital resentencing or lawful noncapital punishment. *Graham* also refused independent positive-character relief on the particular record without declaring that all mitigation must prevail. Neither case held capital punishment categorically invalid, and their correction of mitigation defects must not be described as judicial refusal to enforce mitigation. Their capital-remedy permissions are affirmative evidence that Blackmun then accepted correction within the system; they are not silently reinterpreted as his broader position.

Those earlier remedial joins do not establish a perpetual commitment that every capital defect can be cured. The later personal *Callins* argument expressly tests the stronger counterexample of an eligibility/selection system that permits full mitigation and nevertheless rejects it as insufficient at 1152–1153. Thus the changed *Graham* and *Johnson* premises remove his historical examples of retreat, and weaken the institutional-retreat explanation, without displacing the independently stated selection objection. Retaining the latter in this new modeled commitment is a jurisprudential conclusion supported by that later personal writing, not an inference from disappointment with the outcome of Romano, an assumed historical Romano vote, or a general label. The continuing authority of the earlier holdings remains unchanged unless the Court itself later changes it.

The public *Herrera* entry supplies continuity of concern: Blackmun proposed a probable-actual-innocence claim and remand; his criticism of restrictions on capital review was his alone. Its actual controlling disposition rejects that submission as insufficient while leaving the general claim unresolved. It supplies neither a categorical prohibition nor a holding that all innocence claims are unavailable. The *Callins* account cannot replace that narrower in-world disposition. Its accounts of other post-divergence cases likewise supply no additional in-world holdings or events here.

The January 19, 1994 *Schiro* public material supplies Blackmun's particular acquittal and capital-preclusion objections, not a broader prohibition. The March 22 *Victor/Sandoval* public material resolves instruction questions and expressly excludes unrelated sentencing issues; Blackmun's Sandoval instruction join is therefore not an affirmative rejection of every broader objection to death. The June 6 update supplies no categorical capital rule. *Farmer* shows his published practice of joining an available application while separately preserving a broader Eighth Amendment objection; its conditions rule does not govern capital sentencing. *Nichols* concerns the reliability of prior-conviction use and does not decide the separate-death-judgment question. None of these narrower holdings silently settles the present individual ground.

## Separate-writing function and join envelope

A short separate writing performs necessary legal work: it states why Blackmun rejects death independently of the limited evidentiary issue and why a fresh capital sentencing alone would not satisfy his requested relief. It need not restate the complete historical *Callins* essay, attribute a Callins event to this Court, or reproduce historical accusations inconsistent with the actual mitigation holdings. Preserve the prospect, left theoretically open in *Callins* at 1158–1159, of procedures genuinely delivering consistency, fairness and reliability; his position concerns the existing system.

Blackmun may join severable case-specific responsibility reasoning and the conclusion that this death sentence cannot stand without abandoning the broader ground. A join in those propositions does not imply assent to an assertion that the broader constitutional objection has been rejected or that another death sentence necessarily supplies an adequate remedy. The minimum revision needed for such a join is separation of the case-specific defect from the broader question and an express reservation of his distinct remedial position. He cannot fully join a rationale affirmatively declaring capital resentencing sufficient to answer his broader objection. If the Court leaves death in place, this writing functions as a dissent; if the Court grants only narrower penalty relief, it preserves his agreement in setting aside death and his disagreement with any renewed-capital remedy. Final opinion labels follow the actual assembled judgment and scope of joins, neither of which is assumed here.
