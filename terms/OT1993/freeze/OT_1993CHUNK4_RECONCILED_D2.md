## Kokkonen

The eight original commitments are confirmed: reverse the Ninth Circuit judgment and remand because the asserted ancillary or inherent power does not support enforcement of this private settlement after the stipulated dismissal. The dismissal neither incorporated its terms nor retained jurisdiction. The facts of the new settlement breach are separate from the dismissed agency-contract controversy, and breach does not violate the only judicial command actually entered: dismissal. Judicial awareness, approval, or recording of the agreement does not supply the missing jurisdictional basis. A damages or specific-performance enforcement claim needs its own basis; no independent basis has been established for this motion.

The strongest alternative is that efficient settlement enforcement completes the litigation and vindicates the court's role in securing dismissal. It fails on both available ancillary grounds: there is no relevant factual interdependence requiring joint adjudication, and no existing settlement obligation in the court's order to vindicate. Actual Willy authorizes an independently grounded collateral procedural sanction; it does not transform every later settlement contract into a matter of federal jurisdiction. Julian's reserved authority over judicially conveyed property supplies the distinction rather than a general settlement-enforcement power.

The remedy concerns jurisdiction only. No breach, specific performance, reopening, or independent-action diversity question is resolved. Under Rule 41(a)(2), the court may in its discretion make compliance or retention part of the dismissal order. Under Rule 41(a)(1)(ii), the court may embody the settlement or retain jurisdiction if the parties agree. These are permissible means of making settlement enforcement part of an actual judicial order; party consent alone does not create subject-matter jurisdiction. Absent such action, enforcement belongs in state court unless an independent federal basis exists. Rule 60(b) relief from dismissal remains distinct and undecided.

## Beecham, including Jones

The eight original commitments are confirmed: affirm both Fourth Circuit judgments on the sovereign-of-conviction question. Section 921(a)(20)'s choice-of-law clause governs its related conviction-exclusion clause. The inquiry is whether a qualifying conviction remains on the person's record; the instruction that specified postconviction events cause it not to be considered a conviction remains within that inquiry. Federal law therefore determines whether relief removes a federal conviction for these firearms purposes. State restoration does not remove these federal predicates.

The strongest contrary path separates the two sentences and stresses that States administer ordinary civil rights while federal offenders may have no comparable restoration procedure. The text supplies no separate selector among the State of the federal offense, present residence, or a former residence. Congress listed alternative routes without guaranteeing every route to every offender. The companion pardon, expungement, and set-aside language reinforces the convicting-jurisdiction reading without deciding whether federal habeas relief counts as a set-aside. Residual lenity does not alter a construction that is sufficiently clear after reading the provisions together; actual Granderson does not make a circuit disagreement sufficient by itself.

The full exception remains subject to its enacted qualification: a pardon, expungement, or restoration does not exclude the conviction if it expressly provides that the person may not ship, possess, or receive firearms. No particular federal restoration method is declared available or unavailable; §925(c), federal civil rights, automatic restoration versus affirmative governmental action, and the precise civil-rights bundle remain open. Jones's restored West Virginia convictions are not revived as predicates. No constitutional arms issue or new restoration process is decided.

## Security Services

### Reconciliation correction and changed-premise test

The original all-eight affirmance cannot survive the required Justice-specific comparison. Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter retain affirmance. Thomas and Ginsburg are corrected to reverse the tariff-invalidity judgment and remand. Their separate grounds must remain distinct. The neutral freeze is preserved as the record of the independent first stage.

The narrower proposed ground does not supply a new factual or legal premise. The historical majority already relied on published cancellation before shipment, the carrier's express incorporation of the mileage guide and supplements, the difference between a complete effective tariff and an unavailable essential component, and the absence of retroactive nullification of pre-cancellation shipments. Thomas expressly accepted prospective operation, 511 U.S. 449 n.2, while disputing the ICC's authority to nullify an already effective tariff even prospectively. Ginsburg expressly addressed the incorporated published distances, the participation supplement, and the expiration analogy, id. at 458–460. Neither treated mere private nonpayment as the only ground asserted against the carrier.

Actual Reiter preserves the filed-rate obligation, statutory unreasonable-rate remedies, and ordinary judicial/agency procedure. It does not sustain this participation rule or supersede American Trucking's agency-power limitations. Thomas's Reiter join therefore supplies no changed premise; Ginsburg did not participate. Actual Granderson addresses statutory sentencing measures and provides no rule enlarging tariff-nullification authority. General textual/contextual method cannot outweigh either Justice's direct evidence about this exact statutory scheme. No legal, factual, procedural, remedial, or prior-simulated change capable of answering their objections has been established.

### Affirmance ground and limits

The six affirming Justices support summary judgment for Kmart because the asserted distance tariff lacked an effective essential mileage component after the published cancellation of participation and before every shipment at issue. Sections 10761 and 10762 enforce operative filed rates. Sections 1312.4(d), .10(a), .27(e), and .30 require effective participation for this use of another tariff and distinguish private revocation from its public legal implementation. Until the applicable tariff is lawfully revised, the existing rate remains applicable; published cancellation makes the incorporated component unavailable prospectively. The rule does not erase charges for transportation preceding effective cancellation.

The carrier's ability physically to find the numerical distances is not enough for these six. The same tariff system giving notice of the distances states that the nonparticipant may not use them as its governing publication. Constructive notice extends to the relevant filings and supplement, not merely the pages a shipper finds convenient. Berwind-White and Davis address effective tariffs with independently remediable defects, rather than the absent legal component here. Maislin remains effective for applicable filed rates; the result does not enforce a negotiated price through an equitable exception. American Trucking's restriction on retroactive rejection does not invalidate the prospective effect of this preexisting rule on post-cancellation shipments.

This is no general power to invalidate every tariff defect, no automatic forfeiture for an unpaid private bill, and no automatic consequence of bankruptcy. Carrier or agent revocation must be reflected through the legally required public tariff change. Under §1312.10(a), failure to make the corresponding revision leaves the rates applicable until lawfully changed. A timely renewal before effective cancellation may be reflected through an amended tariff under the applicable notice rule; none is established here. The six need not decide an unrestricted agency power to nullify duly operative tariffs or adopt every agency-law rationale used below.
