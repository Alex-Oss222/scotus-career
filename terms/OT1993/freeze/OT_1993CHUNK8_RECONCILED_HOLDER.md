## Shared scope, record and remedy boundaries

All eight Associates reach the limited statutory review. The question concerns the sole-commissioner structure and a usable comparison under Section 2. The United States' May 1993 merits brief, page 6 note 4, identifies the exclusion of challenges to the appellate cohesion and white-bloc-voting findings. None of the rows below reopens those factual findings, converts demographic share into entitlement, or holds the three Gingles conditions alone sufficient without totality.

The local act authorized **five district-elected commissioners plus an at-large chairman**, conditioned on a referendum that rejected the proposal. The existing five school-board districts are a separate practical example. Neither makes a six-officer commission current law; neither establishes a division of duties not supplied by the sources. References to the proposed five district seats must not erase the chair. The Eleventh Circuit ordered formulation of a remedy and suggested a model; it imposed neither a completed map nor a final board size.

Section 2 statutory coverage, an objectively reasonable dilution comparison, unequal-opportunity proof, and remedy remain separate. Section 5 compares a proposed change with an existing practice; that baseline does not itself establish a Section 2 violation. Section 2's results test dispenses with proof of discriminatory purpose within its applicable scope, retains the totality requirement, permits consideration of protected-class electoral success, and disclaims a right to proportional election of protected-class members. None of the reversal positions decides the constitutional intentional-discrimination claim that the court of appeals did not reach. Their remedy is reversal of statutory liability and remand for that court's consideration of the constitutional claim. The affirmance positions retain liability and the existing remedy-formulation remand; they do not need to reach the constitutional alternative.

## Reconciliation findings

### Thomas and Scalia: the broader statutory ground is supported

The original neutral handoff expressly considered a broader construction excluding dilution but withheld a commitment for want of specific support. The authorized comparator now supplies that support: Thomas's complete concurrence, joined by Scalia, 512 U.S. 891–946, rejects construing Section 2's enumeration of voting qualifications, prerequisites, standards, practices and procedures to reach the allocation of electoral weight. It reads those words in their statutory context, together with the Act's definition of voting, as protecting registration, ballot access, casting and proper counting. It reads subsection (b)'s results test as governing the legality of covered practices rather than enlarging subsection (a)'s subject matter, and treats the proportional-representation proviso and administrability objections as additional support. The broader conclusion entails abandoning Gingles's dilution construction and incompatible subsequent Section 2 holdings. It does not merely exempt sole commissioners, require proof of purpose for covered access claims, or decide the scope of the constitutional claim.

The strongest ground for retaining the provisional narrow position is the Justices' actual Growe/Voinovich participation and the changed Shaw rule. Those considerations were individually tested. **Growe and Voinovich:** Scalia's statutory authorship and Thomas's joins, and both Justices' Voinovich joins, preserve existing law but do not supply a new premise absent from historical Holder. The historical concurrence expressly acknowledges and rejects that line of dilution authority; ordinary adherence in earlier cases therefore cannot alone support a different position now. The actual rules likewise maintain prerequisites, totality and reservations rather than an automatic right to proportionate seats. The reconciled proposal must candidly seek to displace their incompatible statutory holdings, not pretend they already adopted ballot-access-only coverage.

**Shaw:** this timeline's Court rejects the design-only constitutional theory; O'Connor's opposite position, joined by Scalia and Thomas, is a published dissent. The historical Holder concurrence's citations to Shaw as an affirmative Court holding and its descriptions of what that Court had established cannot be imported. Nevertheless, the independent text, scope, subsection relationship and administrability objections do not depend on the constitutional theory being controlling law. Their own actual Shaw dissent preserves their objection to racial separation as an individual constitutional position. Losing a majority on that separate constitutional question supplies no sufficient reason to abandon the statutory construction. No new constitutional injury requirement is imposed on existing Section 2 law through their separate position.

**Later same-day illustrations:** the historical De Grandy decision and its claimed confirmation of proportionality are absent from Holder's entering law. They must be omitted, not replaced by a predicted result. The concurrence's independently complete statutory argument and its critique of Gingles precede and do not logically depend on those illustrations. This changes the available discussion, not the supported statutory vote.

Accordingly, Thomas and Scalia retain reversal but replace the provisional narrow-only sufficient ground with the broader Section 2 construction. They acknowledge the benchmark difficulty but do not thereby join Kennedy's rationale at the same operative level of generality. Their agreement with the judgment cannot be counted as a join in either O'Connor's affirmative coverage proposition or a rationale preserving the dilution construction they reject. This is reconciliation of an expressly preserved lawful path, not a new factual or procedural source correction requiring neutral remodeling.

### O'Connor and Kennedy: coverage and size-comparison ground

O'Connor's historical concurrence, 512 U.S. 885–891, affirmatively treats governing-body size as a covered standard, practice or procedure while concluding that a size-dilution claim lacks an objectively determinable comparison. Her Gingles concurrence supplies the need for an undiluted norm; Houston Lawyers and Chisom support separating threshold coverage from proof. The actual Presley decision preserves broad electoral coverage and the distinction from internal allocation of duties. No changed premise supports reducing her coverage position to an assumption. Her reconciled position adopts coverage and the benchmark bar, rejects the broader Thomas/Scalia proposal on statutory stare decisis grounds, and leaves Section 5's distinct inquiry intact.

Kennedy's historical opinion, 512 U.S. 880–885, and his actual Presley, Growe and Voinovich positions support the absent-comparison ground. The fact that an alternative is practicable does not explain why it supplies the undiluted norm for the number of offices. That objection addresses size as the comparison variable, rather than inviting plaintiffs simply to repeat the same claim with another feasible number. He reserves an affirmative threshold-coverage ruling and does not infer Section 2 dilution cognizability from Section 5 preclearance. His discussion must preserve that distinction; it must not represent O'Connor as joining any disputed coverage implication. No independent record, question or remedy change supports departure from that historical ground.

### Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: contextual benchmark survives

Their historical positions match the original commitments. Blackmun's opinion, 512 U.S. 946–956, distinguishes threshold coverage from reasonable comparison and requires the claimant to establish a reasonable, workable alternative grounded in the case's legal and factual setting. It gives the actual local legislative authorization, school-board practice and county governing functions substantive work while retaining Gingles, totality, the prohibition on proportional entitlement, and a separate remedial inquiry. None is an entitlement to expand every single office or any multimember legislature until a minority can win.

Stevens's separate opinion, 512 U.S. 957–966, supplies statutory stare decisis, repeated congressional reenactments and the difference between interpreting Congress's adopted compromise and replacing it. Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg historically joined that position. Their actual Growe and Voinovich commitments preserve the line it defends; actual Shaw imposes no additional constitutional prerequisite on Section 2. Their coverage and benchmark votes need no change.

Ginsburg's own historical writing, 512 U.S. 956–957, materially resolves the original row's stated lack of an earlier election-specific source: she treats competing statutory purposes as a judicial task to be implemented case by case, with both remedial purpose and limits considered, rather than a basis for deleting coverage. This is newly available same-matter comparator evidence, not an invented prior Supreme Court join or a new neutral fact. It confirms her existing contextual statutory application. No historical authorship or joinder is assigned in this simulation by this evidence.
