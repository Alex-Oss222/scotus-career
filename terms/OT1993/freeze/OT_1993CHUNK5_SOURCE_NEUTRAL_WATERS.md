## Record and disputed statements

Churchill, an obstetrics nurse at public McDonough District Hospital, spoke with nurse Perkins-Graham during a January16,1987 dinner break. Perkins-Graham was considering transferring to obstetrics. Nurses Ballew and Welty and Dr.Thomas Koch overheard parts. Hospital defendants are supervisors Waters and Davis, president Hopper, and the municipal hospital. Churchill was discharged January27; Hopper upheld the discharge after her internal grievance (February12 letter).

**Ballew's reported version:** Churchill spent at least20minutes criticizing Waters and how bad obstetrics was; Waters was trying to find reasons to fire her; Waters had wrongly blamed her for a patient complaint. Ballew reported that the conversation discouraged Perkins-Graham from transferring. **Perkins-Graham's report:** Churchill made unkind/inappropriate comments about Waters, described an earlier negative evaluation and said wiping the slate clean with Waters was not possible, said things were generally bad in obstetrics because of administration, and said Davis was ruining the hospital. These are witness accounts, not findings of what Churchill actually said.

**Churchill's version:** Most of the conversation concerned inadequately implemented cross-training, in which nurses were moved from an overstaffed ward to an unfamiliar specialty to cover shortages rather than receive organized training. She believed that practice threatened patient care and had previously complained to Waters and Davis. She admits criticizing Davis's staffing policies as threatening to ruin the hospital by impeding nursing care, but says she defended Waters and encouraged the transfer. Welty and Koch corroborate her account of the parts they heard. Churchill alleges Ballew was biased against her because Churchill had previously covered for Ballew's error. No factfinder resolved these competing accounts at summary judgment.

## Investigation, belief, and causation are distinct

Ballew first told Waters; Waters met Ballew again to confirm. Waters and Davis also interviewed Perkins-Graham. They did not interview Koch or Welty, and did not obtain Churchill's account before notifying her of discharge. On grievance, Hopper met Churchill and heard her version, reviewed Waters/Davis's written reports, and instructed human-resources vice-president Magin to reinterview Ballew. He then rejected the grievance. This establishes what was done; it does not itself establish reasonableness, subjective belief, or actual motive.

Churchill points to earlier patient-care criticism, management hostility toward her association with Koch (another staffing critic), the abrupt deterioration in evaluations during1986, and the August21 operating-room dispute. Earlier evaluations were generally positive; the December1986 evaluation again rated work satisfactory or better while criticizing negative behavior toward Waters's leadership. In the emergency cesarean-section episode Waters ordered Churchill out to check another patient, Koch objected to interference with his operating room, and subsequent discipline/insubordination disputes followed. The lower court viewed this evidence and reasonable inferences favorably to Churchill; it did not find retaliatory motive after trial. The evidence can bear on whether management acted because of the reported disruptive statements, the allegedly protected cross-training discussion in the same conversation, or earlier criticism. Neither adequate investigation nor proof that some reported words were unprotected necessarily resolves that causal question.

## Lower grounds and positions

The district court granted defendants summary judgment inMay1991: on either version speech was private, and alternatively disruption outweighed any public interest. The Seventh Circuit reversed the free-speech judgment, treated Churchill's account as concerning patient care and state nursing rules, and required a jury determination of disputed speech content before applying the legal Pickering/Connick balance. It rejected a separately created pretermination First Amendment hearing entitlement for an at-will employee, but held employers responsible for firing based on speech later found protected despite ignorance of its exact content. Those are different propositions; do not call the lower court's approach a freestanding mandatory hearing rule.

Petitioners urge reliance on employers' actual factual conclusions and an intentional-retaliation inquiry, citing management needs and Mt.Healthy. Respondents' Supreme Court brief permits reliance on factual conclusions reached reasonably (p.39) but attacks the adequacy of this investigation; this position differs from the Seventh Circuit's jury-truth rule. Available analytical forks thus include actual speech, honestly believed speech, and reasonably investigated/believed speech. Distinguish a judge's legal public-concern/disruption assessment from factfinding about words, context, genuine belief, and causation.

Pickering391U.S.563 and Connick461U.S.138 supply the employee/public-employer balance; Givhan439U.S.410 concerns privately delivered public-employee speech; Mt.Healthy429U.S.274 supplies protected conduct as substantial/motivating factor and the employer's same-decision burden by a preponderance. Perry408U.S.593 preserves constitutional protection despite lack of tenure; Roth408U.S.564 addresses property-based process, a distinct question. Burnup&Sims379U.S.21 is an NLRA mistaken-misconduct case, not itself a First Amendment employment holding. Rankin483U.S.378 addresses context/disruption. Prior opinions and each present Justice's own joins should be verified from eligible sources, not inferred from this report's later discussion.

## Immunity, municipal responsibility, remedy limits

Individuals separately claim qualified immunity because in1987 no clear law forbade acting on the conduct reported to them. The Seventh Circuit rejected their claimed factual framing, reasoning under Harlow457U.S.800 and Anderson483U.S.635 that protected public-concern speech rights were clear. Immunity is a defense to personal damages and must not be equated with whether speech was protected, whether a constitutional violation occurred, or entitlement to prospective relief. The precise level of clearly established right is disputed. Nothing in this packet makes a new duty adopted in1994 clearly established in1987 by assertion.

The municipal policy/custom issue was argued in petitioners' merits brief but omitted from the certiorari petition. The earlier expressive-association claim was dismissed separately below; no present grant component reviving that claim is established. This is review of summary judgment, not a verdict for Churchill. No damages amount, reinstatement entitlement, established actual words, or final factual finding of retaliation is supplied. The full trial/deposition record and all merits briefs have not been independently recovered. The preserved factual disputes are sufficiently documented for the legal forks, but any new factual finding or individualized immunity result requires its own support.
