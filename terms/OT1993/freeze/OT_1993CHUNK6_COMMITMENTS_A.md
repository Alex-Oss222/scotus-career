## Farmer v. Brennan

### Component definitions and available relief

1. **Culpability and objective danger:** Failure to protect against a substantial risk of serious harm requires the serious objective condition and deliberate indifference. On the subjective formulation, the particular official must know facts indicating the substantial danger, actually appreciate that danger, and disregard it. Knowledge of every eventual detail, a named attacker or an express prisoner complaint is not an additional element. An official who actually lacks that appreciation is not liable merely because a reasonable official should have understood the facts.
2. **Proof and summary judgment:** Knowledge may be proved by ordinary circumstantial evidence, including obviousness, duration, prior incidents and information actually reaching the defendant. Obviousness permits an inference; it does not irrebuttably impute knowledge. A defendant may show lack of awareness, a belief the danger was insubstantial, or a reasonable protective response. A reasonable response defeats culpable disregard even if it does not avert injury. The lower court's absence-of-complaint rationale cannot conclusively defeat such proof. Vacate and remand for proper treatment of the discovery request and record; do not find liability or mandate trial.
3. **Relief and defenses:** Leave defendant-specific responsibility, causation, damages and qualified immunity for proper decision below. Meyer prohibits converting the presented personal claims into direct agency/Treasury damages. The Terre Haute warden and Bureau Director are official-capacity-only defendants; the Oxford warden, case manager and two regional officials have both-capacity claims. Prospective relief requires current substantial danger and current culpable nonresponse by officials able to provide protection; changed placement matters. The court may use facts developed during litigation to determine present risk and knowledge, but notice of an allegation alone is not a finding of either. No automatic bar against all penitentiary placement issues.

The governing standards are **Serious future health risks in prison conditions**, present operation effective June 18, 1993 (Helling), and **Physical injury in a nontrivial malicious custodial beating**, present operation effective February 25, 1992 (Hudson). Helling retains Wilson's separate culpability requirement without already defining the exact actual-awareness rule. Hudson supplies no universal conditions mental state. Elder, effective February 23, 1994, requires all relevant legal authority in deciding preserved immunity; this June 1994 clarification is not retroactive notice of a clearly established right in March 1989.

### Enhanced alternative-path comparison

**Actual awareness versus should-have-known culpability.** The broader path would impose liability when the risk was so obvious that an objectively reasonable official would have appreciated it. Its strongest case is the State's exclusive control over prisoners' safety and the danger of rewarding self-protective ignorance. Blackmun and Stevens's Wilson concurrence supports the underlying objection to the intent requirement. But Wilson's majority expressly treats objective gravity and subjective culpability as separate, and Helling retained that division in this timeline. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter personally joined that law; Ginsburg has no verified basis here to discard it. Thomas's narrower judgment position also rejects expanding liability. Effective circumstantial proof answers much of the practical objection without converting what the official should have understood into what the official actually understood. An official cannot obtain judgment merely by denying awareness when supported evidence permits the contrary inference. Conversely, a factfinder's genuine conclusion of no awareness is not defeated by labeling the risk obvious. Blackmun and Stevens accept this presented disposition while preserving their broader view separately.

**Vacatur versus affirmance for insufficient proof.** Officials' strongest argument is that the transfer defendants might not know destination conditions and that silence plus their affidavits leaves no genuine issue. The record does not establish that conclusion for every defendant; Farmer sought the incident-history and knowledge discovery the district court denied under its warning-based reasoning. All eight support reconsideration under the right rule, not a finding that discovery necessarily produces a claim. Thomas's judgment concurrence is especially limited to that procedural defect. A narrower refusal to decide ultimate knowledge satisfies each Justice's individualized-liability objection.

**Prospective protection versus immediate dismissal or blanket transfer ban.** Helling permits prevention before a new injury, but the Government's account of medium-security general-population confinement means the old administrative-detention premise cannot resolve current danger. Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter's Helling joins support current inquiry; Scalia and Ginsburg apply its present authority; Thomas permits the judgment remand under the unchallenged framework without a broader endorsement. No Justice is committed to finding a live threat or granting the requested universal penitentiary exclusion.

Blackmun and Stevens may explain their preserved Wilson objections without changing their join in the narrower application. No author is selected.

## Department of Revenue of Montana v. Kurth Ranch

### Component definitions and entering-law limits

1. **Character of the surviving assessment:** Apply the 1987 Act, not later amendments. Section 15-25-111(1) taxes possession/storage; subsection (2) sets the marijuana charge at the greater of 10% assessed market value or $100 per ounce; subsection (3) postpones collection until fines/forfeitures are satisfied. The arrest-linked 72-hour return rule is distinct from initial assessment and from the officer's report when a taxpayer declines to sign. The case concerns the surviving harvested-marijuana assessment, approximately $181,000 principal, not the whole original claim.
2. **Constitutional consequence:** If this assessment is a second punishment, affirm disallowance as applied following the completed criminal sanctions. A high rate, deterrent aim, taxation of illegal activity, or collection after seizure is not alone sufficient. The proposed punitive ground turns on their combined criminal-process-dependent operation and taxation of property the State already confiscated. Destruction is only reported as presumed and is not indispensable to this ground. Ordinary taxes and authorized cumulative sanctions in one proceeding remain distinct.
3. **Same-offense and remedy scope:** Montana did not raise below its contention that only Richard had a same-offense claim. The other five pleaded conspiracy and invoke withdrawn possession charges and plea arrangements. Do not equate conspiracy and possession, adjudicate those plea terms or make the contention jurisdictional by calling it standing. Decline that new ground in this review. The preserved controversy can be decided on the litigation premise accepted below without declaring a general conspiracy exception. Affirmance leaves the lower disallowance and its remittance consequence intact without fixing an unverified exact accounting amount; reversal would remove the federal bar and return remaining implementation to the courts below, without reviving state-law-rejected drug components.

The standard **Successive prosecutions, evidentiary overlap and criminal contempt** has present operation effective June 28, 1993 (Dixon), with Felix's conspiracy/evidence components effective March 25, 1992. Grady survives and is not imported as a same-evidence rule. Dixon's preservation of Grady does not itself classify the tax or supply a missing plea fact. **Punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review**, effective June 28, 1993 (Austin/Alexander), is an analogy with express clause-specific limits, not a Double Jeopardy holding for all partly punitive exactions. Schiro's initial-sentencing distinction does not turn this later bankruptcy tax collection into one criminal sentencing proceeding.

### Enhanced alternative-path comparison

**Punishment path.** Halper establishes that a civil label cannot protect a second punitive exaction. Its strongest application here is structural rather than a finding of secret motive: criminal arrest triggers the return machinery; collection is subordinated to criminal fines/forfeitures; the object has been taken by the State; the rate produces an eightfold value charge for the low-value shake. Sanchez expressly relied on absence of a criminal-conduct predicate and permits high or deterrent taxes, so neither high rate nor deterrence alone answers the question. Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg accept the combination as crossing the boundary. Kennedy requires this objective explanation; Blackmun and Souter cannot simply substitute Austin's partly-punitive fines standard for Halper's different protection.

**Civil-tax path.** Sanchez and Helvering recognize civil liabilities tied to unlawful conduct and tax enforcement; taxes need not compensate a claimant's measurable loss. The legal incidence is earlier possession; subsequent confiscation and delayed collection need not change its nature. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas accept that account and reject the Ninth Circuit's use of missing cost evidence as dispositive. The strongest answer is that incidence alone does not explain a scheme operated only through criminal arrest and satisfied after sanctions on a now-confiscated object. The three do not adopt that answer, but their position is not a general exemption for any exaction the State labels a tax. Neither their Austin views nor their disagreement with Grady proves a categorical rejection of Double Jeopardy protection against punitive civil forms.

**Accounting/remand alternative.** Halper allowed a governmental accounting on its own record. Here the bankruptcy court afforded an opportunity to prove costs and the State did not provide trial evidence; general national expenditures are not a proved individual cost. An affirming Justice need not give a new opportunity where the decisive ground is the tax's operation rather than an exact compensation ratio. The reversing Justices reject the premise that an ordinary tax requires such accounting. Neither side is committed to new $181,100, $208,105 or $208,150 money commands; the sources' principal/charges discrepancy remains outside this constitutional disposition.

**Only-Richard alternative.** If timely and supported, the distinct conspiracy pleas would require Felix, Grady and any actual plea terms to be examined. ## Key Tronic Corp. v. United States

### Three distinct judgment components

1. **Private cost-recovery litigation fees:** The disputed texts are §107(a)(4)(B)'s necessary response costs incurred by another person **consistent with** the national contingency plan, and §101(25)'s inclusion of enforcement activities related to removal/remedial action. The American Rule requires statutory authorization for shifting litigation fees; it does not require one exclusive verbal formula. The fork is whether these actual terms provide sufficiently explicit authorization for private cost-allocation litigation. Government §107(a)(4)(A)'s **not inconsistent** wording is not substituted for subsection (B).
2. **EPA settlement negotiation:** Reject recovery of the legal work preparing and negotiating the agreement fixing Key Tronic's own liability. Protecting the client's allocation position is not itself necessary removal or remediation or private enforcement against another responsible party. No rule excludes distinct physical cleanup or genuinely separate response work simply because performed while negotiation occurs.
3. **Identification of other polluters:** Permit recovery of professional investigation that independently performs necessary response work, including the proved search that revealed Air Force disposal activity and prompted EPA enforcement. A task does not become a litigation fee simply because an attorney performed it. The claimant must establish the actual response function, necessity, plan consistency and recoverable amount; a mere litigation-preparation label cannot decide this category either way. Reverse the categorical exclusion and remand the amount/category application, without reinstating the entire undifferentiated $155,500 award.

The $4.2 million consent obligation/contribution claim remains dismissed under the admitted concession and §122(g)(5)'s protection for matters addressed in the Air Force settlement. The independent pre-settlement response claim is distinct; settled costs and the unappealed prejudgment-interest award are not reopened. This case creates neither a universal contribution immunity nor a government fee holding. Federal-facility amenability, available cause and recoverable category remain separate.

**No contingency enhancement under the environmental fee provisions**, effective June 24, 1992 (Dague), concerns amount under the RCRA and Clean Water Act provisions, not whether CERCLA creates this fee authorization. Fogerty, effective March 1, 1994, reinforces the American Rule and statute-specific source; its §505 permission does not carry into CERCLA. WVUH's fee/expert distinction likewise demonstrates attention to the actual text rather than an automatic answer to differently worded response costs.

### Enhanced alternative-path comparison

**Private litigation: authorization absent or text already sufficient.** The five denial commitments give Runyon full effect: a law may authorize a private suit and serve an important public purpose while leaving its lawyer's costs where they fall. CERCLA's definitions relate enforcement to removal and remedial action; they do not expressly identify the private cost-allocation suit or its attorney's fees. Congress separately addressed fees elsewhere in the statute. The strongest counterargument is that enforcement ordinarily includes a suit to enforce the recovery obligation and the definition contains no governmental-only limitation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas adopt that reading; their WVUH/Germain/Fogerty positions support giving a general operative phrase its actual breadth without demanding a particular noun. Their position must not be misdescribed as dispensing with statutory authorization: they find it in §§107 and 101(25). Conversely, the five do not establish a rule that Congress must always use the words attorney's fees; they find this text and setting insufficient.

Blackmun and Stevens's WVUH dissent is a substantial counterweight to their denial. It sought the full content of an enacted fee provision in its setting, while their Runyon joins refused creating fee authority from general remedial language. Their commitment here rests on the latter threshold distinction. O'Connor and Souter's WVUH majority joins also support attention to Congress's separately named cost categories. Scalia and Kennedy can distinguish that decision because CERCLA uses an affirmative enforcement definition absent from the expert-fee claim. Thomas's current Fogerty concurrence makes that express-text distinction indispensable to his broader recovery ground. Ginsburg's current joins supply no reason to replace the ordinary American Rule solely with cleanup policy.

**Negotiation: useful settlement work versus necessary response/enforcement.** The best recovery argument is that negotiations can facilitate cleanup and place the financial burden on responsible parties. On the admitted record, however, this category prepared and negotiated Key Tronic's own liability agreement, rather than identifying a responsible participant or enforcing another party's duty. The statutory function therefore fails even for the three Justices who allow litigation enforcement costs. The common denial is record-specific and does not deny that a different task during negotiations could independently qualify as response work.

**Identification: all lawyer bills excluded versus functional response costs.** A categorical exclusion has administrative simplicity and prevents repackaging lawsuit preparation. It fails to account for the established independent discovery of the Air Force and resulting EPA action, work that a nonlawyer investigator could perform to advance response. All eight accept the functional distinction, but none treats a bill, usefulness to the client or a future possibility of recovery as sufficient proof. The remand must separate eligible tasks and amounts and preserve §107(a)(4)(B)'s necessity/plan-consistency requirements. It supplies no freestanding prevailing-party fee award.

**Freeze result:** Litigation fees: Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg affirm denial; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reverse the exclusion and remand for the statutory inquiry. EPA-negotiation fees: all eight affirm denial on the recorded function. Identification costs: all eight reverse categorical exclusion and remand proper category/amount application. No authorship or final opinion topology is selected.
