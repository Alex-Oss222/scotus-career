## Components, exact grounds, and join limits

**1. Trigger and conditioning reach — six affirming joins.** A federally licensed activity that may result in a discharge into navigable waters triggers §401(a)(1); this project concededly has construction-related dredged/fill discharges and a tailrace discharge. Section 401(d)'s applicant-compliance language permits conditions on the activity as a whole as necessary to secure compliance with the listed federal limitations and other appropriate State-law requirements. The certification is not confined to conditions addressing only the particular triggering discharge. The six adopt this applicant/activity construction, not merely the Government's possible discharge-nexus alternative. Historical support: 711–713.

The trigger remains real; no holding classifies every diversion as a discharge. The State may ensure compliance with the specified water-quality requirements, not regulate unrelated matters at will. Lawful certification conditions become conditions of the federal license. Preserve §401(a)(1)'s originating-jurisdiction/EPA qualifications, public notice for all applications, hearings to the extent deemed appropriate, reasonable-period waiver capped at one year after receipt, and the distinction between waiver for failure/refusal to act and denial prohibiting a license. No present breach of these procedural terms is found.

**2. Statutory incorporation and agency interpretation — six affirming joins.** Section 303 standards are enforceable in certification on two stated grounds: §401(d)'s incorporation of §301, whose §301(b)(1)(C) includes the more stringent limitations necessary to meet water-quality standards; and §401(d)'s authorization of other appropriate State-law requirements, which at least includes standards adopted pursuant to §303. These are two independently sufficient routes for including these standards, not a holding about every other State law. Historical support: 712–713 and n.3.

All six accept EPA's interpretation in §121.2(a)(3), requiring reasonable assurance that the activity will meet applicable standards, as a reasonable reading entitled to deference within the actual statutory delegation. The text independently supports their construction; deference reinforces it and does not confer power to contradict the statute. Address §121.2(a)(4)'s discharge language as the dissent's substantial contrary text rather than omitting it. Actual Arkansas supplies respect for a permissible interpretation of the water-quality scheme, not its separate §402 proof or review rules. Actual Health Care does not command an agency-favoring result across all statutes; its textual dissent is fully consistent with O'Connor and Kennedy reaching a different conclusion about this distinct applicant-compliance provision. Historical support: 712 and dissent 728–729. The Government's alternative argument did not itself request deference resolving the entire reach issue; do not describe it as a concession supporting the majority's construction.

**3. Designated uses and criteria — six affirming joins.** Section 303(c)(2)(A) makes designated uses and criteria based on those uses separate components of a water-quality standard. The project must satisfy both; compliance with numerical criteria is not always compliance with the standard. Uses may therefore support a necessary minimum-flow condition even without an allegation that a listed numerical threshold would be exceeded. Criteria themselves can be narrative; §131.3(b) says compliance generally protects designated uses, not invariably. Historical support: 714–718. Preserve the conditional toxic-pollutant numerical requirement and its biological-monitoring alternative; neither every criterion nor every use must be expressed numerically. No blanket enforceability of every unattainable aspirational use is adopted: the complete §131.10 existing/designated-use distinction, attainability grounds and procedures remain applicable. The actual finding here concerns preservation of the approved fish use, not a newly found optimal fishery or universal dam-operation rule.

**4. Existing-use antidegradation — six affirming joins, independently sufficient application within the same statutory authority.** Washington's condition also lawfully maintains and protects the existing instream fish use under §131.12(a)(1) and its state counterpart. Once the conditioning authority in components 1–2 is established, this is an additional ground for sustaining this flow requirement. Historical support: 718–719 expressly accepts that application. It is not merely background or a hypothetical option.

Maintain all qualifications: the federal existing-use floor differs from conditional lowering of higher-quality water after the required findings/participation and controls; outstanding-resource and thermal rules are distinct. The Washington existing-beneficial-use provision and its separate long-term-irreparable-harm provision must not be merged. Class AA status or location on federal land does not establish an outstanding-resource designation. The Court need not announce a new rule for any absent exception or find that a use-removal application has been made. The clean source completion's exact §131.10(g)(4) text qualifies the historical opinion's shorthand at 720; it is not a new duty that every dam attain every designated use regardless of infeasibility.

**5. Water quantity and licensing — six affirming joins.** Enough water can be necessary to protect water quality and an adopted aquatic use. Sections 101(g) and 510(2) preserve allocation rights and jurisdiction; they do not categorically remove necessary water-quality controls over a user's activity. This certificate does not allocate proprietary rights and separately requires a water-right permit before construction. Historical support: 719–721. Section 510(1)'s preservation of stricter pollution regulation retains its express non-less-stringent qualification.

California v. FERC and First Iowa retain force for the federal licensing structure and conflicting independent state requirements. Congress expressly supplies the §401 certification role here; no FERC license or conflicting federal condition yet exists. No implied limitation is adopted merely because a possible future license might conflict; the applicants may pursue available judicial remedies concerning a later license. The Court neither grants a license nor orders construction nor decides the legal outcome of an actual future conflict. Historical support: 721–723; Escondido's review reference at 723. This is a reservation, not approval of any possible State veto on any topic.

**6. Scalia and Thomas — discharge-confined dissent and criteria alternative.** Both maintain the frozen reversal/remand. Their common first ground reads §401(d) within (a)'s certification of discharges; applicant identifies the obligated party, and the specific discharge-related statutory list constrains the general State-law phrase. Residual flow regulates intake/diversion rather than an established water-quality violation caused by the triggering discharge. The Government's alternative is acknowledged but not accepted without the required connection. Historical support: 724–728.

They reject deference as a substitute for identifying statutory ambiguity and an agency construction addressing the precise issue. Section 121.2(a)(4) speaks specifically to discharge conditions, while (a)(3)'s activity assurance does not definitively resolve the conditioning issue. Historical support: 728–729. Their independent City of Chicago and Health Care positions provide personal support for this textual boundary; their actual Arkansas joins concern the different affirmative §402 delegation.

Reconciliation also retains their **alternative §303 ground**: designated uses should be implemented through corresponding protective criteria, rather than treated as a freestanding source of any condition tending toward an aspirational use. They regard the absence of an asserted criteria violation as significant here and fear that separating the use from criteria leaves no adequate statutory limit. Historical support: 729–732. This ground does not assert that all criteria must be numerical, deny all narrative criteria, or erase the valid existing-use policy; it disputes the claimed independent conditioning authority. No changed actual precedent or newly recovered regulation requires these Justices to abandon that objection.

They also preserve the federal-licensing structural argument, including §401(d)'s mandatory effect on the later license and the Federal Power Act's allocation of licensing responsibility. They do not find that FERC has issued a conflicting condition. Historical support: 732–737. Remedy remains reversal of federal authorization of this condition and remand, not issuance of a federal license or invalidation of all separate state water powers.
