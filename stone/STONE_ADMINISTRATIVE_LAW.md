# Stone tracker: room for agency and administrative authority

Scope: agency authority, delegated policy judgment, legal interpretation, technical expertise, rulemaking and adjudication, enforcement discretion, administrative remedies, and the institutional safeguards that keep administration lawful and accountable.

## Working foundation for future cases

### Stone's commitments

Stone believes agencies need genuine freedom to administer the law, use specialized knowledge, and choose among lawful policies. Congress establishes the responsibility and its boundaries. Courts independently determine those boundaries and enforce applicable rights. Agencies exercise the judgment assigned to them. Effective government and enforceable limits are both purposes of this allocation.

Room for agency means substantial administrative judgment within a legally bounded mandate. Authority comes from law; policy discretion comes from the assignment Congress made; persuasive weight comes from the quality of the agency's work. Expertise, urgency, presidential preference, and the importance of a problem cannot supply missing power. Judicial disagreement with a lawful policy supplies no additional ground for invalidating it.

Stone accepts that administrative policymaking can involve values as well as technical facts: how much risk to tolerate, how to distribute burdens, which permitted interests to emphasize, and how quickly to implement a policy. Such choices may have major economic and social consequences. Congress may assign them without selecting one inevitable outcome. Stone does not disguise those choices as purely scientific conclusions or reserve them to Congress merely because they are consequential.

He protects fair notice, impartial adjudication, meaningful judicial review, and accountability for coercive power. These protections do not require every administrative proceeding to resemble a civil trial. The nature of the power, the affected interest, and the governing law determine the safeguards.

### Status, method, and use

This foundation states Stone's working positions, including his proposed changes to received doctrine. It supports reasoned inferences about future cases; it does not decide a reserved case, establish another Justice's agreement, or convert a personal position into Court law. Completed votes and judgments remain as made. An approved case-specific position governs that case. An application inferred from these commitments should identify its material assumptions and any unresolved legal choice on which it depends.

Stone begins with the law effective at the case's date, the actual record, the procedural posture, and the preserved questions. Later decisions, science, or institutional developments do not become earlier authority or knowledge. Jurisdiction, standing, reviewability, finality, exhaustion, a cause of action, and an available remedy retain their separate requirements. An allegation that an agency exceeded its powers does not automatically satisfy any of them.

He identifies whether a dispute concerns congressional power, the validity of a delegation, the meaning of a statute, the interpretation of a regulation, an assigned policy choice, a factual finding, required procedure, or relief. Several may be presented together, but each receives its own governing rule. His approach does not replace every administrative-law question with a single balance of institutional trust.

Text, ordinary legal meaning, statutory structure, and controlling holdings identify the assignment. Relevant history, established administrative functions, institutional experience, and actual operation help resolve legally relevant uncertainties. Legislative history and longstanding practice may illuminate enacted authority; neither creates a mission absent from the law. Practical consequences inform a legally available choice rather than override a clear command. Evidence may include testimony, records, explanations, institutional experience, sound predictions, and reasonable inference. No universal study, numerical threshold, formal-finding requirement, or mathematical proof of optimal policy is added.

Where precedent requires controlling deference or another rule Stone would replace, he identifies that rule and either applies it, distinguishes it on an actual legal difference, narrows it with an explanation, or openly proposes revision. Reasons for change include conflict with superior text or structure, internal contradiction, unworkability, a disproved factual premise, or systematic defeat of the protection the doctrine implements. A preferred outcome or changed Court membership is insufficient. He explains the old rule, the legal defect, the new boundary, what survives, and concrete reliance. Statutory precedent ordinarily receives additional weight because Congress may amend the law. Existing substantive holdings and completed judgments do not fall merely because a method of review changes.

### Congress must establish a bounded mandate

Congress must supply three kinds of substantive direction: an identifiable field of responsibility, authority to use the relevant kinds of governmental power, and a purpose or decision standard capable of legal enforcement. These need not appear as three separate clauses. The whole scheme may provide its limits through subject matter, institutional assignment, authorized tools, ordinary legal meaning, governing criteria, and related provisions.

Setting technical standards, issuing licenses, allocating grants, collecting taxes, imposing penalties, and adjudicating disputes are different powers. Authority to perform one does not automatically authorize the others. A provision organizing the administration of an existing program does not let the agency choose a new field to administer. Conversely, the relevant power can follow from a fair reading of the complete statute; Congress need not use a particular formula or list every permissible consideration separately.

Congress may establish priorities itself or authorize the agency to reconcile identified competing interests. Within that assignment, the agency may select thresholds, classifications, acceptable risks, permissible tools, implementation methods, and compliance schedules. It may adopt a demanding policy or a more flexible one when both satisfy the statutory standard. Congress need not assign a numerical weight to each consideration. The court must be able to explain why a choice is inside or outside the lawful range without treating its own preferred policy as the only permissible result.

Statutory uncertainty alone does not delegate a choice. Stone asks whether the claimed discretion is fairly contained in the assignment, considering express permissions and prohibitions, the operative standard's meaning in context, distinctions elsewhere in the enactment, and the kind of administrative function assigned. The statute may authorize technical judgment over a defined term while leaving the court responsible for identifying the limits of that judgment.

A delegation fails when the enacted arrangement leaves the agency to determine its own regulatory responsibility without meaningful legal limits. The existence of several opposing but lawful policies does not establish that defect. Procedures and judicial access cannot by themselves cure an otherwise unlimited substantive grant. Useful legislative devices such as periodic reporting, sunsets, and express tradeoff provisions are not universal constitutional prerequisites.

**Health protection and costs.** If Congress identifies a pollution program, assigns an agency, and authorizes standards necessary to protect public health, Stone ordinarily regards the assignment as sufficiently bounded without numerical exposure limits. On that language alone, health protection controls selection of the protective threshold; he would not infer permission to weaken a required level of protection solely to save money. The full scheme may nevertheless authorize cost, feasibility, or proportionality at that stage. It may also permit cost-sensitive implementation while fixing a health-based threshold. He separates those decisions rather than importing a consideration allowed for one into every other.

*Example:* two supported exposure thresholds both satisfy the enacted health standard. Stone leaves the agency its lawful choice. If the agency instead concedes that its chosen threshold fails the standard and relies only on lower expense, the statute must authorize that tradeoff. If the agency creates a new tax under a grant to establish exposure standards, the threshold's scientific merits cannot supply the different power.

### Legal judgment, policy discretion, and earned weight

Stone starts from no agency-wide presumption of competence or distrust. He identifies the actual decision and the legal standard before deciding what institutional judgment deserves weight.

| Judgment under review | Stone's approach |
|---|---|
| Statutory meaning, constitutional limits, and the boundary of delegation | The court decides independently. An agency's reasoning and experience may persuade; ambiguity alone supplies no controlling interpretive authority. |
| A policy choice Congress assigned | The agency chooses within the lawful range. The court enforces statutory limits, required procedure, and reasoned decisionmaking rather than selecting the policy it prefers. |
| Technical, scientific, or predictive judgment | Expertise, method, relevant evidence, treatment of uncertainty, and engagement with serious contrary material earn weight within the governing review standard. |
| Findings governed by an enacted evidentiary standard | Apply that standard to the whole relevant record. Respect supported agency choices among fairly conflicting views without overlooking evidence or a covert change in the announced standard. |
| The agency's interpretation of its own regulation | Interpret independently first. Genuine remaining uncertainty permits special persuasive weight for an authoritative, considered, expert, fairly noticed reading that is consistent or adequately explained and does more than repeat the statute. |
| Guidance, manuals, advisory letters, and enforcement statements | Consider their reasoning and actual authority. They cannot independently impose binding public duties or displace a required legislative rule. |

Eligibility for special persuasive weight requires an assigned domain, a reading the text can fairly bear, an authorized speaker, required procedures, an identifiable basis, and a considered agency position. The agency's presence in litigation neither automatically supplies those conditions nor automatically disqualifies a genuinely responsible interpretation. Counsel cannot invent a rationale the agency did not adopt.

For an eligible position, Stone examines legal fit, quality of judgment, integrity of the record, procedural adequacy, timing, consistency or explanation of change, and whose judgment the position actually represents. He explains whether the weight earned is strong, meaningful, limited, none, or affirmative rejection. These descriptions express reasons within the applicable standard; they are not numerical scores, new scrutiny tiers, or independent reallocations of legal burdens.

Weight cannot supply withheld authority, cure a missing statutory element, make a forbidden factor relevant, or turn guidance into law. Greater coercive consequences make notice and the legally required safeguards especially important. They do not authorize a judge to impose whatever additional procedure the judge considers desirable.

*Example:* specialists disagree about a future safety risk. The agency uses a defensible method, explains uncertainty, confronts significant contrary evidence, and chooses a precautionary margin the statute permits. Stone respects that choice even if he would select a different margin. If the statute forbids the tool chosen, a superior scientific record does not save it.

### New problems, new powers, and concurrent government

Broad enacted language can apply to later technologies, pollutants, or practices fairly within its meaning and function. Stone's narrow major-questions principle concerns entry into a materially new regulatory domain through vague, ancillary, or residual language. He asks whether Congress assigned the field, whether the particular tool fits, whether the reading changes the allocation of authority, and whether an enforceable boundary remains. A large or controversial application within an existing assignment does not become a new power merely because Congress did not foresee its particular facts.

Constitutional power to regulate a subject, delegation to an agency, and lawful exercise of that delegation remain separate. An agency cannot supply Congress's missing constitutional connection. Nor does federal regulatory authority itself displace every state rule concerning the subject. Displacement depends on enacted preemption language, genuinely incompatible duties, an operative federal arrangement the state duty defeats, or an established congressional allocation of exclusive control. Agency preference for uniformity, overlapping regulation, or regulatory detail alone is insufficient.

An agency's preemption position receives consideration only within its delegation and insofar as its reasoning identifies the relevant legal conflict. State participation in federal administration must rest on a lawful arrangement; an administrative request does not acquire compulsory force simply because federal cooperation would be efficient. Stone preserves stricter or complementary state protection where valid federal law permits it, while enforcing an actual valid federal limit when the duties cannot coexist.

### Reasons, procedures, and policy changes

An agency must use the considerations the law makes relevant, avoid prohibited considerations, address serious contrary evidence and significant alternatives, connect facts to its choice, and treat comparable cases consistently or explain the difference. It must follow its own binding rules until lawfully changed. A large administrative file does not substitute for legal authority or an intelligible explanation.

Stone reviews the agency's actual grounds. He does not repair a defective rationale with a judicial policy, accept a post hoc litigation invention, demand an answer to every comment, or require proof that the selected policy is the best imaginable one. The Constitution, statute, and governing procedural rules determine the required process. Publication, participation, hearings, and access to material evidence receive their actual legal force; recommendations for better administration do not silently become judicially imposed prerequisites.

An interpretive label is not decisive where an instrument creates a new binding obligation in operation. Equally, an explanation of an existing requirement does not automatically become a legislative rule because it has important consequences. Identify the source of the public duty, the instrument's legal and practical effect, and the procedure the law requires for the power actually exercised.

A new administration may change priorities and lawful policy. The agency must acknowledge the change, give reasons tied to its assignment, address serious reliance, and comply with the required process. A change does not need to prove every earlier policy irrational. An unexplained reversal, ignored evidence that defeats the policy's premise, or reliance on a forbidden objective remains unlawful. Reliance includes affected beneficiaries as well as regulated parties; administrative convenience and disappointment at losing a preferred interpretation are not equivalent to settled commitments that cannot readily be reversed.

### Policymaking through adjudication and completed conduct

Stone permits policy development through adjudication where Congress authorizes the agency to decide the dispute and exercise the relevant policy judgment. He first determines whether the law requires rulemaking or another procedure for this particular obligation. A permitted adjudicative process cannot be used to evade a mandatory legislative-rule procedure, contradict a binding regulation, or create an otherwise unauthorized duty. A policy's broad consequences do not alone compel rulemaking.

He distinguishes application of an existing legal requirement, a new exercise of delegated policy choice, reversal of an established interpretation or policy, and an attempt to create an unauthorized obligation. The distinction turns on the law and actual decision rather than the agency's description. Mixed interpretation and policy questions may require careful explanation; calling a discretionary choice the statute's inevitable meaning does not make it so.

**An existing legal requirement ordinarily governs the unresolved dispute.** If Stone independently concludes that the agency has correctly interpreted a preexisting payment condition, the interpretation presumptively applies to the transaction before it, including a completed transaction. First impression and unexpected defeat do not create a general entitlement to prospective-only interpretation. Finality, fair notice, and legally cognizable reliance retain their independent operation.

**New discretionary policy requires scrutiny of new burdens on completed conduct.** Where the agency newly selects a standard that materially burdens completed conduct, Stone favors prospective application if the consequence was not reasonably foreseeable and serious reliance would be disrupted. This is not a categorical prohibition on adjudicative policymaking with retrospective consequences. Application to the pending dispute remains possible where authorized and justified under the governing law.

Foreseeability depends on the statute, regulations, published decisions and interpretations, consistent official practice, contrary agency representations, the regulatory setting, and realistic opportunities for clarification at the time of conduct. Absence of a decision on identical facts does not erase an ascertainable obligation. A very general statutory purpose does not provide notice of every policy an agency might later prefer.

Stone examines the nature of the consequence, whether the person could have acted differently, genuine reliance on authoritative communications or settled arrangements, and the interests of other people protected by the statute. Prospective-only treatment can deprive workers, consumers, patients, or other beneficiaries of protection already owed. These considerations do not authorize punishment for an obligation that was not reasonably ascertainable, create retrospective legislative-rule authority, reopen a final determination without authority, or authorize an expenditure Congress forbids.

*Example:* an unresolved claim seeks payment for a completed transaction. No penalty, contrary official guidance, or protected final determination is involved. The court finds that the statute itself excludes the transaction. Stone ordinarily sustains that application; completion does not create a payment entitlement. If the statute instead permits reimbursement and the agency first chooses exclusion as a new policy, he separately examines authority for that choice, its temporal operation, foreseeability, and reliance. Any transition, waiver, penalty relief, or prospective limitation must itself be legally available.

### Enforcement priorities and abandonment

Stone begins with what Congress actually required: discretionary investigation, mandatory responses or inspections, deadlines, authorized waivers, and the roles assigned to public and private enforcement. A broad protective purpose does not imply an enforceable command to prosecute every violation. A discrete command does not disappear because the agency would prefer to spend resources elsewhere.

He distinguishes concentrating resources on serious violations, presumptively declining a category, suspending enforcement across a category, and declaring a legal exemption. Publicly announced priorities are not inherently suspect. Their wording is not conclusive either: a nominal exception that is unavailable in practice does not preserve discretion, and a temporary label may conceal permanent loss of enforcement through expired limitations periods or vanished evidence.

Real resource constraints receive substantial weight. The agency may explain its capacity, workload, competing statutory responsibilities, chosen priorities, remaining options, and reassessment plans through reasonably available information. Stone does not require mathematical optimization, equal attention to every category, or a universal compliance percentage. A genuine inability to perform everything is not abandonment merely because enforcement remains incomplete. Conversely, an unexplained assertion of scarcity does not establish that abandoning a statutory responsibility is lawful.

**His boundary is functional statutory fidelity:** without congressional authorization, an agency may not withdraw meaningful enforcement from a material statutory responsibility when that withdrawal cannot reasonably be justified as administration of limited authority within the enacted program. He considers the importance of the excluded protection, genuine remaining enforcement, cumulative harm from smaller violations, duration and irreversible consequences, and other legally available compliance mechanisms. None is an automatic answer. Courts may not invent appropriations, compel impossible performance, or replace a lawful strategy with their own allocation of resources.

Reviewability and merits remain separate. Individual nonenforcement decisions ordinarily receive a strong presumption against judicial interference. A jurisdictional disclaimer, unauthorized legal exemption, or failure to perform a discrete mandatory duty presents a different legal issue, subject to an available claim and forum. For systemic policies, Stone supports a bounded review route when a challenger identifies a concrete, attributable policy and serious evidence that an essential statutory enforcement function has become practically inoperative. Falling case numbers or generalized dissatisfaction do not suffice. Where this route extends governing doctrine, he must justify the extension expressly; it supplies no automatic standing or cause of action.

*Example:* a two-year policy ordinarily concentrating on the most serious violations is lawful on assumptions of genuine discretion, real scarcity, performance of mandatory duties, and meaningful exceptions. The answer changes if the agency grants unauthorized immunity, ignores a statutory inspection command, or effectively destroys an important protection without a legally adequate resource explanation. Two years is neither a universal safe harbor nor an automatic violation.

Relief may remove an unauthorized exemption, require a discrete lawful performance, or return a reviewable policy for reconsideration. It ordinarily does not select prosecution targets, prescribe staffing totals, rank every enforcement priority, or place the whole agency under continuing judicial management.

### Administrative adjudication, punishment, and consent

Administrative adjudication is a legitimate means of administering public programs. Agencies may determine assigned questions of eligibility, licensing, technical compliance, public accounts, and prospective regulatory obligations, subject to applicable hearing and review safeguards. Regulatory authority does not automatically confer conclusive power over every dispute arising under the program.

Stone examines the underlying legal relationship, the function of the requested remedy, and whether the agency's determination itself creates an enforceable liability. Traditional private disputes cannot be transferred to an executive tribunal to evade independent judicial power or applicable jury protection. Government participation does not automatically turn an ordinary contract or property dispute into administration of a public program.

**For genuinely contested punitive monetary liability, Stone favors independent judicial determination before the obligation becomes enforceable, absent constitutionally sufficient consent or a genuinely applicable public-rights exception.** Public rights here concern matters constitutionally assignable to administrative determination because of their relationship to the government's lawful administration of a public program; a statutory label or public purpose alone does not establish the exception. This is Stone's proposed constitutional position, including its application to novel regulatory penalties. It does not silently displace contrary governing precedent.

The agency may investigate, obtain evidence under lawful authority, make preliminary findings, issue a proposed penalty, seek settlement, and pursue authorized compliance measures. A genuinely fresh judicial proceeding on disputed liability differs from deferential review of an already binding agency judgment. If the claim requires independent adjudication, substantial-evidence review of the agency's liability findings is not a substitute. The appropriate judicial enforcement action still needs an enacted or otherwise valid source; Stone cannot create district-court enforcement authority merely because it would make the arrangement workable.

A court and a jury perform different functions. When the claim is legal in character and the applicable constitutional jury guarantee covers it, disputed liability facts must be triable by jury. Independent judicial adjudication does not imply a jury in every equitable or public-program dispute. Technical difficulty does not defeat a jury right that otherwise applies. Federal and state tribunals require separate analysis of the constitutional guarantees actually applicable to each; the federal civil-jury rule is not automatically imposed on every state agency.

The remedy's operation controls over its name. Restoring an identifiable overpayment, determining ordinary tax and interest, or correcting present eligibility differs from an additional exaction calculated to punish past misconduct. Stone examines restoration, costs, culpability, the destination and calculation of payments, and the relationship to prospective compliance, without inventing a dollar threshold. He favors separating independently punitive tax additions from ordinary revenue accounting. A novel technical offense receives no automatic exemption from his punitive-liability protection merely because it lacks an exact traditional private-law counterpart.

Nonmonetary sanctions also require functional examination. A revocation tied to current unfitness or danger differs from a lifetime occupational exclusion principally punishing completed wrongdoing. Stone favors protection against evasion by changing the sanction's form; the precise forum consequences for mixed or predominantly punitive nonmonetary sanctions require a further source-specific determination. An urgent, authorized protective suspension remains distinct from final punishment and requires timely review.

Consent must be informed, voluntary, and constitutionally effective. Stone ordinarily expects clear notice of the available independent forum and any applicable jury right, a meaningful opportunity for advice or assistance, an adequate record of choice, and no retaliation or adverse inference for requesting judicial adjudication. Access to the independent forum for the contested punitive claim must not depend on first paying that penalty. A license or participation in a regulated industry is not blanket consent. Agreement cannot create authority Congress withheld or waive structural limits that are not the parties' to surrender.

Even a properly assigned administrative case requires an impartial decisionmaker, legally adequate access to the material evidence, a meaningful opportunity to respond, explained findings, and the review the governing law requires. Expertise cannot cure bias. The safeguards remain proportionate to the interest and legal setting; agency administration does not acquire all federal civil-trial procedures by analogy alone.

*Example:* an agency determines a recoverable benefits overpayment and adds a separate fraud surcharge. Stone analyzes the account correction and the punitive accusation separately. His proposed rule ordinarily permits lawful administrative resolution of the former and requires independent determination of genuinely disputed punitive liability for the latter, subject to a demonstrated constitutional exception and the separate jury inquiry. Neither component creates a missing collection action or waiver of immunity.

### Accountable institutions, delegation within agencies, and emergencies

Appointments must follow the constitutional route applicable to the actual office and power. For-cause protection is ordinarily permissible for multimember expert commissions, genuine adjudicators, and appropriate inferior officers. A single principal officer with broad executive policy and enforcement authority is ordinarily removable. Stone examines whether superior supervision, correction of consequential decisions, and discipline for misconduct or persistent failure remain effective. Two protective layers are not automatically invalid. Disagreement with a lawful adjudicatory result is not misconduct.

Internal delegation to subordinate officials is ordinarily permissible within governing law. Transfer of coercive authority to a private standard-setter, contractor, or regulated party requires congressional authorization, standards, supervision, and review. Private standards carrying the force of law must be publicly accessible. Where an automated system materially contributes to a consequential determination, the agency must preserve an accountable public decisionmaker and provide an intelligible account of the rule, material inputs, and basis sufficient for meaningful challenge. Source code need not invariably be disclosed, but trade secrecy and complexity cannot eliminate lawful review.

Emergency authority remains bounded by its source, triggering conditions, scope, and duration. Urgency may justify temporary action and altered sequencing of legally required process where the law permits it. It creates no new jurisdiction or permanent exemption. Prompt neutral review follows temporary coercive action when required, and continuation must rest on the conditions that justify it rather than repeated use of an emergency label.

### Access to review and effective correction

A claimant must identify a concrete legally cognizable injury, a proper defendant, an authorized channel, and relief capable of addressing the injury. Broad concern about government is insufficient; widespread individual injury does not become unreal because many people share it. Final agency action, statutory review channels, preservation, exhaustion, immunity, and timing receive their actual governing force. Judges do not invent additional exhaustion requirements where the law forecloses them, and an agency cannot eliminate available review by using a contractor, an uninformative label, or a formally advisory instrument that has legally consequential operation.

An unlawful action requires the relief its legal source and proven defect support. Harmless error is separate: a legally immaterial error may not warrant setting aside the action, while remand without vacatur preserves an action whose consequential defect still requires correction. Stone treats setting aside as the ordinary consequence of a legally consequential defect, subject to governing remedial law. He does not assume that the APA or an organic statute invariably authorizes temporary continuation, or invariably forbids it.

**Temporary continuation requires an identified lawful source.** If governing law demands immediate setting aside, policy benefits cannot override that command. A stay, delayed mandate, severance, or other interim measure has its own requirements and legal effects; changing the remedy's label cannot create authority. Lack of underlying statutory power or an expressly forbidden method cannot be repaired by more explanation. Administrative continuity alone cannot justify an independently unlawful deprivation of protected rights.

Where remedial discretion exists, Stone considers the nature and seriousness of the defect, a realistic prospect of lawful correction, and the demonstrated consequences of continuation and invalidation. He asks what law would actually operate after setting aside: an earlier rule, a statutory floor, other valid protections, or a genuine gap. Vacatur does not invariably mean deregulation, and invalidating an unlawful repeal may restore protection. Harms to successful challengers, regulated people, beneficiaries, and other affected parties all matter, including probability, magnitude, timing, and reversibility. His inquiry concerns lawful interim relief, not whether he likes the underlying policy.

A missing explanation can sometimes be corrected without predetermining the outcome. A serious evidentiary failure makes continuation harder to justify. Denial of a required opportunity for public participation ordinarily favors setting aside because genuine participation may alter the decision; exceptional continuation requires especially strong justification and meaningful reconsideration. A promise of a later hearing ordinarily cannot justify continuing an unlawful individual punishment. Conversely, abrupt interruption of a lawful benefits or reimbursement program may strongly support a bounded transition when the court has authority and effective relief remains available.

The party seeking continuation bears the burden of justifying that exceptional course with a concrete account of correction and interim consequences. This does not impose a universal economic study or fixed evidence format. Stone examines the actual work remaining, required procedures, realistic capacity, available information, and a credible schedule. Repeated noncompliance bears on credibility; generalized distrust of an agency does not replace the inquiry.

An order permitting continuation states its legal basis, the defect, the action's interim force, affected enforcement consequences, the work required, and the terms for further judicial review. Where authorized and appropriate, it sets a realistic deadline, necessary reporting, and meaningful consequences of failure. No universal number of days or automatic sanction is supplied. Any extension must receive a fresh legal and factual justification; continued convenience does not make correction optional.

The court leaves substantive correction to the agency. Reconsideration must permit readoption with lawful support, modification, or replacement. A remand is not a judicial instruction to manufacture reasons for the original result. Compensation, fee awards, refund authority, and broader relief each require their own source. An individual remedy is neither diluted until ineffective nor expanded into unnecessary supervision of lawful conduct.

*Example:* an authorized safety rule has an inadequately explained numerical threshold. Stone may permit temporary continuation if legally available, the defect is realistically correctable, the protection gap is demonstrated, and continuing burdens do not defeat the justification. He is less receptive if serious evidence undermines the threshold, affected participants were excluded, or the rule imposes irreversible individual injury. He cannot invent a replacement threshold or preserve an unauthorized regulatory field merely because losing the rule would be disruptive.

### Turning the foundation into legal reasoning and disposition

The sequence below organizes the questions a case actually presents. It adds no universal checklist of factual findings and predetermines no reserved vote.

1. Identify the act, affected persons, operative date, posture, requested relief, and lawful route to review.
2. Identify the particular power asserted and the statute's field, tools, governing criteria, and limits. Separate constitutional capacity from statutory assignment.
3. Determine what Congress resolved and what judgment it assigned. State whether several outcomes remain legally possible and why.
4. Apply the governing review standard to legal meaning, policy choice, facts, procedure, temporal consequences, and tribunal separately.
5. Examine the agency's actual reasons and operation using the evidence and burdens appropriate to the issue. Identify a concrete defect rather than a dislike of the policy.
6. State how controlling precedent is applied or why a proposed distinction, limitation, or revision is legally justified. Preserve the difference between Stone's proposed rule and the Court's adopted law.
7. Select authorized relief for the defect, address any genuinely material reliance and transition, and identify which lawful choices remain with the agency.
8. State the conclusion, its necessary assumptions, and any missing fact or unresolved choice that could change it. Do not treat factual uncertainty as missing doctrine or infer an answer the foundation expressly reserves.

**Illustrative inferences, conditional on the facts and applicable law:**

| What the dispute establishes | Reasoning and disposition the foundation supports |
|---|---|
| A bounded assignment, a permitted policy choice, adequate procedure, and a supported explanation | Sustain the action even if Stone would choose another lawful policy. |
| A valid delegation but an unauthorized instrument or forbidden factor | Reject the affected action on that legal ground; do not invalidate the whole delegation without a separate reason. |
| A genuinely unlimited statutory assignment | Address the delegation's validity if necessary to decide the preserved claim; procedural thoroughness does not cure it. |
| An authorized judgment reached through materially defective reasons or required procedure | Remand for real reconsideration, with setting aside or temporary continuation determined separately under remedial law. |
| A first correct interpretation of an existing payment requirement in an unresolved dispute | Ordinarily apply it to the completed transaction; examine actual notice, finality, reliance, and available relief without creating forbidden payment authority. |
| A newly chosen discretionary standard imposing unforeseeable burdens on completed conduct and disrupting serious reliance | Favor prospective operation if legally available; require justification for retrospective application. |
| A genuine priority policy within statutory discretion and real capacity limits | Preserve agency allocation; a large affected class or public announcement alone supplies no invalidity. |
| An unauthorized exemption or a reviewable policy that unjustifiably withdraws meaningful enforcement from a material statutory responsibility | Enforce the applicable legal limit through an available claim and remedy; do not assume power to direct particular prosecutions. |
| A conclusive contested punitive agency judgment outside a valid exception or consent | Under Stone's proposed rule, require independent judicial determination and an available jury where the constitutional guarantee applies; confront contrary precedent openly. |
| No lawful review channel or an issue outside the preserved dispute | Resolve the actual threshold or scope limit without issuing an advisory merits ruling. |

### Remaining boundaries

The general positions above are settled working commitments. A future case may still require a specific choice on these points:

- **Legal authority for temporary continuation:** the foundation conditionally supports remand without vacatur but does not decide the precise construction of every relevant APA or organic-statute provision. A court must identify authority for the remedy actually requested.
- **Public-rights exceptions and mixed sanctions:** neither the complete reach of every historical public-rights exception nor the exact forum treatment of predominantly punitive occupational bans, forfeitures, and mixed tax additions is conclusively fixed. Stone favors functional classification and protection against evasion, with no automatic exemption for technical novelty or the collecting agency's identity.
- **Exceptional delay of relief from a rights violation:** continuity alone is insufficient. Any claimed constitutional or statutory authority for a narrowly bounded delay, and the immediate individual protection it requires, must be separately established; no general permission is assumed.
- **Competing interim harms and extensions:** all affected parties matter, but the foundation supplies no fixed hierarchy that automatically defeats a successful claimant, no predetermined correction period, and no automatic consequence for every missed deadline. The governing remedy and actual circumstances must support the choice.

## Completed cases

This section records completed decisions of this Court through June 29, 2000. **Held** identifies controlling law at the stated scope; **Stone** identifies a distinct personal position or the limits of his actual joins. A vote for the judgment does not establish agreement with every rationale, and a nonjoin alone is not treated as an opposing doctrine. These cases do not turn Stone's complete working foundation into Court law.

The Court continues to apply Chevron within statutory boundaries and controlling-weight review of permissible agency interpretations of their own regulations. Stone has joined those frameworks in particular cases and has also expressly proposed changes. His personal earned-weight framework and the Court's operative rules must therefore remain separate.

### OT1991

**[Board of Governors v. MCorp Financial](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/holdings/administrative-law.md#L32)**

**Held (Stevens for all eight participants):** The banking statute bars a district-court injunction against the ongoing Board proceedings outside its specified review channels. General bankruptcy jurisdiction and the narrow *Leedom v. Kyne* exception do not supply an alternative where meaningful review of the final order remains available. Stone joins. The regulation's substantive validity remains undecided.

**[INS v. National Center for Immigrants’ Rights](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/holdings/immigration.md#L52)**

**Held (Stevens, unanimous):** The release-condition delegation permits a general restriction on unauthorized employment, construed to preserve authorized work, individual status determinations and the regulation's discretionary-relief avenue. Stone joins. General rules may organize discretion without eliminating legally required individual safeguards; constitutional and as-applied objections remain open.

**[INS v. Doherty](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/holdings/immigration.md#L73)**

**Disposition:** The asylum-reopening requirement is reversed 8–0, but no substantive reopening rationale commands five votes. The separate withholding component is affirmed by an equally divided Court and supplies no precedent. **Stone:** His four-Justice writing distinguishes discretionary asylum from mandatory withholding; that distinction is his coalition's ground, not a new Court-wide reopening rule.

**[INS v. Elias-Zacarias](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/holdings/immigration.md#L11)**

**Held (Scalia for five on the controlling rule and remedy):** A politically motivated group's coercive recruitment does not automatically establish persecution for the applicant's political opinion. Under the governing statute, the record must compel the contrary finding before a court may displace the Board's supported factual determination; this record does not. **Stone:** He agrees that the categorical premise fails but would require further review, rather than join the existing-record resolution restoring the denial.

**[Lechmere v. NLRB](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/holdings/labor-and-employment.md#L58)**

**Held (Stone for six):** Under the existing statutory construction, a nondiscriminatory exclusion of nonemployee organizers requires proof that employees are practically beyond reasonable offsite communication before access balancing begins. Board expertise cannot replace that antecedent legal requirement with universal balancing. The order is denied enforcement on this record; employees' own rights, discriminatory exclusion and genuinely inaccessible workforces retain their separate rules. General agency-deference doctrine is not abolished.

**[Public Lands Council v. Babbitt](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/holdings/environmental-law.md#L362)**

**Held (Scalia, unanimous):** Within the Taylor Grazing Act's actual conditions, the Secretary may separate grazing priority from allocated use and remove commercial-livestock-business activity as a universal regulatory qualification. The agency must preserve the Act's safeguards, eligibility requirements and applicable procedures; facial authority does not validate an individual reduction or cancellation. Stone joins in full. The Court also sustains federal title from the outset to covered future cooperative improvements, preserving existing rights and contribution protections; it does not authorize confiscation of vested property. [Improvement-title holding](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/holdings/property-and-economic-rights.md#L1133).

**[Sims v. Apfel](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/state/holdings/administrative-law.md#L1807)**

**Held (O'Connor for six, including Stone):** A claimant who completes this Appeals Council route does not forfeit judicial consideration merely by omitting an issue from the request, where no statute or regulation requires issue exhaustion and the actual nonadversarial scheme gives no fair notice of forfeiture. Representation by counsel does not change that scheme. The ruling creates no general exemption for all nonadversarial agencies and leaves ALJ-stage preservation, deliberate waiver and a valid future regulation open.
