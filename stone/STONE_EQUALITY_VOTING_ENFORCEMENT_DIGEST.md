This file extracts Stone's equal protection, Fourteenth and Fifteenth Amendment enforcement, state immunity, private enforcement and Voting Rights Act positions from `STONE_FULL_BUILD_THROUGH_OT2001.md`, checks each against the law the Court actually made through June 29, 2000, and lists the questions Stone still has to answer. ## 1. Bottom line

Stone banked more equality law than the build's own status notes record. His common substantial-justification standard is majority law (Adarand, five Justices) and has been applied to university admissions (Hopwood), contracting (Engineering Contractors), promotions (Dallas Fire Fighters) and K-12 integration (Eisenberg, which he wrote). The territorial-districting line (Shaw, Miller, Vera merits, Hunt v. Cromartie) holds that predominance alone is neither a violation nor a scrutiny trigger. The VRA line is strong: §2 reaches governing-body size, judicial elections, crossover districts and replacement of an existing election; §5 purpose reaches nonretrogressive dilutive purpose.

He lost on sexual orientation (rational basis governs; his intermediate-scrutiny position is a stated minority view), on the Vera standing cutback, on Lawyer, on the Miller v. Albright remedy, and on Ayers.

The largest problem is internal. On state immunity, the build and the master jurisprudence both say Article I ordinarily cannot authorize private suits against States, but Stone voted the other way in Seminole Tribe, College Savings, Florida Prepaid, Alden and Kimel, and those votes made Article I abrogation controlling law at 5-4. On Title VI, the build would affirm a private disparate-impact action in Sandoval, but Stone joined the five-Justice Powell v. Ridge majority rejecting exactly that. On sexual orientation, disability and territorial standing, the master file, the build and his votes point in different directions. Those conflicts have to be resolved before the OT2000 briefs are written, because Garrett, Sandoval, Sinkfield, Sterling and Nguyen each force one of them.

## 2. Stone's doctrine as the build states it

This section extracts the equal protection, Fourteenth Amendment, Fifteenth Amendment and Voting Rights Act positions from `stone/STONE_FULL_BUILD_THROUGH_OT2001.md`, stripped of case narration. Each item is Stone's position, not law; section 3 says which items became law.

### 2.1 Method common to every case

Stone asks four questions in order: what legal right is asserted; what conduct is attributable to this defendant; what treatment or barrier injured this claimant; what remedy the governing law authorizes. A statutory violation is not automatically a constitutional one, constitutional authority does not itself create a private action, and a merits violation does not itself create a remedy. Evidence is not converted into elements: shape, predominance, disparity, demographic data and racial awareness are proof for a claim whose elements make them relevant, never the claim itself.

### 2.2 Governmental racial treatment

The threshold question is whether government actually treats a person differently because of race, national origin, ethnicity operating as race or caste, or a deliberate racial proxy. Awareness, data, disparate effects and attention to racial conditions are not that treatment.

Once actual differential racial treatment and the claim's injury are established, government must show an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and the objective, and must justify the measure's coverage and actual burdens, including a response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. No exhaustion of every alternative, commissioned study, statistical threshold, scientific certainty or universal sunset is required.

Permissible objectives: supported correction of identified discrimination, segregation or exclusion and its persisting effects; prevention of recurrence; overcoming documented barriers to market participation; and genuinely distinct prospective objectives (meaningful integration, demonstrated institutional access or educational benefit) that belong to the institution's lawful function. Impermissible: racial hierarchy, stereotype, hostility, caste maintenance, political favoritism, racial arithmetic for its own sake. A prior judgment against the institution and proof that each beneficiary was personally a victim are not prerequisites. The same obligation binds federal, state and local government; their different powers and evidence affect what objectives they can support, not the grade of equal citizenship.

Precedent treatment in the build: reject Metro Broadcasting's federal benign tier; displace Croson's categorical strict scrutiny while keeping its evidence demand; keep Wygant (actual burdens), Paradise (flexibility, duration, alternatives, third parties) and Fullilove (supported congressional remedial record). The build's Stevens fallback (affirm under inherited federal precedent if the common standard failed to win) is now moot; see section 3.

Same-decision defense (Lesage): a forward-looking equal-process claimant need not prove entitlement to the benefit, but a retrospective claim tied to a discrete denial fails when government conclusively proves the same lawful outcome.

### 2.3 Territorial districting and equal political membership

Predominant racial consideration in drawing a common territorial constituency, even with traditional criteria subordinated, does not by itself deny equal protection or trigger strict scrutiny. The constitutional injuries are racial exclusion, intentional vote dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic status, and other independently cognizable deprivations (the list is not exhaustive). Gomillion and Anderson v. Martin show that a status injury can be complete without electoral loss. Bizarre shape is not a gateway. A State may voluntarily seek to improve minority electoral opportunity even where the VRA does not compel the district; absence of a federal duty is not a constitutional prohibition, and voluntary inclusion creates no right to proportional seats or duty to maximize.

On standing, the build proposed (Vera) that residence in a race-conscious district, without dilution, exclusion or other personal inequality, is not Article III injury. Chen: the design-only theory is foreclosed unless an independent injury is pleaded; total-population apportionment is valid because representatives serve residents.

### 2.4 Voting Rights Act

Three propositions stay separate: what a statute requires when its elements are proved; what Congress may authorize as preventive implementation within a valid category; and what a State may voluntarily do that federal law does not demand. Failure to prove §2 required a district does not make it unconstitutional, and "VRA compliance" does not defend whatever map a legislature or DOJ prefers.

Section 2 is a results statute; a claimant need not prove constitutional intent. It guarantees neither proportional representation, racial officeholding, nor a party's success. The minority group's candidate of choice is identified by voter preference, not the candidate's race. Proportionality is relevant evidence, not a safe harbor or a maximization demand (De Grandy). A dilution comparator must have concrete roots in state law and practice, not be chosen because it produces the desired result (Holder). Judicial elections are covered and a State's linkage interest is weighed, not dispositive (SCLC). The first Gingles condition can be met by a functional ability-to-elect district below fifty percent minority CVAP when dependable crossover support is proved (Valdespino); mere influence is not enough. Withdrawal of an existing election can be challenged under §2 when done discriminatorily, but there is no right to an elected body or to vote for another jurisdiction's official (Mixon).

Section 5: coverage attaches to the covered jurisdiction's implementation of a change even when a noncovered State compels it (Lopez II); discretionary changes made while implementing the NVRA need preclearance (Young); courts cannot give an unprecleared change an implementation route (Lopez I); party conventions exercising a state-delegated nominating role are covered, and §10 supports a private action (Morse). The effect prong is retrogression only and is not a second §2 trial, but evidence of a rejected nondilutive alternative bears on purpose (Bossier I); the purpose prong reaches discriminatory but nonretrogressive purpose (Bossier II). Preclearance does not immunize a change from other challenges, and §5 gives DOJ no power to demand maximization.

### 2.5 Fifteenth Amendment franchise

A State election cannot exclude qualified citizens through ancestry used as a racial proxy, and remedial purposes of the office do not justify a racial electorate (Rice). Benefit programs and genuine tribal elections are reserved.

### 2.6 Sex

Sex classifications keep their own intermediate scrutiny with the exceedingly persuasive justification requirement (J.E.B., VMI). Population-level assumptions cannot be assigned to individuals. A separate program must deliver substantially equal resources, opportunity and prestige (VMI). Sex-separated institutions do not make comparison impossible; service-specific comparison applies (Klinger). Title IX's three athletics routes are alternatives, and current participation does not prove interest where opportunity was restricted (Cohen). A sex-remedial preference needs its own sex-specific predicate (Dallas). Citizenship-transmission rules that impose different conditions on unmarried fathers rest on sex stereotypes and fail heightened scrutiny; the remedy is severance under the INA's severability clause and a declaration of citizenship, not equitable naturalization (Miller v. Albright, Nguyen). Federal sex discrimination in employment supports a Davis v. Passman damages action, without absolute judicial immunity for administrative hiring (Duffy).

### 2.7 Sexual orientation

Romer can be decided on the inadequacy of the justification without strict scrutiny. For military cases the build proposed intermediate scrutiny with substantial respect for demonstrated military needs (Walmer), and that an identity statement cannot itself be proof of prohibited conduct or propensity, nor protected speech the disqualifying fact (Thomasson, Richenberg). Bill of Attainder claims fail.

### 2.8 Other equality and access positions

M.L.B.: a State cannot condition appeal from termination of parental rights on payment for the record; no general right to free civil litigation. Saenz: durational discrimination against new residents violates the Fourteenth Amendment's citizenship protection, and Congress cannot authorize it. Olech: a class-of-one claim exists for intentional, irrational unequal treatment, with ordinary proof of comparability. Campbell: a white defendant may challenge racial discrimination in grand-jury selection. Wisconsin v. City of New York, Commerce v. House, Utah v. Evans: enumeration methods get reasoned discretion; the Census Act's text controls statistical sampling.

### 2.9 Education

Jenkins: remedies track the adjudicated segregation violation, salary support can be reasonably related to the remedy, and release requires good-faith compliance and elimination of vestiges to the extent practicable without national test-score parity; joint development with independent review is available but not mandatory. Fordice/Ayers: traceability, continuing effect, educational justification and practicable alternatives govern each policy; historically Black institutions are not themselves the wrong. Eisenberg/Brewer: preventing serious racial isolation and maintaining meaningful integrated K-12 education are independently important interests, not relabeled remedies, but the district must prove the connection, operation, burdens and alternatives; countywide arithmetic alone is insufficient, and a rigid racial gate can fail. Higher-education admissions are not decided by the school cases.

### 2.10 Congressional enforcement power

Courts define the constitutional guarantee. Congress may remedy violations and enact reasonably fitted preventive categories reaching some conduct that is not independently unconstitutional, when an identifiable constitutional problem, a relevant legislative record or established history, and a defensible relationship among problem, coverage and remedy are present. Congruence and proportionality test that relationship; they do not require a case file proving a violation by every covered State, and no universal sunset follows. Within a valid preventive category the claimant proves only the statutory elements. Boerne: RFRA substitutes a different substantive rule across virtually all state action and fails. Lopez II: preclearance of State-compelled changes is valid Fifteenth Amendment enforcement. Kimel: ADEA abrogation fails because age is rational-basis and the record shows no pattern justifying the breadth. Garrett: ADA Title I abrogation is valid because Congress documented exclusion and stereotype, and the qualification, reasonableness and undue-hardship limits fit the remedy, without making disability suspect. Morrison: VAWA's civil remedy against private actors fails under both powers. Florida Prepaid: patent abrogation fails §5 for want of a pattern of unconstitutional deprivation. Evidence can justify a preventive rule but cannot enact one, create a cause of action, waive immunity or replace statutory text.

### 2.11 State immunity and private enforcement

Article I ordinarily does not let Congress subject a nonconsenting State to private damages suits; valid Reconstruction enforcement legislation can abrogate with unmistakable language; waiver must be actual and specific, so market participation is not constructive consent (College Savings) but acceptance of a clear funding condition is (Bradley); removal waives immunity for claims the State already consented to in its own courts (Lapides). Officer suits, public enforcement by the United States, private causes of action, immunity and damages are separate questions. Sandoval: the existing Title VI private action reaches violations of authorized disparate-impact regulations, while §601 itself requires intent. Gonzaga: FERPA's confidentiality provisions create a §1983-enforceable right; administrative oversight does not automatically displace §1983.

### 2.12 Bush v. Gore

Once a State chooses popular election of electors, legally cast votes must receive substantially equal treatment under a nonarbitrary, consistently applied method. The remedy for inconsistent standards is a uniform statewide count under pre-election law, not termination of the count. Article II review asks whether a state court materially replaced the legislature's pre-election rules or reassigned authority, not whether it interpreted state law correctly; on this record it did not. December 12 is a safe-harbor advantage, not a stop date; disputes left unresolved go to Congress's electoral count, and the Court declares no winner.

## 4. Where Stone's own materials disagree

These are conflicts among the build, the master jurisprudence and Stone's recorded votes. Each needs a decision, because a Section II that silently follows one source will contradict another.

1. Article I abrogation. The build (recurring reasoning, Corrections item 10, the Alden, Kimel and Garrett entries) and master Federalism ("Stone's preferred rule is that Article I ordinarily does not authorize subjecting a nonconsenting State to private suit") say no. Stone's votes say yes: Seminole Tribe (would sustain the direct suit), College Savings Pt IV (alone, Article I sustains), Florida Prepaid Pt III, Alden, Kimel. The yes votes are now controlling law. Master Federalism already anticipates the problem ("where precedent permits Article I abrogation he applies it or openly distinguishes, narrows, or seeks to overrule it under Section 16"), so the master file is not contradicted by the law, only by the preferred rule. The build is contradicted outright.

2. Sexual-orientation tier. Master Section 6 lists orientation as Elevated (intermediate scrutiny). Stone's separate writings in Walmer and Shahar propose intermediate scrutiny as an openly stated change. But he wrote Nabozny for a unanimous Court applying rational basis to orientation, joined Romer and Equality Foundation on rational basis, and joined Stevens's rationality dissents in Thomasson and Richenberg. This is coherent only under a stated practice: apply governing law when writing for the Court, press intermediate scrutiny in separate writings. That practice is nowhere written down.

3. Disability tier and the equal-protection modification duty. Master Section 6 lists disability as Elevated, and master Equal Protection states an equal-protection duty of reasonable modification for government-controlled civic institutions. The build's Garrett entry says Stone "does not make disability a suspect classification." Heller v. Doe, which Stone joined, applied rational basis and did not reach heightened scrutiny.

4. Territorial standing. Stone wrote Miller Pt I-A (nine votes: voters personally subjected to the challenged assignment have standing), then in Vera proposed narrowing it so assignment alone does not supply injury. The build's Vera entry and Corrections item 4 follow the Vera position. Master Elections says the territorial rule "is a position on the electoral claim, not a general restriction on standing," which matches Miller I-A, not his Vera writing.

5. The racial-justification structure. The Adarand holding is one common standard: important objective, substantial evidence-supported relationship, justification of coverage and burdens, and a response to concrete, practicable, comparably effective alternatives. Engineering Contractors Pt II (8 Associates) adds that race-neutral alternatives must be seriously considered. Master Section 9 is a two-route structure: public justification for prospective objectives, and a defined corrective permission whose satisfaction "resolves the corrective justification" so that a court does not "reopen the same justification through an additional impartiality balance." Its corrective conditions do not list an alternatives element, and Stone's Engineering Contractors dissent lists corrective requirements without one either. Either master Section 9 is a restatement of Adarand (and the alternatives inquiry lives inside "reasonably fitted"), or it is a proposed change to his own majority opinion, which Section 16 would require him to say.

6. Remedy in sex-based citizenship cases. The build's Miller v. Albright and Nguyen entries direct recognition of citizenship after severance. Stone's actual Miller dissent rejected directed recognition and would remand for a lawful remedy with three options.

7. Title VI private enforcement. The build would affirm the private action in Sandoval and calls the Powell position coherent with it. Stone joined Powell's holding that the §602 disparate-impact regulation supports no implied action and no §1983 right.

8. Dallas Fire Fighters. The build would vacate the racial component for application of the new standard. Stone joined the five who held the racial promotions unjustified.

9. Bush v. Gore. Master Elections requires counting standards "established by law or valid procedure, communicated before the count, and applied consistently." The build's Bush v. Gore remedy has Florida institutions adopt one statewide operational standard after Election Day, clarifying how the voter-intent rule applies to recurring ballot conditions. Whether a post-election operational standard implementing a pre-election legal rule counts as "fixed in advance" has to be answered, or the remedy contradicts the master rule.

10. Stale status notes in the build. The OT1994 note misstates Adarand. The OT1995 note stops at Chunk 7. OT1996 through OT1999 entries have no status notes and read as live proposals although decided, many differently. The Hunt and Easley deletion instructions are superseded by the actual OT1998 Hunt decision and the OT2000 inventory. Valdespino is placed in OT1999 but was decided June 17, 1999 (OT1998). The OT1998 `CONSEQUENCES.md` records no consequence line connecting Hunt to Easley.

## 5. Questions Stone has to answer

### 5.1 OT2000 matters that force an answer

Brief order follows the case list. Each entry gives the entering law, what the build proposes, and the decision required.

Sinkfield v. Kelley (Nos. 00-132, 00-133; November 27, 2000). Voters in districts adjoining the challenged majority-minority districts assert injury. Hays does not exist in this timeline. Entering law: Miller Pt I-A (personal exposure to the challenged assignment), Vera (mere residence insufficient; nonresident theory reserved), Lawyer (no general resident-standing rule). The build has no entry. Questions: (a) Is a voter assigned to an adjoining district because of the challenged district's design "personally subjected" to that assignment? (b) Does Stone answer under Miller I-A as it stands, or renew his Vera narrowing? (c) If standing exists, is any independent injury alleged, given that a design-only claim fails on the merits under Shaw, Miller, Vera and Hunt? This is Vera's reserved question; Stone's answer fixes his standing doctrine for districting.

Bush v. Palm Beach County Canvassing Board (December 4, 2000) and Bush v. Gore (December 12, 2000). No controlling law on Article II, §1, cl. 2, 3 U.S.C. §5, or equal protection in recounts. The build supplies full positions. Questions: (a) the "fixed in advance" conflict in section 4, item 9; (b) whether the equal-protection rule is limited to presidential elections or states a general recount rule; (c) whether Stone writes the Article II limiting rule (material replacement of pre-election rules or reassignment of authority) as a holding or only as the ground for rejecting this challenge; (d) what Stone does if no majority forms for dissolving the stay or for any remedy.

Brewer v. West Irondequoit (January 9, 2001). QP: whether equal protection permits a voluntary transfer program to restrict eligibility by race to reduce racial isolation, absent de jure segregation. Eisenberg Pt II (6) already answers the abstract question yes, under the common standard. Questions: (a) whether a rigid racial eligibility gate, as opposed to Eisenberg's capacity-first policy, can satisfy fit and burden; (b) whether an interdistrict voluntary program needs authority or a predicate beyond Eisenberg (Milliken limits only court-ordered interdistrict remedies, and the register says nothing about voluntary interdistrict programs); (c) the actual posture: historically the Second Circuit had already vacated the preliminary injunction, so verify which judgment is under review before adopting the build's framing; (d) whether a current state-law authority question is preserved.

Board of Trustees v. Garrett (February 21, 2001). Entering law: Article I authorizes private suits against States when the duty and remedy are within Article I and authorization is unmistakable (Florida Prepaid Pt III, Kimel); Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming support state-employment regulation; Heller gives disability rational basis; §5 framework is Boerne, College Savings Pt II, Florida Prepaid Pt II, Morrison; Kimel's sequencing allows reserving §5. The build reaches §5 on the premise that Article I is unavailable, which is wrong. Questions: (a) Does Stone decide on Article I alone, or also reach §5 as an independently sufficient holding? Keeping it means rewriting the build's immunity section and Corrections item 10 and adjusting master Federalism's preferred rule. 2. Sexual-orientation tier. Choose between (a) accepting rational basis tied to the actual disadvantage as settled and working within it, as Nabozny and the Thomasson dissent did, or (b) continuing to propose intermediate scrutiny in separate writings while applying rational basis when writing for the Court. If (b), write the practice into master Section 16 so later writings are consistent.

3. Disability. Decide whether master Section 6's Elevated listing and the equal-protection modification duty are positions Stone will press (Garrett is the obvious vehicle, and Olmstead-type and Title II cases follow) or aspirational positions he will not state against Heller.

4. Elements of the independent districting injuries. Every territorial decision preserves "racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic status" as actionable, and Hunt left their elements open. Without elements, lower courts have a rejection rule and no replacement. Stone needs at least working elements for materially unequal participation and inferior civic status: what conduct, what proof of purpose or effect, what comparison, what remedy. Easley may force this.

5. VRA compliance as a justification. Miller Pt VI is conditional only. Because predominance triggers no scrutiny, the question arises only when an independent injury is established and the State pleads VRA compliance. Master Section 9 says statutory implementation cannot authorize "intentional destruction of a protected electoral opportunity." Stone should decide whether compliance can ever justify an established independent injury or only defeats the inference that one occurred.

6. Standing in districting. Settle Miller I-A against the Vera narrowing (section 4, item 4) before Sinkfield.

7. The alternatives element in corrective permission. Reconcile master Section 9 with the Adarand holding and Engineering Contractors Pt II (section 4, item 5).

8. Higher-education diversity. Hopwood Pt II (6) says a supported diversity interest can qualify; Kennedy reserves. Master Section 9 says "neither invocation of diversity nor a generalized aspiration to improve society supplies the required connection." Stone needs the concrete-benefit-and-mechanism test stated before an admissions program reaches the Court.

9. Fifteenth Amendment enforcement. Whether Boerne's fit test governs Fifteenth Amendment §2 legislation or Katzenbach and City of Rome rationality still control is unresolved; Stone's only statement is his solo Lopez I concurrence. Also unresolved: whether the Fifteenth Amendment reaches vote dilution (Voinovich).

10. Open §2 and §5 questions likely to recur: influence-only claims; coalition claims combining distinct minority groups (master Elections permits them on proof of durable cohesion against common bloc opposition, but no case holds it); the §2 population denominator and citizenship; the §5 same-decision defense (Stone alone favored it in Bossier II); elective-to-appointive replacement under §5 (Presley, where Stone dissented); bailout.
