## 0. Foundation for future cases: religion claims

**Personal foundation.** This is Stone's prospective doctrine for religion claims. It consolidates his recorded positions and the refinements developed for future cases. It does not convert his separate writings into holdings of the Court, alter completed votes, or decide an unpresented case. Sections 1–3 retain the completed record and the Court's existing law. Section 4 identifies remaining questions against that record; a proposed answer for Stone is not thereby a holding of the Court.

The rules below state his position. The applications expressly labeled **inferences** explain how those rules would likely operate on the stated facts. A case-specific commitment, a different material fact, or an unresolved question of statutory authority may change the disposition.

**Definition.** Stone protects religious exercise and serious nonreligious conscience within a secular government. Government may not conduct worship, prescribe religious truth, finance religious formation as an institutional undertaking, or give religious authority control over civil rights. It must protect independent religious expression, genuine religious self-government, and equal civic access. Public support turns on what the government actually purchases or provides, not the recipient's religious identity, an intermediary's involvement, or the mere possibility of institutional savings. Accommodation protects conscience without making other people surrender their legal entitlements or bear serious involuntary burdens.

### 0.1 Belief, conscience, and ordinary civil obligations

Belief, disbelief, changes of faith, and sincere non-theistic conviction receive protection. Courts may examine sincerity and the actual conduct required by law; they may not decide whether a belief is true, sensible, orthodox, central to an established faith, or shared by a sufficiently large denomination. A recently adopted or unfamiliar belief is not disqualified on that account.

Neutral, generally applicable civil laws ordinarily govern religiously motivated conduct. Sincerity alone supplies no general constitutional exemption. The inquiry changes when government targets religion in its text, purpose, design, or enforcement; treats comparable secular conduct more favorably; administers discretionary exemptions against religion; or acts with religious hostility. Comparability concerns the harm or operational interest the government invokes, not whether the activities look alike in every respect. A government cannot demand restraint from religious conduct while tolerating comparable secular sources of the same harm.

Where the governing constitutional rule requires heightened justification, government must justify the particular restriction and its fit. Where a statute supplies additional protection, that statute receives its own analysis. Neither an Establishment Clause objection nor a Free Exercise claim disposes of the other by label.

### 0.2 Government worship, religious identity, and civil authority

Government may not make prayer, devotional observance, religious profession, or an invitation to conversion part of its own secular program. The rule reaches classrooms, graduations, sporting events, public workplaces, civic ceremonies, and legislative proceedings. An adult audience, nonsectarian wording, tradition, a disclaimer, or permission to leave does not turn official worship into private exercise. A student vote or private delivery does not cure worship selected and organized by the institution.

Private observance remains protected. A public employee's position does not make every personal prayer governmental; conversely, calling a supervisor's or teacher's organized exercise “personal” does not settle its character. Control of the occasion, official duties, use of institutional authority, recruitment of participants, and practical pressure matter. A genuine period of silence that directs no religious observance is permissible.

Government may teach about religion and preserve religious history. It may not adopt a faith's propositions as governmental truth or use a faith's symbol as the present badge of civil authority. A historical exhibit, a sacred work studied as art, and an official devotional display require different treatment. Age alone does not validate present governmental religious identification. A nominal transfer of a monument to private ownership is insufficient if government continues the same religious use.

Genuinely private displays in an actual public forum are assessed by sponsorship, selection, control, and context. Religious content and proximity to a seat of government do not alone make a private display governmental. Officials may use accurate identification of private sponsorship; a disclaimer cannot contradict the program's actual operation.

Religious bodies may govern their religious affairs, but government may not give a denomination civil veto power, regulatory authority over outsiders, or a specially constituted governmental jurisdiction because of its religion. Accommodation does not justify that delegation.

### 0.3 Schools, religious instruction, and private expression

Public education may study religion, religious history, literature, and sacred music for genuine educational purposes. The relevant inquiry is what the lesson or performance does, judged against the educational discipline and the actual setting. An academic or artistic label cannot conceal worship or teaching doctrine as truth.

Exposure to an idea that conflicts with a family's beliefs is not by itself compelled religious exercise. Stone distinguishes learning about a belief from being required to affirm it, perform a religious act, or violate a protected practice. Parents have no general religious veto over legitimate public instruction. Claims involving actual profession, observance, penalties, or a particular statutory accommodation receive separate examination.

Students retain private religious expression on the terms applicable to comparable student expression. Teachers may enforce genuine assignment requirements but may not reject an otherwise eligible contribution merely because its perspective is religious. In after-school activities, the children's age, timing, sponsorship, supervision, and ability to decline help determine whether an activity is genuinely private or institutionally organized.

Government may define a forum's genuine subjects and activities. Within the forum actually opened, it may not exclude a religious treatment of an admitted subject. A meeting does not lose protection merely because it advocates conversion. Conversely, a program limited to specified activities need not become a general venue for every institutional religious service. Any exclusion must rest on a real, consistently applied activity boundary rather than a religious label.

### 0.4 Public money, fungibility, and separable secular work

**The central question is what public resources purchase.** Public payment for worship, devotional teaching, clergy formation, religious conversion as an institutional undertaking, or inseparably devotional education is prohibited, subject to the specifically defined access and expressive-forum rules below. Neutral eligibility and private selection do not authorize an expenditure that actually purchases those activities.

**Fungibility is relevant evidence, not an automatic prohibition.** A religious recipient's ability to save private money, benefit economically, control an item, or conceivably divert aid does not establish unconstitutional financing. Otherwise meals, transport, disability services, and ordinary civic infrastructure would become suspect whenever they benefited religious people. The defect must be connected to what the program funds, replaces, or actually supports.

The distinction is between an independently identifiable secular service that incidentally benefits an institution and support for an integrated religious operation whose devotional work is part of the publicly financed product. An accounting label cannot divide an inseparable activity. Conversely, a secular service does not become religious simply because the institution uses its own remaining resources for religion.

Stone applies five inquiries:

1. **Identify the expenditure.** Determine whether government is buying a service, supporting independent expression, supplying individual access, or underwriting institutional operations.
2. **Identify the funded function.** Establish the actual content, personnel duties, recipients, delivery, and uses of facilities or equipment.
3. **Require meaningful separation.** The secular work must be distinguishable in operation and accounts, with controls proportionate to the risk of diversion.
4. **Protect eligible recipients.** Receiving the publicly funded secular service cannot depend on worship, profession of faith, or surrender of an otherwise applicable legal entitlement.
5. **Verify compliance.** Objective records and ordinary professional oversight must identify public costs and uses without requiring officials to resolve theology.

Separate governance or wholly separate personnel can provide strong safeguards but are not invariably necessary. What matters is whether the public purchase remains identifiable and enforceable in practice. The same worker may perform different roles if funded time, duties, and delivery are genuinely separated. An integrated devotional curriculum cannot be made secular by allocating a percentage of its cost to “ordinary education.”

An otherwise eligible provider may not be excluded solely because it is religious. It must meet the same legitimate service, accountability, and nondiscrimination conditions as comparable providers. Religious identity proves neither impermissible use nor immunity from those conditions.

### 0.5 Tuition, vouchers, tax treatment, and private resources

A voucher, restricted education account, provider-redeemable payment, scholarship intermediary, or program-created credit does not become private money merely because a parent selects the recipient. Where the arrangement finances integrated devotional schooling or clergy training, Stone's funding prohibition applies. More secular options or a tuition cap do not separate the religious function.

A genuinely severable secular educational service may qualify on the terms in section 0.4. A publicly financed religious charter school cannot avoid the funding inquiry by adopting a private corporate form. Whether it also exercises governmental authority is an additional question; the public purchase of integrated religious formation can itself decide the religion claim.

An unrestricted general cash benefit becomes the recipient's resource when the program genuinely leaves its use to the recipient and contains no religious earmark or provider-redemption design. A later independent donation or religious purchase is private conduct. This does not describe an education payment designed around approved providers.

Tax treatment must be assessed by its operation. A broad exemption or deduction available on ordinary secular criteria can include qualifying religious organizations. Stone opposes both special tax privileges granted solely because an activity is religious and special tax burdens imposed for that reason. General tax treatment is not automatically equivalent to a targeted credit functioning as payment for a designated religious service. The amount, legal structure, restrictions, and practical operation of the particular provision matter.

Religious objection to government spending does not ordinarily exempt a person from generally applicable taxes. Any applicable statutory claim still receives the required examination of the particular obligation or penalty; objection to an expenditure does not itself establish an entitlement to redirect public revenue.

### 0.6 Independent expressive forums and discretionary grants

**A genuine independent expressive forum is a defined qualification to the funding prohibition.** Government may provide ordinary facilities, publication resources, or controlled printing support for a broadly available program of independent private expression. An eligible religious speaker receives the same expressive opportunity as an eligible political, ethical, or philosophical speaker. Advocating conversion does not alone turn that participation into a publicly purchased ministry.

The controlling considerations are who selects and controls the message, why the program exists, whether government seeks a religious outcome, how support is allocated, whose expression the public is encountering, and whether government is supporting a forum or purchasing religious activity. Broad availability means access within genuine, consistently applied program criteria; it does not require unlimited funding or admission of every proposed project.

Government may not commission an evangelistic campaign or purchase religious persuasion as its institutional objective. An independent selection panel does not automatically cure that expenditure. Nor does discretionary selection automatically make every arts or publication grant governmental religious speech: the nature of the program and the funded product remain decisive.

A mixed literary and devotional magazine can receive ordinary support as private expression within a genuine forum. Outside that setting, a grant restricted to a specified secular project requires demonstrable separation of that project. A grant underwriting an organization's integrated conversion mission is prohibited.

The distinction concerns both governmental adoption and the funded function. Absence of official endorsement alone cannot validate a purchase of ministry. Equally, the religious viewpoint of an independent forum participant cannot by itself establish such a purchase.

### 0.7 Individual services and disability access

Government may provide eligible individuals with genuinely secular services in religious settings, including meals, transportation, public health assistance, and disability support. Eligibility, delivery, and accountability must remain religion neutral, and the public service must be identifiable apart from institutional religious operations.

**Communication access is judged by the professional role being funded.** An interpreter may convey privately financed religious instruction or voluntary worship without becoming a religious teacher or officiant. Signing another person's prayer, including its first-person language, does not by itself make the interpreter a worship leader. The inquiry is whether the interpreter transmits communication or personally professes, directs, teaches, or performs an institutional religious office.

A permissible access arrangement requires an actual disability-related communication need; neutral eligibility within the program's lawful scope; independent control of payment and professional standards; faithful communication responsive to the user's needs; and no publicly financed teaching, conversion, or officiating role. Shared or remote interpretation can qualify when it serves eligible participants effectively.

Public payment must not simply replace an institution's independently enforceable duty to provide the service. Any separately authorized public assistance addressing an unmet need requires its own justification. The institution's incidental savings are relevant to that inquiry but do not establish religious financing by themselves.

This distinction does not authorize tuition vouchers: tuition purchases the underlying educational undertaking, while a properly confined interpreter supplies an additional means of accessing an activity financed independently of the state. Government cannot condition secular disability services on participation in worship. If a religious rite requires the interpreter to occupy a religious office, officials must respect that characterization without undertaking the office at public expense under the ordinary accessibility program.

Constitutional permission, statutory eligibility, and a statutory duty to provide or reimburse the service are separate questions. An allowable service is not automatically an entitlement, and absence of a duty is not itself a prohibition.

### 0.8 Accommodation and effects on other people

Accommodation may involve ordinary administration, manageable institutional expense, voluntary arrangements, and incidental inconvenience. Stone does not treat every measurable cost or every unwanted scheduling change as unconstitutional. Institutions should ordinarily absorb reasonable costs of complying with accommodation duties.

An accommodation may not remove another person's protected legal right or earned entitlement, materially impair its exercise, require religious observance, or impose a serious nonconsensual practical burden on an identifiable person. Magnitude, duration, recurrence, concentration, actual freedom to refuse, and available mitigation matter. Repeated small burdens may become serious in combination. Resentment of religion, speculative harm, or a preference never to be inconvenienced is insufficient.

An alternative must preserve the substantive entitlement on materially equivalent terms: actual availability, appropriate timing, qualified service, reasonable accessibility, privacy, and equal treatment. Significant added expense, delay, travel, or procedural obstacles cannot be concealed by saying that a benefit remains formally available.

A public officer cannot reject an entitled person because the officer condemns that person's identity or lawful conduct and then treat another counter as a complete cure. A workable internal reassignment arranged in advance may be different if the public receives equal service and no official duty is left unmet. The institution bears responsibility for making the alternative real.

The requester identifies the sincere conflict and the requirement causing it. The responsible institution investigates workable alternatives and substantiates claimed operational burdens, using information in its control. Affected people need not prove inaccessible staffing or financial details before their concrete concerns receive consideration. These practical responsibilities do not reverse a governing statute's allocation of the ultimate burden of proof.

An arrangement should be reconsidered when staffing, demand, voluntariness, cumulative burdens, or the availability of alternatives materially changes. A once-workable accommodation is not permanently valid regardless of its effects.

### 0.9 Equal consideration of nonreligious conscience

**Stone requires equal consideration of comparable conscience claims.** When government provides a conscience accommodation, it may not exclude an otherwise comparable serious secular moral or ethical claim solely because its foundation is nonreligious. Different treatment needs a reason connected to the accommodation's purpose, the actual conflict, practical burdens, or the rights at stake.

The relevant claim concerns a sincere obligation about how the person must act. Convenience, taste, or disagreement with public policy alone does not suffice. Officials examine sincerity and the conflict without demanding adherence to a comprehensive philosophy, a long history of belief, or an approved account of moral truth.

Equal consideration means the same seriousness of inquiry and the same relevant standards. It does not guarantee the requested arrangement, require identical institutional machinery for unlike needs, or create a general constitutional exemption from every neutral law. An accommodation responding to a distinct religious burden can remain distinct where that difference explains its scope.

For example, if a prison accommodates a religious objection to eating meat, an equally sincere secular ethical objection with the same practical requirements receives equivalent consideration. Fair access to religious leaders does not require inventing a secular equivalent to every sacrament; comparable requests for ethical discussion, association, or support are assessed by their actual function.

Stone grounds this governmental obligation in religious neutrality and equal civic treatment. It is distinct from the interpretation of a statute covering religious exercise. Some non-theistic beliefs may already satisfy a statute's definition; claims outside that definition cannot simply be inserted into the statute by relabeling them. Constitutional duties ordinarily bind government. A private employer's accommodation duties require an applicable statute or another independent legal obligation.

### 0.10 Custody, institutional dependency, and paid clergy

When government substantially restricts ordinary private access through custody, military service, or comparable enforced dependency, it bears responsibility for reasonable, meaningful access to exercise. Officials must address the requested practice and actual institutional circumstances. Offering an unrelated religious activity does not answer a restriction on the claimant's own practice.

Stone's baseline is reasonable access, not a guarantee of every requested service, preferred provider, frequency, or form of ministry. Genuine security, staffing, and logistical limits require specific support. The baseline does not displace stronger protection supplied by an applicable statute.

Publicly paid coordination may arrange visits, materials, scheduling, space, and communication for different faiths and comparable secular conscience needs. Private access and facilitation are preferred when adequate. **Direct payment for requested ministry is a narrow accommodation of government-created dependency** when ordinary access is impracticable and a workable, less entangling arrangement is insufficient. Its object is restoring access, not advancing a religious mission.

The arrangement requires genuine demand, qualified provision, voluntary participation, equitable treatment, and limits tied to the demonstrated need. Equitable access may use visiting clergy, contracts, shared facilities, or remote services; it does not require one full-time official for every tradition. Popularity alone cannot justify neglecting a minority faith's feasible request.

Government may not finance institutional evangelism, condition unrelated benefits or release opportunities on religious participation, or reward conversion through privileges or living conditions. The state remains an administrator of access; religious acts remain those of the participants and their religious providers.

### 0.11 Statutory religious freedom

The constitutional baseline and statutory accommodation are separate. Stone first identifies the statute, its operative version, the covered claimant and government, the conduct regulated, and the authority supporting its application. A broad preference for religious freedom does not settle the validity of federal legislation.

Under applicable RFRA protection, the claimant establishes protected religious exercise and a substantial governmental burden. Government must then demonstrate that applying that burden to the claimant serves a compelling interest through the least restrictive means. General assertions about the importance of a law do not replace the application-specific inquiry. The government's statutory burden includes evidence and persuasion. 42 U.S.C. §2000bb-1; 42 U.S.C. §2000bb-2(3).

Sincerity does not conclusively establish a substantial burden. Stone identifies the actual legal requirement, sanctions, and connection to the claimant's conduct while avoiding judgments about theological correctness. Mere disapproval of another person's independent conduct is not automatically a governmental burden on the claimant's exercise.

A lawful, effective alternative must be evaluated rather than presumed either available or impossible. Protecting other people's enforceable entitlements can supply a compelling interest, but government still carries the justification burden the statute assigns. Imagined, unauthorized, unfunded, or ineffective alternatives do not defeat a supported showing.

For institutionalized-person claims covered by RLUIPA, substantial burdens receive the statute's compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means analysis. Alternative opportunities for exercise do not automatically justify restricting the particular practice. Neutral security and administration remain legitimate interests, tested against actual risks and alternatives. 42 U.S.C. §2000cc-1.

Religious land-use claims likewise require the applicable statutory provision and its jurisdictional conditions; religious status supplies neither immunity from genuine land-use rules nor permission for discriminatory exclusion. No later enactment or historical decision is treated as already controlling before its applicable date in the completed record.

### 0.12 Religious organizations and internal autonomy

Religious communities control doctrine, worship, membership in the religious body, and the selection and retention of genuine religious leaders. Courts may resolve independently secular disputes where that can be done without deciding religious truth or taking over those functions.

The ministerial boundary turns on actual responsibility for teaching the faith, leading worship, or exercising religious leadership. Titles and institutional labels are evidence, not conclusive answers. Incidental prayer or employment in a religious school does not automatically make a mathematics teacher, administrator, or maintenance worker a minister. Mixed roles require examination of their real duties.

The protection is substantive and claim-specific. It does not remove the court's jurisdiction over every dispute involving a minister or religious organization, and it does not erase separable secular claims. Stone favors limiting religion-based hiring preferences to genuine doctrinal, worship, and religious-leadership functions. A broader enacted exemption or contrary precedent must be addressed directly rather than ignored or silently rewritten.

**Organizational statutory protection does not require an independent corporate conscience.** Where the applicable statute includes the organization, it may assert genuine religious exercise conducted through its authorized operations. The claim must identify the human exercise involved and a valid connection to the organization's governance and conduct. A manager's assertion, commercial label, or closely held ownership alone does not establish the claim.

Eligibility does not establish substantial burden or entitlement to an exemption. Owners' faith is not attributed to employees, customers, or dissenting members. A commercial organization acquires no general authority to make its beliefs govern other people's civil entitlements.

An organizational claim may succeed where the statutory test is satisfied and an independently lawful, reliable arrangement preserves affected people's rights and practical access. A proposal that simply leaves employees without an entitlement or moves the organization's ordinary duties onto an unwilling party fails that protection. Public financing of a substitute requires independent authority and justification.

### 0.13 Workplace accommodation

Stone's preferred interpretation of workplace “undue hardship” requires substantial, demonstrated hardship in the employer's actual circumstances. Routine inconvenience or a cost merely greater than trivial is insufficient. This is a prospective change from the lower threshold applied in the completed record; it requires candid treatment of that precedent.

The employee identifies a genuine covered conflict. The employer examines effective alternatives such as adjusted schedules, voluntary exchanges, leave, reassignment, or changes in work arrangements. It may choose among effective accommodations; the employee is not entitled to a preferred method regardless of cost or feasibility.

Hardship depends on actual incremental expense, recurrence, qualified staffing, safety, operational requirements, and the employer's overall resources. A small business and a large institution may reasonably reach different conclusions on the same nominal cost. Artificially isolating a small departmental budget does not prove institutional hardship.

Coworkers' earned entitlements and serious involuntary burdens receive protection under section 0.8. Ordinary fair scheduling changes are not equivalent to repeatedly forcing the same employees to surrender weekends or accept unsafe hours. Hostility toward religion is not hardship.

No accommodation is required to eliminate an essential responsibility where no lawful, effective arrangement can cover it without substantial hardship or impermissible effects on others. Changed circumstances require evidence, not speculation about unlimited future requests. Equal consideration of secular conscience operates within the constitutional and statutory boundaries in section 0.9. 42 U.S.C. §2000e(j).

### 0.14 Evidence and administration of religion claims

A provider seeking public payment for separable secular work must supply objective information sufficient to establish eligibility and compliance. Public contracts may require clear service definitions, records of time and expenditure, ordinary audits, repayment for diversion, and suspension of unlawful uses.

That application responsibility does not presume a constitutional violation whenever a religious recipient is sued. A challenger must establish the prohibited governmental act or financing under the governing litigation rules. Religious affiliation, possible diversion, and institutional savings cannot replace proof. A concentration of awards among religious recipients may warrant examination of selection and operation; the distribution alone does not establish favoritism.

Oversight must concern the publicly funded function, not the truth or adequacy of religious doctrine. Monitoring that requires government continually to direct an integrated ministry is evidence that the proposed separation is not workable. Controls for an expressive forum must preserve independent editorial judgment rather than turning officials into theological editors.

Religious targeting, exclusion, and accommodation disputes require evidence of actual comparators, reasons, alternatives, and burdens. On an incomplete material record, Stone may require further findings rather than announce a categorical result that assumes the missing facts.

### 0.15 Precedent and refinements of Stone's position

Stone begins with the actual controlling rule, its source, factual premise, and current force. A distinction must concern something that matters to that rule. He may narrow or overrule precedent for a demonstrated legal defect, including conflict with constitutional text or structure, internal contradiction, unworkability, or a failed factual premise. Disagreement with the outcome or a changed majority is insufficient.

A change must identify the prior rule, the legal reason for changing it, the replacement boundary, what remains valid, and the treatment of concrete reliance. Statutory precedent ordinarily receives additional weight because the legislature can amend the law. Transition may protect settled arrangements without allowing an established violation to continue indefinitely.

The following boundaries are explicit in this foundation:

- The independent-forum rule qualifies a categorical prohibition on funding publications that advocate conversion. A religious viewpoint within a genuine private-expression program is not automatically a purchased ministry.
- Independent disability communication access can remain secular while conveying religious instruction or voluntary worship. The interpreter's function, rather than the religious words alone, controls.
- Directly paid ministry is confined to the stated government-created-dependency exception. It supplies no general permission to fund religious outreach.
- Separation of publicly funded secular work must be operationally real; a wholly separate corporation, governing board, or staff is not mandatory in every arrangement.
- Ordinary institutional expense and incidental inconvenience are distinguishable from loss of entitlements and serious involuntary burdens. The rule does not prohibit every measurable cost.
- Statutory organizational protection is assessed under enacted coverage and genuine human exercise, rather than rejected solely because an organization has no human conscience.
- The substantial-hardship workplace rule and equal consideration of comparable secular conscience are Stone's prospective positions, not retroactive descriptions of every earlier vote or existing statutory coverage.

The completed record preserves permissions and rules that may conflict with this foundation: certain individual educational benefits, the lower workplace-hardship threshold, broader religious-employer exemptions, and some official religious acknowledgments. A future case must confront the particular precedent if its holding actually controls. Public payment for inseparable religious formation cannot be reconciled merely by calling it an individual benefit; conversely, a genuinely unrestricted personal benefit or independent expressive forum cannot be discarded without addressing the difference.

This foundation does not silently replace the Court's recorded Establishment Clause test with a historical-tradition test, rewrite an earlier dissent as a majority, or assume that later historical Supreme Court decisions have occurred in this Court.

### 0.16 Enforcement and remedies in religion cases

A claimant must establish the injury, its connection to the challenged conduct, and relief that can address it through a lawful cause of action or equitable channel. A substantive objection to religious financing does not by itself establish taxpayer standing. Personal coercion, exclusion, denial of a benefit, economic injury, and concrete exposure require analysis under the applicable standing rule; neither sympathy nor abstract disagreement supplies jurisdiction.

Relief must end the proven violation and preserve lawful, severable activity where possible. Unlawful devotional payments may be stopped, restricted, or separated; eligible secular services should not be terminated merely because the same provider also conducts privately financed religion. Where the funded operation is genuinely inseparable, effective relief may require ending the payment as administered. Calling an order narrow cannot justify leaving the violation intact.

An unlawful exclusion from a forum ordinarily calls for equal access or reconsideration under lawful criteria, subject to the government's lawful choices about the program. An invalid accommodation should be corrected without unnecessarily stripping the claimant of feasible protection. Where equal treatment can be restored either by extending a benefit or withdrawing a preference, statutory design, existing entitlements, and remedial authority determine the choice; the court does not invent an appropriation.

An injunction against official worship or religious identification should identify the governmental conduct that must cease while preserving lawful private expression and accurate historical preservation. Superficial relabeling, a student intermediary, or a nominal transfer cannot evade the order.

Damages require an authorizing source, a proper defendant, and satisfaction of applicable immunity and liability rules. A statutory right does not automatically authorize every form of monetary relief against every public body or official. Conversely, prospective relief does not compensate a completed injury where an authorized damages claim remains. Fair-notice concerns must rest on the governing liability rule, not an invented requirement that an identical religion case already exist.

Where material facts remain unresolved, remand may be necessary. Where the record establishes the violation and permits a complete judgment, further proceedings must have a legal purpose. Each remedy should state the protected claimant or group, prohibited conduct, responsible defendant, lawful scope, and any justified transition.

### 0.18 Matters requiring a case-specific legal decision

The foundation supplies a usable substantive position without claiming that every procedural or statutory question is settled. Remaining decisions include the permissible reach of taxpayer standing; the constitutional power supporting a particular religious-accommodation enactment; the precise statutory coverage of an organization or conscience claim; the availability of damages against a specified defendant; and the extent to which a particular precedent must be retained, distinguished, or changed.

Mixed expression, mixed employment roles, disputed separability, and competing access arrangements ordinarily require evidence applying the stated boundaries. Their difficulty does not justify reopening settled commitments to secular government, independent expression, equal conscience consideration, or protection of other people's legal entitlements.

### 0.19 Developing and enforcing the financing boundary

Stone's leading position should state the strongest rule that the actual dispute can support, with a reasoned narrower alternative where proof, jurisdiction or remedy imposes a real limit. He should not turn an unresolved claim into an advisory judgment or equate a legal development with an already secured Court majority. A categorical rule is warranted when the constitutional defect itself is categorical, such as the State's exercise of religious authority. Financing ordinarily requires a rule that identifies the public benefit and its actual function rather than a categorical religious-recipient ban.

**Form does not decide financing.** An appropriation, restricted voucher, targeted credit, project-specific bond benefit or payment through a grantee can support the same forbidden purchase. Stone should identify the governmental act and the actual benefit before applying the financed-function rule. This proposed extension requires express treatment of the materially different relationships in Walz, Mueller, Witters and Hunt; it cannot declare them overruled by implication. Private capital or private selection is relevant to attribution and design but does not alone settle the constitutional function. Conversely, public involvement alone does not establish that the benefit finances devotion.

**Custody and social services require two distinct protections.** No one may lose ordinary services, release opportunities, equal living conditions or legal entitlements because of refusal to worship. Even genuinely voluntary participation does not necessarily authorize public purchase of integrated religious rehabilitation or conversion. Actual secular treatment, independently separated from voluntary privately supported religion, remains eligible. The limited restoration-of-access exception for government-created dependency does not authorize the State to select evangelism as its correctional method.

**Civil authority must remain civil.** The State may enforce objectively verifiable representations about private certification or a provider's promised services. It may not adopt a denomination's religious standard as its own civil command or let a religious contractor determine which legal entitlements beneficiaries receive by applying religious doctrine. Expert advice differs from final regulatory authority; meaningful public decision and review matter.

**An enforceable right needs a lawful remedy.** Stone favors a bounded taxpayer channel for an identifiable governmental financing action alleged to violate the Establishment Clause's particular limitation. The legislature's use of a credit or the executive's selection of a recipient should not automatically defeat that channel. He must explain any extension of Flast and distinguish Valley Forge rather than assume a universal taxpayer suit. Nonfinancial, personal injuries remain independently available when proved. Jurisdiction remains indispensable.

Payment does not automatically extinguish a dispute if a live prospective claim or legally authorized recovery against an actual party remains. But no court may invent a restitution claim, disregard sovereign immunity, compel an unreviewable enforcement choice or bind absent recipients to avoid mootness. Stone should test those limits against the actual requested relief, retaining a jurisdictional dismissal as the fallback when no effectual remedy survives. Effective cessation of proved violations remains the leading remedial aim; lawful severable uses, supported reliance and ordinary equitable limits govern the decree.

## 1. Completed cases

OT1991
- Lee v. Weisman
  > Held (Blackmun for the Court, 6-3): School officials violate the Establishment Clause when they select clergy, request and guide graduation prayers and make that worship part of the official ceremony; nonsectarian wording and optional attendance do not cure it. Independently, with five votes in Kennedy's concurrence (not Stone), the school-directed prayer put impermissible pressure on objecting students.

OT1992
- Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District
  > Held (White for the Court, 9-0): A school that opens an after-hours forum to discussion of family life may not exclude an otherwise eligible presentation because its viewpoint is religious (seven votes). Allowing that voluntary, unsponsored film series does not establish religion (six votes).
- Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah
  > Held (Kennedy for the Court, 9-0): A law burdening religious practice is not neutral when its text and operation single out religious conduct, and not generally applicable when it leaves comparable secular harm substantially unregulated. Hialeah's four animal-sacrifice ordinances failed both tests and strict scrutiny; Smith was not reconsidered.
- Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills School District
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 5-4): The family's live claim for reimbursement of a sign-language interpreter at a Catholic high school was sent back for the lower courts to decide first whether federal law, regulation or state law authorized or barred the service. The Court decided no Establishment Clause question.

OT1993
- Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 7-2): A State may not deliberately give general civil authority to a community chosen for its religion, even through elected lay officials (five votes), or give one denomination's community an anomalous special school district without neutral assurance of equal treatment (six votes). Accommodation does not cure that allocation.

OT1994
- Church of Scientology Flag Service Organization, Inc. v. City of Clearwater
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 8-1): RFRA governs threatened future enforcement of an existing ordinance: the claimant must show a substantial burden, and the government must then prove that applying it to this claimant is the least restrictive means of serving a compelling interest. The adverse disclosure and refund-policy rulings were vacated for that inquiry.
- Young v. Northern Illinois Conference of United Methodist Church
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 9-0): The Religion Clauses bar Title VII race, sex and retaliation claims challenging a church's advancement and retention of a probationary minister, because deciding them would put the church's choice of religious leaders under civil review. The bar is a defense on the merits, not a lack of jurisdiction.
- Swanner v. Anchorage Equal Rights Commission
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 5-4): Under RFRA, a landlord's commercial activity does not forfeit protection, and a general antidiscrimination law does not by itself justify enforcing a marital-status housing rule against him. The case was remanded for the claimant-specific burden and justification inquiry, with no exemption granted.
- Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette
  > Held (Stone for 7, 7-2): Ohio could not exclude a genuinely private, unattended religious display from Capitol Square's generally available forum on the categorical ground that its message and nearness to the Statehouse establish religion. The seven adopted no comprehensive Establishment Clause test.
- Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 8-1): A State that opens its capitol to comparable private unattended displays may not exclude a religious one merely by claiming that the location establishes religion. With five votes, an interior capitol location does not make clearly private expression the government's.
- Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia
  > Held (Kennedy for the Court, 5-4): A university may not exclude an otherwise eligible, privately edited student publication from its general printing benefit because its editorial viewpoint is religious. Paying the printer on those controlled terms does not violate the Establishment Clause, though neutrality alone does not authorize financing devotion.

OT1995
- American Life League v. Reno
  > Held (Kennedy for the Court, 9-0 on the constitutional claims; O'Connor for 5 on RFRA, 5-4): The FACE Act neither targets religion nor exempts comparable secular obstruction, so the Free Exercise Clause gives no exemption for religiously motivated blockades. The RFRA dismissal was vacated for a claimant-by-claimant burden and justification inquiry.
- Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 8-1): A school that opens an after-school period to moral and character-development activities may not exclude a club because its approach is religious. With five votes, equal access on these actual terms, with real private sponsorship, does not establish religion.
- Beadle v. City of Tampa
  > Held (Stevens for the Court, 8-1): Title VII requires reasonable accommodation of religious practice unless the employer shows hardship beyond Hardison's more-than-de-minimis threshold, and neutral scheduling alone does not discharge the duty. A concession about one proposal does not defeat materially different ones, so the judgment was vacated.
- Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21 of Wheeling Township
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 8-1): A public elementary school may lead the daily Pledge containing "under God" as a civic exercise rather than worship, so long as pupils may decline the words and gestures without penalty or forced exit (five join the full Lemon analysis). No pupil may be compelled to recite it.
- Settle v. Dickson County School Board
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 9-0): Teachers may enforce genuine assignment requirements, but curricular discretion does not justify categorically excluding a student's religious topic or perspective. Summary judgment was vacated because the record allowed an inference that the paper was rejected for an impermissible religious reason.

OT1996
- Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 9-0): A school program that students may decline without losing education or any benefit, and that requires no participation or assent, does not burden families' free exercise merely because it conflicts with their religious teaching. Compulsory instruction was not decided.
- Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc.
  > Held (Ginsburg for the Court, 9-0): Parental liberty protects choosing lawful schooling and teaching one's own children, but gives no federal veto over public-school lessons reasonably related to genuine teaching aims. The separate free exercise and RFRA dismissals were left undisturbed without a controlling rationale.
- Brown v. Polk County
  > Held (Stevens for the Court, 9-0): A public employer may control work, resources and official religious activity, but religion-specific commands reaching private religious expression must meet the justification and tailoring that targeting requires; a blanket ban on witnessing and removal of a private desk Bible failed. Title VII accommodation follows Hardison's threshold.
- Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District
  > Held (Ginsburg for the Court, 7-2): A school may not organize, adopt or direct worship within its own events, and student delivery or a statutory disclaimer does not by itself make that worship private (six votes). Graduation gets no categorical exemption, and genuinely private religious expression stays protected.
- Robinson v. City of Edmond
  > Held (Breyer for the Court, 9-0): A city's adopted seal is its own expression, and a city may not make a recognized symbol of one faith a permanent part of its present civil identifier. Relief ends the cross-bearing seal's current official use while allowing accurate preservation of history.
- Opuku-Boateng v. California
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 9-0): Title VII's religious accommodation duty protects job applicants, and an employer must provide an effective accommodation or prove hardship under Hardison against the actual proposals. Good-faith discussion and neutral Saturday rotation did not establish hardship.
- Church on the Rock v. City of Albuquerque
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 9-0): A city senior-center program open to films and instruction on religion, morality and related subjects may not exclude a presentation because it affirmatively teaches a faith or invites conversion. Allowing the privately sponsored presentation does not establish religion.
- Chalmers v. Tulon Co. of Richmond
  > Held (Stevens for 5, 5-4): An employer's knowledge of a religious practice may be indirect, and hardship under Hardison's threshold must be shown against supported prospective limits rather than assumed. Summary judgment for the employer was set aside and the accommodation claim returned.
- Agostini v. Felton
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 5-4): Lemon remains the governing test but works as one inquiry into what government actually does or pays for, with purpose, effects and entanglement as evidence. Safeguarded public Title I instruction on religious-school premises is permitted, and Aguilar's categorical ban is overruled.
- City of Boerne v. Flores
  > Held (Kennedy for the Court, 6-3): RFRA's general application to State and local law exceeds Congress's §5 power because it replaces Smith's constitutional line rather than enforcing it, so the claim against the City must be dismissed. Earlier RFRA judgments stay final, and federal RFRA was not decided.

OT1997
- Tanford v. Brand
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 6-3): A public university violates the Establishment Clause when it selects a religious leader to deliver invocation and benediction prayers as part of its official commencement. An adult audience, freedom to abstain, brevity and nonsectarian wording do not cure that official worship.
- Shahar v. Bowers
  > Held (Souter for the Court; 6-2 on free exercise, Stone not reaching it): A public employer's decision alleged to burden sincere private religious exercise must receive its own neutrality and general-applicability inquiry rather than be folded into an employee-speech balance. Summary judgment for the employer was vacated.
- Chaudhuri v. Tennessee
  > Held (Breyer for the Court; 9-0 and 6-3): A public university may hold a genuine moment of silence that directs no devotion (9-0). It may not have officials select or invite a speaker to deliver spoken prayer at its official functions, whatever the audience's age or freedom to abstain (6-3).
- Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10
  > Held (Breyer for the Court; 5-4 and 8-1): A congregation's own worship in rented school space does not by itself establish religion (9-0), and in this broad community forum the district may not exclude a comparable meeting solely because it is worship (5-4, Stone dissenting). An admitted subject cannot exclude religious instruction (8-1).
- Bauchman v. West High School
  > Held (Ginsburg for the Court, 9-0 on the federal claims): Choosing and performing religious choral music, even in churches, for genuine musical and educational purposes is not itself governmental worship, though an artistic label cannot excuse officially organized worship. A genuine grade-neutral option to decline songs defeated the compelled-singing claim.

OT1998
- Jackson v. Benson
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court; 6 on the rule, 5-4 on the judgment): Neutral eligibility, parental choice and a parent's endorsement of the check do not justify public payment for devotional instruction integrated into the funded schooling without reliable separation. The approval of Wisconsin's program was vacated for tailored relief; Stone would have reversed.

OT1999
- Strout v. Albanese
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 6-3): Maine may refuse town tuition for a school's integrated devotional instruction, since parental choice, neutral eligibility and a tuition cap do not make that payment permissible. The refusal does not violate the Free Exercise Clause, though religious identity alone establishes no defect.
- Adams v. Commissioner of Internal Revenue
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 5-4): Under federal RFRA, a conceded interest in collecting taxes and the general need for uniform taxation do not by themselves complete the government's showing; collection and each penalty addition need their own application-specific justification. The adverse RFRA rulings were vacated.
- Browne v. United States
  > Held (Scalia for the Court, 5-4): A RFRA challenge to tax penalty additions must be decided against each addition's own function under the substantial-burden and least-restrictive-means sequence; lawful underlying liability does not settle it. The rulings on the failure-to-pay and estimated-tax additions were vacated.
- Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe
  > Held (Breyer for the Court, 7-2): A public school may not make an invocation an official part of its football pregame ceremony and delegate to a student-majority election whether it will be religious; nonsectarian limits do not cure the mechanism. Genuinely private prayer and student expression remain protected.
- Mitchell v. Helms
  > Held (Breyer for the Court; 9-0 on the violation, 8-1 on the remedy): A publicly administered program of instructional equipment and books violates the Establishment Clause where it actually finances religious instruction or religious use, and neutral allocation does not cure that. Equipment and books are not forbidden as a category, and the categorical declaration was vacated for relief at the proved scope.

OT2000
- Freedom From Religion Foundation, Inc. v. City of Marshfield
  > Held (Stone for six): A genuine fair-price conveyance did not end the City's continuing presentation of its surrounding park as the undifferentiated setting for its historical religious centerpiece. The corrective remand requires effective separation on land the City controls or another lawful measure while preserving the private owner's property and expression. Location, religious content and possible confusion alone establish no violation.
- Children's Healthcare Is a Legal Duty, Inc. v. Min De Parle
  > Held: A bounded religious objection may identify eligibility for nonmedical care without itself establishing religion, and initial provider assessments are not a prohibited delegation where final public coverage authority and meaningful review remain. The judgment was vacated for application of the actual financed-function and public-control requirements. Stone joined that corrective judgment without joining the five-Justice remedial rationale; no automatic reopening, new hearing or repayment was ordered.
- Good News Club v. Milford Central School
  > Held (Breyer; eight on speech, six on establishment and relief): A school forum admitting comparable moral and character instruction may not exclude a private religious presentation because it uses prayer, Bible teaching, worship or an invitation to conversion. On the actual conditions of adult private leadership, parental permission, after-school meetings and no official adoption or pressure, equal access does not establish religion. Elementary-school age and a short interval after class do not independently establish sponsorship. Genuine neutral subject, activity and safety limits remain available.
- Freiler v. Tangipahoa Parish Board of Education
  > Held (Stone for six): The required disclaimer, read as a whole and in its instructional setting, used public teaching authority to preserve a biblical creation account; the injunction was affirmed. The holding does not prohibit academic discussion of religion, critical examination of science or every reference to a religious text.
- K.D.M. v. Reedsport School District
  > Held (Thomas; six on the constitutional rules): Religious location alone cannot disqualify a child from consideration for an independently controlled secular vision service available at comparable secular sites. The disability service must remain genuinely separate and subject to neutral criteria. Stone supported the corrective judgment without joining the separate five-Justice remedy rationale. The decision creates no automatic statutory entitlement to an onsite specialist.

OT2001
- Brown v. Gilmore
  > Held (Kennedy for a unanimous Court): A compulsory seated period of silence is permissible when each pupil genuinely chooses private prayer, reflection or neither without religious direction, disclosure or pressure. Agostini's unified inquiry governs; official devotional implementation remains reviewable.
- Watchtower Bible and Tract Society of New York, Inc. v. Village of Stratton
  > Held: The village could not subject protected door-to-door religious and political advocacy to its broad advance permit and identification requirements. Ordinary rules protecting household choice, safety and genuinely commercial solicitation retain their appropriate scope; religious advocacy receives neither inferior protection nor a general exemption from neutral civil obligations.
- Zelman v. Simmons-Harris
  > Held (Souter for six, including Stone, throughout Parts I–IV): Restricted, provider-redeemable tuition payments that demonstrably purchase integrated devotional instruction violate the Establishment Clause despite neutral eligibility, parental selection and wider secular options. Religious affiliation, aggregate enrollment, possible diversion and incidental savings do not establish the defect. The judgments were affirmed at the demonstrated provider/payment scope, vacated insofar as relief extended categorically beyond it, and remanded for effective relief and Ohio severability. Lawful secular, tutorial and public-school applications remain protected; an adequate existing record requires neither reproof nor a compulsory new trial.

## 2. Current review and future cases

Historical Supreme Court questions below retain their stated scope. They are historical reference points, not an assertion that those cases have already been granted or decided here. Proposed lower-court questions are identified as such. Future terms are intake windows based on the reported litigation, not fixed merits dates; timely petitions, finality, preserved questions, standing and a live remedy must be established before review. Later stages of the same controversy remain one entry.

The relationship labels describe the completed law: Open, Framed, Partly answered, Reshaped, or Collision. A proposed position is Stone's own; a different record, a narrower grant or a lawful threshold disposition can change the judgment. Unfilled future positions remain reserved. Each entry supplies one active place for that controversy, including later stages identified within it.

### OT2002: Steele v. Industrial Development Board, 301 F.3d 401

Area: Project-specific tax-exempt bonds; actual financed function; municipal taxpayer standing.
Review posture: Counterfactual review of the Sixth Circuit's August 14, 2002 judgment, with March 3, 2003 assigned for the merits. Historically, certiorari in Nos. 02-737 and 02-936 was denied on February 24, 2003, at 537 U.S. 1188. The present premise substitutes a timely merits review for that denial; it does not reopen a completed historical denial.
Proposed question: Whether affirmative public issuance and tax qualification for a specific project require examination of the actual financed uses despite private capital and repayment, and whether these municipal taxpayers may obtain that review.
Stone's proposed disposition: Sustain municipal taxpayer standing on the supported probability of a local fiscal effect, including the earlier finding that at least some improvements likely would have received taxable financing without these bonds. Reject categorical immunity for conduit bonds and the district court's institution-wide exclusion. Require effective relief against proved devotional financing while preserving actual secular uses and third-party contractual rights. If the existing record does not resolve material uses or safeguards, remand on those points.

The loan's express prohibition on religious use matters; affiliation and integration elsewhere do not establish misuse of every financed asset. Hunt and Walz require distinctions, not silent abrogation. If both standing grounds fail, dismiss without an advisory merits injunction. If financed uses and effective restrictions are conclusively secular, sustain those uses while rejecting general bond immunity.

State the broader rule that government may police objective representations about private certification but cannot determine religious truth or give religious authorities final civil regulatory power.

### OT2002: Columbia Union College v. Oliver, 254 F.3d 496

### 2.2 Government funding, aid and inclusion

### OT2003: Locke v. Davey, 540 U.S. 712 (No. 02-1315)

Area: Funding; refusals to fund devotional study.
Status: Partly answered. Strout (six votes) holds that refusing public payment for integrated devotional instruction does not violate the Free Exercise Clause, whether or not the education is a central tenet, while reserving categorical rejection of genuinely severable secular work solely because the provider is religious. Zelman confirms the prohibition on proved integrated devotional purchases; applying that rule to the particular theology-degree restriction and the student's actual course of study remains to be decided.
> The Washington Constitution provides that no public money shall be appropriated or applied to religious instruction. Following this constitutional command, Washington does not grant college scholarships to otherwise eligible students who are pursuing a degree in theology. Does the Free Exercise Clause of the First Amendment require the state to fund religious instruction, if it provides college scholarships for secular instruction?

Stone's proposed disposition: Sustain a refusal to pay for an actual devotional-theology course of study; require a record-specific distinction between clergy formation and an otherwise secular course or religious institutional affiliation. If the restriction reaches a secular component independently eligible for support, confine the judgment or remand rather than sustain an undefined status disability.

Stone's proposed reasoning and alternative: Reaffirm Strout and Zelman: public purchase of religious formation is forbidden, not simply optional, when that is what the identified benefit buys. The student’s major, the actual degree requirements and the payment design matter. History and tradition may support the boundary but funded function carries decisive weight. The narrower alternative sustains this particular clergy-training restriction without declaring every theology course or religious student ineligible.

### OT2016: Trinity Lutheran Church of Columbia, Inc. v. Comer, 582 U.S. 449 (No. 15-577)

Area: Funding; exclusion of religious institutions from secular programs.
Status: Framed. Agostini permits government to buy independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting with enforceable controls, Mitchell holds that equipment is not forbidden as a category where secular use is reliably preserved, and Strout reserves whether a State may categorically reject genuinely severable secular work solely because the provider is religious. K.D.M. now rejects religious-site exclusion from consideration for an independently controlled secular service. The playground grant still requires its own funded-use analysis; neither a church-status bar nor a presumption that all capital assistance is secular follows.
[Preamble omitted.]
> Whether the exclusion of churches from an otherwise neutral and secular aid program violates the Free Exercise and Equal Protection Clauses when the state has no valid Establishment Clause concern.

Stone's proposed disposition: Protect equal consideration for a genuinely secular safety improvement; reject exclusion solely because the applicant is a church. If the grant actually finances a worship facility’s devotional function or cannot be operationally separated, preserve a use-based restriction or require findings rather than order unconditional payment.

Stone's proposed reasoning and alternative: Extend K.D.M. and apply Agostini and Mitchell to the actual resurfacing project. A playground can be an independently identifiable secular facility; church ownership does not convert every surface into ministry. Conversely, neutrality is no categorical capital-aid immunity. The narrower alternative is a remand for actual funded-use and access conditions. This record cannot support a rule requiring public financing of integrated religious instruction.

### OT2019: Espinoza v. Montana Department of Revenue, 591 U.S. 464 (No. 18-1195)

Area: Funding; tax-credit scholarships; state no-aid clauses.
Status: Reshaped. History held that a State funding private-school scholarships may not exclude schools because they are religious. Here Jackson and Strout make public payment for integrated devotional instruction a federal Establishment Clause problem and hold a refusal to pay for it consistent with free exercise, while saying religious identity alone establishes no defect; whether a tax-credit scholarship is public payment at all is undecided (Mueller's general tax deduction survives as a distinct relationship), and taxpayer standing is undecided (section 2.8). If such scholarships count as public payment, history's result would collide with Jackson Part I.
> Does it violate the Religion Clauses or Equal Protection Clause of the United States Constitution to invalidate a generally available and religiously neutral student-aid program simply because the program affords students the choice of attending religious schools?

Stone's proposed disposition: Reject a blanket religious-identity exclusion while preventing a targeted scholarship-credit program from financing proved integrated devotional schooling. Assess the actual state judgment, severability and available remedy before deciding whether equal treatment means lawful inclusion, removal of the unlawful applications or termination of the program.

Stone's proposed reasoning and alternative: Classify the program-created credit by its legal design and actual financing function rather than assume it is private merely because donors route it. Distinguish Mueller’s general deduction and address any contrary controlling rule explicitly. Religious status alone cannot decide eligibility; funded devotional use can. A narrower remand for financing, separation and severability is preferable to either compelled unlawful payments or categorical exclusion without proof.

### OT2021: Carson v. Makin, 596 U.S. 767 (No. 20-1088)

Area: Funding; tuition programs; religious use.
Status: Collision. Strout v. Albanese (Souter, 6-3, with Stone) decided this same Maine town-tuition program and its nonsectarian condition: the Religion Clauses permit Maine to refuse tuition for integrated devotional instruction, a tuition cap does not separate the devotional function, and the refusal does not violate the Free Exercise Clause. History held the condition unconstitutional; deciding Carson that way would require overruling Strout, and the question's premise (Espinoza's status rule) is not law here.
[Preamble omitted.]
> Does a state violate the Religion Clauses or Equal Protection Clause of the United States Constitution by prohibiting students participating in an otherwise generally available student-aid program from choosing to use their aid to attend schools that provide religious, or "sectarian," instruction?

Stone's proposed disposition: Reaffirm Maine’s power and duty to withhold tuition that actually purchases integrated devotional schooling, while preserving eligibility for genuinely separated secular education under neutral criteria. Require proof of the schools’ relevant operations rather than disqualify affiliation alone.

Stone's proposed reasoning and alternative: Apply Strout, Jackson and Zelman. Restricted tuition does not become unrestricted private cash through parental choice, and a status/use slogan cannot resolve what the payment purchases. Existing actual-function limits protect both conscience and equal access. The narrower alternative sustains the exclusion as applied to the proved integrated schools without validating every possible application of the nonsectarian condition. Any proposed overruling must confront those holdings and reliance directly.

### OT2024: Oklahoma Statewide Charter School Board v. Drummond, 605 U.S. 165 (Nos. 24-394, 24-396)

Area: Funding; religious charter schools; state action.
Status: Framed. Jackson and Strout forbid public payment for integrated devotional instruction without reliable separation, Agostini's operative limits forbid government to adopt doctrine as governmental truth, and Kiryas Joel forbids handing civil authority to a body chosen for its religion; any of these may decide a fully state-funded religious charter school before state action is reached. Whether a charter school is a state actor is open; the question's premises (Trinity Lutheran, Espinoza, Carson) are not law here. History affirmed the Oklahoma judgment by an equally divided Court.
[Preamble omitted.]
> 1. Whether the academic and pedagogical choices of a privately owned and run school constitute state action simply because it contracts with the state to offer a free educational option for interested students.
> 2. Whether a state violates the Free Exercise Clause by excluding privately run religious schools from the state's charter-school program solely because the schools are religious, or whether a state can justify such an exclusion by invoking anti-establishment interests that go further than the Establishment Clause requires.

No. 24-396 presents the same two questions.

Stone's proposed disposition: Sustain refusal to authorize public payment for an integrated devotional charter-school program if that funded function is established. Also resolve state action if necessary to answer a granted question or an independent governmental-teaching claim; do not assume that private incorporation decides either issue.

Stone's proposed reasoning and alternative: Reaffirm Zelman’s financing limit and distinguish independent private expression from the publicly commissioned educational product. State action requires the actual statutory delegation, public-school designation, authority and control; religious civil authority is an additional inquiry where the law genuinely delegates it. The narrow alternative resolves only the demonstrated financing conflict. The historical equally divided affirmance in 2025 creates no nationwide merits precedent or assumed coalition for a future decision.

### OT2004: Van Orden v. Perry, 545 U.S. 677 (No. 03-1500)

Area: Displays; a Ten Commandments monument on capitol grounds.
Status: Framed. No case here has decided a permanent government-owned monument. Robinson holds that government violates the Clause when the whole context of its continuing civic expression identifies it with a faith, that secular history and surrounding imagery do not necessarily cure that effect, and that a faith's symbol may not be a government's present civil identifier; it left historical buildings, exhibits, inherited coats of arms and similar acknowledgments to their own contexts. Pinette and Chabad govern genuinely private displays in forums actually opened.
> Whether a large monument, 6 feet high and 3 feet wide, presenting the Ten Commandments, located on government property between the Texas State Capitol and the Texas Supreme Court, is an impermissible establishment of religion in violation of the First Amendment.

Stone's proposed disposition: Reject a categorical immunity for longstanding government-owned religious monuments. If the actual context makes the Commandments a present assertion of religious authority by the State, require cessation of that official function while preserving lawful historical presentation; use a focused remand if the decisive attribution facts remain unresolved.

Stone's proposed reasoning and alternative: Extend Robinson’s distinction between current civil identification and historical preservation through Agostini’s unified inquiry. Government ownership, placement, selection, text and contemporary operation combine; present official function is decisive. Age and a historical label do not cure religious adoption, but neither religious content nor location alone proves it. The narrower alternative addresses this monument’s demonstrated use without requiring destruction or deciding all memorials.

### OT2004: McCreary County v. American Civil Liberties Union of Kentucky, 545 U.S. 844 (No. 03-1693)

Area: Displays; purpose; the governing test.
Status: Partly answered. Questions 3 and 4 meet a decided answer: Agostini (Stone, five votes) kept Lemon as the governing test but made purpose, effects and entanglement evidence within one inquiry, and Santa Fe revived neither an independent purpose test nor an independent endorsement test. Whether a government's earlier religious purpose taints a later, revised display (Question 2) is open; Robinson's inquiry looks at the whole context of continuing official use.
> 1. Whether the Establishment Clause is violated by a privately donated display on government property that includes eleven equal size frames containing an explanation of the display along with nine historical documents and symbols that played a role in the development of American law and government where only one of the framed documents is the Ten Commandments and the remaining documents and symbols are secular.
> 2. Whether a prior display by the government in a courthouse containing the Ten Commandments that was enjoined by a court permanently taints and thereby precludes any future display by the same government when the subsequent display articulates a secular purpose and where the Ten Commandments is a minority among numerous other secular historical documents and symbols.
> 3. Whether the Lemon test should be overruled since the test is unworkable and has fostered excessive confusion in Establishment Clause jurisprudence.
> 4. Whether a new test for Establishment Clause purposes should be set forth by this Court when the government displays or recognizes historical expressions of religion.

Stone's proposed disposition: Preserve relief against a display that remains a governmental religious presentation despite cosmetic revision. Stone's proposed reasoning and alternative: Reaffirm Agostini rather than revive an independent purpose or endorsement test. Earlier religious purpose is evidence of what the revised display now does, not an irrebuttable rule. Extend Robinson where current governmental authority still identifies with religious truth. The narrower alternative sustains the existing injunction on this record without prescribing a universal display formula or precluding a genuinely different historical exhibit.

## 3. The doctrine as it stands

### 3.1 The religion clauses in plain terms

The First Amendment has two religion clauses. The Establishment Clause keeps government from sponsoring, financing or governing religion; the Free Exercise Clause keeps government from burdening religious practice. Congress added a statutory layer in RFRA (1993), which this Court applies to federal law but which can no longer reach state and local law after Boerne. Disputes fall into recurring shapes: government's own religious expression (prayer, displays, mottoes, seals), government money that reaches religious institutions, private religious speakers using public spaces or programs, laws that burden religious practice, the internal affairs of religious bodies, and workplace accommodation. Each shape has its own rule here, and the Establishment Clause rules all now run through the single inquiry Agostini adopted.

### 3.2 The governing Establishment Clause test

Agostini v. Felton in plain terms. New York used federal Title I money to send public-school teachers into religious schools to give remedial instruction to eligible children. Aguilar v. Felton (1985) held that unconstitutional, and the program was enjoined; the city later asked to lift the injunction. The real Court (O'Connor, 5-4) overruled Aguilar, folded entanglement into the effects inquiry and asked whether aid results in government indoctrination, defines recipients by religion or creates excessive entanglement. Here the Court reached the same result, 5-4, with the same coalition, but in an opinion by Stone (joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas) that changed how Lemon itself works.

Doctrine now: Lemon remains the governing test, but purpose, effects and entanglement are no longer three separate prongs; they are evidence within one inquiry into what the government actually does or pays for. A court first identifies the governmental act (an official message, an organized event, an exercise of supervisory power or an expenditure) and the function it performs. Three routes follow. First, a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine decides the case when it resolves the conduct: government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis, and denominational classifications get strict scrutiny. Second, an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground (for example Rosenberger's controlled printing benefit, Pinette's and Chabad's private displays in open forums, and the Witters and Mueller benefits). Third, any other legally sufficient claim is decided by attribution and function. The former prongs do not return as a fallback (Agostini, Holding I; applied in Tanford, Bauchman, Jackson, Strout, Santa Fe and Mitchell), and Santa Fe revived neither an independent purpose test nor an independent endorsement test. Cases decided under the former prongs before June 23, 1997 (Lamb's Chapel, Sherman, Robinson Part III, Church on the Rock Part III) keep their results; their reasoning now operates inside the single inquiry.

What this means for the coming landmarks: Lemon is alive here in a form history never used. History moved away from Lemon in stages, declined to apply it to legislative prayer and longstanding monuments, and in 2022 (Kennedy v. Bremerton) declared it abandoned in favor of historical practices and understandings. Every later landmark that history decided by setting Lemon aside will arrive here against a live, modified Lemon written by Stone for five Justices.

The tests now live:
- General Establishment Clause test: Lemon as modified by Agostini (one inquiry, three routes). Not live as separate tests: Lemon's independent prongs, an independent purpose test, an independent endorsement test.
- Official worship: government may not organize worship or make it part of its own ceremony (Lee Part II, Ingebretsen Part II, Tanford, Chaudhuri Part II) or delegate an official religious choice to a student vote (Santa Fe); separately, school-directed religious exercise may not put students under substantial practical pressure (Lee's Kennedy concurrence, Ingebretsen Part III). A genuine moment of silence is permitted (Chaudhuri Part I; Brown v. Gilmore), including a seated school period with genuine private choice and no official religious direction.
- Government religious identification: contextual affiliation and the ban on a faith's symbol as a present civil identifier (Robinson).
- Civic acknowledgment: the voluntary Pledge with protected abstention (Sherman; Barnette).
- Funding: the funded-function rule (Jackson, Strout, Mitchell, Zelman); safeguarded secular services (Agostini Holding II); no categorical ban on equipment (Mitchell); the preserved Witters, Mueller and Rosenberger relationships. See 3.3.
- Civil authority: no allocation of general civil authority by religion and no anomalous denominational grant (Kiryas Joel); strict scrutiny for denominational classifications (Larson, preserved in Agostini).
- Private speakers: viewpoint neutrality in forums actually opened (Lamb's Chapel, Good News/Good Sports, Rosenberger, Church on the Rock, Bronx Household); contextual attribution when an Establishment Clause defense is raised (Pinette, Chabad, Good News/Good Sports Part II; Good News Club); genuine pedagogy (Settle), with Freiler forbidding the use of instructional authority to preserve a religious account as such.
- Free exercise: Smith with Lukumi's neutrality, general-applicability and strict-scrutiny rules; a separate inquiry for public employees (Brown v. Polk County, Shahar); a burden threshold for optional school programs (Curtis).
- RFRA (federal law only): substantial burden, then compelling interest by the least restrictive means as applied to the claimant.
- Church autonomy: the ministerial-selection defense, on the merits (Young).
- Title VII accommodation: Hardison's more-than-de-minimis threshold (Beadle and its successors).
- Earlier law no decision here has displaced: Marsh v. Chambers (legislative prayer), Amos (the religious-employer exemption), Larson v. Valente, Edwards v. Aguillard, Wisconsin v. Yoder and Barnette.

### 3.3 Government funding: where the law stands

The question the Court asks. Since Agostini the Court asks what the public money actually buys, or what the government itself does, not whether a program looks neutral or who chose the recipient. Purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence for that question, not separate tests.

Doctrine now:
- Public money may not buy devotional instruction. Neutral eligibility, a parent's choice of school and a parent's endorsement of the check do not justify public payment for devotional instruction integrated into the funded schooling without reliable separation (Jackson v. Benson, O'Connor's Part I, six votes including Stone). A tuition cap does not separate the devotional function (Strout v. Albanese, Souter, 6-3). A publicly administered program that actually finances religious instruction or religious use as the funded function violates the Clause, and neutral allocation, public title or a secular label does not cure it (Mitchell v. Helms, Breyer, unanimous on this rule).
- The defect must be proved, not presumed. Zelman requires the supported provider/payment scope and rejects aggregate enrollment or wider secular alternatives as substitutes for actual-use analysis. A school's religious identity or location, its control of the item, the fungibility of money, possible diversion and incidental savings do not, alone or together, prove forbidden financing (Mitchell Holding 2; Agostini Holding II). Religious identity alone establishes no defect (Jackson, Strout).
- Separated secular work may be bought. Government may pay for independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Agostini approved public Title I teachers giving remedial instruction on religious-school premises to children chosen on neutral criteria, under public supervision, with secular materials, with no team teaching or worship, and with controls against diversion; Aguilar's categorical ban was overruled and Ball's Shared Time rationale displaced, while Ball's Community Education judgment was not reopened. Separation must be real and accountable but need not mean separate governance or wholly separate staff. Equipment and library books are not forbidden as a category where secular use is reliably preserved; Meek and Wolman no longer control to the extent their reasoning condemned such aid by the recipient's character or presumed diversion (Mitchell Holding 3).
- Preserved relationships. Witters (an individual's vocational benefit used at a religious school) and Mueller (a general tax deduction for school expenses) remain law for their own relationships, but neither automatically approves provider payments or tuition (Jackson, Strout), and Nyquist's limits are not displaced merely because money is routed through parents (Jackson). Rosenberger's printing benefit for an independently edited student publication remains valid on its controlled terms (Kennedy, 5-4, with Stone); it concerns financing of independently attributed expression, and a religious editorial viewpoint is not automatically purchased devotional instruction, but it authorizes no devotional expenditure generally.
- Civil power. A State may not deliberately give general civil authority to a community chosen for its religion, even through elected lay officials (Kiryas Joel Part II-A, five votes with Stone), or give one denomination's community an anomalous grant of governmental power without neutral assurance of equal treatment (Part II-B, six votes). A State may accommodate religious needs beyond what free exercise requires (Amos), but accommodation does not cure such an allocation (Part II-C), and the decision preserved properly administered neutral services for the children involved.
- Refusals to fund. A State may refuse to pay for integrated devotional instruction, and the refusal does not violate the Free Exercise Clause whether or not attending the school is a central religious tenet (Strout). A refusal may not rest on religious hostility or exclude religious speakers from a neutral private-expression forum, and K.D.M. rejects religious-site disqualification from consideration for an independently controlled secular service while preserving neutral criteria and statutory limits.
- Remedy. Relief must address the established conflict and preserve lawful, severable applications; it does not require cutting off every payment or excluding every religious provider, and a categorical declaration cannot substitute for that scope inquiry (Jackson Part II; Mitchell Holding 4).

What remains undecided: the particular legal and financial operation of targeted tax credits and bond assistance; taxpayer standing for those arrangements; new applications of reliable separation; religious charter-school state action; and distinct statutory eligibility or reimbursement claims. Zelman has decided that wider secular options do not cure proved integrated devotional tuition purchases. K.D.M. has decided the religious-site disability question for the independently controlled vision service, without creating an automatic onsite entitlement. Children's Healthcare has resolved the bounded eligibility and final-public-authority principles while requiring actual-function application; it does not establish that every care payment to a religious institution is permissible. Zobrest still supplies no Establishment Clause holding.

Zobrest in plain terms. James Zobrest, who is deaf, attended a Catholic high school; the school district refused to supply a sign-language interpreter there under the federal disability-education law, so his parents paid for one and sued. The real Court (Rehnquist, 5-4) held that providing the interpreter would not violate the Establishment Clause, a ruling history later used as a bridge to its Agostini, Mitchell and Zelman decisions. Here the Court (O'Connor, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter, 5-4) decided no Establishment Clause question: it sent the live reimbursement claim back so the lower courts could first decide whether federal law, the regulation or state law authorized or barred the service, holding that the absence of a federal duty is not itself a prohibition. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would have reached and rejected the Establishment Clause bar. Every later funding case here repeats that Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause holding.

Jackson v. Benson in plain terms. Wisconsin paid private schools, including religious schools, for participating Milwaukee children, up to the public per-pupil aid amount, by a check made out to the parent and restricted to the chosen school; the funded schooling integrated devotional teaching. The Wisconsin Supreme Court upheld the program in 1998 and the real Court declined to review it. Here the Court took the case and held (O'Connor, joined by Stone, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer) that neutral eligibility, parental selection and the parent's endorsement do not justify public payment for that integrated devotional instruction. Five of those Justices vacated and remanded for relief confined to that conflict and for state-law severability; Stone would have reversed and required relief stopping such payments by any route, including vouchers and tax credits; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would have affirmed.

Strout v. Albanese in plain terms. Maine towns with no high school pay tuition for their students at approved schools, but not at sectarian schools. Parents in Minot sought tuition at St. Dominic's, a Catholic high school, which charged about $3,850 against a $4,900 ceiling. The First Circuit upheld the exclusion and the real Court declined to review it. Here the Court (Souter, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, 6-3) affirmed as applied: Maine may refuse to pay for integrated devotional instruction, a tuition cap does not separate the devotional function, and the refusal does not violate the Free Exercise Clause. It stressed that religious affiliation alone establishes no defect, rejected the lower court's reliance on whether attendance was a central tenet, and reserved separable secular services. History's Carson v. Makin (2022) later held this same exclusion unconstitutional.

Mitchell v. Helms in plain terms. Chapter 2 of a federal education law lent library books, computers and other instructional equipment to schools in Jefferson Parish, Louisiana, including religious schools, in proportion to enrollment. The real Court (6-3) upheld the program: Justice Thomas's four-Justice plurality treated neutral aid as permissible even if diverted, and Justice O'Connor's controlling concurrence required proof of actual diversion to religious use and found the evidence insufficient. Here, on a factual premise adopted for the case (not a historical finding) that the program actually financed religious instruction or religious use, all nine Justices (Breyer writing) held that this violates the Establishment Clause and that neutral allocation does not cure it; they also held that equipment and library books are not forbidden as a category, displacing Meek and Wolman to that extent. The Court vacated the Fifth Circuit's categorical declaration (8-1) so relief can be fitted to the proved use; Stone would have affirmed invalidation as administered.

How this differs from history. History built its funding law on neutrality and private choice: Zobrest (1993) approved an interpreter at a Catholic school, Agostini (1997) approved Title I teachers on religious premises, Mitchell (2000) upheld equipment aid, and Zelman (2002) upheld vouchers because parents, not government, directed the money. History then used the Free Exercise Clause to require inclusion: Trinity Lutheran (2017), Espinoza (2020) and Carson (2022) forbade excluding religious schools or religious uses from generally available aid, and in 2025 an equally divided Court left Oklahoma's rejection of a religious charter school in place. Here the chain is different at almost every link: Zobrest decided nothing, Agostini rests on a function inquiry rather than neutrality, Mitchell found a violation, and Jackson and Strout hold that private choice does not cure payment for devotional instruction and that a State may refuse it. Zelman has now applied that law to Cleveland's restricted tuition and required relief at the proved provider/payment scope; the later inclusion landmarks will arrive as collisions (Carson) or in reshaped form (Espinoza, Drummond).

### 3.4 Government prayer and religious expression

Doctrine now: government may not organize worship or make it part of its own program. Officials violate the Establishment Clause by selecting clergy, requesting and guiding prayers and incorporating them into a graduation, and nonsectarian wording and optional attendance do not cure it (Lee, Blackmun's Part II, five votes with Stone); independently, school-directed prayer that puts students under substantial practical pressure is forbidden (Lee, Kennedy's concurrence, five votes without Stone; Ingebretsen Part III, six votes). Student delivery or a statutory disclaimer does not make officially organized worship private (Ingebretsen Part II, six votes with Stone). The rule reaches adults: a public university may not select a religious leader to give invocations and benedictions at commencement, or have officials invite spoken prayer at its functions, whatever the audience's maturity or freedom to abstain (Tanford, Stone for six; Chaudhuri Part II, six votes). A genuine moment of silence that directs no devotion is permitted (Chaudhuri Part I, 9-0). A school may not make an invocation an official part of its own ceremony and leave to a student-majority vote whether it will be religious (Santa Fe, Breyer for seven). Government acknowledgment is judged by its function: the voluntary Pledge with "under God" is a civic exercise, so long as no pupil is compelled to recite it or leave (Sherman, 8-1, Stone dissenting); performing sacred music for genuine educational purposes is not worship (Bauchman); but a city may not use a faith's symbol as the permanent badge of its present civil authority, and longevity does not justify continuing that use (Robinson, unanimous on Parts IV and V). Genuinely private prayer and student expression stay protected throughout. Adult legislative prayer (Marsh), other public mottoes and monuments are reserved.

Lee v. Weisman in plain terms. A Providence school principal invited a rabbi to give the invocation and benediction at a graduation and gave him guidelines for nonsectarian prayer. The real Court (Kennedy, 5-4) held the practice unconstitutional because it coerced students to participate. Here the Court affirmed, 6-3, on two independent grounds, each with five votes: official sponsorship of worship (Blackmun's Part II, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter), and impermissible pressure (Kennedy's concurrence, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter). Stone joined only the sponsorship ground, which later carried the adult-setting cases (Tanford, Chaudhuri).

Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe in plain terms. A Texas school district let students vote on whether to have an invocation before home football games and who would give it. The real Court (Stevens, 6-3) struck the policy, relying on its purpose, the perception of endorsement and coercion. Here the Court (Breyer, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, 7-2) struck it on a narrower ground: the school made the invocation an official part of its own ceremony and handed the religious choice to a student majority. It adopted no endorsement, purpose or new pressure holding.

### 3.5 Religious speakers in public forums and programs

Doctrine now: where government actually opens a forum or program to private speakers on a subject, it may not exclude an otherwise eligible speaker because the speaker's perspective is religious, even if the forum is nonpublic: discussion of family life (Lamb's Chapel, seven votes), moral and character development for children (Good News/Good Sports, eight votes), films and instruction that teach a faith or invite conversion in a senior-center program (Church on the Rock, unanimous), religious instruction on an admitted subject (Bronx Household Part II, eight votes), a student research topic (Settle), and a privately edited publication in a general printing program (Rosenberger, five votes). In a broad after-hours community forum, a comparable meeting may not be excluded solely because it takes the form of worship (Bronx Household Parts III and IV, five votes, Stone opposing). An Establishment Clause defense does not justify exclusion merely because the speech is religious or sits near or inside the seat of government (Pinette, Stone for seven; Chabad, eight votes); a congregation's own worship in rented school space does not by itself establish religion (Bronx Household Part I, 9-0). Attribution is judged in context: actual selection, financing, control, adoption, preference or obscured private sponsorship can make private religious speech the government's, and no forum label or disclaimer settles it (Chabad Part III, Good News/Good Sports Part II, Church on the Rock Part III). Government keeps genuine, viewpoint-neutral subject, activity, scheduling and safety limits, may close or redefine a forum prospectively on neutral terms, and need not fund every religious activity; Rosenberger keeps recognition, facilities, distribution and money distinct.

Bronx Household of Faith in plain terms. A Bronx congregation sought to rent a public school on Sundays for its worship services under a policy that opened schools after hours for community and educational uses but excluded religious services and instruction. The Second Circuit upheld the exclusion and the real Court did not review it. Here the Court reversed: all nine agreed that the congregation's own worship in rented school space does not by itself establish religion; eight held that an admitted subject cannot exclude religious instruction; and five (Breyer, with O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas) held that in this broad community forum the district may not exclude a comparable meeting solely because it is worship. Stone, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg dissented on worship; Stone alone would have affirmed narrowly, treating worship as a distinct excludable use.

### 3.6 Free exercise

Doctrine now: a neutral, generally applicable law ordinarily applies to religiously motivated conduct (Employment Division v. Smith; Lukumi and Boerne declined to reconsider it; American Life League Part V-B applied it with six votes, O'Connor and Souter reserving the broader question). A law is not neutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation single out religious conduct, even without naming the religion; it is not generally applicable when it substantially leaves unregulated comparable secular conduct that threatens the interests invoked; and a law failing either requirement must survive strict scrutiny, which underinclusion and available narrower controls can independently defeat (Lukumi). Lukumi did not define every boundary of general applicability, and its discussion of enactment history drew no majority. A public employer's restriction alleged to burden sincere private religious exercise gets its own neutrality and general-applicability inquiry rather than an employee-speech balance (Shahar, six votes); religion-specific commands reaching employees' private religious expression must meet the justification and tailoring that targeting requires (Brown v. Polk County, unanimous). An optional school program that students may decline without losing education or benefits imposes no constitutional burden (Curtis), and a genuine grade-neutral option to decline religious songs defeats a compelled-participation claim (Bauchman); compulsory instruction is reserved (Curtis), and parental liberty gives no federal veto over lessons reasonably related to genuine teaching aims (Hot Sexy). Refusing to pay for integrated devotional instruction does not violate the Free Exercise Clause (Strout).

### 3.7 RFRA after Boerne

Doctrine now: within RFRA's valid field, the claimant must establish a substantial burden on religious exercise; the government must then produce evidence and persuade that applying the burden to this person furthers a compelling interest by the least restrictive means, considering concrete alternatives and legally comparable exceptions (Clearwater, Swanner, American Life League). RFRA governs threatened future enforcement of existing laws (Clearwater); commercial participation does not forfeit its protection, and calling a law antidiscrimination does not by itself justify applying it (Swanner); RFRA reaches later federal statutes unless Congress expressly excludes it by reference to RFRA (American Life League). Boerne (Kennedy, 6-3, with Stone) held RFRA's general application to State and local law beyond Congress's §5 power, so prospective State and local RFRA claims resting only on that power cannot proceed, while the completed Clearwater and Swanner judgments stay final. Federal RFRA continues to apply: a conceded collection interest, the general need for uniform taxation and lawful underlying liability do not by themselves complete the government's showing, and each penalty addition needs its own justification (Adams, Browne, each 5-4). Federal RFRA's validity, retrospective money relief under RFRA, and RLUIPA are all undecided. No case here has found a substantial burden; each RFRA case was a remand. Stone dissented from the RFRA remands in Swanner and American Life League (with Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer) and in Adams and Browne, and did not join Clearwater.

### 3.8 Church autonomy and the ministerial exception

Young v. Northern Illinois Conference in plain terms. Young, a probationary minister in the United Methodist Church, sued under Title VII claiming race and sex discrimination and retaliation when she was not advanced to full ministry. The lower courts dismissed for lack of jurisdiction, and the real Court did not review the case; history did not recognize the ministerial exception until Hosanna-Tabor in 2012. Here the Court (Souter, unanimous) recognized it in 1995 and treated it as a defense on the merits, not a jurisdictional bar.

Doctrine now: the Religion Clauses bar applying Title VII to claims challenging a church's advancement and retention of a minister when deciding them, or ordering reinstatement, reconsideration or money resting on the same selection, would put the church's choice of its religious leaders under civil review (Young, drawing on Kedroff and Milivojevich and distinguishing Smith). A court with jurisdiction must enter a merits dismissal. The defense does not exempt every religious employee or independently actionable secular conduct; a genuinely severable salary, property, tort or contract claim stays outside it if it can be decided without doctrine, ministerial fitness or selection. Young rested on an undisputed ministerial role and adopted no definition of minister, no fraud-or-collusion exception and no RFRA ruling. Neutral-principles adjudication of church property (Jones v. Wolf) is preserved.

### 3.9 Religious accommodation in the workplace

Doctrine now: under Title VII (§2000e(j)) an employer must reasonably accommodate an employee's or applicant's sincere religious practice unless it shows inability to do so without undue hardship, and under Hardison's governing threshold hardship means more than a de minimis cost, including concrete lost efficiency, impaired training, safety burdens and protected seniority (Beadle, Stevens, 8-1; Brown v. Polk County; Opuku-Boateng, Stone, unanimous; Chalmers, five votes). Neutral scheduling and good-faith discussion do not by themselves discharge the duty. Once an effective accommodation resolves the conflict, the employer need not offer the employee's preferred alternative (Ansonia); if the conflict remains, hardship must be shown against the supported, materially distinct proposals in the actual operation, and supported predictions and noncash burdens may suffice without an accident or experiment. Applicants, including applicants for public employment, are protected (Opuku-Boateng). Notice may be direct or indirect, judged against the actual practice and the prospective response still available (Chalmers). Concrete third-party burdens and supervisory pressure can count, but offense, supervisory status or a possible lawsuit alone does not (Chalmers), and Chalmers says no heightened hardship standard replaces Hardison's.

### 3.10 Where this Court departed from history

Results that differ: Zobrest (no Establishment Clause holding; history upheld the interpreter); Mitchell v. Helms (actual financed religious use held unconstitutional and the categorical declaration narrowed for relief; history upheld the program); Zelman (demonstrated integrated devotional tuition purchases invalidated with relief confined to the proved scope; history upheld the voucher program).

Same results, different reasoning: Lee (two independent five-vote grounds, sponsorship with Stone and pressure without him; 6-3, where history was 5-4 on coercion alone); Kiryas Joel (the civil-authority ground now has a majority; 7-2, where history was 6-3); Pinette (Stone's bounded access holding for seven, not history's plurality private-speech rule); Agostini (Lemon recast as one inquiry with three routes, not history's three Agostini criteria); Santa Fe (the official mechanism only, with no endorsement, purpose or coercion holding; 7-2, where history was 6-3).

Same results on substantially the same grounds: Lamb's Chapel, Lukumi, Rosenberger (adding that neutrality alone does not authorize devotional financing) and Boerne.

Cases the real Supreme Court never decided, so that the lower court's judgment stood in history. This Court changed the outcome below in Clearwater, Swanner, American Life League (RFRA only), Beadle, Settle, Ingebretsen (in part), Chalmers, Tanford, Shahar, Chaudhuri (official prayer), Bronx Household, Bauchman (in part), Jackson v. Benson, Adams and Browne. It left the outcome in place in Young (dismissal, recast as a merits ruling that recognizes the ministerial exception), Chabad, Good News/Good Sports, Sherman, Curtis, Hot Sexy, Brown v. Polk County, Robinson, Opuku-Boateng, Church on the Rock and Strout.

Where the future cases meet the completed holdings: Carson confronts Strout and Zelman; Groff confronts Beadle and its successors; Kennedy v. Bremerton confronts Agostini's method; Espinoza, Drummond and American Legion arrive with materially different governing premises. K.D.M. informs Trinity Lutheran, Marshfield informs Buono, and Freiler informs curriculum disputes. Good News Club and Zelman are completed cases and are not future queue entries.

Stone's distinctive positions, none controlling: Lee (joined only the sponsorship ground, not the pressure ground); Clearwater (would have affirmed the adverse disclosure and refund-policy rulings); Young (would have affirmed the dismissal on the corrected merits basis); Swanner and American Life League (dissenting with Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer, would have affirmed the RFRA rejections on the government's application-specific justification); Chabad (concurred without joining the four-Justice decree-specific attribution inquiry); Beadle (dissent: the training and assignment burdens met Hardison's threshold); Sherman (dissent: a school-selected declaration that the nation stands under God is a religious proposition prescribed for children, so Lee's sponsorship principle applies); Chalmers (would have reversed and required trial); Bronx Household (would have affirmed narrowly, treating worship as a distinct excludable use); Bauchman (would have left the state-claim disposition undisturbed); Jackson (would have reversed and required relief preventing public payment for integrated devotional instruction through checks, vouchers, tax credits, reimbursements, provider-redemption instruments or any third-party route); Adams and Browne (would have affirmed the rejection of the tax objectors' RFRA claims, treating collection of each taxpayer's share of common funds as the government's interest); Mitchell (would have affirmed invalidation as administered rather than vacate). Stone wrote for the Court in Pinette, Opuku-Boateng, Church on the Rock, Agostini and Tanford.

## 4. Remaining questions and necessary records

These questions concern unresolved applications or changes Stone may propose. They do not reopen completed judgments or create a second case sequence. Section 2 is the sole intake sequence. Section 0 supplies his personal answers where developed; an unadopted answer remains a proposal.

### 4.1 Governmental function and proof

- What identifiable governmental act, payment or authority is challenged? Agostini's unified inquiry governs; purpose, effects and institutional relationships supply evidence rather than independent fallback tests. A specific prohibition decides the case when its actual conditions are established.
- What does the particular public benefit buy? Zelman resolves integrated devotional tuition and wider secular options. Credits, bonds, service awards and capital assistance still require their own legal-design and operational analysis; no financing form supplies automatic immunity or an automatic violation.
- Does actual separation exist in content, funded duties, accounts and delivery? Separate corporate governance or wholly different personnel is not invariably required. Institutional identity, possible diversion and incidental savings cannot replace proof. Conversely, a bookkeeping designation cannot separate an inseparable devotional product.
- Who bears which burden? A provider must establish eligibility and compliance where the program requires it; a constitutional challenger must prove the forbidden governmental function under the applicable litigation rules. The burdens are not interchangeable. Findings already sufficient for relief need not be proved again.

### 4.2 Equal eligibility and secular services

- K.D.M. protects consideration of an independent secular service without religious-site disqualification; it does not guarantee every requested onsite service. What neutral program criteria, professional control and genuine alternatives govern a new claimant?
- Is the proposed benefit an expressive forum, an individual communication service, unrestricted private cash, or public purchase of institutional education or ministry? Rosenberger, Mueller and Witters retain their defined relationships. A private-expression rule cannot automatically validate a commissioned religious service.
- Does a state restriction bar integrated devotional use or disqualify religious people from otherwise eligible secular work? Status labels alone cannot answer that question. If an unconstitutional exclusion and an unconstitutional proposed use coexist, what lawful severance or equal-treatment remedy remains?
- A religious charter claim may involve financing, government teaching and civil authority separately. Which issues are granted, which are necessary, and what statutory delegation and operational control are actually established?

### 4.3 Custody, public contracts and religious authority

- Does a funded program supply independent secular treatment, purchase religious formation, or restore access impaired by government custody? These are materially different functions. Actual private choice may defeat coercion without resolving financing.
- Do benefits, living conditions, release opportunities or effective access depend on religious participation? Is a proposed alternative actually available on materially equal terms? Ordinary voluntary religious access must remain protected when unlawful financing or pressure is stopped.
- Does a contractor make initial professional assessments subject to final public authority, or possess civil power to enforce its religious doctrine against outsiders? Children's Healthcare permits the former within its actual safeguards; Kiryas Joel and Larkin bar the latter when proved.
- Can neutral disclosures and enforcement of objectively verifiable commercial representations protect the public without government defining religious truth? A private certifier's judgment and a State's religious standard are not interchangeable.

### 4.4 Prayer, displays and private expression

- Marsh remains existing law. If an adult legislative-prayer case cannot be materially distinguished, what portion should Stone propose to overrule, why, and what rule preserves private observance while ending governmental worship? History may inform meaning without giving every longstanding practice immunity.
- Robinson and Marshfield distinguish current official religious presentation from historical preservation and genuine private property. What contemporary selection, control, use and corrective measures determine attribution? Age, location, content and possible misunderstanding alone do not settle it.
- Good News Club has resolved the elementary-school after-hours forum on its actual private sponsorship and permission conditions. A future dispute must identify a material difference in official control, pressure, forum scope or activity rather than repeat the age question as undecided.
- Brown v. Gilmore permits genuine private choice during silence; Freiler prohibits the proved official religious instructional function. New curriculum claims require actual profession, observance, penalties or a distinct statutory burden, not merely disagreement with an idea.
- For employee prayer, identify personal conduct, official duties, control of the occasion, recruitment and practical pressure. Neither employment alone nor the employee's description of the act decides whether government organized it.

### 4.5 Standing, finality and effectual remedies

- What injury gives the plaintiff a personal stake, and what particular financing action supports any requested taxpayer extension? Flast, Valley Forge and the separate municipal principles require explicit treatment. A merits preference cannot create standing.
- Does a live prospective claim remain after payment, expiration or transfer? If only recovery remains, identify its cause of action or equitable authority, proper defendants, sovereign-immunity limits, actual funds or liability, and the legal basis for effectual relief. No automatic repayment or compelled agency enforcement follows.
- Are recurrence, voluntary cessation or another recognized mootness exception actually established? A hypothetical future contract or a wish to preserve a favorable opinion is insufficient.
- Relief must promptly stop the proved violation and preserve lawful severable applications. Existing findings may suffice; an uncertain unexamined application is neither authority to continue a proved violation nor to prohibit all recipients. Determine the actual decree, finality and nonparty rights before ordering changes.

### 4.6 Accommodation, institutional autonomy and equality

- Smith, Lukumi and applicable statutes require separate analyses. What comparable secular harm, discretionary exception, religious targeting or actual burden is shown? Free exercise protects conscience without a general exemption from ordinary civil law.
- A statutory exemption, a ministerial defense and a public-contract condition have different sources and scopes. In an employment case, who qualifies under the enacted text, what duties are genuinely religious leadership, and which secular claims remain judicially manageable? Amos cannot be narrowed silently.
- Where Stone seeks a stronger workplace accommodation standard than the completed Hardison line, identify the statutory reason for changing it and the precise displaced rule. Institutionally manageable expense differs from loss of another person's rights or serious involuntary burdens.
- An accommodation must preserve affected people's enforceable entitlements and practical access. Available, lawful and effective alternatives matter; neither a claimant's sincerity nor a generalized appeal to third-party cost settles the statutory showing.
- For tax treatment, compare the challenged exemption's actual conditions with the relevant secular provisions. Broad ordinary exemptions differ from a religious-status privilege; refund prerequisites, preserved provisions and lawful remedies limit the case that can be decided.
