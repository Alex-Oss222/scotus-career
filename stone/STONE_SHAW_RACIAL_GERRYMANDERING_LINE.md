# Justice Stone: post-Shaw racial-gerrymandering line, OT1994 through OT2001

**Concerns Justice Stone only. Instructions for future terms, not a predetermined Court holding.**

## Scope and how to use this build

*Shaw v. Reno*, No. 92-357, was decided June 28, 1993 (OT1992, chunk 10) with a result that departs from history: Stone's Court **affirms dismissal of the racial-design-only Equal Protection theory, 5–4** (`terms/OT1992/records/Shaw_v_Reno_merits_1993-06-28.md`). Historically, the Court reversed and remanded, recognizing that a sufficiently bizarre, race-predominant district shape can itself state an Equal Protection claim even without proof of vote dilution or another independent deprivation. Stone's Shaw rejects that cause of action outright: deliberate or predominant use of race in districting does not, standing alone, state a claim. Racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership, or another independently cognizable deprivation remains actionable, but design alone does not.

Because this simulation is causal, every later historical case that exists only as downstream litigation of the historical Shaw cause of action loses its basis for existing in this timeline. **This file is the checklist to consult when OT1994 through OT2001 come up for adjudication**, so the racial-gerrymandering line is not silently run as if historical Shaw had been decided the historical way. Before running any chunk containing one of the cases below, re-read this file and apply the treatment listed, not the historical posture.

**Deletion chain:** Hays → Miller → Shaw II/Pope → Vera → Abrams → Hunt → Easley. Not every case is causally dependent on every earlier one, but all seven historical merits proceedings depend materially on the historical Shaw cause of action Stone's Court rejected. No other case in the redistricting/voting-rights dossier needs to disappear on this basis: *Growe*, *Voinovich*, *Holder*, *De Grandy*, *SCLC*, *Morse*, *Bossier*, *Valdespino*, *Mixon*, and *Georgia v. Ashcroft* all arise through independent statutory or constitutional theories untouched by Shaw's design-only holding, and should be run on their own historical schedule and terms.

## Why these seven and no others

*Hays*: the Louisiana district court originally rejected the constitutional claims, then reopened them and invalidated the map only after historical Shaw gave it a basis to do so. Stone's Shaw supplies no such basis, so the reopening never happens and the case does not reach the Supreme Court in this posture.

*Miller*: the plaintiffs' January 1994 complaint alleged that Georgia's Eleventh District violated Equal Protection "as interpreted in Shaw." Under Stone's Shaw, that complaint is substantively foreclosed as pleaded.

*Shaw II / Pope*: exists only as the remand and second appeal following the historical Shaw I reversal. No Shaw I remand occurred here, so there is no trial and no second appeal to hear.

*Vera*: Texas's own jurisdictional statement confirms the new plaintiffs sued after Shaw and that by trial no vote-dilution claims remained — it was solely a Shaw Equal Protection challenge.

*Abrams*: explicitly the remedial sequel to Miller's invalidation of Georgia's Eleventh District; if Miller's historical disposition doesn't occur, there is no district to remedy.

*Hunt* and *Easley*: North Carolina's 1997 remedial plan and the resulting litigation exist only in response to Shaw II's invalidation of the prior map.

## Cases to delete outright

**United States v. Hays / Louisiana v. Hays.** Do not run, even as a reconstruction. It is the weakest candidate to preserve: the very judgment being appealed exists only because historical Shaw caused the district court to reverse its own earlier position, a sequence Stone's own opinion in the source material confirms. There is no non-artificial way to get this case to the Supreme Court under Stone's Shaw.

**Shaw v. Hunt / Pope v. Hunt ("Shaw II").** Delete; no substitute needed. Nothing survives to remand.

**Abrams v. Johnson.** Delete, unless a different, independently pleaded Georgia constitutional or statutory violation (not the Shaw design-only theory) produces a remedial map on its own terms — that would be a new, unrelated matter, not a reconstruction of Abrams.

**Hunt v. Cromartie / Easley v. Cromartie.** Delete both; no substitute needed.

## Cases worth keeping as reconstructed simulation grants

If reconstructed, treat these visibly as counterfactual simulated grants (a new cert grant or appeal, not a continuation of the historical docket), because their historical Supreme Court posture would not naturally occur under Stone's Shaw. Each gets a new Question Presented grounded in the actual historical filings, not invented from nothing.

### Miller v. Johnson — merits after a developed record

Historically, the plaintiffs alleged exactly the theory Stone's Shaw rejected, and by trial the evidence of racial predominance was overwhelming — the real Miller opinion called it essentially so. That makes Miller useful for a different reason than history used it: it lets Stone confirm his Shaw rule against a fully developed factual record, not just at the pleading stage.

**Question Presented:** Whether a congressional district violates the Equal Protection Clause solely because race was the predominant factor in assigning voters and traditional districting considerations were subordinated to racial objectives, where the plaintiffs allege no racial exclusion, intentional vote dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership, or other independently cognizable constitutional deprivation.

**Doctrinal job:** Shaw stated the rule at pleading; Miller confirms it holds even when race is proven, overwhelmingly, to have predominated. Stone's expected line: proof that race predominated is not itself the missing deprivation his Shaw already requires. This is the strongest confirmation available for the durability of Stone's Shaw rule, and the recommended case to actually run if only one of the two reconstructions is used.

### Bush v. Vera — jurisdiction and personal injury

Texas's own jurisdictional statement (Question 6) already asked whether standing existed where plaintiffs did not allege vote dilution, were not subjected to invidious discrimination, and identified only an interest in living under a map drawn without race consciousness — a question nearly tailor-made for Stone's Shaw and drawn directly from the real filing, not invented.

**Question Presented:** Whether voters who allege no dilution of their votes, exclusion from political participation, discriminatory electoral treatment, or other concrete personal inequality have Article III standing to challenge congressional districts solely because the legislature consciously used race in constructing them.

**Alternative Question (if a second angle is wanted):** Whether, after Shaw holds that race-conscious territorial design alone is not an Equal Protection deprivation, the existence of unusually shaped minority-opportunity districts can itself supply the individualized constitutional injury necessary to maintain an Equal Protection challenge.

**Doctrinal job:** a standing/injury clarification case rather than another predominance case. The real Texas filing is unusually favorable to Stone's position: Texas emphasized that plaintiffs did not claim vote dilution and quoted testimony suggesting some objections were theoretical or based on bare opposition to any governmental consideration of race.

## Recommended path

Delete Hays, Shaw II/Pope, Abrams, Hunt, and Easley outright — no substitute case needed for any of them. Keep Miller and Vera only if a deliberate two-case reconstructed arc is wanted; they do different jobs (Miller: merits after proof; Vera: jurisdiction/personal injury) and together confirm Stone's Shaw rule without secretly recreating the historical Shaw doctrine it replaced. If only one is used, Miller is the stronger and more natural choice.
