# Stone tracker: commerce power, commandeering and state immunity

Scope: the commerce power and the Necessary and Proper Clause (with the treaty power), commandeering and spending conditions, state sovereign immunity, and Congress's power under §5 of the Fourteenth Amendment as it bears on immunity, with Fifteenth Amendment enforcement where it meets §5. The working foundation also addresses preemption and the dormant Commerce Clause; the numbered case tracker retains its commerce, commandeering, spending, immunity and enforcement focus.

Organization:

- Working foundation for future cases.
- Completed cases.
- Reserved landmark cases, with their historical questions and blank fields for Stone's disposition and reasoning.

## Working foundation for future cases

This section states Stone's working positions, their limits and their application to hypothetical disputes. It does not itself establish Court law or decide a reserved case. Completed holdings and approved case-specific positions retain their authority; a proposed extension must be identified when it changes the governing rule.

Apply the law, record and knowledge available at the date of the case. Later developments do not supply facts or authority to an earlier proceeding.

### Central principle

Federalism distributes public authority so that government can protect liberty, equal citizenship and democratic self-government while remaining answerable for its choices. National authority permits effective action within the Constitution's grants; state authority preserves independent political institutions and room for lawful local decisions. Neither level of government receives a license to deny constitutional protection. The existence of a serious problem does not itself supply federal power, and the label of state sovereignty does not defeat an otherwise valid federal obligation.

Article I and Section 5 of the Fourteenth Amendment perform distinct work. Article I supports legislation within its enumerated powers and appropriate enforcement of the duties that legislation creates. Section 5 supports prevention and correction of constitutional injury, including fitted statutory protections that extend beyond what an individual could obtain directly under the Constitution. Either source may independently support a duty and its enforcement. Both may support the same law for different reasons. Each invoked source must be explained on its own terms; two incomplete theories do not become sufficient merely by being combined.

There is no general preference for a narrow or broad holding, a categorical rule or a standard, or one interpretive method. The holding should have the reach justified by the legal principle and the properly presented dispute. A recurring denial of rights may require a general rule. A materially different factual setting may require a defined distinction. The opinion should explain the choice and, when methods are combined, identify which method decides the disputed question.

### The decision sequence

These are organizing questions, not a new universal constitutional test or a requirement to produce a special factual record in every case.

1. **Identify the dispute.** Fix the relevant date, parties, claims, requested relief, procedural posture and questions properly presented. Determine jurisdiction and applicable threshold rules without assuming the answer to a merits-dependent question.
2. **Identify the actual duty.** State what conduct is required, prohibited, permitted or made actionable, who bears the obligation, and whether the challenged measure regulates conduct, conditions assistance or assigns governmental work.
3. **Identify the sources.** Separate constitutional power, statutory authorization and any valid implementing regulation. Distinguish commerce, taxation, spending, Reconstruction enforcement and other powers. Identify each independent ground properly before the Court.
4. **Explain the relevant connection.** Identify the interstate mechanism, constitutional injury and preventive relationship, actual federal-state conflict or other relationship required by the governing doctrine. Importance, institutional labels and statutory titles cannot substitute for that explanation.
5. **Identify material facts and the governing burden.** Distinguish legal interpretation, legislative and predictive judgments, and disputed facts about the parties. Use the burdens the applicable law supplies; do not create an additional proof requirement by calling it a method of review.
6. **Examine operation where it matters.** Determine who controls implementation, who bears costs, whether participation is genuinely voluntary, what conduct or harm crosses state boundaries, and which practical alternatives are relevant under the applicable rule.
7. **Treat precedent expressly.** Apply, distinguish, extend, narrow or propose revising the actual holding. A change in a legal trigger, burden or remedy must be identified as a change, rather than concealed as factual application.
8. **Answer the presented legal questions.** State the governing rule, its reach and its application. Where both Article I and Section 5 are properly presented, independently support the challenged duty and enforcement, and materially answer the disputed coverage, relief or legal rationale, explain both grounds. An adequate first ground creates no automatic prohibition on deciding the second, and the existence of a second power creates no obligation to decide a question outside the dispute.
9. **Determine relief independently.** Identify the cause of action, forum, defendant, remedy, prerequisites and practical correction of the established violation. A remand must state the governing law and identify the factual or remedial work remaining.
10. **Define genuine boundaries.** A matter outside the question presented, an unpreserved claim or a missing material fact may limit adjudication. Do not describe a resolved legal issue as open merely because its application requires factfinding, or use a boundary to postpone answering a presented question that the record permits the Court to decide.

### Methods and evidence

Method follows the question. Constitutional text, statutory text and controlling holdings identify the authority asserted and the obligation enacted. The actual interstate mechanism decides a disputed commerce connection; institutional experience and functional consequences help establish that mechanism and assess regulatory design. The protection of accountable political choice decides an impermissible assignment of state governmental authority; workload, control and attribution explain how the arrangement operates. The constitutional right and the injury being prevented decide the direction of Section 5 analysis; history, institutional practice and the fit of the statutory response establish whether the legislation enforces that right. Spending questions turn on the enacted offer and the practical reality of the choice. Amenability to suit turns on valid power and unmistakable authorization, while liability and relief retain their own statutory and constitutional requirements.

Rules are appropriate when defined triggering facts reliably identify a recurring constitutional wrong or when discretionary exceptions would defeat the protection. Standards are appropriate when material differences in function, need or burden determine whether the same protected interest is impaired. A standard must identify what matters, who must establish it and what follows if the burden is not met. A presumption, exception or safe harbor requires a stated legal reason and defined operation. Conduct outside a safe harbor is not unlawful merely because the harbor does not cover it.

Tiered scrutiny applies when the underlying constitutional right or classification calls for it; it is not a general test for commerce authority or congressional enforcement. Balancing and multifactor standards require legally relevant considerations and an explanation of their weight, rather than an unlimited comparison of desirable outcomes. A burden-shifting framework identifies its trigger, the explanation or proof required in response, and the consequence of failure; factual disparity does not by itself create a new burden or prove discriminatory intent. State discretion remains meaningful over lawful implementation where federal law permits choices, including effective alternatives, but cannot excuse denial of a protected right. History and tradition illuminate the allocation of power and the mechanisms of exclusion; a tradition maintained by excluding people may explain the injury rather than justify it.

Evidence includes testimony, documents, official directives, admissions, contemporaneous accounts, history, observed practices, comparisons, qualitative material, statistical information and reasonable circumstantial inference. Its weight depends on reliability and relevance to the legal proposition. The absence of statistics does not make other evidence deficient. The presence of statistics does not cure a missing constitutional connection or establish that a particular person suffered a violation.

Congress receives substantial latitude over reasonably supported legislative and predictive judgments within its authority. Courts independently determine constitutional boundaries and assess whether the explanation addresses the enacted measure. Legislative judgment need not reproduce a trial verdict or a catalogue of prior judicial findings. Agencies receive the technical latitude their lawful authority and governing standard permit. None of these principles requires scientific certainty, formal findings, a prescribed number of incidents, proof that every state has failed, or rejection of every imaginable alternative.

### Commerce and Necessary and Proper authority

#### Connection before magnitude

Congress may regulate interstate commerce and protect its channels, instrumentalities, persons and things. Outside those categories, identify the regulated class and a constitutionally sufficient interstate mechanism before using aggregation to measure its significance.

The principal mechanisms include participation in or direct interference with an actual interstate market, prevention of evasion or material impairment of a valid interstate scheme, and substantial interstate spillovers that individual states cannot reasonably confine or internalize. Explain the practical relationship between separate state decisions and the interstate problem. An unsupported assertion that national uniformity would be convenient is insufficient. A supported account of cross-border costs, incompatible regulation, evasion or impairment can establish the connection without proof that Congress first tried every possible state arrangement.

Ecological, public-health and comparable harms may supply the spillover connection when their substantial interstate operation is established and the response reasonably fits that operation. A widespread problem, physical boundary crossing by itself, or a chain through general crime, health, education, productivity or public-expenditure costs does not create a federal police power. An institution's designation as a court, prison or licensing authority likewise does not create a categorical exemption from otherwise valid federal regulation. The actual duty and its constitutional basis control.

Class-level evidence is appropriate for cumulative phenomena. Each person, emission or transaction need not independently cause substantial interstate harm. Once a valid connection exists, Congress has substantial room to address the class effectively, including predictable evasion and cumulative effects. An enacted jurisdictional element must nevertheless be proved when the statute requires it; a court cannot add an omitted element to rescue a conviction.

The combination is constitutional interpretation and functional assessment of the interstate mechanism. The existence of a legally sufficient connection is decisive. The magnitude of the problem and Congress's institutional competence inform the permissible design after that connection is established.

#### Local conduct and market entry

Personal use creates no automatic exemption. Inclusion of a defined local class requires a supported explanation that excluding that class would permit evasion or materially impair an already valid interstate regime. The government need not establish that this individual alone could defeat the scheme. Calling a scheme comprehensive does not establish the necessary relationship.

Compulsory market entry must be evaluated through the particular obligation, source of authority and supported mechanism. Eventual participation alone is insufficient. Practically inevitable participation, uncompensated cost shifting and the relationship of the specific financing obligation to the regulated activity may be material; they must perform actual explanatory work rather than merely restate the desired result. Taxing authority remains a separate ground with its own requirements.

Necessary and Proper authority requires a valid constitutional end and appropriate means. The relationship between the means and that end must be identified, including any material effect on the constitutional independence of another institution. The inquiry is not a demand for the least restrictive conceivable design. Applications beyond commerce, and the outer scope of treaty implementation, remain source-specific questions to be resolved when properly presented.

**Article I alone — hypothetical.** A state operates a parcel-delivery service that carries goods between states. It withholds overtime that a federal wage law requires, and Congress expressly authorizes covered workers to sue state employers. Regulation of this economic activity and its employment obligations falls within the commerce power, and the ordinary wage remedy enforces that valid duty. The workers' action proceeds despite the State's immunity objection. They must establish the statutory wage claim, but they need not prove unconstitutional discrimination or some other Fourteenth Amendment violation. Article I supplies the sufficient basis; the facts establish no separate Section 5 justification.

### State institutions and federal administration

Distinguish the state as employer, proprietor, records custodian, legislature, executive administrator and court. The same employee or institution can act in more than one capacity. These roles help identify the obligation; they are not substitutes for analyzing its constitutional basis.

Under ordinary commerce authority, Congress cannot command a state legislature to enact federal policy or assign unwilling state executives a new investigative or enforcement mission. Direct regulation of the state's own conduct is a separate question. State courts' obligations under valid federal law, and duties independently supported by the Reconstruction Amendments, require their own analysis. Requiring a public institution to stop violating a valid federal protection is not automatically equivalent to conscripting it to regulate unrelated private persons.

Identify who makes the governing policy, exercises coercive authority, bears the operational burden and remains answerable to the affected public. Federal officials cannot obtain the benefits of a national program while transferring its political responsibility to state officials. Conversely, every administrative connection between governments does not destroy state independence. Consider the actual alternative arrangements where relevant, including whether federal administration would displace more state discretion. A speculative threat of greater federal intervention does not validate an otherwise forbidden command.

#### Limited records assistance

A legally authorized, bounded request through an existing institutional records function is permissible when:

- The obligation serves a valid federal responsibility and rests on an actual statute.
- The task concerns information already lawfully maintained and a defined matter or transaction.
- The requested performance is an existing routine task. A new performance may qualify; the matter need not already be a filed lawsuit or criminal case.
- The task does not require new investigation, fact gathering, systems creation or policymaking.
- Federal payment covers attributable assistance costs unless the state genuinely agrees to contribute.
- Federal responsibility for the governing policy and coercive enforcement remains identifiable.

Examine cumulative workload, interference with state priorities, operational control, information safeguards and the ability to challenge excessive requests. Reimbursement does not itself cure institutional conscription. Recurring volume supplies no fixed numerical threshold; the question is whether the arrangement becomes an ongoing federal assignment. No universal workload register, alternatives study or reporting system is required to establish the answer.

The reimbursement principle concerns assistance with federal administration. It does not require federal payment of every cost a state incurs complying with valid regulation of its own conduct.

**Records assistance — hypothetical.** A federal agency administering a valid interstate licensing program asks a state clerk to transmit an existing license record through the clerk's ordinary process. The statute authorizes the request, the federal government pays the attributable cost, lawful information safeguards apply, and federal officers make and enforce the federal decision. The bounded request is permissible. Requiring the same clerk to interview neighbors, investigate suspected violations and maintain a new federal monitoring system would exceed this exception; payment alone would not make that new mission permissible.

### Cooperation and spending

Examine the actual offer, including money, equipment, services and materially equivalent assistance. Identify the legal authority for each form of assistance. Conditions must be authorized, clear, related to the program, consistent with independent constitutional protections and noncoercive. Neither formal agreement nor financial dependence is conclusive.

A state may decline a genuine offer and forgo its associated benefits. There is no automatic entitlement to replacement assistance. Earlier refusal alone does not justify permanent exclusion from a later program; later eligibility follows then-current lawful rules, including justified requirements concerning capacity or past misconduct.

Congress may revise future programs. Evaluate changes to established arrangements through their legal terms, purpose, reliance, magnitude and timing of threatened losses, institutional effects and realistic ability to decline. Losing a new benefit differs from losing independent existing assistance to secure an unrelated demand. The decisive inquiry is whether the enacted arrangement offers a meaningful, constitutionally permissible choice; the practical facts explain the answer.

No fixed funding percentage determines coercion. Fiscal difficulty does not automatically establish compulsion, and the government's label does not establish genuine choice. A lawful appropriation and reasonable notice do not excuse an independently unconstitutional condition. Any additional presumption that changes the governing test must be identified and justified as a doctrinal change.

**Program choice — hypothetical.** Congress offers a new grant for state emergency radios on clear terms requiring the funded radios to communicate with a national emergency network. A state can decline while retaining its independent existing assistance. The condition is permissible because it concerns the funded program and leaves a real choice. If refusal instead triggers immediate loss of unrelated established hospital funding, that added condition fails the required connection to the radio program and cannot be enforced on the stated facts. The hospital program's independent lawful conditions remain in force. Reliance and realistic ability to decline would also matter to a coercion claim, but no numerical funding threshold is needed to decide the identified lack of connection.

### State accountability and judicial access

Unmistakably authorized private suits against states are permissible when both the underlying duty and its enforcement fall within the invoked federal power. Florida Prepaid and Kimel supply the Article I foundation at the scope of their holdings. Alden supplies the stated state-court FLSA application. From February 21, 2001, Garrett independently sustains the presented Title I employment actions under Article I and Section 5 without deciding liability, individual awards or Title II. Further applications retain their statute, forum and remedy questions. Clear authorization and a valid duty do not establish liability or authorize every form of relief.

Separate the existence of a duty, cause of action, jurisdiction, proper defendant, immunity, procedure, liability and remedy. State status does not establish immunity for every related entity. Actual waiver, funding consent and prospective officer relief retain their distinct requirements. Ordinary participation in a regulated activity is not itself consent. Officer relief cannot be used as a label for unauthorized retrospective treasury liability.

Where valid power and unmistakable authorization establish amenability, abstract state dignity or the ordinary cost of compliance cannot recreate the rejected immunity. The claimant must still prove the statutory elements and satisfy the conditions of the requested remedy. Fiscal consequences matter only through a legally relevant remedial or constitutional rule, not as a general excuse to leave an established federal violation unenforceable.

#### Notice and correction

Mandatory pre-suit notice requires lawful legislative authorization. Existing access remains where applicable law imposes no such prerequisite. There is no universal county-to-state-to-federal exhaustion ladder.

An authorized notice process should principally address nonurgent, ongoing, correctable statutory violations. Notice should be accessible and factual, have a defined endpoint, recognize adequate prior complaints, protect against deadline forfeiture and permit early access after definitive refusal or inability to correct. Urgent serious harm requires preservation of legally available immediate protection.

Good-faith but unsuccessful efforts cannot postpone enforcement indefinitely. Nonresponse permits proceeding when the applicable requirements are met; it does not establish liability. Correcting a practice does not automatically resolve an independently actionable past injury or a systemic pattern. These principles guide lawful design and interpretation; they do not authorize courts to invent notice, exhaustion, exceptions or tolling contrary to an existing statute.

#### Reconstruction enforcement

Section 5 protects the practical ability to enjoy the Fourteenth Amendment's guarantees. Its inquiry begins with the constitutional interest and the conduct or institutional practice that threatens it. Equal citizenship includes meaningful access to legal protection; formally neutral procedures may fail that guarantee when their operation sustains an unconstitutional exclusion. History and experience can reveal the mechanism of injury, but neither a historical label nor a present disparity alone establishes every required constitutional element.

Congress may act on evidence of official practices, legally operative customs, recurring exclusion, concealed discrimination and deficiencies in the protection of constitutional rights. Testimony and documented experiences may establish matters that a statistical study would miss. Evidence concerning private or local conduct may illuminate a broader mechanism, but the explanation must connect it to the constitutional injury and the governmental duties the challenged measure actually addresses. There is no mandatory incident count or requirement that each state first be adjudged a violator.

Under the governing Fourteenth Amendment framework, preventive and remedial legislation must be congruent and proportional to the constitutional injury. Congress may reach some conduct beyond the judicially enforceable constitutional minimum without redefining that minimum. Identify the protected right, explain how the statutory duties prevent or correct its violation, and assess the fit of the law's actual coverage, defenses and enforcement. These are substantive relationships, not demands for a particular legislative format or a court-designed model statute.

The degree and character of constitutional protection inform the inquiry. A classification receiving deferential constitutional review may still be addressed by supported preventive legislation; a failure to accommodate is not automatically a constitutional violation, but neither is it automatically beyond preventive enforcement. A right receiving stronger protection may justify a different statutory response. The opinion must explain the relevant injury and fit rather than convert the level of scrutiny into a mechanical tally of incidents.

Congress's constitutional judgment deserves serious consideration because legislation can identify institutional problems and provide preventive measures that individual litigation cannot adequately supply. Courts retain responsibility for the constitutional boundary. Legislative protection may exceed the minimum remedy available in a direct constitutional suit; legislative approval cannot authorize a constitutional violation or reduce an existing constitutional protection. When the court concludes that the existing constitutional rule itself should change, that change must be defended expressly.

Once a preventive statutory category is valid, the claimant proves its statutory elements. No additional personal constitutional violation, unsuccessful constitutional lawsuit or special evidentiary showing may be imposed unless the applicable law actually requires it. The statute's own defenses remain available. The Fifteenth Amendment's relationship to the Fourteenth Amendment framework remains a distinct question to be decided in an appropriate dispute.

**Section 5 alone — hypothetical.** State courts repeatedly refuse to hear women's property claims solely because the claimants are women. Congress responds with a law forbidding that exclusion, requiring equal filing access and expressly authorizing suits against the responsible states. Testimony, rejected filings and official instructions establish the practice. The law validly enforces equal protection and access to adjudication; the exclusion must stop, and the authorized claims for resulting injury may proceed under the statute's remedial terms. No interstate market or other Article I connection supplies the ground in this example. The judicial character of the institution does not place the discriminatory practice beyond Section 5.

#### Independent and combined grounds

Article I and Section 5 should be analyzed separately before explaining their combined operation. Article I asks whether the enacted obligation and its enforcement fall within an enumerated power. Section 5 asks whether the measure appropriately prevents or remedies injury to a Fourteenth Amendment right. The facts relevant to one may also support the other, but their legal work differs.

A complete Article I ground may resolve a dispute when no material Section 5 question requires decision; a complete Section 5 ground may do so without manufacturing an Article I connection. When both grounds are properly presented, independently supported and material to disputed coverage, relief or the legal rationale, the holding should state what each independently sustains. Success under Article I creates no automatic reason to avoid a material Section 5 question. In the Article I analysis, the valid enumerated-power connection is decisive. In the Section 5 analysis, the constitutional injury and preventive or remedial fit are decisive. Neither ground serves merely as an unexplained backup or supplies missing elements of the other. If one fails, the court must determine whether the other independently sustains the actual application rather than assume that both succeed or fall together.

Different provisions or applications of one statute may rest on different powers. Article I may support regulation of both public and private employment, while Section 5 independently supports the relevant state applications. Each challenged component must have sufficient authority for the work it performs; every component need not satisfy both powers.

A combined holding does not double liability, erase statutory defenses, authorize an unprovided remedy or require a claimant to prove two causes of action to obtain one statutory remedy. It also does not decide every application of a different title or statute. Its generality follows the demonstrated legal reasons, not an automatic preference for preserving future questions.

**Both — hypothetical.** A state hospital hires nurses in an interstate employment market but rejects a qualified deaf nurse because of an unsupported assumption that deaf people cannot work safely, although visual alerts would enable her to perform the required tasks. Congress has enacted an expressly enforceable employment law requiring reasonable accommodation, with qualifications and undue-hardship defenses, supported by testimony and records of comparable exclusion by state employers. Those materials show repeated arbitrary exclusions and barriers to proving their true grounds; the qualification and undue-hardship limits keep the response directed to that problem. Article I independently supports regulation of the employment relationship and its private enforcement. Section 5 independently supports the bounded protection against unconstitutional stereotyping and its recurrence. The nurse's statutory claim proceeds on both grounds; she proves the applicable statutory elements, and the hospital may establish an actual statutory defense. She need not prove an additional constitutional tort, and she receives no duplicate recovery because two constitutional powers sustain the same protection.

### Preemption

Concurrent operation is the starting point in traditional state fields. Valid federal authority does not establish exclusive control over every related subject.

#### Grounds and limits

- **Express preemption:** Apply the enacted text, definitions, scope and savings provisions.
- **Impossibility:** Compare the specific duties governing the same conduct at the relevant time.
- **Obstacle preemption:** Identify the operative federal arrangement and the state duty that defeats it. Abstract uniformity, regulatory silence and an agency's preference are insufficient.
- **Field preemption:** Establish Congress's exclusive allocation of the particular subject. Regulatory detail does not prove occupation of every related field, including compensation.

An agency's interpretation carries weight only within delegated authority and insofar as its reasoning establishes the relevant legal conflict.

#### Market withdrawal and compensation

The possibility of ceasing an activity does not by itself reconcile otherwise incompatible duties. Federal permission to conduct the activity does not by itself establish immunity from state liability.

Determine whether federal law prohibits a change, requires prior approval, permits a relevant unilateral change, or merely approves the existing product. Distinguish an actually available compliance path from a speculative earlier opportunity.

A damages claim may impose a substantive requirement and can be preempted. Calling it compensation does not insulate it. Conversely, expense or pressure from litigation does not by itself establish a forbidden technical requirement. Identify the duty the claim would enforce.

Loss of the only otherwise viable compensation remedy weighs strongly against an expansive inference of displacement where interpretation remains uncertain. It cannot defeat clear valid displacement or make genuinely incompatible duties compatible. A request for particularly convincing reasoning must not silently become a new evidentiary burden or universal express-statement requirement.

Review claims separately. Displacement of one design theory does not automatically extinguish distinct manufacturing, fraud, reporting or other duties; each surviving theory must still satisfy its own law.

### Dormant Commerce Clause

The doctrine protects interstate commerce against protectionism and unjustified barriers while preserving meaningful state regulatory authority. It does not require maximum economic efficiency or national uniformity.

#### Discrimination

Examine purpose, text, exemptions, enforcement and actual operation. Formal equality does not cure disguised local favoritism. The fact that most affected firms are elsewhere does not, alone, establish discrimination.

Established discrimination requires a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve. Apply the governing burden without inventing perfect-certainty or every-imaginable-alternative requirements.

#### Market access and conduct elsewhere

A state ordinarily may regulate the terms of sales within its territory, including relevant production practices. Health, safety, environmental protection and animal welfare can support such conditions. An identical finished product does not negate a legitimate transaction-connected interest.

Distinguish conditions on goods actually sold in the state from commands governing unrelated production or business elsewhere. Market participation supplies a connection, not unlimited jurisdiction over the entire producer.

Separate ordinary compliance costs, broader economic effects and demonstrated serious interference with interstate operations. National conformity chosen for commercial convenience is not automatically equivalent to unavoidable legal compulsion. Conflicting requirements must be shown, not assumed from a hypothetical fifty-state patchwork.

#### Neutral burdens and Pike

Pike subjects evenhanded laws to review for interstate burdens clearly excessive relative to local benefits. Stone favors restrained judicial evaluation of legitimate political values and serious examination of factual claims, protectionism, operational incompatibility and market disruption.

Nonmonetary interests do not become valueless because they cannot be priced. Evidence may test causal claims without deciding the moral value of the objective. Practicable, comparably effective alternatives can matter; judges should not redefine the state's objective to make an alternative appear equivalent.

**Continuity limit:** A categorical requirement of a separate constitutional violation, a new heightened proof quantum, or review limited exclusively to operational incompatibility would change the inherited Pike framework. Such a rule requires an express proposal under the precedent method. The power to invalidate a genuinely neutral law under Pike remains. A narrower structural rule remains a proposed direction unless expressly adopted.

#### Public activity, taxation and congressional authorization

Government purchasing is confined to the service or transaction actually bought. Public ownership does not make every independent private transaction a government purchase. Genuine public-service arrangements require examination of actual ownership, control, financial responsibility, revenues and concealed favoritism; public status does not create blanket immunity.

State taxation retains nexus, apportionment, nondiscrimination and service-relationship requirements. Their specialized applications require the pertinent tax precedents, including any governing collection rules.

Congress may unmistakably authorize a particular departure from dormant-commerce restrictions. Other constitutional limitations remain. Protection under a market-participant rule does not immunize a state measure from valid federal preemption.

### Precedent and effective relief

Stone starts with the prior holding, legal foundation, factual premises, subsequent treatment, workability and concrete reliance. A different preferred outcome or changed Court membership is insufficient.

Legally justified change may respond to conflict with superior law, internal contradiction, unworkability, a failed factual premise or systematic defeat of the doctrine's protection. Statutory precedent ordinarily receives added weight. Reliance on equal standing, access and procedural protections receives real protection; an expectation of another person's continued exclusion does not receive equivalent weight.

Every proposed change identifies the prior rule, legal reason, new boundary, unaffected law and treatment of reliance. Remedy remains separate.

Relief must have a lawful source and correct the established violation effectively. An injunction specifies the defendant's required conduct and preserves lawful alternatives. Structural obligations require a demonstrated connection to the violation, administrable measures and conditions for ending supervision. Broader relief requires its own lawful basis.

Compensation, restitution, punitive relief, fees, interest and sanctions have distinct sources and elements. Neither liability nor serious injury supplies every remedy. Whether to adopt a general presumption against treasury punitive damages remains open; no automatic rule applies across every statute.

#### Discriminatory tax benefits

For future periods, lawful correction may extend a benefit, withdraw it or establish another neutral arrangement. Severability, enacted structure, reliance and institutional authority govern the choice.

Prospective change does not itself discharge an established entitlement to correction for past discriminatory collections. Where governing law requires backward-looking relief, assess an authorized refund, constitutionally lawful and effective retrospective equalization, or a valid combination. Preserve lawful procedural conditions and reject unfair withdrawal of a previously held-out remedy.

Do not assume every taxpayer is entitled to a refund regardless of the available process. Do not presume interest, consequential damages, lost profits or a federal forum. Fiscal and administrative concerns can affect lawful implementation without eliminating a remedy the law requires. Economic incidence does not by itself authorize withholding required correction.

Stone's exemption preference in Camps was case-specific. Prospective withdrawal remains available in other cases where lawful, without changing that case-specific position or the Court's judgment.

### Applications in difficult cases

These hypothetical applications assume a proper judicial vehicle and the facts expressly stated. They illustrate the rules and their limits without deciding reserved cases. Translate a merits conclusion into affirmance, reversal, vacatur or remand only after identifying the lower court's judgment.

#### 1 Interstate pollution with valid statutory authority

**Application:** A supported national rule addressing substantial cross-border harm within the agency's actual delegation ordinarily survives the power challenge.

**Reasoning and precedent:** Apply the connection and spillover analysis. The strongest objection is a mismatch between the established mechanism and the particular regulatory instrument, not the absence of individual source-to-victim tracing.

**Relief and changing facts:** Reject the presented power challenge if its premises fail. A missing statutory authorization can instead require setting aside the agency measure on statutory grounds. Resolve that ground without unnecessarily deciding broader constitutional authority.

#### 2 Personal production within a valid national market regime

**Application:** A defined personal-use class may be included where its exclusion would materially enable evasion.

**Reasoning and precedent:** Apply anti-evasion authority; neither personal use nor comprehensive coverage is conclusive. The strongest objection is that the exclusion would not materially impair the regime.

**Relief and changing facts:** Sustain only the supported coverage. Where material facts are unresolved, use the governing burden and posture. Do not invent a defendant-specific element or remand to prove an element Congress omitted.

#### 3 Advance financing for a practically inevitable market

**Application:** The possibility remains open; the particular obligation and record determine the outcome.

**Reasoning and precedent:** Require the specific connection among practically inevitable participation, uncompensated cost shifting and the demanded obligation. The strongest objection is that financing necessity is being used to manufacture commerce authority.

**Relief and changing facts:** Evaluate any independently presented tax ground separately. If the obligation fails, severability and the actual statutory arrangement determine the consequence; do not presume the entire program falls.

#### 4 Paid records requests become a federal operation

**Application:** The bounded existing-record task may proceed. Demands controlling staffing, priorities or new investigations require separate scrutiny and cannot rely on the limited-assistance exception alone.

**Reasoning and precedent:** Apply the limited-assistance holding while identifying any extension beyond its temporary scope. Payment answers fiscal responsibility, not the whole institutional question.

**Relief and changing facts:** Constrain the unlawful assignment through the authorized claim and remedy, preserving severable lawful requests. A genuine voluntary agreement or an independently valid constitutional source can alter the analysis.

#### 5 Existing health funding threatened over an unrelated policy

**Application:** An established unrelated demand cannot be justified merely by threatening independent benefits; authority, relatedness and coercion require separate examination.

**Reasoning and precedent:** Examine statutory authority, relatedness and coercion. The strongest defense would show a genuine program connection or a lawful general revision rather than unrelated leverage.

**Relief and changing facts:** Prevent the unlawful condition or withholding where relief is authorized. Do not create a perpetual funding entitlement, appropriation or replacement program.

#### 6 Authorized state suit after unsuccessful correction

**Application:** Permit the action to proceed once a lawful correction period ends; allow legally available urgent protection where necessary.

**Reasoning and precedent:** Apply the valid-duty, valid-enforcement and unmistakable-authorization framework. State effort does not decide whether the violation persists. The strongest defense may concern the underlying duty, procedural compliance or remedy authorization.

**Relief and changing facts:** Determine liability normally. Correct ongoing violations and award monetary relief only through a valid source. Successful correction may narrow prospective relief without eliminating independently actionable past harm.

#### 7 Preventive civil-rights protection without individual constitutional proof

**Application:** Support statutory enforcement if the preventive category is valid under the applicable Reconstruction power.

**Reasoning and precedent:** Apply the governing fit standard to the category, then apply its enacted elements. The strongest objection challenges the category's scope or connection to constitutional injury.

**Relief and changing facts:** Award only authorized relief against a proper defendant. If category validity fails, separately assess any surviving direct constitutional or statutory claim rather than assuming all protection disappears.

#### 8 Federal product approval and an otherwise viable injury claim

**Application:** Approval, abstract uniformity or litigation expense alone does not establish preemption, particularly where displacement would eliminate the only compensation route.

**Reasoning and precedent:** Compare actual duties under Myrick, Geier and the applicable statute. The strongest defense identifies an affirmative protected federal choice, occupied subject or genuine impossibility.

**Relief and changing facts:** Allow a nonconflicting claim to proceed without deciding negligence or damages. An operative federal requirement forbidding the conduct demanded by the claim can reverse the preemption conclusion.

#### 9 A state duty demands a federally forbidden product change

**Application:** The conflicting state duty is displaced if the legal conflict is genuine and no lawful path satisfies that duty.

**Reasoning and precedent:** Market exit alone does not establish compatibility. Calling the award compensatory does not remove its substantive duty. The strongest objection identifies a genuinely independent claim or available compliance route.

**Relief and changing facts:** Resolve the conflicting theory narrowly and assess other claims separately. A complete compensation gap is acknowledged, not cured by inventing a cause of action.

#### 10 Evenhanded animal-welfare conditions on in-state sales

**Application:** The condition ordinarily survives where the transaction connection is credible and the claimed burden consists of ordinary compliance expense.

**Reasoning and precedent:** No automatic invalidity follows from out-of-state production or nonmonetary benefits. Apply inherited discrimination and Pike rules, distinguishing any proposed refinement.

**Relief and changing facts:** Reject the presented categorical challenge. Local favoritism, control over unrelated operations or demonstrated serious interstate interference can require further proceedings or a different outcome. A failed challenge does not validate every application.

#### 11 Incompatible state requirements for continuous interstate transport

**Application:** The outcome depends on actual incompatibility, justification and feasible accommodations; a demonstrated barrier to continuous interstate transport warrants close scrutiny.

**Reasoning and precedent:** Apply the relevant transportation and Pike authorities. A real barrier to continuous interstate movement differs from ordinary expense. The strongest defense establishes compatibility, a necessary safety justification or a workable accommodation.

**Relief and changing facts:** Address the proven barrier within the proper claim. Do not invent a national technical standard or automatically prefer one state's rule. Additional material facts can require remand.

#### 12 A neutral-looking certification rule favors local competitors

**Application:** Invalidate the discriminatory operation if the exemptions and actual operation establish protectionism without adequate justification.

**Reasoning and precedent:** Apply discrimination analysis rather than disguising the issue as ordinary neutral-cost balancing. The strongest defense establishes genuinely different regulatory circumstances and adequate nondiscriminatory treatment.

**Relief and changing facts:** Remove or prevent the discriminatory operation through lawful severability and relief. Do not presume damages, universal certification approval or invalidity of independent safety requirements.

#### 13 A discriminatory tax exemption is withdrawn prospectively

**Application:** Permit a genuinely lawful neutral future arrangement, while resolving any established entitlement to past correction separately.

**Reasoning and precedent:** Apply the governing tax-remedy and severability law. Camps does not create an automatic extension rule. The strongest dispute concerns the lawful process available to this taxpayer and whether the proposed retrospective correction actually equalizes treatment.

**Relief and changing facts:** Require the meaningful correction legally owed. A promised postpayment remedy cannot be unfairly withdrawn after payment. Limitations, lawful reliance protection and the feasibility of retrospective assessments can determine the available form.

### Reserved boundaries

The precise cumulative-assistance limit, difficult coercion applications, new forums and remedies against states, the outer reach of Necessary and Proper and treaty authority, specialized tax doctrines, and the exact scope of any proposed change to Pike remain for cases that properly present them.

A working position does not settle every application or authorize an unstated proof standard. Identify any genuinely new choice and its consequences before treating it as resolved.

### Reusable case assessment

For each case, identify:

1. The question, relevant date, posture and relief sought.
2. The governing law and Stone's position, with their distinct status.
3. The precise duty and legal source.
4. Material established facts, disputed facts and stated assumptions.
5. Stone's proposed disposition and principal ground.
6. The strongest opposing argument and the fact or legal premise that could change the result.
7. Whether precedent is applied, distinguished or proposed for revision, with the five required explanations for change.
8. The authorized remedy, its necessary scope, any remand and what lawful conduct remains available.
9. Any uncertainty affecting the reasoning, disposition or remedy.
10. Any genuinely unresolved choice requiring Stone's decision.

A case assessment supplies no missing vote, binds no other Justice, creates no case fact and amends no completed judgment.

## 1. Completed cases

OT1991
- Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission
  > Held (Kennedy for 5, 6-2): The Federal Employers' Liability Act's coverage of state-owned interstate railroads remains enforceable in a competent state court. The rule does not revive constructive waiver of immunity in federal court or set a general rule for every Article I damages action against a State.
- New York v. United States
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court; take-title provision 6-3, incentives 9-0): Congress may not use its commerce power to make an unwilling State choose between regulating private radioactive waste under federal instructions and taking title to it, so that provision is invalid and severed. The Act's monetary and access incentives are valid inducements.

OT1992
- Puerto Rico Aqueduct and Sewer Authority v. Metcalf & Eddy
  > Held (White for 7, 8-1): A State or a claimed arm of a State may immediately appeal a conclusive denial of Eleventh Amendment immunity from federal suit. Appealability does not establish that the claimant is immune.

OT1994
- Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp.
  > Held (Stevens for the Court, 6-3): A bistate compact entity shares the States' immunity only if its actual legal, financial and institutional relationship shows that the States made it their arm and Congress concurred; exposure of the state treasury is highly probative. The Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corporation, a financially self-supporting enterprise, lacks Eleventh Amendment immunity.
- United States v. Harris
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 9-0): Congress may protect an instrumentality of interstate commerce against a direct intrastate forcible taking when the offense requires proof of the vehicle's interstate connection, so the original carjacking statute is valid as applied. The Court did not adopt the lower court's aggregate-effects rationale.
- United States v. Lopez
  > Held (Stone for 5, 5-4): Connection before magnitude: for conduct that is not commerce or its channels, instrumentalities, persons or things, the Court first identifies the regulated class and its interstate mechanism, and only then may aggregation measure size. The school-zone gun possession ban fails, since chains through crime and education costs cannot supply the connection, and no commerce element will be read into it.
- United States v. Robertson
  > Held (Breyer for the Court, 9-0): An enterprise is engaged in interstate commerce under the federal racketeering statute, §1962(a), when it directly takes part in interstate acquisition, production or distribution of goods or services. This mine's interstate purchasing, recruiting and shipment of its own output qualified, without a separate showing that it substantially affected interstate commerce.

OT1995
- American Life League v. Reno
  > Held (Kennedy for the Court, 9-0 on the commerce question): Congress may regulate force, threats, obstruction and destruction intentionally aimed at and directly interfering with reproductive health services, because that conduct operates on an interstate medical-services market. The connection comes from that operation on the market, not from generalized health-care or crime costs.
- United States v. Bishop / United States v. Stokes
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 9-0 on the commerce question): Under Harris, the original carjacking statute validly applies to these armed vehicle takings as protection of an instrumentality of interstate commerce. The separate requirement that the vehicle itself have moved in interstate commerce remains necessary.
- United States v. Hale
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 9-0 on the commerce question): Congress may include possession in §922(o)'s ban on additions to the post-1986 civilian machinegun market, because excluding possession would let the banned stock be acquired, kept or circulated and materially impair the ban. No transaction-specific interstate element is required.
- Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida
  > Held (Souter for 5, 5-4; no majority rationale on State amenability): Ex parte Young permits prospective relief against the Governor for an ongoing violation of the gaming statute's good-faith negotiation duty, within the statute's limits. Florida's own dismissal was set aside 5-4 with no controlling rationale on whether it may be sued, and Union Gas was not overruled.
- Lane v. Peña
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 7-2): The Rehabilitation Act does not unequivocally waive the United States' immunity from compensatory damages for discrimination in a federally conducted program. The nondiscrimination duty does not itself supply consent to a money remedy.

OT1996
- Regents of the University of California v. Doe
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 9-0): A third party's promise to reimburse a state instrumentality's litigation costs or judgment does not strip it of Eleventh Amendment immunity. Arm-of-the-State status still turns on the instrumentality's full legal, financial and institutional relationship with the State.
- Pic-A-State Pennsylvania v. Reno
  > Held (Kennedy for the Court, 9-0): Congress may bar a business from transmitting information across state lines to buy chances in another State's lottery, because the transmission is itself interstate commerce. The agreement exception lets the States concerned permit the business and removes dormant Commerce Clause limits on that choice.
- Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe
  > Held (Stone for 5, 5-4): The Eleventh Amendment does not bar prospective relief against officials for an ongoing violation of federal tribal property rights merely because the case requires deciding a disputed title question or touches important sovereign interests. The decree may not bind the State's title, award retrospective compensation or require the State to convey title.
- City of Boerne v. Flores
  > Held (Kennedy for the Court, 6-3): Section 5 legislation may reach some conduct that is not itself unconstitutional, but its means must be congruent and proportional to the constitutional injury and may not replace the right's judicially enforceable content. RFRA's general application to state and local law fails that test, and the claim against the City is dismissed.
- Printz v. United States / Mack v. United States
  > Held (Souter for 5 on Part I; judgment 5-4 with no majority fiscal rationale): Congress is not categorically barred from requiring appropriate, temporary, records-based help within a state executive officer's proper function, where federal law supplies the rule and federal authorities keep coercive enforcement. The judgment was vacated and remanded on the officers' unresolved fiscal objections.
- United States v. Mussari / United States v. Schroeder
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 9-0): Congress may punish willful failure to pay a fixed past-due child-support obligation when obligor and child live in different States, because §228 directly protects that cross-border payment obligation. The statute commands the private obligor, not the State, so it does not commandeer.

OT1997
- United States v. Rybar
  > Held (Kennedy for the Court, 9-0 on the commerce question): Under Hale, §922(o) validly reaches Rybar's possession of two machineguns as an anti-evasion part of the ban on additions to the post-1986 civilian stock, without a transaction-specific interstate element.
- United States v. Kirk
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 9-0): Section 922(o) may reach possession of a post-1986 machinegun without proof of a defendant-specific interstate transaction, because the possession ban is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of the national restriction on that stock.
- United States v. Robinson
  > Held (Kennedy for the Court, 9-0): The Hobbs Act validly reaches robbery of an active interstate business's operating assets when the taking directly impairs its commercial operations and the Act's commerce element is proved. Aggregation measures the size of that connected class, but a business label or generalized costs of crime do not supply the connection.
- United States v. Hinds
  > Held (Stevens for the Court, 8-1): The arson statute, §844(i), requires that the damaged property's own use connect it to interstate commerce; the owner's affiliations, purchases or insurance do not establish that merely by existing. The affirmance was vacated for review of the existing trial record, with acquittal required if the proof falls short.
- United States v. Bird
  > Held (Kennedy for the Court, 9-0 on the commerce question): Congress may apply the clinic-access statute to intentional force and true threats aimed at stopping reproductive health services, because that conduct operates directly on access to an interstate service market. Neither the offender nor each clinic must separately cross a state line.
- National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 5-4): Noneconomic intrastate conduct may be regulated when needed to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid interstate regime (seven votes), or when a supported record shows a substantial interstate externality the States cannot confine (five votes). The projects' commercial features did not supply the connection for the Endangered Species Act's taking class, so the case was remanded to test the record.
- Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht
  > Held (Thomas for the Court, 9-0): A case presenting substantial federal claims is removable even though the Eleventh Amendment bars particular claims against the State or its officials. The bar blocks the affected claims but does not defeat jurisdiction over the rest or require remand of the whole action.

OT1998
- Hiley v. Barrett
  > Held (O'Connor for 8, 9-0): The ban on firearm possession by persons with qualifying domestic-violence misdemeanor convictions is a valid anti-evasion part of the federal ban on supplying firearms to them. It does not commandeer the sheriff, because it commands no state legislation, termination, prosecution, background checks or appropriation.
- Lopez v. Monterey County (1999) (Fifteenth Amendment enforcement)
  > Held (Thomas for the Court, 9-0): A county covered by Section 5 of the Voting Rights Act must obtain preclearance before administering a voting change, even one that a noncovered State requires and leaves it no discretion over. The Court adopted no separate constitutional justification for Section 5.
- South Central Bell Telephone Co. v. Alabama
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 9-0): The Eleventh Amendment does not bar this Court from reviewing federal questions decided by a State's own courts, which differs from a new damages suit against the State. The preclusion and Commerce Clause judgments were reversed and the case remanded for adequate state relief.
- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank
  > Held (Stevens for 5, 5-4): Congress may authorize private suits against States when the duty and the enforcement measure fall within an Article I power and the authorization is unmistakable, so the Patent Clause supports the patent remedy. Section 5 independently sustains it as congruent and proportional prevention of property deprivations.
- College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board
  > Held (Stone for the Court in part; reversed 5-4 with no majority rationale on amenability): Section 5 does not support the Lanham Act suit on the own-product false-advertising theory (eight votes), and a State's continued ordinary commerce is not consent to suit (five votes). Five Justices nonetheless reversed the immunity dismissal on differing grounds.
- Alden v. Maine
  > Held (Souter for 5, 5-4): Acting within its commerce power, Congress may displace a State's immunity from an expressly authorized private FLSA suit in the State's own competent court, which must hear it. The case was remanded for ordinary adjudication without any wage award.

OT1999
- Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents
  > Held (Ginsburg for 5, 5-4): The ADEA unmistakably authorizes private suits against state employers (seven votes), and Article I supports that suit because both the age-employment duty and its enforcement fall within Congress's power. The categorical immunity dismissal was reversed; whether the ADEA is valid §5 legislation was reserved.
- Reno v. Condon
  > Held (Scalia for the Court, 9-0): Congress may regulate disclosure of state motor-vehicle records because they move in an interstate information market, and a valid law may directly regulate a State's own handling of that data without commandeering. Whether such a law must also reach private parties was left open.
- Fraternal Order of Police v. United States (2000)
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 9-0): The domestic-violence firearm possession ban is a fitted anti-evasion part of the valid supply ban, within the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. Applying it to state and local officers does not commandeer, because it requires no state legislation, termination, prosecution or administration.
- Gillespie v. City of Indianapolis
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 9-0): Section 922(g)(9) may reach Gillespie's possession as a fitted anti-evasion part of the national supply restriction. A federal firearm disability does not commandeer a city merely because its own job rules make an affected employee ineligible for armed work.
- United States v. Morrison
  > Held (Kennedy for 5, 5-4): Findings of the economic costs of gender-motivated violence do not supply the interstate connection for §13981's private-assault action. Section 5 does not support it either, because a private assailant's animus is not the State's denial of equal protection.
- Jones v. United States
  > Held (Thomas for the Court, 9-0): Section 844(i) requires that the property's own function connect it to interstate commerce; a home's out-of-state mortgage, insurance and natural gas do not suffice. The conviction was reversed for entry of acquittal, and the constitutional question was reserved.
- Vermont Agency of Natural Resources v. United States ex rel. Stevens
  > Held (Scalia for the Court, 7-2): A relator has standing as partial assignee of the United States' claim (9-0), but the False Claims Act does not authorize a private relator's action against a State. The action was dismissed on that statutory ground without deciding constitutional immunity.
- Bradley v. Arkansas Department of Education
  > Held (Thomas for the Court, 9-0): A State that voluntarily accepts IDEA funds after the express condition takes effect consents to federal suit enforcing that program's obligations, within the recipient, claim and remedy the condition covers. The consent is not a general waiver and is not inferred from ordinary regulated activity.

## 3. The doctrine as it stands

Rules as of June 29, 2000.

### 3.1 Where this Court departed from history

1. The commerce test. Lopez came out as historically, but on a different rule, and Stone wrote it for five (Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas): "connection before magnitude." The Court first identifies the regulated class and the interstate mechanism connecting it to commerce; only then may aggregation measure size. Home Builders added the routes for noneconomic intrastate conduct: an anti-evasion route (Part II-A, seven votes) and an interstate-externality route (Part II-B, five votes). It also held that the economic character of the reviewed projects did not by itself supply the connection for the Endangered Species Act's takings class (Part I, five votes). Historically the D.C. Circuit upheld that application, and the Supreme Court never decided it. Morrison came out as historically, but on these routes.
2. Commandeering. Printz Part I (Souter, joined by Stone, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer) holds that Congress is not categorically barred from requiring appropriate, temporary, records-based assistance within a state executive officer's proper function, where federal law supplies the rule and federal authorities keep coercive enforcement. The judgment vacated and remanded on the fiscal question, with no majority rationale on it. Historically Printz barred commandeering state executives categorically. New York's bar on commanding state legislatures, and its invalidation of the take-title provision, are unchanged.
3. Article I abrogation. Historically, Seminole Tribe (1996) held Congress cannot use Article I to abrogate, and Florida Prepaid, College Savings, Alden and Kimel built on that. Here, Seminole Tribe fractured (the dismissal of Florida was set aside 5-4 with no controlling rationale; Union Gas was not overruled). Florida Prepaid Pt III (Stevens, joined by Stone, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer) then held that Congress may authorize private suits against States when the duty and the remedy are within Article I and the authorization is unmistakable. Kimel applied that to the ADEA, and Alden extended it to state courts for the FLSA.
4. Section 5. Florida Prepaid Pt II sustained the patent remedy as valid §5 legislation (historically it failed). College Savings Pt II (eight votes) rejected §5 for the own-product false-advertising theory, consistent with history. Kimel reserved the ADEA's §5 validity (historically it failed).
5. Officer suits. Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe let the officer claims proceed, 5-4, Stone writing (historically they were barred 5-4). There is no sovereign-lands exception, but a decree may not bind the State's title or award retrospective compensation.
6. Consistent with history: Boerne, Reno v. Condon, Hess, Regents v. Doe, Schacht, Lane v. Peña, Bradley, Vermont Agency, Jones, Robertson.

### 3.2 Commerce power

Doctrine now: Congress may regulate interstate commerce itself and its channels, instrumentalities, and persons or things in it (Harris, Bishop, Pic-A-State, Mussari). For conduct outside those categories, the Court first identifies the regulated class and the interstate mechanism, and only then lets aggregation measure magnitude (Lopez). The recognized mechanisms:
- Direct operation on an interstate market: conduct whose object and operation interfere with an actual interstate market, such as clinic access (American Life League, Bird) or business robbery (Robinson).
- Anti-evasion: intrastate conduct whose inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid interstate regime, such as machinegun possession (Hale, Rybar) and possession by persons barred from receiving firearms (Hiley, Fraternal Order of Police, Gillespie). Home Builders Part II-A states this rule (seven votes).
- Interstate externality: a substantial spillover that individual States cannot reasonably confine, identified on a materially adequate basis, with a fitted prohibition (Home Builders Part II-B, five votes).

Downstream economic consequences, such as the costs of crime or of violence against women, do not supply the connection (Lopez, Morrison). Jurisdictional elements must do real work; findings support but cannot manufacture the connection. Courts may read statutes narrowly to avoid the question (Jones).

How it operates:
1. Identify the class the statute actually regulates.
2. Is it interstate commerce, a channel, an instrumentality, or a person or thing in commerce? If yes, the power exists.
3. If not, identify the mechanism: direct market operation, anti-evasion of a valid regime, or a demonstrated interstate externality.
4. Only then aggregate to measure substantiality.
5. Check that the jurisdictional element, if any, is actually proved.

### 3.3 Commandeering and spending conditions

Doctrine now: Congress may not command state legislatures to enact or administer a federal program; the take-title provision fails, while monetary and access incentives are valid (New York). Congress is not categorically barred from requiring limited, temporary, records-based help from state executive officers within their proper function, where federal law supplies the rule and federal authorities keep coercive enforcement (Printz Part I); who must pay for that help has no majority rule. A valid law may directly regulate a State's own conduct, such as its handling of driver records, without commandeering (Reno v. Condon); whether such a law must also apply to private parties is reserved. A federal restriction that operates directly on individuals does not commandeer a State merely because the State's own rules make an employee ineligible (Fraternal Order of Police, Gillespie). States may knowingly accept clear, program-related funding conditions, and acceptance is consent to the suits and remedies the condition covers (Bradley). Dole's relatedness and coercion limits are preserved.

How it operates:
1. Who does the federal command run to: individuals, the State as a regulated actor, the state legislature, or state executive officers?
2. A command to legislate or to administer a federal program against others fails (New York).
3. A command for limited assistance from executives is measured by Printz Part I's source-and-function test.
4. A funding condition must be clear, related, and not coercive.

### 3.4 State sovereign immunity

Doctrine now:
- Congress may authorize private suits against States when the duty the State broke and the suit enforcing it are within an Article I power and Congress authorizes suit unmistakably (Florida Prepaid Pt III, 5-4; Kimel, 5-4). The four dissenters asked to reconsider it.
- The same rule opens a State's own courts to the authorized FLSA suit (Alden, 5-4).
- Section 5 abrogation is valid when the remedy is congruent and proportional to an identified constitutional deprivation (Florida Prepaid Pt II; College Savings Pt II).
- A State consents by express waiver, by voluntarily invoking federal jurisdiction, or by accepting federal money under a clear condition. Doing business is not consent (College Savings Pt III; Bradley).
- Officer suits for prospective relief are available within the statute's limits and without a sovereign-lands exception, but not for retroactive compensation (Seminole Tribe Pts II-III; Coeur d'Alene).
- Arm-of-the-State status turns on the entity's actual relationship to the State (Hess); a third party's indemnity does not change it (Regents v. Doe).
- Immunity from some claims does not prevent removal of the rest (Schacht). Immunity does not bar Supreme Court review of a State's own judgment (South Central Bell).

How it operates:
1. Is the defendant the State or an arm of it?
2. Is there a valid federal duty, and under which power?
3. Did Congress unmistakably authorize suit against States?
4. Is the authorization within Article I (the duty is within the power) or §5 (congruent and proportional)?
5. If not, did the State consent?
6. If not, is prospective relief available against an officer?

### 3.5 Section 5 and Fifteenth Amendment enforcement

Doctrine now: Congress may enact preventive and remedial legislation reaching some conduct that is not itself unconstitutional, but its means must be congruent and proportional to the constitutional injury, and it may not replace the right's judicially enforceable content. Formal findings, proof in every jurisdiction, sunsets and geographic limits are not required (Boerne). The defendant, the triggering wrong and the relief must bear a remedial relationship to the State's unconstitutional conduct; a private assailant's animus is not the State's denial (Morrison). Patents are property, and a preventive remedy subjecting States to infringement suits is valid (Florida Prepaid Pt II). Whether Boerne's test governs Fifteenth Amendment legislation is unresolved; the older Katzenbach and City of Rome cases remain in place (Boerne; Lopez v. Monterey County, 1999).
