# Justice Stone: full doctrinal build through OT2001

**Working preparation for the continued simulation. Earlier simulated decisions are fixed when they have already been reached; later entries are proposed Stone positions, not predetermined Court holdings.**

## Scope and how to use this build

This build begins with *Shaw v. Reno*, decided June 28, 1993 during **OT1992**, and continues through **OT2001**, including decisions issued in 2002. It covers the voting, equal-protection, affirmative-action, integration, congressional-power, enforcement, institutional-access, campaign-finance, and related procedural cases most likely to develop or test Stone's positions. It does not rewrite unrelated areas merely to make a doctrinal program come out cleanly.

The original version of this file was drafted prospectively. The simulation has now actually decided *Shaw*: Stone's Court **affirmed dismissal of the racial-design-only Equal Protection theory, 5–4**. That judgment is the starting law for the rest of this build. Deliberate or predominant attention to race in territorial districting does not, by itself, supply the constitutional injury. Racial exclusion from the franchise, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic status, statutory voting-rights violations, and other independently cognizable injuries remain governed by their own sources and elements.

The simulation is causal. A changed precedent removes a later historical case only when the later cause of action, standing theory, judgment, remedy, or procedural posture materially depended on the rule that changed. A famous historical caption is not preserved merely because it would be useful. A reconstructed grant is used only when a genuine surviving legal question can be grounded in the historical dispute and is marked openly as counterfactual.

The method ties doctrinal breadth to the question, record, and purpose of the case, with rules courts and institutions can administer. Narrowness is not an independent objective. Begin each case with the law, posture, and record that actually exist at that point in the simulation; preserve precedent and doctrines that the earlier divergence did not disturb; and do not invent an evidentiary prerequisite or factual finding merely because it would make later doctrine cleaner. A broader proposed rule must identify what it changes, why the presented problem warrants that change, and how it fits the rest of the build. Reconstructed *Miller* develops equal political membership and constitutional room for voluntary race-conscious electoral inclusion; reconstructed *Vera* addresses the distinct standing question. *Adarand* presents actual individualized racial treatment in economic competition and is the principal vehicle for working out how such treatment must be justified. None of the three is a license to rewrite the VRA, sex-equality doctrine, or unrelated fields.

## Recurring reasoning and source boundaries

### Identify the right, actor, treatment, and remedy

Begin with four questions: What legal right is asserted? What conduct is attributable to this defendant? What treatment or barrier injured this claimant? What remedy does the governing law authorize?

Race-conscious districting, an unequal contracting opportunity, denial of an election, an inaccessible public service, and an unpaid statutory wage are different legal claims. They may reflect overlapping concerns without sharing elements, burdens, standards, or remedies. A statutory violation does not automatically become a constitutional one; constitutional authority does not itself create a private action; and a merits violation does not create a remedy.

Sex classifications retain their own intermediate-scrutiny framework. Sexual-orientation, disability, voting, speech, due process, and other classifications or rights remain in their own doctrines. Nothing in the racial-treatment analysis silently changes those standards.

### Governmental racial treatment

The first question is whether government actually treats a person differently because of race, national origin, ethnicity operating as race or caste, or a deliberate racial proxy. Racial awareness, demographic data, disparate effects, or attention to racial conditions do not by themselves establish that legal treatment. In territorial districting, residence supplies district membership on the same legal terms for residents of every race. Race may explain why a boundary was chosen and may be powerful evidence in an independently cognizable dilution, exclusion, purpose, or statutory claim, but predominant racial design alone is not the constitutional injury after *Shaw*.

Under Stone's preferred *Adarand* standard, when actual differential racial treatment and the injury required by the claim are established, government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. Government must justify the measure's coverage and actual burdens, including its response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Courts examine serious contrary evidence, the measure's operation, beneficiaries, qualifications, scope, duration, genuine rebuttability, burdens on persons outside the assisted class, and concrete alternatives that materially test the government's explanation. The Constitution does not require exhaustion of every imaginable alternative, a formal commissioned study, a fixed statistical threshold, scientific certainty, or a universal sunset.

Corrective action does not enter a separate tier. A government relying on correction or prevention must support the condition it says it is addressing: identified discrimination, segregation, exclusion, persisting effects, or a serious risk of recurrence in the relevant institution, practice, market, or valid legislative category. The beneficiaries and scope must reasonably correspond to that condition, genuine qualifications remain relevant, persons outside an assisted group must retain meaningful opportunity, and the policy cannot become a permanent racial entitlement after its basis has ceased. A prior judgment against the administering institution and proof that every beneficiary personally suffered discrimination are not universal prerequisites.

Government may also rely on a genuinely distinct prospective objective, including overcoming documented barriers to meaningful market participation, meaningful integration, or demonstrated institutional access or educational benefits, when that objective is important, belongs to the institution's lawful function, and is substantially advanced by the supported racial measure with justified burdens. Racial arithmetic for its own sake, stereotypes about racial beliefs or capacities, hostility, caste maintenance, or private favoritism are not sufficient objectives.

The same equality obligation applies to federal, state, and local government. Their constitutional powers, institutional responsibilities, evidence, and program reach may differ, and those differences matter to what objectives and categories they can support. They do not create different grades of equal citizenship.

### What *Adarand* changes and what it does not

The pre-1995 cases do not supply one clean inherited formula. *Bakke*, *Fullilove*, *Wygant*, *Paradise*, *Croson*, and *Metro Broadcasting* contain competing approaches to remedial racial action, federal institutional responsibility, evidentiary predicates, burdens on third parties, and fit. Stone uses those materials rather than pretending they already established a single rule.

In his preferred *Adarand* position, Stone would openly reject both *Metro Broadcasting*'s federal-status-based intermediate-scrutiny rule and *Croson*'s categorical insistence that every state or local remedial racial classification must be resolved by the same strict-scrutiny formulation. He retains *Croson*'s demand for relevant evidence and rejection of undifferentiated societal discrimination, *Wygant*'s attention to the burden actually imposed, *Paradise*'s attention to flexibility, duration, alternatives, and third-party effects, and *Fullilove*'s recognition that Congress may act on a supported national remedial record within its authority. This is a proposed doctrinal change and must be stated as such.

The resulting inquiry does not guarantee a remedial program's validity. Government carries the justification burden once actual differential racial treatment is established. A weak record, an arbitrary category, an irrebuttable presumption, an excessive burden, a sham remedial label, or continuation after the factual basis disappears can defeat the measure. Conversely, an individual contract, admission, promotion, or assignment does not become categorically invalid merely because race can affect who receives it.

Stone's preferred disposition is vacatur and a remand confined to unresolved matters material to the new standard. If the Court does not adopt that replacement standard, his authorized Stevens-based fallback is affirmance on the preserved record under the inherited federal precedents. The fallback is a distinct alternative, not an exemption from an adverse finding under the preferred standard. Later entries using the new standard remain proposed Stone positions; the law actually established depends on the controlling opinion and judgment.

### Voting Rights Act propositions stay separate

Three propositions remain distinct:

1. **Required:** a statute may require a remedy when its enacted elements are proved.
2. **Authorized:** Congress may authorize preventive implementation within a constitutionally valid statutory category without requiring a new constitutional judgment against every implementing jurisdiction.
3. **Otherwise permissible:** a State may sometimes adopt a lawful voluntary measure that federal law does not demand.

Failure to prove that §2 required a particular district does not automatically establish that drawing it was unconstitutional. Conversely, “VRA compliance” cannot defend whatever map a legislature or DOJ prefers. The official must identify the statutory duty or permission, the relevant evidence, and the measure's relationship to the protected opportunity. *Voinovich* supplies an important statutory foundation for this distinction.

Section 2 remains a results statute. A claimant who proves its statutory elements does not also have to prove constitutional discriminatory intent. Nor does §2 guarantee proportional representation, racial officeholding, or a preferred party's success. A minority group's candidate of choice is identified through evidence of voter preference, not the candidate's race.

After the simulated *Shaw*, demographic awareness and deliberate construction of an effective statutory remedy are not themselves constitutional injuries. Independent constitutional prohibitions remain fully enforceable, and a statute's validity, coverage, implementation, cause of action, and remedy remain separate questions.

### Congressional enforcement

Courts identify the constitutional guarantee. Congress may remedy violations and enact reasonably fitted preventive categories that reach some conduct not independently unconstitutional in every application. A valid category needs an identifiable constitutional problem, a relevant legislative record or established history, and a defensible relationship between the problem, coverage, and remedy.

Congruence and proportionality test that relationship. They do not require a judicially admissible case file proving a constitutional violation by every covered State or person, and no universal sunset follows. Once the statutory category is valid, the individual claimant proves the statutory elements. Requiring a new constitutional violation in every application would erase preventive enforcement power. Fourteenth- and Fifteenth-Amendment enforcement remain distinct from the Commerce, Spending, Elections, and other Article I powers.

### Integrated public education

Remedying proven state segregation is constitutionally available within the scope of the violation. Voluntarily preventing serious racial isolation in public schools may also rest on a distinct educational objective where the district identifies the institutional benefit, supports the connection between integration and that benefit, examines actual enrollment patterns and operation, and justifies the racial burden imposed.

This is not a separate racial tier or a special school exception from equal protection. The same racial-treatment analysis applies. Countywide racial arithmetic alone is insufficient; neither individual assignment nor an individual benefit is categorically forbidden; and a rigid racial gate can fail because its operation and burden are not adequately supported. Higher-education admissions and a general diversity license for every public institution are not decided by the school cases.

### State immunity and enforcement

The build no longer assumes that any Article I power, standing alone, lets Congress subject a nonconsenting State to a private damages action. Stone's preferred position is that Article I ordinarily does not supply that authority. Valid Reconstruction-Amendment enforcement legislation may abrogate state immunity when Congress speaks unmistakably and acts within the relevant enforcement power. A State may also waive immunity.

Prospective officer relief, public enforcement by the United States, statutory private causes of action, the substantive right, jurisdiction, waiver, abrogation, and available damages remain distinct. This changes the old prospective positions for *Seminole Tribe* and *Alden* for reasons independent of *Shaw*.

### Evidence and remedies must remain manageable

Use information institutions can realistically maintain: voting returns, demographic estimates, procurement utilization and availability data, admissions and transfer records, employment pipelines, complaint histories, and implementation costs. Expert assistance and rebuttal are available; methods and assumptions must be exposed when material. Appellate courts review legal standards independently, trial findings under the governing factual standard, and evidentiary administration for abuse of discretion.

A remedy must address the proved or validly prevented mechanism. Government need not exhaust every imaginable alternative, but a concrete, practicable, comparably effective alternative that materially reduces the relevant burden can test the asserted fit. Periodic reconsideration should follow the institution and the legal source rather than become continuous judicial management.

## OT1992: the 1993 foundation

### Shaw v. Reno, 509 U.S. 630 (1993)

**Historical questions.** Did the complaint state an equal-protection claim against North Carolina's unusually shaped congressional plan, alleged to separate voters predominantly by race? Could the related claims against the federal preclearance officials proceed in this action?

**Simulation result already reached.** On June 28, 1993, Stone's Court affirmed dismissal of the racial-design-only Equal Protection theory, 5–4. The federal-defendant claims remain dismissed on their independent jurisdictional and statutory grounds.

**Stone's reasoning.** Territorial boundaries assign residents by geography. Conscious use of racial information, unusual shape, or even predominant racial motivation can be evidence of another constitutional or statutory violation, but none by itself establishes exclusion from the franchise, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic status, or another cognizable personal inequality. Equal Protection does not create a freestanding right to live in a district drawn without racial awareness.

The holding does not immunize intentional vote dilution, racial exclusion, discriminatory administration, deliberate racial proxies that produce differential legal treatment, or statutory voting-rights violations. Those claims proceed under their own elements. Nor does the decision hold that a legislature may invoke the VRA to justify whatever racial design it prefers; statutory authority and compliance remain independently reviewable.

**Downstream consequence.** The historical *Shaw* cause of action does not exist in this timeline. *Hays*, *Shaw II/Pope*, *Abrams*, *Hunt*, and *Easley* are removed for the causal reasons stated at the top of this file. *Miller* and *Vera* return only as reconstructed grants addressing questions the new *Shaw* leaves worth testing.

### Northeastern Florida Chapter, Associated General Contractors v. Jacksonville, 508 U.S. 656 (1993)

**Historical questions.** Must contractors show they would have won particular contracts to challenge a racial contracting barrier? Did replacement of the ordinance moot the dispute?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse and remand. An able and ready competitor may establish injury from unequal competitive treatment; the successor ordinance does not automatically eliminate the controversy.

**Reasoning.** Standing concerns exposure to the challenged process, not proof of ultimate entitlement. The merits remain governed by the racial-treatment analysis applicable when actual differential treatment is established. This protects both the remedy's beneficiaries and those burdened by it from a rule under which an allegedly unlawful process becomes unreviewable because the final award cannot be reconstructed. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/508/656/).

### Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, 509 U.S. 579 (1993)

**Historical question.** Does the Federal Rules of Evidence framework supersede *Frye*'s general-acceptance requirement for scientific expert evidence, and what screening must judges perform?

**Stone's disposition.** Vacate and remand under a flexible reliability-and-relevance inquiry.

**Reasoning.** Empirical civil-rights adjudication needs reliable methods, not a scientific-consensus veto. Validation, error, data quality, and suitability to the legal question matter. These tools cannot mechanically decide legislative facts or make one favored statistical technique mandatory in every voting case. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/579/).

### St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks, 509 U.S. 502 (1993)

**Historical question.** Does disbelief of an employer's stated reasons compel judgment for a Title VII plaintiff who established a prima facie case?

**Stone's disposition.** Vacate the appellate judgment and remand. Rejection of the explanation permits a discrimination finding but does not compel one in every case.

**Reasoning.** The ultimate issue remains discrimination. Stone emphatically rejects an additional universal requirement for direct evidence after the plaintiff proves pretext. A factfinder may infer the actual forbidden reason from the prima facie case, the lie, and the surrounding record. This prepares *Reeves* without converting a permissible inference into an irrebuttable rule. [Opinion and dissents](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/502/).

**Existing foundations to apply, rather than fictional new grants:** *Growe v. Emison*, 507 U.S. 25, respects timely state redistricting and requires proof of the relevant voting cohesion; *Voinovich*, 507 U.S. 146, separates a statutory duty to create a minority district from permission to do so. *United States v. Fordice*, 505 U.S. 717 (1992), requires dismantling traceable segregative university policies when practicable and educationally sound alternatives exist. These precedents supply starting law. [*Growe*](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/25/); [*Fordice*](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/505/717/).

## OT1993: decisions principally issued in 1994

### Holder v. Hall, 512 U.S. 874 (1994)

**Historical questions.** Can §2 reach the size of a governing body, here Bleckley County's sole commissioner? Is there a sufficiently objective alternative against which dilution can be assessed? What remains of the intentional-discrimination claim?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm the Eleventh Circuit's statutory ruling rather than bar the §2 claim categorically; allow the resulting remedial proceedings to continue. Do not reopen liability on the invented premise that the lower courts never examined the remaining elements. An independent constitutional claim need be resolved only insofar as it remains necessary after the statutory disposition.

**Reasoning.** A comparator cannot be selected just because it produces the desired racial result. Here, the five-member alternative had concrete roots in Georgia law, local governmental practice, and the county's institutional setting. Those roots permit judicial comparison without authorizing judges to choose an ideal legislature. Stone follows Blackmun's approach, retaining all *Gingles* and totality requirements. The comparator rule does not itself establish liability in future cases or entitle every group to enlarge a governing body. [Opinion, including Blackmun dissent](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/874/).

**Later use.** *SCLC* must evaluate real alternative election structures; *Mixon* can identify an actual prior election as a benchmark. Neither case receives automatic liability simply because a benchmark exists.

### Johnson v. De Grandy, 512 U.S. 997 (1994)

**Historical questions.** Did Florida's legislative districts violate §2 despite approximately proportional minority opportunity districts? Did the state validation proceeding preclude the federal litigation?

**Stone's disposition.** Preserve the rejection of preclusion; reject the House-district §2 liability finding and preserve the Senate-district no-liability result on the record actually reviewed.

**Reasoning.** Proportionality is relevant evidence, not a safe harbor, entitlement, or demand to maximize districts. Assess the practical opportunity in the relevant geographic area and the entire statutory record. A state proceeding does not automatically bind different federal claimants or decide claims it did not adjudicate. This decision keeps the broader *Holder* rule from becoming a machine for compulsory additional seats. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/997/).

### J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., 511 U.S. 127 (1994)

**Historical question.** May the state exercise peremptory jury challenges based on sex in a paternity and child-support proceeding?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse and remand; prohibit sex-based strikes.

**Reasoning.** Population-level assumptions about men's and women's sympathies cannot justify assigning an individual juror a presumed viewpoint. This is an intentional classification with no supported remedial object. It illustrates why evidence must address the government's actual purpose and legal task, not merely document a statistical correlation. Sex classifications retain their own heightened-scrutiny doctrine. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/127/).

### Harris v. Forklift Systems, 510 U.S. 17 (1993)

**Historical question.** Must a Title VII hostile environment produce serious psychological injury?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse and remand; no such injury requirement.

**Reasoning.** Examine the circumstances, including severity, frequency, humiliation, and interference with work, with both objective and subjective components. Meaningful access to employment is injured before a worker develops a clinically measurable disorder. A legal standard may use observable conditions without imposing a diagnostic prerequisite. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/17/).

### Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244 (1994), and Rivers v. Roadway Express, 511 U.S. 298 (1994)

**Historical questions.** Do the Civil Rights Act of 1991's new damages and jury-trial provisions, and its expansion of §1981, apply to conduct predating enactment?

**Stone's dispositions.** Affirm the nonretroactivity results in both cases.

**Reasoning.** Congress can strengthen civil-rights remedies prospectively, but courts should not create new retrospective liability without the required legislative direction. Distinguish statutes that change legal consequences from judicial interpretations of existing law. This is an administrable reliance rule and a limit on Stone's remedial ambitions. [*Landgraf*](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/244/); [*Rivers*](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/511/298/).

### Livadas v. Bradshaw, 512 U.S. 107 (1994)

**Historical questions.** Was California's refusal to enforce a generally applicable wage-payment protection for a union employee preempted by federal labor law, and could the resulting federal-rights violation be remedied through §1983?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse and remand in the employee's favor on those legal issues.

**Reasoning.** A state cannot burden federally protected collective bargaining by selectively withholding an otherwise available enforcement service. Section 1983 may enforce an identifiable federal right; it is not restricted to constitutional provisions. Later private-enforcement cases must analyze the actual right and any statutory displacement. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/107/).

### Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet, 512 U.S. 687 (1994)

**Historical question.** Did creation of a special school district for a religious enclave violate the Establishment Clause?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm invalidation of the special delegation.

**Reasoning.** Government may accommodate disability and religious practice through generally available arrangements. It may not assign governmental authority through an exceptional arrangement keyed to one religious community without the required neutrality. A legitimate access objective does not cure a constitutionally defective delegation. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/687/).

### Turner Broadcasting System v. FCC, 512 U.S. 622 (1994)

**Historical question.** What First Amendment scrutiny governs cable must-carry rules, and did the summary-judgment record justify them?

**Stone's disposition.** Vacate and remand for the required factual inquiry under content-neutral intermediate scrutiny.

**Reasoning.** Congress may make predictive judgments about market structure and access, but courts need evidence connecting the burden to the identified danger. The lesson is institutional: predictive judgments can be supported and tested without transplanting First Amendment doctrine into equal protection. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/622/).

### Madsen v. Women's Health Center, 512 U.S. 753 (1994)

**Historical questions.** What First Amendment standard governed the clinic-access injunction, and which buffer, noise, visual, and residential restrictions were justified?

**Stone's disposition.** Sustain the supported public-entrance buffer and noise restrictions; invalidate the unsupported or excessive provisions at the scope identified in the historical judgment.

**Reasoning.** An injunction directed at particular actors is not automatically viewpoint discrimination, but its burdens must be no greater than necessary to serve the significant interests involved. Proven obstruction and disruption support concrete access protection. They do not authorize suppression of unrelated expression or broad restrictions unsupported by the record. Apply ordinary limits on who is bound by the injunction. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/753/).

## OT1994: decisions principally issued in 1995

### Adarand Constructors v. Peña, 515 U.S. 200 (1995)

**Historical questions.** What equal-protection standard governs federal race-based contracting incentives? Does the challenged compensation arrangement satisfy that standard, including the use of presumptions of social and economic disadvantage?

**Selected approach and status.** Stone's preferred position combines the common standard of substantial justification discussed as Option 3 with the permission to address documented barriers to market access discussed as Option 5. His preferred disposition is **vacate and remand under that new standard**, with the **Stevens-based affirmance fallback** specified below if the Court does not adopt the proposed replacement standard. These are alternative Stone positions for the simulation, not completed holdings, assumed coalitions, or instructions for another Justice's vote.

**Role in this build.** *Adarand* develops the justification required when governmental racial treatment changes an individual's competitive opportunity. The inquiry must state an enforceable standard, not merely list subjects for judicial discussion. Its breadth is deliberate: supported corrective and prospective action may be permissible even when no judgment has established discrimination against each beneficiary. Government must nevertheless justify the racial treatment and its actual burdens. This does not revive the rejected racial-design-only claim in *Shaw* or replace the VRA's statutory elements, sex-equality doctrine, or other source-specific protections.

**Stone's preferred disposition.** Vacate the Tenth Circuit's judgment sustaining the challenged subcontracting compensation arrangement and remand for application of the new standard. Vacatur does not hold the arrangement unconstitutional or direct a program-wide injunction. The lower courts must identify the unresolved questions material to the new standard, retain the ordinary force of existing concessions and supported findings, and permit additional proceedings only as needed under the governing procedural rules. Any interim or final relief requires its own legal and equitable basis.

**Proposed governing rule.** When government changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification, government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. Government must justify the measure's coverage and the actual burdens it imposes, including its response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone, or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism, and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence, and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination, and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it. This is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality.

**Reasoning.** The contracting incentive can alter an award between competing firms. That is a real burden on competitive opportunity, even though the prime contractor remains free to forgo the incentive. Stone therefore does not treat the arrangement as mere racial awareness. Nor does requiring justification depend on proving that the challenger would certainly have won every affected contract. Equal protection protects the opportunity to compete on lawful terms.

The existence of that burden does not establish that the program is invalid. Government can have substantial reasons to address exclusion that continues through access to credit or bonding, established subcontracting relationships, or opportunities to build a record of successful performance. Those mechanisms must be supported in the relevant market, institution, program, or valid legislative category. General evidence that small firms face difficulties cannot automatically justify every racial category; equally, a court should not assume that present differences in size or experience are unaffected by the very exclusion the evidence identifies. Correcting demonstrated obstacles can require action before every instance of discrimination has been litigated.

The rule gives substantive weight to the distinction between maintaining racial subordination and overcoming supported exclusion, without assigning corrective programs an automatically lower tier. A common equality obligation can take account of different purposes, histories, operations, and burdens. Courts must examine those differences rather than assume either that all racial measures are equivalent or that benevolent intent is enough. An affected competitor's interest remains part of the constitutional justification even when the program has an important corrective purpose.

The same equality obligation applies to federal, state, and local government. Their authority and institutional responsibilities differ. Congress may support a national remedial category through evidence and powers that a municipality cannot claim for an independently designed local measure. An agency implementing a supported congressional program need not reproduce Congress's entire evidentiary case for every project, but must act within its delegation and justify a materially different or unsupported implementation. Constitutional sufficiency remains for the court to decide; respect for institutional evidence and judgment does not surrender that responsibility.

**Why remand under the new standard.** The judgment below applied the inherited federal framework. Stone proposes a different governing inquiry whose application requires attention to the actual racial presumptions, eligibility routes, and burdens. The historical Supreme Court record identified uncertainty about which certification route Gonzales used and how the overlapping rules treated economic disadvantage. Those are concrete reasons to examine the challenged operation before announcing a final merits result under the new rule. They do not establish that any presumption was irrebuttable or that the program lacked a valid evidentiary basis.

The Tenth Circuit recorded Adarand's stipulation that section 502 of the Small Business Act satisfied *Fullilove*'s evidentiary requirements and its concession that the agency had not exceeded its delegated authority. Respect those concessions in their actual scope. A stipulation under the inherited standard does not automatically resolve every new constitutional question, but a change of standard does not license pretending that Congress had no evidence. Remand must explain which genuinely unresolved issue matters and why existing findings or concessions do not resolve it.

**Directions on remand and administration in later cases.**

1. **Identify the actual program and preserved challenge.** Distinguish the subcontracting incentive, the applicable certification rules, and any separately challenged statutory provision. Do not substitute a generic percentage set-aside or a different federal-aid program for the arrangement before the court. Resolve legal standards independently and apply the ordinary rules governing factual findings, preservation, and further proceedings.
2. **Assess the supported objective and reach.** Identify the barriers or conditions the government relies on and the evidence connecting the measure's beneficiaries, categories, and scope to them. Relevant national or shared evidence can suffice where its application is explained. A statewide population percentage, a generalized account of societal discrimination, or a category borrowed without support does not by itself justify the racial treatment.
3. **Examine presumptions in operation.** Determine which certification route applies, what social or economic disadvantage must be established, how presumptions can be challenged, and whether firms outside the presumptive categories have a meaningful route to qualify. Group evidence may support a presumption without requiring each applicant to litigate personal discrimination. Individual review and rebuttability matter to justification but are not automatic constitutional safe harbors. A nominal review process that cannot meaningfully correct unsupported eligibility does not supply the flexibility the government claims.
4. **Examine burdens and concrete alternatives.** Consider how the incentive affects competition, whether genuine qualifications remain required, and whether persons outside assisted categories retain meaningful opportunity. Government must answer a concrete, practicable alternative that would comparably achieve its supported objective with materially less racial disadvantage. It need not exhaust every imagined alternative or accept a less effective measure simply because that measure uses no racial classification.
5. **Examine continued justification.** Determine whether the program can respond when the conditions supporting its coverage or a firm's eligibility materially change. Continued need cannot be established solely by restating the original purpose. Conversely, improved participation while a program operates does not itself prove that its justification has disappeared. The constitutional inquiry requires neither a universal expiration date nor proof that all racial inequality has ended.
6. **State the consequence of a failure.** If government cannot establish the required objective, substantial relationship, or justification for the burden, the challenged racial treatment fails. Relief must address the invalid provision or application under ordinary remedial and severability rules. A court does not acquire authority to redesign all procurement policy or supervise every future contract merely because it finds a defect.

These directions require judgment about evidence and operation, not a numerical score. No single disparity ratio, regression method, commissioned study, least-restrictive-means formula, mandatory consultation procedure, or permanent judicial monitoring system is constitutionalized. Existing records may suffice. Participation, transparent reasons, and independent review can improve administration, but cannot replace the substantive justification owed to an affected bidder.

### Miller v. Johnson, 515 U.S. 900 (1995)

**Status and selected approach.** Reconstructed counterfactual merits matter combining equal political membership with constitutional room for voluntary race-conscious electoral inclusion. The user's September 28, 2026 acceptance of the researched recommendations controls the current six-question position. The supplied three-judge judgment is for Georgia, with no injunction or unresolved independent claim. This is approved Stone planning and brief preparation, not a completed Court decision or a prediction of another Justice's vote.

**Case brief.** The live preparation is [OT1994 chunk 9, Miller / Abrams / United States](../terms/OT1994/briefs/OT_1994CHUNK9.md#97-miller-v-johnson--abrams-v-johnson--united-states-v-johnson). Its single Section II, version 2, contains an express answer, rationale, application, and limit for each of the six questions. The retained captions and historical docket identifiers are inventory cross-references; the losing challengers seek review, and the victorious state defendants defend the judgment. Article III and the lawful review channel require the independent validation identified in the brief.

**QUESTIONS PRESENTED TO THE COURT: ALL SIX MUST BE ANSWERED.**

1. Whether a congressional district violates the Equal Protection Clause solely because race was the predominant factor in assigning voters and traditional districting considerations were subordinated to racial objectives, where the plaintiffs allege no racial exclusion, intentional vote dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic status, or other independently cognizable constitutional deprivation.
2. Whether the asserted racial sorting, stereotyping, and impairment of representation themselves establish an independently cognizable denial of equal political membership on the reconstructed allegations, and how the protections against exclusion, intentional dilution, unequal participation, and inferior civic status apply to that inquiry.
3. Whether a State's voluntary effort to improve minority electoral opportunity through territorial districting is constitutionally impermissible because the Voting Rights Act does not require that particular district, and what follows from the distinction between state constitutional permission and federal statutory authority.
4. Whether an unusually shaped district is a necessary threshold for the asserted constitutional challenge, and what significance district shape, demographics, direct evidence of legislative purpose, and departures from traditional districting considerations have in establishing racial predominance and a cognizable constitutional deprivation.
5. Whether the record supports the district court's finding that race predominated in constructing Georgia's Eleventh District and that traditional districting considerations were subordinated, and what legal consequences follow from that finding under simulated Shaw.
6. Whether strict scrutiny applies to the challenged district; and, conditionally assuming that it applies, whether the district satisfies that standard in light of Georgia's asserted Voting Rights Act compliance justification, DOJ's preclearance demands, and the statutory limits on requiring the creation of additional majority-minority districts.

**Selected theoretical organization.** Equal civic membership and meaningful participation supply the organizing principle. Antisubordination explains the significance of historical exclusion and the actual operation of institutions; individual rights require justification of real racial burdens; lawful authority limits each actor's powers and available remedies. The four injury categories are complementary protections, not rival complete theories or an exhaustive code. A status-imposing act can itself injure without a second act or downstream financial or electoral loss. Corrective purpose does not create a separate immunity, and equal membership does not merge constitutional, statutory, standing, and remedial doctrines into a single test. This is Stone's selected synthesis, not a claim that one scholar or the earlier Shaw majority adopted every component.

**Why reconstruct this case.** The decided *Shaw* rejected racial design alone as an Equal Protection deprivation. *Miller* should develop the substantive account behind that result: what equal political membership protects, why predominant racial attention in a common territorial constituency does not alone deny it, and why the Constitution can permit electoral inclusion beyond what federal voting-rights statutes compel. That is a broader doctrinal choice than asking whether stronger motive evidence changes *Shaw*. Its breadth remains tied to territorial districting and the issues presented. The reconstruction may expressly alter the question and procedural path, but may not invent findings of exclusion, dilution, successful inclusion, or statutory compliance. If the necessary issue or record is missing when the case is prepared, identify that gap rather than deciding an unpresented claim.

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm the reconstructed judgment for the state defendants and denial of injunctive relief once jurisdiction is established. No redrawing order exists, and no unresolved independent claim calls for remand. Reject predominance alone and the asserted automatic status and representation inferences; preserve constitutional room for voluntary inclusion. Accept the supported reconstructed finding of predominance and subordination of ordinary criteria. Give the conditional statutory-justification question the answer below without converting it into a ground for invalidating the district or an alternative holding supporting affirmance.

**Historical questions answered.** All six questions receive separate answers in the brief. Sorting, stereotyping, representation, and status receive substantive examination. Bizarre shape is no mandatory gateway, while direct and circumstantial evidence retain their claim-specific significance. Factual predominance is accepted on the adopted reconstructed record rather than merely assumed. Predominance alone does not trigger strict scrutiny. Conditionally applying the opposing approach, Georgia's asserted §5-compulsion defense does not sustain the district on the admitted record because the required connection to a substantive statutory obligation is not established by DOJ's demand. Failure of that defense does not prove that every supported corrective or prospective justification must fail. Constitutional permission, statutory duty, and agency authority remain distinct; the conditional reasoning supplies no independent basis for the judgment and no direct order against DOJ. Nonretrogression does not resolve every discriminatory-purpose issue, and the later Bossier questions are not imported.

No separate §2, §5, Fifteenth Amendment, or other independent claim is presented in this reconstruction. Such claims in other litigation retain their own elements, burdens, and ordinary procedural rules. Affirmance does not certify that every feature of Georgia's map complies with all law or invite a search for a new claim on remand.

**Proposed governing rule: equal political membership.** Deliberate or predominant use of race to draw a common territorial constituency, even when ordinary districting considerations are subordinated, does not by itself deny Equal Protection. The relevant distinction is between attention to the racial composition of a constituency and governmental treatment that denies equal electoral opportunity, participation, or civic standing. Racial exclusion, intentional vote dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership, and other independently cognizable deprivations remain actionable under the law governing them. Those examples are not an exhaustive code of injuries.

This is a substantive distinction, not a demand for additional downstream harm in every equality case. A legally recognized denial of equal status may itself be the injury, without economic loss or proof that an election would have turned out differently. Ordinary pleading rules remain in place. A plaintiff need not satisfy a new universal requirement of expert evidence, an alternative map, or measurable electoral loss merely to invoke Equal Protection.

**Proposed governing rule: voluntary electoral inclusion.** A State may deliberately seek to improve minority electoral opportunity through territorial districting even when the VRA does not compel the precise district it selects. Neither an adjudicated prior constitutional violation nor proof of federal statutory necessity is a prerequisite to considering race for that purpose. Predominance alone does not trigger strict scrutiny or an obligation to prove that this exact map was indispensable. The absence of a federal duty therefore does not, by itself, establish a constitutional prohibition.

That permission remains subject to independently applicable constitutional and statutory limits. An inclusive or remedial description does not immunize exclusion, intentional packing or fragmentation that unlawfully dilutes voting strength, unequal participation, inferior civic status, or another established deprivation. The State's freedom to pursue inclusion creates neither a right to proportional seats nor a duty to maximize majority-minority districts.

**Reasoning.** Electoral boundaries necessarily assemble residents into political constituencies. Conscious attention to a community's racial composition can address actual exclusion and patterns of voting without making race a condition of an individual's eligibility to vote, participate, or receive representation. All residents of the district remain members of the same constituency. The Constitution does not require a State to wait until federal law compels a particular response before taking account of obstacles to minority electoral opportunity. Otherwise, statutory protections would become a ceiling on permissible inclusion rather than a source of enforceable obligations.

But geography supplies no constitutional immunity. The question is how governmental action operates, not whether officials used a map rather than an explicit eligibility rule. *Gomillion v. Lightfoot* preserves a claim where municipal boundaries were used to deprive Black residents of the municipal franchise. *Anderson v. Martin* preserves the constitutional objection to state-imposed racial ballot labels without requiring proof of a changed election outcome. These decisions prevent equal membership from being reduced either to formal access or to material loss. They also show why the independent deprivation must be identified under the applicable doctrine, rather than presumed from predominant racial design alone.

The strongest contrary argument is that deliberate racial sorting itself stamps citizens with a racial political identity, encourages stereotypes, and tells representatives to serve a racial group. That concern deserves an answer on its own terms. Stone's response is that recognition of real patterns of exclusion or voting does not necessarily prescribe the political identity of every resident, make members of a race interchangeable, or declare that only a representative of the same race can represent them. A district's demographic composition does not, without more, establish those governmental acts. Evidence of actual discriminatory treatment remains available. Generalized disapproval of the map's racial message cannot alone reconstitute the design-only claim rejected in *Shaw*.

Nor does racial predominance establish that an inclusion policy is wise or effective. Descriptive representation, electoral opportunity, participation, and substantive responsiveness are related but distinct. This decision permits a range of lawful political judgments; it does not constitutionalize the assumption that more majority-minority districts always produce better representation. Traditional districting considerations remain relevant evidence, and applicable independent legal requirements retain their force. Their subordination to race is not itself the missing constitutional deprivation.

**Administration in lower courts.** Courts can apply the combined position through familiar claim-specific adjudication:

1. **Identify the challenged treatment and the source of the asserted right.** Keep Article III jurisdiction distinct from the merits. On the merits, explain whether the alleged wrong is racial design alone or a deprivation recognized by the governing constitutional or statutory doctrine. Apply ordinary pleading, proof, and review rules at the case's actual procedural stage.
2. **Use motive and map evidence for the work the particular claim assigns them.** Demographics, departures from traditional criteria, legislative statements, voting patterns, and the plan's practical operation can establish facts material to an independent claim. Predominance is neither a substitute for that claim's elements nor a reason to disregard evidence of discrimination. Burdens of proof remain those supplied by the governing doctrine.
3. **Separate constitutional permission from statutory obligation.** Reject the inference that a district is unconstitutional merely because the VRA does not require it. A Section 2 claim retains its statutory results inquiry and applicable requirements, without an added constitutional-injury gate. Any question about Section 5 or DOJ's authority must be decided under the statute and law then governing that question. State authority to choose an otherwise lawful map does not establish federal authority to demand it.
4. **Tie relief to the wrong established.** Reject a design-only claim without ordering a different racial balance. If an independent violation is proved, apply the remedy authorized for that violation. The combined position imposes no judicial optimization of racial percentages, continuing supervision, mandatory consultation machinery, or universal map-selection formula.

These directions leave room for judgment about purpose, operation, and injury. Administrability comes from identifying the legal claim and deciding the facts it makes material, rather than converting political membership into an unbounded assessment of representational fairness. Legislatures may use participation, transparency, and revisable policy choices to improve their work, but this case does not make those practices new constitutional prerequisites or defenses.

**Fit with the build.** *Shaw* supplies the rejection of design alone; *Miller* adds an affirmative account of equal membership and voluntary inclusion. *Vera* remains the distinct standing inquiry and is not decided through this merits rule. The two vehicles require consistent threshold analysis: a merits defeat is not itself lack of Article III injury, and a different standing outcome cannot be assigned to identical allegations merely because the later roadmap calls for it. Warth supplies this distinction; the actual Miller threshold record must be settled before adjudication, and no new injury is invented for that purpose. *Adarand* concerns actual individual racial allocation of competitive opportunity and its justification; this districting decision does not replace that inquiry with a general exemption for benevolent motives. *Jenkins* concerns correction of an adjudicated segregation violation through a bounded remedy and a meaningful route to release; *Miller* neither imports that remedial predicate into every voluntary districting choice nor extends continuing judicial supervision to ordinary mapmaking. The common commitment is to identify the legal wrong, respect lawful institutional choices, and administer relief through the governing source of law.

### Missouri v. Jenkins, 515 U.S. 70 (1995)

**Historical questions and scope.** Did the district court exceed its remedial authority through the challenged salary orders, including funding for noninstructional personnel? Did continuation of the quality-education programs improperly make an unspecified level of student achievement a condition of partial unitary status? Stone addresses those questions on the established remedial record. He follows Souter's objection to using them to reopen the broader foundation of the magnet-school remedy without adequate notice and briefing.

**Stone's disposition: affirm now, preserve a meaningful route to release.** Affirm the Eighth Circuit's judgment sustaining the challenged salary and quality-education orders on the record as understood by the dissent. Missouri has not established an entitlement to partial release on that record. The affirmance leaves the district court's continuing remedial jurisdiction in place and leaves Missouri free to seek partial or full release through a proper motion and supporting evidence. National test-score parity is not an indispensable condition of release. Affirmance does not make every program permanent or predetermine the result of a later release motion.

**Reasoning supporting affirmance.** The remedial obligation arises from Missouri's and the district's established unconstitutional segregation and its continuing consequences. Educational measures may address the achievement deficits caused by that system, and salary support may be reasonably related to delivering those measures. Low salaries need not themselves constitute a separate constitutional violation. On Souter's reading, the findings connected salary support to recruitment, retention, and implementation of the remedy, including the contribution of noninstructional staff. That supports the challenged orders without establishing that every future salary increase is justified. Improvements within the victim district do not become an impermissible interdistrict remedy merely because they may attract students voluntarily. [Souter's dissent](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/93-1823.ZD.html).

The release question concerns the effects of the remedy as well as implementation. Establishing programs and spending money do not by themselves show that segregation's consequences have been eliminated to the extent practicable. Conversely, low test scores alone do not establish that a constitutional remedial obligation continues: their causes and significance require evaluation in the record. Stone does not invent a finding that the district court made national score parity a universal legal condition. The present failure of proof permits affirmance while leaving later relief available on an adequate showing.

**Relation to the simulated Shaw.** Jenkins begins with an independently established constitutional injury from intentional school segregation. The simulated rejection of a racial-design-only districting claim does not remove that injury or decide the permissible scope of its remedy. The connection is the need to identify the actual constitutional wrong and justify relief in relation to it. The duration and means of school-desegregation relief remain governed by the remedial principles applicable to that wrong.

**Selected remedial approach: joint development with independent review.** This is Stone's preferred method for developing and revising the continuing remedy within the district court's equitable discretion. It is compatible with the disposition because the three elements perform different work: affirmance resolves the challenged orders on the present record; the release standard governs the eventual withdrawal of supervision; joint development and independent review improve proposals and evidence while jurisdiction continues. Stone would recognize this method as available and explain its limits. He would not make affirmance conditional on creating a prescribed supervisory body. An order requiring a particular new arrangement would require its own procedural authority, record support, and opportunity for the parties to be heard.

**Participation and joint development.** State and district officials and the plaintiff class, through its representatives and counsel, should develop proposed revisions with meaningful participation by affected families and relevant educators. Participants need timely access to proposals and supporting information, an opportunity to offer alternatives, and a record of material disagreements. Families need a practical opportunity to explain how a program operates in their schools; a general invitation to attend a hearing may be insufficient. School officials retain responsibility for administration. Participation supplements the class's legal representation. Agreement does not displace the governing legal obligation, and refusal to cooperate does not veto compliance. When joint development does not resolve a dispute, the parties present their positions and the judge decides.

**Independent review and transparency.** A qualified neutral reviewer, selected by the court after hearing the parties about qualifications and conflicts, may assess implementation, the reliability of the evidence, and proposed alternatives. The reviewer should be independent of those responsible for designing or administering the measures under review. The appointment must rest on applicable procedural authority, with a defined scope, access to relevant information, and an opportunity for the parties to challenge the report and its evidentiary basis. Reports and judicial reasons should explain the remaining injury, what the remedy actually delivers, its burdens, and why continuation, revision, or withdrawal is justified, with appropriate protection for student information. The reviewer advises; the judge makes the findings, enforces the decree, and decides compliance and release. Review itself should remain proportionate to the unresolved remedial work. Ex parte Peterson supports judicial assistance with defined duties; La Buy supplies the limit against displacement of the judicial function. Neither supplies unrestricted authority for any proposed appointment. [Ex parte Peterson, 253 U.S. 300 (1920)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/253/300); [La Buy v. Howes Leather Co., 352 U.S. 249 (1957)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/352/249).

**Revisable measures.** The participants may propose changes to educational programs, staffing measures, implementation schedules, and the evidence used to assess progress. An ineffective program need not be preserved merely because it appeared in the original decree. A changed measure of progress requires an explanation of what the earlier measure missed and how the new evidence bears on the same remedial question; comparisons over time should remain intelligible. For example, a salary proposal may be assessed through evidence about recruitment, retention, and delivery of remedial instruction, alongside educational effects and practicable alternatives. Improved retention alone does not establish that the educational injury has been remedied. These are possible sources of evidence, not a compulsory checklist. The object remains curing the established consequences of unconstitutional segregation. Revising methods cannot become a way to declare an uncured injury cured or to enlarge the court's mission into general educational improvement.

**A meaningful route to partial and full release.** The defendant seeking release must make the applicable showing of good-faith compliance and elimination of the identified vestiges to the extent practicable. For partial withdrawal, the court considers full and satisfactory compliance in the area proposed for release, the need for retained control to remedy connected areas, and the defendant's good-faith commitment to the decree and its underlying legal obligations. The state and district's respective responsibilities must be assessed; evidence of one defendant's conduct does not automatically establish the other's compliance. Dowell supplies the temporary remedial purpose and dissolution standard; Freeman permits withdrawal in stages while accounting for interdependence. [Board of Education v. Dowell, 498 U.S. 237 (1991)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/498/237); [Freeman v. Pitts, 503 U.S. 467 (1992)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/503/467).

A release motion does not require the plaintiffs' agreement, the reviewer's endorsement, or consensus among participants. The judge must resolve it on the evidence and explain any continuing need for control; participation and review cannot become additional substantive conditions of release or a reason to postpone decision indefinitely. If further evidence is needed, the court should identify the material deficiency. Monitoring should narrow with the remaining remedial responsibility and end when that responsibility has been discharged. Neither a universal sunset nor a demand for guaranteed future perfection governs. Release ends the relevant decree obligations while leaving the defendants subject to ordinary constitutional requirements.

**Administrability and limits.** Stone's judgment is that this arrangement is administrable because it assigns recognizable responsibilities: officials administer; participants develop and challenge proposals; independent review examines the evidence; the judge resolves legal disputes. Reviews should occur at intervals suited to implementation and the school calendar, with earlier consideration when material problems require it. Written obligations and reasons must be sufficiently clear for the parties to understand what remains required and for appellate review of the court's decision. The approach still demands resources and judgment. Negotiations can be dominated by better-resourced participants, and review can add cost or delay. Meaningful participation, a focused assignment, and judicial resolution of unresolved disputes address those risks. The Supreme Court should articulate the legal boundaries and leave the particular administrative arrangement to properly supported district-court proceedings, without imposing one organizational form, numerical benchmark, or mandatory evidentiary checklist on every case.

**Contemporaneous theoretical context.** Susan Sturm's [A Normative Theory of Public Law Remedies (1991)](https://scholarship.law.columbia.edu/faculty_scholarship/1110/) provides a relevant account of participation and reasoned dialogue in public-law remedies. It informs the choice of remedial process as scholarship, not as legal authority or an additional condition for release. It supports the legitimacy of the approach without establishing that collaboration will succeed in every institution.

### McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co., 513 U.S. 352 (1995)

**Historical question.** Does after-acquired evidence of employee wrongdoing defeat an ADEA claim arising from an unlawfully motivated discharge?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse and remand; liability is not erased, but relief is limited by proof of what the employer lawfully would have done once it learned the facts.

**Reasoning.** Separate the original discrimination from the counterfactual remedial loss. Backpay and reinstatement must reflect their actual legal predicates. This prevents the equal-access project from turning into a windfall while preserving an enforceable prohibition. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/513/352/).

### City of Edmonds v. Oxford House, 514 U.S. 725 (1995)

**Historical question.** Was a zoning rule defining permitted household composition exempt from the Fair Housing Act as a maximum-occupancy restriction?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm that the exemption does not cover the challenged family-composition rule; leave the discrimination and accommodation merits for further proceedings.

**Reasoning.** Apply the statutory distinction between crowding limits and rules governing who may live together. An access claim becomes administrable through the enacted accommodation standard, not a judicial declaration that all zoning must yield. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/514/725/).

### Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, 514 U.S. 211 (1995)

**Historical question.** Could Congress require federal courts to reopen final judgments dismissing securities claims?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm invalidation of that reopening requirement.

**Reasoning.** Congress can change prospective law and sometimes govern pending cases. Reopening final Article III judgments crosses a distinct separation-of-powers boundary. A remedial aim cannot dissolve finality. Later modification of a continuing injunction must be analyzed separately. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/514/211/).

### Kelley v. Board of Trustees, 35 F.3d 265 (7th Cir. 1994)

**Simulation vehicle: OT1994, May 22, 1995.**

**Historical questions.** Did eliminating the men's swimming team while retaining the women's team violate Title IX or equal protection? Were the governing athletics regulations and policy interpretation valid?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm rejection of these challenges.

**Reasoning.** Title IX permits institutions to address unequal athletic opportunity in an overall program. ### Day v. Holahan, 34 F.3d 1356 (8th Cir. 1994)

**Simulation vehicle: OT1994, May 15, 1995.**

**Historical questions.** Did Minnesota's responses to independent expenditures, including additional financing and adjusted limits for affected candidates, burden protected speech? Were related contribution and nonprofit-corporation restrictions constitutional?

**Stone's disposition.** Preserve invalidation of the actual responsive-financing arrangement and linked reporting mechanism on its insufficiently fitted justification, while rejecting a categorical rule that public financing of an opponent's speech is itself unconstitutional. Also preserve invalidation of the $100 annual PAC contribution restriction and the improper as-applied restriction on the qualifying nonprofit's spending.

**Reasoning.** Financing more speech is not identical to penalizing the original speaker. But the actual Minnesota scheme lacked the Maine cap later examined in *Daggett*, and the offered participation rationale was poorly connected to a program already attracting nearly all candidates. ### Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia, 515 U.S. 819 (1995)

**Historical questions.** Did exclusion of a student publication because of its religious viewpoint violate free speech, and would payment of its printing costs through the generally available program violate the Establishment Clause?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse and require viewpoint-neutral access to the printing benefit on the historical program's terms.

**Historical question.** Could Massachusetts require private parade organizers to include an identifiable contingent conveying a message the organizers did not wish to include?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the compelled-inclusion judgment.

**Reasoning.** A parade is expression even without a single narrow message or rigorous editorial selection. ## OT1995: decisions principally issued in 1996



### Bush v. Vera, 517 U.S. 952 (1996)

**Status.** Reconstructed counterfactual simulated grant. The historical Shaw merits posture does not naturally survive the simulated *Shaw*. The reconstruction uses the standing issue actually present in the Texas litigation rather than recreating the historical racial-gerrymandering cause of action.

**Question Presented.** Whether voters who allege no dilution of their votes, exclusion from political participation, discriminatory electoral treatment, or other concrete personal inequality have Article III standing to challenge congressional districts solely because the legislature consciously used race in constructing them.

**Why reconstruct this case.** The Texas litigation itself contained a distinct personal-injury and standing issue. That makes *Vera* a plausible second clarification after *Miller*: *Miller* develops equal political membership and the constitutional room for voluntary race-conscious electoral inclusion; *Vera* addresses whether residence in a race-conscious district supplies Article III injury when the voter alleges no dilution, exclusion, unequal treatment, or other concrete harm. The reconstruction should stay on that jurisdictional question unless the new record independently presents something more. It is not a back door for reviving the historical Shaw merits inquiry or for narrowing statutory voting rights.

**Stone's position.** No. An ideological objection to governmental consideration of race, or residence in a district whose lines were consciously drawn with racial data, is not by itself a concrete personal injury after *Shaw*. A plaintiff must identify the individualized injury supplied by the governing constitutional or statutory claim.

**Reasoning.** Standing and merits must not collapse into one another. If Equal Protection does not recognize race-conscious territorial design alone as a deprivation, residence inside the resulting district cannot automatically create Article III injury by relabeling the same theory as standing. Dilution, exclusion, materially unequal participation, differential legal eligibility, or another cognizable personal inequality can supply injury when properly alleged and traced to the plan. Unusual shape and demographic design remain evidence where such a claim makes them relevant.

**Boundary.** This does not hold that voters can never challenge race-conscious districting. It holds only that the Court needs a personal legal injury beyond objection to the government's use of racial information.

### Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia, 517 U.S. 186 (1996)

**Historical questions.** Did the party's fee for participation in a convention nominating a U.S. Senate candidate require §5 preclearance? Could private plaintiffs sue under §10's poll-tax provisions?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse and remand on both issues: preclearance is required, and the private §10 claim is cognizable. Leave whether the particular fee is an unlawful poll tax for initial merits determination below.

**Reasoning.** A party exercising a state-authorized role in selecting candidates cannot automatically move a regulated voting practice beyond the VRA by using a convention. The Act's structure, contemporaneous remedial context, and express recognition of private enforcement support the §10 action. Statutory coverage is not an adjudication of the ultimate poll-tax claim. This establishes a concrete private-enforcement foundation before *Sandoval*. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/517/186/).

**Historical questions.** Did excluding women from VMI violate equal protection, and did the proposed separate program cure the violation?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the judgment approving the proposed remedy and require a constitutionally adequate response.

**Reasoning.** Government must provide an exceedingly persuasive justification grounded in actual objectives, not broad generalizations about women. ### Romer v. Evans, 517 U.S. 620 (1996)

**Historical question.** Did Colorado's Amendment 2 violate equal protection by broadly withdrawing ordinary legal protections from gay, lesbian, and bisexual people?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm invalidation.

**Reasoning.** The breadth and structure of the exclusion do not fit the legitimate interests offered. A group cannot be placed outside ordinary protective law through a sweeping disability. ### Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, 517 U.S. 44 (1996)

**Historical questions.** Could Congress authorize the IGRA suit against Florida through its Indian Commerce power? Could a suit against the governor proceed under *Ex parte Young*?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm dismissal of the direct action against Florida, but reverse the categorical rejection of the prospective officer action and remand that claim for enforcement within IGRA's statutory sequence.

**Reasoning.** Stone answers both questions. Congress has substantive authority over Indian commerce and unmistakably authorized the IGRA action, but Article I authority does not by itself remove the State's immunity from being made the defendant in a private federal suit. A federal duty, a cause of action, the defendant, immunity, and relief are separate legal questions. The officer action is different: a responsible officer maintaining an ongoing violation of federal law may be ordered prospectively to comply. That route does not enlarge IGRA. The court must respect Congress's negotiation, good-faith determination, mediation, timing, and other remedial limits rather than use *Young* to invent a broader bargaining decree. Valid Reconstruction-Amendment abrogation and actual state waiver remain separate sources for later cases.

### Wisconsin v. City of New York, 517 U.S. 1 (1996)

**Historical question.** Was the Secretary's refusal to statistically adjust the 1990 census constitutionally permissible, or was heightened judicial review required?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the Second Circuit and uphold the refusal on this record.

**Reasoning.** The Constitution permits reasoned discretion over enumeration methods. Numerical accuracy and distributive accuracy are not identical, and the court cannot select a statistical adjustment merely because it might improve one measure. Review for a reasonable relationship to an actual enumeration, while preserving challenges to proven discrimination or arbitrary action. This case permits adjustment where lawful and justified; it does not require it. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/517/1/).

## OT1996: decisions principally issued in 1997

### Lopez v. Monterey County, 519 U.S. 9 (1996)

**Historical question.** Could the district court order elections under an unprecleared judicial-election plan in a covered county?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse and remand; enforce the preclearance requirement before implementation, subject to the recognized rules for genuinely court-fashioned relief.

**Reasoning.** A federal court cannot ordinarily give an unprecleared legislative change an implementation route that the covered jurisdiction itself lacks. Coverage, appropriate interim relief, and ultimate substantive validity are distinct. This case does not yet decide the later question whether a covered county must preclear changes required by a noncovered state. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/519/9/).

### Young v. Fordice, 520 U.S. 273 (1997)

**Historical questions.** What was Mississippi's operative preclearance benchmark, and did discretionary registration changes in implementing the NVRA require separate §5 review?

**Stone's disposition.** Vacate and remand for preclearance of the relevant changes.

**Reasoning.** Implementing a federal statute does not exempt additional discretionary state changes from another applicable federal requirement. Identify the procedure actually in force or effect under the governing benchmark rule; do not turn a briefly used administrative practice into the benchmark without legal analysis. NVRA duties and §5 review can operate together. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/520/273/).

### Reno v. Bossier Parish School Board, 520 U.S. 471 (1997), Bossier I

**Historical questions.** Could §5 preclearance be denied solely because a plan violated §2? Was evidence of a §2 violation relevant to discriminatory purpose under §5?

**Stone's disposition.** Preserve the distinction between the two statutes' operative inquiries, vacate the preclearance judgment, and remand for consideration of the relevant purpose evidence.

**Reasoning.** Section 5's effect inquiry protects against retrogression; it need not become a second, complete §2 trial. But evidence that the jurisdiction rejected a workable nondilutive alternative can illuminate purpose. The adjudicator must examine that evidence rather than exclude it categorically. Stone flags that statutory purpose is not necessarily limited to an intent to worsen an already discriminatory baseline. The broader purpose issue can be reserved here and squarely decided in *Bossier II*. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/520/471/).



### City of Boerne v. Flores, 521 U.S. 507 (1997)

**Historical question.** Was RFRA's imposition of its substantial-burden/compelling-interest command on state and local government a valid exercise of Fourteenth Amendment §5 power?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the Fifth Circuit. RFRA's across-the-board state and local command exceeds §5 on the justification offered. The city cannot be held liable solely by treating that command as a valid §5 enactment. Preserve independently available constitutional claims and distinct congressional powers not decided here.

**Reasoning.** Congress may prevent and remedy constitutional wrongdoing without proving a completed violation in every application. But it cannot simply substitute a different substantive constitutional rule for the judicially defined Free Exercise guarantee across virtually all state and local government activity. The breadth of RFRA's command, the demanding burden it imposes, and its connection to the constitutional wrong must be considered together. Stone retains judicial responsibility to interpret the Constitution and the distinction between enforcement and redefinition. [Historical opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/95-2074.ZO.html).

He then states an affirmative rule: Congress may define a preventive class when evidence and established experience reasonably connect the class to a constitutional mechanism of exclusion, evasion, or recurrence. A results rule, burden allocation, or preimplementation review may be appropriate because case-by-case proof of intent is predictably ineffective. The governing inquiry is whether the intervention is fitted to that problem, including the relevant government conduct and the remedy's reach. The VRA precedents illustrate real enforcement legislation that reaches some conduct not independently unconstitutional. [*South Carolina v. Katzenbach*, 383 U.S. 301](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/383/301/); [*City of Rome*, 446 U.S. 156](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/446/156/).

**Express future rule.** “Within a constitutionally valid preventive category, the claimant proves the enacted statutory elements. The claimant need not additionally establish the constitutional violation that the category was designed to prevent.” This is Stone's proposed formulation, not a quotation from the historical opinion.

**The important boundary.** Evidence can justify a preventive legal rule. Evidence alone cannot enact that rule, create a cause of action, waive immunity, or authorize a court to replace statutory text. The constitutional analysis of governmental racial treatment and the separate analysis of Congress's enforcement authority answer different questions. Neither substitutes for the other.

### Thomasson v. Perry, 80 F.3d 915 (4th Cir. 1996)

**Simulation vehicle: OT1996, June 9, 1997.**

**Historical questions.** Did §654 and its implementing statements policy violate equal protection or free speech, and was Thomasson's discharge lawful under the statute's rebuttable presumption?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the discharge and direct reinstatement, remanding only for implementation of lawful personnel and monetary consequences. Sustain military regulation of prohibited conduct, but invalidate the use of Thomasson's identity statement as sufficient proof of prohibited conduct, propensity, or intent.

**Reasoning.** Apply *Walmer*'s heightened equality review with substantial respect for demonstrated military needs, not automatic acceptance of a status proxy. The military may regulate actual conduct and may use speech as evidence when the statement admits conduct or expresses a concrete intent to violate a valid rule. It may not make the sentence "I am gay" the operative ground for discharge by shifting to the officer the burden of proving that his orientation will never result in prohibited conduct. That mechanism burdens a historically disfavored status through a stereotype about future behavior and is not adequately fitted to discipline or readiness when the government has no conduct evidence and the officer's service record is concededly excellent.

The First Amendment leads to the same administrable boundary. Speech does not become immune from evidentiary use merely because it is speech, but an identity statement and political disagreement cannot themselves be punished as misconduct. The government must prove the legally relevant conduct or intent; it cannot presume it conclusively or effectively conclusively from protected expression. Thomasson's refusal to deny his orientation is therefore not a failure of constitutional proof. This holding does not repeal §654 or prevent discharge based on proved prohibited acts or a genuine statement of intent to commit them. [Actual appellate opinion and dissents](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/80/915/627632/).

### Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network of Western New York, 519 U.S. 357 (1997)

**Historical question.** Were the fixed and floating buffer zones in the clinic-access injunction consistent with the First Amendment?

**Stone's disposition.** Sustain the supported fixed entrance zones and invalidate the floating zones on the historical record.

**Reasoning.** Apply *Madsen* to the actual obstruction and the practical burden on speech. A fixed boundary is knowable and can protect an entrance; a moving exclusion zone around every person creates different burdens and uncertainty. Counseling exceptions and commands to cease unwanted approaches must be interpreted consistently with the specific record and the injunction's lawful scope. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/519/357/).

## OT1997: decisions principally issued in 1998

### Foster v. Love, 522 U.S. 67 (1997)

**Historical question.** Was Louisiana's system, which could conclusively elect members of Congress before the federally prescribed election day, preempted?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm preemption of the conflicting arrangement.

**Reasoning.** Congress may set a uniform federal election day under its constitutional authority. Distinguish completing the election before that day from permissible preliminary procedures. This reinforces *Voting Rights Coalition* without granting Congress unlimited authority over all state electoral choices. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/522/67/).

### General Electric Co. v. Joiner, 522 U.S. 136 (1997)

**Historical question.** What appellate standard applies to exclusion of scientific expert evidence, and was the exclusion within the trial court's discretion?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the appellate court's overly searching standard and remand consistently with abuse-of-discretion review.

**Reasoning.** Judges may examine whether a conclusion follows from the studies offered, including a large analytical gap. They should not exclude evidence merely because experts disagree. The legal allocation of responsibility remains consistent with district-specific findings in voting and remedial cases. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/522/136/).

### Bragdon v. Abbott, 524 U.S. 624 (1998)

**Historical questions.** Was asymptomatic HIV a disability under the ADA, and what evidence governed the dentist's claimed direct-threat justification?

**Stone's disposition.** Uphold disability coverage and remand the threat analysis to the extent required by the record.

**Reasoning.** Assess the relevant major life activity and actual medical risk. A professional's sincere belief is not the same as an objectively supported threat. Use knowledge available at the time of the decision, not later science to manufacture retrospective fault. This is statutory access adjudication with a concrete safety defense. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/524/624/).

### Pennsylvania Department of Corrections v. Yeskey, 524 U.S. 206 (1998)

**Historical question.** Does ADA Title II cover state prisoners and the prison program at issue?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm statutory coverage.

**Reasoning.** The enacted language includes state public entities and their services and programs; involuntary participation does not create an unstated prison exemption. Coverage does not decide a particular accommodation's reasonableness or the distinct immunity question. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/524/206/).

### Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District, 524 U.S. 274 (1998)

**Historical question.** When is a federally funded school district liable in damages under Title IX for a teacher's sexual harassment of a student?

**Stone's disposition.** Vacate the no-liability judgment and remand for application of ordinary, appropriately bounded agency principles to the teacher's exercise of delegated educational authority. Reject a universal requirement that a separate corrective official receive actual notice before the recipient can be liable for its agent's abuse of that authority.

**Reasoning.** Stone adopts the central position of the historical dissent. The recipient is already on notice that intentional sex discrimination in its educational program is prohibited. The harder issue is attribution, not whether Congress used the exact later judicial formula for administrative notice. The district is not automatically liable for every private act by an employee: plaintiffs must establish actionable harassment, a legally sufficient agency connection, and compensable injury. This distinction will matter in *Davis*, involving other students rather than a teacher's delegated authority. [Opinion and dissent](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/524/274/).

### Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, 523 U.S. 75 (1998)

**Historical question.** Can same-sex workplace harassment violate Title VII?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the categorical exclusion and remand.

**Reasoning.** Apply the statutory prohibition to the actual discrimination, even if the precise scenario was not Congress's principal anticipated example. The claimant must still establish harassment because of sex and an actionable environment. Statutory generality is not permission to ignore its elements. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/523/75/).

### Faragher v. City of Boca Raton, 524 U.S. 775 (1998), and Burlington Industries v. Ellerth, 524 U.S. 742 (1998)

**Historical questions.** When is an employer responsible under Title VII for supervisor harassment, and what role do a tangible employment action and preventive/corrective measures play?

**Stone's dispositions.** Apply vicarious responsibility for the supervisor's legally attributable conduct; recognize the limited affirmative defense where no tangible employment action occurred. In *Faragher*, reverse the appellate judgment; in *Ellerth*, affirm the remand under the appropriate framework.

**Reasoning.** Institutions should have incentives to prevent discrimination, provide usable complaint routes, and correct it. Claimants likewise must act reasonably where the applicable defense requires that inquiry. Neither a paper policy nor an employee's job title conclusively establishes effective prevention or employer responsibility. These are Title VII rules; transfer to Title IX requires a separate statutory argument. [*Faragher*](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/524/775/); [*Ellerth*](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/524/742/).

### Miller v. Albright, 523 U.S. 420 (1998)

**Historical questions.** Could the citizenship claimant challenge different statutory requirements for an unmarried citizen father and mother? Were the requirements constitutional, and could a court supply the claimed citizenship remedy?

**Stone's disposition.** Permit the sex-equality challenge, hold the paternal-only requirements unconstitutional, sever the discriminatory conditions, and direct recognition of Miller's citizenship at birth under the remaining statutory grant.

**Reasoning.** The differential conditions rest on sex-based assumptions about which unmarried parent will prove parentage, assume responsibility, or form the relevant relationship. Those assumptions do not satisfy heightened scrutiny where Congress can require neutral proof of biological parentage and whatever parent-child connection it considers lawfully necessary without assigning different legal burdens by sex. Stone also answers the remedy question. The INA contains a broad severability command, and the challenged provisions operate as restrictions on an otherwise automatic statutory grant of citizenship at birth. Excising the unconstitutional paternal-only conditions does not ask a court to naturalize someone in equity; it leaves Congress's remaining grant to operate according to its own terms. Where the neutral parentage and citizen-parent residence predicates are established on this record, the proper judgment is a declaration of citizenship, not another remand to decide whether courts possess a remedy. [Opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/523/420/).

### FEC v. Akins, 524 U.S. 11 (1998)

**Historical questions.** Did voters denied allegedly required campaign information have standing to challenge the FEC's dismissal, and how should the agency's political-committee determination be reviewed?

**Stone's disposition.** Recognize the concrete informational injury and remand for the appropriate agency consideration.

**Reasoning.** An injury does not become nonjusticiable merely because many voters share it. The statutory disclosure entitlement and its relevance to voting supply a concrete basis. Recognition of standing does not establish that the organization must ultimately be regulated as a political committee. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/524/11/).

### Crawford-El v. Britton, 523 U.S. 574 (1998)

**Historical question.** Could courts impose a clear-and-convincing proof requirement for unconstitutional motive in damages litigation against public officials?

**Stone's disposition.** Reject the extra burden and vacate and remand.

**Reasoning.** Qualified immunity does not authorize judges to rewrite the proof standard for intent. Courts can sequence and limit discovery, require relevant evidence, and use summary judgment. Administrability comes from those existing tools, not an evidentiary rule that systematically prevents proof of concealed discrimination. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/523/574/).

### Campbell v. Louisiana, 523 U.S. 392 (1998)

**Historical question.** Could a white criminal defendant challenge racial discrimination in selecting a grand-jury foreperson and grand jurors?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the standing barrier and remand.

**Reasoning.** The defendant's exposure to the discriminatory criminal process and the recognized relationship to excluded jurors support the challenge. This is not the generalized injury rejected in *Hays*. The different institutional setting supplies its own personal and third-party-standing analysis. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/523/392/).

### Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes, 523 U.S. 666 (1998)

**Historical question.** Did a public broadcaster violate the First Amendment by excluding an independent candidate from a televised candidate debate?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the appellate judgment requiring inclusion; sustain reasonable, viewpoint-neutral selection in the nonpublic-forum debate on the historical record.

**Reasoning.** The register's question assumed that satisfying an announced participation standard settled entitlement. The actual case required forum classification and examination of the broadcaster's journalistic selection. Low public support can be a relevant neutral criterion; hostility to a candidate's viewpoint cannot. Equal access does not require every specialized forum to admit every willing speaker. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/523/666/).

### Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht, 524 U.S. 381 (1998)

**Historical question.** Did the presence of a potentially immune state claim prevent removal of the entire action to federal court?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse that categorical jurisdictional rule.

**Reasoning.** Assess the actual jurisdiction and immunity of each claim. State immunity is not a device for erasing federal jurisdiction over different, otherwise cognizable claims. Preserve the defense where it properly applies. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/524/381/).

### Duffy v. Wolle, 123 F.3d 1026 (8th Cir. 1997)

**Simulation vehicle: OT1997, June 3, 1998.**

**Historical questions.** Could a male applicant pursue his constitutional sex-discrimination claim concerning a federal probation-office appointment, and was summary judgment proper without requested discovery into the asserted hiring reasons? What immunity and alternative-remedy objections applied?

**Stone's disposition.** Vacate the premature summary judgment and remand for focused discovery on discriminatory purpose. Hold that the Fifth Amendment sex-discrimination claim is cognizable under *Davis v. Passman*, that the judges lack absolute judicial immunity for this administrative hiring act, that the Judicial Conference EEO plan does not displace the constitutional damages action absent a congressional decision to make it exclusive, and that qualified immunity does not supply a categorical bar because the right of a federal applicant to be free from intentional sex discrimination was clearly established.

**Reasoning.** Answer the remedial questions before remanding the factual one. Hiring a chief probation officer is important to judicial administration, but it is an employment decision rather than adjudication between parties, so *Forrester* forecloses absolute judicial immunity. *Davis* already recognizes a damages remedy for federal sex discrimination in a setting without an exclusive congressionally created remedial system. An administratively adopted EEO process can provide useful relief without silently extinguishing that constitutional action; displacement requires a legally attributable congressional choice, not the existence of any alternative complaint procedure. Qualified immunity protects reasonable legal error, not intentional sex discrimination under a clearly established equal-protection rule. The unresolved question is factual: whether the panel actually selected the successful applicant because of sex. Duffy is entitled to discovery sufficient to test the asserted nondiscriminatory reasons, not a general inquiry into judges' adjudicative work. [Actual appellate opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/123/1026/624823/).

### Richenberg v. Perry, 97 F.3d 256 (8th Cir. 1996)

**Simulation alias: Richenberg v. Cohen; OT1997, December 8, 1997.**

**Historical questions.** Did the statements policy and this Air Force discharge violate due process/equal protection, free speech, or the Bill of Attainder Clause? Was the discharge lawful under the APA and governing statute?

**Stone's disposition.** Reject the Bill of Attainder claim, but reverse the discharge on equal-protection, First Amendment, and APA grounds and direct reinstatement, with remand limited to implementation of the remedy.

**Reasoning.** *Thomasson* now supplies the governing rule rather than another reason to postpone it. A statement of orientation may open an inquiry, but government must prove the conduct, propensity, or concrete intent that the valid military rule makes relevant. Richenberg expressly denied an intent to engage in prohibited acts, produced strong testimony concerning discipline, honesty, performance, and abstention, and faced no evidence of prohibited conduct. Sexual attraction and equivocal answers to hypothetical questions do not constitutionally substitute for proof of future misconduct. Treating those facts as enough makes the statutory presumption effectively irrebuttable and reproduces the status inference *Thomasson* rejected.

The First Amendment permits evidentiary use of speech to prove a legally relevant fact; it does not permit the government to make protected self-identification itself the disqualifying fact. The same defect makes the agency decision arbitrary under the governing statute and unsupported by substantial evidence once orientation is removed as a sufficient proxy. The attainder theory still fails because the personnel rule is not legislative punishment of a named person or closed class in the constitutional sense. [Actual appellate opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/97/256/555028/).

## OT1998: decisions principally issued in 1999

### Lopez v. Monterey County, 525 U.S. 266 (1999)

**Historical questions.** Must a covered county obtain §5 preclearance for voting changes required by a noncovered state, even where the county lacks discretion? Is that application within Congress's Fifteenth Amendment enforcement power?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse and remand; require preclearance.

**Reasoning.** Coverage attaches to the county's implementation of the voting change. Allowing a noncovered state to dictate an unreviewed change inside a covered jurisdiction would defeat the preventive mechanism. The relevant constitutional justification concerns the coverage category and the risk of discriminatory change; it does not require proof that this county official personally acted with discriminatory intent. Congress's Fifteenth Amendment power supports that fitted protection, consistent with *City of Rome*. [Opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/97-1396.ZO.html).

**Boerne connection.** Judicially defined voting equality supplies the constitutional target. Preclearance supplies a preventive method. The method validly reaches some innocent changes because waiting for completed discrimination would undermine its function. The county retains the statute's routes to approval, and this decision does not grant DOJ substantive power to demand racial maximization.

### Department of Commerce v. U.S. House of Representatives, 525 U.S. 316 (1999)

**Historical questions.** Did the challengers establish standing? Did the Census Act permit the planned sampling for congressional apportionment? Was a constitutional ruling necessary?

**Stone's disposition.** Sustain the injunction on the statutory ground in the private-plaintiff litigation; handle the companion House case consistently with that dispositive ruling. Leave the Enumeration Clause question unresolved.

**Reasoning.** The actual holding turned on the Census Act's restriction, particularly §195, not a constitutional ban on statistical methods. Stone cannot use studies favoring sampling to rewrite the statute. Congress may consider changing the law; the Court may decide the resulting constitutional question if properly presented. Standing requires the concrete representational injury supported in the record. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/525/316/).



### Saenz v. Roe, 526 U.S. 489 (1999)

**Historical question.** Could California limit new residents' welfare benefits to the level paid by their former state during their first year of residence, including under federal statutory permission?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm the injunction against the unequal newcomer rule.

**Reasoning.** The Fourteenth Amendment's protection of equal citizenship upon becoming a state resident limits durational discrimination. Congress cannot authorize a state to violate that constitutional protection. This is an important instance where a formally enacted congressional permission still cannot supply a valid gateway. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/526/489/).

### Olmstead v. L.C., 527 U.S. 581 (1999)

**Historical questions.** Can unjustified institutional isolation constitute ADA Title II discrimination, and what limits follow from professional suitability, the individual's wishes, reasonable accommodation, and fundamental alteration?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm the integration principle, vacate the overly restrictive treatment of the state's resource defense, and remand.

**Reasoning.** A service may be formally available while its unjustified mode of delivery denies meaningful community participation. Apply the enacted integration and modification rules where community placement is appropriate, is not opposed by the individual, and can reasonably be accommodated. Consider the system's resources and other people with disabilities. A credible plan and a waiting list moving at a reasonable pace may matter; a perpetual administrative promise is not compliance. This is a bounded statutory service obligation, not a constitutional guarantee of every requested placement. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/527/581/).

### Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank, 527 U.S. 627 (1999)

**Historical question.** Did Congress validly remove state immunity for the patent-infringement damages action?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the Federal Circuit and dismiss the direct damages action against the nonconsenting state entity on sovereign-immunity grounds.

**Reasoning.** *Seminole Tribe* removes the Article I shortcut. The Patent Clause validly imposes federal patent obligations on States, but a private damages action against the State requires waiver or a separate constitutional source for abrogation. Section 5 does not supply that source here. Congress did not identify a sufficiently developed pattern of States intentionally or otherwise unconstitutionally depriving patent owners of property without adequate process, while the remedy applies to infringement categorically without tracking unconstitutional deprivation, culpability, or the availability of process. Patent infringement and a Fourteenth Amendment deprivation therefore remain distinct legal concepts. The patent duty survives; waiver, federal enforcement, and properly available prospective officer relief remain separate mechanisms. [Opinion and dissents](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/527/627/).

### College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid, 527 U.S. 666 (1999)

**Historical questions.** Did Congress validly abrogate immunity for the Lanham Act false-advertising claim? Had the State waived immunity by participating in the relevant market?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm the immunity dismissal on both theories.

**Reasoning.** Congress clearly attempted to authorize the action, but after *Seminole Tribe* the Commerce Clause does not provide power to make a nonconsenting State a private damages defendant. The false-advertising claim also supplies no independent § 5 basis for retrospective state liability. Waiver is different from abrogation and must be an actual, voluntary, sufficiently clear choice by the State. Participation in a federally regulated market cannot be converted into fictional consent to suit merely because Congress could regulate the activity. Substantive federal false-advertising rules may still govern the State's conduct through constitutionally available enforcement channels. [Opinion and dissents](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/527/666/).

### Alden v. Maine, 527 U.S. 706 (1999)

**Historical question.** Could Congress authorize the employees' FLSA action against a nonconsenting State in its own courts?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm Maine's immunity dismissal of the private damages action.

**Reasoning.** Congress may validly regulate a State as an employer under Article I without thereby acquiring authority to subject the nonconsenting State to a private damages suit. The immunity principle is constitutional structure rather than a federal-forum venue rule, so moving the same Article I damages action into state court does not cure the defect identified in *Seminole Tribe*. Public federal enforcement, actual waiver, valid Reconstruction-Amendment abrogation, and properly available prospective officer relief remain separate mechanisms. The substantive FLSA obligation is not erased merely because this private enforcement route is unavailable.

**Doctrinal change from the earlier build.** This decision must also confront *Hilton* candidly. To the extent Stone's earlier *Hilton* holding treated FELA coverage and statutory stare decisis as sufficient to compel a nonconsenting State to answer a private Article I damages action in its own courts, that remedial proposition is overruled. FELA's substantive coverage of state-owned railroads can remain, but statutory precedent cannot create constitutional power to impose a private damages forum that Article I does not supply. The governing sequence is source of duty → private action → defendant and forum → immunity, waiver, or valid abrogation → relief.

### Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael, 526 U.S. 137 (1999)

**Historical question.** Does reliability screening apply to technical and other specialized expertise, and how flexibly may a trial court conduct it?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the ruling limiting *Daubert* to scientific testimony and sustain the flexible gatekeeping approach.

**Reasoning.** The expert's label cannot substitute for a reliable method fitted to the task. Experience can be probative, but the expert must explain the inference. Do not impose laboratory-style validation on every demographic, historical, or administrative judgment. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/526/137/).

### Sutton v. United Air Lines, 527 U.S. 471 (1999), and Murphy v. UPS, 527 U.S. 516 (1999)

**Historical questions.** Must mitigating measures be considered when identifying an ADA disability? Did the applicants or employee satisfy the actual-disability or regarded-as definition on their records?

**Stone's dispositions.** Reject the mitigating-measures exclusion and remand the actual-disability claims for application of the statute without treating successful treatment as erasing the underlying limitation. Address regarded-as claims independently.

**Reasoning.** Stone adopts the contemporaneous dissenting interpretation, supported by the agency guidance then available. Coverage and ability to perform a particular job are separate inquiries. The condition must still substantially limit an identified major life activity; impairment alone is insufficient. An employer's belief that a person cannot perform one narrow job does not automatically prove that the employer regards the person as substantially limited in working generally. This proposed interpretation uses the original statute, not the later ADA Amendments Act as if it already existed. [*Sutton* and dissent](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/527/471/); [*Murphy*](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/527/516/).

### Albertson's, Inc. v. Kirkingburg, 527 U.S. 555 (1999)

**Historical questions.** What showing establishes disability for a monocular driver, and must an employer participate in the experimental DOT waiver program despite the otherwise applicable federal vision standard?

**Stone's disposition.** Uphold the employer's ability to rely on the applicable federal safety qualification in the circumstances presented. Reject any categorical rule that monocularity always establishes disability, while applying Stone's *Sutton* approach to mitigating measures.

**Reasoning.** Distinguish coverage from qualification. The ADA does not require the employer to disregard an applicable federal safety rule merely because an experimental waiver route exists. This does not authorize employers to invent unsupported safety rules of their own. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/527/555/).

### Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education, 526 U.S. 629 (1999)

**Historical question.** Could a school district be liable in damages under Title IX for deliberate indifference to known student-on-student sexual harassment?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse dismissal and permit the claim under a bounded recipient-liability rule.

**Reasoning.** Where the recipient controls the relevant setting and has actual knowledge of sufficiently serious harassment that deprives a student of educational access, deliberate indifference can be the recipient's own discrimination. Ordinary peer misconduct is not automatically attributable to the school. This differs from *Gebser*, where a teacher may act through delegated institutional authority. Schools need reasonable corrective action, not a judicial guarantee of a harassment-free environment or the victim's preferred sanction. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/526/629/).

### NCAA v. Smith, 525 U.S. 459 (1999)

**Historical question.** Did receipt of dues from federally funded member institutions make the NCAA a recipient of federal financial assistance under Title IX?

**Stone's disposition.** Vacate and remand; dues alone do not establish recipient status. Leave properly preserved alternative recipient theories for consideration below.

**Reasoning.** Statutory coverage must have a defensible stopping point. An entity does not become a federal funding recipient whenever money once associated with federal assistance enters its accounts. This limit is compatible with holding a genuinely covered institution responsible for its own program. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/525/459/).

### American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan, 526 U.S. 40 (1999)

**Historical questions.** Were private insurers' decisions to withhold disputed workers' compensation payments state action, and did claimants possess the asserted present property entitlement before medical necessity was established?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the appellate due-process ruling on the historical showing.

**Reasoning.** State permission and extensive regulation do not automatically attribute each private payment decision to government. Identify the actual entitlement and decisionmaker. *Lebron*'s government-control rule does not convert all regulated businesses into public actors. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/526/40/).

### Buckley v. American Constitutional Law Foundation, 525 U.S. 182 (1999)

**Historical question.** Were Colorado's petition-circulator registration, identification, and disclosure restrictions consistent with the First Amendment?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm invalidation of the challenged restrictions at the scope adjudicated.

**Reasoning.** Initiative circulation involves political advocacy and association. Examine each restriction's actual relation to fraud prevention, identification, and disclosure rather than presume all election regulations are harmless. Preserve narrower accountability measures that do not impose the same burden. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/525/182/).

### Dallas Fire Fighters Association v. City of Dallas, 150 F.3d 438 (5th Cir. 1998)

**Simulation alias: City of Dallas v. Dallas Fire Fighters Association; OT1998, June 18, 1999.**

**Historical questions.** Were the challenged out-of-rank promotions constitutional and consistent with applicable employment law? What separate result followed for the deputy-chief appointment?

**Stone's disposition.** Vacate the race-based promotions ruling for the supported racial-treatment analysis analysis; affirm rejection of the sex-based promotions on the insufficient sex-specific predicate actually presented. Preserve the separate judgment sustaining the deputy-chief appointment under the statutory analysis unless a preserved, independently supported issue requires further review.

**Reasoning.** The real racial record included a 1976 consent decree and upper-rank underrepresentation. That can justify investigation and a remedial predicate without proving every promotional preference substantially fitted. Assess the discriminatory hiring pipeline, relevant qualified workforce, progress under alternatives, burdens, and duration. The real appellate opinion described a weaker record concerning discrimination against women. Stone cannot replace that with the favorable factual findings asserted in the simulation register. ### Russell v. Burris, 146 F.3d 563 (8th Cir. 1998)

**Simulation vehicle: OT1998, April 28, 1999.**

**Historical questions.** Were Arkansas's candidate-contribution limits, the $200 annual PAC-receipt limit, and the preference for small-donor PACs constitutional? Did plaintiffs have standing to challenge the independent-expenditure-committee limit, and was the local-delegation challenge ripe?

### Cedar Rapids Community School District v. Garret F., 526 U.S. 66 (1999)

**Historical question.** Did IDEA require the district to provide the continuous school-day nursing services needed for the student to attend school, or were they excluded medical services?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm the required services.

**Reasoning.** Apply the physician-services distinction developed under the statute, rather than invent a complexity or expense exception that Congress did not enact. ## OT1999: decisions principally issued in 2000

### Adarand Constructors v. Slater, 528 U.S. 216 (2000)

**Historical question.** Did the plaintiff's later disadvantaged-business certification moot its challenge, given uncertainty about federal acceptance and continued eligibility?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the mootness judgment and remand if the historical controversy still exists.

**Reasoning.** Uncertain administrative relief does not establish that recurrence is sufficiently foreclosed. Government must satisfy the applicable mootness burden. The Court should not decide the program's merits in a procedural ruling. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/528/216/).

### Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents, 528 U.S. 62 (2000)

**Historical questions.** Did the ADEA clearly authorize private suits against States, and did Congress possess constitutional authority for that abrogation?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm the immunity dismissal of the private retrospective damages claims.

**Reasoning.** The ADEA clearly includes state employers, so Stone answers the power question rather than hiding behind statutory ambiguity. *Seminole Tribe* and *Alden* foreclose Article I as the abrogation source. Section 5 also does not sustain this remedy. Age classifications generally violate equal protection only when irrational, while the ADEA broadly prohibits many age distinctions that the Constitution permits. The state-employment record does not establish a recurring pattern of unconstitutional age discrimination strong enough to justify that breadth as a preventive § 5 damages remedy. This does not invalidate the ADEA's substantive regulation of state employment under the Commerce Clause or foreclose federal enforcement and other constitutionally available remedies. [Opinion and Stevens dissent](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/528/62/).

### Reno v. Condon, 528 U.S. 141 (2000)

**Historical question.** Did the Driver's Privacy Protection Act's restrictions on disclosure of personal motor-vehicle data violate federalism limits?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse invalidation and uphold the statute as applied to the state databases.

**Reasoning.** Congress regulated the state's activity involving information in interstate commerce rather than conscripting the state to regulate its citizens. This implements the line preserved in *Printz* and shows that administrable federalism does not require categorical immunity for state institutions. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/528/141/).

### Reno v. Bossier Parish School Board, 528 U.S. 320 (2000), Bossier II

**Historical questions.** Did the case remain live after the challenged plan's election cycle? Does §5's purpose prong prohibit discriminatory but nonretrogressive purpose?

**Stone's disposition.** Reject mootness; vacate the preclearance judgment and remand under a purpose standard that reaches discriminatory maintenance or adoption of unequal electoral opportunity, even absent intended retrogression.

**Reasoning.** The plan can remain relevant as the next benchmark. The purpose and effect prongs perform different functions: the effects rule prevents backsliding, while the purpose rule prevents government from deliberately using a new enactment to discriminate. Stone follows the historical dissent's central statutory argument. Evidence of a more favorable alternative is relevant but not conclusive proof of forbidden purpose. The covered jurisdiction bears the statutory preclearance burden; this is not a newly imposed plaintiff-intent element in §2. [Opinion and dissent](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/528/320/).

**Dependency.** If the restarted *Bossier I* proceedings already applied this purpose rule, review their findings rather than invent the same interpretive error again.

### Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC, 528 U.S. 377 (2000)

**Historical question.** Were Missouri's candidate-contribution limits constitutional, and what evidentiary justification was required?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the appellate invalidation and sustain the limits on the record presented.

**Reasoning.** Closely drawn contribution regulation does not require proof of a completed bribery prosecution before lawmakers can respond to corruption and its appearance. Evidence, experience, and the practical size of the limits matter. Neither *Buckley*'s historical dollar amount nor an abstract claim of “leveling” determines the answer. Preserve a real opportunity to show that a particular limit prevents effective political association. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/528/377/).

### Gutierrez v. Ada, 528 U.S. 250 (2000)

**Historical question.** Did the Organic Act's majority requirement for Guam's gubernatorial election count ballots not expressing a vote in that contest?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the construction requiring a runoff based on the broader ballot denominator.

**Reasoning.** Identify the statutory electoral unit and the votes cast for the office. ### Rice v. Cayetano, 528 U.S. 495 (2000)

**Historical question.** Could Hawaii limit voting for Office of Hawaiian Affairs trustees to persons satisfying the ancestry restriction?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse and invalidate the franchise restriction under the Fifteenth Amendment.

**Reasoning.** A state election cannot exclude otherwise qualified citizens through ancestry used as a racial proxy. ### Village of Willowbrook v. Olech, 528 U.S. 562 (2000)

**Historical question.** Could an individual state an equal-protection claim alleging intentionally unequal treatment without a rational basis, even without membership in a protected group?

### United States v. Morrison, 529 U.S. 598 (2000)

**Historical question.** Could Congress authorize the VAWA civil damages action against private perpetrators through the Commerce Clause or Fourteenth Amendment §5?

### Valdespino v. Alamo Heights Independent School District, 168 F.3d 848 (5th Cir. 1999)

**Simulation vehicle: OT1999, May 16, 2000.**

**Historical questions.** Did *Gingles* categorically require a proposed district exceeding fifty percent Hispanic citizen voting-age population? Could current demographic evidence overcome the census figures? Was exclusion of the plaintiffs' late survey evidence an abuse of discretion?

**Stone's disposition.** Reject a categorical numerical bar and remand the §2 claim for proof of a functional ability-to-elect district. Preserve the use of reliable updated demographic evidence and the discretionary exclusion of unfairly late rebuttal evidence on this record. Do not declare a crossover district proved.

**Reasoning.** The actual litigation involved changes since the census and an updated expert estimate of 47.9% Hispanic citizen voting-age population in the discussed district, not a stipulated successful crossover district. Stone must respect the existing demographic findings unless independently erroneous. The proposed rule requires a concrete, geographically workable district in which the minority group, with sufficiently dependable crossover support, has a practical ability to elect its candidates of choice. Mere influence on another group's choice is insufficient. The plaintiff still must establish cohesion, legally relevant bloc voting in the challenged arrangement, and the statutory totality. [Actual appellate opinion](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/us-5th-circuit/1020497.html).

There is no contradiction in showing defeat under the existing at-large electorate and effective opportunity in a different, lawful district. The electorate and electoral denominator differ. Use several probative elections, turnout and citizenship information, uncertainty estimates, and plausible alternatives. Do not treat racial identity as an immutable political preference, require victory guarantees, or add unrelated minority groups without proving the necessary coalition.

**Constitutional justification.** This construction protects an opportunity that the challenged structure can systematically defeat. It avoids making government separate voters into numerical racial majorities when a less segregative, effective alternative is proved. It rests on the statutory protection of electoral opportunity and the *Boerne* preventive framework, not a judicial entitlement to party advantage. Stone's rule is a deliberate extension of the first *Gingles* condition, not settled historical law. The remand should permit proof under that newly clarified rule while retaining ordinary litigation fairness.

### Eisenberg v. Montgomery County Public Schools, 197 F.3d 123 (4th Cir. 1999)

**Simulation vehicle: OT1999, June 20, 2000.**

**Historical question.** Was denial of a preliminary injunction proper where the county refused Jacob Eisenberg's requested magnet-school transfer because of his race and the sending school's diversity profile?

**Stone's disposition.** Vacate the appellate categorical prohibition and remand for the preliminary-injunction inquiry under the ordinary racial-treatment analysis. Require the district to identify the educational objective, the evidence connecting the transfer policy to meaningful integration, the policy's actual operation and burdens, and concrete alternatives that materially test the justification. Do not decide in the abstract that all voluntary integration is permissible.

**Reasoning.** The actual policy considered enrollment and capacity before a racial profile, allowed some exceptions, and used annually reviewed enrollment trends. It nevertheless could make race decisive for an individual transfer. Stone's abandoned discrete-benefit rule would send this automatically to strict scrutiny. His new rule asks whether preventing serious isolation and maintaining meaningful integrated education actually justify the burden.

The county must explain the relationship between its countywide comparisons, the sending school's trajectory, transfer flows, and the educational objective. A decline in white enrollment is evidence of a pattern, not conclusive proof that denying this class of transfers is necessary or substantially effective. Consider a practicable alternative that would preserve the educational objective with a materially smaller burden. The court should not require every single transfer denial to produce a measurable systemwide effect, but should test the policy that generates those denials. [Actual appellate opinion](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/197/197.F3d.123.-98-2797-.98-2503.html); [district-court record](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/19/449/2530370/).

**The express extension.** Stone recognizes meaningful integrated public education and prevention of serious racial isolation as independently important interests. They are not restricted to compensation for proved constitutional wrongdoing. The extension is legally contestable but doctrinally candid. It does not confer automatic validity on the county's numerical rule or create a general racial-balancing power.

### Mixon v. Ohio, 193 F.3d 389 (6th Cir. 1999)

**Simulation vehicle: OT1999, June 27, 2000.**

**Historical questions.** Did the shift from an elected Cleveland school board to mayoral appointment violate the VRA, federal equal protection, or the asserted state-law rules? Could residents outside the city but within the school district demand a vote for the appointing mayor? Which claims and defendants were immune?

**Stone's disposition.** Reject a general constitutional entitlement to an elected school board or to vote for another jurisdiction's mayor. Vacate the categorical exclusion of a §2 challenge to discriminatory elimination of an existing election, and remand for pleading and merits analysis under that narrower theory. Preserve defendant-specific immunity and state-law limitations.

**Reasoning.** There is a difference between demanding creation of an election for a never-elected office and challenging a law that withdraws an existing electoral opportunity discriminatorily. The prior election identifies the change; it does not, by itself, establish unequal opportunity on account of race. Plaintiffs must identify the challenged selection practice, appropriate comparison, racial inequality, and the relevant totality. State educational-reform reasons remain relevant. An intent claim requires its own pleaded and proved basis; the court should permit appropriate amendment under the clarified rule rather than invent allegations. [Actual appellate opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/193/389/477494/).

**Constitutional and remedial fit.** A bounded results rule can prevent discriminatory evasion through the elimination of elections without federalizing every choice between election and appointment. An eventual remedy addresses a demonstrated statutory violation. It does not follow automatically from racial population shares or from a jurisdiction's departure from its former structure. The state itself is not a §1983 “person”; an officer action needs a proper defendant, while a valid VRA abrogation presents a separate route. Federal courts cannot use *Young* to enforce state law against a state defendant.

### Bush v. Palm Beach County Canvassing Board, 531 U.S. 70 (2000)

### Bush v. Gore, 531 U.S. 98 (2000)

**Historical questions.** Did the ordered presidential recount treat materially equivalent ballots under inconsistent standards in violation of equal protection? Did the Florida Supreme Court materially depart from the manner of appointing electors established by the Florida Legislature in violation of Article II, § 1, cl. 2? What remedy follows, and what legal significance should be given to the federal safe-harbor date?

**Stone's disposition.** Dissolve the stay. Vacate only those portions of the recount procedure that permit materially inconsistent treatment of equivalent ballots, reject the Article II challenge, and remand immediately for continued counting under a uniform statewide implementation of Florida's pre-election election law. December 12 is not a judicial stop-counting date. Florida should proceed with extraordinary expedition through the institutions authorized by its pre-election law. If the State ultimately cannot produce a lawful final electoral determination in time for the federal electoral process, the resulting question concerning which electoral return receives federal recognition belongs to the constitutionally assigned electoral-count process in Congress, not to a Supreme Court declaration of the winner.

**Equal-protection rule.** Once a State chooses popular election as its method of appointing presidential electors, it must administer that election so that legally cast votes receive substantially equal treatment. Equal protection does not require counting every ballot regardless of state law; it requires a nonarbitrary method for determining which ballots contain a legally cognizable vote and applying that method consistently to materially equivalent ballots. A constitutional defect in the recount therefore ordinarily calls for correction of the counting procedure, not termination of the count. [Historical opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/531/98/).

Florida law already supplied the substantive rule: a ballot is not invalid when there is a clear indication of voter intent, and the contest statute gives the state judiciary substantial remedial authority to investigate and correct rejected legal votes. The constitutional problem is the absence of sufficiently uniform implementation, not the existence of a voter-intent standard itself. On remand, the Florida institutions authorized by pre-election law should establish one statewide operational standard, apply equivalent criteria to equivalent ballot conditions, use trained counters subject to common instructions, permit observation and recorded objections, preserve disputed ballots and rulings for prompt review, and ensure that materially equivalent categories of potentially discernible presidential votes are not excluded merely because of county boundaries or the happenstance of where a contest began. The State may clarify how its existing legal-vote rule applies to recurring ballot conditions; it may not change voter eligibility or invent a materially new substantive definition of a legal vote after Election Day. [Florida Supreme Court contest decision](https://law.justia.com/cases/florida/supreme-court/2000/sc00-2431.html).

**Article II.** Reject the claim that the Florida Supreme Court violated Article II on this record. Article II assigns the State Legislature responsibility for directing the manner of appointing electors, but a legislature ordinarily acts through a legal system that includes election administrators and courts charged with interpreting and enforcing enacted law. Federal Article II review therefore asks whether the state judiciary has materially replaced the legislature's pre-election rules or reassigned decisionmaking authority the legislature itself placed elsewhere. It does not authorize de novo federal correction of ordinary state-law interpretation.

Florida's pre-election code already recognized election contests, authorized judicial relief to correct rejected legal votes, and used voter intent as the governing substantive criterion. Requiring statewide implementation of those enacted rules does not substitute a new manner of appointing electors. A materially different case would arise if a state court changed who was entitled to vote, adopted a new substantive vote-validity rule inconsistent with enacted law, or transferred final authority to an institution the legislature did not authorize. That limiting rule gives Article II real force without turning this Court into a general supervisor of state election-law interpretation. The separate Article II theory advanced by the Chief Justice therefore does not justify reversal here. [Bush v. Gore, Rehnquist concurrence and dissents](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/531/98).

**Safe harbor, timing, and institutional responsibility.** The December 12 date associated with 3 U.S.C. § 5 provides a federal advantage to a State that has completed a qualifying determination under pre-existing law: its timely determination receives the statute's specified conclusive treatment in the federal electoral-count process. It is not a constitutional forfeiture date and does not itself command state officials or courts to stop counting legal votes. Missing that protection exposes the State's eventual return to the federal dispute-resolution rules; it does not convert the then-current certified total into an irrevocable constitutional result.

Time nevertheless matters. Florida must proceed expeditiously because the electors' meeting and Congress's electoral-count responsibilities are real institutional events. The State does not receive an indefinite recount license. The administrable rule is reasonable expedition under the pre-election legal framework, with federal courts correcting federal constitutional defects but not choosing the President. If Florida cannot complete a lawful determination within a reasonable period compatible with the federal electoral process, the consequence is loss of guaranteed safe-harbor conclusiveness and possible resolution of disputed electoral returns by Congress under the federal electoral-count framework. If the electoral votes ultimately counted do not produce the constitutionally required majority, the Twelfth Amendment supplies its own contingent-election process. The Supreme Court does not create a substitute ballot code, select a slate of electors, or declare a presidential winner.

**What this establishes.** Election law separates rulemaking, administration, adjudication, and federal counting. The legislature establishes the governing election framework before the vote; authorized state election institutions implement it uniformly; state courts enforce and interpret it; federal courts remedy federal constitutional violations without taking over election administration; and Congress bears the constitutionally assigned federal responsibility for disputed electoral returns that remain unresolved when the electoral votes are counted. A deadline designed to protect a State's federal evidentiary position cannot be transformed into a judicial device for disenfranchising legal votes that the State can still lawfully count.

**Outcome limit.** Stone's judgment determines neither candidate's entitlement to Florida's electors nor the winner of the Presidency. Those consequences follow from the lawful count, the State's resulting electoral return, and the constitutionally assigned federal electoral process.

### Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett, 531 U.S. 356 (2001)

**Historical questions.** Could state employees recover money damages under ADA Title I, given sovereign immunity and Congress's asserted enforcement authority? What should the Court do with the portion of the grant concerning Title II employment claims when the antecedent statutory-coverage issue was not adequately briefed?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm the appellate rejection of the Title I immunity barrier and allow those damages claims to proceed because the Title I abrogation is valid § 5 enforcement. Dismiss the inadequately presented Title II portion as improvidently granted.

**Reasoning.** Stone reaches the § 5 question directly; Article I cannot do the remedial work after *Seminole Tribe*. Congress compiled a substantial record of exclusion, stereotype, and unequal treatment of people with disabilities by public institutions, including employment, and Title I responds through bounded rules rather than an unlimited preference. The claimant must be qualified; employers may maintain job-related standards; accommodation must be reasonable; and undue hardship remains a defense. Those limitations make the remedy sufficiently fitted to the recurring constitutional danger of irrational and stereotype-based exclusion by state employers.

The rule is broader than the constitutional minimum, as preventive legislation often is, but it is not detached from that minimum. Stone does not make disability a suspect classification or hold that every failure to accommodate is independently unconstitutional. The distinction from *Kimel* is evidentiary and remedial: Congress identified a more substantial pattern of constitutionally suspect exclusion and used qualification and hardship limits that focus the preventive rule on the mechanism producing that exclusion. ### Alexander v. Sandoval, 532 U.S. 275 (2001)

**Reasoning.** Stone adopts the historical dissent's reading of the established statutory remedial framework. ### Nguyen v. INS, 533 U.S. 53 (2001)

**Historical questions.** Were the different statutory citizenship requirements for unmarried citizen fathers and mothers constitutional? What relief followed for Nguyen's nationality claim in removal proceedings?

**Stone's disposition.** Hold § 1409(a)(4)'s paternal-only condition unconstitutional, sever that condition, recognize Nguyen's citizenship at birth under the remaining statutory requirements on this record, and vacate the removal order premised on alienage.

**Reasoning.** Congress may require reliable proof of biological parentage and may demand a genuine connection between the citizen parent and child, but it must do so through means adequately fitted to those objectives rather than an assumption that an unmarried mother necessarily forms the relevant relationship while an unmarried father does not. Neutral proof of paternity directly addresses the biological question. Evidence of an actual parent-child relationship can address connection. A sex-specific pre-eighteen formality is an imprecise proxy for both and fails heightened scrutiny.

Stone also answers the remedial question left open in the old build. The INA's general severability clause creates a strong presumption that an invalid condition can be excised. Removing § 1409(a)(4) does not amount to equitable naturalization; the remaining statute itself confers citizenship at birth when its valid predicates are met. Under Nguyen's applicable statutory version and the established parentage and citizen-parent predicates in this record, the Court should recognize the citizenship Congress's remaining text supplies rather than remand the same legal question. [Opinion and dissent](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/533/53/).

### United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 (2001)

**Historical question.** Did the customs classification ruling receive controlling *Chevron* deference, and what other persuasive weight could it have?

**Stone's disposition.** Vacate and remand under the distinction between delegated force-of-law authority and persuasive agency reasoning.

**Reasoning.** Congress's actual delegation, the decision's legal form, consistency, expertise, and reasoning matter. An agency cannot obtain controlling authority just by asserting specialized knowledge. This reinforces the VRA distinction between Congress's statutory authorization and DOJ's litigating or administrative preferences. Apply the administrative-law framework available in 2001; do not insert later changes to deference doctrine into Stone's opinion. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/533/218/).

### FEC v. Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee, 533 U.S. 431 (2001), Colorado II

**Historical question.** Could Congress limit a political party's expenditures coordinated with its candidate?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the appellate invalidation and uphold the coordinated-expenditure limits.

**Reasoning.** Actual coordination can make spending a substitute for a contribution and permit circumvention of candidate limits. The evidence supports regulation of that relationship. Maintain the independent-spending protection in *Colorado I* and require an administrable definition of coordination rather than infer it solely from party identity. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/533/431/).

### Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez, 531 U.S. 533 (2001)

**Historical question.** Could a federal legal-services funding restriction bar lawyers from challenging existing welfare law in the representation at issue?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm invalidation of the restriction at issue.

**Reasoning.** Congress funded private representation within an adversarial legal system, not transmission of a government-approved message. The restriction distorted the lawyer's role and the court's access to legal arguments. This protects meaningful legal access while preserving Congress's authority to define a program's subject matter within constitutional limits. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/531/533/).

### Booth v. Churner, 532 U.S. 731 (2001)

**Historical question.** Must a prisoner exhaust an available administrative process when seeking money that the process cannot award?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm the exhaustion requirement where the process can provide some responsive relief.

**Reasoning.** The enacted exhaustion requirement gives the institution an opportunity to address the underlying problem. It does not depend solely on whether the process can supply the precise judicial remedy demanded. An actually unavailable process is different. This is a statutory prerequisite, not a judicial judgment that damages are unimportant. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/532/731/).

### Saucier v. Katz, 533 U.S. 194 (2001)

**Historical questions.** How should courts analyze constitutional violation and clearly established law in qualified-immunity cases, and was the officer entitled to immunity here?

**Stone's disposition.** Accept qualified immunity on the historical excessive-force record, but decline to require constitutional merits adjudication first in every case.

**Reasoning.** Courts should usually clarify legal duties where doing so is necessary and suitably presented, but a mandatory sequence can force unnecessary constitutional decisions on incomplete records. Officials retain protection where the law did not give adequate notice; that protection does not make clearly wrongful conduct immune merely because no factually identical reported case exists. *Hope* completes that clarification. [Opinion and separate writings](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/533/194/).

### Daggett v. Commission on Governmental Ethics & Election Practices, 205 F.3d 445 (1st Cir. 2000)

**Simulation vehicle: OT2000, February 12, 2001.**

**Historical questions.** Were Maine's contribution ceilings, responsive matching funds, and voluntary public-financing system constitutional, including the allegation that the combined scheme coerced participation?

**Stone's disposition.** Sustain the challenged financing structure and limits at the scope supported by the historical record, preserving any specifically unresolved applications.

**Reasoning.** The matching amount was capped; the nonparticipant remained free to speak and spend. Evaluate practical voluntariness and the entire scheme, rather than count the existence of an opponent's responsive subsidy as an automatic constitutional injury. Financing a functioning alternative to dependence on private contributions supplies the relevant purpose. This does not make every escalating subsidy, unusually restrictive private limit, or major-party preference valid. The cap and the whole program distinguish the actual Maine record from the earlier Minnesota vehicle. [Actual appellate opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/205/445/559226/).

### Mariani v. United States, 212 F.3d 761 (3d Cir. 2000)

**Simulation vehicle: OT2000, March 12, 2001.**

**Historical questions.** Were FECA's direct corporate-contribution and contribution-in-another-person's-name prohibitions constitutional despite the growth of soft money and issue advocacy? Were the certified challenges properly entertained?

**Stone's disposition.** Sustain the challenged prohibitions and permit consideration of the nonfrivolous certified questions.

**Reasoning.** Remaining loopholes do not make a directly connected anticorruption or anti-evasion rule irrational or unconstitutional. A conduit prohibition protects source identification and contribution limits. The actual case did not supply a free-standing Supreme Court holding on all coordinated corporate spending; references to coordination explain contribution doctrine and should not be presented as a separate adjudicated claim unless the simulation supplies that issue lawfully. [Actual en banc opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/212/761/632624/).

### Brewer v. West Irondequoit Central School District, 212 F.3d 738 (2d Cir. 2000)

**Simulation vehicle: OT2000, March 26, 2001.**

**Historical question.** Was the mandatory preliminary injunction admitting a white student, Jessica Haak, through the Rochester-area interdistrict transfer program justified under the governing constitutional and preliminary-relief standards?

**Stone's disposition.** Preserve a limited remand for a developed merits record under *Eisenberg*'s integrated-education branch. Vacate the mandatory preliminary injunction to the extent it depended on the categorical proposition that reducing de facto racial isolation could never justify race-conscious action. Permit the lower court to reconsider interim relief promptly under the correct standard and actual enrollment circumstances.

**Reasoning.** This was not a final judgment establishing the program's constitutionality. The program's urban-suburban design and long operation supplied a factual setting worth examining. Its rigid racial eligibility practices and withdrawal of the child's admissions after identifying her race also created serious fit concerns. Neither proposition can be omitted. The government must explain why the actual eligibility rule substantially advances integration, the educational opportunities available, and the feasibility of less burdensome design. [Actual appellate opinion and separate writings](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/212/738/632474/).

An older federal funding history is not a continuing congressional mandate after the relevant program has ended. Current legal authority must be identified. The school-specific integration interest supplies the constitutional theory where supported; it does not itself override applicable state law. Any interim order must concern the child's actual present school year, not copy a deadline from the real 2000 litigation into the simulation's later date.

### Kansas v. United States, 214 F.3d 1196 (10th Cir. 2000)

**Simulation vehicle: OT2000, April 16, 2001.**

**Historical question.** Did the federal child-support enforcement requirements and their relationship to federal welfare funding exceed the Spending Clause, including through coercion?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm rejection of the state's facial challenge on the historical record.

**Reasoning.** Identify the actual statutory conditions, notice, programmatic relationship, and financial choice. A substantial incentive is not automatically coercion. Do not declare coercion conceptually impossible or establish a universal percentage threshold. This is a funding condition, distinct from the direct executive command invalidated in *Printz*. [Actual appellate opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/214/1196/533464/).

### Chen v. City of Houston, 206 F.3d 502 (5th Cir. 2000)

**Simulation vehicle: OT2000, September 10, 2001.**

**Historical questions.** Was Houston's use of total population rather than eligible-voter population constitutional? Did the plaintiffs' separate racial-gerrymandering theory survive summary judgment?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm the judgment for Houston on the total-population issue. Reject the separate design-only racial-gerrymandering theory under the simulated *Shaw* unless the preserved pleadings identify an independent dilution, exclusion, unequal-participation, or other cognizable injury requiring further adjudication.

**Reasoning.** Representatives serve residents, including children and people who cannot presently vote. Substantial eligible-voter disparities do not alone invalidate a properly equalized total-population plan. Separately, racial awareness, preclearance history, and evidence that officials considered demographic composition can support an independently valid statutory or constitutional claim where its elements make those facts material. They do not revive the freestanding Shaw claim that the simulation rejected in 1993.

### Harris v. Garner, 216 F.3d 970 (11th Cir. 2000)

**Simulation vehicle: OT2000, September 24, 2001.**

**Historical questions.** How did the PLRA's physical-injury restriction apply to claims brought while incarcerated, including plaintiffs later released? What claims and forms of relief did the provision reach, and was it constitutional?

**Stone's disposition.** Apply the filing-time rule and the limitation on compensation for mental or emotional injury without the required physical showing. Reject an interpretation extinguishing every underlying constitutional claim or every other independently available remedy; remand remedy-specific issues as necessary.

**Reasoning.** The text limits recovery for the specified injury; it is not a universal immunity provision for prison misconduct. Prospective relief, nominal relief, independently proved economic loss, and other remedies require their own legal basis and elements. Release after filing does not rewrite the plaintiff's status when the action was brought. Preserve appropriate treatment of dismissal without prejudice and later lawful litigation rather than imply an automatic perpetual bar. [Actual en banc opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/216/970/570374/).

## OT2001: decisions from late 2001 through 2002

### Adarand Constructors v. Mineta, 534 U.S. 103 (2001)

**Historical questions.** Could the Court review a challenge to direct federal procurement when the judgment below concerned materially different state and local federal-aid procurement regulations, and the necessary standing and merits issues had not been presented in that posture?

**Stone's disposition.** Dismiss the writ as improvidently granted if the historical procedural mismatch remains.

**Reasoning.** The Court should not use a familiar party name to decide a different program in the first instance. The 1995 substantive position does not cure standing, preservation, or record defects. This case belongs within OT2001 even though it was missing from the supplied sequence. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/534/103/).

**Dependency.** The actual 1995 disposition and subsequent proceedings may eliminate the later mismatch; do not assume a remand if the Stevens fallback instead produces affirmance. If a properly presented merits dispute arises, identify the controlling standard and Stone's position under the branch actually reached, then examine the challenged program's objective, evidence, operation, and burdens. Do not force a dismissal solely to reproduce history.

### Toyota Motor Manufacturing, Kentucky, Inc. v. Williams, 534 U.S. 184 (2002)

**Historical question.** What constitutes a substantial limitation in performing manual tasks, and did the Sixth Circuit correctly grant partial summary judgment on disability?

**Stone's disposition.** Vacate the partial summary judgment and remand for a complete functional inquiry. Reject both a test confined to the employee's narrow assembly-line tasks and a newly heightened requirement that the impairment prevent or severely restrict activities central to most people's daily lives.

**Reasoning.** The enacted term “substantially” requires a meaningful limitation, not total incapacity. Assess the person's actual range of major activities, duration, and degree of limitation. The ability to perform some household tasks does not automatically defeat coverage. Stone's approach extends his original-statute reading in *Sutton*; it does not retroactively invoke the 2008 amendments. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/534/184/).

### EEOC v. Waffle House, 534 U.S. 279 (2002)

**Historical question.** Did an employee's arbitration agreement prevent the EEOC from seeking victim-specific relief in its public enforcement action?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the restriction on the EEOC's statutory enforcement authority.

**Reasoning.** The agency was not a party to the employee's agreement and acts under its own congressional mandate. Courts must prevent duplicate recovery and account for relevant settlements or litigation, but private contracting cannot automatically extinguish public enforcement. This adds an institutional enforcement route alongside the private actions protected elsewhere in the build. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/534/279/).

### Swierkiewicz v. Sorema, 534 U.S. 506 (2002)

**Historical question.** Must an employment-discrimination complaint plead the evidentiary elements of the *McDonnell Douglas* prima facie case?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse dismissal under that heightened pleading demand.

**Reasoning.** An evidentiary method is not a universal pleading code. Apply the Federal Rules then in force and require fair notice of the discrimination claim. Do not require a plaintiff to possess evidence uniquely held by the employer before relevant discovery. This preserves the practical operation of *Hicks*, *Reeves*, and *Crawford-El*. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/534/506/).

### Porter v. Nussle, 534 U.S. 516 (2002)

**Historical question.** Does PLRA exhaustion apply to a prisoner's claim about an individual excessive-force incident as well as general prison conditions?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the categorical incident-based exception.

**Reasoning.** The statute's coverage does not depend on whether the complaint describes a single episode or a continuing condition. Apply *Booth*'s available-process requirement and retain meaningful inquiry into actual availability. This is a procedural statutory duty, not a finding that the force was lawful. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/534/516/).

### Owasso Independent School District v. Falvo, 534 U.S. 426 (2002)

**Historical question.** Did students' peer grading violate FERPA's rules concerning education records maintained by an educational institution?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the ruling that ordinary peer grading itself violates the provision.

**Reasoning.** Read “maintained” records in its statutory setting. A statute protecting educational privacy need not sweep in every classroom exchange. Reserve the general private-enforcement question for a case presenting it, as *Gonzaga* subsequently does. This demonstrates that an enforceable right can remain bounded and practically administrable. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/534/426/).

### US Airways, Inc. v. Barnett, 535 U.S. 391 (2002)

**Historical question.** When does an accommodation conflicting with an employer's seniority system remain reasonable under the ADA?

**Stone's disposition.** Vacate and remand under a rebuttable ordinary rule: a genuine seniority system usually makes the requested departure unreasonable, but the employee may establish special circumstances.

**Reasoning.** Accommodations can require preferential treatment; that fact alone does not defeat them. Stable seniority expectations nevertheless impose a concrete burden on other workers. Frequent exceptions or the system's actual operation may change the analysis. This is a useful model of administrability: a practical presumption with an evidentiary opportunity for a justified exception. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/535/391/).

### Lapides v. Board of Regents, 535 U.S. 613 (2002)

**Historical question.** Did a state's voluntary removal waive its Eleventh Amendment objection to state-law claims for which it had already consented to suit in its own courts?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the contrary immunity ruling at that limited scope.

**Reasoning.** A state may not voluntarily invoke the federal forum and then use the forum choice to defeat the already permitted claims. Do not turn this into a decision that removal waives every possible immunity for every unconsented federal claim. Stone's broader congressional-abrogation rule remains a separate route. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/535/613/).

### Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission, 535 U.S. 635 (2002)

**Historical questions.** Did federal jurisdiction exist over the asserted federal-law challenge to the commission's order, and could prospective relief against commissioners proceed under *Ex parte Young*?

**Stone's disposition.** Vacate the dismissal and permit the properly framed federal claim to proceed.

**Reasoning.** The threshold officer-suit inquiry asks whether the complaint alleges an ongoing federal violation and seeks prospective relief. It does not require the claimant first to win the merits. The commission's own immunity and the lawfulness of its order remain distinct questions. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/535/635/).

### Federal Maritime Commission v. South Carolina State Ports Authority, 535 U.S. 743 (2002)

**Historical question.** Did state sovereign immunity bar the FMC from adjudicating a private party's Shipping Act complaint against the state port authority?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the appellate extension of immunity to this federal administrative proceeding and allow the statutory process to continue.

**Reasoning.** Stone follows the historical dissent's rejection of that extension into executive regulatory adjudication. The agency's authority and any resulting judicial enforcement must be identified under the statute. Do not assume a private complainant controls federal enforcement merely because it initiated the complaint. This position fits Stone's rejection of immunity as a general power to disregard valid federal obligations. [Opinion and dissents](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/535/743/).

### Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Echazabal, 536 U.S. 73 (2002)

**Historical question.** Could the EEOC permissibly authorize a defense based on a significant threat to the employee's own health?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse rejection of the regulation and remand its application.

**Reasoning.** The safety defense can fit the statutory delegation, but requires an individualized, objectively supported assessment, including accommodation. It is not a permission for paternalistic assumptions about disability or a general excuse to exclude people who may face some additional risk. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/73/).

### National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Morgan, 536 U.S. 101 (2002)

**Historical questions.** How do Title VII filing deadlines apply to discrete discriminatory acts and a hostile environment extending across time?

**Stone's disposition.** Apply timely filing to each discrete act, while allowing consideration of the complete actionable hostile environment where a component act falls within the filing period; remand accordingly.

**Reasoning.** The unit of the legal wrong matters. A continuing environment is not merely a collection of independently complete employment decisions. Preserve equitable doctrines where properly established, without converting every old discriminatory act into a timely claim. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/101/).

### Barnes v. Gorman, 536 U.S. 181 (2002)

**Historical question.** Were punitive damages available in the private action under ADA Title II and §504 of the Rehabilitation Act?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the punitive-damages award under the remedial framework governing those statutes.

**Reasoning.** A valid substantive access right and compensatory remedy do not establish an additional punitive remedy. The statutory cross-references and notice to covered recipients matter. Stone can preserve ordinary compensation for proved injury without using this case to authorize every remedy under every civil-rights statute. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/181/).

### Gonzaga University v. Doe, 536 U.S. 273 (2002)

**Historical question.** Did FERPA's education-record confidentiality provisions create a right enforceable through §1983 in the damages litigation before the Court?

**Stone's disposition.** Reject the categorical no-right holding and preserve §1983 enforceability of the specifically protected confidentiality interest, following the historical dissent. Remand any unresolved state-action, statutory policy-or-practice, causation, and remedial issues rather than assume that every disclosure supports the verdict.

**Reasoning.** Congress's identification of protected students, confidential records, and required conditions can create an individual right even when the enforcement scheme also directs the funding agency. Administrative oversight does not automatically displace §1983. But the statute's actual limits remain: a general program objective is not a right to perfect institutional compliance, and a private university does not become a §1983 state actor merely by accepting federal money. The issue granted concerned the existence of an enforceable right, not resolution of every other element. [Opinion and Stevens dissent](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/273/).

**Relationship to Sandoval.** *Sandoval* concerns an existing implied statutory action and implementing regulations. *Gonzaga* concerns the separate, legislatively supplied §1983 remedy. Stone must explain both routes; he cannot claim that the word “rights” has the same procedural function in every statute. *Blessing* and *Owasso* preserve enforceable boundaries.

### Utah v. Evans, 536 U.S. 452 (2002)

**Historical questions.** Did Utah have standing to challenge the apportionment result? Was hot-deck imputation prohibited sampling under the Census Act, or inconsistent with an actual enumeration?

**Stone's disposition.** Affirm the judgment for the Census Bureau, recognizing standing but rejecting the statutory and constitutional objections.

**Reasoning.** The imputation technique filled missing information about particular census units rather than replacing the enumeration with the prohibited sampling method. The Constitution does not require officials to treat every unresolved household as containing zero people. A reasoned accuracy method can be lawful within the statute. This fits both the discretion recognized in *Wisconsin* and the statutory restriction enforced in *Commerce v. House*. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/452/).

### Christopher v. Harbury, 536 U.S. 403 (2002)

**Historical question.** Did the complaint adequately plead a backward-looking denial-of-access claim, including the lost underlying legal claim and a remedy unavailable through another surviving action?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse approval of the access claim as pleaded, with appropriate consideration of any procedurally available amendment.

**Reasoning.** Access-to-courts liability requires identification of what legal opportunity was actually lost and what this claim can remedy. Official deception is not automatically proof of a distinct access injury with unlimited damages. This is consistent with *Lewis* and does not resolve the underlying allegations of official wrongdoing. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/403/).

### Watchtower Bible & Tract Society v. Village of Stratton, 536 U.S. 150 (2002)

**Historical question.** Could the village require a permit before the broad range of door-to-door advocacy covered by its ordinance?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse and invalidate the permit requirement at the challenged scope.

**Reasoning.** The ordinance burdens spontaneous, anonymous, religious, and political advocacy more broadly than the stated interests justify. Preserve targeted fraud, trespass, and homeowner-choice protections. Broadening access does not mean disabling ordinary neutral safeguards. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/150/).

### Republican Party of Minnesota v. White, 536 U.S. 765 (2002)

**Historical question.** Did Minnesota's prohibition on judicial candidates announcing views on disputed legal or political issues violate the First Amendment?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse and invalidate the broad announce clause; preserve appropriately drawn rules concerning commitments, recusal, and impartial adjudication.

**Reasoning.** An elected judiciary cannot obtain complete insulation from public discussion through a sweeping speech prohibition. Voters' information and judicial impartiality are both relevant; the rule must address the actual threat without suppressing broad issue discussion. This does not constitutionalize judicial elections or require campaign promises about particular cases. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/765/).

### Zelman v. Simmons-Harris, 536 U.S. 639 (2002)

**Historical question.** Did the Cleveland school-choice aid program violate the Establishment Clause because participating families could select religious schools?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the appellate invalidation on the historical program's neutral eligibility and private-choice structure.

**Reasoning.** Distinguish aid reaching religious instruction through the independent choices of beneficiaries from governmental delegation to a religious group in *Kiryas Joel*. This decision establishes constitutional permission, not a finding that vouchers improve education or remedy unequal school opportunity. Actual program efficacy remains an empirical and legislative question. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/639/).

### Hope v. Pelzer, 536 U.S. 730 (2002)

**Historical question.** Were prison officials entitled to qualified immunity for the hitching-post treatment because no prior case matched the facts closely enough?

**Stone's disposition.** Reverse the immunity judgment.

**Reasoning.** Clearly established law can give fair warning through general principles, directly relevant authority, and the obvious character of the wrong. A factually identical precedent is unnecessary. This preserves meaningful accountability without making officials retrospectively liable for every reasonable mistake about unsettled law. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/730/).

“Minimum useful record” is a litigation guide, not a newly mandatory administrative dossier for every individual decision. Existing evidence can suffice. Small jurisdictions may use relevant shared studies or existing election data where the connection is explained. An appellate opinion should not invent a costly reporting system as the price of exercising an otherwise established statutory right.

### A numerical illustration of the crossover issue

These numbers are illustrative, not findings about Alamo Heights. Suppose minority voters cast 48% of the valid votes in a proposed district, 88% support their candidate of choice, and 16% of other voters support that candidate. The candidate's estimated share is `(0.48 × 0.88) + (0.52 × 0.16) = 50.56%`. In a larger electorate where the minority casts only 20% of the votes, the same preference rates yield `30.4%`.

That explains how bloc-voting defeat in an at-large system can coexist with crossover opportunity in an alternative district. It also shows the evidentiary difficulty: reducing crossover support in the proposed district from 16% to 14% lowers the estimate to 49.52%. A fragile point estimate is not a guarantee of an effective district. Turnout assumptions, electoral variation, and uncertainty must be examined.

The example uses shares of **votes cast**, not population shares. Census CVAP cannot simply be inserted as though registration and turnout were identical across groups. The law should require evidence of practical ability, without pretending that exact electoral certainty is possible.

## Where the plan is strongest, and where it remains vulnerable

**Strongest: the separation of authority, proof, and remedy.** The framework uses established judicial tasks. Trial courts already evaluate intent, voting patterns, comparators, agency, statutory rights, reasonable accommodation, and remedial scope. Congress already develops legislative records and makes predictive judgments. Stone is changing some governing standards, not inventing an entirely new factfinding institution.

**Most substantial departure: the preferred *Adarand* racial-treatment analysis.** Stone's primary position rejects both the federal-status-based rule of *Metro Broadcasting* and the categorical strict-scrutiny formulation he would otherwise inherit from *Croson*. It requires an important lawful objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship, and justification of actual coverage and burdens, with supported prospective market access among the permissible objectives. Its administrability depends on courts explaining those determinations and the concrete unanswered questions requiring remand, not merely listing factors. The authorized Stevens fallback would instead affirm this challenge on the preserved record and inherited federal precedents if the replacement standard is not adopted. That alternative does not silently place the preferred rule into law.

**Most vulnerable extension: voluntary public-school integration.** A doctrine limited to remediation of an adjudicated wrong cannot honestly do all the work sought in *Eisenberg* and *Brewer*. Stone therefore treats supported integration as a distinct prospective objective rather than relabeling it remediation. The difficult question remains whether the particular educational benefit and evidence justify the individual's racial treatment. Demographic arithmetic alone will not answer it.

**Most important narrowing after *Shaw*: districting requires an independently cognizable injury.** This makes the doctrine more administrable and reduces the collision between the VRA and a freestanding racial-design claim, but it also creates an underreach risk if courts ignore evidence of intentional dilution or racial exclusion simply because the boundary is territorial. The answer is to enforce those independent constitutional and statutory claims fully, not to recreate the rejected design-only injury through motive evidence alone.

**Most demanding voting application: crossover districts.** A fifty-percent line is easy to apply but can exclude real electoral opportunity. A functional alternative better serves the project and may reduce pressure for racial packing, but requires more analysis and can generate disagreement about elections, turnout, and coalition stability. Stone should retain a meaningful ability-to-elect requirement, ordinary error review, and a remedy that survives plausible variations in the evidence. He should not promise easy administration in every jurisdiction.

**Most important institutional limit: private enforcement is not self-executing.** *Morse*, *Powell*, *Sandoval*, *Gonzaga*, fees, and immunity help turn duties into usable remedies. But successful litigation still requires claimants, counsel, evidence, lawful defendants, and implementation capacity. The Court cannot provide congressional appropriations or guarantee effective executive enforcement merely by strengthening legal language.

**The record includes deliberate government losses.** The proposed positions reject unsupported maximization, racial franchise exclusion, an insufficient sex-remedial record in the actual Dallas case, inadequately justified financing restrictions, legislative commands outside the asserted power, and remedies or claims beyond their statutory footing. That is necessary for an evidence-based rule that can stand independently of Stone's preferred social outcomes.

### Short glossary for the opinions

- **VRA §2:** the statutory prohibition on racially unequal voting opportunity, including the 1982 results framework.
- **VRA §5:** the historical preclearance requirement for covered jurisdictions' voting changes. Its constitutional authority is a separate question.
- **Fourteenth Amendment §5:** Congress's power to enforce that Amendment. It is not VRA §5.
- **Fifteenth Amendment §2:** Congress's power to enforce the prohibition on racial denial or abridgment of voting rights. It is not VRA §2.
- **Predominance:** a factual conclusion that race controlled district design relative to competing considerations. After the simulated *Shaw*, predominance is evidence; it is not a freestanding constitutional injury or automatic trigger.
- **Predicate:** the objective factual and legal basis supporting a remedial, preventive, statutory, or separately supported prospective objective.
- **Fit:** the relationship between the government's supported objective and the measure's actual coverage, operation, burdens, alternatives, and duration.
- **Preclearance:** approval to implement a covered voting change under the statute. It does not immunize the change from every other constitutional or statutory challenge.
- **Abrogation:** Congress's valid withdrawal of an otherwise available immunity through a constitutional source that permits it and unmistakable statutory language. It is different from state consent or waiver.
- **Disposition:** what Stone would do to the judgment under review. A remand identifies issues to be resolved; it is not a predicted victory.

## Opinion sequence Stone can reuse

First identify the historical question and procedural posture. Then state the governing right and congressional or institutional authority. Specify who bears each burden. Explain the relevant evidence and what inference it supports. Apply the appropriate standard to the particular treatment and remedy. State what remains unresolved and what the lower court must do next.

Across these cases, government may take supported action to remedy exclusion, prevent recurrence, or carry out other lawful institutional responsibilities, but the court must keep authority, evidence, actual burden, statutory duty, constitutional injury, and remedy in their proper places. Permission to act and limits on that action are both enforced through the sources that supply them.
