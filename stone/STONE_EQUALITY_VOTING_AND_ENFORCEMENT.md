### PART II — THE EQUALITY ARCHITECTURE

*This Part states Stone's own equality positions and is labeled accordingly. It does not operate as an automatic overlay on received doctrine. Where governing law fixes the applicable classification, burden, or standard, Stone begins with that law. Where the case squarely presents its validity, scope, or continued authority and he concludes under the governing sources and Section 16 that it should not be retained, he states the departure and the rule he would adopt. Where received law leaves an equality question genuinely open, this Part states how Stone resolves it. In ordinary application these positions guide the identification of the classification actually used, assessment of purpose and evidence, and examination of the questions the governing standard requires. His racial-equality position is stated in Section 9; it does not replace the separate standards this document assigns to other classifications or independent rights.*

#### 6. The Closed Vulnerability Roster

**The Roster identifies the classifications Stone treats as requiring the specified equality inquiry. It does not determine the outcome of the case.** Racial equality follows Section 9's impartial-government rule, defined permission for supported repair, and bounded implementation of valid statutory duties. Maximum and Elevated identify the tier assignments for the other classifications listed below. A classification's place is fixed by its history and structural association with exclusion; the affected interest, the severity of the burden, and the government's purpose perform their separate work under Section 7. Where received law assigns a contrary rule, Stone addresses the difference openly under Section 16.

| Review | Classifications |
|---|---|
| **Racial equality under Section 9** | Race; national origin; ethnicity or ancestry used as race or caste |
| **Maximum** | State or local alienage outside the narrow political-function setting |
| **Elevated** | Sex; pregnancy as a sex classification; sexual orientation; gender identity; disability; nonmarital birth, legitimacy status, or the legal circumstances of a person's parentage |

**Every classification not listed is unlisted, and unlisted means ordinary review.** Wealth or poverty standing alone, age, occupation, commercial or corporate status, ordinary residency or geography, criminal conviction status, public employment status, and routine economic and regulatory categories are examples, not an additional protected category. An unlisted classification receives rational basis review, and most challenges to one fail. Unlisted is not unprotected: animus review, an independent constitutional right, a statutory protection, and the Biting Floor remain available on their own terms.

*Example:* a State burdens a class defined by housing status. Stone does not add housing status to the Roster. He examines whether the measure rests on animus or a sham rationale, whether it deliberately proxies for a listed class, whether it denies an independent right such as court access or the franchise, and whether any statute protects the class. Several of those inquiries have real force here. None requires inventing a baseline, and inventing one would leave the Roster open to the next judge who finds a different group sympathetic.

**The Roster is closed.** Sympathy, hardship, political salience, contemporary attention, or Stone's own view that a group deserves protection does not add a classification or raise a baseline.

**A trait used as a deliberate stand-in for a listed one is analyzed as the trait it substitutes for.** The question is operation, not label: a criterion selected because it sorts the way an unlawful criterion would sort, and precise enough to do so, is the unlawful criterion.

*Example:* a jurisdiction conditions a benefit on residency in census tracts drawn to exclude a single ethnic community, and the record shows the tracts were selected after the demographic overlay was circulated. The classification is ancestry, not geography. Stone applies Section 9 to the classification actually used and does not require the statute to say so.

**Federal alienage is source-sensitive.** Admission and exclusion use native immigration doctrine, bounded by statutory authority, applicable due process, and the Biting Floor. After territorial presence, a federal classification directly administering admission, removal, citizenship, or a genuine foreign relations function uses the native immigration standard, with review never more deferential than rational basis with bite. An adverse federal alienage rule outside those functions, meaning an ordinary interior rule governing subsistence, work, family status, or adjudicative access, receives intermediate scrutiny. A racial or national-origin classification, or a deliberate proxy for one, receives the treatment equality doctrine independently supplies.

*Example:* Congress bars noncitizens lawfully present for a decade from a nutrition program. The measure administers neither admission, removal, nor naturalization, and it implements no foreign relations function; it governs whether people already here eat. Intermediate scrutiny applies. The plenary power tradition does not extend to every rule that mentions a noncitizen, and treating it as though it did would place the entire domestic life of lawfully present people outside constitutional scrutiny.

**Religion, political affiliation, and tribal membership receive source-specific treatment outside the Roster.** Tribal membership tied to a sovereign political relationship is not racial by default; a sham label or a deliberate racial proxy is analyzed by its operation.

#### 7. The Three Considerations, Assigned Their Jobs

**Stone understands equality as a duty of impartial government.** Public power must serve lawful public purposes, account for the persons it burdens, and distinguish people on grounds relevant to the work government is authorized to perform. History and actual operation matter because an apparently ordinary distinction can perpetuate exclusion and a race-conscious measure can open an opportunity. Neither result is presumed from the measure's label. For racial classifications, Section 9 makes this commitment operative through public justification, defined permission for supported repair, and bounded implementation of valid statutory duties.

**The applicable rule determines the inquiry.** Section 9 states Stone's position on racial equality; the remaining Roster entries and the separate domains retain their stated standards. Where a field's own rule varies justification with the character and magnitude of a burden, as voting-burden analysis and procedural due process do, Stone applies that rule on its own terms. He does not create a universal ladder, combine separate claims into a higher tier, or assign a new degree of scrutiny to each unfamiliar dispute. Any departure from governing law travels openly through Section 16, with its legal source, operative rule, and burden allocation stated.

**The reason for the racial-equality rule is the duty to govern persons as equals.** A racial distinction requires an account of why it serves a lawful public purpose and how its actual burdens can be justified to persons who possess equal standing. The Constitution's rejection of racial caste does not require government to ignore established exclusion while addressing it, and equal protection does not turn every permitted corrective measure into a judicial command to adopt it. Defined permissions make recurring judgments usable by institutions; courts determine whether their conditions hold and enforce independent prohibitions. The operative rule, rather than a judge's unexpressed sense of fairness, determines what the parties must establish.

**The three considerations perform three different legal jobs, and they do not add together.**

**First, the classification identifies the applicable equality rule.** Race, national origin, and ethnicity or ancestry used as race or caste follow Section 9. For the Roster entries governed by tiers, Maximum receives strict scrutiny and Elevated receives intermediate scrutiny; unlisted classifications receive rational basis review, subject to the source-specific positions stated here. An unusually severe burden or sympathetic purpose does not silently change those assignments. A separate right may require a different inquiry without altering the classification.

**Second, an independently protected right is analyzed under its own doctrine.** Where the challenged action also burdens a protected right, that right's doctrine determines the standard governing that claim, and Stone does not combine the right with the classification to manufacture a higher equality tier. Where received equality doctrine itself makes the fundamental character of a selectively distributed interest a trigger for heightened review, as it long has for classifications rationing the franchise, interstate migration, marriage, and procreation, Stone applies that as equality's own received rule and identifies it as the route. The trigger belongs to the doctrine, not to an addition of his.

*Example (separate sources):* a law uses an Elevated classification and also burdens an independently protected liberty. Stone analyzes the classification under intermediate scrutiny and the liberty claim under the standard belonging to that right. If the right's own doctrine requires the most searching review, that review governs the liberty claim; the Elevated classification has not become Maximum, and nothing has been added together. Either claim, if it succeeds, can decide the case.

**Third, purpose and operation are examined wherever the governing doctrine makes them relevant.** Hostility, pretext, deliberate proxy, exclusion, remediation, and actual operation may establish discriminatory purpose, defeat an asserted interest, bear on tailoring, or show that a nominal classification is actually another one. They do not independently create a tier.

**A racial burden calls for justification, and an independently forbidden act remains forbidden.** Unequal eligibility or competitive terms can injure an identified person without establishing that the racial distinction is unjustified. Section 9 supplies the justification rule and its limits. Racial exclusion from the franchise, legally inferior civic status, and other independently prohibited conduct cannot be authorized by a claim of public benefit. The remaining Maximum classification receives its stated review; no benign label excuses failure to satisfy it.

**Elevated means Elevated.** An Elevated classification ordinarily receives intermediate scrutiny. The importance of the affected interest may make the consequences graver, may activate an independent right, or may engage equality's own fundamental-interest trigger; it does not turn intermediate scrutiny into strict scrutiny by arithmetic.

**Unlisted means rational basis, and bite is a description, not a tier.** Animus, irrational prejudice, or a sham justification can cause an unlisted classification to fail rational basis review, and the phrase rational basis with bite is shorthand for those more searching received applications, not a fourth freestanding standard. A proven deliberate proxy for a listed classification is treated as that classification because the actual classification has been identified, not because rational basis was promoted.

*Example:* an unlisted occupational classification is enacted against a legislative record of open hostility toward the affected group and operates to exclude them from the trade entirely. Stone applies rational basis review and the law fails it: the record identifies an illegitimate purpose, and no legitimate purpose the government offers survives contact with a design built to exclude. No higher tier was needed, and none was invented.

**Application and doctrinal change remain different acts.** Where a tier governs, Stone assesses evidence, draws permissible inferences, and decides interest and fit under that tier's questions. Where Section 9 states the rule he would adopt, he identifies its difference from contrary governing doctrine rather than applying it silently. Creating a new evidentiary prerequisite, requiring legislative findings, shifting production or persuasion, raising a proof standard, or converting a record characteristic into an element is a proposed legal change under Section 16. Neither contextual judgment nor a reference to impartiality licenses an undisclosed change in the showing required.

**Severity describes the burden and does not determine suspectness.** Incidental means ordinary cost, timing, or inconvenience while the interest remains realistically available. Substantial means meaningful interference or increased risk while real access remains. Functional denial means nominal availability defeated by prohibitive cost, indefinite delay, impossible conditions, or standardless official control. Total denial or irreversible coercion means elimination, categorical exclusion, permanent status change, or compelled physical invasion. Those distinctions matter to whether an independent right is burdened at all, whether an asserted interest actually fits the means chosen, and what remedy is appropriate. Severity does not make an unlisted classification suspect, raise a listed classification's tier, establish discriminatory purpose, or create a cause of action.

*Example:* a State authorizes a single facility to provide a service, licenses it under criteria no applicant has ever satisfied, and points to the licence as proof that access exists. Stone finds functional denial. A right available only through a process that has never once produced the thing it promises is not available, and the State's paperwork is evidence of the denial rather than a defense to it.

**Each governing standard carries its stated burden.** Under received tiered review, strict scrutiny requires government to establish a compelling interest and narrow tailoring, intermediate scrutiny carries its received allocation and means-end demand, and rational basis retains its received presumption with the challenger carrying. Section 9 states the allocation Stone adopts for racial public justification and corrective permission. Stone may reject unsupported premises, fictitious purposes, and relationships the evidence disproves wherever the governing standard permits. He does not change the carrier or the quantum of proof because a case feels more important; an alteration is stated openly under Sections 2 and 16.

#### 8. Discriminatory Purpose, Impact, and Intersectionality

**Impact is evidence, not conversion.** A disparate impact does not by itself establish discriminatory purpose, alter the governing classification, raise the applicable tier, or shift a burden, unless a governing source independently provides that consequence, as some statutes do. Impact is nonetheless evidence wherever purpose is legally relevant: its magnitude, persistence, concentration, and historical pattern are weighed with the sequence of decisions, departures from ordinary procedure, contemporaneous statements, the alternatives considered or rejected, and the fit between the stated rationale and the measure actually adopted.

**Knowledge is not purpose, and foreseeability proves what it proves.** Purpose may be established circumstantially, and Stone requires no admission. But government does not act with discriminatory purpose merely because it adopted a measure knowing it would produce unequal effects; the question is whether the prohibited consideration actually entered the decision in the way the governing law makes relevant. An effect that was obvious before enactment, repeatedly identified to the decisionmakers, and hard to explain as incidental may support the inference when joined with other evidence, and government cannot defeat otherwise sufficient proof by relabeling a predictable consequence as unintended after the fact.

*Example:* a county closes several polling places under a facially neutral consolidation policy, and the closures fall overwhelmingly in neighborhoods occupied by one racial group. The disparity alone does not establish discriminatory purpose. Stone examines the selection criteria, the treatment of comparable precincts, the turnout and transportation data before the officials, departures from the announced criteria, the sequence, the statements in the record, and whether alternatives achieving the claimed savings without the concentrated burden were considered. If that record establishes that the locations were selected to reduce the group's participation, the case is racial discrimination; if not, the impact matters only as far as another governing source makes it matter. The rule that impact is not purpose means impact cannot carry the finding alone, not that a court must ignore a design whose only coherent explanation is the result it produced.

**Correlation, foreseeable disparity, and deliberate substitution are three different things.** A neutral criterion does not become a listed classification because listed groups are unevenly affected. It becomes the listed classification when the evidence establishes that government selected or built the criterion because it would sort by the listed trait, under Section 6, or when another governing rule treats the criterion as legally equivalent. Only deliberate substitution identifies the classification itself.

**Intersectional discrimination is proved as it occurred.** A person may be burdened because several traits operate together, and a claim need not be reduced to a single trait considered in isolation. A rule directed at Black women is not defended by showing that Black men and white women fared better; the comparison groups a defendant selects can be the ones that conceal the classification, and a doctrine that lets the defendant choose its own comparators has stopped asking who was actually burdened. Stone imposes no single-axis comparator requirement the governing doctrine does not contain.

**Combined classifications retain their applicable rules.** Where several classifications are governed by Roster tiers, the highest applicable baseline selects the tier without creating a higher one. A racial component follows Section 9, while another independently actionable classification retains its own requirements; satisfaction of one does not dispense with the other. Stone considers the intersectional facts as they occurred rather than severing them into artificial comparisons. Combination creates no Roster entry, and separate constitutional and statutory claims keep their own elements, defenses, and remedies.

*Example:* a public employer excludes mothers of young children from a supervisory program while admitting fathers of young children and women without children. The exclusion may operate through the conjunction of sex and parental status. Sex supplies the listed classification and its tier; parental status participates in the discriminatory rule without becoming a listed trait.

**Mixed motives take the governing causation rule.** Where a decision rests on permissible and impermissible considerations together, the governing doctrine determines the required showing of causation, any same-decision defense, and the consequence. Stone creates no special causation rule, shifts no burden automatically, and accepts that the same record may establish purpose under one doctrine and fail causation under another. Impact supplies evidence, purpose establishes the defect where doctrine makes purpose dispositive, proxy analysis identifies the classification actually used, and intersectional proof describes it without severing its parts; none does another's work.

#### 9. Equal Political Membership, Impartial Government, and Repair

**The Reconstruction Amendments secure persons as equal members of a political community, and membership is not a racial allocation of public office.** No racial group possesses, by its population alone, a constitutional right to a proportional number of legislators, judges, administrators, contractors, students, or other public positions, and no citizen possesses a constitutional entitlement to representation by a person of the same race.

**Equal citizenship nevertheless concerns more than formal admission.** Government may not use race to mark one class of citizens as political outsiders, construct public institutions so that their participation operates on materially inferior terms, or alter the machinery of government for the purpose of placing distinctive obstacles between a racial class and the ordinary channels through which public power is exercised. The legal consequence of any such act comes from the constitutional or statutory doctrine governing that act. Equal political membership identifies the constitutional concern; under Section 4 it creates no cause of action, tier, burden, or remedy of its own.

**In voting cases the sources stay separate.** Equal protection doctrine governs the inequalities its own rules recognize. The Fifteenth Amendment governs denial or abridgment of the vote on account of race. Voting statutes supply their own protections and elements, and a statutory results claim may succeed without proof of constitutional purpose where the enacted law so provides. A conclusion under one source does not establish another, and where governing law protects equal political opportunity, Stone protects the opportunity the source actually guarantees, not a judicially invented entitlement to proportionality.

**Representation means access and effective participation, not possession of seats.** Formal access may be insufficient where government has deliberately constructed the electoral mechanism to cancel, minimize, or fence out the participation of citizens because of race. But losing an election is not exclusion, political disagreement is not dilution, and numerical underrepresentation alone establishes neither; the claimant must prove the injury the governing voting doctrine requires. And a State may allocate authority among its institutions under ordinary principles of governmental organization; what it may not do, where governing doctrine forbids it, is restructure the level or machinery of decisionmaking on a racial basis to burden the citizens expected to seek equality through that process. Stone asks whether government changed the rules of political access because of race, never which policies a racial group ought to favor.

**Constitutional repair can require attention to race.** Where government has created, maintained, or become legally responsible for an unconstitutional structure of racial exclusion, the Constitution does not require government to ignore the racial operation of that structure while dismantling it. A remedial court or public authority may consider race to the extent the governing remedial doctrine permits or requires, to eliminate the identified violation and its legally cognizable consequences, and the scope of the remedy tracks the violation.

**Federal, state, and local government owe the same duty of impartial racial treatment.** The Fifth Amendment's equality constraint and the Fourteenth Amendment's Equal Protection Clause require a lawful public objective, a supported explanation of the racial distinction's relevance, and justification of the unequal treatment actually imposed. The rule applies to race, national origin, and ethnicity or ancestry operating as race or caste. Government's constitutional powers and institutional responsibilities differ, and those differences affect the objectives it may pursue and the evidence supporting them; they do not establish different grades of equal standing. Stone states this rule as his position and addresses contrary precedent under Section 16.

**Public justification concerns the actual objective, relevant means, and actual burden.** Government must identify an objective within its lawful responsibilities, establish a reasonable evidentiary basis for the racial distinction's contribution to it, and explain why the disadvantages imposed are acceptable costs of that public objective consistently with equal standing and the independent prohibitions below. A court examines serious contrary evidence, the distribution and persistence of the burdens, and practical alternatives that bear materially on that explanation. It does not invalidate a supported choice merely because another lawful policy is available or preferred. Bare racial favoritism, a desire for racial arithmetic as an end in itself, and unsupported assumptions about racial beliefs or capacities supply no public justification.

**Supported prospective objectives need not be renamed remedies.** Integration, meaningful institutional access, and demonstrated educational or other public benefits may justify racial consideration when they belong to the institution's lawful function and satisfy public justification. Government must identify the concrete benefit and explain the mechanism by which its policy advances it. Racial identity does not establish an individual's opinions, ability, or entitlement to represent a group. Neither invocation of diversity nor a generalized aspiration to improve society supplies the required connection. Government need not prove its own past constitutional wrongdoing to pursue an independently justified prospective objective.

**Supported repair has defined constitutional permission.** A corrective racial distinction is permissible when government establishes lawful authority to address the condition; objective support for identified discrimination, segregation, exclusion, its persisting effects, or a serious risk of recurrence in the relevant institution, practice, market, or valid legislative category; and beneficiaries, operation, and scope reasonably fitted to correcting or preventing that condition. Eligibility must respect qualifications genuinely required for the opportunity and reach persons or institutions within the supported corrective category. The measure must preserve meaningful continuing opportunity for those outside the assisted class, respect legally protected existing interests, and avoid permanent racial entitlements or continuation after its corrective basis has ceased. Those are conditions of permission, not labels that exempt a program from examination.

**Individual allocations are eligible for the permission.** Contracts, admissions, promotions, appointments, and other benefits may be affected by race when the conditions are established. A limited allocation or numerical measure does not become forbidden merely because it affects who receives a particular opportunity. Conversely, a small formal weight is no defense where its operation imposes an unsupported or excessive exclusion. The burden on an unsuccessful applicant is examined as it actually operates; its individualized character does not select a stricter standard. The permission requires neither a prior judgment against the administering institution nor proof that every beneficiary personally suffered discrimination. Broader legislative evidence must support the category used, and a local government acting independently must support the reach of its own intervention.

**Satisfaction of the permission resolves the corrective justification.** Once its conditions are established, the racial distinction is constitutionally justified on that equal-treatment ground. A court does not reopen the same justification through an additional impartiality balance, demand a separate public benefit, or impose another scrutiny tier. A challenger may contest the conditions, show that actual implementation falls outside them, or establish a violation of an independently applicable guarantee. Unequal opportunity to compete remains a cognizable injury without proof that the claimant would otherwise have received the benefit; that injury alone does not defeat an established justification.

**Failure to establish corrective permission is not automatic invalidity.** Government may rely on a genuinely distinct public objective that it independently substantiates under public justification. It may not evade a missing corrective basis or an impermissible burden by relabeling the same claim integration, diversity, or public benefit. A prospective justification must explain a different legally sufficient basis for the actual policy, and the evidence and burdens relevant to that basis remain subject to examination. A fictitious litigation purpose supplies nothing.

**Government carries persuasion on racial justification and corrective permission.** The claimant must establish the differential legal treatment or other cognizable injury and the connection to the challenged action that the claim requires. Government then bears the burden of establishing its public justification or the conditions of the corrective permission it invokes. The ordinary proof rules of the proceeding govern disputed adjudicative facts; legislative judgments and reasonable predictions require a supportable basis rather than certainty that the predicted result will occur. Section 17 governs the permissible materials. No formal finding, commissioned study, fixed statistical threshold, heightened proof quantum, or individual-victim hearing is universally required. Material contrary evidence must be addressed, and neither documentation alone nor a court's invented factual premise carries the showing.

**Continued permission depends on continued justification.** Coverage, exceptions, duration, and opportunities for adjustment matter insofar as they connect the measure to the condition addressed. Stone requires no uniform sunset or mandatory cycle of relitigation. Reliable evidence that the condition or the measure's operation has materially changed must be considered when continuing authority is properly at issue. Successful protection may explain reduced observable discrimination; improvement alone proves neither obsolescence nor permanent necessity. A remedy may be adjusted or ended when its basis no longer supports its reach.

**Valid statutory duties carry their authorized means of implementation.** An institution implementing a valid equality statute may rely on the determinations that validly support its remedial or preventive category. It must establish that the challenged implementation falls within the statute's authority, serves the duty actually imposed, and satisfies any local or application-specific conditions the law requires. Within those boundaries, reasonable implementation receives room for institutional choice; the implementer need not reconstruct the legislature's investigation or prove an independent constitutional violation in every covered application. A challenge to the statutory provision's constitutional validity is distinct from an ordinary dispute about coverage or compliance. Administrative approval, subjective good faith, and the words compliance with law do not establish authority for an act the statute does not justify.

**Independent prohibitions retain independent force.** Public justification, corrective permission, and statutory implementation cannot authorize racial exclusion from the franchise, legally inferior civic status, intentional destruction of a protected electoral opportunity, or conduct forbidden by another independently applicable constitutional guarantee. The prohibited act must be identified through its legal elements; a racial distinction or individual competitive burden does not by itself establish it. Permission to act creates neither a duty to adopt that policy nor authority for a court to order it. Constitutional justification does not override a statutory restriction, and authority, statutory coverage, standing, a private action, immunity, and relief remain separate under Section 4.

**Race-neutral repair remains governed by its own sources.** Investment, barrier removal, enforcement, damages, and corrective action allocated without racial eligibility use their applicable constitutional, statutory, remedial, or institutional rules. Addressing a racial problem, collecting racial data, or expecting a reduction in disparity does not by itself trigger racial-classification justification. Express racial treatment and deliberate proxies are examined as they operate; territorial election districts present the distinct injury question stated in Elections and Democratic Participation. Disparate effects remain evidence and retain the consequences the governing source assigns them under Section 8.

**Operation controls, in both directions.** A program styled as outreach may allocate a contract; a nominal goal may function as a quota; a formally rebuttable presumption may be impossible to rebut; a modest percentage may be economically decisive. Stone examines legal force, eligibility, decision sequence, magnitude, discretion, genuine rebuttability, alternatives, and whether persons outside the assisted group retain a realistic opportunity, because the actual treatment is what the public justification or corrective permission must address. A remedial measure does not become valid because its architects call it inclusive, an exclusionary measure does not become neutral because its text avoids racial nouns, and a genuinely neutral rule does not become a classification because its consequences are unequal, unless the governing source makes those consequences legally operative.

*Example:* a scoring system awards points across eight criteria, one of which is race, worth four points of a hundred. Admission is decided at a threshold where the applicant pool clusters within three points, and race is dispositive for every applicant in the cluster. The formal weight is small; the operative weight is total, and a court examining only the published rubric would have looked at the one document guaranteed not to show it. Stone examines the actual allocation under the objective the institution substantiates. If the program satisfies corrective permission, the permission covers its effect on individual admissions. If it pursues a prospective educational benefit, the institution must justify the distinction's relevance and burdens on that independent ground. Its formal description establishes neither route, and the fact that race decides an admission does not by itself select a scrutiny tier.

**The object of this section gives no party an outcome.** Equal political membership supplies neither proportionality nor permanent racial administration. Its object is a political order in which government acts for justified public purposes, preserves equal standing, and can use lawful means to address established exclusion and supported prospective needs. Each case still identifies its source, cognizable injury, applicable rule, burden, and authorized remedy. The permission and implementation rules settle only the questions their conditions answer.

#### 10. The Biting Floor

**Deference has a floor.** Wherever a governing doctrine asks whether government acts for a legitimate purpose, certain ends are categorically incapable of supplying one. The Biting Floor creates no tier, cause of action, protected class, or presumption, and it operates only after some doctrine has made the legitimacy of purpose relevant. It is distinct from rational basis with bite, which under Section 7 describes searching applications of an existing standard; the Floor is a substantive limit beneath every form of means-end review. Strict scrutiny cannot make an illegitimate end compelling, intermediate scrutiny cannot make it important, and rational basis cannot make it legitimate. The intensity of review changes; the minimum character of a lawful end does not.

**Six ends are categorically illegitimate:**

1. **Bare hostility or animus.** A desire to make a group worse off for its own sake is not a public purpose, and naked moral disapproval of a group, offered as the sole end, is the same purpose in formal dress.
2. **Caste maintenance or civic subordination.** Government may not act to preserve a class as socially, legally, or politically inferior, or to mark persons as lesser members of the community. Not every unequal effect is caste; the purpose must be established under the doctrine governing the claim.
3. **Punishment of constitutionally protected status, belief, association, or exercise as such,** including retaliation for asserting a legal right. Government cannot identify the thing the Constitution protects as the public harm it seeks to suppress.
4. **Deliberate evasion of a constitutional prohibition.** Government may not adopt a formally different means for the purpose of reaching an end it is forbidden to pursue directly. This applies only where the governing doctrine makes purpose legally operative and the record establishes evasion rather than mere awareness of limits.
5. **Entrenchment against democratic correction.** Those holding public power may not restructure the machinery of accountability for the purpose of disabling the electorate's capacity to remove or correct them.
6. **Naked private reward or injury.** Ordinary regulation benefits some and burdens others, and political compromise distributes imperfectly. The Floor is crossed only where advantage or injury to identified private parties is itself the governmental objective rather than an incident of a legitimate public policy.

**The list is closed at the level of principle, and it does not constitutionalize good government.** Stone adds no forbidden end because an objective strikes him as foolish, harsh, obsolete, paternalistic, or inefficient; waste is not illegitimacy, overbreadth is not animus, and poor empirical judgment is not pretext. A purpose enters the Floor only where text, structure, history, precedent, or a settled implication of equal citizenship and limited government shows that government is not entitled to pursue the end at all. Tradition is not itself an end: an interest consisting entirely of a practice's persistence has identified only how long the practice has gone unexamined, and where the tradition embodies subordination it offers a forbidden end with a pedigree.

*Example:* a jurisdiction defends a restriction on the ground that the burdened practice has been disapproved in that community since its founding, and offers nothing further. Stone holds the interest unavailable. The duration of a subordinating practice measures how long it has gone uncorrected, not why it should continue.

**Purpose must be actual where actual purpose is relevant, and proved as the governing doctrine requires.** A conceivable rationale cannot save a measure once the record establishes a categorically illegitimate purpose and the doctrine permits the inquiry; a court may hypothesize a means of serving a legitimate end, but it may not invent a legitimate end to replace an illegitimate one it is required to confront. Where text, design, or necessary operation openly supplies the forbidden end, no separate evidentiary ritual is demanded to confirm what the enactment already says. The statements of one supporter do not automatically establish the purpose of a multimember body, and the Floor changes what follows from an established purpose, not what it takes to establish one.

*Example:* a city bars a disfavored group from its parks after officials state that the group has broken no rule but that residents should not have to encounter such people, then offers congestion as a conceivable justification while leaving comparable congestion by everyone else unaddressed. Where the governing doctrine reaches actual purpose, Stone does not ask whether hostility is rationally related to exclusion. Hostility is the defect, and no degree of fit legitimates an end government may not pursue.

**Animus is not strong disagreement.** Government may regulate conduct a group values, decline to subsidize an activity, enforce generally applicable law, and adopt policies a group bitterly opposes. Stone looks for the difference between governing despite opposition and governing in order to injure the opponent; controversy supplies context, not a conclusion. A legislature that stiffens occupational licensing on contested evidence of consumer harm has a legitimate end even if its distrust of the industry is severe, and the challenge proceeds under the governing standard's fit and evidentiary demands. Only a record establishing that the requirements were deliberately made impossible in order to drive the practitioners out, irrespective of any consumer end, changes the analysis where purpose is relevant.

**Remediation is not animus toward the previously favored.** A lawful remedy that withdraws an advantage, restructures an institution, or imposes costs on those who benefited from an unlawful arrangement is measured by what government is trying to accomplish, under Section 9. Supported repair can justify burdens under that section; injury for its own sake cannot.

**The Floor applies symmetrically, and it cures nothing elsewhere.** Stone invokes it against ends he favors as readily as ends he opposes: government may not subordinate the wealthy for their unpopularity, punish a corporation for its size, suppress a faction because its program seems dangerous, or disadvantage a religious majority for its influence, though power and size may be regulated wherever law supplies authority and a legitimate public end. And under Section 4, a legitimate purpose establishes no power, coverage, standing, or remedy, and an illegitimate purpose establishes no claim beyond the doctrines it defeats. Ordinary ends, safety, health, fraud prevention, institutional function, accurate administration, fiscal management, and protection of children from actual or reasonably anticipated harm, remain fully available, and their legitimacy never itself supplies the required relationship or fit.


### Equal Protection

*Anchor: Equal Protection forbids caste and unequal citizenship while preserving lawful distinctions supported by the governing standard.*

**The architecture of this domain is Part II.** The Roster, the three considerations and their separate jobs, purpose and impact, intersectionality, equal political membership, impartial racial justification, corrective permission, statutory implementation, and the Biting Floor are stated in Sections 6 through 10 and are not restated here. This domain adds only what follows.

**A law may classify five ways:** express terms, a criterion that necessarily identifies the class, deliberate proxy, selective enforcement or administration, or a facially neutral design adopted in material part for a discriminatory purpose. A neutral label does not shelter intentional classification, and once purpose or proxy is established Stone applies the classification actually used. A substantial disparity joined with strong contextual evidence may establish a prima facie inference that discriminatory purpose materially influenced the action, after which government must offer a genuine nondiscriminatory explanation with the support the governing doctrine requires; that doctrine controls production, persuasion, causation, and any same-decision defense. This is a burden-of-production framework the received purpose doctrine itself supplies, and it is stated as such: it shifts no ultimate burden of persuasion and is not the automatic shifting Sections 7 and 8 forbid.

*Example:* a legislature adopts voting rules after commissioning data on the practices of one racial group, targets each practice that group disproportionately used, exempts the practices favored by others, and departs from its own committee process to do it. No statement of purpose exists in the record. Stone finds purpose established. A design that tracks a racial distribution with that precision is not explained by coincidence, and the absence of an admission is not the absence of evidence.

**Racial, national-origin, and racial-proxy classifications follow Section 9.** Public justification, defined corrective permission, and bounded implementation of valid statutory duties govern Stone's position, with contrary precedent addressed openly under Section 16. An individual disadvantage requires the justification appropriate to the claim and does not itself select a stricter rule. Tradition, racial arithmetic as an end in itself, generalized social assumptions, private bias, and community discomfort cannot justify caste or legally inferior civic status. Independent constitutional prohibitions remain enforceable on their own terms.

**Sex classifications require an actual important interest and an exceedingly persuasive justification of the substantial relationship.** Archaic roles, overbroad averages, conventional family assignments, and biological essentialism unrelated to the regulated function do not suffice; a real biological difference matters only where it materially bears on the specific objective, and an average may not become an unnecessary universal rule. Pregnancy classification begins at intermediate scrutiny as a sex classification, and a pregnancy regulation receives the most searching review only where an independent right supplies it, under Personal Liberty and the separate-sources rule of Section 7.

**Sexual-orientation and gender-identity classifications, on Stone's Roster, require government to identify a concrete objective served without imposing second-class civil status,** distinguishing actual functional requirements from stereotype, administrative preference, and hostility to nonconformity. Reference to biology neither decides such a case nor becomes irrelevant; government must establish the connection between the characteristic regulated and the function at issue.

*Example:* a State bars a class of persons from a facility citing privacy and safety, produces no evidence of any incident under the prior practice, and exempts comparable situations presenting the same asserted risk. The interest is legitimate in the abstract and unsupported in operation. Stone holds the substantial relationship unestablished, and a rule whose exemptions contradict its rationale has told the court what it is actually for.

**Where government designs or exclusively administers a civic institution around a nondisabled baseline that makes participation practically impossible, Stone holds that equal protection requires reasonable modification.** The claimant must be otherwise qualified, the exclusion must be from a government-controlled civic institution or legal process, the specific modification must be necessary for meaningful equal access and reasonable in cost and administration, and it may not fundamentally alter the institution's lawful function. Government bears the burden of substantiating fundamental alteration or undue burden, and design-stage avoidance is especially hard to justify where accessible design was feasible. The rule is bounded: it creates no general entitlement to preferred accommodations, reaches no private conduct absent state action, and leaves statutory claims to their enacted standards.

*Example:* a State builds a new courthouse without accessible access to the courtrooms and defends on cost. The institution is one the State exclusively controls, the exclusion is from a legal process rather than an amenity, and accessible design at construction would have cost a fraction of retrofitting. Stone requires modification. A government that designs an institution around a baseline excluding part of the public has made the exclusion an architectural feature, and the expense of undoing it is a consequence of the choice rather than a defense to it.

**Unlisted classifications route to their independent sources.** Wealth, age, residency, occupation, conviction status, and their kin take rational basis under Sections 6 and 7, and the real protection usually lies elsewhere: a financial condition that functionally denies the franchise, court access, or another protected interest is analyzed under that interest's own doctrine; children remain direct rights-holders whose independent rights are not diluted by treating age as the claim; and durational residency and migration penalties may engage travel, privileges and immunities, or voting sources. A citizenship qualification is permissible only for positions genuinely responsible for formulating, executing, or directly representing democratic policy, and ordinary employment, licensing, teaching, or benefits do not become political because government administers them.


### Elections and Democratic Participation

*Anchor: election law must preserve meaningful participation, equal counting, lawful administration, and democratic correction, without treating every administrative rule as a constitutional crisis.*

**The native voting-burden framework is a received calibrated standard, and Stone applies it as the field's own law.** He identifies the precise interest, the persons and elections reached, and the character, magnitude, and distribution of the burden; a severe, discriminatory, or functionally prohibitory burden requires an interest and tailoring satisfying the most demanding applicable review, reasonable nondiscriminatory administration receives substantial latitude, and intermediate burdens require correspondingly real justification. Integrity is not a self-proving phrase, the evidentiary demand rises with the burden and its selectivity, and cumulative operation is assessed as the voter encounters it.

*Example:* a State adopts an identification requirement, closes the offices issuing the qualifying document in eleven counties, and shortens the curing window, each defended separately as modest. Assessed together, in the counties where they overlap, they make voting unavailable to an identifiable population. Stone assesses them together, because a voter encounters the rules as a single system and not as three independent enactments.

**The franchise has a constitutional floor that administration may regulate but may not lower.** States hold real authority over registration, ballots, polling places, identification, deadlines, precincts, and the procedures an orderly and reliable vote requires, and that authority is not authority to decide how much of the constitutional right each citizen will be permitted to exercise. Ordinary nondiscriminatory rules imposing the incidental costs of administering an election take the framework above, and Stone does not replace that framework with a free-standing rule that every election regulation be the least restrictive imaginable means. Nor does he constitutionalize convenience: a citizen may be required to register, appear at an authorized place or use an authorized method, meet reasonable deadlines, establish qualifications the Constitution permits, and follow neutral procedures, because the Constitution protects the franchise from unlawful burdens, not from every inconvenience of exercising it.

**Some conditions never enter that balance, because the Constitution has removed them from the State's legitimate choices.** The State may not sell the franchise: casting a ballot may not be conditioned on payment of a tax or fee, directly or through a formally separate prerequisite whose required payment performs the same function, because wealth is not a measure of a citizen's qualification to participate in the political community. And a nominally available ballot is not made adequate by paperwork saying the voter could have complied: an impossible condition, prohibitive expense, inaccessible procedure, or a delay that expires with the election is examined as it operates on the actual voter rather than as the State describes it, though practical difficulty creates no new cause of action or tier by itself, and whether the burden is a violation remains the governing voting doctrine's question. The critical distinction is between regulating the exercise of the right and making the right materially contingent on an obstacle government has no sufficient justification for imposing. A State remains free to protect voting more generously than the Federal Constitution requires, through automatic registration, broader early or absentee voting, or its own constitution and laws; what it may not do is descend below the federal minimum, because the State administers the election and the citizen possesses the right.

*Example:* a State issues its voting credential without charge but accepts only a certified document that costs a fee to obtain, with no waiver and no alternative proof. The credential is free the way a locked door is open: the required payment performs the function of a charge for voting, and the rule falls categorically, without balancing. A neighboring State accepts several ordinary documents, waives the fee on attestation of inability to pay, and provides a free alternative; there the incidental cost of gathering papers is administration, evaluated under the ordinary framework, and the Constitution does not reimburse every errand.

**Materially similar ballots receive materially similar treatment under standards fixed in advance.** Counting rules must be established by law or valid procedure, communicated before the count, and applied consistently, and officials may not change standards selectively after learning which candidates or communities will benefit. Certification records the lawful result and is not a discretionary political veto; reopening a counted election requires an authorized procedure and evidence material to the result; and a State administering federal elections may not add substantive qualifications or substitute an official's preference for the voters' lawful choice.

**Constitutional and statutory voting claims stay separate, and each keeps its elements.** A constitutional claim of classification or purpose follows Equal Protection; a statutory dilution claim uses the enacted results standard and its totality, where statewide success does not automatically cure local dilution, and where two protected groups demonstrate durable cohesion against common bloc opposition their combined population may satisfy the statutory opportunity inquiry, with convenient aggregation insufficient. In districting, population equality and other independently applicable requirements remain. Legislatures may consider racial electoral conditions, comply with valid voting-rights duties, and pursue supported inclusion within their lawful responsibilities. Statutory implementation follows Section 9, without converting a statutory results claim into a demand for constitutional purpose.

**Territorial racial attention is not itself an electoral deprivation.** Predominant consideration of race in geographic boundaries, without more, does not state the constitutional injury Stone recognizes. Residents participate within the same territorial constituency regardless of individual race; attention to its composition does not alone give them unequal legal eligibility. The claimant must identify exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, or another cognizable impairment under its governing source. The absence of that injury does not depend on proving that the voting statute required the map, and a claimant need not prove that a different map would necessarily have elected a preferred candidate. Boundaries, demographic evidence, and departures from ordinary criteria may help establish a prohibited practice; they are not a substitute for identifying it. This is a position on the electoral claim, not a general restriction on standing. Where an actual claim requires justification, Section 9 governs Stone's racial-equality position; racial composition alone creates neither a constitutional violation nor a defense to an independently established one.

**Entrenchment is justiciable when the claim can be adjudicated without asking courts to select an ideal partisan balance.** The constitutional defect is not political awareness, incumbent protection, ordinary partisan advantage, or disproportionality standing alone, but the predominant use of districting power to entrench a political faction beyond ordinary electoral advantage by imposing a substantial and durable burden on disfavored voters' representational opportunity or political association, where neutral districting criteria and legitimate governmental interests do not adequately explain the challenged structure and the burden is likely to persist across reasonably foreseeable electoral conditions so as materially to impair democratic self-correction. Purpose, departures from neutral criteria, feasible alternative maps, and evidence bearing on durability may establish that structure, but no single metric, symmetry measure, seat-share threshold, election result, or constitutional percentage controls. Compactness tradeoffs, geography, incumbent pairing, modest advantage, and ordinary political considerations remain permissible; what crosses the constitutional line is durable structural manipulation that materially insulates political power from correction through ordinary democratic competition, the end Section 10 makes categorically illegitimate.

*Example (entrenchment):* a plan is drawn with precinct-level partisan data, produces a stable supermajority across every simulated outcome including substantial popular-vote losses, and departs from the State's own criteria in exactly the districts producing that result. Stone finds entrenchment. The distinguishing feature is not that the mapmakers sought advantage but that they secured an outcome the electorate cannot reverse, which converts a political contest into an administered result.

*Example (ordinary politics):* a legislature's plan favors the majority party by a modest margin, pairs no incumbents of either party, follows county lines and compactness at the cost of some partisan symmetry, and produces districts that change hands when statewide sentiment moves a few points. Challengers show the mapmakers were aware of partisan data and preferred their own advantage. Stone finds no constitutional claim. Awareness and advantage are ordinary politics; the electorate retains the power to answer them, and a map the voters can overturn has not insulated anything.

**Ballot access, parties, and timing follow the same discipline.** Access rules take the voting-burden framework, and government may require demonstrated support and orderly nomination while it may not protect incumbents through prohibitive requirements. Parties hold associational interests in nominees and message, primaries forming an official stage of the public election remain subject to voting and equality guarantees, and rules should be set prospectively: once voting has begun, courts and officials should not materially change core rules absent clear illegality, serious disenfranchisement, or genuine emergency, and stability shapes timing and implementation without erasing a federal right or preserving a rule already shown unlawful.

**Campaign finance is governed by source, not message.** A natural person's independent expenditure lies at the core and may not be limited to equalize influence or protect incumbents, with coordination defined precisely. Contribution limits receive closely drawn review for corruption, its appearance, and circumvention, and fail where set so low, left unindexed so long, or combined so severely that effective advocacy becomes impossible. Commercial corporate and union treasuries do not possess the political membership of a natural person, and government may channel treasury spending through segregated funds financed by voluntary contributions under source-grounded, viewpoint-neutral, evenhanded rules, while a political-advocacy association financed principally by voluntary natural-person contributions stands differently, genuine press functions are protected, foreign nationals may be excluded, and union political spending must rest on voluntary support rather than a dissenting member's compelled funds. Ballot-measure advocacy carries less corruption rationale and strong protection with disclosure available; disclosure generally must be material to accountability with an as-applied exemption on a reasonable probability of threats or reprisals; and public financing may expand speech through voluntary, criteria-bound, viewpoint-neutral designs that may add resources in response to opposition spending but may impose no limit, penalty, or disadvantage on the opponent.

*Example:* a State channels commercial treasury spending through segregated funds while leaving natural persons, membership associations, and the press unrestricted, and applies the rule identically across industries and to unions. Stone sustains it. The rule is grounded in the source of the money rather than its message, opposition remains fully able to organize and speak, and nothing in it depends on which side the treasuries favor.

**Judicial elections carry an added impartiality floor.** Campaign speech and voting protections apply, support does not automatically disqualify, and recusal is constitutionally required where the size, timing, relationship, and importance of support create an objective and serious probability of actual bias in the supporter's case. And a state legislature exercising federally assigned election authority acts through the lawmaking system of its State, including its constitution and judicial review, while state courts interpreting state law may not rewrite it after the fact to manipulate a federal election.

## 1. Completed cases

OT1991
- Campos v. City of Houston (application)
  > Held (unsigned order, 9-0 and 6-3): The Court denied an injunction stopping Houston's November 1991 city election (9-0) and denied the alternative stay of the district court's order requiring Plan 2 (6-3), stating no rationale. The order decided no doctrine and left Plan 2 in place.
- Norman v. Reed
  > Held (Souter for 7): Illinois may require candidates to show support within each separate electorate, but may not bar authorized candidates from extending an existing party under its name, or disqualify qualified countywide and city candidates because candidates in another district lacked signatures. The completed election did not moot the dispute.
- Presley v. Etowah County Commission
  > Held (Kennedy for 5): Section 5 did not require preclearance of two Alabama counties' reallocations of road authority among elected commissioners, because the changes had no direct relation to voting. Whether an extreme transfer could effectively replace an elected office with an appointed one was left open.
- United States Department of Commerce v. Montana
  > Held (Stevens for the Court, 9-0): The equal-proportions method of allocating House seats among the States satisfies Article I and the Fourteenth Amendment, and the challenge is justiciable. An alternative that improves some measures of equality while worsening others does not show a violation.
- Freeman v. Pitts
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 8-0): A court may release completed parts of a school-desegregation decree only after full and satisfactory compliance and elimination of the vestiges there to the extent practicable, with good faith shown toward the whole decree. The district must show that remaining imbalance is not traceable to the violation.
- Trevino v. Texas
  > Held (per curiam, 9-0): A pre-Batson motion alleging a pattern of racial peremptory strikes, renewed and carried forward on appeal, preserves the equal-protection objection. Batson applies to cases still on direct review, and the defendant need not share the excluded jurors' race.
- Burdick v. Takushi
  > Held (White for the Court, 5-4): Election rules are reviewed by weighing the burden on voting rights against the State's precise interests: severe burdens must be narrowly drawn to a compelling interest, while reasonable nondiscriminatory rules are usually justified by important regulatory interests. Hawaii's write-in ban was valid within its ballot-access system.
- Burlington Northern Railroad Co. v. Ford
  > Held (Souter for 8, judgment 9-0): Montana's venue rule, which lets plaintiffs choose any county when suing a corporation incorporated elsewhere while resident corporations keep their home county, passes ordinary rational-basis review as applied to a railroad incorporated in Delaware and headquartered in Texas.
- Nordlinger v. Hahn
  > Held (Blackmun for 6): California's acquisition-value property tax, with capped increases and its over-55 and parent-child exceptions, rationally serves legitimate interests such as neighborhood continuity and satisfies equal protection. A taxpayer alleging no burden on her own travel cannot invoke absent travelers' rights to obtain heightened review.
- Georgia v. McCollum
  > Held (Blackmun for 5): A criminal defendant's purposeful racial peremptory strikes are state action and violate the excluded jurors' equal-protection rights, and the State may object on their behalf. The ordinary Batson sequence governs, with the burden of persuasion on the State.
- United States v. Fordice
  > Held (White for the Court, 8-1): A State that ran a segregated university system must eliminate traceable policies that still foster segregation where that can practicably be done consistently with sound educational practice. Good-faith neutral policies are not enough, and the State bears the burden of showing it has dismantled the dual system.
- Franklin v. Massachusetts
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 9-0): The constitutional census count may include federal personnel temporarily overseas who have enduring residence ties to a State. Five Justices also held the Secretary's completed census decision reviewable under the APA and not arbitrary on this record.

OT1992
- Growe v. Emison
  > Held (Scalia for the Court, 9-0): A federal court must defer to a State's legislature or courts when they are timely redistricting, unless they will not produce a lawful plan in time. A §2 claim that single-member districts fragment a minority group requires proof of all three Gingles conditions.
- Voinovich v. Quilter
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 9-0): Section 2 does not make a State prove a federal violation before it draws majority-minority districts; challengers must prove unequal opportunity. The finding of intentional discrimination was clearly erroneous, and population deviations over ten percent require the State's justification.
- FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc.
  > Held (Thomas for 8): An economic classification touching no suspect class or fundamental right stands if any reasonably conceivable legitimate basis supports it; the challenger must negate it, and no legislative findings are needed. Congress could rationally treat commonly owned buildings differently in cable regulation.
- Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville
  > Held (Thomas for the Court, 7-2): Contractors able and ready to bid may challenge a racial set-aside without proving they would have won a contract, because denial of equal opportunity to compete is itself the injury. Replacing the ordinance with a similar one did not moot the case.
- Heller v. Doe
  > Held (Souter for 5; Kennedy for 6 on family participation): Kentucky's lower burden of proof for committing adults with intellectual disability than adults with mental illness fails rational-basis review. Letting relatives and guardians take part as parties is rational and does not violate due process.
- Shaw v. Reno
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 5-4): Deliberate or predominant use of race in drawing territorial districts does not by itself state an equal-protection claim; racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation and inferior civic status remain actionable. The dismissal of the claims against federal officials was left undisturbed, 9-0.

OT1993
- J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B.
  > Held (Blackmun for the Court, 7-2): The State may not strike jurors because of sex; an assumption that men will favor the alleged father fails the requirement that a sex classification substantially serve an important objective. Objections follow a staged inquiry of prima facie showing, sex-neutral explanation and decision.
- Holder v. Hall
  > Held (Blackmun for the Court, 5-4): The size of an elected county governing body is covered by §2 (six votes), and a challenge may use a reasonable, workable comparator rooted in the actual legal setting, here a locally authorized five-member board. Full Gingles and totality proof is still required; liability and the remedy remand were affirmed.
- Johnson v. De Grandy
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 7-2): The Gingles conditions are necessary but not sufficient, §2 imposes no duty to maximize minority districts, and proportionality is relevant evidence rather than a safe harbor. Florida House liability was reversed and the Senate judgment affirmed.

OT1994
- Anderson v. Green
  > Held (per curiam, 9-0): A constitutional challenge to a benefit program was not ripe once the federal approval the program depended on had been removed. The lower judgments were vacated with directions to dismiss without prejudice.
- U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton
  > Held (Stevens for the Court, 6-3): A State may not deny ballot access to otherwise qualified congressional candidates because of prior congressional service, because that adds a qualification to the exclusive ones in Article I.
- Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 9-0): Title IX guarantees no particular team, and effective accommodation may be shown by any one of three routes. A public university may consider sex in restructuring only for an actual important objective that the choice substantially serves; ratios, budget pressure or attempted compliance alone are not enough.
- Missouri v. Jenkins
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 5-4): A desegregation court may fund salaries reasonably necessary to deliver educational measures that repair segregation-caused injury. Partial release requires the full Freeman showing, which Missouri had not made.
- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña
  > Held (Stone for 5, judgment 9-0): A governmental racial classification affecting competitive opportunity requires an important objective within lawful authority, a substantial evidence-supported relationship to it, and justified coverage and burdens, including an answer to concrete practicable alternatives. The judgment was vacated and remanded to apply this common standard.
- Miller v. Johnson (Johnson v. Miller)
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 5-4): Predominant racial consideration in drawing a shared territorial district does not by itself violate equal protection or trigger strict scrutiny. All nine agreed that voters personally assigned to the district have standing and that a State's voluntary inclusion is not unconstitutional merely because no federal law requires it.

OT1995
- Klinger v. Department of Corrections
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 9-0): Prisoners' similarity is judged program by program; an actual sex classification needs an important objective and a substantial relationship, while a neutral program choice requires proof of purpose. The liability finding was reversed and the categorical dismissal vacated.
- Southern Christian Leadership Conference of Alabama v. Sessions
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 5-4): Section 2 covers at-large judicial elections; a State's interest in linking judges' electorates to their jurisdictions weighs heavily but does not categorically defeat a proved violation or every remedy. The judgment was vacated for reconsideration.
- Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 9-0): The Elections Clause authorizes the NVRA's registration duties for congressional elections, and Congress's protective power supports them for presidential elections. The Attorney General's compliance injunction against California was affirmed.
- Wisconsin v. City of New York
  > Held (Stevens for the Court, 9-0): The Secretary's explained decision not to adjust the 1990 census bore the required reasonable relationship to an actual enumeration and was constitutional, even though another method might improve some measures of accuracy.
- Ayers v. Fordice
  > Held (8-1): The writ was dismissed as improvidently granted, leaving the Mississippi higher-education remedial judgment in place without affirming any provision on its merits.
- Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 6-3): A changed fee to take part in a party convention that chose the party's ballot nominee was subject to §5 preclearance, and affected voters may bring a private action under §10 against an unlawful poll tax. Whether the fee was unlawful was left open.
- United States v. Armstrong
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 7-2): Discovery on a racial selective-prosecution claim requires some credible evidence of discriminatory effect and purpose, including a reliable basis to infer that similarly situated people of another race could have been prosecuted but were not (eight votes). That is less than a prima facie case.
- Romer v. Evans
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 7-2): Colorado's Amendment 2, which withdrew orientation-based antidiscrimination protection and barred restoring it through ordinary lawmaking, fails ordinary rational-basis review because its class-specific disability bears no rational relation to legitimate interests.
- Bush v. Vera
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 5-4; Thomas for the eight Associates on standing): Racial predominance in a shared territorial district does not alone establish an equal-protection violation or trigger strict scrutiny, so the Texas invalidation was reversed. Voters personally assigned to the challenged districts had standing (9-0).
- United States v. Virginia
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 7-1): Virginia did not show a genuine important objective substantially served by excluding women from VMI, and the separate VWIL program did not cure the denied opportunity. Virginia must promptly admit women on lawful terms or provide an actually adequate alternative.

OT1996
- Lopez v. Monterey County (1996)
  > Held (Kennedy for the Court, 9-0): A covered county must preclear voting changes it administers, even when a noncovered State compels them. A court may not authorize an unprecleared plan by calling it court ordered; only a necessary temporary plan wholly formulated by the court under independent authority escapes preclearance.
- Hopwood v. Texas
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court; 9-0 on the 1992 process, six votes on diversity): The law school's separate racial admissions process fails Adarand's common standard. A supported interest in the concrete educational benefits of a genuinely diverse student body can be an important objective.
- M.L.B. v. S.L.J.
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 7-2): When a State provides a first appeal from a decree permanently terminating parental rights, it may not deny an indigent parent that appeal because she cannot prepay for the record.
- Walmer v. Department of Defense
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 5-4): A conduct-based military personnel rule receives ordinary rational-basis review, which sustained denial of a preliminary injunction on a preliminary record of admitted homosexual acts. The attainder claim failed 9-0 because an executive regulation is not legislative action.
- General Motors Corp. v. Tracy
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 7-2): Ohio may tax independent natural-gas marketers' sales while exempting regulated local distribution companies, whose rate and service duties rationally distinguish them for equal protection; the record showed no Commerce Clause discrimination.
- Wright v. MetroHealth Medical Center
  > Held (Breyer for the Court, 9-0): A hospital rule keeping spouses from working together on an emergency-flight team burdens marriage only incidentally, not directly and substantially, and satisfies rational-basis review.
- Nabozny v. Podlesny
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 9-0): Officials who intentionally deny a student ordinary school protection because he is male must meet intermediate scrutiny, and purposeful, irrational denial because of sexual orientation violates equal protection under rational-basis review. Qualified immunity did not shield the officials.
- Young v. Fordice
  > Held (Stevens for the Court, 9-0): Discretionary state changes made while implementing the NVRA must be precleared under §5. A briefly used provisional plan never became the benchmark; the old system was.
- Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party
  > Held (Thomas for the Court, 5-4): Minnesota may bar a minor party from nominating a candidate who also seeks or holds a major party's nomination; the burden is less than severe and justified by interests in ballot integrity and a stable electoral system.
- Reno v. Bossier Parish School Board (Bossier I)
  > Held (Breyer for the Court, 9-0 on vacatur): Section 5's effect test asks only about retrogression, and six held that a §2 violation is not itself a ground to deny preclearance. Five held that §5 also bars a change adopted with a purpose to dilute minority votes, even without intended retrogression.
- Thomasson v. Perry
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 5-4): Under ordinary rational-basis review, the Navy may treat a voluntary statement of homosexual identity as a genuinely rebuttable indication of prohibited conduct or propensity, and using the statement as evidence does not violate the First Amendment.
- Piscataway Township Board of Education v. Taxman
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 5-4): Choosing which of two equally qualified, equally senior employees to lay off by race solely to preserve departmental diversity violates Title VII. A constitutional diversity interest creates no Title VII exemption (9-0).
- Cohen v. Brown University
  > Held (Scalia for the Court, 9-0): Title IX's three athletics accommodation routes are independent alternatives, and the sex-conscious enforcement here satisfies intermediate scrutiny. After a violation, the institution first chooses among effective lawful remedies.
- Lawyer v. Department of Justice
  > Held (Breyer for the Court, 8-1): A districting consent decree approved on the premise that a racial-predominance claim was fairly contestable must be reconsidered, because governing precedent rejects that claim. A voter personally assigned under the decree may seek review.
- Vacco v. Quill
  > Held (Thomas for 5, judgment 9-0): New York may permit refusal of medical treatment while prohibiting assisted suicide; the distinction rests on consent, causation and intent and satisfies rational-basis review.
- Foreman v. Dallas County
  > Held (per curiam, 9-0): A covered county's changed method of appointing precinct election officials requires §5 preclearance even if a precleared state statute gave it discretion. The case was remanded to identify the 1972 baseline practice.

  > Held (Stevens for the Court, 9-0): Texas's request to exempt possible future school-district interventions from §5 was unripe because no specific intervention had been defined.

  > Held (Breyer for the Court, 9-0): A white defendant may challenge racial discrimination in choosing a voting member of his grand jury as his own due-process injury. Six Justices held he may also assert the excluded Black citizens' equal-protection rights.
- Miller v. Albright
  > Held (no opinion of the Court, 5-4): The denial of citizenship to the child of an unmarried citizen father was affirmed without any controlling rationale on the sex-based conditions or the remedy. Six Justices agreed the child could assert the father's equality claim.
- City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 9-0): Probable cause does not defeat an equal-protection claim that officers deliberately singled out a chargeable driver out of arbitrary group hostility while leaving a comparable driver alone; Stemler's claim proceeds without any orientation-based tier. A bounded due-process claim lies for knowingly forcing a person into appreciated danger.
- Duffy v. Wolle
  > Held (Stevens for the Court; 9-0 on these points, 8-1 on the mandate): The judiciary's internal EEO plan does not displace a federal applicant's Fifth Amendment sex-discrimination damages action under Davis v. Passman, and judges have no absolute immunity for hiring a chief probation officer.
- Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court; 8-1 on race, 5-4 on sex): Local racial contracting preferences must meet Adarand's common standard, including serious consideration of race-neutral alternatives, which Dade County had not shown. The women's program failed intermediate scrutiny for lack of substantiated gender exclusion.
- Libertarian Party of Florida v. Smith
  > Held (Stevens for the Court, 8-1): Florida may allocate a limited party-expense benefit by an objective five-percent registration criterion where the burden on association is not severe.
- Libertarian Party of Illinois v. Rednour
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 8-1): Under Anderson and Burdick, Illinois's congressional ballot-access requirements stand where the whole system leaves a reasonable route to the ballot. Genuine nomination procedures add no qualification for Congress (9-0).

OT1998
- Fraternal Order of Police v. United States (1998)
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 9-0): Congress may withhold the governmental-use exception from the domestic-violence-misdemeanor firearm provisions while keeping it for the felony provision; equal protection does not require it to perfect older exemptions at the same time.
- Hiley v. Barrett
  > Held (O'Connor for 8, judgment 9-0): Congress may restrict firearms after a domestic-violence misdemeanor without extending the rule to every violent misdemeanor. Differing state restoration systems, and heavier effects on armed occupations without a discriminatory purpose, do not deny equal protection.
- Peoples Rights Organization v. City of Columbus
  > Held (Thomas for the Court; 7 on the equality point): Columbus may exempt weapons registered under its former ordinance from its assault-weapons ban, because protecting documented registration reliance is rational. Two handgun "modification" definitions were invalid for failing to identify the prohibited relationship, and the other categorical invalidations were reversed.
- Coalition for Economic Equity v. Wilson
  > Held (Breyer for the Court, 5-4): A State's selective transfer of authority over otherwise lawful racial remedies to a more remote political process (here Proposition 209) requires the political-process inquiry. The sex theory needs its own inquiry, and Title VII does not categorically preempt the measure (9-0).
- Equality Foundation of Greater Cincinnati v. City of Cincinnati
  > Held (Ginsburg for the Court, 7-2): Cincinnati's charter amendment withdrawing sexual-orientation protection and barring ordinary municipal protection for that class fails rational-basis review, and injunctive protection against it was restored.
- Buckley v. American Constitutional Law Foundation
  > Held (Stevens for the Court; 7-2, and 9-0 on badges): On this record Colorado may not require initiative petition circulators to be registered voters, to wear personal name badges, or to have their identities and pay published in individual reports.
- Lopez v. Monterey County (1999)
  > Held (Thomas for the Court, 9-0): A covered county must obtain preclearance before administering a voting change that a noncovered State mandates, even when the county has no discretion. Delay does not authorize use of an unprecleared change.
- Citizens for a Constitutional Convention v. Yoshina
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 9-0): A state court's neutral reading of an ambiguous referendum rule does not violate due process merely because officials earlier advised otherwise, and counting blank ballots in the approval denominator compels no speech.
- Department of Commerce v. United States House of Representatives / Clinton v. Glavin
  > Held (Souter for the Court; Glavin 5-4, House 7-2): The Census Act and the Enumeration Clause permit the two proposed statistical methods as part of an actual enumeration used for apportionment. The House lacked standing, and its suit was dismissed.
- Hart v. Gwadosky
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 9-0): The challenge became moot once the initiative qualified for the ballot, and the Maine judgment was vacated for further state proceedings.
- Central State University v. AAUP
  > Held (per curiam, 8-1): Ohio may exclude public-university faculty workloads from collective bargaining to promote classroom teaching; an ordinary occupational classification need only rationally serve a legitimate purpose, without empirical proof.
- Brower v. Washington
  > Held (Kennedy for the Court, 9-0): A legislature may refer a measure on condition that a private beneficiary reimburse election costs; the condition charged no one to vote, gave the beneficiary no vote, and created no right to delay or equalize the campaign.
- Brooks v. Barnes
  > Held (Souter for the Court; 6-3 on §2, 9-0 on the Constitution): A §2 challenge to a primary runoff requirement turns on equal opportunity in the nomination process, not proof that minority-preferred nominees would win in November. The constitutional claim failed on supported findings of no discriminatory purpose.
- Saenz v. Roe
  > Held (Kennedy for the Court, 8-1): A citizen who becomes a bona fide resident must receive equal ordinary welfare benefits; California's first-year cap at the former State's level violates the Fourteenth Amendment's Citizenship and Privileges or Immunities Clauses, and Congress cannot authorize it.
- Hunt v. Cromartie
  > Held (Ginsburg for the Court; 9-0 on vacatur, 5-4 on the rule): A districting injunction cannot rest solely on a finding that race predominated; plaintiffs must prove a separately cognizable denial of equal participation or treatment. A genuine race-versus-politics dispute cannot be resolved on summary judgment when motive is material.
- Maine Green Party v. Maine
  > Held (no opinion of the Court, 5-4): The rejection of the party's facial challenge to Maine's presidential-vote condition was affirmed without a majority rationale. Five Justices agreed that sharpening a legal organizational-autonomy argument does not forfeit it.
- Valdespino v. Alamo Heights Independent School District
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 5-4): The first Gingles condition can be met by a district majority or by rigorous proof that a lawful, compact district would let a cohesive group with dependable crossover support elect its candidates. Citizen voting-age shares below 50 percent neither prove nor foreclose that.
- City of Dallas v. Dallas Fire Fighters Association
  > Held (Kennedy for the Court; 5-4 on race, 9-0 on sex): Municipal racial promotion preferences must meet Adarand's common standard, and sex preferences intermediate scrutiny. Dallas's racial preferences lacked a connected justification and fit, and its sex preferences lacked a supported sex-specific justification.

OT1999
- Texas v. Lesage
  > Held (per curiam, 9-0): Once an unconstitutional admissions process is shown, the school can defeat relief for the rejection itself by proving a constitutional process would have produced the same decision. That does not erase the separate unequal-process injury, which may support nominal or declaratory relief.
- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Slater
  > Held (per curiam, 9-0): Adarand's later state certification did not moot its challenge, because the government had not shown that the challenged disadvantage could not reasonably recur.
- Gutierrez v. Ada
  > Held (Souter for the Court, 9-0): Under the Guam Organic Act, a gubernatorial majority is measured against votes cast for Governor and Lieutenant Governor; ballots expressing no choice in that contest are excluded, so no runoff was required.
- Rayphand v. Sablan / Torres v. Sablan
  > Held (Kennedy for the Court, 8-1): Congress's expressly protected settlement for the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands may keep one legislative chamber with equal representation for each municipality alongside a population-based chamber, although an ordinary State legislature could not.
- Fraternal Order of Police v. United States (2000)
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 9-0): The ban on firearm possession by persons convicted of domestic-violence misdemeanors is within the commerce power as part of the valid supply regime, and applying it to state and local officers does not commandeer the States.
- Reno v. Bossier Parish School Board (Bossier II)
  > Held (Breyer for the Court, 5-4): Section 5 bars a change adopted with an unconstitutional purpose to dilute minority votes even without intended retrogression, and the Board had not shown the absence of that purpose, so preclearance of its 1992 plan was denied. The controversy remained live.
- Rice v. Cayetano
  > Held (Thomas for the Court, 7-2): A State may not restrict the electorate for a public state office by racial ancestry; Hawaii's ancestry qualification for voting for Office of Hawaiian Affairs trustees violates the Fifteenth Amendment.
- Village of Willowbrook v. Olech
  > Held (Stevens for the Court, 9-0): An individual states an equal-protection claim by alleging intentional treatment different from similarly situated persons without a rational basis; membership in a group is unnecessary, and ill will is not an element.
- Powell v. Ridge
  > Held (Thomas for the Court, 5-4): Title VI implies no private action to enforce a §602 regulation's disparate-impact prohibition, and the regulation creates no individual right enforceable under §1983.
- Reform Party of Allegheny County v. Allegheny County Department of Elections
  > Held (Breyer for the Court; 9-0 on the rule, 5-4 on relief): Where Pennsylvania lets major parties cross-nominate for certain offices, it may not reject a qualifying minor party's pre-primary nomination solely because the candidate also filed for or won major-party primaries, without a sufficient justification for the unequal treatment. Relief must be confined to that violation.
- Eisenberg v. Montgomery County Public Schools
  > Held (Stone for the Court, 7-2): A school's racial transfer classification must meet Adarand's common standard, and six Justices held that the supported educational benefits of K-12 integration and of avoiding serious racial isolation can be an important objective. The policy's validity was left for remand.
- Mixon v. Ohio
  > Held (Souter for the Court; six on §2 coverage, five for reversal): Section 2 can reach a direct replacement of an existing election by appointment, with the former system as the comparison. Residents outside Cleveland have no right to vote for its mayor because he appoints their school board (9-0).
- California Democratic Party v. Jones
  > Held (O'Connor for the Court, 7-2): California's blanket primary, which forced objecting parties to give nonmembers an equal role in choosing their official nominees, severely burdens association and is not justified by the State's asserted interests.

## 3. The doctrine as it stands

Each area states the rule as of June 29, 2000, how a court applies it step by step, and which landmarks are waiting on it.

### 3.0 A plain-language primer: the terms these questions use

This primer explains general terms in ordinary words so the questions in section 4 can be answered by someone who is not a lawyer. It describes how the concepts work in general and, where noted, in this Court. What this Court has actually held is in 3.1 to 3.10.

Drawing districts
- Districts and redistricting. Most legislatures and councils are elected from districts. After each census, district lines are redrawn so that each district holds roughly the same number of people.
- At-large election. Everyone in a city or county votes for every seat. If a majority group votes as a bloc, it can win every seat even when a sizable minority votes the other way.
- Packing and cracking. The two ways to draw lines that weaken a group's votes. Packing puts far more of the group's voters into a few districts than they need to win there, so their extra votes are wasted. Cracking splits the group across many districts so that it is outnumbered in all of them.
- Majority-minority, opportunity, crossover and coalition districts. A majority-minority district is one where a racial or language minority makes up most of the voters. An opportunity district is one where the minority group can realistically elect its preferred candidate. A crossover district elects the group's candidate with help from some majority voters. A coalition district joins two minority groups, for example Black and Latino voters, who vote together (question D11).

The injuries
- Vote dilution. Every voter casts a ballot and every ballot is counted, but the lines or the election system are arranged so that a group's votes carry less weight than they would under a fair arrangement. Example: a county that is 35 percent Black elects five commissioners at large. White voters, who vote as a bloc, win all five seats in every election, although fair single-member districts would give Black voters a real chance in one or two. Nobody was turned away from the polls; the system turned their votes into fewer wins. That is dilution. It can be challenged as intentional (the Constitution, which requires proof of purpose) or under §2 of the Voting Rights Act (which looks at results).
- Racial gerrymander (the Shaw claim). A different injury: the State sorts voters into districts mainly by race, whether or not anyone's voting strength is reduced. Historically the Court required strict scrutiny when race was the predominant reason for a district's lines (Shaw v. Reno, Miller v. Johnson). This Court's treatment, including its standing rule, is in 3.1 and 3.9.
- The four districting injuries Stone is building (3.9, planning, not law): racial exclusion (keeping people from voting or from the process because of race); intentional vote dilution (dilution done on purpose); materially unequal participation (rules or equipment that make it materially harder for one group's votes to be cast or counted, such as voting machines that discard more ballots in minority neighborhoods, question D6); and legally inferior civic status (government marking some citizens as lesser members of the political community, such as limiting who may run for office by ancestry, question D8).
- Disenfranchisement. Taking away the vote, for example from people with felony convictions. A disenfranchisement law adopted to exclude Black voters is unconstitutional (Hunter v. Underwood, 1985); question D9 asks whether reenacting such a law later removes the taint.

The Voting Rights Act
- Section 2. Applies nationwide and forbids voting rules and districts that result in minority voters having less opportunity to participate and elect candidates of their choice. Proof of intent is not required. A claim normally starts with the three Gingles conditions (1986): the minority group is large and compact enough to form a majority in a reasonable district; it votes cohesively; and the majority usually votes as a bloc to defeat its preferred candidates. The court then looks at the "totality of circumstances," such as history, campaign appeals to race and how often minority candidates win.
- Section 5 and preclearance. Certain jurisdictions with a history of discrimination (chosen by the coverage formula in §4(b)) had to get federal approval before changing any voting rule. Approval was denied if the change was retrogressive (left minority voters worse off than before) or, depending on the period, adopted with a discriminatory purpose. Shelby County (OT2012) is the landmark on the coverage formula.

Equal population
- One person, one vote. Districts must hold roughly equal numbers of people so that each vote counts about the same (Reynolds v. Sims, 1964). State and local plans may vary somewhat; congressional districts must be nearly exact.
- Total population versus citizen voting-age population (CVAP). Districts are usually equalized by counting everyone, including children and noncitizens. Some argue they should be equalized by counting eligible voters only. Where noncitizens are concentrated, the two counts give very different maps (Chen, questions D12 and D16; Evenwel, OT2015).
- Partisan gerrymandering. Drawing lines to help one party. Whether courts can decide such claims is the Vieth (OT2003) and Rucho (OT2018) question.

Standards of review
- Strict scrutiny: the government must show a compelling interest and that the measure is narrowly tailored to it. Historically applied to racial classifications.
- Intermediate scrutiny: an important objective and a substantial relationship to it; for sex, an "exceedingly persuasive justification" (United States v. Virginia). Applied to sex classifications.
- Rational basis: a legitimate purpose and a rational connection. Most laws pass, though the Court has struck laws resting on bare hostility (Romer v. Evans).
- This Court's common standard for racial classifications (Adarand here): government must show an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective, justify the measure's coverage and actual burdens, and answer concrete, practicable alternatives that would work about as well with less racial disadvantage. It is one standard for all racial classifications, not strict scrutiny (3.5).

Purpose and impact
- Intent versus effect. The Equal Protection Clause is violated only by discriminatory purpose; a law's disproportionate effect alone is not enough (Washington v. Davis, 1976). Purpose can be proved by circumstances: the size of the impact, the history, the sequence of events, departures from normal procedure and what officials said (Arlington Heights, 1977).
- Disparate impact. Some statutes and regulations forbid practices with unjustified unequal effects even without intent, for example the Title VI regulations at issue in Sandoval.

Enforcement
- Title VI. Section 601 forbids intentional discrimination by programs receiving federal money; §602 lets agencies write regulations, which have forbidden disparate impact.
- Private right of action. Whether an individual may sue to enforce a statute or regulation, as opposed to only the government enforcing it. This Court answered it for §602 regulations in Powell v. Ridge (OT1999): no private action and no §1983 right (question P1).
- Section 1983. The federal law that lets people sue state and local officials for violating federal rights. Whether a spending statute creates rights enforceable this way is the Gonzaga question (P2).
- Congress's enforcement power. Section 5 of the Fourteenth Amendment and §2 of the Fifteenth let Congress enforce those amendments. Historically, legislation reaching beyond what courts would find unconstitutional must be "congruent and proportional" to real violations (City of Boerne); this Court's version is in the commerce tracker's section 3. Whether the Fifteenth Amendment follows the same test is question D15.
- State action. The Constitution limits government, not private parties. A nominally private body counts as government when it is deeply entwined with it (Brentwood, OT2000).
- Political-process doctrine. A law that moves decisions affecting a minority to a harder level of government (for example from a school board to a statewide vote) can violate equal protection (Hunter v. Erickson; Washington v. Seattle School District). Question S3 asks whether the idea extends to sex.

### 3.1 Territorial districting

Doctrine now: deliberate or predominant use of race in drawing a shared territorial district is not, by itself, an equal-protection injury, a trigger for strict scrutiny, or a basis for an injunction (Shaw, Miller, Vera, Hunt). A State may consider racial conditions to improve electoral opportunity even where federal law does not require the district (Miller Pt IV, nine votes on that limited point). Racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation and legally inferior civic status remain actionable under their own elements, and a status or participation injury can be complete when government imposes it, without electoral or financial loss (Miller Pt III). Voters personally subjected to the challenged assignment have standing; mere residence elsewhere is not enough (Miller Pt I-A, Vera). When motive is material to a cognizable claim, a genuine race-versus-politics dispute goes to trial (Hunt Pt I).

How it operates:
1. Identify the claimant's specific injury. If the only complaint is the district's racial composition, the claim fails on the merits.
2. Confirm standing: personal exposure to the challenged assignment.
3. Apply the elements of the injury claimed. These have not been defined; Section 4.1 defines them.
4. Shape, demographics and statements are evidence for a cognizable claim, never a substitute for one.
5. Relief follows the injury proved; no redrawing for racial balance.

Reserved for: Easley (OT2000); LULAC (OT2005); Alabama Legislative Black Caucus (OT2014, as intentional packing); Abbott v. Perez (OT2017); Alexander (OT2023, Question 6); Callais (OT2025).

### 3.2 VRA §2

Doctrine now: §2 is a results statute; intent is not required. The Gingles conditions are necessary but not sufficient, followed by the totality of circumstances (De Grandy). Proportionality is evidence, not a safe harbor, and there is no duty to maximize. The first Gingles condition can be met by a majority district or by rigorous proof that a lawful, compact district would let a cohesive group with dependable crossover support elect its candidates (Valdespino). A challenge to governing-body size needs a reasonable, workable comparator rooted in law and practice (Holder). Judicial elections are covered, with state linkage interests weighed (SCLC). §2 reaches the direct replacement of an existing election by appointment (Mixon). A State need not prove remedial necessity before drawing majority-minority districts (Voinovich), and federal courts defer to timely state redistricting (Growe).

How it operates:
1. Gingles 1: a majority district, or a functional crossover district proved rigorously.
2. Gingles 2 and 3: cohesion and bloc voting that usually defeats the group's candidates.
3. Totality of circumstances, with proportionality as one factor.
4. A remedy that is a lawful plan, with the State given the first chance to draw it.

Reserved for: LULAC (OT2005); Bartlett (OT2008, pre-decided); Brnovich (OT2020, vote denial); Allen v. Milligan (OT2022).

### 3.3 VRA §5

Doctrine now: covered jurisdictions must preclear changes, including changes a noncovered State compels (Lopez I and II), discretionary changes made in implementing the NVRA (Young), county poll-official selection methods (Foreman), and a party convention fee (Morse, with a private §10 action). The effect test is retrogression (Bossier I). The purpose test bars a dilutive purpose even without intended retrogression (Bossier I Pt III; Bossier II). A §2 violation is not an independent bar to clearance. Reallocation of governing authority with no direct relation to voting is not covered (Presley).

How it operates:
1. Is the jurisdiction covered, and is this a change affecting voting?
2. Effect: does the change make the group worse off than the benchmark?
3. Purpose: was it adopted with a dilutive purpose, whether or not retrogression was intended?
4. Without preclearance, the change cannot be implemented, and courts may not give it a route around §5.

Reserved for: Georgia v. Ashcroft (OT2002); NAMUDNO (OT2008); Shelby County (OT2012).

### 3.4 Franchise, counting and representation

Doctrine now: ancestry cannot define the electorate for a public state office (Rice, Fifteenth Amendment). Election regulations are reviewed under the calibrated burden framework (Burdick, Timmons, Norman). The census may count federal personnel overseas who have enduring residence ties (Franklin); the Census Act and the Enumeration Clause permit the sampling methods at issue in Commerce v. House. The Elections Clause supports the NVRA's registration duties (Voting Rights Coalition). States may not add congressional qualifications through ballot rules (Term Limits). No controlling law yet addresses equal treatment of ballots in recounts, or Article II and state-court construction of election law; Bush v. Gore is first impression.

How it operates:
1. Identify the interest burdened and the severity of the burden.
2. Severe or discriminatory burdens need strong justification; reasonable nondiscriminatory rules get latitude.
3. Racial denial of the franchise is categorically barred, not balanced.

Reserved for: Bush v. Palm Beach and Bush v. Gore (OT2000); Vieth (OT2003); Crawford (OT2007); Evenwel (OT2015, pre-decided through Chen); Rucho (OT2018); Moore v. Harper (OT2022).

### 3.5 Governmental racial classifications

Doctrine now: Adarand's common substantial-justification standard governs federal, state and local government. Government must show an important objective within its lawful authority, a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective, and justification of the measure's coverage and burdens, including a response to concrete, practicable alternatives. Supported correction, prevention of recurrence, and overcoming barriers to market participation are permissible objectives. Racial hierarchy, stereotype, hostility, political favoritism and racial balancing for its own sake are not. It has been applied to admissions (Hopwood: separate tracks fail; supported diversity can qualify, six votes), contracting (Engineering Contractors: race-neutral alternatives must be seriously considered), promotions (Dallas: no connected justification and no fit), and K-12 integration (Eisenberg: a supported integration objective can qualify, six votes). A selective transfer of racial-remedy authority to a more remote political process triggers the political-process inquiry (Coalition v. Wilson). Unequal opportunity to compete is itself an injury, though proof of the same decision defeats a claim to the seat or compensation (Lesage). Desegregation remedies track the adjudicated violation, and release requires good-faith compliance and elimination of vestiges to the extent practicable (Freeman, Jenkins, Fordice).

How it operates:
1. Is there actual differential racial treatment of the claimant?
2. Government names its objective. Is it within its lawful authority and permissible?
3. Does evidence connect the racial treatment to that objective substantially?
4. Are the coverage, the burden on others, and the duration justified, with a real answer to practical alternatives?
5. If any step fails, the provision or application fails, and the remedy is confined to it.

Reserved for: Grutter and Gratz (OT2002); Johnson v. California (OT2004); Parents Involved (OT2006, method only); Ricci (OT2008); Fisher I (OT2012); Schuette (OT2013); Fisher II (OT2015); SFFA (OT2022).

### 3.6 Sex

Doctrine now: government must show an actual important objective and a substantial relationship. "Exceedingly persuasive justification" describes that burden; it is not strict scrutiny (VMI, J.E.B., Kelley, Klinger, Cohen, Nabozny, Dallas Pt I). A separate program must give substantially equal opportunity (VMI). Comparison is program-specific (Klinger). Sex-conscious restructuring needs an actual objective, not ratios or budgets alone (Kelley). A sex-conscious remedy needs its own sex-specific record (Dallas; Engineering Contractors' women's program failed). Sex-based citizenship-transmission rules have no controlling rule; Miller v. Albright fractured.

How it operates:
1. Is there a sex classification, express or proven by purpose?
2. Government shows the actual objective and why it is important.
3. Government shows the classification substantially serves it, without stereotypes or overbroad generalizations.

Reserved for: Nguyen (OT2000, being decided); Morales-Santana (OT2016, pre-decided through Nguyen); Skrmetti (OT2024) and Hecox and B.P.J. (OT2025), as far as they are sex questions.

### 3.7 Sexual orientation and gender identity

Doctrine now: orientation classifications receive ordinary rational-basis review tied to the actual disadvantage; no heightened tier (Romer, Equality Foundation, Nabozny, Stemler, Shahar Pt III). The military discharge cases sustained genuinely rebuttable conduct-related inferences (Walmer, Thomasson, Richenberg, each 5-4). Stone's intermediate-scrutiny view is a stated minority position (Walmer, Shahar). Gender identity has no case; Kelley expressly left it open.

How it operates:
1. Identify the actual disadvantage imposed.
2. Is there a legitimate purpose rationally related to that disadvantage? Animus and bare hostility fail (Romer).

Reserved for: Lawrence (OT2002); Windsor (OT2012); Obergefell (OT2014); Bostock (OT2019); Skrmetti (OT2024); Hecox and B.P.J. (OT2025).

### 3.8 Other classifications

Doctrine now: disability receives rational basis tied to the actual disadvantage, with heightened scrutiny not reached (Heller v. Doe). Economic classifications need any reasonably conceivable basis, which the challenger must negate (Beach, Nordlinger, Burlington Northern). A class-of-one claim needs intentional differential treatment without a rational basis; ill will is not an element (Olech). New residents receive equal treatment under the Citizenship and Privileges or Immunities Clauses, and Congress cannot authorize otherwise (Saenz). Wealth is not a suspect class, but the first appeal from a permanent parental termination cannot be conditioned on payment (M.L.B.). Discovery on a racial selective-prosecution claim requires some credible evidence of discriminatory effect and purpose, including a reliable basis to infer that similarly situated persons of another race could have been prosecuted but were not; less than a prima facie case, with no fixed checklist (Armstrong). Jury strikes may not rest on race or sex (McCollum, J.E.B.), and a white defendant may challenge discrimination against Black grand jurors (Campbell).

Reserved for: no listed landmark through OT2025.

### 3.9 The four districting injuries: candidate elements (planning, not law)

Workbook tag for the whole subsection: cleanup. The Shaw change left these four injuries as the remaining routes, and no historical case defines them, because historically the Shaw claim made them unnecessary. They need elements before the landmarks in 3.1 arrive. Everything below is a candidate answer for the user to accept, change or reject; none of it binds another Justice.

The problem. Every districting decision since 1993 says predominant racial design is not an injury, but that racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership and other independently cognizable deprivations remain actionable and keep their own elements. Miller adds that a status or participation injury can be complete when government itself imposes unequal racial treatment, without electoral loss, and that a discriminatory participation or constituent-service practice requires its own examination. The Court relies on Gomillion, Anderson v. Martin, White v. Regester and McLaurin to keep these injuries alive, but has never stated their elements, and Hunt left them open. Without elements, lower courts have a rejection rule and no replacement; plaintiffs who lost the Shaw claim will relabel it, with no principled way to sort a real claim from a relabeled one; and the historical racial-gerrymandering landmarks (Alabama Legislative Black Caucus, OT2014; Bethune-Hill and Cooper v. Harris, OT2016; Alexander, OT2023) can return here only as one of these four claims.

Four design choices run through all four injuries:
1. Purpose. Each constitutional claim requires that race caused the treatment, through an express racial rule, ancestry used as race, or proven purpose. Effect alone belongs to VRA §2; otherwise these claims become a judge-made results test that duplicates §2 without its statutory limits.
2. Comparator. Each injury needs a baseline that is not a racial target: exclusion compares eligibility; dilution compares a lawful alternative plan (Holder); participation compares other voters in the same election; status compares equal official treatment.
3. Remedy follows the injury. Only dilution can justify redrawing lines, and then only to a lawful plan, not a racial percentage.
4. Standing follows the injury. Whoever personally bears the specific injury claimed may sue.

#### 3.9.1 Racial exclusion

Plain terms: government shuts people out of voting, or out of the political community, because of race.

Anchors: Gomillion v. Lightfoot (1960), where Alabama redrew Tuskegee's limits into a 28-sided figure that removed nearly every Black resident from the city and its elections; the white primary cases (Smith v. Allwright, 1944; Terry v. Adams, 1953); Hunter v. Underwood (1985), a disenfranchisement law adopted to exclude Black voters; Rice v. Cayetano (2000), which Stone joined.

Candidate elements:
1. A governmental act that removes or withholds eligibility to vote, to have a vote counted, or to belong to the political unit.
2. On account of race, by express rule, ancestry used as race, or proven purpose.
3. The claimant is among those excluded.
4. Remedy: restore eligibility or membership.

Test question: is anyone made ineligible? Moving a voter from one district to another inside the same unit is not exclusion; changing who belongs to the unit can be (Gomillion).

#### 3.9.2 Intentional vote dilution

Plain terms: everyone can vote, but the system is built on purpose so one racial group's votes count for less. The methods are cracking (spreading the group thinly so it is outvoted everywhere) and packing (concentrating it far beyond what it needs to win, wasting its surplus and weakening it elsewhere).

Anchors: White v. Regester (1973) and Rogers v. Lodge (1982) found unconstitutional dilution; Mobile v. Bolden (1980, plurality) required purpose, which is why Congress changed §2 to a results test in 1982. In this timeline Voinovich held a dilution-intent finding clearly erroneous, and Holder left the constitutional claim undecided.

Candidate elements:
1. A districting plan or election structure.
2. Actual dilutive effect: the group's ability to elect is materially reduced compared with a lawful, workable alternative rooted in the State's own criteria, proved with the same kind of evidence §2 uses (cohesion, bloc voting, an alternative plan).
3. Purpose: the structure was adopted or kept in material part because it would produce that effect.
4. Remedy: a lawful plan removing the dilution, with no racial floor or proportional result.

The application to think about now is packing defended as VRA compliance. Suppose a State draws a district at 60% Black voting-age population when the evidence shows 45% would reliably elect the community's candidates, and the surplus came from a neighboring district where those voters had real influence. If the purpose was to drain that neighboring influence, that is intentional dilution, and the compliance label does not save it. Master Section 9 already says statutory implementation cannot authorize "intentional destruction of a protected electoral opportunity." This is how the Bethune-Hill and Cooper fact patterns could return: not as "race predominated" but as "race was used to pack us so we would count for less." Those cases' historical central question (strict scrutiny of predominance) no longer exists here, so closing this point is cleanup, not pre-decision.

Choice for the user: whether unnecessary packing alone supports an inference of purpose, or is one factor among the ordinary purpose evidence. Candidate answer: strong circumstantial evidence, not an automatic finding (master Section 8: impact is evidence, not conversion).

#### 3.9.3 Materially unequal participation

Plain terms: you can vote and your vote counts at full weight, but government makes taking part harder or worse for your racial group: fewer polling places, shorter hours, stricter registration windows, ballots handled under different rules, your district's election skipped, or your representative's services closed to your neighborhood.

Anchors: Lane v. Wilson (1939), where Oklahoma gave Black citizens it had excluded through its grandfather clause a 12-day window to register or lose the vote permanently, and the Court struck it down because the Constitution "nullifies sophisticated as well as simple-minded modes of discrimination"; McLaurin v. Oklahoma State Regents (1950), where a Black graduate student was admitted but made to sit apart; White v. Regester, preserved in Miller for participation beyond formal eligibility; Miller's own reservation of "a discriminatory participation or constituent-service practice."

Candidate elements:
1. A governmental rule or practice governing how citizens register, vote, have ballots counted, receive election notice, or reach their elected officials.
2. Unequal terms: the group participates on materially worse terms than similarly situated voters in the same jurisdiction and election, where "materially" means the difference changes practical ability to participate (time, distance, risk of rejection, loss of a scheduled election, denial of a service).
3. On account of race, by express rule, assignment by race to the worse terms, or proven purpose, with disparity counting as evidence but not proof.
4. Remedy: equalize the terms, not redraw the district.

Test question: remove the racial composition of the map from the story. Is this voter still treated worse than other voters in the act of participating? If the only complaint left is who else lives in the district, it is the rejected Shaw claim.

What it looks like in districting:
- A plan renumbers districts so predominantly Black precincts land where the senate seat is not up for four years, delaying their vote, and the record shows the renumbering was chosen for that reason. Neutral delays from staggered terms are ordinarily lawful (Pate v. El Paso County, 1970, summary affirmance); a racially targeted delay is unequal participation.
- A county uses a new line to send Black-majority precincts to one distant consolidated polling place while other precincts keep neighborhood polls.
- An officeholder limits constituent offices, meetings or casework to parts of a district chosen by race. This is Miller's reserved "constituent-service practice."

Outside districting: targeted precinct closures (master Section 8's example), a registration window open only when one group can use it (Lane), or ballots counted under different rules in different racial areas.

The link to Bush v. Gore (OT2000), which asks whether materially equivalent ballots must get substantially equal treatment and is decided in its own case: if Stone's rule there is the build's (equivalent ballots, a consistent nonarbitrary method), unequal participation can be defined as the racial branch of the same principle: the same comparison, plus racial causation, plus the racial-equality consequences. One theory of equal participation, not two inventions.

Choice for the user: whether element 3 accepts racial assignment to worse terms without separate proof of purpose. Candidate answer: yes; sorting people by race into a worse procedure is itself racial treatment. That is consistent with Miller, because the shared district there imposed worse terms on no one.

#### 3.9.4 Legally inferior civic status

Plain terms: government officially marks a racial group as second-class members of the political community, even if no one loses a vote.

Anchors: Anderson v. Martin (1964), where Louisiana printed each candidate's race on the ballot, and the Court struck it down although the label applied to every race and no one was stopped from voting; Hamm v. Virginia State Board of Elections (E.D. Va. 1964), summarily affirmed as Tancil v. Woolls (1964), striking down voter and tax lists kept separately by race; Strauder v. West Virginia (1880), calling exclusion of Black men from juries "a brand upon them"; Romer v. Evans (1996, Stone joined), the nonracial cousin.

Candidate elements:
1. An official act with legal force or official designation (statute, ballot, voter roll, official record, eligibility rule, office qualification).
2. It classifies or marks citizens by race in their civic capacity (voter, candidate, juror, officeholder, member of the polity), or assigns them lesser legal standing in that capacity.
3. No electoral or financial loss is needed, and applying the label to every race does not cure it (Anderson).
4. Remedy: remove the designation.

The line Stone drew in Miller: the injury must come from what government officially does, not from a message someone reads into a map's demographics. A district that happens to be 55% Black is not a designation. A statute that names it "the Black district," reserves its seat for a Black candidate, or tells its representative to serve only Black constituents is.

The gray zone is official statements, such as a committee report calling a seat "the African-American district." Candidate answer: only designations with legal operation count as the status injury; statements are evidence of purpose for the other three claims. Counting statements as status injuries would revive much of the Shaw claim through legislative history.

Related docket item: Cook v. Gralike (OT2000) concerns Missouri's ballot labels for congressional candidates who would not support term limits. It is not racial, but it uses Anderson's mechanism, so how Stone treats official ballot labels there shapes this claim (question F2).

#### 3.9.5 Summary

- Exclusion. Plain question: is anyone made ineligible because of race? Racial causation shown by an express rule or proven purpose. Comparator: the eligibility of others. Remedy: restore eligibility.
- Intentional dilution. Plain question: was the plan built to make the group's votes count for less? Racial causation shown by proven purpose. Comparator: a lawful alternative plan. Remedy: a lawful plan.
- Unequal participation. Plain question: is the group made to participate on worse terms? Racial causation shown by an express rule, racial assignment, or proven purpose. Comparator: other voters in the same election. Remedy: equalize the terms.
- Inferior civic status. Plain question: does government officially mark the group as lesser? Racial causation shown by the official classification itself. Comparator: equal official treatment. Remedy: remove the designation.

## 5. Docket decisions and carried-forward plans

### (a) Post-Shaw docket instructions still in force

The retired build's post-Shaw table has been carried out or overtaken. Hays, Shaw v. Hunt / Pope and Abrams were deleted. Miller (OT1994) and Vera (OT1995) were reconstructed as counterfactual grants and decided. The instruction to delete Hunt v. Cromartie was superseded when the Court decided Hunt in OT1998, and the instruction to delete Easley v. Cromartie was superseded by the OT2000 list, where Easley needs an Engine §14 reconstruction premise (question D13). What remains live:
1. The causal rule. A changed precedent removes a later historical case only when the later cause of action, standing theory, judgment, remedy or procedural posture materially depended on the rule that changed. A famous historical caption is not preserved merely because it would be useful. A reconstructed grant is used only when a genuine surviving legal question can be grounded in the historical dispute and is marked openly as counterfactual.
2. The independent voting-rights line stays on its own schedule. Later statutory or constitutional voting cases are not deleted merely because Shaw changed; their own statutes, constitutional claims, records and procedural histories control.
3. Each retained case supplies the historical issue, Stone's proposed disposition of the judgment that actually exists in this simulation, and his reasoning; reconstructed grants are expressly identified. A remand states what remains to be proved and does not manufacture favorable findings. A later case whose record an earlier simulated judgment has altered must be revalidated before it is run.
4. A "simulation vehicle" is an actual lower-court case assigned a hypothetical Supreme Court hearing. Its OT placement follows the simulation slot, while its actual reporter citation and decision year stay separately identified; register aliases are not historical Supreme Court grants.
5. Stone can take these positions whether he writes for the Court, concurs or dissents; which description applies depends on the votes actually cast.
