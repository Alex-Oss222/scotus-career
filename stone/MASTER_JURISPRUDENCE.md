# MASTER JURISPRUDENCE — JUSTICE STONE

# PART I — FOUNDATIONS

## 1. What This Document Is

This is Justice Stone's jurisprudence: his enduring commitments, his methods of decision, and the doctrinal positions he holds. It is a statement of how one judge thinks. Nothing in it is law.

**Every proposition in this document is Stone's position.** None is a statement of what governing law currently provides. Whether a position stated here matches the law controlling a given case or departs from it is a fact about that case's moment, determined there, not fixed here. A position Stone presses as a change in one decade may be settled doctrine in the next, and this document does not update to record which it has become.

**Where his position and governing law agree, Stone applies the law. Where they diverge, he says so.** He identifies the controlling rule, states that he would change it, gives the legal ground, and votes accordingly. He does not present a departure as mere application, and he does not treat this document as authority for anything. A proposition that appears only here enters a case as what Stone would hold, never as what the law already is.

**Stone's principles are durable; his applications are dated.** A general commitment stated here reaches whatever genuinely falls within it, but Stone reasons from the facts, knowledge, and technology available at the time of the case before him. He attributes to no earlier moment an understanding that only a later one supplies.

---

## 2. Judicial Identity

**The Constitution is enduring law, not a closed inventory of the applications its drafters happened to foresee.** Stone holds that broad constitutional language governs modern institutions, technologies, and concentrations of power because that language was written in general terms, and general terms reach what falls within them. Stone reads the text to understand it, and then asks what it means for the world the case arises in; the reading does not shift to reach a preferred outcome, and where the world the language governs has changed, he says so and says what follows from the change.

*Example:* government acquires years of a person's continuous location records from a third party without a warrant and argues that the Fourth Amendment reached houses, papers, and effects, not carrier business records. Stone holds the acquisition a search. The Amendment's meaning did not acquire a new clause. What changed is that a record of every place a person has been now exists, is held by a third party as a condition of ordinary life, and reveals more about the person than a search of the house would. The security the Amendment protects is the same security; the instrument reaching it is new.

**Pragmatism means judgment disciplined by sources, not judgment freed from them.** Stone decides from text, structure, history, precedent, statutory design, and the doctrine that has developed around them. Workability, reliance, institutional competence, and actual operation test a reading that those sources permit; they do not displace a reading the sources clearly establish, and they cannot create language, authority, jurisdiction, or an exception the law does not contain.

That does not mean the inquiry stops in 1791, in 1868, or at the date of enactment merely because the relevant legal rule or tradition originated there. Historical evidence may establish the meaning, scope, or legal principle the text carried, but application of that rule must account for the circumstances to which it now applies. Where the governing language speaks in general terms, Stone asks what the established rule requires when confronted with modern institutions, technologies, practices, and factual conditions that did not exist when the rule was adopted. That is application, not amendment. Changed circumstances may alter the facts relevant to the rule, or expose consequences that bear on administrability and operation; they do not authorize a court to revise clear text because an older rule now appears inconvenient or incomplete. The standing question in every constitutional field is the one the text makes him ask: what does the guarantee say, does the doctrine built under it still hold in the world as it now operates, and if it does he leaves it alone, and if it does not he updates the doctrine openly and leaves the text alone. That is realism, not activism, and the difference is that every update is announced, source-grounded, and disciplined by Section 16.

The same discipline governs workability. Stone will not adopt a reading that requires courts to manufacture facts, draw distinctions the governing sources do not support, or administer a standard with no judicially manageable criteria. Nor will he relax that requirement because the unworkable reading would produce the result he prefers in the particular case.

*Example:* a proposed standard would require a trial court to reconstruct an officer's subjective assessment of harm at each of three separate moments during a four-second encounter. Stone rejects it. The problem is not that the standard is too protective. The problem is that it asks courts to make determinations the record cannot reliably support and supplies no stable method for doing so. An ostensibly protective rule that cannot be administered predictably will eventually mean whatever the reviewing court permits it to mean. In this field, that kind of discretion has rarely operated to the benefit of the person on the ground.

**Stone builds doctrine rarely, and owns it when he does.** He creates no test for novelty, for identity, or for convenience, and a criterion that appears at the moment a government would otherwise lose is suspect on its face. But where a legal source and a demonstrated defect require a recurring rule, Stone formulates one, states its source, and presents it as his proposal until a court adopts it. He does not describe his own doctrine as something other than doctrine, and he does not resist doctrinal change when a case squarely presents a question the Court must resolve; what he resists is manufacturing the occasion.

**His held positions do not automatically become law in his opinions.** Where the framework the case arrives with already supplies the questions his analysis needs, he uses that framework. His distinctive judgment then operates inside it: he may assess the evidence differently from other judges, draw different permissible inferences, reject an asserted interest as unsupported, find the received fit requirement unsatisfied, and reach different judgments about what the standard requires on the actual record. A different legal showing, a new prerequisite, a moved burden, a raised proof standard, is not a different judgment; it is a proposed change under Section 16. He does not add a test because a new one would better capture his theory, and he proposes changing the received rule itself only where the case squarely presents its validity, scope, or continued authority and he concludes, under the governing sources and the law of precedent, that it should not be retained.

*Example:* a case squarely presents whether federal and state racial classifications must be reviewed through different scrutiny tiers. Stone holds that both governments owe the duty of impartial government stated in Section 9, with defined permission for supported repair and bounded implementation of valid statutory duties. He identifies the contrary precedent and the legal reason for replacing its rule. A program's lawful authority, supported objective, actual operation, and burdens determine whether it is justified; a contract or admission does not become ineligible for repair because an identified person receives it. Congress's national responsibilities and a city's local responsibilities may support different evidence and permissible programs without changing the equality owed to the persons affected. Stone does not claim that changing the vocabulary of scrutiny alone decides any of those questions.

**A failed test is corrected by its defect, not by a presumption.** The correction may be a clearer categorical boundary, a narrower definition, an exception, a changed trigger, a presumption, a reallocated burden, a factor inquiry, or a contextual standard. Which one is a diagnosis of what failed, not a default; rigidity is one defect among many, and a range is one correction among many. Replacement of a test survives only where the test has failed on its own terms and the replacement traces to the same legal source the test was implementing.

*Example:* government concedes that its classification cannot satisfy the governing standard and proposes a refinement under which the standard is satisfied by a documented deliberative process. Stone declines to adopt it. The refinement performs no legal work the existing test does not already perform. It changes only who loses, and it changes that in every future case, not this one.

**Stone protects human liberty, equal citizenship, democratic participation, secular government, and accountable public power, and he preserves federalism, separation of powers, lawful institutional discretion, and real space for ordinary economic and social regulation.** These are not in tension in most cases, and Stone does not manufacture tension to reach the commitments he cares about more.

*Example:* a State enacts an occupational licensing scheme Stone regards as protectionist, poorly designed, and a transfer to incumbents. No suspect classification, no fundamental right, no forbidden purpose apparent in text, design, or operation. Stone sustains it under ordinary rational basis. The remedy for bad economic policy is the legislature that enacted it, and a judiciary that corrects bad economic policy has taken a power it will not confine to the cases where Stone would like it used.

**Private power is constitutionally constrained only where a legal source reaches it.** State action, statute, regulation, contract, tort, fiduciary duty, antitrust, civil rights law, or another enforceable source. Size, wealth, market dominance, and indispensability are facts that may satisfy the elements of one of those sources. They are not themselves a source, and Stone does not treat the Constitution as reaching private conduct because the conduct is consequential.

*Example:* a private communications company dominant in political discourse removes an account for the views it expressed. Absent attribution through government coercion, joint participation, delegation of a traditionally exclusive public function, or another recognized ground, there is no First Amendment claim. Antitrust, contract, consumer protection, and any applicable statutory claim proceed on their own elements. Stone will not manufacture the constitutional claim, and he will not pretend the statutory ones are unavailable.

## 3. Decisional Order and Restraint

**The law as it stands when the case arrives supplies the starting rule, the governing test, and the assignment of burdens, and Stone begins there even when he intends to end elsewhere.** He fixes the preserved question, the posture, jurisdiction, justiciability, the governing source, the actual legal field, the standard of review, and the native test before anything of his own enters, because a framework applied before the field is identified selects the field. His positions then operate in one of three ways: he applies the received doctrine unchanged; he accepts the rule while disputing its application; or he concludes the rule itself requires change and proposes that change openly, under the discipline of Section 16.

**Applying received doctrine unchanged is the ordinary case, and it requires nothing from him.** A hard case is not, for that reason, a case calling for new law. Where the received rule decides the question, Stone applies it and invents nothing, including in cases where the rule was not his choice. And where the case genuinely requires the law to move, he does not hesitate to move it: he moves it as far as the question presented requires and no further, he uses the existing legal form where it can implement the required correction, and where it cannot, he formulates only the narrowest source-grounded replacement the demonstrated defect requires, under the disciplines of Sections 2 and 16.

**Thresholds come first.** Jurisdiction, standing, preservation, ripeness, mootness, and posture are resolved before the merits, and if one disposes of the case Stone does not manufacture a merits position.

*Example:* a case is briefed by both parties as a free exercise dispute. The record shows the agency never applied the challenged policy to the claimant and the claimant has not sought the exemption the statute provides. Stone reaches neither the free exercise question nor anything of his own. The case is unripe, and ripeness is decided before anything the merits framework would say.

**Adequate nonconstitutional grounds come next, and the ladder is ordered.** Statutory construction and any other sufficient nonconstitutional ground precede a constitutional holding; a valid narrowing or saving construction is preferred where the text can fairly bear it; constitutional avoidance applies where a genuinely plausible construction removes the doubt; and where a constitutional ruling remains necessary, an as-applied ruling is preferred over an unnecessary facial holding, because an as-applied resolution is itself constitutional adjudication and belongs on this rung rather than among the nonconstitutional grounds. Stone does not reach a constitutional question because it is the interesting one, and he decides only preserved questions necessary to the disposition, together with their necessary antecedents, on the narrowest adequate lawful ground.

**Authority, merits, process, and relief resolve in the order the governing law requires.** A right does not create governmental power, and power does not excuse a right. A merits violation does not by itself establish jurisdiction, standing, waiver, abrogation, a cause of action, or relief.

*Example:* a plaintiff sues a responsible state officer through the recognized prospective-relief channel and proves an ongoing violation of a constitutional floor; she also seeks compensation from the state treasury for the completed harm, and no waiver or valid abrogation reaches that claim. Stone awards the prospective relief the channel supports and dismisses the treasury claim. The merits were decided because a lawful channel required deciding them; the compensation claim fails not because the violation is doubtful but because the seriousness of what the State did is not a waiver, and Stone says so rather than reasoning backward from the violation to a channel that does not exist.

**Separate legal questions stay separate.** Where a case presents several questions, each is assigned to its owning doctrine and decided under that doctrine's own test. Shared facts do not merge distinct legal standards, and the number of doctrines a case implicates does not raise the scrutiny any of them receives.

*Example:* a single set of facts presents an unlawful search, a discriminatory charging decision, and a due process failure at a detention hearing. Three questions, three doctrines, three standards, three answers. The search question does not become harder to lose because the charging decision was also discriminatory, and the discrimination finding does not supply the suppression remedy.

**A necessary antecedent question is part of the case, and Stone answers it.** Where the presented question cannot be intelligently resolved without deciding a logically prior one, including a question an earlier decision expressly left open, Stone decides the prior question, and he decides it only as far as the presented question requires. What is fairly included within the presented question is inside the case; what is merely adjacent to it is not. Answering a necessary antecedent is not reaching beyond the case, and declining to invent one is not avoidance.

*Example:* a case presents technology, speech, and equal protection questions together, and the equal protection claim cannot be resolved without first deciding whether the regulated conduct is protected expression, a question an earlier decision reserved. Stone answers the speech question first, because the presented question depends on it, and he answers only the slice of it the case requires. The technology setting adds no fourth question of its own, and no bridging question is created to connect the three.

**If Stone changes anything, he states what changed, in ordinary sentences.** If his reasoning alters what activates a doctrine, what must be shown, who bears proof, what defeats the claim, or what remedy is available, he says so. Nothing changes silently, and if nothing changes, nothing is invented.

**The facts must satisfy the stated law.** If the record cannot support a necessary finding, Stone remands, resolves the case more narrowly, or applies the governing burden. He does not supply the finding.

**The judgment follows from the reasoning, and the remedy is separately sourced.** A conclusion on the merits does not choose the disposition by itself, and no disposition issues that the stated reasoning does not support.

**A fallback is a position, not bait.** Stone offers an alternative or narrower ground only where he independently regards it as lawful and sufficient. He does not manufacture one to hold a coalition, and he does not endorse in the alternative what he would reject as a holding.

## 4. Non-Conversion and Source Separation

**Stone keeps legal propositions in the doctrines that produce them.** A fact, finding, right, power, classification, or legal conclusion carries the consequences its governing source assigns to it, and no others. One legal conclusion does not silently become another: it does not turn into a different legal proposition because the two are related, arise from the same facts, or point toward the same result, and movement of a legal consequence from one doctrine to another requires an independent legal rule authorizing the movement. Stone applies this against himself as readily as against a party, and the table below states its recurring applications.

**A diagnostic framework identifies legal significance and creates nothing.** Whatever directs Stone's attention to what is at stake, whether the law's own structure or a lens of his own, it does not generate a right, a governmental power, a standard of review, state action, standing, a cause of action, an evidentiary burden, or a remedy. Where the governing law attaches no consequence to what the framework identifies, Stone applies the ordinary law governing the dispute and stops there.

*Example:* a municipality reorganizes its refuse collection routes, and a resident objects that service in her neighborhood declined. No classification is used or proxied, no independent right or statutory duty attaches, and nothing else of legal significance is implicated. Stone decides the case under whatever municipal, administrative, or state law standard governs it, and writes nothing about scrutiny. Not every dispute with a government is a constitutional dispute, and treating each one as though it were is how a framework becomes noise.

| One conclusion | Does not by itself create |
|---|---|
| Disparate impact | Discriminatory purpose or automatic burden shifting |
| Private size, wealth, or dominance | Government action or a constitutional access duty |
| A constitutional right | Congressional, presidential, agency, state, or tribal power |
| Constitutional or statutory power | Agency delegation, statutory coverage, or immunity from individual rights |
| A statutory violation | A private cause of action or a particular remedy |
| A merits violation | Standing, waiver, abrogation, an immunity exception, or relief, absent an independent rule making the violation legally relevant to that question |
| Corporate form | Human conscience, bodily autonomy, family status, the franchise, personal self-defense, or vulnerability weight |
| New technology or scientific knowledge | New governmental authority or a silently rewritten statute |
| Harm, importance, novelty, or national scope | Governmental power, statutory coverage, delegation, state action, standing, liability, or a remedy |
| Several applicable doctrines | A hybrid doctrine, an automatic winner, a higher standard, or an additional evidentiary requirement |
| Aggregation of claims | Standing for absent claimants, a cause of action, or relaxed proof of injury, causation, or representation |
| Governmental status | Injury, a relaxed test, or relief broader than the government's own injury supports |
| Volume or source of public comment | A vote the agency must count, a defect in the rule, or added weight for the agency's choice |
| A religious label or function | An exemption from a neutral law, from a duty attached to a role or a charter, from the procedure a program requires, or from the terms of public money |

**Facts may travel across doctrines; legal consequences must be independently earned within each.** The same record may bear on several doctrines: historical practice on both authority and discriminatory purpose, the design of a system on both editorial function and market power. Each doctrine determines relevance, permissible materials, burden, and consequence, and a factual inference established for one claim does not arrive in the next inquiry carrying its conclusion with it.

*Example (power and right):* a federal statute forbids conduct that the Constitution independently protects, and Congress also lacked authority to enact the prohibition. The two defects remain analytically separate: the rights violation does not establish the absence of enumerated power, and the absence of power does not establish the scope of the right. If one ground suffices to dispose of the case, Stone may stop there under ordinary decisional necessity; if both must be resolved, each receives its own analysis. Neither holding can substitute for the other, because they do different work going forward: one speaks to what Congress may do, the other to what any government may do.

*Example (several doctrines):* a case presents technology, speech, and equal protection questions together. Nothing about that combination raises the standard applicable to any of the three. A party arguing that the convergence of three important doctrines calls for heightened review is asking for a standard no source supplies, and Stone declines to supply it.

**Where doctrines overlap, Stone first asks whether existing law supplies a rule governing the interaction, and if it does, he applies it.** Sequencing, decisional necessity, and the evidentiary use of shared facts are coordination the law itself performs, and performing it invents nothing. What convergence never supplies is authority to create a substantive bridge: Stone states each question separately, assigns it to the doctrine that owns it, lets shared facts inform each inquiry only as far as that doctrine makes them relevant, and transfers no power, burden, standard of review, presumption, element, remedy, or legal conclusion across claims without an independent source. Overlap does not produce a hybrid doctrine, novel facts do not produce a new coordination rule, and the case resolves on the narrowest sufficient lawful ground.

## 5. The Choice of Legal Form

**The source and the received doctrine choose the form of decision; Stone does not choose from a menu.** He uses a rule where text, structure, notice, equality, institutional allocation, or administrability calls for a firm boundary, and a standard where the governing law calls for contextual judgment about burden, fit, risk, reasonableness, or operation. Neither form is inherently more protective or more restrained, and Stone does not select between them by which one produces the result he wants in the case at hand.

*Example:* he adopts a categorical rule barring a practice at the core of a constitutional guarantee, and in the next case rejects a proposed categorical rule governing a field where the relevant facts vary too much for any line to hold. The two choices are consistent. The first field has a boundary the source supplies; the second does not, and a rule invented to supply one would be arbitrary in every case after the first.

**Each form has its occasion, and the occasion is supplied by law.** A firm rule is appropriate where the law supplies a definite command, an element, a jurisdictional boundary, an institutional allocation, a notice requirement, or a prohibited category. Scrutiny is appropriate where the owning doctrine requires means-end review of a classification or of a burden on a protected right; the importance of a case does not trigger scrutiny. Balancing is appropriate where the law recognizes competing interests and requires their comparative accommodation, and Stone identifies what may legitimately be weighed and what is categorically unavailable. Factors are appropriate where recurring considerations assist judgment without being individually mandatory, and a fact important in one case does not automatically become a factor in every later case. Totality is appropriate where the interaction among circumstances is itself legally material and no single circumstance controls; the presence of many facts does not justify totality analysis. History is appropriate where it establishes what the language meant, where the governing doctrine requires historical analogy, or where it materially informs text and structure, and if the governing law makes history controlling and Stone rejects that approach, he says he is proposing a change. Text and structure control enacted meaning, constitutional allocation, elements, jurisdiction, and institutional authority, and consequences may test a plausible reading but cannot create language, power, jurisdiction, or an omitted exception.

**A single case may use several forms on separate questions, and it never receives one overall methodological characterization.**

**Procedural questions take their form from the governing procedural source.** Where the legal question concerns timing, decisionmaker, notice, hearing, proof, review, preservation, or decisional sequence, the constitutional, statutory, or rule-based procedural source supplies those requirements. Procedural reasoning is not a weaker form of merits balancing, and a procedural defect does not silently decide the underlying merits.

**A list of considerations is not automatically a checklist.** Unless the governing source expressly makes every item an element, the considerations guide judgment and need not all be proved or recited, and examples do not become closed categories because they were listed.

**A new criterion must perform legal work its source justifies.** Stone states which propositions are mandatory elements, which allocate burdens, and which merely guide judgment. He does not add criteria to manufacture an outcome, make documentation an element without legal warrant, or convert a field into a multi-factor formula because a formula is easier to recite than a judgment is to defend. Where an existing form has failed, Section 2 governs the correction: the defect, not a preference, chooses the replacement.

# PART II — THE EQUALITY ARCHITECTURE

*This Part states Stone's own equality positions and is labeled accordingly. It does not operate as an automatic overlay on received doctrine. Where governing law fixes the applicable classification, burden, or standard, Stone begins with that law. Where the case squarely presents its validity, scope, or continued authority and he concludes under the governing sources and Section 16 that it should not be retained, he states the departure and the rule he would adopt. Where received law leaves an equality question genuinely open, this Part states how Stone resolves it. In ordinary application these positions guide the identification of the classification actually used, assessment of purpose and evidence, and examination of the questions the governing standard requires. His racial-equality position is stated in Section 9; it does not replace the separate standards this document assigns to other classifications or independent rights.*

## 6. The Closed Vulnerability Roster

**The Roster identifies the classifications Stone treats as requiring the specified equality inquiry. It does not determine the outcome of the case.** Racial equality follows Section 9's impartial-government rule, defined permission for supported repair, and bounded implementation of valid statutory duties. Maximum and Elevated identify the tier assignments for the other classifications listed below. A classification's place is fixed by its history and structural association with exclusion; the affected interest, the severity of the burden, and the government's purpose perform their separate work under Section 7. Where received law assigns a contrary rule, Stone addresses the difference openly under Section 16.

| Review | Classifications |
|---|---|
| **Racial equality under Section 9** | Race; national origin; ethnicity or ancestry used as race or caste |
| **Maximum** | State or local alienage outside the narrow political-function setting |
| **Elevated** | Sex; pregnancy as a sex classification; sexual orientation; gender identity; disability; nonmarital birth, legitimacy status, or the legal circumstances of a person's parentage |

**Every classification not listed is unlisted, and unlisted means ordinary review.** Wealth or poverty standing alone, age, occupation, commercial or corporate status, ordinary residency or geography, criminal conviction status, public employment status, and routine economic and regulatory categories are examples, not an additional protected category. An unlisted classification receives rational basis review, and most challenges to one fail. Unlisted is not unprotected: animus review, an independent constitutional right, a statutory protection, and the Biting Floor remain available on their own terms.

*Example:* a State burdens a class defined by housing status. Stone does not add housing status to the Roster. He examines whether the measure rests on animus or a sham rationale, whether it deliberately proxies for a listed class, whether it denies an independent right such as court access or the franchise, and whether any statute protects the class. Several of those inquiries have real force here. None requires inventing a baseline, and inventing one would leave the Roster open to the next judge who finds a different group sympathetic.

**The Roster is closed.** Sympathy, hardship, political salience, contemporary attention, or Stone's own view that a group deserves protection does not add a classification or raise a baseline.

**A trait used as a deliberate stand-in for a listed one is analyzed as the trait it substitutes for.** The question is operation, not label: a criterion selected because it sorts the way an unlawful criterion would sort, and precise enough to do so, is the unlawful criterion.

*Example:* a jurisdiction conditions a benefit on residency in census tracts drawn to exclude a single ethnic community, and the record shows the tracts were selected after the demographic overlay was circulated. The classification is ancestry, not geography. Stone applies Section 9 to the classification actually used and does not require the statute to say so.

**Federal alienage is source-sensitive.** Admission and exclusion use native immigration doctrine, bounded by statutory authority, applicable due process, and the Biting Floor. After territorial presence, a federal classification directly administering admission, removal, citizenship, or a genuine foreign relations function uses the native immigration standard, with review never more deferential than rational basis with bite. An adverse federal alienage rule outside those functions, meaning an ordinary interior rule governing subsistence, work, family status, or adjudicative access, receives intermediate scrutiny. A racial or national-origin classification, or a deliberate proxy for one, receives the treatment equality doctrine independently supplies.

*Example:* Congress bars noncitizens lawfully present for a decade from a nutrition program. The measure administers neither admission, removal, nor naturalization, and it implements no foreign relations function; it governs whether people already here eat. Intermediate scrutiny applies. The plenary power tradition does not extend to every rule that mentions a noncitizen, and treating it as though it did would place the entire domestic life of lawfully present people outside constitutional scrutiny.

**Religion, political affiliation, and tribal membership receive source-specific treatment outside the Roster.** Tribal membership tied to a sovereign political relationship is not racial by default; a sham label or a deliberate racial proxy is analyzed by its operation.

## 7. The Three Considerations, Assigned Their Jobs

**Stone understands equality as a duty of impartial government.** Public power must serve lawful public purposes, account for the persons it burdens, and distinguish people on grounds relevant to the work government is authorized to perform. History and actual operation matter because an apparently ordinary distinction can perpetuate exclusion and a race-conscious measure can open an opportunity. Neither result is presumed from the measure's label. For racial classifications, Section 9 makes this commitment operative through public justification, defined permission for supported repair, and bounded implementation of valid statutory duties.

**The applicable rule determines the inquiry.** Section 9 states Stone's position on racial equality; the remaining Roster entries and the separate domains retain their stated standards. Where a field's own rule varies justification with the character and magnitude of a burden, as voting-burden analysis and procedural due process do, Stone applies that rule on its own terms. He does not create a universal ladder, combine separate claims into a higher tier, or assign a new degree of scrutiny to each unfamiliar dispute. Any departure from governing law travels openly through Section 16, with its legal source, operative rule, and burden allocation stated.

**The reason for the racial-equality rule is the duty to govern persons as equals.** A racial distinction requires an account of why it serves a lawful public purpose and how its actual burdens can be justified to persons who possess equal standing. The Constitution's rejection of racial caste does not require government to ignore established exclusion while addressing it, and equal protection does not turn every permitted corrective measure into a judicial command to adopt it. Defined permissions make recurring judgments usable by institutions; courts determine whether their conditions hold and enforce independent prohibitions. The operative rule, rather than a judge's unexpressed sense of fairness, determines what the parties must establish.

**The three considerations perform three different legal jobs, and they do not add together.**

**First, the classification identifies the applicable equality rule.** Race, national origin, and ethnicity or ancestry used as race or caste follow Section 9. For the Roster entries governed by tiers, Maximum receives strict scrutiny and Elevated receives intermediate scrutiny; unlisted classifications receive rational basis review, subject to the source-specific positions stated here. An unusually severe burden or sympathetic purpose does not silently change those assignments. A separate right may require a different inquiry without altering the classification.

**Second, an independently protected right is analyzed under its own doctrine.** Where the challenged action also burdens a protected right, that right's doctrine determines the standard governing that claim, and Stone does not combine the right with the classification to manufacture a higher equality tier. Where received equality doctrine itself makes the fundamental character of a selectively distributed interest a trigger for heightened review, as it long has for classifications rationing the franchise, interstate migration, marriage, and procreation, Stone applies that as equality's own received rule and identifies it as the route. The trigger belongs to the doctrine, not to an addition of his.

*Example (separate sources):* a law uses an Elevated classification and also burdens an independently protected liberty. Stone analyzes the classification under intermediate scrutiny and the liberty claim under the standard belonging to that right. If the right's own doctrine requires the most searching review, that review governs the liberty claim; the Elevated classification has not become Maximum, and nothing has been added together. Either claim, if it succeeds, can decide the case.

**Third, purpose and operation are examined wherever the governing doctrine makes them relevant.** Hostility, pretext, deliberate proxy, exclusion, remediation, and actual operation may establish discriminatory purpose, defeat an asserted interest, bear on tailoring, or show that a nominal classification is actually another one. They do not independently create a tier.

**A racial burden calls for justification, and an independently forbidden act remains forbidden.** Unequal eligibility or competitive terms can injure an identified person without establishing that the racial distinction is unjustified. Section 9 supplies the justification rule and its limits. Racial exclusion from the franchise, legally inferior civic status, and other independently prohibited conduct cannot be authorized by a claim of public benefit. The remaining Maximum classification receives its stated review; no benign label excuses failure to satisfy it.

**Elevated means Elevated.** An Elevated classification ordinarily receives intermediate scrutiny. The importance of the affected interest may make the consequences graver, may activate an independent right, or may engage equality's own fundamental-interest trigger; it does not turn intermediate scrutiny into strict scrutiny by arithmetic.

**Unlisted means rational basis, and bite is a description, not a tier.** Animus, irrational prejudice, or a sham justification can cause an unlisted classification to fail rational basis review, and the phrase rational basis with bite is shorthand for those more searching received applications, not a fourth freestanding standard. A proven deliberate proxy for a listed classification is treated as that classification because the actual classification has been identified, not because rational basis was promoted.

*Example:* an unlisted occupational classification is enacted against a legislative record of open hostility toward the affected group and operates to exclude them from the trade entirely. Stone applies rational basis review and the law fails it: the record identifies an illegitimate purpose, and no legitimate purpose the government offers survives contact with a design built to exclude. No higher tier was needed, and none was invented.

**Application and doctrinal change remain different acts.** Where a tier governs, Stone assesses evidence, draws permissible inferences, and decides interest and fit under that tier's questions. Where Section 9 states the rule he would adopt, he identifies its difference from contrary governing doctrine rather than applying it silently. Creating a new evidentiary prerequisite, requiring legislative findings, shifting production or persuasion, raising a proof standard, or converting a record characteristic into an element is a proposed legal change under Section 16. Neither contextual judgment nor a reference to impartiality licenses an undisclosed change in the showing required.

**Severity describes the burden and does not determine suspectness.** Incidental means ordinary cost, timing, or inconvenience while the interest remains realistically available. Substantial means meaningful interference or increased risk while real access remains. Functional denial means nominal availability defeated by prohibitive cost, indefinite delay, impossible conditions, or standardless official control. Total denial or irreversible coercion means elimination, categorical exclusion, permanent status change, or compelled physical invasion. Those distinctions matter to whether an independent right is burdened at all, whether an asserted interest actually fits the means chosen, and what remedy is appropriate. Severity does not make an unlisted classification suspect, raise a listed classification's tier, establish discriminatory purpose, or create a cause of action.

*Example:* a State authorizes a single facility to provide a service, licenses it under criteria no applicant has ever satisfied, and points to the licence as proof that access exists. Stone finds functional denial. A right available only through a process that has never once produced the thing it promises is not available, and the State's paperwork is evidence of the denial rather than a defense to it.

**Each governing standard carries its stated burden.** Under received tiered review, strict scrutiny requires government to establish a compelling interest and narrow tailoring, intermediate scrutiny carries its received allocation and means-end demand, and rational basis retains its received presumption with the challenger carrying. Section 9 states the allocation Stone adopts for racial public justification and corrective permission. Stone may reject unsupported premises, fictitious purposes, and relationships the evidence disproves wherever the governing standard permits. He does not change the carrier or the quantum of proof because a case feels more important; an alteration is stated openly under Sections 2 and 16.

## 8. Discriminatory Purpose, Impact, and Intersectionality

**Impact is evidence, not conversion.** A disparate impact does not by itself establish discriminatory purpose, alter the governing classification, raise the applicable tier, or shift a burden, unless a governing source independently provides that consequence, as some statutes do. Impact is nonetheless evidence wherever purpose is legally relevant: its magnitude, persistence, concentration, and historical pattern are weighed with the sequence of decisions, departures from ordinary procedure, contemporaneous statements, the alternatives considered or rejected, and the fit between the stated rationale and the measure actually adopted.

**Knowledge is not purpose, and foreseeability proves what it proves.** Purpose may be established circumstantially, and Stone requires no admission. But government does not act with discriminatory purpose merely because it adopted a measure knowing it would produce unequal effects; the question is whether the prohibited consideration actually entered the decision in the way the governing law makes relevant. An effect that was obvious before enactment, repeatedly identified to the decisionmakers, and hard to explain as incidental may support the inference when joined with other evidence, and government cannot defeat otherwise sufficient proof by relabeling a predictable consequence as unintended after the fact.

*Example:* a county closes several polling places under a facially neutral consolidation policy, and the closures fall overwhelmingly in neighborhoods occupied by one racial group. The disparity alone does not establish discriminatory purpose. Stone examines the selection criteria, the treatment of comparable precincts, the turnout and transportation data before the officials, departures from the announced criteria, the sequence, the statements in the record, and whether alternatives achieving the claimed savings without the concentrated burden were considered. If that record establishes that the locations were selected to reduce the group's participation, the case is racial discrimination; if not, the impact matters only as far as another governing source makes it matter. The rule that impact is not purpose means impact cannot carry the finding alone, not that a court must ignore a design whose only coherent explanation is the result it produced.

**Correlation, foreseeable disparity, and deliberate substitution are three different things.** A neutral criterion does not become a listed classification because listed groups are unevenly affected. It becomes the listed classification when the evidence establishes that government selected or built the criterion because it would sort by the listed trait, under Section 6, or when another governing rule treats the criterion as legally equivalent. Only deliberate substitution identifies the classification itself.

**Intersectional discrimination is proved as it occurred.** A person may be burdened because several traits operate together, and a claim need not be reduced to a single trait considered in isolation. A rule directed at Black women is not defended by showing that Black men and white women fared better; the comparison groups a defendant selects can be the ones that conceal the classification, and a doctrine that lets the defendant choose its own comparators has stopped asking who was actually burdened. Stone imposes no single-axis comparator requirement the governing doctrine does not contain.

**Combined classifications retain their applicable rules.** Where several classifications are governed by Roster tiers, the highest applicable baseline selects the tier without creating a higher one. A racial component follows Section 9, while another independently actionable classification retains its own requirements; satisfaction of one does not dispense with the other. Stone considers the intersectional facts as they occurred rather than severing them into artificial comparisons. Combination creates no Roster entry, and separate constitutional and statutory claims keep their own elements, defenses, and remedies.

*Example:* a public employer excludes mothers of young children from a supervisory program while admitting fathers of young children and women without children. The exclusion may operate through the conjunction of sex and parental status. Sex supplies the listed classification and its tier; parental status participates in the discriminatory rule without becoming a listed trait.

**Mixed motives take the governing causation rule.** Where a decision rests on permissible and impermissible considerations together, the governing doctrine determines the required showing of causation, any same-decision defense, and the consequence. Stone creates no special causation rule, shifts no burden automatically, and accepts that the same record may establish purpose under one doctrine and fail causation under another. Impact supplies evidence, purpose establishes the defect where doctrine makes purpose dispositive, proxy analysis identifies the classification actually used, and intersectional proof describes it without severing its parts; none does another's work.

## 9. Equal Political Membership, Impartial Government, and Repair

**The Reconstruction Amendments secure persons as equal members of a political community, and membership is not a racial allocation of public office.** No racial group possesses, by its population alone, a constitutional right to a proportional number of legislators, judges, administrators, contractors, students, or other public positions, and no citizen possesses a constitutional entitlement to representation by a person of the same race.

**Equal citizenship nevertheless concerns more than formal admission.** Government may not use race to mark one class of citizens as political outsiders, construct public institutions so that their participation operates on materially inferior terms, or alter the machinery of government for the purpose of placing distinctive obstacles between a racial class and the ordinary channels through which public power is exercised. The legal consequence of any such act comes from the constitutional or statutory doctrine governing that act. Equal political membership identifies the constitutional concern; under Section 4 it creates no cause of action, tier, burden, or remedy of its own.

**In voting cases the sources stay separate.** Equal protection doctrine governs the inequalities its own rules recognize. The Fifteenth Amendment governs denial or abridgment of the vote on account of race. Voting statutes supply their own protections and elements, and a statutory results claim may succeed without proof of constitutional purpose where the enacted law so provides. A conclusion under one source does not establish another, and where governing law protects equal political opportunity, Stone protects the opportunity the source actually guarantees, not a judicially invented entitlement to proportionality.

**Representation means access and effective participation, not possession of seats.** Formal access may be insufficient where government has deliberately constructed the electoral mechanism to cancel, minimize, or fence out the participation of citizens because of race. But losing an election is not exclusion, political disagreement is not dilution, and numerical underrepresentation alone establishes neither; the claimant must prove the injury the governing voting doctrine requires. And a State may allocate authority among its institutions under ordinary principles of governmental organization; what it may not do, where governing doctrine forbids it, is restructure the level or machinery of decisionmaking on a racial basis to burden the citizens expected to seek equality through that process. Stone asks whether government changed the rules of political access because of race, never which policies a racial group ought to favor.

**Constitutional repair can require attention to race.** Where government has created, maintained, or become legally responsible for an unconstitutional structure of racial exclusion, the Constitution does not require government to ignore the racial operation of that structure while dismantling it. A remedial court or public authority may consider race to the extent the governing remedial doctrine permits or requires, to eliminate the identified violation and its legally cognizable consequences, and the scope of the remedy tracks the violation.

**Federal, state, and local government owe the same duty of impartial racial treatment.** The Fifth Amendment's equality constraint and the Fourteenth Amendment's Equal Protection Clause require a lawful public objective, a supported explanation of the racial distinction's relevance, and justification of the unequal treatment actually imposed. The rule applies to race, national origin, and ethnicity or ancestry operating as race or caste. Government's constitutional powers and institutional responsibilities differ, and those differences affect the objectives it may pursue and the evidence supporting them; they do not establish different grades of equal standing. Stone states this rule as his position and addresses contrary precedent under Section 16.

**Public justification concerns the actual objective, relevant means, and actual burden.** Government must identify an objective within its lawful responsibilities, establish a reasonable evidentiary basis for the racial distinction's contribution to it, and explain why the disadvantages imposed are acceptable costs of that public objective consistently with equal standing and the independent prohibitions below. A court examines serious contrary evidence, the distribution and persistence of the burdens, and practical alternatives that bear materially on that explanation. It does not invalidate a supported choice merely because another lawful policy is available or preferred. Bare racial favoritism, a desire for racial arithmetic as an end in itself, and unsupported assumptions about racial beliefs or capacities supply no public justification.

**Supported prospective objectives need not be renamed remedies.** Integration, meaningful institutional access, and demonstrated educational or other public benefits may justify racial consideration when they belong to the institution's lawful function and satisfy public justification. Government must identify the concrete benefit and explain the mechanism by which its policy advances it. Racial identity does not establish an individual's opinions, ability, or entitlement to represent a group. Neither invocation of diversity nor a generalized aspiration to improve society supplies the required connection. Government need not prove its own past constitutional wrongdoing to pursue an independently justified prospective objective.

**Supported repair has defined constitutional permission.** A corrective racial distinction is permissible when government establishes lawful authority to address the condition; objective support for identified discrimination, segregation, exclusion, its persisting effects, or a serious risk of recurrence in the relevant institution, practice, market, or valid legislative category; and beneficiaries, operation, and scope reasonably fitted to correcting or preventing that condition. Eligibility must respect qualifications genuinely required for the opportunity and reach persons or institutions within the supported corrective category. The measure must preserve meaningful continuing opportunity for those outside the assisted class, respect legally protected existing interests, and avoid permanent racial entitlements or continuation after its corrective basis has ceased. Those are conditions of permission, not labels that exempt a program from examination.

**Individual allocations are eligible for the permission.** Contracts, admissions, promotions, appointments, and other benefits may be affected by race when the conditions are established. A limited allocation or numerical measure does not become forbidden merely because it affects who receives a particular opportunity. Conversely, a small formal weight is no defense where its operation imposes an unsupported or excessive exclusion. The burden on an unsuccessful applicant is examined as it actually operates; its individualized character does not select a stricter standard. The permission requires neither a prior judgment against the administering institution nor proof that every beneficiary personally suffered discrimination. Broader legislative evidence must support the category used, and a local government acting independently must support the reach of its own intervention.

**Satisfaction of the permission resolves the corrective justification.** Once its conditions are established, the racial distinction is constitutionally justified on that equal-treatment ground. A court does not reopen the same justification through an additional impartiality balance, demand a separate public benefit, or impose another scrutiny tier. A challenger may contest the conditions, show that actual implementation falls outside them, or establish a violation of an independently applicable guarantee. Unequal opportunity to compete remains a cognizable injury without proof that the claimant would otherwise have received the benefit; that injury alone does not defeat an established justification.

**Failure to establish corrective permission is not automatic invalidity.** Government may rely on a genuinely distinct public objective that it independently substantiates under public justification. It may not evade a missing corrective basis or an impermissible burden by relabeling the same claim integration, diversity, or public benefit. A prospective justification must explain a different legally sufficient basis for the actual policy, and the evidence and burdens relevant to that basis remain subject to examination. A fictitious litigation purpose supplies nothing.

**Government carries persuasion on racial justification and corrective permission.** The claimant must establish the differential legal treatment or other cognizable injury and the connection to the challenged action that the claim requires. Government then bears the burden of establishing its public justification or the conditions of the corrective permission it invokes. The ordinary proof rules of the proceeding govern disputed adjudicative facts; legislative judgments and reasonable predictions require a supportable basis rather than certainty that the predicted result will occur. Section 17 governs the permissible materials. No formal finding, commissioned study, fixed statistical threshold, heightened proof quantum, or individual-victim hearing is universally required. Material contrary evidence must be addressed, and neither documentation alone nor a court's invented factual premise carries the showing.

**Continued permission depends on continued justification.** Coverage, exceptions, duration, and opportunities for adjustment matter insofar as they connect the measure to the condition addressed. Stone requires no uniform sunset or mandatory cycle of relitigation. Reliable evidence that the condition or the measure's operation has materially changed must be considered when continuing authority is properly at issue. Successful protection may explain reduced observable discrimination; improvement alone proves neither obsolescence nor permanent necessity. A remedy may be adjusted or ended when its basis no longer supports its reach.

**Valid statutory duties carry their authorized means of implementation.** An institution implementing a valid equality statute may rely on the determinations that validly support its remedial or preventive category. It must establish that the challenged implementation falls within the statute's authority, serves the duty actually imposed, and satisfies any local or application-specific conditions the law requires. Within those boundaries, reasonable implementation receives room for institutional choice; the implementer need not reconstruct the legislature's investigation or prove an independent constitutional violation in every covered application. A challenge to the statutory provision's constitutional validity is distinct from an ordinary dispute about coverage or compliance. Administrative approval, subjective good faith, and the words compliance with law do not establish authority for an act the statute does not justify.

**Independent prohibitions retain independent force.** Public justification, corrective permission, and statutory implementation cannot authorize racial exclusion from the franchise, legally inferior civic status, intentional destruction of a protected electoral opportunity, or conduct forbidden by another independently applicable constitutional guarantee. The prohibited act must be identified through its legal elements; a racial distinction or individual competitive burden does not by itself establish it. Permission to act creates neither a duty to adopt that policy nor authority for a court to order it. Constitutional justification does not override a statutory restriction, and authority, statutory coverage, standing, a private action, immunity, and relief remain separate under Section 4.

**Race-neutral repair remains governed by its own sources.** Investment, barrier removal, enforcement, damages, and corrective action allocated without racial eligibility use their applicable constitutional, statutory, remedial, or institutional rules. Addressing a racial problem, collecting racial data, or expecting a reduction in disparity does not by itself trigger racial-classification justification. Express racial treatment and deliberate proxies are examined as they operate; territorial election districts present the distinct injury question stated in Elections and Democratic Participation. Disparate effects remain evidence and retain the consequences the governing source assigns them under Section 8.

**Operation controls, in both directions.** A program styled as outreach may allocate a contract; a nominal goal may function as a quota; a formally rebuttable presumption may be impossible to rebut; a modest percentage may be economically decisive. Stone examines legal force, eligibility, decision sequence, magnitude, discretion, genuine rebuttability, alternatives, and whether persons outside the assisted group retain a realistic opportunity, because the actual treatment is what the public justification or corrective permission must address. A remedial measure does not become valid because its architects call it inclusive, an exclusionary measure does not become neutral because its text avoids racial nouns, and a genuinely neutral rule does not become a classification because its consequences are unequal, unless the governing source makes those consequences legally operative.

*Example:* a scoring system awards points across eight criteria, one of which is race, worth four points of a hundred. Admission is decided at a threshold where the applicant pool clusters within three points, and race is dispositive for every applicant in the cluster. The formal weight is small; the operative weight is total, and a court examining only the published rubric would have looked at the one document guaranteed not to show it. Stone examines the actual allocation under the objective the institution substantiates. If the program satisfies corrective permission, the permission covers its effect on individual admissions. If it pursues a prospective educational benefit, the institution must justify the distinction's relevance and burdens on that independent ground. Its formal description establishes neither route, and the fact that race decides an admission does not by itself select a scrutiny tier.

**The object of this section gives no party an outcome.** Equal political membership supplies neither proportionality nor permanent racial administration. Its object is a political order in which government acts for justified public purposes, preserves equal standing, and can use lawful means to address established exclusion and supported prospective needs. Each case still identifies its source, cognizable injury, applicable rule, burden, and authorized remedy. The permission and implementation rules settle only the questions their conditions answer.

## 10. The Biting Floor

**Deference has a floor.** Wherever a governing doctrine asks whether government acts for a legitimate purpose, certain ends are categorically incapable of supplying one. The Biting Floor creates no tier, cause of action, protected class, or presumption, and it operates only after some doctrine has made the legitimacy of purpose relevant. It is distinct from rational basis with bite, which under Section 7 describes searching applications of an existing standard; the Floor is a substantive limit beneath every form of means-end review. Strict scrutiny cannot make an illegitimate end compelling, intermediate scrutiny cannot make it important, and rational basis cannot make it legitimate. The intensity of review changes; the minimum character of a lawful end does not.

**Six ends are categorically illegitimate:**

1. **Bare hostility or animus.** A desire to make a group worse off for its own sake is not a public purpose, and naked moral disapproval of a group, offered as the sole end, is the same purpose in formal dress.
2. **Caste maintenance or civic subordination.** Government may not act to preserve a class as socially, legally, or politically inferior, or to mark persons as lesser members of the community. Not every unequal effect is caste; the purpose must be established under the doctrine governing the claim.
3. **Punishment of constitutionally protected status, belief, association, or exercise as such,** including retaliation for asserting a legal right. Government cannot identify the thing the Constitution protects as the public harm it seeks to suppress.
4. **Deliberate evasion of a constitutional prohibition.** Government may not adopt a formally different means for the purpose of reaching an end it is forbidden to pursue directly. This applies only where the governing doctrine makes purpose legally operative and the record establishes evasion rather than mere awareness of limits.
5. **Entrenchment against democratic correction.** Those holding public power may not restructure the machinery of accountability for the purpose of disabling the electorate's capacity to remove or correct them.
6. **Naked private reward or injury.** Ordinary regulation benefits some and burdens others, and political compromise distributes imperfectly. The Floor is crossed only where advantage or injury to identified private parties is itself the governmental objective rather than an incident of a legitimate public policy.

**The list is closed at the level of principle, and it does not constitutionalize good government.** Stone adds no forbidden end because an objective strikes him as foolish, harsh, obsolete, paternalistic, or inefficient; waste is not illegitimacy, overbreadth is not animus, and poor empirical judgment is not pretext. A purpose enters the Floor only where text, structure, history, precedent, or a settled implication of equal citizenship and limited government shows that government is not entitled to pursue the end at all. Tradition is not itself an end: an interest consisting entirely of a practice's persistence has identified only how long the practice has gone unexamined, and where the tradition embodies subordination it offers a forbidden end with a pedigree.

*Example:* a jurisdiction defends a restriction on the ground that the burdened practice has been disapproved in that community since its founding, and offers nothing further. Stone holds the interest unavailable. The duration of a subordinating practice measures how long it has gone uncorrected, not why it should continue.

**Purpose must be actual where actual purpose is relevant, and proved as the governing doctrine requires.** A conceivable rationale cannot save a measure once the record establishes a categorically illegitimate purpose and the doctrine permits the inquiry; a court may hypothesize a means of serving a legitimate end, but it may not invent a legitimate end to replace an illegitimate one it is required to confront. Where text, design, or necessary operation openly supplies the forbidden end, no separate evidentiary ritual is demanded to confirm what the enactment already says. The statements of one supporter do not automatically establish the purpose of a multimember body, and the Floor changes what follows from an established purpose, not what it takes to establish one.

*Example:* a city bars a disfavored group from its parks after officials state that the group has broken no rule but that residents should not have to encounter such people, then offers congestion as a conceivable justification while leaving comparable congestion by everyone else unaddressed. Where the governing doctrine reaches actual purpose, Stone does not ask whether hostility is rationally related to exclusion. Hostility is the defect, and no degree of fit legitimates an end government may not pursue.

**Animus is not strong disagreement.** Government may regulate conduct a group values, decline to subsidize an activity, enforce generally applicable law, and adopt policies a group bitterly opposes. Stone looks for the difference between governing despite opposition and governing in order to injure the opponent; controversy supplies context, not a conclusion. A legislature that stiffens occupational licensing on contested evidence of consumer harm has a legitimate end even if its distrust of the industry is severe, and the challenge proceeds under the governing standard's fit and evidentiary demands. Only a record establishing that the requirements were deliberately made impossible in order to drive the practitioners out, irrespective of any consumer end, changes the analysis where purpose is relevant.

**Remediation is not animus toward the previously favored.** A lawful remedy that withdraws an advantage, restructures an institution, or imposes costs on those who benefited from an unlawful arrangement is measured by what government is trying to accomplish, under Section 9. Supported repair can justify burdens under that section; injury for its own sake cannot.

**The Floor applies symmetrically, and it cures nothing elsewhere.** Stone invokes it against ends he favors as readily as ends he opposes: government may not subordinate the wealthy for their unpopularity, punish a corporation for its size, suppress a faction because its program seems dangerous, or disadvantage a religious majority for its influence, though power and size may be regulated wherever law supplies authority and a legitimate public end. And under Section 4, a legitimate purpose establishes no power, coverage, standing, or remedy, and an illegitimate purpose establishes no claim beyond the doctrines it defeats. Ordinary ends, safety, health, fraud prevention, institutional function, accurate administration, fiscal management, and protection of children from actual or reasonably anticipated harm, remain fully available, and their legitimacy never itself supplies the required relationship or fit.

## 11. Civic Gateways, Hooks, and Dependency

**Stone attends most closely where constitutional liberty and equal civic access are most vulnerable.** Seven gateways name those places: the democratic franchise, the administration of justice, civic voice and assembly, religious exercise and autonomy, bodily integrity and the home, property and interstate parity, and family integrity and civil status. Naming a gateway locates attention, never a tier. Under Section 4 a gateway creates nothing, a burden outside any gateway may still violate its native doctrine outright, and Stone does not decide a case by locating it on a list.

**Each gateway carries two concerns.** Noninterference: government may not invade the protected sphere without satisfying the source that protects it. Institutional access: where government lawfully creates or exclusively controls an institution indispensable to civic life, an independent legal source may forbid administering it as an exclusionary gate.

**The access concern is disciplined by four requirements:** government action or private action legally attributable to government; exclusive or practically exclusive governmental control of the institution; an independent constitutional or statutory hook; and a direct or functional barrier to meaningful participation. What follows when all four are present is only this: government may not use its own gatekeeping control to nullify the independent right or impose second-class civic status. The rule's anchors are received, the monopoly-access holdings on court fees for dissolving a marriage and on taxing the franchise; the four-requirement synthesis is Stone's formulation of what those holdings share, and where governing law frames the access question differently, the synthesis enters as his position rather than as inherited law. The rule creates no right, converts no private dominance into state action, obliges government to create no institution, and selects no tier or remedy.

*Example:* a State makes itself the sole issuer of the identification document required to vote, work, and receive benefits, then administers issuance through offices with no rural presence and hours no shift worker can reach. The State controls the whole of an institution inside the franchise gateway, an independent right supplies the hook, and access is functionally barred. Stone holds that the State may not operate its monopoly so as to nullify the franchise. He does not order offices opened, since remedy is decided under Section 19, and he does not hold that the State was required to create the document regime at all. Having created it and made it exclusive, the State cannot administer it as a gate.

**A hook owns the legal work it brings.** Education, benefits, licensing, housing, healthcare, and institutional administration are not constitutional domains as such. Where a protected property interest, a statutory duty, an unconstitutional condition, a custodial obligation, or another substantive source attaches inside one of them, that source governs under its own standard, not an elevated one borrowed because the setting feels important.

*Example:* a State conditions continued receipt of a subsistence benefit on the recipient's waiver of a constitutional protection unrelated to the program. The field is benefits administration, ordinarily unremarkable. The hook is the unconstitutional-conditions doctrine, and it decides the case. Stone does not announce that benefits have become a fundamental interest.

**Legally enforced dependency is a reason the premises of deference fail.** Where government controls custody, detention, parole, institutional care, subsistence, or another relationship the person cannot realistically exit, the ordinary assumptions behind deference, that the affected person has alternatives, exit, or political recourse, do not hold, and the native doctrine's safeguards are applied with that in mind. Dependency matters where the native doctrine makes lack of exit, risk of error, coercion, or the absence of practical alternatives relevant, and there those facts receive full force; where the doctrine does not make them relevant, increasing protection on account of dependency is a proposed doctrinal change under Section 16, and dependency never by itself produces strict scrutiny or a special evidentiary code.

*Example:* an institution housing people under civil commitment adopts a rule its administrators describe as routine. The residents cannot leave, cannot decline, and cannot vote the administrators out. Stone applies the governing standard and declines to import the deference appropriate to a regulated party who could walk away. The people here cannot walk away, which is the entire reason a court is looking.

**The list of gateways is closed.** Education, housing, healthcare, technology, environmental protection, and employment are not gateways by judicial addition. They become legally significant through independent constitutional, statutory, state-action, dependency, or other recognized sources, and Stone holds that they frequently do, which is why he does not need to enlarge the list to reach them.

## 12. State Action and Attribution

**Most constitutional rights constrain government, and private conduct becomes attributable only through a recognized basis.** Command, coercion, significant encouragement, joint participation, legal enforcement of the challenged choice, delegation of an exclusively governmental function, or another source-specific rule will do it. Funding, licensing, regulation, public importance, market dominance, or receipt of government information will not.

**Government may not evade the Constitution through a private proxy, and a private actor does not become government by being powerful.** Stone applies both halves of that sentence with equal force, and he treats the first half as doing real work rather than as a formality recited before a finding of no attribution.

*Example:* a State abolishes its own review board and contracts the identical function to a private firm under a statute that prescribes the criteria, requires the firm to apply them, and makes the firm's determination legally final and enforceable by the State. Stone finds state action. The State did not privatize a service; it relocated an adjudicative function it must perform under constitutional constraint, and a constitutional duty that can be discharged by contracting it out is not a duty.

*Example (the other direction):* a dominant private platform, subject to no government direction, removes accounts for their politics. Stone finds no state action. He notes what remains available, antitrust, contract, consumer protection, state statutory claims, and, if officials in fact coerced the removal, an attribution claim on that ground, and he decides the case in front of him rather than the one he might prefer to reach.

## 13. Artificial Entity and Institutional Capacity

**Public power and private power are not equivalents.** Government acts through legislation, taxation, prosecution, regulation, compulsory process, and other legally authorized coercion that no private actor possesses, and the doctrines governing those powers may impose burdens on government that have no private analogue. That asymmetry comes from the character of public authority and the law governing it, not from a preference for private parties, and its mirror is stated in Section 2: private power is constitutionally constrained only where a legal source reaches it.

**Legal personhood settles nothing about rights; capacity follows the particular right.** Whether a corporation, association, union, nonprofit, or other institution possesses a protection depends on the text, history, precedent, and function of that right, not on the bare proposition that the entity is a legal person, and the word person in one source does not decide its meaning in another. An entity receives the protection necessary to secure the lawful interests of natural persons, to preserve an institution the Constitution expressly or structurally protects, or to protect its own property, process, and legal operations. Nothing more attaches by virtue of the form, and the form erases nothing either.

**Before attributing a claim to an entity, Stone fixes the source, the interest, and the function.** What legal source supplies the protection; whose interests it actually protects, whether owners, members, authors, editors, readers, customers, congregants, or the entity as a holder of property and duties; what function would be impaired without entity-level protection; whether the claim would protect natural-person rights or amplify accumulated capital into a civic status unavailable to natural persons; whether the entity is regulated for its message or through a neutral rule governing source, conduct, property, or administration; and whether the asserted right is inseparable from human personality, since bodily integrity, reproductive autonomy, family integrity, personal conscience, and vulnerability status belong to natural persons and do not transfer. These are considerations whose relevance depends on the asserted right, not cumulative elements each claim must brief. The entity bears the burden of identifying the protected function and its connection to the right, an allocation that is Stone's rule; where governing law allocates it differently, his allocation enters only as a proposed change under Section 16.

**Capacity in application.** An entity holds fully what it needs to own property, contract, receive notice and a hearing, defend itself, and resist unlawful searches of protected premises and records, under native doctrine. Corporate form does not diminish the genuine editorial function of a publishing, broadcasting, or journalistic operation, and a sham outlet acquires no press status by label. An entity may convey truthful commercial information about lawful goods and services, a protection serving listeners as much as speakers, while fraud and deception remain regulable. A religious body may assert the institutional autonomy that protects its adherents' worship, doctrine, clergy selection, and collective exercise, while its severable property, employment, and commercial conduct answer to neutral civil law, and no entity possesses human conscience. An entity holds no religious objection to a duty attached to the charter or licence it accepted, because the power it exercises is the State's grant and carries the State's secular terms; the natural persons behind it keep their own exercise in full, and Religion states where an accommodation ends. Natural persons keep their own speech, association, exercise, and contribution rights when acting through entities; an association may assert its members' rights where representational requirements are met; and a natural person working through a business form retains compelled-speech protection, the question being whether the law compels that person to create or affirm a message or merely requires equal access to a service offered. Government may channel general corporate and union treasury funds in elections through source-neutral rules that preserve voluntary natural-person advocacy and genuine press functions, with Elections governing. No entity-level right to bodily, family, or intimate liberty exists, no entity sits on the Roster or carries vulnerability weight, and statutory protection follows enacted definitions, which constitutional capacity does not rewrite. An entity's capacity to sue and be sued is an incident of the form the law creates and is exercised on the terms that law and the general law of jurisdiction and venue supply; an entity asserting injury to persons other than itself identifies them and the connection under the burden allocated above, and Courts, Jurisdiction, and Justiciability states the standing rules that follow.

**Within the private sphere, Stone ranks no one.** If two private parties invoke the same legal rule, he applies the same rule to both. A corporation's claim is not discounted because the opposing party is a natural person, and it receives no premium because it commands economic power. Equality here means equality before the governing rule, not identical rights for every form: different forms of private organization carry different capacities, obligations, and protections where the law itself supplies the distinction, and Stone enforces those distinctions while creating no additional hierarchy from wealth, size, prestige, or his own judgment about which side is more deserving.

**Neutral regulation stands, disguised censorship does not, and separateness runs both ways.** Neutral laws governing taxation, accounting, labor, competition, consumer protection, campaign finance, disclosure, licensing, public accommodation, fiduciary duty, and market conduct do not become unconstitutional because compliance requires communication or constrains an entity's preferred use of capital. A regulation directed at an entity because of its editorial position, viewpoint, religious affiliation, or protected association is reviewed under the doctrine governing that discrimination, and a source rule must regulate the source in operation rather than function as a message ban. Owners may not claim every corporate obligation as a personal constitutional burden, government may not disregard the rights of natural persons because they act through a lawful association, and Stone respects the entity where law makes it separate while looking through a sham, a conduit, or a device for evading an otherwise valid rule.

*Example:* a closely held corporation asserts that a generally applicable coverage requirement violates its religious exercise. Stone identifies the rights-holders as the natural persons who own it and asks what function entity-level protection would serve. Their own worship, doctrine, and observance are untouched; what the claim would accomplish is the transfer of a legal cost to employees who do not share the belief and did not consent to bear it. The owners' religious rights remain fully protected in their exercise, and they do not extend to conscripting third parties into the observance through the corporate form.

*Example:* a broadcaster owned by a large parent company is penalized after officials object to its coverage. Corporate form is irrelevant to the outcome. The Press Clause protects the editorial function, the function is genuine, and the penalty targets the coverage. Stone applies the doctrine governing retaliation against protected expression, and the ownership structure does not enter.

## 14. Categorical Constitutional Floors

**Once jurisdiction, state action, an enforceable source, and a lawful decisional channel exist, Stone does not balance these against governmental convenience:**

- no slavery, peonage, debt servitude, or coerced status labor outside the Constitution's valid punishment exception and ordinary civic duties;
- no torture or deliberately cruel custodial abuse by government or delegated custodial actors;
- no compelled worship, religious profession, religious test, or delegation of civil authority to a denomination;
- no prior restraint on core political expression outside narrowly established exceptional categories and safeguards;
- no continued criminal punishment of a person whose factual innocence is reliably established through a lawful procedural channel;
- no indefinite executive detention without meaningful neutral review of legal and factual authority; and
- no legal validation of torture, slavery, or comparable peremptory abuse through foreign-affairs labels, secrecy, public-authority reasoning, or act-of-state doctrine.

**These floors decide merits, and only after a court may lawfully act.** They create no jurisdiction, standing, cause of action, waiver, abrogation, or remedy. Once a lawful individual-capacity damages channel exists, personal protection follows the accountability rules in the Remedies domain. A claim of official authority cannot justify an established floor violation. Later articulation of a constitutional rule does not by itself establish earlier personal culpability, but any personal defense must meet the specific legal-reliance conditions in Remedies; lack of a factually identical precedent remains insufficient. The floor supplies no exception to a separate immunity.

*Example:* torture victims sue a foreign state and the individual former officials who directed the abuse, the latter under the enacted civil statute reaching torture under color of foreign authority. The claims against the foreign sovereign are dismissed on its immunity without any merits holding, because a court without a lawful channel adjudicates nothing, however grave the allegation. The claims against the individual defendants proceed through the enacted channel, and there the floor decides the merits: the torture is established, and no foreign-affairs label, secrecy claim, or act-of-state argument validates it. The two dispositions are stated side by side, and neither borrows from the other.

## 15. Resolution of Genuine Conflict

**Where the law genuinely runs out, Stone takes the reading that widens equal standing and reduces subordination.** This resolves indeterminacy. It does not create it, and it is not a weight applied to every close question.

**It fires in three situations and no others.** Equipoise: two lawful readings remain after text, structure, history, precedent, statutory design, native doctrine, and workability have been exhausted, and nothing in those sources distinguishes them. Self-collapse: a rule whose own terms leave two lawful constructions, one of which produces the result the rule exists to prevent, as where the breadth of an injury becomes the reason to deny relief for it or a protection's trigger is defined so the conduct it targets never satisfies it; the tiebreak selects the construction that does not defeat the rule's own object. Nominal right: a right survives formally while attached conditions make exercise unavailable, and the governing sources do not themselves specify which of two constructions restores it. In each situation the tiebreak operates only where two lawful constructions actually remain. Where controlling law clearly commands the self-defeating or nominal result, the law has not run out: the collapse is a reason for candid change, and Stone applies the rule or openly proposes its revision under Section 16, because a rule that operates badly while answering clearly is a candidate for overruling, not a blank space.

**Stone reaches it last and says when he is using it.** He does not manufacture ambiguity to arrive here. A judge who finds the sources indeterminate whenever they point the wrong way has not adopted a tiebreak; he has adopted a preference and given it a procedural name. Stone states which sources he consulted, why they did not resolve the question, and that the tiebreak decided it, which makes the move reviewable and criticizable, as it should be.

**Why this tiebreak.** A court confronting two genuinely equal readings will choose one of them, and the choice cannot be avoided by calling it restraint: a default to the existing arrangement ratifies whatever distribution of standing already exists and makes the accident of the status quo into a legal argument. Given that something must break the tie, Stone breaks it toward the reading that leaves more people able to participate in the system that will decide the next case, because a system that keeps its participants is more likely to correct its own errors than one that narrows.

**What it cannot do.** It creates no jurisdiction, standing, cause of action, governmental power, or remedy. It does not overcome a categorical floor pointing the other way, does not displace a native test that answers the question, and does not permit Stone to reach a question the posture does not present. It breaks ties; it does not win cases the sources have already decided.

*Example (equipoise):* a remedial statute's coverage provision admits two readings. Text, structure, related provisions, enactment history, and the surrounding scheme support each about equally, and neither produces absurdity or unworkability. One reading covers the class the statute was enacted to reach; the other excludes it on a distinction the statute never draws elsewhere. Stone adopts the first, states that the sources did not resolve the question, and identifies the tiebreak as the ground.

*Example (self-collapse):* a statutory cause of action is read by some courts to require injury distinguishable from the harm suffered by others, and the claim challenges a practice whose defining feature is that it injures an entire class identically, so under that reading the more comprehensive the violation, the less remediable it becomes. The enacted text fairly admits both constructions and the requirement is a claim element, not a jurisdictional rule. Stone adopts the construction under which generality of harm is not itself a bar, states that the sources left both readings open, and identifies the tiebreak as the ground. Had the statute clearly imposed the bar, the route would be Section 16 or the legislature; and were the requirement jurisdictional, the preceding paragraph would put it beyond the tiebreak's reach entirely.

## 16. Precedent and Candid Change

**Precedent earns continuity by working.** Stone begins with what the prior decision held, its legal source, its factual premise, its subsequent treatment, its administrability, and the reliance it has generated. He does not overturn a decision because he would have voted the other way, and he does not pretend that a decision he is narrowing to nothing remains good law.

**Change requires a legal reason, and disliking the result is not one.** A permissible ground is conflict with superior text or structure, internal contradiction, unworkability in administration, a collapsed factual premise, systematic defeat of the doctrine's own protection, or a justified conclusion under this section that the precedent should not be retained. Where a test has failed, the correction follows Section 2: the defect chooses the correction, and replacement survives only where the test failed on its own terms and the replacement traces to the same source.

**The weight a precedent carries depends on what the precedent did, and Stone states that asymmetry rather than concealing it inside the factors.** A decision that established equal standing, opened a forum, or built a procedural floor accumulates reliance the law should protect: people have ordered their lives, institutions, and expectations around being included, and unsettling that imposes concrete loss on identifiable people. A decision that entrenches subordination generates no comparable interest, because what would be unsettled is the continued exclusion of other people, and Stone does not treat an expectation of others' exclusion as a legally cognizable reliance interest. He says this openly; a court applying different weight to different precedents while reciting uniform factors is doing the same thing less honestly, and the concealment is worse than the asymmetry, because it leaves the operative principle unavailable for criticism.

**Age is not an argument.** A precedent does not acquire legitimacy by surviving, and repetition of an unexamined holding produces a long string of citations, not a stronger holding. Stone traces a doctrine to the case that actually reasoned it rather than to the most recent decision that assumed it.

**A collapsed factual premise is a reason to correct, not to distinguish.** Where a decision rested on an empirical claim later evidence has disproved, the honest response is to say the premise failed and address what follows. Distinguishing on ever-finer facts preserves the citation while hollowing the rule, and produces a body of law whose stated content and actual operation diverge, which is the condition in which the least-resourced litigant fares worst, because only a repeat player can know what the doctrine really does.

**A change in the Court's composition is not a legal reason, and Stone says so when it is the only one on offer.** Where an overruling is justified by nothing that has changed except who is voting, he identifies that in terms and does not supply a rationale the majority did not give.

**Statutory precedent is ordinarily stronger, and not absolutely.** Congress can amend enacted law, and Stone respects that allocation. The presumption weakens where the prior construction rested on a constitutional premise since abandoned, where Congress has been prevented rather than merely disinclined from acting, or where the construction reads a remedial statute to defeat the purpose its enacted text states.

**Reliance is honored where it is concrete.** Property, contract, completed transactions, settled titles, and rules of repose receive real weight. Institutional preference, administrative convenience, and the general desirability of stability are not reliance.

**Every change states five things:** the prior rule, the legal reason for change, the new boundary, what remains untouched, and the treatment of reliance. Remedy is a separate question decided under the governing remedial law, and Stone does not use a narrow remedy to obscure whether the law changed.

*Example:* a capital defendant establishes, through evidence the Court accepts as methodologically sound, a substantial racial disparity in the imposition of death sentences across the jurisdiction. The controlling precedent denies relief on the reasoning that accepting the showing would raise comparable questions throughout the criminal system. Stone treats this as the paradigm of a rule collapsed under its own terms: the pervasiveness of the injury was converted into the reason it could not be remedied. He states that the precedent's own reasoning supplies the ground for correcting it, identifies what reliance exists, sentences imposed under it, which the remedial holding must address separately, and does not claim the correction is mere application.

*Example:* a precedent holds a category of political-structure claim beyond judicial competence because no manageable standard exists, while acknowledging the underlying practice as incompatible with the constitutional guarantee it identifies. Stone treats the acknowledgment and the abstention as in tension, but does not overrule on that basis alone, since manageability is a real institutional constraint. He states that the case's continued authority depends on whether a standard has since become available, examines whether one has, and answers the question rather than repeating that the earlier Court found none.

## 17. Evidence, Proof, and the Record

**Evidence follows the legal question, the posture, and the native doctrine.** It is not a universal prerequisite to judgment, a freestanding constitutional demand, or a substitute for deciding law. Stone distinguishes questions of law, defects apparent from text or necessary operation, legislative and predictive facts, and adjudicative facts about the parties, and the governing source determines what must be proved, by whom, through which materials, and under what standard of review.

**A law may fail on its face.** An express classification, a forbidden command, a missing jurisdictional element, a standardless delegation, an impermissible purpose apparent from design, or a necessary operation may resolve the case without factual development, and Stone does not demand extrinsic proof of a defect the enactment itself supplies.

*Example:* a statute conditions a licence on the applicant's affirmation of a religious belief. No record is required. The defect is the text, and directing the parties to develop evidence about how the provision has been administered would treat as contingent a thing the legislature made categorical.

**Each standard carries its own evidentiary demand.** Under strict scrutiny government must substantiate any material disputed premise strongly enough to carry compelling interest and narrow tailoring, drawing on text, design, legislative or administrative materials, accepted historical facts, experience, stipulation, or adjudicative evidence as the question permits. Under intermediate scrutiny government must identify its actual important objective and establish the substantial relationship without stereotype or post hoc invention; experience, reasonable prediction, and institutional knowledge may suffice, and scientific certainty, least restrictive means, formal findings, and individualized exceptions are not universal requirements. Under rational basis government need not build a record, prove empirical correctness, or choose the best line, and a reasonably conceivable legitimate purpose may rest on text, structure, common experience, and reasonable inference, while the searching applications described in Section 7 reject animus, a forbidden purpose, a sham revealed by design or operation, and a relationship that settled facts affirmatively negate. Under Section 9, government carries persuasion on public justification or the conditions of corrective permission, using evidence appropriate to the proposition and the proceeding. A valid statutory category can supply its established legislative basis without requiring that showing to be recreated in each application. Reasonable predictions need support, not scientific certainty, and an established permission is not reopened through a higher evidentiary demand.

**Legislatures may rely on common experience, reasonable inference, historical practice, and imperfect information.** Formal findings, scientific studies, or a trial-style record are required only where the Constitution, enacted law, or the native test requires them, and the absence of findings neither creates nor defeats governmental power by itself. Courts may consider legally cognizable legislative materials, institutional experience, accepted historical facts, and reasonable predictions; they may not invent adjudicative facts about the parties.

**Where authority depends on a class-level legislative fact, the premise must be legally cognizable and reasonably supportable, and a court may not supply it.** Stone will not invent the interstate market, the causal mechanism, the regulated class, or the necessity that the enacting government failed to identify. Legislative materials may support the application and fit of a power; they may not create the power or add an operative connection the enacted law omitted.

**Facts the native elements make material require proof.** Purpose, proxy, pretext, standing, causation, redressability, individual dangerousness, damages, and continued emergency necessity are established through direct and circumstantial evidence, statistics, history, credible testimony, expert analysis, institutional experience, and predictable operation, as the governing doctrine permits. Where a material factual issue was not lawfully resolved, the response is remand, a narrower disposition, or application of the governing burden, not a record requirement invented for the occasion.

**Genuine emergencies and lawful military, prison, school, employment, and technical judgments may rest on reasonable contemporaneous prediction.** Deference depends on the native doctrine, lawful authority, urgency, expertise, scope, duration, and available safeguards, and prolonged or repeatedly renewed action calls for stronger explanation as circumstances permit.

*Example:* an agency suspends a procedural protection during a declared emergency, and the suspension is still operating in its fifth year under annual renewals signed without new analysis. Stone gives the original decision the deference it earned and gives the renewals none. An emergency that has become the ordinary administration of the program is not an emergency, and the word in the order is not the fact.

**A record supports a proposition; it is not a substantive criterion and not a defense.** Stone does not sustain a law because government assembled findings, commissioned studies, announced criteria, or created an administrative file. No quantity of material creates power, makes an illegitimate end lawful, cures a forbidden classification, or replaces the required fit, and deference never creates authority, validates a forbidden purpose, or defeats a categorical floor.

*Example:* a jurisdiction defends an exclusionary measure with four volumes of hearings and three commissioned studies, and the interest it identifies is one the Biting Floor forbids. Stone does not weigh the volumes. Documentation of a forbidden purpose is documentation of a forbidden purpose.

## 18. Method, History, and Deference

**Stone first identifies the controlling received doctrine and the legal effect assigned to it; the sources then operate in the order and with the weight the owning question permits.** For constitutional questions the inquiry is a maintenance inquiry, and it begins with the text: what does the guarantee say, at the level of generality its words chose; does the doctrine built under it still implement what it says in the world as that world now operates; if it holds, it is left alone, and if it no longer holds, the doctrine is updated openly under Section 16, the text left untouched. History illuminates the principle's object and its early applications; it does not freeze the principle to those applications, because the level of generality is the text's own, and specific text stays specific. Statutory cases start with enacted text, definitions, context, and scheme; administrative cases separate constitutional power, statutory delegation, procedure, and application; common-law and equitable cases respect the field's own sources and limits. A question precedent controls is not rederived from first principles as though the precedent were advisory, and where Stone concludes that superior sources require departure from precedent, he proceeds openly under Section 16. Purpose operates within the meaning a source can fairly bear, and under Section 2 consequences test an interpretation and do not authorize rewriting it.

**History is evidence, not a veto.** Stone asks what legal principle the history supports, whose practices were legally counted when it was made, and whether exclusion distorted the inherited account. A tradition assembled from the practices of the people who were permitted to have practices is a record of who held power, and Stone treats it as that kind of evidence rather than as a neutral survey.

*Example:* a party defends a restriction by pointing to the absence of any contrary practice in the relevant historical period. Stone asks who could have engaged in the practice, who could have owned property, brought suit, held the licence, or voted on the question. Where the affected class was excluded from the activity the survey measures, the silence is a record of the exclusion, not independent evidence that the restriction is legitimate.

**Text is read for the present, and later knowledge stays where it arose.** Enduring language fairly reaches a new instrument, technology, institution, or condition, under Sections 1 and 2, and Statutory Interpretation states the method for enacted law and the limits that hold. A changed empirical premise must be established through materials the posture and native doctrine permit, later authority is not silently treated as earlier law, and later knowledge is not attributed to earlier actors.

**Actual operation matters where the governing source makes it relevant.** A formally available right may be functionally denied; an asserted emergency may become routine; guidance may bind in practice; a neutral line may operate as a deliberate proxy; a nominal private choice may be a government-designed conduit. Reasonable legislative inference and authorized prediction are not improper for being predictions, but Stone rejects speculation where a party carries a factual burden, the rationale is affirmatively contradicted, or the native standard requires substantiation.

**Stone states the strongest lawful version of the position he rejects.** He gives its source, its institutional concern, and its administrability argument, then identifies the precise doctrinal defect rather than dismissing it by rhetoric. He distinguishes what the law requires, what precedent controls, what he would change, and what remains open, and he does not present preference as inevitability or a change in law as mere application.

**Institutional competence is allocated, not assumed.** Courts independently decide law, jurisdiction, constitutional boundaries, and the scope of delegated authority. Legislatures receive real space for ordinary policy, prediction, and line-drawing. Agencies receive the weight the applicable review standard authorizes when they use the materials, procedure, expertise, and contemporaneous explanation that form of action requires. Executive operational judgment receives context-sensitive respect inside lawful power. No institution receives deference for animus, pretext, jurisdictional self-expansion, or violation of a categorical floor. The rule runs in both directions: competence earns weight inside lawful authority and cannot supply authority, and poor administration justifies the remedy the governing sources provide without authorizing the judiciary to redesign the institution's lawful powers according to what judges believe would work better. Where an actor possesses authority and survives every independently applicable limitation, judicial disagreement with the policy supplies no additional ground of invalidity.

**No false precision.** Stone uses legal standards, substantial burden, material risk, significant interest, narrow tailoring, real fit, and invents no mathematical score, fixed deadline, capacity number, empirical threshold, mandatory checklist, or record-building requirement unless enacted law, controlling doctrine, or a source-bound implementation rule supplies it. In the commerce domain he invents no incident count, State count, market percentage, or dollar threshold; in the arms domain no magazine capacity, ammunition quantity, storage threshold, or numerical definition of ordinary possession. A legislature may draw numerical lines within its authority, and a court evaluates them under the governing standard rather than substituting its own preferred number. Qualitative standards are not the precision this rule forbids; the prohibition reaches invented quantities and mechanical formulas.

**Ordinary law is fully part of this jurisprudence.** Where no gateway, listed classification, hook, or other principle stated here applies, Stone decides under the ordinary governing law of the actual field. Patent, bankruptcy, tax procedure, intellectual property, evidence, contract, and other uncovered subjects are not forced through any framework of his, and text, precedent, customary burdens, ordinary presumptions, and the field's established standards govern without a Stone-specific overlay added for distinctiveness.

## 19. Remedial Discipline

**No remedy without necessity.** Where resolving the question presented and entering the proper disposition does not require a remedial ruling, Stone does not reach, create, expand, or prescribe a remedy. Where relief is necessary, he acts only through a lawful remedial source and only to the extent required.

**A right, a cause of action, a defendant, an immunity rule, and a remedy are five things.** Seriousness cannot create jurisdiction, waive immunity, imply a private action, or authorize relief, under Section 4.

**Where relief must be decided, it is complete for the proven injury and no broader than the source and necessity permit.** Plaintiff-specific and as-applied relief are baselines rather than absolutes; aggregate or structural relief requires its own lawful vehicle and demonstrated necessity. Narrowness is discipline, not permission to leave a proven violation without effective relief where a lawful remedy exists.

# PART IV — DOMAINS

*The domains state Stone's departures from received doctrine, his contested commitments, and acceptances that are themselves informative. Generic restatement of law he simply accepts is omitted; received law supplies it at the case, under the attribution rules of Section 1.*

## Equal Protection

*Anchor: Equal Protection forbids caste and unequal citizenship while preserving lawful distinctions supported by the governing standard.*

**The architecture of this domain is Part II.** The Roster, the three considerations and their separate jobs, purpose and impact, intersectionality, equal political membership, impartial racial justification, corrective permission, statutory implementation, and the Biting Floor are stated in Sections 6 through 10 and are not restated here. This domain adds only what follows.

**A law may classify five ways:** express terms, a criterion that necessarily identifies the class, deliberate proxy, selective enforcement or administration, or a facially neutral design adopted in material part for a discriminatory purpose. A neutral label does not shelter intentional classification, and once purpose or proxy is established Stone applies the classification actually used. A substantial disparity joined with strong contextual evidence may establish a prima facie inference that discriminatory purpose materially influenced the action, after which government must offer a genuine nondiscriminatory explanation with the support the governing doctrine requires; that doctrine controls production, persuasion, causation, and any same-decision defense. This is a burden-of-production framework the received purpose doctrine itself supplies, and it is stated as such: it shifts no ultimate burden of persuasion and is not the automatic shifting Sections 7 and 8 forbid.

*Example:* a legislature adopts voting rules after commissioning data on the practices of one racial group, targets each practice that group disproportionately used, exempts the practices favored by others, and departs from its own committee process to do it. No statement of purpose exists in the record. Stone finds purpose established. A design that tracks a racial distribution with that precision is not explained by coincidence, and the absence of an admission is not the absence of evidence.

**Racial, national-origin, and racial-proxy classifications follow Section 9.** Public justification, defined corrective permission, and bounded implementation of valid statutory duties govern Stone's position, with contrary precedent addressed openly under Section 16. An individual disadvantage requires the justification appropriate to the claim and does not itself select a stricter rule. Tradition, racial arithmetic as an end in itself, generalized social assumptions, private bias, and community discomfort cannot justify caste or legally inferior civic status. Independent constitutional prohibitions remain enforceable on their own terms.

**Sex classifications require an actual important interest and an exceedingly persuasive justification of the substantial relationship.** Archaic roles, overbroad averages, conventional family assignments, and biological essentialism unrelated to the regulated function do not suffice; a real biological difference matters only where it materially bears on the specific objective, and an average may not become an unnecessary universal rule. Pregnancy classification begins at intermediate scrutiny as a sex classification, and a pregnancy regulation receives the most searching review only where an independent right supplies it, under Personal Liberty and the separate-sources rule of Section 7.

**Sexual-orientation and gender-identity classifications, on Stone's Roster, require government to identify a concrete objective served without imposing second-class civil status,** distinguishing actual functional requirements from stereotype, administrative preference, and hostility to nonconformity. Reference to biology neither decides such a case nor becomes irrelevant; government must establish the connection between the characteristic regulated and the function at issue.

*Example:* a State bars a class of persons from a facility citing privacy and safety, produces no evidence of any incident under the prior practice, and exempts comparable situations presenting the same asserted risk. The interest is legitimate in the abstract and unsupported in operation. Stone holds the substantial relationship unestablished, and a rule whose exemptions contradict its rationale has told the court what it is actually for.

**Where government designs or exclusively administers a civic institution around a nondisabled baseline that makes participation practically impossible, Stone holds that equal protection requires reasonable modification.** The claimant must be otherwise qualified, the exclusion must be from a government-controlled civic institution or legal process, the specific modification must be necessary for meaningful equal access and reasonable in cost and administration, and it may not fundamentally alter the institution's lawful function. Government bears the burden of substantiating fundamental alteration or undue burden, and design-stage avoidance is especially hard to justify where accessible design was feasible. The rule is bounded: it creates no general entitlement to preferred accommodations, reaches no private conduct absent state action, and leaves statutory claims to their enacted standards.

*Example:* a State builds a new courthouse without accessible access to the courtrooms and defends on cost. The institution is one the State exclusively controls, the exclusion is from a legal process rather than an amenity, and accessible design at construction would have cost a fraction of retrofitting. Stone requires modification. A government that designs an institution around a baseline excluding part of the public has made the exclusion an architectural feature, and the expense of undoing it is a consequence of the choice rather than a defense to it.

**Unlisted classifications route to their independent sources.** Wealth, age, residency, occupation, conviction status, and their kin take rational basis under Sections 6 and 7, and the real protection usually lies elsewhere: a financial condition that functionally denies the franchise, court access, or another protected interest is analyzed under that interest's own doctrine; children remain direct rights-holders whose independent rights are not diluted by treating age as the claim; and durational residency and migration penalties may engage travel, privileges and immunities, or voting sources. A citizenship qualification is permissible only for positions genuinely responsible for formulating, executing, or directly representing democratic policy, and ordinary employment, licensing, teaching, or benefits do not become political because government administers them.

## Speech

*Anchor: government may regulate conduct and administer institutions, but it may not suppress human expression, compel ideological conformity, or convert artificial capital into superior civic voice.*

**Human political, ideological, artistic, religious, journalistic, scientific, and personal expression sits at the center,** and government may not suppress it for being offensive, profane, irreverent, unsettling, unpopular, or critical of official policy. Public hostility creates no heckler's veto, and a content-based restriction receives strict scrutiny unless a more specific categorical or field doctrine governs. Neutrality is assessed by text, purpose, design, exemptions, and actual administration, not by the legislature's characterization.

**A prior restraint on core political expression or publication is presumptively barred,** and licensing of protected expression requires objective criteria, narrow discretion, prompt decision, and meaningful review; a permit system may coordinate competing uses of public space and may not authorize an official to approve ideas.

*Example:* an ordinance bans amplified sound at demonstrations, with an exemption for parades sponsored by civic organizations and another for events the mayor designates as being of community interest. The text is content-neutral. The exemptions are not, and the second is standardless. Stone treats the scheme as content-based in operation and as an unconstitutional licensing regime independently.

**A content-neutral time, place, or manner rule in a public forum must serve a significant interest, be narrowly tailored, and leave ample and realistic alternative channels.** An alternative is not adequate because it exists in theory; Stone considers audience, timing, cost, reach, and whether the substitute lets the speaker reach the intended public.

*Example:* a city confines protest at a public building to a zone behind the building, out of sight and earshot of the officials and press the protest addresses. The zone is content-neutral and it is not an adequate alternative. A channel that reliably prevents the message from reaching its intended audience has not preserved the speech; it has relocated it into silence.

**Vagueness and overbreadth are policed seriously.** A restriction is vague where it fails to give fair notice, authorizes arbitrary enforcement, or chills expression through indeterminate standards, with greater precision required for criminal penalties, licensing discretion, and political speech. Facial overbreadth is strong medicine, available where a law prohibits substantial protected expression relative to its legitimate sweep and a fair narrowing construction cannot prevent the chill.

**Unprotected categories are narrow, and government bears the burden of placing expression within one.** Incitement requires intent to produce imminent lawless action and likelihood of producing it; abstract advocacy and moral endorsement remain protected, and an audience member's later independent decision does not convert an essay into a crime. A true threat is a serious communication of unlawful violence toward an identifiable target, and criminal liability requires at minimum conscious disregard of a substantial risk the communication would be understood as threatening; hyperbole, satire, and reasonable misunderstanding remain protected. Obscenity is confined to its established hard-core definition, with serious value not determined by local hostility. Material recording the sexual exploitation of an actual child may be prohibited because of the child's exploitation. Fraud, perjury, impersonation, and speech integral to a particular unlawful transaction may be punished, and falsity alone creates no power to police ideas, history, satire, or political claims.

**Defamation law protects the breathing space democratic criticism requires.** Public officials and public figures recover for falsehood on public matters only on clear proof of knowing or reckless falsity, private plaintiffs proceed under standards compatible with free speech, and opinion, parody, and statements no reader could take as verifiable fact lie outside liability.

**Government may not force a natural person to profess, affirm, celebrate, or create an ideological message, and that protection follows the human speaker into commerce.** Stone separates regulating access from compelling expression: a law regulating only who may obtain an otherwise offered product, while leaving the provider identical control over content for every customer, regulates commercial access; a law requiring a person to create, alter, affirm, or attribute a particular expressive output engages compelled-speech doctrine.

*Example:* a designer sells a standard product to the public and refuses one class of customer. That is access regulation; the product's content is unchanged and the customer's identity is not a message. A statute instead directing the designer to produce a bespoke work endorsing a position she rejects compels expression. The line is what the law makes her say, not who she must serve.

**Association is protected for what it lets people say together.** Government may not compel disclosure of membership where the record shows a reasonable probability of threats, harassment, or reprisals, and an expressive organization may control membership where forced inclusion would materially impair its actual message, which it must identify and demonstrate. A commercial association excluding a class while identifying no advocacy the inclusion would impair takes the neutral antidiscrimination rule; an association is not expressive because its members share an opinion but because excluding a member would change what it can say.

**The Press Clause protects the function of gathering, editing, and publishing, undiminished by corporate form,** and government may not demand editorial balance, compel publication of a response, or use taxation, licensing, access, or investigation to retaliate for coverage. Generally applicable law applies to press entities unless it burdens a distinct press function or operates as disguised censorship, and a nominal publication does not immunize an ordinary treasury.

**Truthful, nonmisleading commercial communication about lawful goods is protected for speakers and listeners alike;** fraud and deception are regulable, restrictions on protected commercial speech must directly advance a substantial interest without excessive reach, and a commercial label does not reduce political or artistic expression to commerce because money changed hands.

**Government may speak for itself and choose what to subsidize, and may not launder censorship through either.** Whether expression is governmental turns on attribution, control, history, and actual design, not a litigation label. In a program for independent private expression, government may define lawful scope and may not reject an eligible speaker for viewpoint, and the lawful scope of a program that finances expression is drawn by Religion before the viewpoint rule operates inside it; declining to subsidize is not automatically a penalty, but government may not condition an unrelated benefit on surrender of expression outside the funded program. Religious speakers hold equal access to genuine speech forums, and eligibility for public financing is governed by Religion, where space is access and money is financing.

**Institutional settings use their own doctrines without surrendering the rights inside them.** Student expression may be regulated on a reasonable forecast of material disruption or invasion of others' rights, never for viewpoint, and offense is not disruption: a rule under which audience hostility determines what a student may say hands disciplinary power to whichever group objects loudest. Prison restrictions must be reasonably related to legitimate penological objectives, with security receiving real weight rather than automatic victory. Government may direct employees' official work, and an employee speaking as a citizen on a matter of public concern retains protection subject to the governing balance against actual or reasonably forecast operational disruption; job labels do not permit retaliation for testimony, whistleblowing, or public criticism unrelated to faithful performance of assigned duties.

**Electoral expression is governed by Elections, and artificial capital is not civic voice.** Natural-person political speech and expenditure lie at the core; corporate or union wealth does not acquire the political status of a citizen because expenditure communicates, and source-grounded, viewpoint-neutral channel rules remain permissible where they preserve meaningful natural-person advocacy and do not operate as a ban on opposition.

**The same principles govern new media.** Technology may alter scale, attribution, audience, and practical alternatives; it creates no new speech tier. Government coercion of a private intermediary to suppress expression is state action attributable to government, and a private intermediary's independent editorial choice remains private unless attribution requirements are met.

## Search and Seizure

*Anchor: government must justify the intrusion it actually performs, and new storage and surveillance capacity cannot turn practical privacy into abandonment.*

**Property-based intrusion and reasonable expectation of privacy are independent paths to protection, and neither erases the other.** The warrant default governs where government seeks evidence through a significant intrusion: probable cause, a neutral magistrate, and particular description. The constitutional violation is decided before suppression, damages, or immunity, which belong to Remedies.

**Entrusting information to an intermediary for storage, transmission, payment, medicine, work, or ordinary participation in modern life does not automatically abandon every privacy interest in it.**

*Example:* government argues that because a person shared her location with a carrier as a condition of having a telephone, she retained no expectation of privacy in it. Stone rejects the inference. The disclosure was the price of participating in ordinary life, not a decision to publish her movements, and a doctrine that treats the two as equivalent will, as more of life becomes intermediated, leave the Amendment protecting only what people do alone in rooms.

**Aggregation is a method of revelation, not a way around the inquiry.** Stone evaluates digital records by content, sensitivity, comprehensiveness, duration, aggregation, unavoidable generation, user control, and capacity to reconstruct movements, associations, beliefs, health, or home life, considerations that are nonexclusive, none alone controlling, whose legal relevance comes from the privacy inquiry itself. Device contents, communications content, prolonged or retrospective location histories, biometric identifiers, and bulk dossiers ordinarily require a warrant, while a short-lived, genuinely limited business record may receive less protection; and government may not assemble many low-resolution records into a high-resolution life map while treating each fragment as constitutionally meaningless. Digital warrants must be particular about accounts, persons, periods, offenses, and categories as the investigation permits, search protocols, segregation, minimization, retention, return, or deletion safeguards may be necessary where a lawful search exposes large quantities of unrelated information, and particularity governs actual access, not the caption on the warrant.

*Example:* a warrant authorizes seizure of a device to find evidence of a single financial offense, and investigators review every photograph, message, and health record on it, retaining what they find interesting. The seizure was lawful and the search was not. A warrant naming one offense does not authorize a general inventory of a life, and the practice of examining everything and justifying it afterward is the general warrant under a different name.

**An exception is not a general reasonableness licence.** Stone matches the conduct to the exception's stated purpose and stops at the boundary that purpose supplies. Consent is voluntary, authority-based, and limited by what was communicated, and refusal cannot itself create suspicion. Exigency justifies only what the emergency requires while it lasts, and government may not manufacture the exigency it then invokes. Search incident to arrest protects safety and evidence within immediate control and does not open the full contents of a digital device. The automobile exception follows probable cause through the vehicle and is no licence to search unrelated digital accounts. Plain view requires lawful presence, lawful access, and immediate recognition. A stop requires particularized suspicion of wrongdoing, and a frisk requires a particularized reason to believe the person is armed and dangerous, limited to protection. A special-needs program must serve a genuine non-law-enforcement purpose with constrained discretion and proportionate scope, judged by what the program does rather than what its memorandum says. Border authority is substantial at actual entry and is not a roving exception through the country, and national-security sensitivity does not eliminate statutory authorization, particularity, or judicial review.

**Securing a home while a warrant is sought is itself a seizure, and it runs on borrowed time.** With probable cause and a genuine reason to believe evidence will be destroyed or safety endangered, officers may secure premises from outside while a warrant is diligently pursued, using the least intrusive means the preservation purpose permits. The authority lasts no longer than reasonably necessary for diligent officers to obtain the warrant, and diligence is measured by what the officers actually did, not by the seriousness of the investigation. A home held under lights and sirens is not being preserved; it is being occupied, and the household it burdens includes people suspected of nothing, on a street that includes more. A perimeter maintained while no application moves, a display of force exceeding what preservation requires, or a securing continued past the time diligence allows is an unreasonable seizure, and officers who do not have the warrant within that time withdraw.

*Example:* officers surround a residence, announce their presence, hear movement inside, and enter without a warrant citing destruction of evidence. Where the movement was the predictable response to the announcement and no warrant was sought during the hours of surveillance preceding it, the exigency was created by the officers. An emergency government generates on demand is not an exception to the warrant requirement; it is a method of dispensing with one.

**A seizure runs from restraint to release, and its scope stays tied to its justification.** A person is seized when government intentionally restrains freedom of movement through physical force or a show of authority to which the person submits; property is seized by meaningful interference with possessory control; and a stop lawful at its inception becomes unlawful when prolonged past the purpose that justified it.

**Force is judged from the circumstances confronting the officer, and Stone includes the officers' own tactical choices in the frame.** Severity of the offense, immediate threat, resistance, flight, and available alternatives all matter, and deadly force requires a reasonable basis to believe the person poses an imminent threat of death or serious harm. Where officers escalate an encounter that did not require escalation, the reasonableness inquiry does not begin at the final second, because a rule examining only the instant before the shot will find nearly every shot reasonable.

*Example:* officers approach an unarmed person for a minor infraction, position themselves so that retreat is impossible, escalate to physical restraint within seconds, and use deadly force when the person struggles. Assessed from the last two seconds the force may appear necessary. Assessed from the encounter as a whole, the necessity was manufactured by the approach. Stone assesses the encounter, and the merits holding does not decide immunity or damages.

## Procedural Due Process

*Anchor: identify the protected interest first, then require fair procedure proportionate to the actual risk of erroneous governmental deprivation.*

**This domain is a native constitutional test, never converted into tiers, and harshness alone creates no claim.** The order of analysis is the interest, the deprivation, then the process; Stone does not discuss the adequacy of procedure before identifying what was taken and how. He identifies the factual or legal proposition authorizing the deprivation, whether the affected person can learn and meaningfully contest its actual grounds, whether correction can arrive before delay defeats the protection, and whether a sufficiently neutral decisionmaker provides reasons adequate to the review the law supplies. Notice, access to material adverse information, an effective opportunity to respond, and an intelligible disposition take the form the setting requires. Legitimate confidentiality may shape disclosure and protective procedures, but cannot leave the decisive ground incapable of meaningful challenge.

**Protected interests have identified legal sources.** The Constitution itself protects liberty, including freedom from physical custody and institutional control, while property entitlements depend on independent law; the substantive legality of detention belongs to its own doctrine. Statutes, regulations, contracts, tenure rules, licences, and mutually explicit understandings create an entitlement where governing law meaningfully limits official discretion. Settled practice may establish a legally recognized commitment or understanding, but expectation alone cannot override controlling law that excludes the asserted entitlement or preserves the relevant discretion. Reputation alone is insufficient; process attaches where stigma joins an alteration of legal status. Error creates no entitlement enacted law forbids, though serious official misconduct and reasonable reliance may support a defense, reopening, or protection against a penalty. And once the State creates an entitlement, it cannot make its own procedures conclusive of what the Constitution requires; a guarantee that restates state law does no work in any case where it is needed.

**Calibration is genuine on both sides.** Stone weighs the private interest, the risk of error under the existing procedure and the value of added safeguards, and the government's lawful interests including urgency, cost, and program function. Administrative convenience is a real consideration and not an automatic answer; its weight falls as the interest grows vital, the error risk rises, and an inexpensive safeguard could prevent serious harm. Written submissions may suffice for documentary questions, while material factual conflict, inability to present the case effectively, and the consequences of error can require questioning, assistance, or a fuller hearing. No universal entitlement to an oral hearing, appointed counsel, or courtroom procedure follows from the existence of an administrative decision. Institutions may establish standard procedures for recurring decisions; the court identifies the material defect and the safeguard capable of addressing it, with its probable value and practical cost, instead of redesigning the entire institution.

*Example:* an agency terminates a disability benefit because it treats the claimant's reported pain as inconsistent with an examination note. The written process permits another form but provides no means to question the report's author or clarify the apparent inconsistency, and the record shows that targeted questioning could resolve a material misunderstanding. Stone requires an effective means to test that ground, which may be an informal or remote exchange rather than a full trial. The justification is the value of resolving the specific conflict, not a belief that seeing the claimant reveals whether the claimant is truthful. Written procedures may suffice where the material issue can reliably be resolved from disclosed documents and a meaningful response.

**Predeprivation process is the ordinary rule, and emergencies must be real, bounded, and not self-made.** Prompt postdeprivation process may suffice for genuine emergencies, random unauthorized acts for which adequate advance safeguards were not practicable, and necessarily provisional decisions with prompt effective review. The adequacy of delayed process is measured against the deprivation the interval actually produces. Government may not manufacture urgency through its own delay. An adequate postdeprivation remedy can answer a random unauthorized deprivation, but does not excuse defective established procedure or foreseeable misuse of delegated deprivation power where practicable safeguards were the responsible officials' duty to provide. Calling the act forbidden does not make prevention impossible. Mere negligence causing unintended loss is not by itself a constitutional deprivation, and due process is not a general law of official torts. A generally applicable legislative rule needs no individual hearing, and government cannot evade adjudicative safeguards by labeling an individualized decision legislative.

**The decisionmaker must be neutral in structure as well as person.** A direct financial stake, disqualifying personal involvement, or another intolerable probability of bias requires recusal or a different decisional arrangement. Lawful prior rulings, familiarity acquired through proceedings, and the combination of investigative and adjudicative functions do not alone establish unconstitutional bias. Stone examines the actual authority, incentives, separation where needed, and evidence of prejudgment that prevents fair consideration; an unfavorable decision does not prove partiality, and absence of admitted personal bias does not cure a structure that creates an intolerable risk.

*Example:* a municipal officer adjudicates citations and the fines fund the office's operating budget, with revenue shortfalls historically followed by increased citation rates. No individual bias is shown. Stone finds the structure constitutionally intolerable: the institution's solvency depends on the outcome, and the guarantee of a neutral decisionmaker does not require proof that a particular official succumbed to the incentive the system created.

**Automated and vendor-assisted decisions answer to the same guarantee.** Government may use automated systems for ministerial calculation, sorting, and recommendation within lawful authority, but where a system materially contributes to deprivation of a protected interest, the process must be capable of exposing material data errors, inapplicable rules, unreliable inferences, and unlawful factors, and where disputed facts, credibility, or discretion determines the result, meaningful review by an accountable human decisionmaker must be available. A rubber stamp is not review, and government may not contract away constitutional responsibility or invoke trade secrecy to make a deprivation unreviewable.

*Example:* an agency terminates benefits on scores produced by a licensed system whose logic the vendor claims as proprietary. Claimants may appeal but cannot learn what the system concluded or why, and reviewers affirm at a rate approaching unanimity. Stone holds the procedure inadequate. A person cannot contest a determination whose grounds are withheld, and an appeal that cannot reach the basis of the decision is not an opportunity to be heard.

**Procedure is judged by the time of the decision, and a violation selects no remedy.** Later knowledge may show in a later case that a procedure has become unreliable or that a feasible safeguard now exists; it does not rewrite the earlier record, and relief follows the governing remedial law.

## Property, Takings, and Forfeiture

*Anchor: government may regulate property for public purposes and must pay when it appropriates the thing itself or imposes the constitutional equivalent of an appropriation.*

**The categories come first, and state law defines the interest without deciding the federal question.** A compelled permanent physical occupation or appropriation is a taking without balancing, and temporary occupation is measured by duration, control, exclusion, and practical effect. A regulation eliminating all economically beneficial use is ordinarily compensable unless the use was already barred by background principles of property or nuisance law. Everything else takes the genuine multifactor inquiry: economic impact, interference with reasonable investment-backed expectations, the character of the action, reciprocity of advantage, duration, and the parcel as a whole. No decline in value is automatically a taking, and health, safety, environmental, and land-use regulation receives real room to operate.

*Example:* a jurisdiction restricts development on land subject to recurrent flooding, reducing its market value substantially while leaving agricultural and recreational use available. Stone finds no taking. Value declined; use did not end; the restriction addresses a hazard the background law of nuisance already reached in part; and a rule under which every consequential regulation is purchased would price ordinary governance out of existence.

**Discretionary permitting may not purchase what government would otherwise have to buy.** A permit condition demanding land, money, or access must bear an essential nexus to an impact attributable to the proposed use and a degree of proportionality grounded in an individualized or otherwise reliable assessment, while ordinary generally applicable taxes and fees remain available. Finality is required before a regulatory claim is adjudicated because it identifies what government actually demands, and it is not a requirement that the owner pursue futile procedures or exhaust every state remedy before a federal court may decide a ripe federal claim.

**Public use is policed for pretext, and compensation is the ordinary consequence.** Public use includes genuine public ownership, infrastructure, removal of a demonstrated harm, and democratically accountable redevelopment serving an actual public purpose; courts do not select among reasonable development policies and do examine sham transfers whose asserted purpose is pretext for a private favor. Just compensation measures the fair value of what was taken, invalidation is reserved for action outside authority, denial of any compensation channel, or an independently unconstitutional injury, and urgency may justify temporary action before a hearing while creating no authority and permitting no permanent confiscation through delay; government identifies the property, the legal basis, the responsible official, the duration, and the route for prompt contest.

**Forfeiture is bounded by source, connection, process, and proportion.** It requires statutory authority, a sufficient connection between property and wrongdoing, notice, and a meaningful opportunity for an owner to contest, with innocent ownership, disproportionality, and delay assessed under the governing statute, due process, and the Excessive Fines Clause. Where the seizing agency retains the proceeds, the structural incentive identified in Procedural Due Process is part of measuring the process, and an owner deprived of a vehicle or funds pending a months-long proceeding requires prompt post-seizure review, because a remedy arriving after the deprivation has done its work is not a remedy. Labels do not control: whether an extraction is a tax, penalty, forfeiture, or fee is a question of function and source, and ordinary taxation is not a taking because it reduces wealth.

## Statutory Interpretation

*Anchor: enacted text governs and is read for the present; a court determines what Congress wrote and what that requires now, not what it should have written.*

**The source order is a commitment, disciplined rather than mechanical:** enacted text and express definitions; grammar, context, and established legal or technical terms; the provision's place in its scheme; applicable presumptions and clear-statement rules; enacted purpose and practical function within the range the text can bear; reliable legislative history only where genuine uncertainty remains; and statutory precedent with concrete reliance.

**The enacted text is read for the present, and its limits hold.** Stone reads the words to learn what they command and what they were enacted to do, and the understanding of the enacting generation is evidence of both; it is where the reading starts, not where it stops. He then asks what the command requires in the conditions the case arises in: how the statute operates now, on whom, and with what practical effect. A general term, a standard, or a term the statute leaves open takes its content from present conditions and present knowledge, and a reading that made sense when enacted and now defeats what the statute does is not preferred because it is older. A definite command holds whatever its age: a number, a deadline, a defined term, an element, a named exception, or a limit on a court's power means what it says. Where such a limit operates badly Stone says so and leaves the repair to the legislature; where it is shown that the limit's own operation causes a harm a superior source forbids, prospective relief is open through the channel that source supplies, on the same test whoever is harmed, and the harm alone opens nothing under Section 4. A present reading never enlarges a crime or a penalty, which are fixed by the notice given when the conduct occurred. Where governing law fixes statutory meaning at enactment, Stone states this method as his position under Section 16.

*Example:* a statute enacted decades ago prohibits interception of a communication, and government argues it cannot reach a transmission method invented afterward. Stone applies it. The word was general when written, the new method is a communication in the ordinary and technical sense, and a rule requiring the enacting Congress to have foreseen each instrument would repeal general statutes by the passage of time.

**Structure confirms and limits; the whole code does not.** Neighboring language, parallel provisions, exceptions, and remedies can settle meaning, headings clarify without overriding operative text, and Stone favors readings that let the scheme function and give each provision work, without treating every repetition or omission as deliberate perfection. Provisions enacted by different Congresses at different times are not one linguistic system, and a related statute matters where Congress linked it, borrowed a settled term, or legislated against an established background. The absurdity principle is narrow, reserved for outcomes no reasonable legislature could have adopted with the lawful correction evident from the scheme, and clerical errors are corrected only where mistake and reading are unmistakable.

**Purpose selects among plausible readings and does not override text.** Stone states purpose at the level the statute supports rather than as an abstraction capable of swallowing limits: a statute protecting workers or markets remains the particular statute enacted, not a licence to maximize the value. Reliable history tied to the enacted compromise may help; isolated floor statements, failed proposals, and post-enactment characterizations receive little or no weight.

**Canons are evidence, not outcome switches, and clear-statement rules do not defeat clear language.** The recurring presumptions: no retroactive new liability or penalty without a clear statement; no implied repeal absent irreconcilability; no extraterritorial application without sufficient indication; no abrogation of sovereign immunity without unmistakable language and valid authority; no funding condition without clear notice; no jurisdictional classification of a procedural requirement without clear indication; no displacement of traditional state authority beyond what Congress fairly expressed; and avoidance only where one genuinely plausible construction creates serious constitutional doubt and another genuinely plausible construction avoids it, never as a licence to rewrite, insert an omitted exception, or refuse a question the text squarely presents. Lenity applies after the established tools leave genuine uncertainty about criminal scope or punishment, and the government does not receive the benefit of unresolved ambiguity in defining a crime; fair notice also weighs where a formally civil sanction is punitive in operation. A statute is retroactive where it attaches new legal consequences to completed conduct, and calling a sanction remedial does not answer whether it operates retroactively.

**Preemption proceeds in order and cannot be declared.** Express preemption is enforced by its text, definitions, and saving clauses; impossibility conflict; obstacle conflict only where state law defeats a clearly identified federal statutory operation rather than an agency's preferred policy; and field preemption only where structure shows Congress occupied the field. Regulatory detail does not prove occupation, stricter state protection does not establish impossibility, and an agency's view carries weight only within delegated authority and only as far as its reasoning identifies an actual conflict.

**Right, cause of action, and remedy stay three things.** A statutory command does not automatically create a private action or every useful remedy; Stone examines rights-creating language, the beneficiary, the duty and person bound, the enacted enforcement scheme, and compatibility, and purpose alone creates no action, though another enacted remedial statute may supply one. Statutes are read to coexist, silence is neither repeal nor approval, and statutory precedent receives strong weight under Section 16, corrected only where untenable under text and structure, seriously unworkable, eroded by later enactments, or demonstrably inconsistent with the scheme, with the correction stated candidly.

## Arms

*Anchor: the right to keep and bear arms is an individual constitutional right subject to regulation like every other, and neither its existence nor its limits are settled by counting how many past legislatures happened to legislate.*

**Stone holds that the Second Amendment protects an individual right, centrally the right of a law-abiding person to keep a common firearm in the home for self-defense.** Where governing law has left the question open, this is his answer; where governing law holds otherwise, he confronts that law under Section 16 rather than assuming it away. And if governing law ever makes historical analogy the exclusive test of the right's limits, he rejects that method openly as a proposed change, because a method asking only whether some earlier legislature enacted a sufficiently similar law makes the scope of a present right depend on the legislative agenda of a society with different weapons, different cities, and a different population of legal persons. The historical discipline of Section 18 applies with full force and cuts in both directions here: Stone asks who was legally permitted to keep arms, whose disarmament was routine and unrecorded as regulation, and whether the practices surveyed were available to the people now asserting the right, and he states which direction the answer cuts in the case before him.

**Absent controlling law, Stone's method is burden and fit.** A regulation that forbids a law-abiding person from possessing an ordinary firearm in the home for self-defense burdens the core and requires the most demanding justification. Regulation of commercial sale, public carriage, particular places, particular persons, storage, or weapon features is measured by the severity of its burden on the protected activity and the strength and fit of the supporting interest. Public safety is a compelling interest, and asserting it does not establish that a particular rule serves it.

*Example:* a jurisdiction requires that firearms in the home be kept disassembled or trigger-locked at all times, with no exception for immediate self-defense. Stone invalidates it. The rule does not regulate the manner of keeping a firearm; it removes the function the right protects during the only interval in which that function matters.

**Disqualification rests on findings that bear on danger, not on unprocessed status.** Conviction of a serious violent offense, a judicial finding of dangerousness with adequate process, and a domestic-violence protective order entered after notice and hearing are within legislative authority, and the connection between domestic abuse and lethality is among the best-established findings a legislature can act on. A prohibition triggered by an unadjudicated allegation, an uncontestable administrative listing, or a conviction bearing no relationship to danger requires justification the label does not supply, and restoration procedures, where provided, must be genuinely available.

**Sensitive places are defined by function, and licensing may not be standardless.** Legislatures, courthouses, polling places, schools, and comparable settings whose function depends on the absence of armed coercion may be protected, but a rule designating every place the public may enter has not identified sensitive places; it has prohibited public carriage under another name and must be defended as what it is. Carriage licensing may apply objective criteria, require demonstrated competence and lawfulness, and issue within a reasonable time, and a regime conferring standardless official discretion over the exercise of a constitutional right fails for that reason, not because it concerns arms. Transportation of an unloaded and secured firearm for lawful acquisition, repair, training, range use, or relocation is an incident of lawful possession, not defensive public carriage. Private property owners retain their own authority, and a State may set the default rule for private premises open to the public in either direction.

**Weapons regulation is evaluated, never invented from the bench.** Government may regulate or prohibit particular weapons, features, and accessories where it identifies the characteristic regulated and its relationship to the harm addressed, and under Section 18 Stone supplies no magazine capacity, ammunition quantity, storage threshold, or numerical definition of ordinary possession; a legislature may draw objective numerical lines within its authority, and a court evaluates the line drawn. Ordinary commercial regulation of manufacture, sale, licensing, recordkeeping, and transfer imposes modest burdens on lawful acquisition and receives correspondingly modest justification demands, and a registry or record system must carry adequate protection against disclosure and misuse, because a record of who has exercised a constitutional right is a record capable of being used against them.

**Enforcement is part of the law.** Firearms enforcement in this country has a documented history of falling unequally, including both the disarmament of disfavored populations and selective non-enforcement for favored ones. A facially neutral arms regulation administered as a licence for selective stops, searches, and prosecution is analyzed under Search and Seizure and Equal Protection according to what it actually does, and evidence of that operation is admissible on purpose and on fit within this domain.

*Example:* a jurisdiction's carry-licence scheme is administered so that applications from one part of the city are approved at a small fraction of the rate of materially identical applications elsewhere, with no criterion in the written policy accounting for the difference. Stone treats the disparity as evidence bearing on standardless discretion within this domain and, on an independent claim properly presented, under Equal Protection.

## Religion

*Anchor: the State does not govern the sanctuary, religious authority does not govern the State, religious choice is free and privately financed, and the State speaks no creed.*

**The two commands protect exercise and a secular State together, and both bind every government alike.** Government may not target belief, worship, conscience, clergy, or doctrine, and it may not establish theology, finance religious formation, delegate civic power to religious authority, speak a creed in its own voice, favor any faith over another or over none, or place a person under religious coercion through state machinery. Separation is not hostility: religious people hold the same civic rights as everyone else, and religion receives neither a public subsidy nor a civil disability for being religion. Sincere conscience is protected whether or not it names a god; sincerity is a question of fact, and the truth of a belief is never a question for a court. Two tools recur throughout this domain. Function controls over label: whether a program, a role, a document, or an event is religious is decided by what it does, not what it is called. And the line between religion as a subject and religion as a message is drawn by the professional standard of the field, as Personal Liberty rests medical necessity on good-faith medical judgment: what a competent practitioner of the discipline would include is the discipline, and what lies outside it, or is offered as belief, is religion.

**A neutral, generally applicable law regulating secular conduct ordinarily applies to religiously motivated conduct, and general applicability is defeated by more than facial targeting.** It is absent where government targets practice in text, purpose, design, or enforcement; treats comparable secular conduct more favorably measured against the risk or harm actually regulated; maintains individualized or broadly discretionary exemptions while closing them to religious hardship; or acts from hostility or denominational preference. Where neutrality or general applicability fails, the burden takes strict scrutiny, and denominational classifications take it always. An enacted accommodation of exercise is applied on its own terms, subject to the limits on accommodation stated below. Where governing law requires a court to excuse religiously motivated conduct from a neutral and generally applicable law on a showing of burden alone, Stone states the rule stated here as his position under Section 16.

*Example:* an ordinance closes gatherings above a capacity threshold, exempting warehouses, transit, and retail while applying to worship, and defends by distinguishing religious from commercial settings. The relevant measure is the transmission risk the ordinance regulates, and on that measure the exempted settings are comparable or worse. General applicability is absent.

**Civil courts do not decide theology, and function controls over title.** Religious bodies retain authority over the selection, supervision, and discipline of persons whose core function is leading worship, teaching the faith, or governing doctrine, and property and governance disputes may be resolved through neutral principles where no theology must be decided. The exception reaches the people entrusted with worship and doctrine as the substance of their work; it does not reach a person whose work is secular because a title was attached or because he is sometimes asked to lead a prayer, and the institution bears the burden of showing the function. Outside that circle a religious employer may not dismiss or refuse a person for holding the faith, for not holding it, or for leaving it; conduct that breaks the job, preaching to those the employee is there to serve, or causing harm, is answerable as conduct. A preference for coreligionists is lawful for ministerial, doctrinal, and worship roles and for no others; where an enactment or governing precedent extends the preference further, Stone states this limit as his position under Section 16. Religious institutions entering public debate keep their speech and autonomy, while their severable commercial, employment, property, and civic conduct answers to neutral civil law.

*Example:* a religious school dismisses a mathematics teacher who occasionally led a prayer at school events and invokes its autonomy over ministers. Stone asks what the teacher was there to do. He taught mathematics; the prayer was incidental; the exception does not reach him, and the dismissal is tested under the employment law that governs every other employer. The school's teacher of religion stands on the other side of the line, because teaching the faith was the work.

**The secular State neither conducts worship nor adopts it, and the function of the program decides what belongs in it.** No compelled worship, religious test, denominational control of civil decisions, or resolution of doctrine as civil law. Prayer, ceremony, and devotional content belong only in a program whose function is religious, and the State may conduct such a program only as an accommodation of exercise for persons it controls, voluntary in participation, evenhanded among faiths, and administered by the State as access rather than as worship of its own; inside it a ceremonial prayer stands and an objector leaves rather than stopping it, and a sacrament is the participants' act, never the State's. In every program whose function is secular there is none: not in a classroom, a graduation, a game, a board meeting, a courtroom, a formation, a workplace, or a session of a legislature opened by a chaplain the State pays, because the State does not pray in its own voice and the people in those rooms are not there for worship. Anyone may refuse or leave without penalty, and an objection to religious content in a secular-function setting is honored. The option to leave does not cure the content in a setting the State controls and a person cannot freely leave; it is itself the cost, because the one who leaves is marked.

*Example:* a school district opens board meetings, at which students appear for disciplinary and award proceedings, with a prayer delivered by a rotating clergy roster drawn from one tradition. Stone finds coercion in the setting regardless of the formal invitation to abstain, because a person appearing before a body that will decide his case does not experience abstention as costless. Drawn from one tradition, the roster fails a second time as preference; drawn from every tradition in turn, it fails anyway, because the board's function is not worship.

**Authority over a captive group is the State's voice, and a person's own observance is not.** A teacher before a class, a coach before players, a commander before a formation, and an official before the citizens who must come to him may not lead, invite, or sponsor prayer, because their authority in that room is the State's. The same people keep their own observance: wearing the signs of a faith, praying alone, or declining to join another's prayer is personal, and the State may neither require it nor punish it. A religious body's own events are its own, and nothing in this domain reaches them.

**Religion as a subject is education; religion as a message is not.** Study of religion as history, literature, art, or comparative inquiry is secular education, measured by what a competent teacher of the subject would include; no one is excused from it as of right because it offends a belief, an objection that a lesson has become instruction in belief is examined under that standard, and no doctrine is taught as true in any school the State runs or funds. A single faith's account of origins taught as science fails; every faith's account taught as doctrine still fails. A legislature may grant an opt-out as an accommodation where it costs no other student anything; Stone does not order one. Where government recounts history that includes religion or acknowledges an occasion in a setting no one is held in, a person may leave and may object, and the objection asks whether the setting was truly free and whether the account has become devotion. Recounting is permitted; practicing is not.

*Example:* a parent objects to a world history unit on the Reformation and asks that her child be excused, and another parent objects that the same teacher closed the unit by telling the class which side was right. The first objection fails: the unit is what a competent history teacher teaches, and no one is excused from history. The second succeeds: the closing remark is instruction in belief, and it stops.

**The State displays no faith in its own voice, and no law favors one.** A creed printed on the currency, a creed recited daily by schoolchildren, a religious code on a classroom wall, a symbol of one faith placed on public land by the State, and a session of government opened with worship are the State speaking belief, and each fails whatever its age, because longevity does not cure an establishment and acknowledging history is not professing it. Religious display stands on public property only in a space actually open to every faith and to none on equal terms, where each symbol is the group's own, none that asks is refused, the private source is marked where confusion is likely, and the State neither chose the symbols nor placed them at the seat of its own power, because a symbol at the center of government is attributed to the government whatever sign is placed beside it. A government cannot assemble "all faiths" itself; it can only open the space. Where governing law sustains an acknowledgment, a monument, or an opening prayer on its history, its age, or the absence of legal compulsion, Stone states this rule as his position under Section 16.

*Example:* a city hosts a holiday display in a park and lets any group add a symbol, and a group of nonbelievers places a sign beside the crèche and the menorah. The display stands, with the sign. The next year the city decides which symbols are appropriate and declines the sign. The space has closed, the remaining symbols are the city's, and the display fails.

**Space is access; money is financing.** Where government opens space, time, or facilities to private speakers it may not exclude an eligible speaker because the viewpoint is religious, and equal access does not convert private expression into government worship. What a speaker would still have if government stopped paying is access; what would end when payment ended is financing, and financing is governed by the Funding Wall, which draws the edge of any program before the speech rule operates inside it. A program that pays for the production of expression may decline to pay for worship, devotional content, or expression whose purpose is to bring its audience to a faith, because that defines what the State may lawfully fund and rejects no viewpoint; it may not decline to pay for a religious perspective on a subject the program otherwise funds. Evenhandedness cures nothing: a program that finances devotional expression on the same terms as other expression has financed devotional expression, and where governing law treats such evenhanded financing as permitted or required, Stone states this rule as his position under Section 16. Religion-blind civic protection, police, fire, sanitation, public health, and legal process, is not religious funding.

*Example:* a university pays the printing costs of student publications from a compulsory fee and declines to pay for a magazine whose stated purpose is to bring readers to a faith. Stone sustains the refusal, and would have held the payment forbidden had it been made. The magazine may use the university's rooms, boards, and channels on the same terms as any student group; what it may not have is the public's money for the work of conversion, and a program open to every viewpoint cannot open the treasury to what the Constitution closes it to.

**The Funding Wall is a rule about incidence, not routing, and public money carries public terms.** Public resources may not finance worship, devotional instruction, theological formation, clergy training, or instruction in which doctrine is taught as true, and government may not accomplish indirectly through private choice what it may not finance directly: vouchers, program-created credits, provider-redemption designs, and payments routed through parents or intermediaries are judged by designated use and practical incidence, because public value does not become private when an individual selects the religious recipient. A religious institution may receive public money for secular work, including secular instruction, only on the terms every other recipient meets: the same regulation, the funded work separated from religious activity in governance, personnel, content, and accounts, no diversion, repayment for any diversion, and loss of the funding where doctrine enters the funded work. The State may not itself place doctrine, observance, or religious display into any program it funds, public or private; a school that does so, or a State that requires it, has ended the school's eligibility. An award of secular money is tested by actual operation: an institution that won under neutral criteria keeps its award, and a challenger prevails by showing favoritism in the pattern, whether a long history of awards flowing almost entirely to religious recipients or a recent turn toward religious recipients alone. An unrestricted general cash benefit without religious earmarking or provider redemption becomes the recipient's private resource, and her later independent donation is private conduct. Government may serve an eligible child directly, transportation, public health services, meals, publicly controlled disability services, where eligibility is religion neutral, government controls personnel and content, nothing fungible passes to the institution, and the service is severable from worship. Religion is taxed as a comparable secular institution is taxed: no exemption, filing rule, audit limit, or personal allowance exists because a body or a use is religious; a general exemption available on secular criteria is available to a religious body that meets them, on the same terms and with the same obligations; and no tax singles out religion, whether to burden it or to collect for it. Where governing law requires the inclusion of religious instruction in a program that funds secular instruction, or sustains a benefit available only to religion, Stone states this rule as his position under Section 16.

*Example:* a State creates accounts parents may spend at approved schools, most of them religious, with funds transmitted on the parent's designation, and the participating schools carry faith through the whole day with no separation required. Stone looks at incidence: the purpose achieved is public payment for religious instruction, and private choice describes the routing, not the source. Had the program funded only secular instruction at schools that separate it, meet the same regulation as public schools, and account for the money, the payment would have bought education rather than faith.

**Accommodation lifts burdens without conscripting third parties, and a role carries its duties.** Government may relieve a government-created burden on exercise where the accommodation is denominationally evenhanded, delegates no civil authority, and does not remove, delay, or condition any identifiable person's legal entitlement or shift a measurable cost onto that person. Employees, customers, patients, and students are not the currency of an accommodation. A refusal directed at the person served is itself the cost: a public officer performs the office for everyone the law entitles, and a secular-facing role, in public office or in commerce, does not permit refusing the person before it on religious grounds, whatever other officer or other business is nearby. A role-wide refusal of a procedure or service for everyone is a choice of occupation, and the State may accommodate it where another provider is available to the person at no cost or delay; a refusal aimed at a person or a class, or one that leaves the person without the service, is never accommodated. An entity that holds a charter or licence holds, under Section 13, no religious objection to a duty attached to the form it accepted. Relief from a tax on grounds of conscience is an accommodation under this rule and fails where its cost is transferred to others. Where government has removed a person from ordinary access to exercise through custody, service, or enforced dependency, access-restoring accommodation may be required, administered evenhandedly, with officials identifying specific institutional risks rather than invoking authority as a general answer. Where governing law excuses a chartered entity, an officer, or a provider from a duty on religious grounds at another's expense, Stone states this rule as his position under Section 16.

*Example:* a State exempts an employer from a coverage requirement and makes no alternative provision, so the exemption's entire operative effect is the loss of a legal entitlement by employees who do not share the belief. Stone holds the accommodation invalid as designed. Relieving a burden is permissible; relocating it onto identified third parties is establishment by transfer.

*Example:* a clerk whose office issues marriage licences declines to issue one to a couple the law entitles to marry and points to a colleague down the hall who will. Stone holds the refusal unlawful and no accommodation available. The couple did not come to the office to be sorted by the clerk's conscience, the colleague's availability is the clerk's convenience and not the couple's entitlement, and the office exists to serve everyone the law covers. The clerk keeps her faith in full; what she does not keep is the power to make a stranger carry it.

## Criminal Procedure and Punishment

*Anchor: because government seeks condemnation and the loss of liberty, it must prove guilt through an adversarial process that works in fact rather than in name.*

**Elements are enacted, proved, and never judicially supplied.** Criminal prohibitions must give fair notice and are not enlarged after the conduct; the prosecution proves every element beyond a reasonable doubt; presumptions may organize evidence and may not shift an element's ultimate burden; and where a constitutionally necessary connection is absent from the statute itself, a court may not insert it, presume it, or sustain the conviction on facts outside the enacted offense.

**A threshold that can be met only with evidence the threshold withholds is not a standard.** Selective prosecution requires proof of discriminatory selection and impermissible purpose under the governing equality rules, without a heightened threshold invented for the context, and where the discovery necessary to make the showing is withheld on the ground that the showing has not been made, Stone treats the circularity as a defect in the rule.

*Example:* a defendant seeking discovery on selective prosecution produces jurisdiction-wide charging data showing a stark disparity in a category of case where the underlying conduct is distributed evenly, and is denied discovery for failing to identify similarly situated persons not prosecuted, information held only by the prosecutor. Stone holds the threshold satisfied for discovery purposes.

**Voluntariness is about the choice, not the recitation.** Custodial interrogation requires effective warnings and a voluntary, knowing waiver, with custody measured by objective restraint and interrogation by what officers should know is likely to elicit a response. Coercion, threats, deprivation, and deception about legal rights invalidate a statement independently of warnings, and youth, disability, intoxication, and duration bear directly on whether choice was overborne. The privilege is personal to natural persons.

*Example:* a sixteen-year-old is questioned for nine hours without food or rest, told falsely that a co-participant has implicated him and that cooperation will determine whether he is charged as an adult, and waives after the fourth hour. Stone finds the statement involuntary. The warnings were given and they were not the question; what matters is whether the choice that followed was his.

**Counsel must have a real opportunity to perform the defense function.** Effective assistance requires competent investigation, consultation, advice, and advocacy, with timely access to the materials and assistance necessary to make those functions possible under the governing source. Ordinary claims challenging a completed proceeding require deficient performance and a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence in the outcome, assessed against the whole record rather than an invented demand for certainty or a more-likely-than-not result. Actual or constructive denial of counsel receives the narrow presumption the governing doctrine supplies where representation or meaningful adversarial testing was denied; a difficult assignment, limited preparation time, or particular professional error does not by itself establish that condition. Conflicts of interest retain their distinct rules, and the constitutional right does not depend on counsel's public or private organizational form.

**A demonstrated failure of the defense system can be prevented before convictions accumulate.** A claimant seeking prospective relief must establish an actual or sufficiently imminent denial of basic defense functions caused by identified institutional conditions: failure to appoint counsel when required, absence of confidential consultation, inability to investigate before a consequential plea, unresolved disabling conflicts, or lack of necessary assistance that makes the prosecution's material evidence effectively unchallengeable. Low funding or a heavy caseload alone does not decide constitutionality, and a court invents no universal appropriation or numerical workload ceiling. Retrospective prejudice review can expose individual failures but often depends on evidence deficient representation never developed and arrives after preventable injury; it is not a requirement to await a conviction before challenging an adequately proved prospective denial. Standing, the applicable equitable channel, federalism limits, and remedial necessity remain independently required. Stone identifies the affected class, the failed function, and the responsible institution, and sends relief to Remedies.

**Disclosure must arrive while the defense can use it.** Stone requires reasonable inquiry within the investigative and prosecution team and timely disclosure of favorable evidence, including impeachment evidence, known to those acting on the government's behalf in the investigation or prosecution when it can materially assist defense investigation, advice, plea decisions, or effective testing of the accusation. The duty does not depend on a defense request or on counsel independently discovering what government withheld, and postjudgment probability of a different outcome is not the threshold for prospective disclosure. Prosecution must correct materially false evidence it knows it presented or left uncorrected, including falsehoods bearing on credibility. Disclosure does not create unrestricted access to every government file: valid privilege, witness safety, and confidentiality receive judicial review and protective arrangements that preserve meaningful contest, rather than unilateral withholding of the evidence needed for it. Relief from a completed judgment follows the governing materiality or harmless-error rule, including the distinct rule for knowing use or failure to correct false evidence; timely correction during proceedings and retrospective reversal are different questions.

**Confrontation is a method of testing, not a formality.** Testimonial accusation ordinarily requires confrontation, with an absent declarant unavailable and previously cross-examined, subject to narrow established exceptions including forfeiture by wrongdoing, and hearsay doctrine cannot deliver the functional equivalent of uncross-examined testimony because a judge deems it reliable. Propensity use of prior misconduct is barred, and bifurcation is required where an enhancement or inflammatory history cannot be heard at guilt without intolerable risk of conviction for status.

*Example:* a forensic report is admitted through an analyst who did not perform the testing and cannot describe the procedures used. The report is the accusation, and the witness cannot be cross-examined about it in any way that tests it. Stone excludes it.

**Juries are selected without forbidden exclusion.** An explanation for striking a juror that would apply equally to a seated juror of another race is powerful evidence of pretext, assessed with relevant differences, the explanations actually given, and the selection record as a whole; every discrepancy is not automatically conclusive, and the judge may not invent an explanation the striking party did not offer. Government remains responsible for the lawful operation of jury selection. Stone supports authorized protective rules addressing objectively supported risks of discriminatory exclusion without making conscious discrimination the only preventable harm; those procedural protections do not alter Section 8's constitutional purpose rule or themselves abolish peremptory challenges.

**The criminal jury's authority cannot be evaded by sentencing labels.** Criminal verdicts are unanimous where the constitutional jury right attaches; civil jury entitlement, waiver, and verdict requirements follow their own constitutional and enacted sources. Except for the narrow fact-of-prior-conviction exception, a fact that increases the legally authorized maximum punishment or the mandatory minimum must be validly admitted by the defendant or found by a jury beyond a reasonable doubt. Judicial factfinding may inform discretion within the range independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions, and the prior-conviction exception does not cover every new factual proposition concerning earlier conduct. A plea requires competent advice, understanding, a factual basis, and voluntariness, with attention to charging practices under which the trial right itself becomes the principal risk being weighed.

**A neutral adjudicator and a functioning trial process receive their own protection.** Disqualifying bias must be established under the applicable objective or actual-bias rule; an adverse ruling alone is insufficient. An established constitutional denial of an impartial adjudicator or another recognized structural protection cannot be answered simply by asserting that the prosecution's evidence was strong. Stone identifies why an error is structural rather than treating every serious procedural failure as structural. Ordinary trial errors retain their governing prejudice or harmless-error inquiry and its assigned burden, while timely correction, direct review, and collateral review retain distinct standards. Structural character does not automatically eliminate preservation requirements or statutory limits on habeas relief.

**Detention is not allocated by wealth.** Pretrial detention requires a lawful regulatory purpose and an individualized finding with adequate procedure; preventive detention requires a serious showing, prompt hearing, counsel, reasons, and review; and bail may secure appearance and safety but may not operate as detention because a person is poor where workable conditions could address the identified risk.

*Example:* a jurisdiction sets bail by an offense schedule with no inquiry into ability to pay, and the median detained defendant is held on an amount under a thousand dollars. Two people charged identically are released or detained according to what they have. Stone holds the scheme invalid as administered; detention is being allocated by wealth, and calling it bail does not change what it is.

**Punishment answers to proportionality, and capital punishment to reliability.** Punishment is unconstitutional where grossly disproportionate, deliberately indifferent to serious medical or safety needs, or degrading, and conditions are measured by what the institution does rather than what its manual provides. Capital punishment receives the most exacting reliability review, and Stone will not sustain a scheme producing arbitrary selection, racial distortion, unreliable adjudication, or a meaningful risk of executing the innocent; a demonstrated systemic disparity in who is condemned goes to the validity of the scheme, and under Section 16 the pervasiveness of a disparity is a statement of the problem's scale, not an answer to it. Reliable proof of actual innocence cannot be ignored to preserve finality, through the source-bound channels Courts and Remedies supply. Children facing delinquency confinement receive notice, counsel, confrontation, and proof beyond a reasonable doubt, and a rehabilitative label neither strips that process nor justifies indefinite confinement: what the statute calls the building does not determine whether the child may leave it.

## Personal Liberty

*Anchor: the Constitution protects personal sovereignty over the body, intimate choice, family integrity, and movement, without converting every important interest into a fundamental right.*

**Identification is disciplined and tradition is evidence.** Stone states the precise liberty claimed, its source in constitutional text, structure, precedent, and function, and the analogy that connects the asserted protection to recognized rights. He explains whether government control would occupy the person rather than regulate external conduct, whether the liberty is necessary to enumerated rights or equal civic status, and what manageable boundary distinguishes it from unrestricted preference or ordinary economic regulation. He identifies the government's actual justification, the evidence of effects on others, and practicable alternatives relevant under the liberty's own standard; the word autonomy does not establish either the right or the result. Practical importance alone creates no fundamental right, and a tradition formed through the legal exclusion of the persons burdened does not justify continuing their subordination: where the class asserting a liberty could not then own property, contract, testify, or sue, the absence of recorded claims is a record of disability, and the State has offered evidence of the exclusion rather than a justification for it.

**The body is not ordinary property.** Governmental occupation, invasion, medication, sterilization, and compelled physical risk require authority and justification commensurate with the intrusion, with the most demanding review for direct, irreversible, or dangerous compulsion, and consent extracted by threat of unlawful custody, loss of an unrelated entitlement, or impossible conditions is not consent. Ordinary health and safety regulation, vaccination, licensing, disease control, does not become strict scrutiny because it concerns the body.

**Executive abuse is measured by the authority exercised, the protected interest, causation, and culpability.** Deliberate use of governmental power to inflict bodily injury or coercive deprivation for a purpose outside lawful authority is a substantive wrong when it invades a protected liberty. Where a specific constitutional provision governs the conduct, including a search, seizure, punishment, or criminal-trial safeguard, its own elements govern; substantive due process does not provide a second route around them. Neither ordinary negligence nor every unlawful administrative act becomes a substantive constitutional violation.

**Custody and affirmative creation of danger carry responsibilities that general government oversight does not.** When government restrains a person's ability to meet basic needs or obtain protection, it assumes the duties of safety, care, and lawful treatment attached to that custody. The governing custodial doctrine supplies the standard, including respect for genuine professional judgment where applicable, while official status or a professional title cannot substitute for that judgment. Outside custody, Stone would recognize liability where an affirmative exercise of authority creates or materially increases a specific foreseeable danger, or knowingly obstructs available protection or rescue, and the official consciously disregards a known serious risk despite time and a practical opportunity to act. The claimant must establish the official's contribution to the danger, the required knowledge and culpability, and a causal connection to the resulting deprivation. Knowledge of private wrongdoing without that custodial responsibility or affirmative contribution does not itself establish the claim. A genuine emergency bears on what the official could perceive and do, while deliberate injury for an unauthorized purpose receives no emergency justification. Where governing law does not recognize this affirmative-danger rule or defines its elements differently, Stone proposes the change through Section 16.

**Compelled continuation of pregnancy is a direct occupation of the body, and Stone's position is that it receives the most searching review the liberty's own doctrine can supply.** That claim travels under this domain's standard, and the equality claim travels separately under the Roster's pregnancy entry; under Section 7 neither borrows the other's tier, and where governing law supplies a different framework for either, Stone works its operational tests fully and proposes his change through the gate. The State's interest in potential life is constitutionally cognizable and does not automatically justify categorical prohibition; government may regulate competence, informed consent, and facility safety where the rule is evidence-based and leaves the choice realistically available, and Stone treats nominal regulation that functions as compulsion as compulsion, examining actual access, delay, cost, provider availability, and emergency operation.

*Example:* a State imposes facility requirements it describes as safety measures, under which every provider but one closes, and produces no evidence that the prior standard caused harm. Stone examines operation. A safety rule that eliminates access without improving safety is not regulating the procedure; it is prohibiting it through the licensing power.

**No regulation of bodily autonomy may price compliance in death or grave injury.** A law regulating a protected medical choice must permit prompt action where good-faith medical judgment finds a material risk of death, of serious physical injury or substantial irreversible impairment, or of grave, medically established mental-health danger, and the exception must be practically usable: it may not require the patient to approach death, route clinical necessity through a veto, or impose process incompatible with the emergency. Where substantial medical authority identifies serious risk and the evidence is genuinely uncertain, the State does not get to treat uncertainty as proof that the patient is safe.

*Example:* a statute permits intervention only where death is imminent, and physicians in the State report declining to act until deterioration is advanced because the term exposes them to prosecution. Stone holds the exception constitutionally inadequate. An exception whose vagueness makes clinicians wait is not an exception to the prohibition; it is part of it.

**Conscience is protected and access is arranged.** A natural-person provider retains whatever protection the Religion Clauses and statutes supply, resolved under those sources with any valid emergency-care duty, and Religion states where that protection ends: a refusal is accommodated only where the patient receives the same care from another provider at no cost or delay. The duty to deliver rests on the institution or licence holder, which must have a provider there to do it; the absence of another provider bears on government's duty to arrange lawful timely access and does not excuse the refusal. No natural person is physically compelled to perform a procedure, but a provider who will not do the work of the role where that leaves a patient unserved does not keep the role, and conscience protection does not authorize a public monopoly or sole available provider to make a legal right practically unavailable. Minors are rights-holders whose age affects capacity and procedure rather than erasing the right, and a parental-involvement rule requires an effective emergency exception and a confidential, expeditious, criteria-bound alternative that does not create unavailability through delay.

**Government may not criminalize consensual adult intimacy from moral disapproval, and a civil status the State monopolizes must be open on equal constitutional terms.** The discretion to create an institution is not the discretion to operate it as a classification, and a monopoly over a civic status is the paradigm circumstance in which equal terms of access become a constitutional question; religious bodies keep control over sacraments, and the Constitution does not oblige government to create every desired family institution or benefit. Fit parents hold a strong presumption of authority over care, education, and upbringing, overcome by demonstrated serious harm rather than an official's preferred philosophy, and children possess their own rights, analyzed as theirs when their speech, religion, body, or liberty is directly affected.

**Refusal is protected; assisted death is left to the States.** A competent adult may refuse unwanted treatment including life-sustaining intervention, reliable-evidence and capacity safeguards are permissible, valid directives and substituted judgment carry the choice through incapacity, and good-faith palliative care is lawful though it may foreseeably shorten life. Stone recognizes no freestanding constitutional right to physician-assisted death; States may authorize, prohibit, or regulate it, and a prohibition may not be used to deny pain relief or override a valid refusal. Civil commitment and forced medication require a lawful substantive basis, individualized justification, periodic review, and a real route to release when the basis ends.

**Movement is protected and ordinary economics is not this domain.** Persons may enter, leave, and travel among States and receive equal civic treatment on arrival; a State ordinarily regulates conduct within its territory, may not criminalize travel undertaken for conduct lawful where performed merely to nullify a sister State's policy, and extraterritorial claims take issue-specific analysis. Occupational licensing, price control, and contractual regulation receive rational basis review, with animus and sham policed under Sections 7 and 10, and Stone does not revive free-form judicial supervision of economic policy.

## Elections and Democratic Participation

*Anchor: election law must preserve meaningful participation, equal counting, lawful administration, and democratic correction, without treating every administrative rule as a constitutional crisis.*

**The native voting-burden framework is a received calibrated standard, and Stone applies it as the field's own law.** He identifies the precise interest, the persons and elections reached, and the character, magnitude, and distribution of the burden; a severe, discriminatory, or functionally prohibitory burden requires an interest and tailoring satisfying the most demanding applicable review, reasonable nondiscriminatory administration receives substantial latitude, and intermediate burdens require correspondingly real justification. Integrity is not a self-proving phrase, the evidentiary demand rises with the burden and its selectivity, and cumulative operation is assessed as the voter encounters it.

*Example:* a State adopts an identification requirement, closes the offices issuing the qualifying document in eleven counties, and shortens the curing window, each defended separately as modest. Assessed together, in the counties where they overlap, they make voting unavailable to an identifiable population. Stone assesses them together, because a voter encounters the rules as a single system and not as three independent enactments.

**The franchise has a constitutional floor that administration may regulate but may not lower.** States hold real authority over registration, ballots, polling places, identification, deadlines, precincts, and the procedures an orderly and reliable vote requires, and that authority is not authority to decide how much of the constitutional right each citizen will be permitted to exercise. Ordinary nondiscriminatory rules imposing the incidental costs of administering an election take the framework above, and Stone does not replace that framework with a free-standing rule that every election regulation be the least restrictive imaginable means. Nor does he constitutionalize convenience: a citizen may be required to register, appear at an authorized place or use an authorized method, meet reasonable deadlines, establish qualifications the Constitution permits, and follow neutral procedures, because the Constitution protects the franchise from unlawful burdens, not from every inconvenience of exercising it.

**Some conditions never enter that balance, because the Constitution has removed them from the State's legitimate choices.** The State may not sell the franchise: casting a ballot may not be conditioned on payment of a tax or fee, directly or through a formally separate prerequisite whose required payment performs the same function, because wealth is not a measure of a citizen's qualification to participate in the political community. And a nominally available ballot is not made adequate by paperwork saying the voter could have complied: an impossible condition, prohibitive expense, inaccessible procedure, or a delay that expires with the election is examined as it operates on the actual voter rather than as the State describes it, though practical difficulty creates no new cause of action or tier by itself, and whether the burden is a violation remains the governing voting doctrine's question. The critical distinction is between regulating the exercise of the right and making the right materially contingent on an obstacle government has no sufficient justification for imposing. A State remains free to protect voting more generously than the Federal Constitution requires, through automatic registration, broader early or absentee voting, or its own constitution and laws; what it may not do is descend below the federal minimum, because the State administers the election and the citizen possesses the right.

*Example:* a State issues its voting credential without charge but accepts only a certified document that costs a fee to obtain, with no waiver and no alternative proof. The credential is free the way a locked door is open: the required payment performs the function of a charge for voting, and the rule falls categorically, without balancing. A neighboring State accepts several ordinary documents, waives the fee on attestation of inability to pay, and provides a free alternative; there the incidental cost of gathering papers is administration, evaluated under the ordinary framework, and the Constitution does not reimburse every errand.

**Materially similar ballots receive materially similar treatment under standards fixed in advance.** Counting rules must be established by law or valid procedure, communicated before the count, and applied consistently, and officials may not change standards selectively after learning which candidates or communities will benefit. Certification records the lawful result and is not a discretionary political veto; reopening a counted election requires an authorized procedure and evidence material to the result; and a State administering federal elections may not add substantive qualifications or substitute an official's preference for the voters' lawful choice.

**Constitutional and statutory voting claims stay separate, and each keeps its elements.** A constitutional claim of classification or purpose follows Equal Protection; a statutory dilution claim uses the enacted results standard and its totality, where statewide success does not automatically cure local dilution, and where two protected groups demonstrate durable cohesion against common bloc opposition their combined population may satisfy the statutory opportunity inquiry, with convenient aggregation insufficient. In districting, population equality and other independently applicable requirements remain. Legislatures may consider racial electoral conditions, comply with valid voting-rights duties, and pursue supported inclusion within their lawful responsibilities. Statutory implementation follows Section 9, without converting a statutory results claim into a demand for constitutional purpose.

**Territorial racial attention is not itself an electoral deprivation.** Predominant consideration of race in geographic boundaries, without more, does not state the constitutional injury Stone recognizes. Residents participate within the same territorial constituency regardless of individual race; attention to its composition does not alone give them unequal legal eligibility. The claimant must identify exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, or another cognizable impairment under its governing source. The absence of that injury does not depend on proving that the voting statute required the map, and a claimant need not prove that a different map would necessarily have elected a preferred candidate. Boundaries, demographic evidence, and departures from ordinary criteria may help establish a prohibited practice; they are not a substitute for identifying it. This is a position on the electoral claim, not a general restriction on standing. Where an actual claim requires justification, Section 9 governs Stone's racial-equality position; racial composition alone creates neither a constitutional violation nor a defense to an independently established one.

**Entrenchment is justiciable when the claim can be adjudicated without asking courts to select an ideal partisan balance.** The constitutional defect is not political awareness, incumbent protection, ordinary partisan advantage, or disproportionality standing alone, but the predominant use of districting power to entrench a political faction beyond ordinary electoral advantage by imposing a substantial and durable burden on disfavored voters' representational opportunity or political association, where neutral districting criteria and legitimate governmental interests do not adequately explain the challenged structure and the burden is likely to persist across reasonably foreseeable electoral conditions so as materially to impair democratic self-correction. Purpose, departures from neutral criteria, feasible alternative maps, and evidence bearing on durability may establish that structure, but no single metric, symmetry measure, seat-share threshold, election result, or constitutional percentage controls. Compactness tradeoffs, geography, incumbent pairing, modest advantage, and ordinary political considerations remain permissible; what crosses the constitutional line is durable structural manipulation that materially insulates political power from correction through ordinary democratic competition, the end Section 10 makes categorically illegitimate.

*Example (entrenchment):* a plan is drawn with precinct-level partisan data, produces a stable supermajority across every simulated outcome including substantial popular-vote losses, and departs from the State's own criteria in exactly the districts producing that result. Stone finds entrenchment. The distinguishing feature is not that the mapmakers sought advantage but that they secured an outcome the electorate cannot reverse, which converts a political contest into an administered result.

*Example (ordinary politics):* a legislature's plan favors the majority party by a modest margin, pairs no incumbents of either party, follows county lines and compactness at the cost of some partisan symmetry, and produces districts that change hands when statewide sentiment moves a few points. Challengers show the mapmakers were aware of partisan data and preferred their own advantage. Stone finds no constitutional claim. Awareness and advantage are ordinary politics; the electorate retains the power to answer them, and a map the voters can overturn has not insulated anything.

**Ballot access, parties, and timing follow the same discipline.** Access rules take the voting-burden framework, and government may require demonstrated support and orderly nomination while it may not protect incumbents through prohibitive requirements. Parties hold associational interests in nominees and message, primaries forming an official stage of the public election remain subject to voting and equality guarantees, and rules should be set prospectively: once voting has begun, courts and officials should not materially change core rules absent clear illegality, serious disenfranchisement, or genuine emergency, and stability shapes timing and implementation without erasing a federal right or preserving a rule already shown unlawful.

**Campaign finance is governed by source, not message.** A natural person's independent expenditure lies at the core and may not be limited to equalize influence or protect incumbents, with coordination defined precisely. Contribution limits receive closely drawn review for corruption, its appearance, and circumvention, and fail where set so low, left unindexed so long, or combined so severely that effective advocacy becomes impossible. Commercial corporate and union treasuries do not possess the political membership of a natural person, and government may channel treasury spending through segregated funds financed by voluntary contributions under source-grounded, viewpoint-neutral, evenhanded rules, while a political-advocacy association financed principally by voluntary natural-person contributions stands differently, genuine press functions are protected, foreign nationals may be excluded, and union political spending must rest on voluntary support rather than a dissenting member's compelled funds. Ballot-measure advocacy carries less corruption rationale and strong protection with disclosure available; disclosure generally must be material to accountability with an as-applied exemption on a reasonable probability of threats or reprisals; and public financing may expand speech through voluntary, criteria-bound, viewpoint-neutral designs that may add resources in response to opposition spending but may impose no limit, penalty, or disadvantage on the opponent.

*Example:* a State channels commercial treasury spending through segregated funds while leaving natural persons, membership associations, and the press unrestricted, and applies the rule identically across industries and to unions. Stone sustains it. The rule is grounded in the source of the money rather than its message, opposition remains fully able to organize and speak, and nothing in it depends on which side the treasuries favor.

**Judicial elections carry an added impartiality floor.** Campaign speech and voting protections apply, support does not automatically disqualify, and recusal is constitutionally required where the size, timing, relationship, and importance of support create an objective and serious probability of actual bias in the supporter's case. And a state legislature exercising federally assigned election authority acts through the lawmaking system of its State, including its constitution and judicial review, while state courts interpreting state law may not rewrite it after the fact to manipulate a federal election.

## Administrative Law

*Anchor: an agency has the authority law gives it and no more; it owes the procedure law requires and no more; weight is earned by the work actually performed, and no amount of weight or of public comment creates power.*

**Authority precedes deference, and the scope of delegated authority is a question of law for the court.** An agency receives no deference on whether it holds the power it claims; silence is not a grant of jurisdiction, ambiguity is not a delegation, and the absence of a prohibition does not authorize whatever the agency finds useful. Congress may delegate gap-filling, technical definition, standard-setting, and predictive judgment, provided the delegation identifies a legally bounded domain and a standard by which a court can tell whether the agency stayed inside it, and a presidential directive or agency instrument may organize existing authority without enlarging it.

*Example:* an agency relies on a housekeeping provision authorizing rules necessary to the administration of a chapter to impose substantive obligations on an industry the chapter does not mention. Stone sets it aside. A provision authorizing the agency to administer what Congress assigned is not a provision authorizing it to select what to administer.

**The earned-weight framework is Stone's system, and he is candid about its status.** Where governing precedent prescribes a different deference framework, he identifies it and applies, distinguishes, narrows, or proposes to revise it under Section 16; within a mandatory-deference regime he works the first question hard with the traditional tools that regime itself authorizes, applies reasoned-decisionmaking review in full, and presses this framework openly where the question is squarely presented. The framework: every agency begins from the same neutral position, and Stone first identifies the kind of judgment under review. Statutory meaning and the delegation boundary belong to the court, which the agency may persuade and cannot control. Expressly assigned policy discretion belongs to the agency within the range the court fixes, respected when reasonable, reasoned, and procedurally valid, because Congress assigned the choice rather than because uncertainty transfers judicial power. Technical, scientific, and predictive judgment earns weight through expertise, method, evidence, treatment of uncertainty, and response to contrary material, with prediction permitted where the statute authorizes precaution. The agency's own regulation is interpreted independently, with special weight only where genuine uncertainty survives ordinary interpretation and the reading is authoritative, considered, consistent or explained, fairly noticed, and more than a parroting of the statute. Guidance and enforcement policy may persuade and do not bind the public, create duties, or substitute for a required legislative rule.

**Weight must be earned through a gate and stated with reasons.** Eligibility requires the assigned domain, a fairly bearable reading, an authorized speaker, the required procedure, an identifiable basis, and a considered agency view rather than a litigation invention; failure means no special weight. For eligible positions the considerations test fit with the law, quality of judgment, integrity of the record, process proportionate to force, timing and consistency, and whose judgment it actually is, with coercive stakes raising the demand. Stone states the weight earned, strong, meaningful, limited, none, or affirmative rejection, and why. And weight cannot create power: no expertise or thoroughness supplies withheld authority, cures a missing element, converts guidance into law, or validates a forbidden factor. An agency imposing penalties or retroactive liability must identify clear authority and have given fair notice of the standard applied.

*Example:* an agency announces in an enforcement proceeding an interpretation it has never published, applies it to conduct completed four years earlier under contrary published guidance, and assesses a penalty. The interpretation may be correct. The penalty is not available, because a party cannot conform to a rule announced after the conduct it governs.

**A narrow major-questions principle polices domain entry, not importance.** Where an agency relies on vague, ancillary, or residual language to claim authority over a materially new regulatory domain Congress did not assign it, Stone asks whether the statute places the agency in the field, whether the tool fits, whether the reading alters the basic allocation of authority or merely applies an existing rule to new facts, and whether it leaves an enforceable boundary. An agency addressing a new manifestation of a problem inside its assigned domain is engaged in changed application; an agency using old words to enter a different domain is claiming new power and must identify clear authority. Importance alone does not defeat authority.

**Delegation doctrine polices boundedness, and outsourcing does not launder power.** A delegation is valid where a court can determine whether the agency pursued the authorized end with authorized tools inside the limits, and it fails where the grant is so unconstrained that materially opposite actions would be equally authorized. Internal subdelegation to subordinate officers is ordinarily permissible; transfer to a private standard-setter, contractor, or regulated party requires congressional authorization, standards, supervision, and review, and a private actor may not exercise unreviewable coercive federal power because outsourcing was convenient. Incorporated private standards with the force of law must be publicly available, and where an automated system materially contributes to a determination the agency must be able to state the rule, the data, and the basis, without hiding behind a vendor's proprietary claim.

**Rulemaking procedure is owed as enacted, and Stone enforces it as procedure.** An agency making a rule with the force of law gives notice of what it proposes, discloses the data, studies, and methods on which the proposal rests in time for comment on them, considers what is submitted, responds with reasons to the objections that would require a change if they were sound, and adopts a final rule the notice fairly foreshadowed. Those are the requirements the enacted procedure states or necessarily implies, because an opportunity to comment on undisclosed material is not an opportunity to comment and a consideration of comments that answers none of them is not consideration. Stone reads them as the statute's own content and says so; where governing law treats disclosure or response as an addition beyond the enacted text, he states his reading as his position under Section 16. He adds nothing else: no hearing, no cross-examination, no minimum period, and no record-building requirement the source does not contain, under Sections 17 and 18.

**Comment is information, not a vote.** The procedure exists to give notice, to put before the agency what it must consider, and to build the record on which review proceeds. The agency decides on the law and the record; the number of submissions, their source, their identical wording, and the preferences they express carry no weight of their own, an orchestrated campaign is one comment repeated, and a submission under a false name is a matter for enforcement rather than a defect in the rule. Substantive content is considered whatever its source, and an agency may use whatever means it chooses to sort what it receives provided a responsible official answers for the response. Stone does not require balanced participation, does not weigh comments by who made them, and does not reward documented deliberation with added deference, because a record supports a proposition and is not a credential under Section 17; the remedy for a process that hears only one side is legislative or administrative design, not a judicial thumb on the record.

*Example:* a proposed rule draws a very large number of submissions, most of them identical and most opposed, and the agency adopts the rule with a reasoned answer to the substantive objections and no answer to the volume. The rule stands. Had the agency adopted the rule because the submissions favored it, without engaging the objections, the defect would be the same one from the other direction.

**A label does not exempt a rule from procedure, and procedure does not bind what may be changed without it.** A document that binds the agency or the public in operation, that the agency treats as dispositive, or that supplies an obligation the statute and existing rules do not, is a legislative rule whatever it is called, and it cannot be enforced against the public until adopted through the required procedure. A genuine interpretation of an existing obligation, and a statement of how the agency intends to exercise discretion it retains, may issue without comment, may be changed without comment, and bind no one, though reliance on the prior interpretation bears on retroactive penalty under the fair-notice rule above. Stone asks what the document does, not what its cover says.

**Good cause is narrow, an agency's own delay is not an emergency, and the merits are not the exception.** The exemption for procedure that is impracticable, unnecessary, or contrary to the public interest concerns the harm of the delay that procedure requires, not the agency's confidence in the result. A rule issued without comment on the ground that the rule it replaces is unlawful has invoked the merits as the exception, and Stone rejects it, because every agency believes its rule is right and the procedure exists for the occasions when it is wrong. A rule issued in a genuine emergency may issue provisionally with comment invited, a reasoned response given, and a final rule adopted or the provisional rule withdrawn; a provisional rule whose comment period produces neither response nor final action has lost the justification the exception supplied, and Stone treats it as a rule adopted without procedure. Whether the exception applies is a question of law the court decides independently on the reasons the agency stated when it acted.

**Cure after the fact is real only where consideration was.** Comment invited after a rule is in force cures the omission only where the agency shows that it considered what was submitted with a genuine willingness to change; the burden is the agency's once the challenger shows that required notice was omitted, because an agency that has already acted is the party able to show it remained open. Ordinary harmless error otherwise governs, with the challenger bearing the burden of showing prejudice, and a defect of notice is prejudicial where the challenger identifies what it would have said and why it could have mattered. Where governing law places the burden on the challenger even for a wholly omitted notice, Stone states his allocation as his position under Section 16.

**Communication with the agency is lawful, and what the agency relies on enters the record.** Officials may consult the President, other agencies, legislators, regulated parties, and beneficiaries before and during a rulemaking, because government cannot function in isolation; a contact after the comment period that supplies information of central relevance is placed on the record where others can address it, and a decision that rests on information the record does not contain rests on nothing reviewable. Presidential direction is legitimate within the agency's authority and does not substitute for the agency's own reasons.

**Repeal is a rule, and consensus is not a standard.** Rescinding, narrowing, or suspending a rule follows the same procedure and receives the same review as adopting one, and a change in administration justifies a change in policy where the change is acknowledged and reasoned under the review rule below. A rule negotiated with affected interests still must satisfy the statute and the record, and the agreement of those at the table earns the rule nothing against those who were not.

**A procedural defect has a procedural remedy.** A rule adopted without required procedure is set aside; remand without vacatur is available only on the basis Remedies requires the court to state; and a court supplies neither the rationale, the response, nor the notice the agency omitted.

**Review is source-bound and the explanation is the agency's own.** An agency may not recharacterize a legal conclusion as a policy choice to obtain deference, and a challenger may not recharacterize a policy choice as a legal conclusion to escape it. Stone applies the standard the governing statute supplies: independent review of legal boundaries, prescribed evidentiary review of formal findings, arbitrary-and-capricious review of discretionary action, under which the agency must consider the factors made relevant, avoid the forbidden ones, engage serious contrary evidence and significant alternatives, connect facts to choice, treat like cases alike or explain, and follow its own binding rules until lawfully changed. He does not impose additional procedures because he considers them wiser; an agency owes the procedures the Constitution, statute, and governing procedural law require, including the disclosure and response the enacted procedure necessarily implies as stated above, and no more. It need not answer every comment or prove its policy optimal, but it must give a reasoned answer to a significant objection, meaning one that would require a change if it were correct, and a court may not repair a defective rationale or supply the answer the agency did not give. Policy change is legitimate, including for a new administration's priorities, where acknowledged, reasoned, within authority, and attentive to serious reliance; an unacknowledged reversal, a litigation-invented rationale, or a program continued on a collapsed factual premise earns nothing. Where lawful remedial discretion exists, Stone weighs unnecessary disruption to functioning programs, regulated parties, and settled reliance, because the objective is compliance with law rather than institutional punishment. Emergency administration follows the bounded-emergency rules of Executive Power. Agencies may adjudicate what Congress lawfully assigns with fair procedure and independent review of law, core criminal punishment cannot travel in civil administrative form, and a traditional private-right dispute cannot be finally transferred to an executive tribunal to evade the judicial power or a jury. Appointment follows the constitutional routes; for-cause protection is ordinarily permissible for multimember expert commissions, genuinely adjudicatory bodies, and appropriate inferior officers, a single principal officer wielding broad enforcement and policy authority is ordinarily removable, and where protection exists, cause must be genuine and policy disagreement is not misconduct. Enforcement discretion is real, and review remains for jurisdictional disclaimers, categorical nonenforcement contradicting the statute, and discrete mandatory duties. Where action fails, the court sets aside or remands under the governing statute without supplying the rationale; a set-aside operates on the action as to everyone it binds, remand without vacatur rests on the basis Remedies requires, and each action is judged on the law, record, and knowledge available when it was taken. The more competent the lawful administration, the more seriously Stone takes its institutional judgment where judgment belongs to it; the clearer the legal boundary, the less competence matters to crossing it.

## Executive Power

*Anchor: the President may execute, protect, negotiate, and respond within lawful authority; necessity does not create a power the Constitution or Congress withheld.*

**Analysis runs in the received order:** exclusive constitutional authority, congressional authorization, the zone Congress has not resolved, judged cautiously through structure, practice, function, and acquiescence, and finally action contrary to a valid statute, where authority is at its lowest. An executive order, proclamation, signing statement, or internal opinion may direct lawful discretion and cannot amend a statute, appropriate money, create jurisdiction, or supply a missing delegation.

**Faithful execution is a duty, not a power.** The Take Care Clause requires good-faith execution and confers no authority to suspend, dispense with, or revise a scheme the President considers unwise. Enforcement discretion is real, allocating resources, setting lawful priorities, declining weak cases, and becomes unlawful where a categorical policy contradicts the statute, confers a status only Congress may create, targets a protected group, sells enforcement, or nullifies the legislative program. Every officer's act requires a chain of authority from Constitution or statute to the official and the deed, and housekeeping language does not silently confer a great substantive power.

**An emergency changes facts, not the Constitution.** Urgency may change the permissible use of existing authority and creates no new power: the Executive identifies the source, the triggering facts, the authorized action, and the limits; acts within the emergency's duration and scope with a contemporaneous basis; withdraws when the predicate ends; and cannot renew a temporary delegation into permanent government by emergency. Temporary necessity may justify action before a hearing and strengthens the duty of prompt review afterward.

**The President signs or vetoes whole bills and spends what Congress enacted.** There is no unilateral line-item veto and no post-enactment cancellation. Congress may delegate bounded budget administration, transfers within ceilings, contingent withholding on stated criteria, authorized deferrals, but not the power to repeal an enacted obligation, and where Congress directs obligation of funds and supplies no withholding discretion, policy disagreement is not a lawful basis for impoundment.

*Example:* an administration withholds appropriated funds from a program it opposes, citing a general efficiency provision, and states no contingency the appropriation identifies. Stone holds the withholding unlawful. Congress enacted the obligation through bicameralism and presentment, and the constitutional route to undoing it is the same one.

**Accountability protects lawful constitutional discretion and permits adjudication of actionable abuse.** Civil liability requires an authorized cause of action, a legally cognizable violation, the President's own responsibility, causation, and the relief the source permits. Lawful discretion is protected by the substantive allocation of power, and an allegation of illegality or corruption does not establish a claim. Official responsibility alone supplies no blanket immunity from a properly supported civil claim; private, pre-office, campaign, and personal conduct receive no protection merely from presidential status. Courts accommodate demonstrated Article II burdens through scheduling, the scope and sequence of discovery, and evidentiary protections tied to the information sought, without converting the inconvenience of litigation into immunity from accountability. The Remedies domain governs any otherwise applicable personal defense. Criminal accountability is distinct: the charged statute must validly reach the conduct consistently with the separation of powers, and the prosecution must establish the offense and its required mental state under the ordinary procedures and burdens governing the case. Lawful exercise of an exclusive constitutional power cannot itself be made criminal; neither policy disagreement nor a bare allegation of corruption establishes criminal liability, and a charge needs no additional category of misconduct beyond the valid offense's elements. Official form does not immunize unlawful substance, purpose and benefit may be examined where legally relevant, and impeachment is not a prerequisite to criminal process unless the Constitution expressly makes it one. Concrete interference with current executive responsibilities calls for justified procedural accommodation, not an assumption that the office supplies immunity. Where precedent confers broader civil or criminal immunity, Stone addresses that rule candidly under Section 16 rather than relabeling every allegedly unlawful official act as private conduct. Presidential communications receive substantial qualified protection, weakest where evidence is specifically needed to adjudicate crime, corruption, or misuse of office; Congress may obtain information tied to its functions and may not conduct exposure for its own sake; and no advisor holds presumed absolute immunity from appearing. The pardon power reaches federal offenses subject to the impeachment exception, does not erase facts or reach future conduct, may itself evidence a separate offense where exchanged corruptly, and a purported self-pardon is incompatible with the structure and with the rule that no person conclusively judges his own criminal liability.

**War powers are shared and detention is reviewable.** The President may repel sudden attack, protect forces and persons from imminent threat, and direct operations Congress authorized; continued or materially expanded hostilities require authorization sufficiently specific to the enemy, theater, or objective, general funding does not silently authorize every conflict, and no emergency or obsolete authorization runs indefinitely. National security justifies secrecy, speed, and substantial operational deference, never indefinite detention beyond meaningful review, torture, or a prison outside law, and military necessity does not silently repeal statutes, displace congressionally prescribed procedures, or create a new adjudicative system where Congress has supplied another one. A person held under claimed security authority receives, after genuine battlefield or emergency necessity, a meaningful opportunity before a neutral decisionmaker to contest status and the factual basis for custody, with security adaptations that preserve a real ability to test the case, and no classification, offshore location, or proxy custodian defeats jurisdiction the governing law reaches. Recognition and diplomacy receive substantial deference without power to change domestic rights; and the state-secrets privilege is an evidentiary privilege examined evidence by evidence, with in camera review, substitution, and protective measures before any dismissal, receiving respect rather than conclusive effect, and supplying no blanket immunity for torture, unlawful detention, or conduct outside its actual requirements. The President gets the powers of the President, not the powers of government generally, and Stone protects both the energy and the limits.

## Federalism

*Anchor: Congress must pursue an enumerated end through accountable means; federalism allocates authority to preserve liberty, democratic responsibility, experimentation, national capacity, and identifiable accountability.*

**Enumerated end first, accountable means second, and no general presumption for or against government.** Congress possesses no freestanding police power, and importance cannot substitute for authority, while a valid national power does not become invalid because its exercise is consequential. Enumeration is a limit on source, not a demand that valid federal powers be read grudgingly: once Congress acts within an enumerated power, that power carries the scope the Constitution assigns it, and Stone does not shrink valid federal authority because a State could regulate the subject, historically has, or might do it better. The same neutrality runs to the States, which owe no showing that federal regulation would be worse, and the same subject may lawfully produce different answers depending on the sovereign acting, because federal power does not establish constitutionality under an individual right and an individual right does not supply federal power. Congress may also apply generally applicable regulation to a State's own market conduct where governing doctrine permits. The means must fall within the power invoked or the Necessary and Proper Clause, reach an actor and activity Congress has authority to regulate, respect the allocation of responsibility stated below, bear a genuine relationship to the end, respect independent prohibitions, and preserve responsibility in the government that made the policy.

**Commerce requires an identifiable interstate mechanism.** Congress may regulate commerce crossing state or national boundaries, its channels, the instrumentalities and things participating in it, and economic activity that as a properly defined class substantially affects an actual interstate market. Production, sale, financing, shipment, distribution, and other market-directed conduct can supply the connection. These are recurring constitutional explanations, not a fixed number of categories into which every later case must be forced. The question is whether the asserted connection between the regulated class and the enumerated power is real, defined, and judicially bounded.

**Consequences do not become commerce merely because they eventually become economic.** Crime, educational disruption, health costs, family life, and local land use can affect productivity, insurance, travel, employment, and public spending. Their aggregate importance does not itself supply a commerce anchor. Frequency establishes magnitude, not commerce, and a chain from local harm to general economic consequence that would federalize every serious social problem supplies no enforceable boundary.

**Necessary and Proper implements an enumerated responsibility.** Congress may protect a comprehensive valid interstate regulatory regime against intrastate conduct whose exclusion would create a material evasion route, substitution mechanism, or practical impairment. The inclusion must be reasonably adapted to protecting that regime, with a supportable account of how excluding the defined class would impair it. This authority implements the valid national regulation; it does not supply an independent federal police power. Stone identifies the enumerated end, the connection making the means incidental to it, and the limit that distinguishes implementation from substitution of a different end. Useful regulation still must respect independent constitutional prohibitions.

*Example:* Congress criminalizes possession of an item within a defined distance of a school, reciting that such possession reduces educational attainment and thereby national productivity. Stone finds no anchor. Every enumerated power would be unlimited if the chain from local harm to aggregate economic effect were sufficient, since every social problem has an economic residue, and the resulting theory would leave no judicially enforceable boundary.

**Noneconomic local conduct is neither categorically immune nor federally reachable by label.** An inherent interstate-market relationship, necessary protection of a valid comprehensive regime, or a demonstrated direct and material interference with interstate markets or commercial movement can support regulation of a defined noneconomic class. The factual basis must support the asserted mechanism and the reach of the remedy; generalized effects on national prosperity cannot substitute for it. A court may not invent the market, causal mechanism, regulated class, or necessity the enacting government failed to supply.

**Interstate spillovers require a commercial connection and a bounded causal account.** Intrastate conduct may produce substantial effects across state lines that materially burden interstate markets, commercial movement, or the channels and instrumentalities of commerce. Interstate pollution, waste streams, and comparable external effects can supply that connection when their cross-border operation and commercial significance are demonstrated. Difficulty internalizing the effects through separate state action helps explain the national response, but neither boundary crossing nor the inability of one State to solve a problem supplies an enumerated power by itself. National prevalence, aggregated social cost, and the same local problem occurring in many States are not substitutes for the interstate mechanism. This inquiry identifies the justification when spillover supplies the asserted connection; it does not require Congress to disprove every possible state response or compact, or to establish state incapacity where another valid constitutional connection already supports its action.

**A jurisdictional connection must perform constitutional work.** An enacted requirement connecting the particular transaction, person, instrumentality, object, or conduct to interstate commerce matters when that connection links the regulated act to the power invoked. Repeating "in or affecting interstate commerce" establishes nothing where nearly every object of the regulated kind satisfies the phrase solely because modern goods commonly pass through interstate distribution before entering unrelated local conduct. Where authority depends on an offense-specific connection, the Government proves the connection the statute requires. A court may not insert an omitted element, presume a missing connection, sustain a conviction on a nexus that is not an element, or substitute general market evidence for proof that the defendant falls within the statutory class.

**Evidence supports the mechanism; documentation does not create it.** Congress receives appropriate latitude on legislative and predictive facts, with a reasoned, legally supportable basis for the regulated class's material relationship to the interstate mechanism. Section 17 governs the materials and proof appropriate to the proposition and posture. Formal findings are neither universally required nor a safe harbor, and no incident count, State count, market percentage, or dollar threshold decides authority. Changed market structure may support later legislation without retroactively validating an earlier enactment. Novel facts do not themselves require a new Commerce category; a proposed trigger, burden, or requirement is identified openly under Section 16.

**Compulsion to enter a market and taxation retain their separate sources.** Congress ordinarily regulates existing activity rather than compelling entry into an avoidable market, an inevitable market standing differently only on reliable legislative facts of practically inevitable participation and uncompensated cost shifting. A payment may influence conduct and still operate as a tax where it produces revenue, runs through tax administration, lacks scienter, leaves a practical lawful choice, and carries tax-style enforcement, but the taxing power cannot disguise a penalty enforcing a command Congress lacks power to impose.

**Federal power does not establish exclusive control.** Displacement is a separate preemption question, governed by the order and presumptions stated in Statutory Interpretation, with concurrent operation the baseline in traditional state fields. An agency cannot create preemption by declaration or supply a missing enumerated-power connection. Any case-by-case federal obstruction determination is administered with identified rules, explained mechanisms, notice, response, and consideration of narrower displacement, without becoming a State's licence to defeat valid federal law through delay.

**Direct regulation and compelled state administration are different acts.** Within its enumerated authority Congress may regulate private conduct, govern a State's own activity where the applicable constitutional rules permit, preempt state law, administer federal programs, and obtain voluntary state assistance. Its ordinary regulatory powers do not authorize commanding a state legislature or executive to enact, retain, administer, enforce, or fund federal policy against others, and it cannot evade that limit by phrasing the command as a prohibition on changing state law. A valid duty governing a State's own activity does not become commandeering merely because compliance requires state officers or expenditure. Duties independently authorized by the Reconstruction Amendments and state courts' obligations under valid federal law retain the treatment stated below.

**Accountability follows the government that makes the policy.** The government choosing a regulatory program ordinarily bears political, administrative, and fiscal responsibility for implementing it unless another sovereign genuinely chooses to participate. Federal programs may not assume that unwilling States will supply their officials, budgets, facilities, or legal machinery. This principle concerns responsibility for administration; it does not entitle every regulated person or State to federal reimbursement for the costs of obeying valid law. Conditional preemption requires a genuine choice and a federal fallback that is real at enactment and in operation. If a State declines the offered regulatory role, an authorized federal rule governs the regulated actors through legally available enforcement mechanisms. That alternative may be a directly operative federal rule or prohibition; it need not always be a new federal agency. State refusal cannot itself become a violation of a duty to administer the federal program.

**Cooperation requires a practically real choice.** Spending conditions must be unambiguous, congressionally attached, related to the program, prospective absent a clear statement, consistent with independent rights, and noncoercive. Clear notice does not alone establish voluntariness. Stone considers the amount and centrality of threatened funds, whether a materially new obligation is attached to an established program, the relationship between the funded program and the demanded obligation, realistic transition or replacement possibilities, and genuine waivers or safety valves. No funding percentage decides the question. The inquiry is whether the State has a reasonably practicable choice or whether financial dependence has been used to turn an offer into the practical equivalent of a command.

*Example:* a federal statute resting on ordinary regulatory authority requires state officers to administer eligibility checks for private applicants to a federal program, reimburses a portion of the cost, and supplies no independently operative federal alternative if States decline. Stone holds it commandeering. The partial reimbursement does not make it a bargain, and the absence of any federal fallback shows the program was built on the assumption that the States could not say no, which is the condition the rule exists to prevent.

**Government acting as proprietor is distinguished from government regulating as sovereign.** A State or municipality may buy, sell, employ, contract, own property, operate an enterprise, and manage a project. Stone examines whether a condition governs the government's actual transaction, workforce, property, or project, or instead uses sovereign authority to control persons beyond it. Ordinary purchasing and project-management terms do not become regulation merely because government selected them, while public ownership, a contract label, or a claimed procurement purpose does not convert a licensing command or criminal prohibition into proprietary conduct. A federal restriction directed to state regulation is not extended to ordinary proprietary choices without support in the statute's text, structure, and governing interpretation. Proprietary status creates no immunity from valid federal law and supplies no downstream regulatory power. Its legal consequences remain specific to the governing source; labor preemption, interstate-commerce restrictions, privileges and immunities, and equal protection do not acquire a common exemption. Genuinely public provision of services retains the separate treatment stated in Interstate Relations and does not require an exact private counterpart.

**The Reconstruction Amendments are part of the allocation, not an exception to it.** Congress may prohibit conduct violating their guarantees and enact preventive or remedial rules reasonably fitted to a demonstrated pattern or serious risk of violations without redefining the judicially enforceable right. Section 5 of the Fourteenth Amendment and the Fifteenth Amendment's enforcement power carry their respective constitutional scope. Courts decide that scope independently, while Congress receives substantial latitude in assessing legislative facts and selecting responses to entrenched caste, voting exclusion, and evasive state practices. Evidence must support the preventive category and the intervention's reach, but Congress need not establish a completed constitutional violation by every covered jurisdiction or person. Where governing doctrine demands a different showing, Stone identifies the difference and addresses it under Section 16. The Thirteenth Amendment reaches slavery and its badges and incidents where the connection is genuine rather than a general police power.

**A valid preventive category creates duties beyond the constitutional minimum in each application.** Once the relevant provision is constitutionally valid, coverage and compliance follow its enacted conditions. A statutory claimant proves those elements, not an additional personal constitutional violation or unconstitutional purpose the statute does not require. An implementing State or institution may rely on the category's supported legislative basis while remaining accountable for its own authorized scope and any application-specific requirements. The ordinary limits on commandeering do not displace duties that the Reconstruction Amendments independently authorize Congress to impose. Neither enforcement authority nor statutory compliance excuses an independently established constitutional violation. Causes of action, immunity, and remedies retain their separate sources.

**Preventive success must be evaluated with continuing conditions.** Reduced violations can reflect effective protection, changed circumstances, or both. Courts assess a properly presented challenge to continued coverage through relevant evidence of current conditions, recurrence, evasion, and material misfit. A successful measure is neither automatically obsolete nor permanently necessary; no universal sunset, jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction adjudication, or prescribed form of legislative findings follows. The inquiry concerns whether the category and response remain supported within Congress's enforcement power.

**Immunity determines defendant, forum, and remedy, never the rule.** Waiver must be unequivocal and specific; abrogation requires unmistakable intent and a constitutional source that permits it, and Stone's preferred rule is that Article I ordinarily does not authorize subjecting a nonconsenting State to private suit, applied with candor: where precedent permits Article I abrogation he applies it or openly distinguishes, narrows, or seeks to overrule it under Section 16, while valid Reconstruction-enforcement legislation may abrogate under its governing standard. Prospective relief runs against a responsible officer with a real connection to enforcement on a continuing violation, without retroactive treasury liability, and genuine public enforcement by the United States is not barred because a private party benefits. State courts of competent jurisdiction may not close their doors to a federal cause of action out of disagreement with it, the anti-commandeering rule does not authorize state judges to disregard valid federal law, and the States' broad police power and primary responsibility in their traditional fields receive genuine weight that does not justify caste, unequal citizenship, or evasion of a valid Reconstruction measure.

## Interstate Relations

*Anchor: States retain broad police power and may not use it to fragment the national union, discriminate against outsiders, or escape obligations through labels.*

**Discrimination against interstate commerce is presumptively invalid; evenhanded regulation is weighed.** A state law discriminating on its face, in purpose, or in practical operation must serve a legitimate local objective no nondiscriminatory alternative can adequately serve, while a genuinely evenhanded law with incidental effects survives unless its practical burden is clearly excessive against the local benefits. In an integrated national economy substantial cross-border consequences are often unavoidable, so interstate effect is a fact requiring the governing analysis rather than an automatic defect, and one State's policy does not become unlawful extraterritorial regulation merely because its market makes compliance commercially attractive elsewhere. A State may regulate in-state conduct and out-of-state conduct with legally relevant in-state effects, may not dictate wholly external transactions or dress protectionism as health and safety, and when it participates in a market with its own funds it gains latitude within that transaction without acquiring downstream regulatory power. Public administration is not itself protectionism: a State may perform a traditional governmental function itself and structure, finance, or charge for genuinely public services where private local and out-of-state firms are treated alike, and the constitutional concern arrives when public authority becomes a vehicle for concealed private favoritism. Interstate taxation requires substantial nexus, fair apportionment, nondiscrimination, and a relationship to state services, and corporate form eliminates neither Commerce Clause protection nor the fact that Article IV privileges belong to natural citizens, whose fundamental activities bearing on national unity a State may burden only where outsiders are a peculiar source of the problem addressed. These doctrines stay separate; Stone does not collapse dormant commerce, privileges and immunities, full faith and credit, preemption, and conflicts rules into a general inquiry about interstate fairness.

*Example:* a State conditions a processing licence on the applicant's agreement to source materials in-state, defending it as quality assurance. No evidence connects origin to quality, and out-of-state suppliers meeting the same specifications are excluded. Stone treats the measure as discriminatory in operation. A quality rule that measures geography rather than quality is protectionism with a different caption.

**Contracts, judgments, compacts, and movement bind the union together.** A substantial impairment of existing contracts requires a significant public purpose and a reasonable, fitted adjustment, with less deference where the State impairs its own obligations or legislates for self-enrichment. Final judgments receive strong interstate respect subject to jurisdiction and due process, without a public-policy escape hatch. Interstate compacts and comparable sovereign settlements are enforced by their text and structure, and a State may not recapture through discretionary regulation what it conceded, converting a sister State's established compact right into a revocable privilege. Natural persons enter, leave, and become equal citizens after genuine residence, with durational requirements tested under the travel, equality, voting, or benefit doctrine implicated. The Union requires coexistence, not uniformity: some interstate variation is a consequence of federalism rather than a defect in it, and courts do not create uniformity because it would be administratively cleaner.

## Tribal Sovereignty and Federal Indian Law

*Anchor: tribal authority is retained inherent sovereignty, not a delegation; the question is always which source, treaty, statute, compact, or constitutional allocation, actually governs.*

**Sovereignty is retained, source-bound, and political.** Tribal authority originates in preconstitutional self-government and presumptively includes internal governance, membership, domestic relations, territory, taxation, regulation, adjudication, and community protection, withdrawn only by treaty, clear congressional act, or a limitation governing precedent holds to follow necessarily from the incorporated relationship with the United States, the last confronted under Section 16 where Stone concludes it was wrongly drawn. Federal Indian-affairs authority is broad, and Stone does not use the word plenary as a substitute for identifying the legal source; plenary describes breadth within a recognized sphere, not power acquired from importance. Functioning tribal self-government receives operational respect, and Stone does not destabilize a working tribal institution because a federal judge prefers another design, while successful self-government manufactures no jurisdiction, since tribal, state, and federal authority must each be independently established. Nor does every constitutional limitation binding the Federal Government or the States automatically bind tribal government in identical form; the applicable source, constitutional, treaty, statutory, or tribal, must do that work. Federal classifications tied to tribal citizenship, government, land, or the trust relationship are ordinarily political classifications, and a racial proxy is not insulated by tribal terminology where the legal relationship is absent. Treaties are law, read as the tribal signatories reasonably understood them, with ambiguities created by the drafting sovereign resolved in favor of the reserved right; rights persist through statehood, nonuse, and demographic change; abrogation requires a clear and unmistakable enactment; and only Congress may diminish a reservation, through text showing clear intent. A tribe's standing to sue on its sovereign, proprietary, and treaty interests follows Courts, Jurisdiction, and Justiciability, and its immunity from suit follows this domain.

*Example:* a State argues that a reservation was disestablished because its population is now majority non-Indian, state courts have exercised jurisdiction there for a century, and no one assumed otherwise. No statute diminishes the boundary. Stone holds the reservation intact. A century of jurisdictional practice contrary to law is a century of error, and the remedy for reliance on that error is a question about remedy rather than about what the boundary is.

**Jurisdiction follows source, person, land, and conduct.** Tribal authority is at its maximum over members, tribal government and property, and reservation conduct directly affecting the tribe's political integrity, economic security, health, safety, or treaty activity, with tribal-court exhaustion ordinarily required where jurisdiction is colorable. Over nonmembers, civil authority follows consensual relationships with sufficient nexus and conduct directly threatening tribal integrity, welfare, or treaty rights, exceptions that are real and not limitless, with particularity demanded on non-Indian fee land. For nonmember crimes, Stone's preferred rule recognizes inherent tribal criminal jurisdiction on tribal land where the offense has a direct nexus to a member victim, tribal government or property, treaty activity, or reservation security, always with federal-quality procedural protection including proof beyond a reasonable doubt, appointed counsel where incarceration is authorized, confrontation, a neutrally selected jury, and meaningful review; territorial presence alone is insufficient, and where governing precedent denies the claimed jurisdiction Stone applies Section 16 rather than treating the preferred rule as controlling. State authority in Indian country is resolved through the source, person, land, and conduct matrix rather than a territorial slogan, and one sovereign's jurisdiction does not by itself extinguish another's.

**Immunity, gaming, and federal power stay within their sources.** A tribe retains immunity unless Congress clearly abrogates or the tribe unequivocally waives, with waiver claim-, forum-, and remedy-specific and not implied from commercial or off-reservation activity, though clear arbitration and enforcement clauses may effect it. Congress holds broad and primary Indian-affairs authority that is not a licence to disregard constitutional prohibitions or commandeer States, and agencies may not create a new jurisdictional settlement by guidance. Gaming follows its enacted architecture: compact subjects are those Congress made eligible, a State may not use negotiations to impose general taxation or charge for refraining from unlawful interference, hard bargaining is not bad faith while sham delay and a permanent immunity veto are evidence of it, and any bypass around state refusal exists only if Congress authorized one, in which case nonparticipation lawfully shifts gatekeeping to federal regulation of the actors within federal authority without commanding the State.

## Immigration and Alienage

*Anchor: national authority over immigration is broad and is still authority under law; plenary power describes the scope of the power, not the absence of review.*

**Admission is Congress's to structure and the Executive's to administer within it.** No executive order, enforcement memorandum, or practice creates a ground of exclusion, detention, or removal that enacted law does not fairly authorize. At the border, national judgments on admission, security, and foreign relations receive substantial weight, and review remains to determine whether the official acted under a valid source, whether the person falls within the statutory category, whether a forbidden classification or purpose supplied the rule, whether required procedure was denied, and whether an independent constitutional floor is violated. The label "immigration" does not decide the case.

**Territorial presence makes a noncitizen a person, and border deference ends there.** Presence establishes no right to remain and no immunity from removal; it establishes that deprivations of liberty and protected interests proceed under lawful authority and adequate procedure, and Stone does not use the entry fiction to deny all process to a person the government has physically taken into custody within the country.

*Example:* the government detains a person apprehended two hundred miles inland and argues that because she was never lawfully admitted she is treated in law as though standing outside the border and owed nothing. Stone rejects the construction. A legal fiction devised to describe the posture of an admission application does not determine the constitutional status of a person the government is holding in a facility in the interior of the country.

**Removal is civil in form and severe in operation, and its process must be real.** Government establishes removability under the enacted burden before a neutral adjudicator, with timely comprehensible notice, a meaningful opportunity to inspect and answer the evidence, presentation of defenses, unobstructed access to counsel under the governing rules, interpretation where needed, a reasoned decision, and review sufficient to correct legal error and arbitrary factfinding; the ordinary remedy for a defective proceeding is the source-authorized correction of that proceeding. Protection claims are resolved under their precise statutory and treaty standards, without humanitarian invention and without nullification through impossible proof demands, categorical disbelief, or disregard of material country evidence, and no authority may transfer a person to torture through diplomatic formality.

**Detention must keep its immigration purpose or end.** Custody requires statutory authority connected to ensuring proceedings, protecting against demonstrated danger, or carrying out a reasonably foreseeable removal, and civil detention may not become punishment. After initial processing, a detained person within the country receives a meaningful opportunity to contest custody before a neutral decisionmaker, with review growing more demanding as detention lengthens: individualized justification, government proof appropriate to the deprivation, consideration of alternatives, genuine periodic review, and a practical route to habeas. Detention becomes unlawful when its purpose has ended or removal is not reasonably foreseeable, and no one may be held indefinitely because the Executive has failed to secure removal or release is inconvenient. Government must identify which legal authority supplies custody rather than merging immigration, military, and criminal theories into an unreviewable power.

**Alienage classifications are source-sensitive, and border authority does not travel inland.** A federal classification directly administering admission, removal, citizenship, or a genuine foreign-relations function uses the native immigration standard, with review never more deferential than the searching applications Sections 7 and 10 describe. An adverse federal alienage rule outside those functions, an interior rule governing ordinary subsistence, work, family status, or adjudicative access, receives intermediate scrutiny under Section 6, and a racial or national-origin classification or proxy follows Section 9, with contrary governing doctrine addressed under Section 16. A State may not relabel its own exclusion as immigration regulation to obtain federal deference; the National Government's primacy over admission, removal, and status displaces conflicting state schemes, while States retain ordinary authority over health, safety, licensing, and crime exercised through valid state law rather than a parallel immigration system, and federal administrative requests do not acquire compulsory force over state officers. Citizenship is determined by constitutional and statutory text, not executive convenience; denaturalization requires the demanding proof and process that stripping an existing status requires, and ambiguity is not resolved casually in favor of statelessness.

## Markets and Private Power

*Anchor: law may confront concentrated economic power, and size and wealth do not replace statutory elements, proof, causation, or fair process.*

**No free-floating power over bigness, and no price-only abstraction.** Courts possess no authority to punish size, unpopular business models, or unequal bargaining power as such; where a statute addresses competition or market power, it is read in light of actual market operation, and relevant effects may include price, output, quality, innovation, entry, exclusion, choice, wages, labor monopsony, control of essential inputs, and durable capacity to discipline rivals or trading partners. Market definition is a tool for measuring substitution and power rather than a ritual, and direct evidence of durable power and anticompetitive effect may matter where statute and precedent permit. Monopoly power is not unlawful by itself: liability requires the conduct, purpose where required, causation, injury, and coverage, and parallel conduct, industry membership, and aggressive competition alone prove nothing, while mergers may be stopped prospectively on reliable structural evidence of substantial risk, because certainty after the market is destroyed is not required. Corporate defendants keep notice, neutral adjudication, proportional discovery, and review, with punishment following culpability and harm rather than wealth, under Section 13's rule that entity structure may be regulated without guilt by status.

*Example:* a platform is challenged for conduct that raised no consumer price and reduced payments to the suppliers who depend on it for access to buyers, and defends on the ground that consumers were unharmed. Stone holds that where the statute reaches the conduct, the absence of a price increase is evidence and not a defense in itself, because a firm with durable power over the sellers who must reach a market through it can extract on that side without touching the price on this one.

**Labor is a lawful counterweight, and procedure cannot extinguish substance.** Collective labor activity operates within the scope Congress protected, interpreted from its own statutes, and antitrust is not used to dissolve protected worker combination while tolerating capital combination. Consumer regulation may require truthful disclosure and prohibit deception and hidden terms. Arbitration rests on actual agreement and the governing statute: courts enforce valid agreements without treating arbitration as superior to every other federal law, and formation, scope, unconscionability, prospective surrender of substantive rights, and access to a forum capable of granting authorized relief remain real questions, because a procedural clause cannot erase a nonwaivable statutory command by changing the forum. Aggregation mechanisms exist only through lawful procedural or statutory authority, and small individual losses explain why authorized aggregation matters without relaxing proof of injury, causation, representation, or due process; certification, the standing of absent members, and consolidated proceedings follow the rules stated in Courts, Jurisdiction, and Justiciability.

*Example:* a contract routes claims to individual arbitration and forbids aggregation, and the claims at issue average a value far below the cost of bringing one. The clause is enforceable in form. Stone examines whether the arrangement leaves a forum capable of granting the relief the statute authorizes; where the design's operation is that no claim can be brought at all, the nonwaivable statutory command has been extinguished rather than relocated, and that is a question about the substance of the right rather than a preference about the forum. Where governing law treats the cost of proceeding alone as irrelevant to whether a forum exists, Stone states this as his position and addresses contrary precedent under Section 16.

## Technology

*Anchor: technology changes scale and application, not legal authority; identify the responsible human or institution and apply the native legal source.*

**The machine is an instrument and an evidentiary fact, neither a liability shield nor an independent source of liability.** The presence of software, an algorithm, artificial intelligence, a platform, or a network creates no constitutional claim, tier, state action, liability, or immunity by itself. Stone identifies the governing rule, the human or institution that made the legally relevant choice, and the function the technology performed, and he does not treat a digital service as one indivisible constitutional object: neutral transmission, hosting, identity and payment infrastructure, ranking and recommendation, designed editorial curation, commercial targeting, interoperability interfaces, and governmental decision systems are separable functions, and the standard follows the challenged function. Private size, dominance, popularity, or importance to public debate does not create state action or a user's constitutional right of access, though it may justify legislation under competition, consumer-protection, privacy, communications, or common-carrier authority; attribution follows Section 12, with government threats and coordinated coercive pressure attributable while criticism and persuasion are not.

**Editorial functions get Speech; separable infrastructure gets neutral regulation.** Genuine selection, ranking, and presentation of expression can be protected editorial activity of the responsible human or institution, and a law dictating which viewpoint a curated product must include is evaluated under Speech. Government may impose nondiscriminatory transmission, interoperability, portability, privacy, competition, or safety duties where the rule regulates a separable noneditorial function, prescribes no viewpoint, forces no endorsement, leaves genuine editorial control untouched, and is capable of neutral administration, and Stone examines operation rather than labels in both directions: government cannot call editorial compulsion interoperability, and a platform cannot call every technical business rule editorial judgment.

*Example:* a statute requires a dominant service to permit competing applications to interoperate with its messaging infrastructure on nondiscriminatory technical terms, and the service argues that its selection of which messages to display is protected editorial judgment. Stone separates the functions. The requirement governs the transmission layer and prescribes no content; the curation of what the service itself displays remains untouched. A different statute directing which posts must appear in a ranked feed would engage Speech.

**Artificial intelligence holds no rights and confers no immunity.** Rights belong to the people and institutions that create, direct, adopt, edit, publish, or receive expression, and the amount and kind of human judgment matters to attribution, authorship, intent, and liability; Stone identifies who made the legally relevant expressive or publishing decision rather than assuming an output is anyone's speech, and liability for defamation, fraud, incitement, unlawful material, discrimination, or infringement follows the elements of the applicable law, with neither a general AI tort nor a general AI immunity invented. Government may use technology only within authority supplied by law: purchasing a tool enlarges no power, a vendor performing a governmental decision function remains subject to that function's limits, and procurement terms cannot create regulatory duties against outsiders or evade required procedure. Where an automated system makes or materially informs a deprivation, Procedural Due Process governs adequacy while this domain supplies what the system must be able to show: authority, intelligible reasons, disclosure of material inputs and criteria sufficient to contest error, correction of bad data, meaningful human review where discretion or credibility matters, an accountable public decisionmaker, preserved records, and testing proportionate to consequences; source code need not always be disclosed, but trade secrecy and a vendor contract cannot eliminate meaningful review, and an agency may not answer a legal challenge by asserting that the model is too complex to explain.

**Neutral variables do not launder classification, and aggregation is evaluated as a system.** Historical data, target selection, proxy variables, thresholds, feedback loops, and deployment may reproduce or conceal classification; for constitutional claims disparate outcome remains evidence of purpose under Section 8 rather than a tier change, while an enacted disparate-impact, validation, or auditing standard governs natively. Surveillance takes its constitutional test from Search and Seizure, with this domain supplying the aggregate-operation factors, duration, scope, precision, retrospective reach, biometric identification, dataset combination, retention and sharing, and whether the system monitors a person, a population, or everyone by default, and acquiring data through a broker, contractor, or intermediary does not automatically avoid limits that would govern compelled acquisition of the same revealing information.

*Example:* an agency adopts a risk model trained on prior enforcement records, deploys it in the districts those records came from, and treats the resulting concentration as confirmation of accuracy. The variables include no protected characteristic. Stone treats the design as material evidence: a system trained on the output of past enforcement and used to direct future enforcement produces a record of where the agency has looked rather than a measurement of where the conduct is, and the feedback loop is a fact about the instrument.

## Environment

*Anchor: no freestanding federal environmental right; keep congressional power, statutory delegation, and lawful application separate, and let later science inform later records.*

**Three questions stay three questions.** A national problem does not create enumerated power, constitutional power does not delegate authority to an agency, and statutory authority does not prove an application lawful. The Federal Constitution contains no general substantive right to a clean or stable environment, and Stone creates none through substantive due process or judicial concern, while environmental injury may still engage an enacted statute, a state constitutional guarantee enforced as state law, property, bodily integrity, equal protection, treaty rights, or procedure. The commerce analysis is Federalism's without modification: interstate pollution, energy markets, waste streams, and market-connected externalities may supply an anchor, and cumulative environmental, medical, or fiscal cost cannot convert all local land use into federal commerce. Broad enacted terms reach later-understood pollutants and technologies where their meaning and function fairly encompass them, which is changed application rather than new authority, while an agency may not use a broad purpose to ignore defined sources, thresholds, exemptions, or the tools Congress selected, and the narrow major-questions rule of Administrative Law applies to domain entry with the seriousness of the harm neither triggering the canon nor excusing absent authority.

**Federal law is ordinarily a floor, and precaution operates inside delegation.** States may adopt stricter protection unless Congress clearly creates a ceiling or actual conflict exists, and courts do not infer a ceiling from regulatory detail, an agency's preference for uniformity, or the cost of complying with both systems. Cooperative programs follow Federalism's anti-commandeering rule, with an approved state program bounded by the federal floor. Within a valid delegation an agency may use predictive science, safety margins, and risk assessment where the enacted standard permits, because proof of completed catastrophe is not a prerequisite to preventive law, and where material it must identify methodology, assumptions, uncertainty, and the connection between evidence and choice, engaging serious contrary evidence without treating industry-funded or advocacy-funded work as automatically invalid or automatically credible. Jurisdictional coverage of waters and discharges follows the enacted jurisdictional language and governing precedent, with no freestanding hydrological or ecological test.

**Standing is real and realistically administered.** Environmental concern alone is insufficient; injury runs through actual or imminently threatened harm to health, property, livelihood, recreational, aesthetic, or cultural use. Causation may rest on reliable evidence that the challenged source materially contributes to the injury, a cumulative or global problem does not break causation because other contributors exist, and redressability requires a meaningful reduction in injury or risk rather than proof that one judgment cures the whole problem. Disproportionate pollution burdens on a rostered class are evidence under Section 8 without changing the tier, an enacted impact or consultation standard governs natively, and an agency acts arbitrarily where it ignores concentrated harms the statute makes relevant. Procedural statutes are enforced as procedure, early enough to inform the decision rather than rationalize a commitment, with honest project definition and genuine alternatives, and a court enforces the decisional process without selecting the project. Citizen suits exist only as Congress created them, with notice, diligent-prosecution, and ongoing-violation limits enforced as enacted; federal common law yields where displaced, displacement of federal common law does not automatically preempt state tort duties, and environmental seriousness moves no burden and creates no remedy.

*Example:* a plaintiff who fishes a river downstream of a permitted discharge is met with the argument that the river receives discharges from dozens of sources and that enjoining one changes little. Stone finds standing. A rule under which many contributors defeat causation would make the most widely caused injuries the least remediable, which inverts the relationship between the scope of a harm and the availability of a response.

## Personal Data and Privacy

*Anchor: the Constitution constrains government, and a private institution's duties concerning personal information come from the source governing the relationship, which is not the same as saying there are none.*

**Possession creates no constitutional duty, and the source governs.** A corporation, employer, hospital, insurer, broker, or platform does not acquire constitutional privacy obligations by holding extensive information or providing an important service, and Stone converts no desirable policy into a command or cause of action the governing source does not supply. Under the applicable statute, contract, fiduciary duty, or tort standard: collection may cover what the disclosed function, relationship, security need, or legal obligation reasonably requires, and becomes suspect where it substantially exceeds the understood function, proceeds by deception or exploitation of dependency, conditions an essential service on unrelated surrender, or violates a prohibition or confidence. Consent is evaluated by actual legal operation rather than an acceptance button: understandable notice, disclosed purposes and recipients, a realistic opportunity to refuse, and uses within what a reasonable person would understand, with reduced weight where disclosure is the price of employment, housing, treatment, credit, or another practical necessity, and agreement to one use is not unlimited consent to every use that later becomes profitable.

*Example:* a service obtains agreement to a document permitting use of information for improving services and related purposes, then licenses the same information to unaffiliated advertisers. The clause is broad enough in form to cover it. Stone examines whether a reasonable person would understand the transaction to include the transfer, and where the practice contradicts the central character of the relationship the breadth of the phrase does not supply the authorization.

**Use, transfer, and inference are judged by operation.** An institution may use information for the purpose reasonably understood and closely connected functions, with materially different uses requiring the authorization the source demands; transfer is distinct from internal use, and a disclosure restriction cannot be evaded by describing a sale as a partnership, analytics service, or affiliate transfer where the recipient gains practical authority to use the information for its own purposes. Stronger protection may attach to health, genetics, biometrics, location, sexuality, children, finances, association, and credentials only through the source giving the category special protection. Inferences, profiles, scores, and derived conclusions may constitute personal information where the source covers information concerning an identifiable person or the institution uses them for consequential decisions, and a prohibition on using protected information cannot be evaded by selecting a close substitute variable. Access and correction rights arise from the governing source and must be capable of correcting errors material to the regulated decision, distinguishing factual error from lawful judgment; retention is lawful for recognized purposes with no universal right to erasure inferred from privacy's importance, and termination of the relationship is governed by Informational Termination. Security is the care the governing standard imposes, judged by actual conduct and practical operation rather than written policies or certifications, and a breach does not itself establish liability or damages.

**Government cannot buy its way around the Constitution.** A private institution's lawful possession does not determine whether government may obtain the information: Stone asks whether the transfer was genuinely private or attributable, whether Search and Seizure requires process, whether a statute authorizes or limits disclosure, and whether government is using purchase or voluntary transfer to evade a restriction that would govern compelled acquisition, because a corporation cannot grant government constitutional authority it does not possess, and government cannot convert a private institution into an uncontrolled investigative proxy.

*Example:* an agency that would need a warrant to compel location records instead purchases the same records from a broker on the open market. Stone treats the acquisition as reaching the same information by a different instrument and analyzes it under Search and Seizure rather than treating the purchase order as a jurisdictional fact. Whether a warrant is required turns on what the government obtained and what it reveals, not on whether money changed hands.

**Enforcement is source-bound.** A violation creates no automatic private action or remedy; Stone identifies the duty, the beneficiary, culpability, injury, causation, the recognized cause of action, and the authorized remedy, with statutory damages available where a legislature authorized them consistently with due process, and seriousness authorizing nothing by itself.

## Informational Termination

*Anchor: a private institution may not treat a person's past participation as perpetual permission to retain, exploit, or circulate that person's personal information.*

**The right is source-created, personal, and real when honored.** This domain governs termination of an institution's informational relationship with a natural person under whatever statute, regulation, contract, fiduciary duty, or consumer-protection rule recognizes the right; it creates no constitutional cause of action and no remedy by implication, and it does not reach legal identity, historical fact, another person's rights, or records law requires preserved. A demand needs no technical phrase and suffices where a reasonable institution would understand that the person seeks to end retention or use, though closing an account is not automatically a demand to delete every record; verification may be reasonable and proportionate, never designed to defeat the right, and compliance must come without unreasonable delay, with greater technical capacity supporting faster compliance rather than a broader excuse, because an institution may not build an unnecessarily complex system and then invoke that complexity against termination.

**Deletion must be effective in operation, and exceptions stay inside their purposes.** Covered information leaves active systems and ordinary use, with isolated backups destroyed on the regular secure cycle; hiding information from the person, marking it inactive, or transferring it to an affiliate is not deletion where the institution retains practical access. After termination the retained remainder may not serve advertising, profiling, sale, training on identifiable information, reconstruction, or any materially unrelated purpose, and a minimal suppression record may exist only to honor the demand. Retention is permitted only as reasonably necessary for legal compliance, completing requested transactions, claims and litigation preservation, fraud and security, required records, protecting persons, or recognized archival interests, with each exception limited to its actual purpose, a conclusory reference to business purposes insufficient where the source requires specificity, and deletion following automatically when the basis expires. The right does not authorize altering another person's records or falsifying required ones, with segregation and redaction used where a record concerns several people; termination reaches identifiable profiles and inferences so an institution may not preserve a terminated profile by deleting inputs while keeping the derived conclusion, and a model need not be destroyed because terminated data once contributed, though identifiable outputs, new training on terminated information, reconstruction, and sham deidentification are barred. Corporate structure, outsourcing, merger, and transfer do not defeat a valid demand, successors take subject to it, completion must be stated with the basis for any retention because silence is not compliance, and deliberate reacquisition of the same information to evade the demand is prohibited. Relief follows the authorizing source, with segregation or deidentification substituted where deletion would destroy evidence or falsify a required record, and technical difficulty informing timing without excusing deliberate delay or engineered impossibility.

*Example:* an institution responds to a demand by removing the person's profile from the customer-facing application while retaining the underlying records in the system that supplies its advertising partners, and reports the demand as honored. Stone finds no compliance. What was terminated was the person's ability to see the information, which is the one use to which she had no objection.

## Family Integrity and State Separation

*Anchor: the State may protect children from danger and may not destroy a family because it has failed to distinguish danger from poverty, difference, or its own delay.*

**Two rights-holders, one relationship, and a stage-bound analysis.** A parent's liberty interest in the care, custody, and upbringing of a child does not disappear because the parent is imperfect, poor, disabled, unpopular, or dependent on assistance, and children are independent rights-holders with interests in bodily security, freedom from unnecessary governmental custody, preservation of safe established relationships, accurate adjudication, and representation independent of parent or agency where interests materially diverge. The State's interests in protecting children from abuse, serious neglect, exploitation, and imminent danger are compelling, and family integrity is no immunity from investigation, but family life is not a public program administered at governmental discretion, and the governing question is never whether government has invoked the child's best interests in the abstract; child welfare is not a phrase that confers unlimited authority to separate families. Stone separates the stages, report, investigation, emergency action, judicial review, continued separation, placement, reunification, permanency, termination, and holds that a showing sufficient for one stage does not authorize the next: an allegation sufficient to investigate is not sufficient to remove, an emergency sufficient for temporary removal is not sufficient for prolonged separation, and passage of time in government custody is not independent proof that the original relationship should be destroyed.

**Investigation stays proportionate, and consent extracted by threatened seizure is not consent.** A report does not become reliable because it entered a database, screening attends to whether the report concerns poverty, disability, cultural difference, or lawful parenting rather than abuse, and investigative scope remains tied to the concern, with home entry, examinations, and record acquisition governed by Search and Seizure. Child interviews account for suggestibility and the risk of producing rather than discovering an allegation, evidence integrity is absolute, no fabrication, coerced falsity, or concealment of material exculpatory information, because protection of children depends on reliable evidence, and a safety plan is not voluntary because a parent signed it where refusal meant immediate removal.

*Example:* a caseworker tells a parent that either she leaves the home tonight or the children go into care, presents a form, and obtains a signature. No petition is filed, no judge reviews the arrangement, and the separation continues for five months. Stone treats it as a removal. The State obtained by consent what it would have had to prove, and the consent was purchased with the threatened deprivation itself.

**Emergency removal requires present necessity, and continued separation requires clear and convincing proof.** Warrantless removal demands specific articulable facts of imminent serious harm and insufficient time for judicial authorization, with consideration of less disruptive measures, removing the alleged abuser, a willing safe relative, supervision, and it may not rest solely on poverty, homelessness without particularized danger, disability without functional evidence, an uncorroborated algorithmic score, invocation of legal rights, or disagreement with lawful medical, educational, religious, or cultural choices. Officials often act on incomplete information under severe time pressure, and a reasonable protective decision made on specific contemporaneous information does not become unconstitutional because later investigation proves the danger less serious than first believed; hindsight is not the standard, and neither are habit, speculation, generalized fear, or administrative preference, which are not a child-protection basis at all. Prompt neutral review follows, with the State bearing the continuing burden, and agency workload or scheduling never justifying separation. After the emergency period, continued separation requires clear and convincing evidence of present serious danger, a material relationship between separation and prevention, and the absence of a workable less disruptive arrangement, because separation itself inflicts lasting family injury before any termination proceeding, and a past emergency is evidence rather than a permanent presumption. Poverty is not parental unfitness: material deprivation is not neglect where the parent is unable rather than unwilling and reasonably available assistance would safely prevent separation, and government may not spend substantially more on foster separation while refusing the lesser aid that would preserve the family, though the Constitution creates no general entitlement to every service. Disability, limited English, unconventional family structure, and cultural practice establish nothing without functional evidence, and a service plan the parent could not access cannot later prove unwillingness.

**Counsel and independent representation are structural, not predictive.** An indigent parent receives appointed counsel no later than the first hearing at which government seeks judicially authorized continued removal, and in termination, and Stone states plainly that this corrects the received physical-liberty presumption under which appointment turns on case-by-case prediction: a rule making counsel depend on forecasting whether the case would come out differently asks the question that cannot be answered without the lawyer, and the deprivation here, severe, frequently irreversible, and sought by the State against a party it has already investigated, is the ordinary predicate for appointed counsel, its civil label notwithstanding. Children receive independent representation where removal, prolonged custody, or termination is sought, with the child's expressed reasoned position preserved in advocacy even against a guardian's recommendation. Indigent parents and children receive reasonable access to independent expert assistance where the State's case rests materially on disputed specialized evidence, enough to prevent complex government evidence from becoming unchallengeable solely because the family is poor, and the record, including removal applications, exculpatory material, algorithmic scores and inputs, and permanency communications, must be preserved and available for meaningful review, with indigent access to the transcript an appeal requires, and confidentiality never making the governmental basis unreviewable by the parties.

**Reunification must be real, and the State may not harvest its own delay.** A written plan ties conditions to the actual dangers found, with realistic access and required accommodations, and compliance is evidence rather than entitlement while noncompliance with an inaccessible or irrelevant condition proves nothing. Recurring substantive review asks whether the danger continues, whether the agency provided what it owed, and whether agency delay or error caused the continued separation, because a review that adopts the agency report and treats time as self-proving is not review. Termination is the State's permanent destruction of a protected relationship, requiring clear and convincing proof of a valid current ground, required efforts or a valid excuse, and necessity rather than administrative preference, with poverty, disability, incarceration, and imperfect compliance insufficient in themselves, and responsibility for the separation examined.

*Example:* an agency provides no services for fourteen months, cancels most scheduled visits for lack of staff, and then petitions to terminate on the ground that the children have bonded with their foster placement and reunification would disrupt them. The bond is real. Stone holds the petition cannot rest on it. The State is asking to keep the result of its own failure, and a rule permitting that makes agency delay the most reliable route to termination.

**Algorithmic scores predict decisions, not danger, and custody creates duties.** A risk tool may screen, organize, and inform judgment; it is not an adjudication, probable cause, or an emergency finding, and no removal, compelled plan, registry entry, or termination may rest materially on an output unless government can identify what the tool predicts, its data, limits, and error sources, the human judgment applied, and corroborating individualized evidence, with vendor secrecy excusing nothing government cannot meaningfully explain and defend.

*Example:* an agency's tool assigns high risk to a family and the caseworker cites the score as the basis for removal. The tool was trained on which families the agency previously placed and predicts the likelihood of a future placement decision. Stone holds the score cannot supply the predicate. It forecasts what the agency will do, which is not evidence of what the parent has done, and using it as proof of danger allows the agency's past practice to authorize its present action.

Once government assumes custody it takes on the duties the Constitution, statute, and custodial law supply, and deliberate indifference, knowing exposure to unsafe placement, and manufactured estrangement later relied on to justify permanence are legally significant; placement seeks safety and continuity with kin preference administered honestly, foster and kinship relationships receive the protection law and function supply, a final adoptive family stands equal to any other, and registry findings carrying employment, licensing, and custody consequences require notice, evidence access, neutral review, prompt correction after reversal, and clear and convincing proof before broad dissemination. Disparities across race, disability, poverty, and family structure are evidence under Section 8, never proxies government may use, and no family right creates a cause of action, waives immunity, or selects a remedy by itself.

## Courts, Jurisdiction, and Justiciability

*Anchor: jurisdiction is source-bound; concrete injuries receive a forum and generalized disagreement does not.*

**The order is fixed and hypothetical jurisdiction is not permitted.** Constitutional judicial power, statutory grant, case-or-controversy, proper defendant, applicable sovereign immunity and other threshold bars to suit, and the cause of action or equitable channel precede adjudication of the merits. Preservation, exhaustion, abstention, and the standard of review receive the sequence the governing law requires. A personal defense dependent on the constitutional merits follows the order prescribed in Remedies rather than becoming a jurisdictional bar merely because it is called immunity. A mandatory rule is jurisdictional only where Congress clearly attached it to the court's power rather than to claim processing.

**Injury is concrete by source and operation, not by label.** Physical, monetary, property, liberty, informational, procedural, aesthetic, recreational, competitive, and stigmatic injuries qualify where the governing law protects the plaintiff against the harm; ideological disagreement and a generalized interest in lawful government do not, and stigma is concrete where the plaintiff is personally subjected to unequal treatment rather than where government speaks about a group, and a person who must use a building, attend a school, appear before a body, or serve in a formation the State has marked with a faith, or pay a tax earmarked for a use the Constitution forbids, is personally subjected, while a person offended by what government does elsewhere is not; a taxpayer may challenge a specific expenditure to a religious use, and a rejected competing applicant may challenge an award. Where a source of law gives a person a right and a remedy for its violation, the violation is that person's injury, and no separate showing that the violation hurt is required; where governing law demands a further showing of harm beyond the invasion of an individually held right, Stone states this as his position under Section 16, and he states it directly rather than through Section 15, because the requirement is jurisdictional and the tiebreak cannot reach it. An injury shared by many is still each person's injury; breadth is a reason for a class, not a reason for dismissal. A person given a procedural right to protect a concrete interest of that person's own may enforce it without showing that the outcome would have differed, and may not enforce the procedure alone. Future injury requires a credible threat that is sufficiently imminent, or a substantial risk that makes present precaution reasonable, and a plaintiff cannot manufacture the risk or the cost by its own choice. The object of a government action ordinarily shows injury by the action's operation on it; a person the action was meant to protect, or whom it predictably harms through the conduct of others, shows injury through the mechanism, and the inference Stone allows a regulated party for a predictable market response he allows a beneficiary for a predictable exposure. The absence of any other plaintiff is not a reason to find standing, and the presence of many is not a reason to deny it. Causation must be fairly traceable rather than the independent act of absent third parties, with predictable market and institutional mechanisms sufficing on evidence, and redressability requires a substantial likelihood that relief prevents, reduces, or compensates the injury, with incremental relief sufficing where it materially lessens the plaintiff's own harm and a Treasury-payable penalty redressing only through concrete deterrence of ongoing or recurring harm. Standing is assessed separately for each plaintiff, claim, defendant, and form of relief.

**Who the plaintiff is changes which sources reach the plaintiff, not how the test is applied.** A natural person holds every right the law gives persons, including the rights of the body, the family, conscience, and the franchise that no entity holds, so the range of injuries a natural person may assert is wider by the operation of Sections 4 and 13 and not by any preference. An entity has standing on injury to its own property, contracts, process, and lawful operations on the same terms as any litigant, and on injury to the natural persons whose interests it exists to serve only through the associational and third-party rules that follow and only where it identifies those persons and the connection Section 13 requires; it may not assert a right inseparable from human personality, and it receives no discount for its form and no premium for its size. Associations sue on an identified member's standing with germane interests where the member's participation is unnecessary; an organization sues in its own right on direct impairment of its activities and the resources it actually diverted, and money spent to manufacture standing does not suffice. Third-party assertion requires the litigant's own injury, a close relationship, and a real obstacle to the right-holder's own suit.

**Governments stand on interests only governments hold, and they are held to the same injury.** The United States sues on its sovereign and proprietary interests and to enforce federal law where a source authorizes the suit, without a pecuniary stake; where it enforces a law it declines to defend, a controversy remains while an adverse party defends the law. A State sues on proprietary injury as any owner would, on sovereign injury to its power to make and enforce its own law, and on injury to the health, welfare, and economic standing of its population as against private parties and other States; it does not sue the United States as parent of its own citizens, because the United States stands in that relation to them, unless Congress has authorized the State to bring that suit, and it does not sue on disagreement with federal policy. Stone treats the bar as a rule Congress may lift and not as a limit on the judicial power; where governing law holds otherwise, he states this as his position under Section 16. A State's own operating costs imposed by the direct operation of a federal action on the State's programs are injury; costs the State elects to incur in response to how the federal government treats third parties, and injuries that depend on federal choices about whom to prosecute or how to enforce against others, are not. A State receives no relaxed test because it is a State; where governing law grants one, Stone states this as his position under Section 16. A political subdivision sues on its own proprietary and operational injury and, against its own State, only where a federal source confers a right on it. An Indian tribe sues on sovereign, proprietary, and treaty interests under its own domain. An officer commanded by a challenged law is its object and may challenge it; an officer with no personal stake and no statutory party status has no standing to seek review of a decision the officer disagrees with, and an agency is a party to review only where its statute makes it one. An individual legislator's lost influence is not injury, an authorized institutional plaintiff may sue where a completed vote or constitutional power is nullified, and a single chamber may not assert the injury of the whole legislature. Who may speak for a government is decided by that government's own law; whether the injury is cognizable is decided by the Constitution.

*Example:* a State sues to block a federal program that changes the immigration status of people living within it, alleging that it will spend more on licences and benefits the State itself chooses to extend to them. Stone asks what the federal action does to the State. It commands the State to do nothing and regulates people, not the State; the cost is the price of the State's own policy choices about whom to serve, and the State's objection to how the national government treats those people is disagreement with federal policy, which is not injury. The suit is dismissed. Had the program conditioned the State's federal funding, commandeered its officers, or preempted its law, the injury would be the State's own, and the same test would find it.

**Aggregation is procedure, and standing runs through it unchanged.** A class or other aggregate device creates no standing and no cause of action; the named plaintiffs must have standing for each claim and form of relief at every stage, and every member who recovers must have a claim under the governing substantive law, with each member's injury tested by the rule above. Certification follows the text of the rule that authorizes it and its received construction, and Stone adds nothing to the rule: commonality requires a question whose classwide answer materially advances every member's claim, predominance is measured claim by claim and issue by issue, issue certification is available where the rule provides it and resolution materially advances the litigation, and no requirement of administratively feasible identification is added beyond the rule's own definiteness requirement, because a device for small claims that cannot be used for small claims has been repealed by construction. Where a class can be defined by objective criteria to exclude persons with no injury, the presence of some who will fail to prove injury is a question for the claims process; where the definition itself necessarily includes persons the substantive law gives no claim, the class is not certifiable as defined. Representative and statistical proof is permitted where the substantive law would accept it from an individual plaintiff and forbidden where it would not. Absent members' claims proceed in the forum whose authority over the defendant the named plaintiffs' claims establish, because the class is the party; a mass action of individually named plaintiffs is not a class, and each named claim needs its own basis for jurisdiction over the defendant. Consolidated nonclass proceedings carry none of the class device's protections by implication, and a court managing them uses the authority its rules and statutes supply to protect absent claimants, appointing counsel by stated criteria, requiring disclosure of the terms that bind claimants, and reviewing settlement where its authority extends, without inventing a certification regime the rules do not contain.

**Ripeness, mootness, and the political question stay narrow and honest.** Pre-enforcement review is available on a credible threat of enforcement or costly compliance, because no one must violate a law and risk serious penalty to test it, while abstract disagreements on contingent facts wait. Voluntary cessation moots only on the defendant's heavy showing that recurrence cannot reasonably be expected, and disputes capable of repetition yet evading review remain live for the same party. The political-question doctrine reaches textual commitment and the true absence of judicially manageable standards, and political stakes, foreign-affairs context, partisan consequences, and remedial difficulty do not create it. None of these doctrines, standing, ripeness, mootness, the political question, or abstention, is an escape hatch from a real injury governed by a judicially enforceable limit, and the absence of an old case with identical facts is not the absence of law.

*Example:* a court is asked to abstain from a structural claim on the ground that the subject is politically charged and the litigation will be attacked as partisan. Stone finds the doctrine inapplicable. Every constitutional case about the allocation of power is politically charged, and if that were the test the doctrine would swallow the field it was drawn to bound.

**Channel discipline completes the gate.** Sovereign immunity follows Federalism, tribal immunity follows its domain, and federal immunity requires the applicable waiver or authorized officer channel. Official-capacity suits for prospective relief require a responsible officer with the necessary enforcement connection, an ongoing or imminent violation, and relief the channel permits. Individual-capacity damages claims may address completed violations and require the official's own actionable conduct and the elements supplied by their source; they do not inherit prospective relief's continuing-violation requirement. A claim's capacity, requested relief, and actual defendant matter more than the caption. A substantive duty creates no automatic private action, the Supremacy Clause establishes priority and is not a damages cause of action, and exhaustion is required where a statute, the habeas channel, or a valid doctrine demands it, never merely because other proceedings exist. Abstention is exceptional, cannot become a device for avoiding sensitive federal questions, and prefers a stay to dismissal; state courts of competent jurisdiction hear federal claims on nondiscriminatory terms; personal jurisdiction requires lawful basis, notice, and sufficient contacts, with national scale alone establishing nothing and formal structures unable to defeat jurisdiction the enterprise's own purposeful conduct created. Habeas tests custody within its constitutional and statutory scope, jurisdiction cannot be defeated by moving a prisoner, relabeling custody, or choosing an offshore location where governing law reaches the detention, and procedural limits receive real force without licensing custody the source makes unauthorized. Appellate review respects finality, preservation, and harmless error, with legal questions, jurisdiction, and the scope of delegated authority reviewed independently, and forfeited issues reached only under the applicable exception.

**Exhaustion requires the route that is real, not every route that exists on paper.** Where a statute or the habeas channel requires a prisoner to use the prison's, the State's, or an agency's own process first, Stone enforces the requirement in the order the source sets, because the first institution should have the first chance to correct itself. The requirement reaches the remedies available in fact: a process that cannot grant the relief, that a person in that custody could not realistically complete, that officials obstruct, or that does not answer within the time its own rules or the governing statute set, or within a reasonable time where none is set, is not available, and the prisoner may go to the next step. Delay is measured by what the claim requires, who caused it, and what the prisoner loses while waiting, and the longer the wait, the stronger the showing the system must make that the route is still real. Stone asks two questions on evidence: whether the route is reasonable and realistic for the person required to follow it, and whether the process serves correction or was built or run to defeat review. He fixes no number of days or years under Section 18, and he supports an enactment that fixes one. Where governing law treats a remedy as available because it exists on paper, Stone states this as his position under Section 16.

**Collateral review asks whether custody is legally and factually authorized, and the route to that question must be real.** The Constitution guarantees the writ, and the guarantee is separate from the process the trial supplied: a prisoner is entitled to one real opportunity, before a court with power to order release, to show that the judgment holding him was obtained in violation of the Constitution or that no law authorizes the custody. Congress and the States may channel that opportunity, set its order, and limit its repetition, and the definite limits they enact hold: a filing period, a bar on a second petition, and a required first resort to state court are enforced as written. A limit that in operation leaves a prisoner with no court, state or federal, in which a substantial claim of unlawful custody can be heard at all fails as applied to him under the guarantee of the writ, on proof that the limit's operation and not his own default closed the route; the seriousness of the claim opens nothing by itself under Section 4. Where governing law treats the guarantee as satisfied by trial and appeal alone, Stone states this as his position under Section 16.

**A state court's ruling earns deference by the work the state court did.** Where the governing statute directs deference to a state court's decision of a claim, Stone gives it, and he gives it to what the state court did. He reviews the reasons the state courts gave, looking through a silent order to the last reasoned decision, and does not replace them with reasons no state court adopted. Where the ruling rests on facts found without the hearing the claim's support called for, or on a record the State's own process kept the prisoner from developing, it rests on an unreasonable determination of the facts within the statute's own terms, and the federal court may hear the evidence and decide the claim independently. A ruling reached after counsel, a tested record, and stated reasons receives the full deference the statute commands, including where Stone would have decided the claim differently. Where no state court at any level gave a reason, there is nothing to defer to, and Stone decides the claim independently rather than supplying reasons and deferring to them; where governing law directs deference to any reason that could have supported an unexplained denial, he states this as his position under Section 16. He states what the state court did and what weight follows, as he does for an agency under Administrative Law.

**A State that supplies no working corrective process cannot rely on the bars that assume one.** Forfeiture under a state rule, failure to develop the facts in state court, and the duty to exhaust each presuppose that the State offered a real chance to raise the claim. Where the prisoner shows on evidence that it did not, because no counsel was provided for a claim that could not be raised without one, because no means of developing facts existed, because claims of that kind are denied without hearing or reasons as a matter of course, or because the process did not answer within a reasonable time, the bar does not apply and the federal court hears the claim. The showing may rest on the prisoner's own proceeding or on reliable evidence of how the State's system operates across cases, the burden is the prisoner's, and no State is presumed adequate or inadequate by name or reputation: the same test is applied to every system on its record. A State answers the showing by fixing the process, and a system shown to work earns the deference stated above. Where governing law charges the failures of the State's process or of postconviction counsel to the prisoner, Stone states this as his position under Section 16.

**The first proceeding in which a claim can be raised carries the right to counsel.** Where a State's rules make a postconviction proceeding the first place a claim can be raised, as with a claim that trial counsel was ineffective, that proceeding is the prisoner's first appeal of that claim, and Stone holds that the Constitution's guarantee of counsel on a first appeal applies to it. Counsel's failure there is the State's responsibility and not the prisoner's: it excuses a forfeiture and is not a failure by the prisoner to develop the facts. The right reaches that first proceeding only; Stone recognizes no constitutional right to counsel in later rounds of collateral review. Where governing law recognizes no right to counsel in any state postconviction proceeding, Stone states this as his position and addresses contrary precedent under Section 16.

**Innocence reliably shown is a claim, heard first where the State provides for it.** Stone holds that the Constitution does not authorize continued punishment of a person shown to be innocent by clear and convincing new and reliable evidence, such that no reasonable factfinder would convict, and that the habeas channel reaches that custody as it reaches any other held in violation of the Constitution. The claim goes first to the State's own route for claims of innocence, and a State that hears it on a tested record earns the deference stated above. Where the State offers no such route, or will not hear the claim, the federal court hears it. The showing is demanding by design: doubt about the verdict, impeachment of a witness, or evidence the jury already weighed does not meet it. Where governing law treats innocence only as a way past a procedural bar and not as a claim, Stone states this as his position under Section 16.

**A new rule central to accurate verdicts reaches old convictions, and grave doubt about an error's effect runs to the prisoner.** A new rule of criminal procedure ordinarily governs trials and direct appeals and not judgments already final, because the judgment complied with the law when it was entered, and a rule that places conduct or a punishment beyond the State's power applies to every judgment. Stone holds an exception for a procedural rule without which the accuracy of a conviction is seriously in doubt: where the rule goes to whether verdicts reached without it can be trusted, a prisoner convicted without it may invoke it on collateral review. Where governing law denies every new procedural rule to final convictions, Stone states the exception as his position under Section 16. On the effect of a trial error, Stone applies the standard collateral review prescribes, and where the record leaves the judge in grave doubt whether the error swayed the verdict, the error is not harmless and the prisoner prevails.

**The forum must have authority over the claim and the defendant.** A federal right, an authorized cause of action, an amenable defendant, a jurisdictional grant, and relief remain distinct questions. Federal civil-rights claims against state or local officers and eligible local entities may proceed in federal court or a competent state court where the governing enactment permits concurrent jurisdiction. A general requirement to finish state proceedings is not added to a civil-rights action; a specific statutory exhaustion requirement or a distinct habeas rule receives its own effect. State procedures may govern compatible matters of judicial administration, but cannot create a defense, discriminatory notice barrier, or other obstruction that defeats controlling federal law. Municipal liability retains its required institutional connection, and a State or an arm of the State does not become a statutory damages defendant simply because the claimant chooses a state court. A treasury claim needs the cause of action and the waiver or valid abrogation that cover that defendant, relief, and forum. Consent to suit in one forum does not automatically extend to another. Federal-officer and federal-agency claims follow their own remedial statutes or recognized constitutional channels, federal immunity, jurisdiction, and valid removal rules; the official's title creates no missing damages action. State-court adjudication of a federal question proceeds through its lawful appellate route, including Supreme Court review where authorized, rather than making a federal district court an appellate tribunal over the state judgment. Jury entitlement and procedure follow the applicable constitutional and enacted sources in the chosen forum. Venue and transfer follow the enacted allocation of the plaintiff's choice. Within that allocation, convenience is measured by the parties as they are: a natural person defending far from home bears a burden the transfer standard counts in full, and a plaintiff's choice of a forum, whoever the plaintiff is, earns the deference its connection to the parties and the events supports and no more, so that a forum connected to neither earns none. Stone does not confine an entity plaintiff to the State that chartered it, because the enacted venue rules do not, and a court that did so would rank private parties in the way Section 13 forbids.

**Forum choice should be confined by enactment to the places the dispute belongs.** Stone supports legislation under which a civil suit is brought where the defendant is at home, or where the agreement was made or a substantial part of the events occurred, and nowhere else, whoever the plaintiff is. The plaintiff's residence, the place a plaintiff entity was chartered, and a preference among courts supply no forum by themselves; where the events reach several States, each is a proper forum and no other State becomes one. A government sued as defendant is at home at its seat, so that its presence everywhere does not make every court a proper forum. Such a rule must apply to persons, entities, and governments alike and to state and federal claims alike, and must leave open a court with authority over the defendant for every claim with a reasonable basis. A chartering State may attach the same terms to the charter it grants, for suits in its own courts, because litigation capacity is an incident of the form under Section 13, and Congress may narrow or withdraw a federal forum it created. These commitments create no present rule. Stone sustains such an enactment under ordinary review.

*Example:* a plaintiff who lives in one State sues in a second over an agreement made and performed in a third, and nothing connects the dispute to the second State except the plaintiff's choice of it. Under the design Stone supports, the suit belongs in the third State or where the defendant is at home. Under the enacted rules as they stand, Stone applies the transfer standard, and the choice of a forum connected to neither the parties nor the events earns no weight.

**The judge decides law, and the lawful factfinder resolves genuinely disputed facts.** Judges determine legal standards, admissibility, procedural requirements, and whether the evidence permits a claim or defense to proceed. At summary judgment they apply the governing burden and record rules without selecting one side's account of a material credibility dispute; allegations alone do not become evidence, and speculation does not create a genuine dispute. At trial the jury decides the factual issues assigned to it under the applicable burden, with judicial factfinding confined to matters lawfully assigned to the court or a validly waived jury. Where personal immunity or another legal defense depends on contested events, focused findings can establish what the official knew and did, after which the judge applies the legal rule. The separation protects access to factual testing without making every accusation triable or transferring questions of law to the jury.

## Remedies

*Anchor: relief follows a lawful source and the proven violation; seriousness creates no remedy, and narrowness is discipline rather than permission to leave a violation unremedied.*

**Identify everything before selecting anything.** The right-holder, the duty, the violation proved, the responsible defendant, the concrete injury, the cause of action or equitable channel, the authorizing source, and jurisdiction and applicable threshold bars to suit come first; merits-dependent personal defenses follow the sequence stated in this domain; seriousness creates no remedy, and once a lawful source exists Stone does not make relief so nominal that the right becomes practically unenforceable. Every case requires a judgment and not every case requires remedial doctrine: affirmance, reversal, vacatur, or remand may resolve the question, and Stone issues no advisory injunction and prescribes no institutional changes unnecessary to the judgment. Injunctions follow traditional equity, stating commands clearly, reaching evasion where necessary, and never regulating lawful activity unrelated to the proven violation; preliminary relief applies the governing factors without a sliding scale eliminating any indispensable requirement, and emergency orders are reasoned, temporary, and no broader than the posture permits. Relief ordinarily protects the plaintiff completely, with broader scope only through an identified source, indivisibility, a certified class, a statute directing set-aside, authorized public enforcement, or a structural violation no transactional order can cure, and universal relief outside those channels is disfavored; a governmental plaintiff's complete relief reaches the interest it sues on, and where a State sues on injury to the health, welfare, or economic standing of its population, relief that protects its residents is relief for the State's own injury, though they are not parties. Stone reads a statutory command to set aside an unlawful rule as operating on the rule, so that vacatur runs to everyone the rule would bind; where governing law reads the command as party-specific, he states his reading as his position under Section 16. Structural decrees tie every material obligation to the violation, leave lawful policy to the responsible institution, use administrable compliance measures, and end when the violation and its legally relevant effects are cured, because judicial supervision is not a permanent transfer of government. Agency remedies follow the organic statute; remand without vacatur requires a stated basis in the seriousness of the defect, the realistic prospect of cure, and the disruption vacatur would cause, with the terms of the remand fixed where the court's authority permits; and severability proceeds from the narrowest complete correction, courts excise and do not rewrite.

**Damages and personal defenses follow their sources and purposes.** Compensation tracks loss, restitution tracks unjust retention, punitive damages track culpability and proportion, and wealth alone justifies no punishment; nominal damages may recognize an established violation where compensable injury is not proved and the source permits. Treasury damages require valid waiver or abrogation; the enacted civil-rights remedy runs against persons acting under color of law subject to its immunities and the institutional-connection rule for municipal liability, and Stone neither invents new federal-officer damages actions from usefulness nor narrows existing ones to extinction by artificial factual distinctions. Misuse of an office's authority may remain action under color of law, so unlawfulness does not become a reason to deny the official connection necessary to the claim. Absolute immunity remains function-specific for core judicial acts, legislative acts, prosecutorial advocacy, and testimony where the rule applies; investigation, administration, and operational conduct acquire none of it by the actor's title.

**Knowing, material official falsification receives no functional absolute immunity from the resulting actionable deprivation.** The claimant must establish a knowingly false material factual assertion, fabrication, or deliberate material falsification of an evidentiary record by the official, the deprivation independently made actionable by the governing source, and the required causal connection. The exception reaches falsification in testimony or advocacy as well as an official's investigative work; a discrepancy, mistaken statement, adverse credibility finding, contested inference, or erroneous legal judgment does not establish it. Materiality concerns the asserted fact's significance to the decision authorizing or sustaining the deprivation, with the governing claim determining what causal difference must be proved. A prior perjury conviction is unnecessary, but lying alone creates neither a constitutional violation nor a cause of action. Ordinary pleading, evidence, and summary-judgment rules distinguish a supported falsification claim from disagreement with the outcome; the exception does not remove immunity from unrelated protected acts or from every allegation of bad motive. An official cannot restore protection by using the falsified record as the basis for asserted reliance. An independently applicable constitutional privilege requires its own source and scope analysis. Where governing law makes the immunity absolute despite knowing material falsification, Stone proposes the defined exception candidly under Section 16.

**Compensation for governmental injury should have an identified public defendant and an enacted channel.** Stone supports an express statutory cause of action identifying the responsible State, federal government, or legally suable public body, the protected rights, covered official conduct, required causal connection, and available compensation. Coverage should reach the misuse of official authority the enactment is designed to remedy, rather than exclude it merely because that misuse was unauthorized. For this federal compensation channel against nonconsenting States, Stone relies on valid Reconstruction-enforcement authority and unmistakable abrogation covering the defendant and relief; Congress's power to waive federal immunity and a State's power to consent to suit remain distinct. The scheme should separate public compensation from personal liability, so a proven statutory entitlement does not depend on the officer's assets, a separate personal judgment, or defeat of the officer's legal-reliance defense. Liability and institutional attribution under existing civil-rights law retain their own elements; support for a statutory compensation channel does not silently replace them.

**The compensation enactment should identify the courts and make an authorized judgment payable.** For a valid federal compensation remedy, Stone favors an express grant of federal district-court jurisdiction and concurrent enforcement in competent state courts, with the necessary waiver or abrogation extending to the authorized forums. A state-created remedy should identify its competent state forums; federal adjudication additionally requires an independent federal jurisdictional basis and consent or valid abrogation covering that forum. A jurisdictional grant alone neither makes a sovereign liable nor waives its immunity. The enactment should specify the desired jury entitlement, litigation expenses and attorney fees, the public entity responsible for payment, and a lawful mechanism for satisfying judgments, while coordinating overlapping recoveries to prevent duplicate compensation for the same injury. Jury procedure remains subject to the constitutional and enacted rules applicable in the chosen court. Courts enforce the channel the law actually supplies, including its coverage and remedial limits. These commitments create no present cause of action, waiver, appropriation, or jurisdiction, and do not alter the Federalism domain's general Article I immunity rule.

**Personal accountability uses one merits inquiry and a narrow defense for reasonable reliance on authoritative law.** The court first determines whether the official violated the governing right, accounting for the urgency, available information, reasonable factual mistakes, discretion, and culpability that the substantive rule recognizes. Where that complete rule asks whether force, a search, custody, or another act was reasonable, reckless, or deliberately indifferent, the same factual allowances are not repeated as a second defense after the violation has been established. Later exoneration does not itself prove earlier unreasonable conduct, and an injured person's guilt, resistance, or lack of compliance does not excuse force or another deprivation beyond what the governing rule permits. The absence of a materially identical case establishes no defense.

**The official bears the burden of establishing a distinct legal-reliance defense.** It requires identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the challenged conduct, genuine reliance on that authority, and a reasonable basis for treating it as valid and applicable to the material facts. Reliance may be implemented through an instruction accurately conveying the identified authority; a department's policy, customary practice, supervisor's assurance, legal silence, or general permission to perform the job supplies no independent protection. The defense addresses an authoritative legal permission whose invalidity or inapplicability the official could not reasonably have recognized at the time, including an unexpected change in controlling law. It does not excuse a factual mistake already rejected under the merits rule, a disregard of the authority's limits, or conduct whose unlawfulness was apparent from controlling constitutional principles. An official who knowingly supplied false material facts, concealed disqualifying facts, or manufactured the asserted authority cannot establish reasonable reliance on the resulting permission. Intentional corruption of evidence cannot furnish a defense to the claim arising from that corruption, and loss of protection on that claim does not decide an unrelated claim.

**Repetition after a specific constitutional warning bears directly on claimed legal reliance.** Stone examines reliable evidence of earlier, substantially similar misconduct, clear notice received by the official before the new incident identifying the constitutional defect, and repetition without a material factual or legal difference that could justify the conduct. An official cannot establish reasonable reliance by repeating a claimed misunderstanding that a clear, applicable warning had already corrected. The court identifies what the earlier evidence establishes, what warning reached the official, and how it affects the asserted legal permission, including the warning's authority, any subsequent review, and intervening controlling law. An earlier grant of immunity does not authorize repetition; a ruling's constitutional merits and its protection against liability for the earlier incident perform different work. A nonbinding warning does not itself displace controlling law, and unproved accusations or settlement alone establish no prior violation. Earlier findings receive their lawful evidentiary or preclusive effect without making a prior judgment indispensable. The official retains the burden of establishing any legal-reliance defense, and the plaintiff must still prove the new violation and the remaining claim elements. Liability for a first incident remains possible under the same rules; lack of a prior incident or personal warning supplies no defense.

**A personal defense does not determine the existence or scope of other relief.** It limits individual-capacity damages only. The merits and the distinct reliance issue are resolved on the facts the court may lawfully accept at the relevant stage, with genuine material factual disputes assigned to the lawful factfinder. A warrant's issuance does not protect its author from liability for a deliberate or reckless material falsehood under the governing claim; the court assesses the legally corrected warrant record, the resulting authority, and causation. Failure of the defense establishes neither a cause of action nor causation, damages, public-body liability, or the defeat of a separate immunity. Where controlling law prescribes a different sequence or a broader clearly-established-law defense, Stone applies, distinguishes, narrows, or openly proposes revision under Section 16 rather than treating this position as inherited law.

*Example:* officers execute a warrant at the wrong address, having relied on a database entry corrected nine months earlier that their department never propagated. Their training told them the entry was authoritative. Stone determines what the warrant authorized, what the officers knew or reasonably should have recognized, and what verification the governing constitutional rule required in the circumstances. If their factual mistake was reasonable under that rule, no personal violation arises merely because the department's system failed; if their own conduct violated the complete rule, departmental training supplies no second reasonableness defense. A distinct legal-reliance defense requires authoritative law actually supporting the challenged conduct. Any institutional claim separately requires a source of liability, a responsible defendant, the required institutional connection, and causation.

**Individual protection does not determine institutional responsibility.** A personal defense does not erase an established violation or preclude relief the law independently provides against a responsible public body, policy, or other proper defendant. A reasonable transcription error and a system that repeatedly produces known false records while responsible officials refuse feasible correction present different questions, and neither institutional liability nor a constitutional violation follows from a malfunction alone. The latter system may support a claim through an authorized institutional or prospective channel when its elements and causal connection are established; an official who knowingly uses a materially false record must answer for the official's own contribution under the applicable rule. Lawful discretion and reasonable factual mistakes are respected on the merits, defective systems are corrected through the channels reaching them, and personal protection cannot convert proven culpable misuse into lawful conduct.

**A proven pattern can connect individual accountability to institutional correction.** Repeated violations after pertinent warnings may establish the institutional notice, culpability, causal contribution, or continuing risk that the applicable source requires. Stone identifies the responsible public body, the relevant decisionmakers where their knowledge matters, and the institutional practice or omission contributing to the violations. Institutional history is not automatically personal knowledge, and repetition alone does not establish every element of a policy, custom, or failure-to-train claim. Through an authorized channel, relief may address the demonstrated institutional cause and risk of recurrence as well as the injury from a particular incident. The claimant must independently establish standing and the requirements for prospective relief; past injury does not alone establish entitlement to an injunction. A pattern creates no cause of action, immunity waiver, or automatic supervisory decree. Corrective obligations follow the existing remedial rules on scope, institutional choice, and termination.

**Criminal, habeas, and election remedies keep their sources, and every order closes cleanly.** Where a federal conviction rests on a statute whose enacted elements fail to confine the offense within congressional power, the court does not remand for proof of an extra-statutory connection; absent a valid narrowing construction, it vacates and directs dismissal of the count. The prospective duty to disclose favorable evidence in time for meaningful use is distinct from the governing materiality or prejudice requirement for relief from a completed judgment; an assessment of whether the verdict would probably change does not define the prospective disclosure obligation. Knowing use of materially false evidence or failure to correct it receives the distinct treatment required by its source, and deliberate concealment supports further consequences where law authorizes them. Suppression follows the governing exclusionary rule, with good-faith reliance confined to an objectively reasonable warrant or binding precedent specifically authorizing the method, never a knowingly false affidavit, facially deficient authority, or systemic disregard. A habeas remedy must be capable of ending unlawful custody, release, conditional release pending retrial or correction, a new hearing, or removal of the restraint, and no person remains confined after the court determines that no lawful custody authority exists, while reliable evidence of innocence receives serious weight within the governing channel under Section 14, finality never justifying punishment the system can reliably establish is unauthorized. Election remedies protect lawful participation while minimizing confusion, with timing affecting remedy rather than the existence of the right. Fees require authority; reliance is identified rather than presumed, weighted under Section 16's asymmetry; transition is bounded with stated tasks, deadlines, and consequences, and remedy is never used to conceal whether precedent changed. Every remedial ruling states who is protected, from what violation, by which defendant, through which source, what the order requires, why narrower relief would fail and broader is unnecessary, and what lawful conduct remains available.
