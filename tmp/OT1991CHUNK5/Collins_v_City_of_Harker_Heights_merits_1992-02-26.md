# Canonical Decision Record — Collins v. City of Harker Heights

## Event and chronology

Collins v. City of Harker Heights; No. 90-1279; OT1991. Historical reporter anchor: 503 U.S. 115 (1992). Merits decision, 1992-02-26. Argued November 5, 1991. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, 916 F.2d 284. The Court reviews dismissal of a widow’s municipal substantive-due-process claim following her husband’s death while working in a sewer.

Questions: Whether §1983 imposes an independent governmental-power-abuse element on public employees, and whether the pleaded deliberate failures to train, warn and equip establish a federal substantive-due-process deprivation.

Chronology: The four February 26 events are uncoordinated and share a start-of-day baseline including the committed February 25 decisions. None uses another February 26 decision as earlier law.

Render form: full; the judgment is unanimous, but Stone withholds the historical whole-opinion join and states a materially distinct constitutional-duty ground; Part C’s controlling rationale has eight votes, while Parts A and B have nine.

Relief sought and material record: Larry Collins died from asphyxia after entering a manhole to clear a sewer on October 21, 1988. The complaint alleges knowing policies of deficient training, warnings and equipment, a supervisor’s prior loss of consciousness, and intentional failure to comply with the Texas Hazard Communication Act. The city’s alleged knowledge and deliberate omissions are accepted at dismissal. There is no allegation that officials deliberately injured Collins or directed him into a known dangerous sewer. The district court found no constitutional violation; the Fifth Circuit affirmed on an additional governmental-abuse element without deciding that issue. Collins seeks reversal; the city defends both statutory and constitutional grounds.

Stage before: plenary merits review. Stage after: Supreme Court review ends with affirmance of dismissal of the federal claim. No federal compensation or supervision is ordered. Independently available state-law remedies and their procedural treatment remain for the proper courts; their merits and timeliness are not decided.

## Law of the decision

### No extra governmental-abuse element under §1983

**Holding and operative rule:** A municipal employee who establishes deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and municipal responsibility need not also prove an independent, freestanding abuse-of-governmental-power element. The existence of the constitutional wrong and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries.

**Authority:** Part A of Stevens’s opinion, joined by all other Justices; unanimous statutory holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that public employment does not remove the protections Congress made enforceable through §1983. The statute does not distinguish a federal violation by an employer from a federal violation by a government acting in another role. Monell recognizes municipal responsibility for a qualifying policy or custom that causes a federal deprivation, while excluding liability merely because a city employs the wrongdoer. Canton addresses when inadequate training can supply that policy and causal connection; it does not establish a constitutional right whenever training is deficient. An additional abstract governmental-abuse element could defeat otherwise valid speech, equality or bodily-liberty claims solely because the injured person works for government. The circuit’s added requirement therefore cannot sustain dismissal. That error does not establish this plaintiff’s substantive right or require reversal if the actual complaint alleges no constitutional deprivation. The Court assumes attribution only for purposes of deciding that separate question and makes no final municipal-liability finding.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Monell v. Department of Social Services*, 436 U.S. 658 — applied: municipal policy and causation remain distinct from employment alone.
- *Canton v. Harris*, 489 U.S. 378 — clarified as an attribution doctrine, not a source of every asserted substantive right.
- *Oklahoma City v. Tuttle*, 471 U.S. 808 — preserved for the separation between a constitutional wrong and proof of municipal responsibility.

### Voluntary public employment creates no general constitutional safety duty

**Holding and operative rule:** The substantive Due Process Clause does not impose an independent general obligation on a municipality to provide its voluntary employees a workplace free from unreasonable risks of harm. Accepting public employment does not itself create the custody or deprivation of liberty that supports affirmative constitutional duties of care.

**Authority:** Part B of Stevens’s opinion, joined by all other Justices; nine Justices adopt this duty limitation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Constitution does not turn the employment relationship itself into protective custody. DeShaney distinguishes a restriction on the State’s own deprivations from a general guarantee that a person’s interests will not suffer harm. Youngberg’s affirmative care duties arise in involuntary confinement, where the State limits the person’s ability to protect himself; that premise is absent when a person voluntarily accepts municipal employment. Daniels also cautions that substantive due process does not replace ordinary tort duties, and Bishop confirms that the Constitution does not guarantee correct public-employment decisions. The complaint alleges serious risks and knowing failures of protection, but those allegations do not make Collins’s employment a custodial restraint. The contrary approach would constitutionalize a general safe-workplace duty without the necessary constitutional source. This holding does not insulate public employers from applicable federal rights, decide actual compulsion or deliberately inflicted bodily injury, or determine the availability of state safety and tort remedies.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services*, 489 U.S. 189 — applied to the absence of a general protective duty; distinct custody and affirmative-deprivation questions remain.
- *Youngberg v. Romeo*, 457 U.S. 307 — distinguished because voluntary employment is not involuntary custody.
- *Daniels v. Williams*, 474 U.S. 327 — preserved: constitutional due process does not replace ordinary tort law; the knowing allegations are not redescribed as mere negligence.
- *Bishop v. Wood*, 426 U.S. 341 — applied against treating employment mistakes as a general constitutional violation.

### These pleaded omissions do not establish constitutional arbitrariness

**Holding and operative rule:** The pleaded municipal failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers against known hazards do not constitute arbitrary or conscience-shocking deprivation under substantive due process in this voluntary-employment setting. Even assuming the Texas safety statute creates the asserted duties and a protected liberty interest, breach alone does not establish that distinct substantive constitutional wrong.

**Authority:** Part C of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices control this alternative rejection of the substantive claim. Stone agrees with the judgment on his separately stated ground, without joining this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court also holds that the alleged omissions are not constitutionally arbitrary on the theory presented. Rochin’s protection against conscience-shocking government intrusion does not convert every knowing failure of workplace protection into that kind of deprivation. The complaint does not allege deliberate bodily injury or an order knowingly sending Collins into a dangerous sewer. Decisions about training and equipment occur within public programs whose administration ordinarily reflects competing social, political and economic choices. That legal presumption is not a factual finding that this city considered the risks responsibly or that a particular budget justified its conduct. Bishop and Daniels explain why federal substantive due process does not generally replace state employment and tort rules. Even assuming the Texas statute creates the claimed liberty interest, Harrah’s distinction between an entitlement and arbitrary deprivation leaves the same constitutional deficiency. The Court therefore affirms without approving the city’s conduct, adjudicating state remedies or resolving a different claim of affirmative injury.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Rochin v. California*, 342 U.S. 165 — distinguished: the pleaded protective omissions are not its form of conscience-shocking bodily intrusion.
- *Bishop v. Wood*, 426 U.S. 341 — applied to preserve the distinction between employment duties and substantive constitutional arbitrariness.
- *Daniels v. Williams*, 474 U.S. 327 — applied against substituting substantive due process for ordinary state duties.
- *Harrah Independent School District v. Martin*, 440 U.S. 194 — applied to distinguish an assumed state-created interest from constitutionally arbitrary deprivation.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm dismissal of the federal substantive-due-process claim on the constitutional-duty ground, rejecting the Fifth Circuit's separate governmental-abuse element.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** No federal damages or supervision order. Leave any independently available state-law remedies and their procedural treatment to the proper courts.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: No employer exemption from constitutional responsibility

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the §1983 element relied on below.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply §1983 and Monell; reject an additional categorical circuit requirement unsupported by the statute.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A municipality acting as employer can violate federal rights; a plaintiff who establishes such a violation and municipal responsibility need not prove an independent, additional sovereign-power-abuse element.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Public employment does not erase applicable speech, equality or bodily-liberty guarantees. The relevant constitutional source determines the wrong; §1983 supplies the action and Monell the city's responsibility. Adding a second abstract governmental-abuse requirement risks defeating recognized rights. That error does not require reversal if the complaint lacks a constitutional deprivation in the first place.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Municipal responsibility is not assumed merely because employment exists.

### Issue 2: The alleged omissions do not establish the required deprivation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the properly presented alternative ground necessary to the judgment.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply source-specific duties and distinguish his affirmative-danger commitment from a general constitutional safe-workplace guarantee. No categorical rejection of affirmative-danger liability is proposed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A voluntary public-employment relationship and a knowing policy of inadequate safety training, warnings or equipment do not, without the necessary custodial or affirmative-deprivation predicate, establish the pleaded substantive-due-process right to workplace safety.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The prior incident supports knowledge, and deliberate omission is more than negligence. Those points satisfy neither custody nor an affirmative exercise of authority creating the specific danger. The complaint does not allege coerced entry, deliberate bodily injury, obstruction of available rescue or another affirmative act materially increasing the hazard beyond the failure to provide protection. A general employment arrangement cannot itself supply that additional element without turning all knowingly deficient public workplaces into constitutional custodial settings. Stone's Master would recognize a properly presented affirmative contribution to a specific foreseeable danger with conscious disregard, practical opportunity and causation; those elements cannot be inserted into this complaint. Canton addresses when the city owns a qualifying constitutional wrong and cannot fill the missing substantive duty. The Texas statute supplies its own legal protection without converting its standards into federal substantive due process.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Preserve claims of actual compulsion, deliberately inflicted bodily injury or a properly alleged affirmative danger. No factual approval of the city's conduct or assumption that budget choices justified it is needed. A broader new danger-creation rule need not be adopted to decide this omission-based pleading.
