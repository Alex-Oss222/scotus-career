## Event and chronology

Willy v. Coastal Corp.; No. 90-1150; OT1991. Historical reporter anchor: 503 U.S. 131 (1992). Merits decision, 1992-03-03. Argued December 3, 1991. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, 915 F.2d 965. The Court reviews the power to preserve a Rule 11 sanction after the federal court was held to lack subject-matter jurisdiction over a removed employment action.

Questions: Whether the Rules authorize this sanction and whether Article III permits it despite the established absence of jurisdiction over the underlying claims.

Chronology: All seven February 25–26 decisions are effective. Their public holdings do not change the independent source or scope of collateral Rule 11 authority. No same-day coordination issue arises.

Render form: compact; the judgment and opinion are unanimous, with no material change in rule, precedent treatment or remedy; the author substitution alone does not require full form.

Relief sought and material record: Willy sued his former employer in Texas state court, alleging discharge for refusing to participate in violations of environmental law. Coastal removed the action. The district court rejected Willy’s jurisdictional objection, dismissed the claims and imposed Rule 11 fees jointly and severally on Willy and his lawyer. The Fifth Circuit, 855 F.2d 1160, reversed the merits disposition for want of federal jurisdiction, directed return to state court and upheld sanctioning authority while requiring recalculation. The district court fixed $19,307, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed in the judgment now reviewed. The sanctioned filings included disorganized voluminous materials and careless legal pleading; Willy’s successful jurisdictional objection was not the sanctioned conduct. The propriety of the Rule 11 violation, reasonable fee response and joint liability is taken as given on this limited review. Willy seeks elimination of the sanction for lack of jurisdiction; Coastal seeks affirmance.

Stage before: plenary merits review. Stage after: Supreme Court review of the sanction ends with affirmance of $19,307, jointly and severally, on the reviewed terms. The prior jurisdictional direction returning the employment claims to state court remains operative. No new merits judgment, enlarged sanction or retained Supreme Court supervision is entered.

## Law of the decision

### This collateral Rule 11 sanction survives the jurisdictional ruling

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal district court may impose and preserve a valid Rule 11 sanction for litigation misconduct before it even when its assertion of subject-matter jurisdiction over the action is later held erroneous. The sanction must address an independently authorized procedural violation collateral to the merits; it does not validate the jurisdictional error or authorize adjudication of the underlying claim.

**Authority:** Stone’s opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices; unanimous holding on both the Rules’ authorization and Article III objection.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this procedural sanction survives the loss of the underlying federal action. Rules 1 and 81(c) apply the Federal Rules to civil proceedings after removal, and Rule 11 supplies the particular sanctioning authority. Sibbach preserves the limit that a procedural rule cannot enlarge substantive rights or jurisdiction. This order respects that limit because it determines misuse of the court’s procedure, not the legal merits of the employment claim. Cooter & Gell establishes that a Rule 11 violation presents an independent issue surviving termination of the action; that reasoning extends to the jurisdictional defect presented here. Mine Workers likewise recognizes the need for orderly proceedings while disputed authority is determined. Catholic Conference does not require cancellation: its coercive discovery served a merits action the court lacked power to pursue. Here the misconduct was complete and did not consist of Willy’s correct jurisdictional objection. The Court sustains the existing sanction without relying on general inherent power or reviving the invalid merits judgment.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Sibbach v. Wilson & Co.*, 312 U.S. 1 — applied: procedural rules cannot enlarge substantive rights or jurisdiction.
- *Cooter & Gell v. Hartmarx Corp.*, 496 U.S. 384 — extended from voluntary dismissal to the presented lack of merits jurisdiction because Rule 11 adjudication is collateral.
- *United States v. Mine Workers*, 330 U.S. 258 — applied for orderly determination of disputed judicial authority, not assumed power over the ultimate merits.
- *United States Catholic Conference v. Abortion Rights Mobilization, Inc.*, 487 U.S. 72 — distinguished: coercive discovery serving an unauthorized merits action lacks this completed sanction’s independence.

### Authority limits and questions not reached

The holding concerns the then-effective Rule 11 and this established procedural violation. It supplies neither hypothetical merits jurisdiction nor a universal collateral-order exception. Any different order requires its own lawful source and genuine independence. The Court does not decide inherent sanctioning power, reconsider the accepted misconduct or amount, or restore the dismissed federal merits judgment.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A source for procedure without assumed merits jurisdiction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Accept the established absence of merits jurisdiction and independently identify authority for the collateral order.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the Rules and collateral-proceeding principles to their proper procedural object. This is an extension of Cooter & Gell's collateral rationale to the presented jurisdictional defect, not permission to assume merits jurisdiction.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The court may impose this Rule 11 consequence for litigation conduct before it even though its earlier assertion of subject-matter jurisdiction was erroneous, because the order adjudicates a distinct procedural violation and does not decide the underlying claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A court must process a removed action sufficiently to determine its power, and valid procedural rules govern the parties while that process occurs. Rule 11 has an enacted procedural basis; its limited collateral function does not supply authority over the employment merits. Cooter & Gell identifies the independence of the procedural issue, while Mine Workers supports orderly determination of disputed authority. Catholic Conference does not require the opposite result: compelling merits discovery serves the unauthorized substantive action, whereas this completed sanction addresses misuse of the forum. Willy's correct jurisdictional position is not the sanctioned conduct. Recognizing this distinction prevents forum abuse without making a jurisdictional mistake self-validating.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No hypothetical jurisdiction, no revival of the invalid merits disposition and no broad permission to issue any collateral order. Each order needs its own lawful source, procedural safeguards and genuine independence from adjudication the court lacked power to undertake.
