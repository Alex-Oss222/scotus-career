# Canonical Decision Record — McCarthy v. Madigan

## Event and chronology

McCarthy v. Madigan; No. 90-6861; OT1991. Historical reporter anchor: 503 U.S. 140 (1992). Merits decision, 1992-03-04. Argued December 9, 1991. On writ of certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, 914 F.2d 1411. The Court reviews dismissal of a federal prisoner’s damages-only constitutional medical-care action for failure to complete the Bureau of Prisons’ general grievance process.

Questions: Whether Congress requires this exhaustion and, if not, whether the particular grievance procedure’s purposes and practical effects justify making it a prerequisite to this damages action.

Chronology: All February 25–26 decisions and the committed March 3 Willy decision are effective. None changes statutory exhaustion or the established medical-care damages channel; the relevant public-law refresh preceded final assembly.

Render form: full; the unanimous disposition rests on a seven-Justice Court rationale and a two-Justice narrower concurrence; Stone’s approved reasoning changes the controlling coalition relative to history.

Relief sought and material record: McCarthy alleges deliberate indifference by four Leavenworth prison employees to needs following a back operation and psychiatric problems. He expressly requests only money damages. The district court dismissed for nonexhaustion and denied reconsideration; the Tenth Circuit affirmed on the usefulness of administrative consideration and fact development even without damages authority. The general BOP regulations provide investigation and written responses but no hearing, formal evidentiary findings or monetary award. They require an initial complaint within 15 days, then appeals within 20 and 30 days, with extensions for valid reasons. An earlier grievance concerned a private cell; a later medical grievance was accepted despite lateness and denied by the warden, but its regional appeal was rejected as late. That sequence does not establish misconduct by grievance officials. McCarthy seeks reversal and ordinary damages litigation; respondents invoke agency autonomy, information, general statutory delegation, §1997e, possible FTCA conversion and settlement.

Stage before: plenary merits review. Stage after: Supreme Court review ends with reversal and remand. The federal damages complaint may proceed without exhaustion of this general grievance process, subject to ordinary adjudication and defenses. No damages amount, deliberate-indifference finding, immunity decision or retained Supreme Court supervision is entered.

## Law of the decision

### Statutory design governs the exhaustion inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** An express congressional exhaustion requirement controls according to its terms. Where Congress has not required exhaustion, courts must exercise sound discretion consistent with the statutory scheme, weighing prompt access to court against the actual interests in agency authority and judicial efficiency; neither the general BOP delegation nor the then-current §1997e imposes a mandatory gate for this federal prisoner’s Bivens medical-care claim.

**Authority:** Blackmun’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices control the governing inquiry and statutory application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a judicial exhaustion rule must fit Congress’s design and the purposes served by the actual administrative process. Patsy makes congressional intent central, while McKart and McGee explain why administrative expertise, correction of error and useful fact development matter when courts retain discretion. Coit distinguishes that discretion from a statutory command. Congress’s general assignment of prison administration to the Bureau says nothing requiring this prisoner to exhaust before seeking damages. Section 1997e’s limited treatment of certain state-prisoner actions does not enact a broader federal rule by implication. Carlson supplies the established medical-care damages channel; Bush and Schweiker concern comprehensive remedial choices Congress made in different programs. The Bureau’s own grievance procedure is not an equivalent congressional substitute. Respondents’ appeal to administrative autonomy therefore requires examination of what this procedure can accomplish, rather than automatic dismissal. The Court does not displace an express statutory requirement, announce a new Bivens action or determine the medical claim’s merits.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *Patsy v. Board of Regents of Florida*, 457 U.S. 496 — applied for congressional primacy and the actual limited scope of contemporaneous §1997e.
- *McKart v. United States*, 395 U.S. 185 — applied for the purposes of agency authority, expertise, correction and efficiency.
- *McGee v. United States*, 402 U.S. 479 — applied for sound judicial discretion where Congress has not clearly required exhaustion.
- *Coit Independence Joint Venture v. FSLIC*, 489 U.S. 561 — applied to distinguish statutory exhaustion and the adequacy of an administrative route.
- *Carlson v. Green*, 446 U.S. 14 — preserved as the existing medical-care damages channel, without extending it to a new context.
- *Bush v. Lucas*, 462 U.S. 367 — distinguished: no comparable comprehensive congressional remedial scheme displaces this existing channel.
- *Schweiker v. Chilicky*, 487 U.S. 412 — distinguished on the same absence of a congressional replacement scheme.

### This damages-only claim need not pass through the general BOP process

**Holding and operative rule:** Absent a statutory mandate, a federal prisoner seeking only damages for the completed medical-care wrong alleged here need not exhaust the described BOP process: it cannot award compensation, its short filing periods risk forfeiture, and its limited adjudicative benefit does not outweigh access to court. The result does not decide a request for prospective relief, a procedure able to grant the sought compensation, or a materially different administrative adjudication.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices join Blackmun’s particular balancing and application. Scalia and Thomas agree with the result solely because no money remedy is available; their judgment concurrence does not join the deadline or broader balancing rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this grievance process provides too little countervailing benefit to justify barring the damages complaint. McKart requires attention to the process’s real function, not merely its administrative label. Investigation and written responses may help officials improve care, but they neither compensate this completed injury nor create formal findings on which a court can conclusively dispose of the claim. The successive filing periods also risk forfeiture; extensions exist, but their valid-reason condition does not remove that practical burden, illustrated by the rejected regional appeal. Coit’s concern for an adequate route and Turner’s protection of valid prisoner claims inform the balance. The proposed conversion to an FTCA claim and possible settlement do not establish an effective damages remedy in these regulations or this record; Hillsborough cautions against requiring an uncertain route to relief. Ordinary litigation remains available for developing facts and asserting defenses. The Court decides no medical violation, improper grievance handling or exhaustion rule for prospective relief.

**Precedent treatment:**

- *McKart v. United States*, 395 U.S. 185 — applied through a claim-specific comparison of individual and institutional interests.
- *Coit Independence Joint Venture v. FSLIC*, 489 U.S. 561 — its adequacy principle informs the practical evaluation; the filing-period application is not a categorical invalidation of short deadlines.
- *Turner v. Safley*, 482 U.S. 78 — applied for continued judicial cognizance of valid prisoners’ constitutional claims, without deciding this claim’s validity.
- *Hillsborough Township v. Cromwell*, 326 U.S. 620 — applied against forcing resort to an uncertain administrative avenue for the requested relief.
- *Union Pacific Railroad Co. v. Board of County Commissioners*, 247 U.S. 282 — applied for the significance of uncertainty in the available administrative remedy.

The Court makes no finding of bias, deliberate indifference, damages entitlement or loss of a defense. It does not transform negligence into a constitutional violation or extend Bivens to a new context.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A procedural gate must serve its lawful purpose

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide only the exhaustion ground that prevented adjudication.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply statute-first and purpose-sensitive entering exhaustion doctrine. Reject a categorical circuit rule for this procedure and claim; do not create a universal exception to congressionally mandated exhaustion.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Absent a statutory mandate, this federal prisoner seeking only damages need not exhaust the described BOP grievance procedure, which cannot supply monetary relief and provides insufficient countervailing adjudicative benefit to justify the bar.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress's limited treatment of other prisoner litigation does not authorize importing an unexpressed universal rule here. Carlson supplies an established damages channel for the relevant constitutional setting. The Bureau's opportunity to investigate and improve care is useful, but this claimant requests compensation for completed harm, and the grievance process cannot award it. Its written investigations do not create a formal evidentiary record binding on a later court. The short deadlines can instead foreclose judicial consideration for reasons unrelated to the constitutional claim; the later rejected appeal demonstrates that practical concern without proving the grievance officials acted unlawfully. On this specific balance, administrative convenience does not justify closing the recognized court channel. The government retains ordinary opportunities to develop facts and assert defenses in litigation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** An express statutory requirement controls according to its own terms. The result does not decide exhaustion where prospective relief is requested, the agency can grant the sought compensation, or a materially different administrative scheme performs an adjudicative function. It does not expand Bivens into a new context or treat alleged medical negligence as deliberate indifference.
