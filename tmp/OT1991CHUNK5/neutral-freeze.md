## Alternative-path conclusions frozen for reconciliation

Hudson: Wilson supports a genuine objective limitation, but transferring a medical-injury threshold to malicious force risks insulating the very conduct prohibited; Whitley's culpability and a trivial-force boundary offer a distinct route. Stevens and Blackmun retain their recorded barriers to universal language. Nordic: explicit application language and §550 favor recovery, while §106(c)'s narrower meaningful operation and neighboring monetary language defeat unequivocal consent for the opposing group; neither theory needs to validate the diversion. Franklin: cause recognition and remedy are separate; the 1986 enactment supports even the narrower path without a new general presumption. Arkansas: affected-state common-law preemption is distinct from federal permit authority, and existing degradation alone does not negate supported no-detectable-violation findings. Collins: rejecting the appellate element does not create the underlying right, and no unpleaded coercion may supply it. Willy: lack of merits jurisdiction is accepted, but completed procedural misconduct differs from coercive discovery in aid of merits jurisdiction. McCarthy: agency autonomy and information have real value, but the available administrative relief and actual damages-only complaint delimit the judicial gate.
