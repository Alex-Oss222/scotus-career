# PFZ Properties, Inc. v. Rodriguez

## Event

PFZ Properties, Inc. v. Rodriguez; No. 91-122; October Term 1991. Dismissal of writ after plenary argument, 1992-03-09; argued February 26, 1992. On writ of certiorari to First Circuit, 928 F.2d 28, affirming dismissal reported at 739 F.Supp. 67.

Question: Whether the operative allegations of delay and irregular land-use administration state a substantive-due-process claim; the grant excludes procedural due process and equal protection, and the takings theory was abandoned.

Record and relief sought: PFZ relied on preliminary project approvals and asserted an entitlement to development permits after submitting drawings. Its amended complaint alleged prolonged administrative delay and irregular treatment. The district court dismissed, and the First Circuit affirmed. On rehearing the circuit refused to enlarge the complaint through belated reliance on a pretrial order. The official argument exposed disagreement over the source and nature of the property interest, which drawings were properly before the agency, and whether asserted deliberate obstruction was within the operative pleading. Alleged bad faith and a sham process are party characterizations, not adjudicated findings. PFZ sought reversal; respondents defended dismissal. The limited grant does not permit resolution of the separate procedural claim.

Render form: full; the published procedural disposition now includes Stone’s dissent seeking a merits decision; the procedural and merits positions must remain distinct.

## Separate Writings

Stone would reach the granted question and affirm dismissal while assuming the asserted property entitlement. Favorably read allegations of delay and irregular administration do not, in his view, establish the further constitutionally oppressive or legally irrational deprivation required for the substantive claim. A party’s label of sham review is not itself a finding of improper purpose or the missing constitutional conclusion. He would not hold that available state review cures every substantive wrong, import the rejected pretrial enlargement, or decide procedural due process, equal protection, takings, damages, permits or unnecessary officer defenses.
