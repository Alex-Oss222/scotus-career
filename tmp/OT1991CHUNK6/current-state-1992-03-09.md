## Dawson v. Delaware

Effective March 9, the constitutional relevance rule is applied to this stipulation; no categorical association-evidence ban is added. The remand’s seven-Justice reservation leaves both availability and application of harmless-error treatment open. Stone’s mandatory Chapman/new-sentencing sequence is not the Court’s instruction. No earlier simulated rule is displaced.

Supreme Court review of the sentence ends; the case returns to Delaware for further proceedings. No resentencing date, harmlessness finding, stay, or retained Supreme Court supervision is created. The convictions remain intact.

## General Motors Corp. v. Romein

Effective March 9, existing contract-threshold and rational-retroactivity doctrine is applied without changing its tests. The employers have no protected offset obligation on these agreements; the repayment law is enforceable. No substantial-impairment balancing rule is added and no categorical validation of retrospective legislation follows.

Supreme Court review ends with affirmance. Michigan’s repayment obligation may be enforced on its existing terms; the Court orders no additional award, contract modification, or retained supervision.

## Williams v. United States

Effective March 9, §3742(f) now expressly requires separate causal harmlessness and departure-reasonableness review. The changed components are the effect-of-error inquiry, burden sequence after demonstrated reliance, and the relationship between paragraphs (1) and (2). The valid-ground requirement and the district court’s sentencing role remain. No constitutional sentencing-fact rule or blanket policy-statement rule is added.

Supreme Court sentence review ends with remand to the Seventh Circuit. That court must decide whether the same sentence would have been imposed and whether the departure is independently reasonable; if either required condition fails, it must remand for resentencing. No new sentence or automatic district-court hearing is imposed now.

## Stringer v. Black

Effective March 9, the Court applies Teague to this specific Mississippi claim and makes the weighing/nonweighing remedial distinction controlling at the stated scope. No universal retroactivity exception or automatic cure from surviving aggravators is added. Genuine reweighing and constitutional harmlessness remain different routes. Actual cure, procedural default and any eventual conditional-writ terms are unresolved.

Supreme Court review ends with remand to the Fifth Circuit. The preserved procedural-default defense remains undecided; the lower courts must consider it and any adequate state correction. If relief is otherwise warranted, a conditional writ may permit resentencing or authorized appellate cure. No immediate release, automatic life sentence or finding that a particular state cure failed is made.

## Connecticut National Bank v. Germain

Effective March 9, the Court rejects an implied categorical §158(d) exclusion of §1292 review for district-court bankruptcy appellate orders. The text/coexistence explanation has five votes, while the reversing judgment has nine. Certification, timeliness, circuit discretion and subject-matter limits remain; no jury-trial or bankruptcy-appellate-panel holding is added.

Supreme Court review of the jurisdictional dismissal ends. The case returns to the Second Circuit to consider §1292’s applicable requirements and whether to permit the appeal. The underlying jury dispute remains undecided here.

## PFZ Properties, Inc. v. Rodriguez

No substantive Supreme Court holding results. The lower judgment remains operative for the parties; its reasoning is not adopted as national law. Roth, Ewing, Daniels and the limited simulated Collins holdings remain unchanged. Stone’s published pleading analysis is a noncontrolling dissent from the procedural disposition.

The writ is dismissed; Supreme Court review ends. The First Circuit’s judgment remains in force without an affirmance on the merits. No permit, damages, new hearing, retained supervision or reopening of excluded claims is ordered.
