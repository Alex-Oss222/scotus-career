## Merits cases

### 1. *Arizona v. California*

**Question presented.** Whether the Court should enter the proposed supplemental decree implementing previously adjudicated Colorado River rights and settled tribal-boundary matters.

**What the case is about.** In this original-jurisdiction proceeding, the parties submitted a supplemental Colorado River decree implementing previously adjudicated rights and settled Fort Mojave and Colorado River Reservation matters. No lower-court judgment is involved; the question is whether the proposed language faithfully administers the existing decree without reopening settled allocations.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *Arizona v. California*, 530 U.S. 392, and the existing decrees.

**Old sim.** Stone previously took this disposition: Enter the supplemental decree. His proposed rule was: The Court may enter a supplemental interstate-water decree faithfully implementing prior adjudications and valid settlements without reopening matters fixed by the controlling decree. The old Court adopted the position in substance on an independently held ground; Stone caused no material change.

**Stone innovation.** None. This is an ordinary decree-administration and remedial choice.

**Stone's position now.** Enter the supplemental decree. The prior adjudications and valid settlements already supply the authority, and the submitted provisions may be made binding while continuing jurisdiction is retained for administration. Rungs 2, 5, and 7 control: apply the existing decree and use the narrow remedial instrument presented rather than reopen the merits.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply prior decree; choose a supplemental remedy; reserve matters outside the decree.

**Proposed holding.** The Court may enter a supplemental interstate-water decree that faithfully implements prior adjudications and valid settlements while preserving rights and questions not expressly modified.

**Limit.** The position does not adjudicate claims outside the submitted text, alter allocations fixed by earlier decrees, or convert retained jurisdiction into authority to reopen the apportionment.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old disposition and its limited administrative rationale survive unchanged.


### 2. *Artuz v. Bennett*

**Question presented.** Is a state collateral application “properly filed” under 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2) when particular claims may be rejected under state procedural bars?

**What the case is about.** Bennett delivered a state collateral application that New York accepted for filing, although the State argued that particular claims were barred from relief. The Second Circuit held that the accepted application tolled AEDPA's federal limitations period and the State sought review.

**Precedent applied.** None supplied on the precise question.

**Old sim.** Stone previously took this disposition: Affirm. His proposed rule was: A collateral application is properly filed when it satisfies state rules governing delivery and acceptance; a claim-level merits or procedural bar does not retroactively make it unfiled. The old Court adopted the position in substance on an independently held ground; Stone caused no material change.

**Stone innovation.** None. Stone uses ordinary statutory interpretation and separates filing conditions from claim-level defenses.

**Stone's position now.** Affirm. Section 2244(d)(2) asks whether the application was properly delivered and accepted under state filing rules, not whether every claim in it can obtain relief. Treating a later merits or procedural defense as a filing defect would collapse two distinct statutory questions. Rung 9 controls through text, grammar, and procedural sequence.

**Doctrinal intent.** Interpret text; distinguish filing requirements from conditions governing relief; reserve unresolved tolling questions.

**Proposed holding.** A state collateral application is properly filed when its delivery and acceptance comply with the State's filing requirements, even if particular claims are later denied under procedural or merits rules.

**Limit.** Untimeliness, the wrong court, a required unpaid fee, defective form, or another rule governing delivery and acceptance may still prevent proper filing; the duration of pending status and deadline exceptions remain unresolved.

**Novelty.** Statutory or constitutional interpretation.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old statutory distinction remains correct.


### 3. *Cleveland v. United States*

**Question presented.** Are Louisiana’s unissued video-poker licenses property in the State’s hands for purposes of 18 U.S.C. §1341?

**What the case is about.** Cleveland obtained Louisiana video-poker licenses through alleged misrepresentations, and the Fifth Circuit affirmed mail-fraud convictions on the theory that the State's unissued licenses were property in its hands. The convictions arrive for review before any decision about issued-license interests or other counts.

**Precedent applied.** None supplied on this statutory question.

**Old sim.** Stone previously took this disposition: Reverse. His proposed rule was: A sovereign’s power to issue, withhold, or condition a license is regulatory authority, not property in the sovereign’s hands before issuance. The old Court adopted the position in substance on an independently held ground; Stone caused no material change.

**Stone innovation.** None. Stone reads the enacted property element and classifies the State's preissuance interest by legal function.

**Stone's position now.** Reverse and remand. Before issuance, Louisiana possessed regulatory authority to grant, deny, and condition a license, not a proprietary asset the scheme obtained from the State. Application fees and anticipated tax receipts do not transform the regulatory decision itself into property. Rungs 3 and 9 control, and the criminal-law clarification requires enforcement of the enacted element rather than enlargement of the offense.

**Doctrinal intent.** Interpret the mail-fraud property element; distinguish regulatory authority from an existing proprietary interest; reserve other theories and counts.

**Proposed holding.** A State's unissued regulatory license is not property in the State's hands for purposes of the mail-fraud statute merely because the State controls issuance and expects fees or revenue.

**Limit.** The position does not decide the treatment of an issued or transferable license, money already owed, an existing franchise or proprietary asset, honest-services fraud, or independently valid counts.

**Novelty.** Statutory or constitutional interpretation.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old result follows the current enacted-elements discipline.


### 4. *City of Indianapolis v. Edmond*

**Question presented.** Whether the Fourth Amendment permits suspicionless vehicle checkpoints whose primary programmatic purpose is detecting illegal narcotics, and what disposition follows from the Seventh Circuit's reversal of the denial of relief.

**What the case is about.** Indianapolis operated suspicionless vehicle checkpoints whose primary programmatic purpose was narcotics interdiction. Officers briefly stopped motorists, observed them, asked questions, and used a drug-detection dog; the District Court denied relief and the Seventh Circuit reversed and remanded. The supplied material confirms that no injunction had yet been entered.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *Vernonia School District 47J v. Acton*, simulated *Chandler v. Miller*, and the supplied special-needs search standard.

**Old sim.** The old simulation produced no merits position or disposition because it paused the matter over a conflict in the stated lower-court posture.

**Stone innovation.** None. Stone applies the established purpose boundary of the special-needs and checkpoint cases.

**Stone's position now.** Affirm the Seventh Circuit's judgment and remand for ordinary equitable proceedings. The checkpoint seizure was designed to uncover evidence of ordinary crime, so it cannot be justified as a non-law-enforcement special need and requires individualized suspicion absent a recognized checkpoint category. The exterior dog sniff does not cure the antecedent seizure. Rungs 2 and 6 control through application of the existing purpose rule.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply the checkpoint and special-needs boundary; distinguish roadway safety, border, and emergency checkpoints; choose a corrective remand.

**Proposed holding.** A suspicionless checkpoint whose primary programmatic purpose is general narcotics detection violates the Fourth Amendment because ordinary criminal investigation is not a special need dispensing with individualized suspicion.

**Limit.** The position does not invalidate checkpoints directed to border control, roadway safety, immediate public danger, or another recognized non-law-enforcement purpose, and it does not itself prescribe the final injunction.

**Novelty.** Application disagreement only.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes. The old simulation produced no merits position because it paused over the posture conflict; the corrected supplied posture permits Stone to reach the ordinary Fourth Amendment question.


### 5. *Eastern Associated Coal Corp. v. United Mine Workers of America*

**Question presented.** Is an award reinstating a truck driver who twice tested positive for marijuana unenforceable as contrary to an explicit, well-defined, dominant public policy?

**What the case is about.** An arbitrator ordered conditional reinstatement of a truck driver who twice tested positive for marijuana, with treatment, testing, and disciplinary safeguards. The Fourth Circuit enforced the award, and the employer argued that reinstatement violated a dominant public policy of transportation safety.

**Precedent applied.** None supplied; actual *W.R. Grace* and *Misco* supply the real-law baseline.

**Old sim.** Stone previously took this disposition: Affirm. His proposed rule was: A court may refuse enforcement only when the award itself violates an explicit, well-defined, dominant public policy found in law; a general safety policy does not create a discharge requirement absent from positive law. The old Court adopted the position in substance on an independently held ground; Stone caused no material change.

**Stone innovation.** None. Stone applies the narrow public-policy exception and refuses to add a discharge command absent from positive law.

**Stone's position now.** Affirm. Public-policy review asks whether the award itself requires conduct forbidden by an explicit, well-defined legal rule, not whether a court disapproves the employee's past conduct. The governing transportation regime required safety measures but did not require permanent discharge, and the award's conditions addressed the identified risk. Rungs 2 and 9 control.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply the labor-arbitration public-policy exception; interpret positive law; distinguish illegality from general policy concern.

**Proposed holding.** A labor-arbitration award may not be refused enforcement on public-policy grounds unless the award itself contravenes an explicit legal rule; a general safety policy does not create a mandatory-discharge rule omitted from law.

**Limit.** An award compelling unlawful operation, defeating required testing, or retaining an employee whom law expressly orders removed would present a different case.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old outcome remains the source-bound application of existing labor law.


### 6. *Bush v. Palm Beach County Canvassing Board*

**Question presented.** Did the Florida Supreme Court impermissibly alter the legislature’s Article II framework or misunderstand 3 U.S.C. §5 when construing Florida’s presidential-election statutes?

**What the case is about.** The Florida Supreme Court extended a presidential-election certification deadline and required acceptance of amended county returns. The federal objections asserted that the state court altered the legislature's Article II framework or misunderstood 3 U.S.C. §5, but the opinion under review did not clearly identify which state-law, state-constitutional, or federal premise drove its construction.

**Precedent applied.** None supplied on the Article II or §5 questions.

**Old sim.** Stone previously sought vacatur and clarification while also proposing substantive Article II and §5 rules. The old Court adopted the judgment and those substantive rules in substance but narrowed the detailed clarification sequence; Stone caused no material change because the operative grounds were independently held.

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary judicial move: vacatur and a clarifying remand, with issue reservation. Stone does not create an Article II framework on an ambiguous state-law record.

**Stone's position now.** Vacate and remand for prompt clarification. Responsible federal review is impossible without knowing whether the state judgment rests on ordinary statutory construction, the Florida Constitution, or an asserted federal command. The narrowest adequate disposition is to obtain that answer from the authoritative state court and reserve the merits. Rungs 1, 5, 6, and 7 control.

**Doctrinal intent.** Vacate; remand for clarification; reserve Article II, §5, and the ultimate state-law deadline.

**Proposed holding.** When a preserved federal objection to a state high court's presidential-election judgment depends on materially unclear state-law or federal premises, the judgment should be vacated and remanded for clarification rather than decided on assumptions about state law.

**Limit.** The position does not adopt an independent-state-legislature theory, decide whether §5 is mandatory in every setting, prescribe Florida law, select a deadline or slate, or determine an election result.

**Novelty.** Issue reservation or minimalist disposition.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes. The disposition survives, but the old simulation unnecessarily decided broad Article II and §5 rules; current Stone stops at the clarifying remand.


### 7. *Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph*

**Question presented.** Is an order compelling arbitration and dismissing the action final under 9 U.S.C. §16, and does contractual silence about arbitration costs make the agreement unenforceable?

**What the case is about.** Randolph's financing agreement required arbitration but was silent about arbitral costs. The District Court compelled arbitration and dismissed the entire action; the Eleventh Circuit treated the order as final and held the agreement unenforceable on the undeveloped cost record.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *Doctor's Associates, Inc. v. Casarotto* and the supplied FAA equal-treatment rule. No supplied precedent fixes the prohibitive-cost showing.

**Old sim.** Stone previously sought the same split disposition and a concrete cost showing on remand. The old Court adopted finality and the claimant-burden rule, and it adopted Stone's requested opportunity for record development before enforcement; that remedial revision was Stone's only material effect.

**Stone innovation.** None distinctive. Stone applies the effective-vindication principle, keeps the claimant's burden, and uses record development rather than a categorical rule.

**Stone's position now.** Affirm appellate finality, reverse the categorical cost ruling, and remand. Dismissal ended the federal action and therefore produced a final decision; contractual silence alone establishes neither access nor foreclosure. Randolph must make a concrete, claimant-specific showing that unavoidable costs are likely to make the arbitral forum practically unavailable, and the record should be developed before enforcement. Rungs 5 and 7 control.

**Doctrinal intent.** Interpret FAA finality; apply effective vindication; require a concrete evidentiary showing; remand for record development.

**Proposed holding.** An order compelling arbitration and dismissing all claims is final, and a cost objection requires concrete evidence that mandatory arbitral charges are likely to make the forum practically unavailable to the claimant; contractual silence and speculation alone are insufficient.

**Limit.** Ordinary inconvenience or uncertain expense is insufficient; lawful cost allocation or severability may cure a demonstrated problem, and hostility to arbitration cannot supply the result.

**Novelty.** Remedial or dispositional difference.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old position already used the narrow evidentiary and remand route rather than treating silence as dispositive.


### 8. *Bush v. Gore*

**Question presented.** Did the Florida recount order violate Equal Protection or Article II, and what remedy follows?

**What the case is about.** The Florida Supreme Court ordered a statewide manual recount in the presidential contest under a general voter-intent standard. The supplied record showed materially varying county and counting-team standards, differing ballot universes, and no adequate statewide method for reconciling comparable ballots; the case arrived on Equal Protection and Article II objections with an urgent remedial dispute.

**Precedent applied.** None supplied. The actual December 4 clarifying disposition is part of the real-law background, but no position reconstructed earlier in this run is precedent.

**Old sim.** Stone previously sought corrective remand, an equal-treatment rule, rejection of the Article II claim, and a nonmandatory reading of §5. The old Court adopted those propositions in substance while narrowing his detailed administrative framework; Stone's draft materially changed the old remedy from termination to corrective remand.

**Stone innovation.** Genuine doctrinal move: extend existing equal-protection principles to state-administered legal valuation of ballots in one statewide recount. The corrective remand is an ordinary remedy, and no named multi-part framework is needed.

**Stone's position now.** Vacate the defective recount directions and remand for prompt, written, public, sufficiently objective, and reviewable statewide standards if Florida law still permits a recount. Once the State undertakes one adjudicative recount for one statewide office, materially comparable ballots cannot receive materially different legal treatment because of county happenstance. Section 5 is a conditional federal safe harbor rather than an independent command to terminate state proceedings; the broader Article II claim is unnecessary and reserved. Rungs 5, 6, 7, 10, and 11 control: the equal-treatment extension is stated openly, and the remedy remains corrective rather than terminal.

**Doctrinal intent.** Extend equal-protection application; interpret §5; reserve Article II; choose corrective remand rather than termination.

**Proposed holding.** When a State conducts a statewide adjudicative recount for one office, materially comparable ballots must be valued under sufficiently uniform and reviewable standards. A curable defect calls for vacatur and remand, and 3 U.S.C. §5 does not itself require federal termination of otherwise lawful state proceedings.

**Limit.** The position creates no general federal right to a recount, selects no ballot universe or technology, does not require identical treatment of materially different ballots, supplies no federal winner, and does not decide the broader Article II theory.

**Novelty.** Extension of existing doctrine.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes. The corrective disposition survives, but current Stone replaces the old elaborate framework with the minimum equal-treatment rule, interprets §5 only as necessary to remedy, and reserves Article II.


### 9. *Fiore v. White*

**Question presented.** Does Due Process permit a conviction when the State’s highest court confirms that the prosecution never proved an element that was always part of the offense?

**What the case is about.** Fiore held a permit but was convicted under a Pennsylvania offense that, as authoritatively construed, required operation without a permit. After the Pennsylvania Supreme Court confirmed that this element had always existed and had not been proved, the Third Circuit's denial of federal habeas relief remained before the Court.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *United States v. Gaudin* and the supplied rule requiring proof of every offense element; the authoritative Pennsylvania answer fixes state law.

**Old sim.** Stone previously sought reversal because an existing element was never proved. The old Court adopted the merits ground but used a narrower remand that left more procedural and double-jeopardy consequences unresolved; Stone caused no separate change.

**Stone innovation.** None. Stone applies the existing element-and-proof rule to the authoritative state-law definition.

**Stone's position now.** Reverse and remand. The state court's answer explains what the statute meant when Fiore acted and was tried; it is not a later change. A conviction cannot stand when the prosecution failed to prove conduct within an element the offense always required. Rungs 2, 3, and 6 control.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply due-process element proof; accept authoritative state-law construction; remand remedy and any double-jeopardy consequence.

**Proposed holding.** Due Process forbids a conviction when authoritative state law confirms that the prosecution failed to prove an element that was part of the offense at the time of conduct and trial.

**Limit.** The position does not decide the retroactivity of a genuinely new construction, authorize appellate factfinding, or predetermine every remedial and double-jeopardy consequence on remand.

**Novelty.** Application disagreement only.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old position remains a direct application of the enacted-elements rule.


### 10. *Glover v. United States*

**Question presented.** May a court reject Strickland prejudice because counsel’s error increased imprisonment by less than a judicially defined “significant” amount?

**What the case is about.** Glover alleged that counsel's failure to object to an erroneous grouping increased his federal prison term, but the Seventh Circuit rejected prejudice because the increase was not judicially deemed significant. The collateral case arrives without a finding on the ordinary deficiency and counterfactual-sentencing questions under the correct rule.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *Williams v. Taylor* and the supplied *Strickland* prejudice standards.

**Old sim.** Stone previously took this disposition: Reverse and remand. His proposed rule was: A reasonable probability that deficient performance produced any greater lawful term of imprisonment may establish prejudice; a court may not impose a minimum number of months, percentage increase, or categorical significance requirement. The old Court adopted the position in substance on an independently held ground; Stone caused no material change.

**Stone innovation.** None. Stone rejects an extra quantitative prerequisite not found in *Strickland*.

**Stone's position now.** Reverse and remand. A reasonable probability of any actual increase in lawful imprisonment is constitutionally material; courts may not add a minimum number of months, percentage, or free-standing significance threshold. Glover still bears the ordinary burdens of showing deficient performance and a reasonable probability of a lower lawful sentence. Rungs 2 and 6 control.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply *Strickland*; reject an extra prejudice threshold; remand ordinary deficiency and counterfactual sentencing.

**Proposed holding.** A defendant may establish *Strickland* prejudice by showing a reasonable probability that counsel's deficient performance produced any greater term of imprisonment; no separate quantitative significance requirement applies.

**Limit.** Speculation about a lower sentence is insufficient, and the position does not presume deficiency or dictate the sentence on remand.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old position correctly removed the judge-created significance requirement.


### 11. *Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers*

**Question presented.** Does the Clean Water Act authorize federal jurisdiction over isolated, nonnavigable, intrastate ponds solely because migratory birds use them?

**What the case is about.** A local public entity sought to use isolated former gravel-pit ponds as a disposal site. The Corps asserted Clean Water Act jurisdiction solely because migratory birds used the ponds, and the Seventh Circuit sustained that assertion without identifying a tributary, adjacency, hydrological connection, or other water-based link to covered waters.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *Christensen v. Harris County*, simulated *United States v. Lopez*, simulated *United States v. Morrison*, and the supplied agency-interpretation standards.

**Old sim.** Stone previously sought reversal on statutory coverage and informal agency authority while reserving Congress's outer power. The old Court adopted those components in substance but declined his more detailed connected-waters categories; Stone caused no material change.

**Stone innovation.** None distinctive. Stone interprets the jurisdictional text, applies the informal-guidance rule, and reserves the constitutional boundary.

**Stone's position now.** Reverse and remand. Migratory-bird use alone does not make isolated intrastate ponds 'waters of the United States,' and an informal agency rationale cannot create a jurisdictional category Congress did not enact. The environmental importance of the ponds cannot substitute for statutory coverage, and neither Chevron nor Skidmore permits the agency to supply the missing water-based connection. Rungs 3, 8, and 9 control.

**Doctrinal intent.** Interpret statutory jurisdiction; apply informal-guidance deference limits; distinguish adjacent wetlands; reserve Congress's outer commerce power.

**Proposed holding.** The Clean Water Act does not reach isolated intrastate ponds solely because migratory birds use them, and an informal agency rationale cannot enlarge the Act beyond a legally cognizable connection to covered waters.

**Limit.** The position does not categorically exclude every geographically isolated wetland, prevent state regulation, or decide Congress's maximum Commerce Clause authority.

**Novelty.** Statutory or constitutional interpretation.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old statutory disposition remains consistent with current environmental, federalism, and agency-law commitments.


### 12. *Seling v. Young*

**Question presented.** May a person confined under a facially civil statute prove that its actual administration is punitive for Ex Post Facto or Double Jeopardy purposes?

**What the case is about.** Young was confined under Washington's sexually violent predator statute, which was facially classified as civil, but alleged that prisonlike conditions, inadequate treatment, duration, and release administration made his actual confinement punitive. The Ninth Circuit allowed an as-applied Ex Post Facto and Double Jeopardy challenge.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *Kansas v. Hendricks* and the supplied post-sentence commitment punishment and civil-commitment standards.

**Old sim.** Stone previously took this disposition: Affirm and remand. His proposed rule was: A facially civil commitment statute remains subject to a clearest-proof showing that its combined conditions, treatment structure, duration, release mechanics, and administration operate as punishment. The old Court adopted the position in substance on an independently held ground; Stone caused no material change.

**Stone innovation.** None. Controlling simulated precedent already makes actual punitive operation legally relevant.

**Stone's position now.** Affirm the availability of the as-applied challenge and remand for focused factfinding. The statute's civil label does not foreclose the clearest-proof showing that combined conditions, treatment structure, duration, and release mechanics operate as punishment in this confinement. Relief must be tied to the proven punitive defect rather than presume release or facial invalidity. Rungs 2, 3, and 7 control.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply simulated *Hendricks*; assess punitive operation as applied; choose defect-specific relief.

**Proposed holding.** A facially civil commitment statute remains subject to an as-applied Ex Post Facto or Double Jeopardy challenge upon clearest proof that its actual administration operates as punishment.

**Limit.** Ordinary treatment deficiencies, isolated misconduct, unpleasant conditions, or a disagreement with clinical judgment do not alone establish punishment, and the position does not order automatic release.

**Novelty.** Application disagreement only.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old position is compelled by the supplied simulated *Hendricks* holding.


### 13. *Illinois v. McArthur*

**Question presented.** May police with probable cause temporarily prevent unaccompanied entry into a home while diligently obtaining a warrant?

**What the case is about.** After McArthur's wife reported marijuana inside the family trailer, officers had probable cause and a concrete concern that evidence would be destroyed. They prevented McArthur from entering unaccompanied for a brief period while a warrant was diligently obtained, allowed supervised access, and did not search before the warrant; the Illinois court suppressed the evidence.

**Precedent applied.** None supplied on this precise temporary premises restraint.

**Old sim.** Stone previously took this disposition: Reverse. His proposed rule was: Police with probable cause may impose a brief, minimally intrusive restraint against unaccompanied entry while diligently obtaining a warrant, provided they do not search and permit reasonable supervised access. The old Court adopted the position in substance on an independently held ground; Stone caused no material change.

**Stone innovation.** None. Stone applies ordinary Fourth Amendment reasonableness to a narrowly bounded securing measure.

**Stone's position now.** Reverse. A brief outside restraint was reasonable because probable cause, a genuine evidence-preservation risk, diligent warrant pursuit, minimal intrusion, and supervised access kept the seizure tied to its purpose. The home remained protected from entry and search until the magistrate acted. Rungs 2 and 6 control.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply reasonableness; distinguish securing from entry or search; preserve the warrant default.

**Proposed holding.** Police with probable cause may briefly prevent unaccompanied entry into a home while diligently obtaining a warrant when a concrete evidence-preservation risk exists and the restraint uses the least intrusive practical means.

**Limit.** The position authorizes no prolonged exclusion, unsupported restraint, warrantless entry, manufactured delay, or continued seizure after diligent warrant pursuit should have ended.

**Novelty.** Application disagreement only.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old disposition remains the narrow purpose-bounded application Stone's current search jurisprudence requires.


### 14. *Brentwood Academy v. Tennessee Secondary School Athletic Association*

**Question presented.** Is a statewide athletic association a state actor when public schools and officials are pervasively entwined in its membership, governance, and enforcement?

**What the case is about.** The Tennessee Secondary School Athletic Association regulated interscholastic athletics statewide, was composed overwhelmingly of public schools, and was governed and administered through public-school officials. After it sanctioned Brentwood Academy, the Sixth Circuit held that the Association was not a state actor.

**Precedent applied.** The supplied Lugar-Blum private-conduct attribution inquiry.

**Old sim.** Stone previously took this disposition: Reverse and remand. His proposed rule was: Pervasive public entwinement in an association’s composition, governance, and enforcement makes the challenged regulatory choice fairly attributable to the State. The old Court adopted the position in substance on an independently held ground; Stone caused no material change.

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary judicial move: extend existing attribution principles to pervasive public entwinement in composition, governance, and enforcement.

**Stone's position now.** Reverse and remand. The challenged regulation is fairly attributable to the State because public institutions and officials pervasively constitute, govern, and enforce the Association's authority. Public membership or funding alone would not suffice, but the combined institutional control does. Rungs 2, 3, and 8 control.

**Doctrinal intent.** Extend and clarify state-action attribution; remand the First Amendment merits.

**Proposed holding.** A nominally private statewide association is a state actor when public schools and public officials are pervasively entwined in its composition, governance, and enforcement of the challenged rule.

**Limit.** The position does not constitutionalize every private association receiving public funds, serving public institutions, or including public members; attribution remains tied to the challenged function and actual public control.

**Novelty.** Extension of existing doctrine.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old position remains a legally grounded attribution extension rather than a general public-importance rule.


### 15. *Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett*

**Question presented.** Did Congress validly abrogate state sovereign immunity for private Title I damages claims as enforcement of the Fourteenth Amendment?

**What the case is about.** State employees sought Title I damages for alleged disability discrimination in employment, and the Eleventh Circuit sustained Congress's abrogation of state sovereign immunity. The case requires identifying the constitutional equality standard Congress was enforcing before applying the §5 congruence-and-proportionality inquiry.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents*, simulated *City of Boerne v. Flores*, and the supplied state-sovereign-immunity enforcement ladder.

**Old sim.** Stone previously proposed intermediate scrutiny for disability classifications and, alternatively, claim-specific validity under the received rational-basis rule. The old Court rejected the scrutiny change but adopted the bounded fallback after a draft revision prompted by Stone.

**Stone innovation.** Genuine doctrinal change: modify the received disability tier from rational basis to intermediate scrutiny. This is a different scrutiny assignment, stated openly.

**Stone's position now.** Affirm and remand for ordinary Title I adjudication. Stone would modify *Cleburne*'s nominal rational-basis assignment because its own operation was more searching, the sealed-tier description is internally inconsistent, and the lower assignment systematically understates disability's history as a governmental exclusion device. Under intermediate scrutiny, Title I's bounded prohibition of unjustified disparate treatment and its reasonable-accommodation duty, limited by qualification and undue-hardship defenses, are congruent and proportional §5 enforcement. If the received rational-basis assignment is retained, Stone would independently preserve damages actions at least for intentional unequal treatment and denial of a specific necessary accommodation to an otherwise-qualified employee. Rungs 10 and 11 govern the candid scrutiny change; rung 7 supports the narrower fallback.

**Doctrinal intent.** Change scrutiny; apply §5 congruence and proportionality; preserve a claim-specific fallback; remand merits and remedies.

**Proposed holding.** Governmental disability classifications should receive intermediate scrutiny, and Congress validly abrogated state immunity for Title I's bounded employment duties enforcing equal treatment and reasonable accommodation of otherwise-qualified employees.

**Limit.** The position does not establish Title I liability, eliminate qualification, undue-hardship, or fundamental-function defenses, validate every statutory application, or authorize relief beyond the enacted cause and valid abrogation.

**Novelty.** Different scrutiny assignment.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old simulation already stated the same primary scrutiny change and the same narrower fallback.


### 16. *Buckman Co. v. Plaintiffs’ Legal Committee*

**Question presented.** Does federal law preempt state claims whose asserted duty exists solely because a consultant allegedly defrauded the FDA?

**What the case is about.** Patients alleged that a regulatory consultant made fraudulent representations to the FDA concerning orthopedic bone screws and pleaded state-law claims whose asserted duty existed only because of the federal regulatory relationship. The Third Circuit held those fraud-on-the-FDA theories not preempted.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *Geier v. American Honda Motor Co.*, simulated *Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick*, and the supplied conflict-preemption inquiry.

**Old sim.** Stone previously took this disposition: Reverse. His proposed rule was: Claims existing solely because of alleged fraud on a federal agency are conflict preempted, while independently grounded traditional state duties remain available. The old Court adopted the position in substance on an independently held ground; Stone caused no material change.

**Stone innovation.** None. Stone performs source-specific conflict preemption and preserves independently grounded state duties.

**Stone's position now.** Reverse. A claim whose duty and breach exist solely because a private party allegedly defrauded the FDA intrudes on the federal agency's exclusive enforcement relationship and conflicts with the enacted regulatory scheme. Traditional state duties to patients or consumers remain distinct when they exist independently of the federal disclosure duty. Rungs 3 and 9 control.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply conflict preemption; distinguish a federal-agency duty from traditional state duties; sever claims by source.

**Proposed holding.** State claims existing solely because a party allegedly defrauded a federal regulatory agency are conflict preempted, while independently grounded traditional state-law duties remain available.

**Limit.** The position does not preempt every claim using FDA evidence, immunize independently tortious conduct, or decide the merits of preserved design, warning, manufacturing, or misrepresentation duties owed under state law.

**Novelty.** Statutory or constitutional interpretation.
