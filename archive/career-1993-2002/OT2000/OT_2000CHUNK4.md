- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies current law without changing it. #### Issue 2: Coercion

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The coercion claim was dismissed at the pleading stage. Stone states the governing standard and decides only that Kansas is entitled to factual adjudication under it.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone openly resolves the question left open by *Printz* and *Bradley*. A spending condition is coercive when the structure and magnitude of the threatened financial loss materially eliminate the State's reasonably practicable ability to decline, so that the formal offer operates in practical effect as a command to administer federal policy. Relevant, nonexclusive considerations include the amount and centrality of threatened funds; dependence on the existing stream; whether Congress offers genuinely new money or leverages an established program; the relationship between the old funds and the new demand; the scope of the added obligations; transition time; realistic replacement, withdrawal, or restructuring options; effects on separately funded functions; whether Congress is leveraging dependency to obtain state administration it could not directly command; and any genuine waiver or safety valve. No fixed percentage controls. Kansas carries the ultimate burden of proving coercion.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State's theoretical legal power to refuse funds does not defeat a coercion claim when the pleaded structure and magnitude of the threatened loss may have eliminated its reasonably practicable ability to decline. Coercion is determined contextually, without a fixed percentage threshold.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Kansas alleges that approximately $29.3 million in Title IV-D funds and $101.9 million in TANF funds were placed at risk, while federal funds supplied a substantial share of operating and systems costs. Those allegations do not establish coercion, but they make it improper to assume at Rule 12(b)(6) that Kansas could replace the funds or withdraw without material disruption. The remand must examine the practical structure of the offer rather than equate formal legal choice with genuine voluntariness. Spending and commandeering remain separate: Congress may obtain voluntary state administration through valid conditions even when it could not command the same administration directly.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding creates no numerical safe harbor or presumption, does not invalidate cooperative federal programs, and does not make a new condition coercive merely because compliance is costly. Clear, related conditions remain lawful when the State retains a genuine practical choice.
