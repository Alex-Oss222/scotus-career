## 41. Ratzlaf v. United States

### Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** Ratzlaf v. United States
- **Citation or Docket:** 510 U.S. 135 (1994); Supreme Court No. 92-1196; useful lower-court citation: 976 F.2d 1280 (9th Cir. 1992).
- **October Term:** 1993.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits case, argued or submitted, entering the simulated decision event.
- **Simulated Event Date:** January 11, 1994.
- **Question Presented:** Whether conviction for willfully violating the federal anti-structuring law requires proof that the defendant knew the structuring itself was unlawful.
- **Question Granted:** Whether conviction for willfully violating the federal anti-structuring law requires proof that the defendant knew the structuring itself was unlawful.
- **Legal Field:** Criminal statutory interpretation, mens rea, currency-transaction reporting, and willfulness.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed Ratzlaf's conviction after the jury was instructed that he had to know of and intend to evade the bank-reporting requirement, but did not have to know structuring was unlawful.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Ratzlaf asks the Court to reverse the conviction and remand because the instruction omitted an element of willfulness.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Ratzlaf owed a casino $160,000 and was told that cash transactions over $10,000 would be reported. With a casino employee, he purchased multiple cashier's checks under $10,000 at different banks. The Government proved deliberate evasion of reporting but the jury was not required to find that Ratzlaf knew the structuring method was illegal.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The instructional objection was preserved and concerns the elements Congress enacted. The Court may not affirm by treating statutory willfulness as surplusage.

- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Ginsburg wrote for five Justices. Justice Blackmun dissented, joined by Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices O'Connor and Thomas.
- **Historical Holding:** The willfulness requirement in 31 U.S.C. § 5322 required proof that the defendant knew the structuring was unlawful, not merely that he intended to evade a bank's reporting duty.

- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not create a general ignorance-of-law defense, alter civil reporting duties, or prevent Congress from amending the mens rea through clear enacted language.

The statutory balance therefore falls against disclosure.

Prospective announcement gave notice of possible punishment but did not convert punishment into a coercive civil remedy.

#### Issue 1: Objective custody under Miranda

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the governing custody standard. He leaves the record-intensive custody application and remedial consequences to the California court in the first instance.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** What role, if any, an officer's uncommunicated belief about suspect status plays in the Miranda custody inquiry.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the received objective test. Custody turns on the circumstances manifested to the person being questioned and whether a reasonable person in that position would understand freedom of movement to be restrained to the degree associated with arrest. An officer's private belief cannot affect that perception. The belief matters only when communicated by word or deed, or when it bears on the credibility of the officer's account of the objective circumstances.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An officer's undisclosed view that an interviewee is or is not a suspect is irrelevant to Miranda custody; communicated words or conduct may be considered only insofar as they would affect a reasonable person's perception of freedom to terminate the encounter and leave.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Miranda responds to the coercive setting created by custody, not the strength of the government's private suspicions. A person cannot respond to an intention that was never manifested. The California court's focus inquiry therefore used a legally irrelevant consideration. Because the remaining circumstances permit competing record-based arguments, the state court should apply the objective standard before this Court determines suppression or harmlessness.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not make stationhouse questioning automatically custodial, render suspect status dispositive when communicated, or decide whether Stansbury was in custody on this record.
