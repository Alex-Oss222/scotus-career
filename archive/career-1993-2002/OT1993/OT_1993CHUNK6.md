## 51. Florence County School District Four v. Carter

### Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** Florence County School District Four v. Carter
- **Citation or Docket:** 510 U.S. 7 (1993); Supreme Court No. 91-1523; useful lower-court citation: 950 F.2d 156 (4th Cir. 1991).
- **October Term:** 1993.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits case, argued or submitted, entering the simulated decision event.
- **Simulated Event Date:** November 9, 1993.
- **Question Presented:** Whether the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act permits reimbursement when parents reject an inappropriate public-school program and obtain an appropriate private education at a school that is not state approved and does not satisfy every statutory requirement governing public placements.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act permits reimbursement when parents reject an inappropriate public-school program and obtain an appropriate private education at a school that is not state approved and does not satisfy every statutory requirement governing public placements.
- **Legal Field:** Education and disability law, IDEA, unilateral private placement, equitable reimbursement, and state approval.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit affirmed a judgment reimbursing Shannon Carter's parents. It held the school district's proposed individualized education program inadequate and the education at Trident Academy appropriate, despite Trident's lack of blanket state approval and noncompliance with procedures designed for public placements.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The school district asks the Court to reverse and hold reimbursement categorically unavailable for an unapproved private placement that does not satisfy every element of the statutory definition of a free appropriate public education.
- **Material Facts and Record:** The district proposed an IEP with limited individualized instruction and modest annual goals. Carter's parents challenged it and placed her at Trident Academy, where the district court found she made substantial educational progress. The public placement's inadequacy and Trident's substantive appropriateness are settled for purposes of review; the remaining dispute is whether Trident's regulatory status defeats reimbursement.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The case presents a narrow statutory and remedial question. The findings that the proposed IEP was inappropriate and that Trident provided an education otherwise proper under IDEA are not before the Court. The reasonableness and equitable amount of reimbursement remain subject to the district court's remedial discretion.

#### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O'Connor wrote for a unanimous Court.
- **Historical Holding:** Parents are not barred from reimbursement merely because their unilateral private placement lacks state approval or does not meet requirements that sensibly apply only to public-agency placements. #### Issue 1: IDEA reimbursement for a parent-selected private placement

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory eligibility for reimbursement and the role of equitable reasonableness. He accepts the settled findings about the two placements and does not create a general judicial power to select educational programs.

The public-supervision and agency-IEP requirements describe what government must provide, not what parents must recreate on their own. The settled record establishes that Trident produced meaningful educational benefit. Reimbursement therefore restores the promised free education rather than awarding a windfall, while equity protects the district from unreasonable cost.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not reimburse a merely preferred placement, dispense with proof that the public IEP was unlawful and the private education appropriate, require payment of unreasonable costs, or prevent States from regulating private schools through otherwise valid law.

#### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Second Circuit.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Remand proceedings may determine whether Hancock breached ERISA duties and what relief the statute authorizes. The courts must exclude genuinely guaranteed-benefit components while treating the free funds according to Hancock's actual authority and control.

#### Issue 1: Guaranteed benefit exclusion and fiduciary control over free funds

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches statutory classification and functional fiduciary status. He does not decide breach, damages, restitution, or the treatment of a materially different annuity contract.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the statutory exclusion attaches to an insurance contract as an indivisible label or only to the components that actually guarantee an aggregate benefit and place investment risk on the insurer.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone reads ERISA by text and operation. 'To the extent' requires component analysis. A policy component is excluded when the insurer commits to a definite benefit and assumes the corresponding investment risk. A pool remains a plan asset when the insurer guarantees neither a reasonable return nor conversion into defined benefits and instead exercises authority or control over the plan's value. Fiduciary status then follows functionally to that extent, not from the insurer's corporate label.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** ERISA's guaranteed-benefit-policy exclusion covers only the portion of an insurance contract that genuinely guarantees plan benefits and allocates investment risk to the insurer; unconverted free funds lacking those guarantees remain plan assets, and an insurer is a fiduciary to the extent it exercises discretionary authority or control over them.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Treating the entire contract as excluded would delete Congress's limiting words and permit mixed instruments to shelter plan assets by attaching one guaranteed component. Here the free funds could fluctuate with Hancock's investment performance, and the contract supplied no genuine guarantee that a defined benefit would be paid from them. Hancock's control over those funds is the function ERISA's fiduciary definition reaches. Whether that control was exercised loyally and prudently remains for later proceedings.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not make every insurer a fiduciary, treat assets backing genuinely fixed benefits as plan assets, or decide how another contract with a guaranteed return and fixed conversion mechanism should be classified.

#### Issue 1: Evenhanded discretionary fee awards under Copyright Act § 505

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the governing legal standard. He does not decide Fogerty's final entitlement or adopt an exhaustive checklist of fee factors.

- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The dual rule lacks a textual basis and treats equally described prevailing parties differently. Copyright law serves public access to lawful expression as well as enforcement of valid rights, so a meritorious defense can clarify boundaries as usefully as a successful infringement claim. Automatic awards would equally disregard Congress's use of discretionary language. Remand permits the trial court to exercise the judgment Congress assigned without the Ninth Circuit's one-sided threshold.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not import Title VII's Christiansburg rule, create a presumption for prevailing defendants, make fees routine, or convert illustrative equitable considerations into mandatory elements.

- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not prevent Florida from requiring accurate identification of a certifying body or another concise disclosure when the State establishes a real risk of deception and a reasonable relationship between the disclosure and that risk.

## 55. Livadas v. Bradshaw

#### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit and remand for further proceedings consistent with the three holdings below.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The lower courts must provide relief through the lawful § 1983 and equitable channels, while separately respecting immunity and limits on official-capacity monetary relief. California may administer its wage law through valid neutral priorities, but it may not categorically deny enforcement because an employee exercised federal collective-bargaining rights.

#### Issue 1: NLRA preemption of the nonenforcement policy

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches conflict and Machinists-style preemption of the actual policy. He does not invalidate § 229 on its face or hold that every state distinction involving union representation is preempted.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the policy makes the exercise of federally protected bargaining and arbitration rights the condition for losing state enforcement.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone separates the state statute from the administrative policy. Federal labor law protects the process of collective bargaining and the resulting agreement against state penalties. California may allocate resources neutrally, but it may not condition enforcement of a nonwaivable wage right on an employee's refusal to enter a collective-bargaining agreement with arbitration. The policy's practical operation, not its state-law label, controls.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The NLRA preempts a state policy that categorically withholds enforcement of otherwise available, nonwaivable wage protections solely because an employee is covered by a collective-bargaining agreement containing an arbitration clause.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The policy forced employees to choose between the benefit of federal collective bargaining and the State's ordinary wage-enforcement service. That is the same structural penalty federal preemption forbids when a State withdraws benefits because a person used an NLRA right. The Commissioner's later resource-allocation and pro-arbitration rationales do not match a rule triggered categorically by agreement coverage rather than by any actual contractual dispute.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require a State to adjudicate a claim actually preempted by § 301, prevent neutral enforcement priorities, or invalidate a genuine opt-out regime chosen through bargaining where federal and state law permit it.

#### Issue 2: Section 301 preemption of the wage-penalty claim

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches § 301 preemption on the undisputed record. He does not resolve a future case in which liability or the wage rate genuinely depends on contested contract interpretation.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Livadas's state statutory right and liability elements arise independently of the agreement even though the agreement supplies a number used to compute the penalty.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the distinction between interpreting a collective-bargaining agreement and merely consulting it. Section 301 protects federal uniformity in contract meaning; it does not extinguish nonwaivable state rights whose elements arise independently. Livadas had to show a willful delay in final wage payment. The calendar and state statute decided liability, while the undisputed contractual wage rate supplied arithmetic only.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** LMRA § 301 does not preempt an independent state wage-payment claim when liability requires no interpretation of a collective-bargaining agreement and the agreement is consulted only for an undisputed wage rate used to calculate relief.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** No party disputed a contractual term, the scope of arbitration, or whether the union bargained away the right, which state law made nonwaivable. Treating every reference to a wage rate as preemptive would allow § 301 to erase ordinary state protections whenever a union agreement records compensation. That expansion is unnecessary to preserve federal uniformity and contrary to Lingle's source-based distinction.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not prevent § 301 preemption when resolving liability actually requires interpreting disputed agreement terms, when the asserted right is contractual, or when a lawful waiver question depends on the agreement.

#### Issue 3: Section 1983 cause of action for interference with federal labor rights

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the existence of the § 1983 cause of action. He leaves immunity, damages, prospective relief, fees, and the exact remedial order to lawful lower-court proceedings.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Livadas may use § 1983 to seek redress from a state official for a policy that penalizes her federally protected completion of collective bargaining.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone keeps the right, cause of action, defendant, and remedy separate. The NLRA protects employees against state interference with collective bargaining, the duty is concrete enough for judicial enforcement, and Congress supplied no comprehensive remedial scheme for this form of state action that displaces § 1983. That establishes a cause of action, not an automatic entitlement to every requested remedy.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An employee may invoke § 1983 against an appropriate state official to redress a state policy that infringes the concrete federal labor right to complete collective bargaining without governmental penalty, absent congressional displacement; immunity and remedy remain separate questions.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Golden State already recognizes that federal labor law can secure rights enforceable against government through § 1983 even though the NLRB governs many private labor disputes. The Commissioner's obligation not to penalize completed bargaining is neither vague nor dependent on NLRB factfinding. Because Congress created no exclusive route for this state interference, the ordinary statutory remedy remains available within its established limits.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not make every preemption claim actionable under § 1983, create damages against the State, waive immunity, or predetermine the proper form or scope of relief.
