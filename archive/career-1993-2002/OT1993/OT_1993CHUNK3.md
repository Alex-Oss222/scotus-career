#### Issue 1: Standard for a content-neutral speech injunction

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the governing standard because the final, contempt-backed injunction directly burdens the named petitioners' speech.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** What First Amendment standard governs a content-neutral injunction directed at identified speakers based on prior conduct.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies injunction-specific First Amendment tailoring. Each provision must serve a significant interest and burden no more speech than necessary to remedy the demonstrated violations. Because the order is party-specific and based on an existing record, the court must compare each restraint with the conduct proved; it may not treat the injunction as a unit or use misconduct supporting one provision to justify another.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A content-neutral speech injunction must be reviewed provision by provision, and each restraint must burden no more speech than necessary to remedy the demonstrated unlawful conduct and protect the affected access, safety, operations, or residential interests.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The injunction responded to obstruction and related misconduct rather than disagreement with the anti-abortion message, but content neutrality does not establish fit. A court crafting relief for identified parties can target the conduct shown and therefore must justify each geographic, communicative, and operational restriction against that record. Contempt enforcement makes unsupported breadth especially consequential.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The standard does not invalidate party-specific injunctions, protect threats or obstruction, or disable generally applicable trespass, harassment, and noise laws.

#### Issue 2: Validity of the separate injunction provisions

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches each separable provision to the extent the record permits and remands any necessary redrafting to the issuing court.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Which provisions are sufficiently tied to the demonstrated misconduct and which suppress substantially more speech than the record justifies.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone upholds access, obstruction, and procedure-time noise restrictions only to the extent supported by the record. The broad no-approach zone, any unsupported extension onto private property, the image restriction, and the 300-foot residential zone are invalid because their breadth is not tied to the proved need. The lawful principle is conduct-triggered restraint, not a judicially selected nationwide personal bubble.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The supported access, obstruction, and tailored noise provisions may remain; the broad no-approach, unsupported private-property, image, and residential restrictions must be vacated because they burden substantially more speech than necessary to remedy the established conduct.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The record permits protection of clinic access and medical operations from obstruction and disruptive noise. It does not justify suppressing images, barring all uninvited approaches across 300 feet, extending restraints onto property without a demonstrated basis, or excluding speech throughout a 300-foot residential area. The issuing court may address threats, crowding, persistent close pursuit, and comparable conduct, but ordinary consensual communication cannot be prohibited merely because a listener would prefer not to hear it.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone adopts no eight-foot or other fixed personal-bubble rule. The case leaves the issuing court to determine on evidence whether any specified distance is necessary, how long a conduct-triggered restriction may operate, and what narrower wording will provide complete relief without suppressing lawful communication.

#### Issue 3: Persons acting in concert with named enjoined parties

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the separately preserved challenge because the injunction expressly extends beyond the named petitioners to persons acting in concert with them. He treats the question as one of the lawful reach of a contempt-backed injunction, not as a basis to reconsider the validity of each substantive restriction.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the injunction may bind a nonparty who acts in concert with a named enjoined person without violating protected association or extending contempt authority to independent speakers.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the traditional active-concert principle subject to First Amendment and notice limits. A court may bind a nonparty who has actual notice of the injunction and actively coordinates with, aids, or participates with an enjoined party in conduct the injunction lawfully prohibits. Mere association, shared viewpoint, presence at the same protest, organizational membership, or independent parallel advocacy is insufficient. The phrase “acting in concert” is therefore valid only in its conventional narrow sense of actual coordinated participation in prohibited conduct.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The injunction may constitutionally apply to persons with actual notice who act in active concert or participation with named enjoined parties to accomplish conduct lawfully prohibited by the injunction, but it may not reach persons solely because of association, shared advocacy, presence, or independent conduct.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Equity must be able to prevent an enjoined party from evading a valid order through agents or collaborators, and traditional injunction law permits contempt against persons who knowingly assist such evasion. The First Amendment does not permit the same language to become guilt by association. Construed to require actual notice and coordinated participation in conduct that the injunction may lawfully restrain, the provision enforces the order rather than suppressing a political association. On that construction, the separate challenge does not require invalidating the acting-in-concert language.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not authorize contempt based on ideological affiliation, organizational membership, attendance at a demonstration, sympathy with the named parties, or simultaneous but independently chosen protest activity. A nonparty remains bound only to the extent traditional injunction law and actual notice make that person's coordinated participation subject to the order.
