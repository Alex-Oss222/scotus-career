##### Issue 1: Granted merits question

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** A backward-looking access claim must identify the lost underlying cause of action and an unavailable remedy. The case may be resolved at the pleading stage if those elements are absent.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** What must a plaintiff identify to state a backward-looking denial-of-access claim alleging that official deception caused the loss of an earlier legal action?
- **Stone’s Legal Position:** Court access protects the ability to pursue a concrete claim and remedy, not an abstract interest in truthful government; where the same remedy remains available, the alleged access injury is missing. Stone reserves the constitutional source and the creation of a new federal-officer damages action.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A backward-looking access claim must identify a specific nonfrivolous underlying action, official conduct that caused its loss, and a remedy that cannot now be obtained through surviving litigation. The claim fails when the alleged lost remedy remains available.
- **Stone’s Reasoning and Record Application:** Court access protects the ability to pursue a concrete claim and remedy, not an abstract interest in truthful government; where the same remedy remains available, the alleged access injury is missing. Stone reserves the constitutional source and the creation of a new federal-officer damages action. On this record, Harbury alleged that federal officials concealed information about her husband's detention and death in Guatemala and thereby frustrated earlier litigation. Surviving claims still offered the remedy she described as lost. The Court may determine injury and pleading sufficiency without deciding whether every backward-looking access claim exists constitutionally or whether *Bivens* extends to this context.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The position does not decide whether every backward-looking category is constitutionally recognized, whether *Bivens* extends here, or whether amendment must be allowed under ordinary procedural law.
