##### Issue 1: Affirmative child-placement danger

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Pinder v. - **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Standards and Tests, **Affirmative official creation or intensification of a private danger**: substantive due process may be violated when an official affirmatively creates or materially intensifies a specific, immediate, grave private danger, acts with the culpability required by the time for deliberation, and causes the exposure; promise and reliance are not universal elements for direct forced exposure. #### B. Stone’s approved position

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the en banc judgment of the Eighth Circuit and remand.
- **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Vacate the Rule 12 dismissal and remand for defendant-specific proceedings. The lower court must assess each official’s knowledge, affirmative placement authority, personal participation, culpability, causation, and qualified immunity under the plaintiff-supported allegations and later developed record. ##### Issue 1: Affirmative child-placement danger

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Court accepts well-pleaded allegations as true at the Rule 12 stage. Stone reaches the substantive constitutional boundary before qualified immunity and keeps each defendant’s personal responsibility separate. Negligence, poor investigation, and a failed protective judgment are not constitutional violations.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether officials may incur substantive-due-process liability when, after time to deliberate and with alleged knowledge of a specific sexual danger, they use state child-placement authority to return a child from custody to a placement that materially increases exposure to the identified danger.
- **Stone’s Legal Position:** Stone applies and clarifies the existing affirmative-danger rule. A State has no general constitutional duty to protect a child from private violence, but it may not use its legal custody and placement authority affirmatively to create or materially intensify a specific, immediate, grave danger. Where officials have time to investigate and deliberate, the plaintiff must prove deliberate indifference to the known or obvious danger, an affirmative placement act materially increasing vulnerability, personal participation, and causation. Promise and reliance are unnecessary because the theory rests on direct use of placement authority, not induced forbearance. Satisfying those elements establishes the constitutional violation; failure on any element leaves only state tort or administrative law.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Child-welfare officials violate substantive due process when, with time to deliberate, they affirmatively place or return a child into a known, specific, serious danger, materially increase the child’s vulnerability through that placement, and act with deliberate indifference that causes the resulting harm. No reliance on a specific governmental assurance is required. An official proved to have knowingly caused such a placement is not immune merely because no earlier case replicated the precise custody facts.
- **Stone’s Reasoning and Record Application:** The complaint alleges that the State held S.S. in legal custody, that responsible officials knew an identified convicted child molester had repeated access to her through her father, that warnings and professional assessments identified the risk, and that the officials nevertheless used their placement authority to return her to the father’s custody shortly after the identified offender personally complained about contact restrictions. Those allegations describe more than passive failure to protect and more than an unsuccessful prediction. They describe an affirmative legal transfer alleged to have increased S.S.’s exposure to a known abuser after ample time for deliberation. The Eighth Circuit therefore erred by treating the return as constitutionally identical to doing nothing and by resolving culpability against the complaint. Fair notice must be assessed from the governing affirmative-danger principles and obviousness, not a demand for an earlier case with the same family-placement facts.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding creates no general federal negligence action for child-welfare decisions, no constitutional guarantee of successful reunification, and no liability for an official lacking relevant knowledge, authority, participation, or causal responsibility. Qualified immunity and damages remain defendant specific.

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The facial overbreadth challenge is properly reached because the provisions directly regulate protected expression and their invalid applications are substantial in relation to their legitimate sweep.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** May the Federal Government prohibit sexually explicit images that appear to depict minors when no real child was used in their production?
- **Stone’s Legal Position:** *Ferber* is distinguished by the absence of an exploited child, and *Miller* cannot be bypassed by labeling protected nonobscene expression child pornography. Stone rejects a categorical rule based on resemblance alone and leaves direct laws against actual exploitation, fraud, solicitation, and narrowly defined pandering intact.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A nonobscene image falls within *Ferber*'s categorical child-pornography rule only when its production exploits an actual minor. Realistic appearance or suggestive marketing alone does not supply that harm.
- **Stone’s Reasoning and Record Application:** *Ferber* is distinguished by the absence of an exploited child, and *Miller* cannot be bypassed by labeling protected nonobscene expression child pornography. Stone rejects a categorical rule based on resemblance alone and leaves direct laws against actual exploitation, fraud, solicitation, and narrowly defined pandering intact. On this record, the challenged CPPA provisions reached nonobscene virtual images and material marketed in a way that conveyed the impression of child pornography. The Ninth Circuit held the provisions overbroad. Actual-child pornography, obscenity, morphing that uses an identifiable child, solicitation, fraud, and a properly confined pandering offense remain separate.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The position does not protect actual-child pornography, morphing that uses a real child, obscenity, solicitation, fraud, or a narrowly defined offer to provide unlawful material.
