##### Issue 1: Significant contribution, cost, and state implementation choice

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved statutory-authority, administrative-record, notice, and federalism objections. He does not decide technical matters unsupported by the rulemaking record or an unpresented challenge to every aspect of emissions trading.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether EPA may use comparative cost-effectiveness in defining the amount of an upwind State’s significant contribution to downwind nonattainment and may assign a statewide emissions budget while leaving the State to choose the means of compliance.
- **Stone’s Legal Position:** Section 110 first requires EPA to identify a measurable causal contribution from the State to downwind nonattainment. Comparative cost-effectiveness may then help determine the portion of that contribution reasonably required to be eliminated, coordinate regionally effective reductions, and distinguish trivial from significant contributions. Cost cannot itself create the interstate contribution, replace atmospheric or causal analysis, or include a State merely because reductions there are inexpensive. EPA may establish a supported statewide emissions outcome without dictating a State’s implementation plan when the State remains free to select sources, controls, trading arrangements, schedules, and other lawful means, and a federal implementation plan supplies the statutory fallback. Unsupported state-specific modeling, unexplained assumptions, and material information introduced without adequate notice fail ordinary administrative-law review and require remand.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 110 permits EPA to consider comparative cost-effectiveness after identifying a State’s measurable contribution to downwind nonattainment, but cost cannot itself create the contribution Congress required. EPA may assign a record-supported statewide emissions budget while leaving the State free to choose control measures. Unsupported state-specific budgets and materially inadequately noticed assumptions must be remanded.
- **Stone’s Reasoning and Record Application:** The phrase “contribute significantly” combines a causal predicate with an evaluative judgment about significance. Air-quality modeling must establish the interstate linkage before cost enters. Once the linkage exists, a uniform cost threshold can identify reductions that are practical and regionally effective without forcing every State to eliminate its full physical contribution. A statewide budget regulates the emissions outcome, while the State’s retained choice among lawful implementation tools prevents the rule from commandeering state legislative or administrative machinery. The record nevertheless must support inclusion and budget calculation for each State. Where the modeling supports only part of a State, assumptions are unexplained, or affected parties lacked adequate notice of material data, the court cannot repair the record itself. Those components must be vacated and remanded.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not permit cost alone to establish interstate contribution, authorize unsupported statewide extrapolation, compel a State to enact a particular control program, or decide every feature of a regional trading system. EPA may impose a lawful federal implementation plan when the statutory predicates are met and the State does not submit an adequate plan.

- **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the ADA ordinarily require reassignment that conflicts with an established seniority system, and what workplace-specific showing can make the reassignment reasonable?
- **Stone’s Legal Position:** Current simulated law leaves the interaction between reassignment and seniority unresolved. Stone openly adopts a rebuttable presumption rather than treating the result as ordinary application. The employer bears the burden of establishing a genuine, consistently administered seniority system and an actual conflict with the requested reassignment. That showing ordinarily makes the reassignment unreasonable because it would disrupt settled employee expectations. The employee may rebut by proving frequent exceptions, material unilateral employer discretion, inconsistent administration, or other workplace facts showing that the requested exception would not materially impair those expectations. Failure to rebut defeats the requested reassignment; successful rebuttal returns the case to the ordinary accommodation inquiry. The statutory undue-hardship defense remains separately the employer’s burden.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Reassignment to a vacant position may be reasonable, but actual conflict with a genuine seniority system ordinarily defeats facial reasonableness unless the employee proves workplace-specific circumstances materially weakening the system or the expectations it creates.
- **Stone’s Reasoning and Record Application:** The ADA identifies reassignment to a vacant position as a possible accommodation but does not make it automatically controlling over a seniority system. A conclusive seniority bar would protect nominal or routinely disregarded systems, while no presumption would undervalue the expectations that a genuine system creates for other employees. A rebuttable presumption fits the statutory reasonableness inquiry without rewriting the separate undue-hardship defense. Barnett sought a vacant mailroom position, US Airways invoked its seniority system, and the Ninth Circuit gave that system no presumptive force. Vacatur and remand are required so the parties can litigate whether the system was genuine and consistently administered and whether workplace-specific circumstances rebut the presumption.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Seniority is not conclusive; a nominal or inconsistently administered system receives no automatic protection, and the ADA does not require creation of a vacancy or displacement of an incumbent.

#### B. Stone’s approved position

- **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the categorical immunity judgment and remand.
- **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The lower court shall determine the Port Authority’s legal and fiscal status, the statutory source and scope of prospective compliance authority, and the independently available route for any retrospective compensation. ##### Issue 1: Granted merits question

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The granted merits question is preserved and was passed upon below. No independent jurisdictional or vehicle defect appears in the supplied materials. The Court reaches the question no more broadly than the record and the stated limit permit.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Does state sovereign immunity categorically bar an FMC adjudication initiated by a private complainant alleging that a state port authority violated federal shipping law?
- **Stone’s Legal Position:** Stone does not begin with the dignity-laden abstraction whether a sovereign may be “haled before an agency.” He identifies the federal Shipping Act obligation, what Congress validly authorized the Commission to decide, the particular relief requested, and the Port Authority's legal and fiscal character. If Congress acted within its enumerated power and unmistakably authorized prospective compliance adjudication against a state port participating in interstate shipping, the State cannot defeat that federal obligation merely because a private party supplied the complaint. Retrospective compensation remains separate: the lower tribunal must determine whether the Port is a qualifying commercial state enterprise and, if not, apply remedial autonomy and the federal fallback. Private initiation is therefore relevant to procedure, but it neither creates liability nor selects a remedy.
- **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A private party's initiation of an FMC complaint does not by itself convert the proceeding into a forbidden private damages action or establish liability. Where Congress validly and unmistakably authorized federal prospective regulation, the Commission may determine a state port's compliance, while retrospective compensation must be analyzed separately under the port's legal and fiscal status, the commercial-state-enterprise rule, remedial autonomy, and the federal fallback. The categorical dismissal is vacated and the matter remanded.
