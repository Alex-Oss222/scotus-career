## Clinton v. Goldsmith

- **October Term:** 1998
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued; merits decision pending.

- **Question Granted:** Whether the Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces may use the All Writs Act to enjoin the President and military officials from administratively dropping a servicemember from the rolls when that action is not part of the court-martial judgment under review.
- **Legal Field:** Military justice, jurisdiction and the All Writs Act

James Daniel Good Real Property separates forfeiture authority from the process required to seize property and requires particularized justification for dispensing with prior judicial process. - **Other Authority Used:** Carroll, Chambers, and Coolidge distinguish evidence searches based on mobility from planned seizures where officers have time to obtain process.

#### B. Stone's approved position

#### Issue 1: Planned warrantless forfeiture seizure of a parked automobile

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone rejects conversion of automobile mobility into a standing license for a planned civil forfeiture. Probable cause establishes the basis for a warrant; it does not by itself excuse a neutral judicial determination when no immediate mobility or evidence-loss risk is shown.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Police may not make a planned warrantless seizure of a parked automobile for civil forfeiture solely because probable cause exists and the vehicle is inherently mobile; absent consent or another exception, the Government must obtain a warrant when officers had a reasonable opportunity to do so and no concrete exigency existed.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** More than two months after the last observed drug transaction, officers arrested White at his workplace on unrelated charges and seized the car without a warrant from his employer’s parking lot because they regarded it as forfeitable. The State identified no contemporaneous evidentiary-search rationale or exigency requiring immediate seizure rather than prior judicial process. A warrant therefore was practicable on Stone’s view, and the later inventory search cannot validate the antecedent seizure.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require a warrant for an exigent roadside seizure, a contemporaneous evidence search supported by the automobile exception, contraband whose possession itself is unlawful, or a vehicle secured briefly while a warrant is diligently sought.
