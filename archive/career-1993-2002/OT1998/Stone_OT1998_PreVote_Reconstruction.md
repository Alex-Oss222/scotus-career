# Justice Stone — October Term 1998 Pre-Vote Reconstruction

This report reconstructs Stone’s position before the vote in every merits case and petition-stage matter contained in the supplied 1998 material. The old simulation is used only as evidence of Stone’s earlier objective and of what the old simulated Court did; it is never treated as law, a forecast, or a source of precedent. For each matter, the simulated precedent set is exactly the precedent supplied for that matter, and no reconstructed position elsewhere in this report is treated as a holding.

## Confirmed Step 0: jurisprudence ladder

1.  **Thresholds, source, question, and posture first.** Identify the legal source, the exact question taken, jurisdiction, standing, preservation, finality, the governing burden and standard of review, and the remedy actually requested before reaching broader merits.

2.  **Use a narrow adequate nonconstitutional or ordinary dispositional ground before a broader merits rule.** If a source-bound threshold, statutory construction, native procedural rule, or ordinary disposition fully resolves the case, Stone uses it rather than deciding a wider constitutional proposition.

3.  **Reserve unnecessary issues and decide only necessary antecedents.** Stone assumes or expressly leaves open a proposition that does no work in the judgment. He decides an antecedent only when the selected route genuinely requires it.

4.  **Read enacted text before invoking purpose.** Text, definitions, conventional grammar, legal terms, and statutory context control before general purpose. Purpose helps resolve a real textual uncertainty; it may not insert an omitted element, exception, remedy, or prohibition.

5.  **Apply received doctrine unchanged when it can honestly decide the case.** This includes ordinary application, clarification, the use of an already available scrutiny level, and faithful administration of existing burdens and review standards.

6.  **Prefer a native record, proof, evidentiary, review, remand, or remedy-specific correction to doctrinal change.** A missing showing is resolved through the burden the governing source already assigns; a deficient or unclear record ordinarily produces the narrow disposition or remand that source permits, not a new merits rule.

7.  **Distinguish only when the difference is real; do not disguise revision as distinction.** The jurisprudence supplies no universal ranking of distinguishing, narrowing, and overruling. A genuine source- or fact-based distinction is used when available; if the rule itself is defective, Stone identifies whether the accurate move is narrowing, modification, or overruling and proceeds only through rung 8.

8.  **Depart from controlling simulated precedent only candidly and on a demanding showing.** The issue must be squarely presented, and Stone must identify a source conflict, internal contradiction, unworkability, collapse of a premise, or systematic defeat of the protected right or enacted command. Preference, changed personnel, a better policy, or disagreement about application is insufficient; any change in scrutiny, burden, review standard, or governing rule is named as such.

9.  **Choose remedy separately and last.** Relief must come from an authorized channel, address the proved violation, respect defendant, forum, immunity, causation, and equitable limits, and extend no further than necessary. Practical need cannot create remedial authority.

**Run-wide tiebreak.** Where the jurisprudence does not settle a conflict among otherwise lawful routes, Stone uses the route that alters the fewest legal propositions and decides the fewest unnecessary issues while still fully resolving the case. Any case-specific departure from this ladder is identified rather than silently absorbed.

## Confirmed domain refinements

- **Bodily autonomy and abortion:** Stone improves protection incrementally through the issues and record presented, fully using existing access, practical-operation, medical-emergency, delay, cost, and provider-availability rules before proposing change. If a received test no longer protects the liberty it purports to protect, any update must satisfy rungs 8 and 9.

- **Qualified immunity:** An ordinary reasonable mistake may remain protected. An obvious violation, action outside lawful authority, knowing illegality, intentional corruption of the record, reliance on a defect the officer created, or conduct whose complete merits standard already establishes objective unreasonableness receives no second reasonableness escape. A materially identical case is unnecessary when unlawfulness is obvious, subject always to rung 8 where simulated precedent conflicts.

- **Church–state separation through third parties:** This is an especially high commitment, and practical incidence controls. Public financing of worship, devotional instruction, clergy formation, or inseparably sectarian education does not become private merely because value passes through parents, vouchers, credits, intermediaries, or provider redemption. Genuine neutral forum access, direct severable public services, and unrestricted general cash benefits remain distinct.

- **Criminal and evidentiary cases:** Stone has no freestanding preference for a conservative outcome. Enacted elements, definitions, grammar, context, precedent, evidentiary rules, burdens, and institutional allocation control. His orientation is ordinarily defendant-protective where those sources permit it: he inserts no omitted element, relaxes no constitutional protection, and applies lenity and fair notice when the established tools leave genuine criminal ambiguity.

# Merits cases

## 1. *Marquez v. Screen Actors Guild, Inc.*

**Case** *Marquez v. Screen Actors Guild, Inc.*, decided November 3, 1998.

**Question presented.** (1) Does a union breach its duty of fair representation by using § 8(a)(3)’s statutory language in a union-security clause without restating the qualifications recognized in *General Motors* and *Beck*? (2) May a statutory grace-period challenge proceed initially in federal court merely because it is pleaded as a fair-representation claim? Stone would not reach the separate enforcement and notice claims already remanded.

**What the case is about.** Marquez attacked SAG’s union-security clause on its face because it tracked § 8(a)(3) without reproducing later judicial qualifications and alleged that its grace period violated the NLRA. The Ninth Circuit rejected those two theories while remanding distinct allegations concerning enforcement and notice; those remanded allegations are not part of the judgment now under review.

**Precedent applied.** None. No supplied simulated holding resolves the clause-drafting or primary-jurisdiction question.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed affirmance on both issues: statutory terms were permissible drafting shorthand, and an NLRA-only theory belonged initially to the NLRB unless facts independently established arbitrary, discriminatory, or bad-faith representation. The old Court adopted both propositions in substance, but Stone caused nothing because the same grounds independently entered through another Justice.

**Stone innovation.** None. This is ordinary application of the *Vaca–O’Neill* fair-representation rule and *Garmon* primary jurisdiction, with issue reservation.

**Stone's position now.** Affirm on the two questions presented and preserve the Ninth Circuit’s remand of the distinct notice and enforcement claims. Rung 5 controls: tracking an enacted statutory term with an established legal meaning is not by itself arbitrary, discriminatory, or bad faith under *Vaca* and *O’Neill*, and the substance of the grace-period allegations presents only an NLRA question assigned in the first instance to the Board under *Garmon*. Stone distinguishes *Beck*, where operative collection and representation conduct supplied more than a facial drafting objection, and does not turn a CBA into a restatement of the entire governing code.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply existing fair-representation and primary-jurisdiction doctrine; distinguish *Beck*; reserve the separately remanded claims.

**Proposed holding.** A union does not breach its duty of fair representation merely by using § 8(a)(3)’s statutory terms without restating every judicial qualification. A claim alleging only an NLRA violation remains initially for the NLRB unless its operative facts independently show arbitrary, discriminatory, or bad-faith representation.

**Limit.** The position does not approve misleading notice, collection of nonchargeable fees, compelled formal membership, unlawful enforcement, or any grace-period practice the Board finds inconsistent with the NLRA.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No; the old affirmance remains an ordinary application of the controlling real-law doctrines and preserves the same unresolved claims.

## 2. *Pfaff v. Wells Electronics, Inc.*

**Case** *Pfaff v. Wells Electronics, Inc.*, decided November 10, 1998.

**Question presented.** When is an invention “on sale” under the governing version of 35 U.S.C. § 102(b) when a commercial offer occurs before actual reduction to practice?

**What the case is about.** Before the critical date, Texas Instruments placed a commercial order for more than 30,000 sockets based on Pfaff’s detailed drawings; no physical prototype yet existed, but working sockets were later manufactured from those drawings. The Federal Circuit held the asserted patent claims invalid under the on-sale bar, and the legal sufficiency of the precritical-date offer and technical readiness is before the Court.

**Precedent applied.** None. No supplied simulated holding fixes § 102(b)’s on-sale trigger.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed affirmance when a precritical-date commercial offer concerns an invention already ready for patenting, shown either by reduction to practice or an enabling description. The old Court adopted the position in substance, but Stone caused nothing because the same formulation independently commanded full support.

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary judicial move: interpret § 102(b) and clarify that actual construction is sufficient but not indispensable. No distinctive Stone doctrinal overlay.

**Stone's position now.** Affirm. Rung 4 controls: the enacted phrase “on sale,” the patent scheme, and the settled boundary between completing an invention and commercially exploiting it require both a genuine commercial offer before the critical date and an invention sufficiently definite to be patented; enabling drawings can establish that readiness without a prototype. The accepted mass order and manufacturable drawings satisfy those predicates on this record, while Stone leaves experimental use and noncommercial technical development to their existing doctrines.

**Doctrinal intent.** Interpret § 102(b); clarify the readiness-for-patenting boundary; apply it to the undisputed commercial and technical facts.

**Proposed holding.** An invention is on sale before the critical date when it is the subject of a commercial offer and is ready for patenting, shown by reduction to practice or an enabling drawing or description. Pfaff’s accepted order and enabling drawings satisfy that rule.

**Limit.** The position does not reach incomplete conceptions, preliminary research, noncommercial negotiations, nonenabling descriptions, or a properly presented experimental-use question.

**Novelty.** Statutory or constitutional interpretation.

**Changed from old sim.** No; the old disposition remains the narrow statutory clarification needed to resolve the record before the Court.

## 3. *Wright v. Universal Maritime Service Corp.*

**Case** *Wright v. Universal Maritime Service Corp.*, decided November 16, 1998.

**Question presented.** Does a general collective-bargaining agreement arbitration clause clearly and unmistakably waive an employee’s right to litigate an independent federal statutory discrimination claim?

**What the case is about.** Wright brought an ADA claim, and the Fourth Circuit affirmed dismissal on the ground that a general CBA grievance-and-arbitration clause required arbitration. The agreement did not identify the ADA or federal discrimination claims; because that language never clearly made the asserted waiver, the broader enforceability of an explicit union-negotiated waiver is not necessary to decide.

**Precedent applied.** None. No supplied simulated holding resolves a union-negotiated waiver of the judicial forum for an independent statutory claim.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed vacatur and remand because the CBA contained no clear and unmistakable statutory-forum waiver, while preserving contractual arbitration and reserving whether an explicit waiver would be enforceable. The old Court independently adopted that narrow contractual ground and reservation, so Stone caused nothing.

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary judicial move: extend the received clear-and-unmistakable waiver principle to the CBA’s asserted surrender of an independent statutory forum, then reserve the validity of language not present here.

**Stone's position now.** Vacate and remand so Wright’s ADA action may proceed subject to other preserved defenses. Rung 3 chooses contract coverage over the unnecessary validity question: *Gardner-Denver* distinguishes contractual from independent statutory rights, *Metropolitan Edison* requires clarity before a CBA waives protected employee rights, and *Gilmer* is materially different because it involved an individual arbitration agreement. A general workplace grievance clause neither identifies the ADA nor unmistakably transfers that cause of action, so Stone decides nonwaiver and reserves whether a materially explicit provision could lawfully do so.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply and extend the clear-and-unmistakable waiver requirement; distinguish *Gilmer*; interpret the CBA; reserve enforceability of an explicit waiver; vacate and remand.

**Proposed holding.** A CBA does not waive a judicial forum for an independent federal statutory discrimination claim unless the waiver is clear and unmistakable. This general arbitration clause contains no such waiver.

**Limit.** The position does not restrict arbitration of contract-based grievances, decide individually negotiated agreements, or resolve whether a materially explicit CBA waiver of a statutory judicial forum would be enforceable.

**Novelty.** Extension of existing doctrine; issue reservation or minimalist disposition secondary.

**Changed from old sim.** No; the old nonwaiver disposition and reservation remain the narrowest adequate lawful ground.

## 4. *Minnesota v. Carter*

**Case**

*Minnesota v. Carter*, 525 U.S. 83 (decided December 1, 1998).

**Question presented.**

May Carter and Johns invoke the Fourth Amendment as short-term, invited users of Thompson’s apartment, and did the officer conduct a search by watching activity inside through a gap in drawn blinds from the common grassy area? Stone would not reach the guest-status question because the observation issue independently disposes of the suppression judgment.

**What the case is about.**

Thompson admitted Carter and Johns to her apartment, where for about two and one-half hours they packaged cocaine. An officer, acting on a passerby’s report, watched through a gap in the blinds for roughly twelve to fifteen minutes from a grassy area the supplied record describes as publicly accessible; the ordinary passerby had seen the same activity from outside. The Minnesota Supreme Court suppressed the resulting evidence in the two prosecutions, and Minnesota seeks review.

**Precedent applied.**

Simulated *United States v. Fields* (together with its treatment of *United States v. Pinson*) controls the observation issue: deliberate acquisition of otherwise unavailable home-interior information is a search when officers use technology or enter a fenced, nonpublic vantage, while ordinary unaided observation from a lawful public vantage remains unaffected.

**Old sim.**

Stone proposed affirmance, reasoning both that host-authorized private residential use gave these guests a personal privacy interest and that prolonged viewing through drawn blinds was a search. The old Court independently accepted the guest-interest proposition but rejected Stone’s search characterization and reversed suppression; Stone caused no recorded change.

**Stone innovation.**

None. The available move is ordinary application of simulated *Fields*, with an issue reservation; the old simulation’s duration-and-blinds distinction cannot be preserved as application without adding a new rule.

**Stone's position now.**

Reverse and remand on the no-search ground, assuming without deciding that respondents had a personal privacy interest. *Fields* makes lawful vantage and otherwise unavailable information decisive, and this record supplies a public grassy area, unaided sight, and an ordinary passerby who obtained the same view. Although sustained peering through drawn blinds could in theory be distinguished from a casual glance, making duration, proximity, or a partial barrier dispositive here would create an unsupported duration/barrier rule beyond *Fields*; the controlling-law and narrow-disposition rungs therefore require application of *Fields* and reservation of guest privacy.

**Doctrinal intent.**

Apply simulated *Fields*; reject an unsupported duration/barrier extension; reserve the invited-guest privacy question.

**Proposed holding.**

Unaided observation of what is visible from a lawful public vantage is not a Fourth Amendment search on this record. The suppression judgments are reversed, without deciding whether respondents otherwise possessed a legitimate expectation of privacy in the apartment.

**Limit.**

The position does not authorize entry onto fenced or nonpublic property, sense-enhancing technology, physical intrusion, or acquisition of information unavailable to ordinary public observation, and it does not decide the Fourth Amendment status of short-term invited guests.

**Novelty.**

Application disagreement only; secondarily, issue reservation or minimalist disposition.

**Changed from old sim.**

Yes. Simulated *Fields* preserves ordinary observation from a lawful public vantage, and the supplied record does not support Stone’s old result without inventing a new duration/barrier rule.

## 5. *Knowles v. Iowa*

**Case**

*Knowles v. Iowa*, 525 U.S. 113 (decided December 8, 1998).

**Question presented.**

May an officer conduct a full search of a driver and vehicle incident to issuing a traffic citation when no custodial arrest occurs and no other search exception is established?

**What the case is about.**

An Iowa officer stopped Knowles for speeding, issued a citation rather than arresting him, and then performed the full search Iowa law allowed incident to a citation, finding marijuana and a pipe. The Iowa courts upheld the search and conviction by treating citation authority as equivalent to arrest authority; the case arrives on review of that judgment.

**Precedent applied.**

None. No supplied simulated precedent authorizes a citation-only evidentiary search; the real controlling authorities were *Chimel v. California*, *United States v. Robinson*, and *Terry v. Ohio*.

**Old sim.**

Stone proposed reversal because categorical search-incident authority requires an actual custodial arrest, while preserving particularized protective searches and other exceptions. The old Court adopted that position independently and unanimously; Stone caused no recorded change.

**Stone innovation.**

None. This is ordinary application and clarification of the search-incident-to-arrest trigger.

**Stone's position now.**

Reverse and remand. *Chimel* and *Robinson* tie the categorical search to the safety and evidence risks created by actual custody; an unexercised power to arrest cannot do the legal work of an arrest, while *Terry* remains available on particularized safety facts. The ladder’s apply-received-law rung resolves the case without a new exception or test.

**Doctrinal intent.**

Apply and clarify the existing search-incident-to-arrest rule.

**Proposed holding.**

Issuing a traffic citation without a custodial arrest does not authorize a full search incident to arrest. The judgment is reversed and the case remanded.

**Limit.**

The position leaves intact searches incident to lawful custodial arrest, consent, probable-cause automobile searches, and *Terry* measures supported by particularized safety suspicion.

**Novelty.**

No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.**

No. The old disposition and actual-custody boundary remain consistent with controlling law and Stone’s source-bound Fourth Amendment method.

## 6. *Haddle v. Garrison*

**Case**

*Haddle v. Garrison*, 525 U.S. 121 (decided December 14, 1998).

**Question presented.**

Does retaliatory termination of an at-will employee for participating in federal proceedings constitute injury to “person or property” under the first clause of 42 U.S.C. § 1985(2)?

**What the case is about.**

Haddle alleged that Garrison and others conspired to have him discharged from at-will employment because he obeyed a federal grand-jury subpoena and was expected to testify. The District Court dismissed and the Eleventh Circuit affirmed because at-will employment was not constitutionally protected property; the sufficiency of the remaining conspiracy, retaliation, causation, and damages allegations was not finally adjudicated.

**Precedent applied.**

None. No supplied simulated decision construes § 1985(2)’s “injured in his person or property” language.

**Old sim.**

Stone proposed reversal, separating traditional compensable injury from procedural-due-process entitlement doctrine. The old Court adopted that statutory distinction independently; Stone caused no recorded change.

**Stone innovation.**

None. The position uses ordinary textual, contextual, and tort-law interpretation and corrects the lower court’s cross-doctrinal conversion.

**Stone's position now.**

Reverse and remand. Section 1985(2) asks whether the retaliatory conspiracy injured person or property, and the loss of an existing at-will job is a conventional economic injury even though the employee lacks a constitutional entitlement to indefinite continuation. The text-and-source-separation rungs foreclose importing Fourteenth Amendment property doctrine into this statutory injury element.

**Doctrinal intent.**

Interpret statutory text and distinguish constitutional property-entitlement doctrine.

**Proposed holding.**

Termination of at-will employment in retaliation for federal testimony may constitute injury to person or property under the first clause of § 1985(2). The dismissal is reversed and the case remanded.

**Limit.**

The position does not establish conspiracy, retaliatory purpose, causation, truth of the allegations, damages, or the availability of any defense or immunity.

**Novelty.**

Statutory or constitutional interpretation; secondarily, no meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.**

No. The old statutory result follows directly from enacted language, context, and source separation.

## 7. *NYNEX Corp. v. Discon, Inc.*

**Case**

*NYNEX Corp. v. Discon, Inc.*, 525 U.S. 128 (decided December 14, 1998).

**Question presented.**

Does the per se group-boycott rule apply to a single buyer’s substitution of one supplier for another without horizontal concerted exclusion, and what consequence does rejection of that classification have for the derivative Sherman Act § 2 ruling?

**What the case is about.**

Discon alleged that NYNEX-related entities stopped buying removal services from it and favored AT&T Technologies as part of fraud against regulators and customers. The Second Circuit revived a per se § 1 boycott theory and a related § 2 ruling; the case arrives on a limited grant concerning that per se classification, not final adjudication of every competitive-injury or pleading issue.

**Precedent applied.**

None. No supplied simulated antitrust holding changes the received distinction between historically established per se categories and ordinary rule-of-reason analysis.

**Old sim.**

Stone proposed vacatur and remand: reject per se boycott treatment, vacate the derivative § 2 ruling, and leave adequately pleaded ordinary theories for proceedings below. The old Court adopted that approach independently; Stone caused no recorded change.

**Stone innovation.**

None. This is ordinary classification under settled antitrust categories plus a limited-grant reservation.

**Stone's position now.**

Vacate and remand. *Klor’s*, *Indiana Federation of Dentists*, and *Business Electronics* confine per se condemnation to categories whose coordinated exclusionary structure and predictable anticompetitive operation are established; a single buyer’s supplier choice does not enter that category merely because fraud or favoritism allegedly motivated it. The narrow-disposition rung also requires leaving any adequately pleaded rule-of-reason or monopolization theory to the lower courts.

**Doctrinal intent.**

Apply and clarify the boundary of the per se group-boycott category; reserve out-of-grant claims.

**Proposed holding.**

A single buyer’s substitution of one supplier for another, without horizontal concerted exclusion, is not a per se group boycott under Sherman Act § 1. The contrary § 1 classification and derivative § 2 ruling are vacated, with other adequately preserved theories left for remand.

**Limit.**

The position does not immunize horizontal boycotts, purchaser cartels, monopolistic conduct, fraud, or conduct proved unreasonable under ordinary antitrust analysis, and it does not finally assess the entire complaint.

**Novelty.**

No meaningful Stone novelty; secondarily, issue reservation or minimalist disposition.

**Changed from old sim.**

No. The old limited vacatur follows settled per se-category discipline and the scope of the grant.

## 8. *El Al Israel Airlines, Ltd. v. Tsui Yuan Tseng*

**Case** *El Al Israel Airlines, Ltd. v. Tsui Yuan Tseng*, decided January 12, 1999.

**Question presented.** May local law provide personal-injury damages for an occurrence within the Warsaw Convention’s temporal and substantive field when the claimant cannot satisfy Article 17’s conditions for recovery?

**What the case is about.** El Al searched Tseng before her international flight during embarking operations. She sought New York personal-injury damages but alleged no qualifying bodily injury; the Second Circuit permitted the local-law claim despite the absence of Convention recovery. The separate baggage judgment is outside the grant.

**Precedent applied.** None. No supplied simulated holding resolves Convention exclusivity.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed reversal, Article 24 exclusivity within Article 17’s field, dismissal of the New York personal-injury claim, and reservation of the baggage judgment, occurrences outside the Convention, and treaty-preserved willful-misconduct issues. The old Court adopted that position in substance, but Stone caused nothing because the same ground independently entered through another Justice.

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary judicial move: treaty interpretation. No distinctive Stone doctrinal innovation.

**Stone's position now.** Reverse as to the personal-injury claim and reinstate its dismissal. Rung 4, governing text before purpose, resolves the available routes: Article 24’s reference to actions “however founded,” the allocation of personal-injury claims to Article 17, and the Convention’s structure make Article 17’s conditions limits on recovery rather than invitations to replace failed Convention claims with local tort law. *Saks* supplies the shared-expectations method, *Floyd* supplies the existing no-bodily-injury boundary, and *Zicherman* is distinguished because its use of local law to measure rights after Convention liability does not authorize local law to create liability when Article 17 is unsatisfied; uniformity confirms this reading but does not override the text. Stone does not revisit whether the search was an Article 17 accident or decide Article 25.

**Doctrinal intent.** Interpret treaty text and structure; distinguish *Zicherman*; apply *Saks* and *Floyd*; reserve adjacent treaty issues.

**Proposed holding.** When a passenger-injury occurrence falls within Article 17’s temporal and substantive field, Article 24 permits an action, however founded, only subject to the Convention’s conditions and limits. Local law cannot supply recovery solely because the claimant fails one of those conditions.

**Limit.** The position does not govern events outside embarking, carriage, or disembarking; baggage matters outside the grant; or the operation of Article 25 and other treaty-preserved consequences.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No; the old disposition and limits survive the controlling law without doctrinal change.

## 9. *Buckley v. American Constitutional Law Foundation, Inc.*

**Case** *Buckley v. American Constitutional Law Foundation, Inc.*, decided January 12, 1999.

**Question presented.** May Colorado require initiative-petition circulators to be registered voters, require their personal names on badges during solicitation, and compel public final and monthly reporting of each paid circulator’s identity, address, and individual compensation? Stone reaches all three questions.

**What the case is about.** The Tenth Circuit invalidated those three parts of Colorado’s initiative system. The supplied record shows that registration excluded a substantial class of otherwise eligible speakers, personal-name badges deterred circulation and exposed circulators during contentious solicitation, and Colorado retained affidavits, sponsor identification, aggregate expenditure and payment-method disclosures, subpoena authority, and fraud sanctions. The age rule, circulation period, affidavit, residence question, paid-or-volunteer badge notation, sponsor identification, and unchallenged aggregate disclosures are outside the judgment.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission*. It prescribes exacting review, requiring an overriding interest and narrow fit for a content-defined identification mandate imposed directly on independent political advocacy; it does not prescribe the strict-scrutiny rule stated in the old export.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed affirmance on all three provisions, with residence-linked enforcement tools, affidavits, fraud sanctions, sponsor disclosure, aggregate reporting, and narrower evidence-supported rules preserved. The old Court adopted all three results and limits in substance, but Stone caused nothing because equivalent grounds independently supported them.

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary judicial moves. Apply *Meyer* to registration and *Buckley v. Valeo* to reporting; narrowly extend simulated *McIntyre*’s existing exacting-review rule from personal attribution on political literature to personal-name attribution during equivalent live ballot advocacy. No new test, burden, or scrutiny level.

**Stone's position now.** Affirm the three invalidations. Rung 5, using received doctrine before changing it, controls: the registration rule produces *Meyer*’s speaker-reduction injury while retained affidavits and residence-linked process address enforcement; the badge imposes *McIntyre*’s compelled identification at the moment of natural-person political advocacy and fails that rule’s narrow-fit requirement; and the individual reports fail *Buckley v. Valeo* exacting scrutiny on this record because sponsor identity and aggregate spending disclose who finances the initiative while Colorado showed no substantial incremental relation between its interests and public disclosure of every circulator’s address and earnings. Stone rejects a categorical immunity from election disclosure and reserves the untouched parts of the regulatory system.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply *Meyer*; extend simulated *McIntyre* without changing its exacting-review standard; apply *Buckley v. Valeo*; reserve provisions outside the judgment.

**Proposed holding.** Colorado may not exclude otherwise qualified resident-adult circulators solely because they are unregistered when retained safeguards address identification and amenability to process. It may not compel a circulator’s personal name during solicitation where post-circulation identification serves enforcement with materially less burden. Sponsor and aggregate spending disclosure remain permissible, but the challenged individual reports fail because the record does not substantially connect each circulator’s public address and earnings to an additional governmental need.

**Limit.** The position does not decide a residence requirement, the age rule, circulation period, affidavits, fraud or signature-verification rules, paid-or-volunteer badge notation, sponsor attribution, aggregate expenditures, or a narrower individual-reporting rule supported under the governing scrutiny.

**Novelty.** Extension of existing doctrine for the badge component; application disagreement only for registration and individual reporting.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes as to scrutiny and rationale, not disposition. Controlling simulated *McIntyre* requires exacting review, so Stone cannot preserve the old export’s strict-scrutiny characterization even though all three invalidations survive.

## 10. *City of West Covina v. Perkins*

**Case** *City of West Covina v. Perkins*, decided January 13, 1999.

**Question presented.** Once owners receive notice identifying seized property and the responsible agency, must the government also give them detailed instructions for using published state-law procedures to obtain its return?

**What the case is about.** City officers lawfully seized respondents’ personal property and left an itemized notice identifying the search, responsible police agency, issuing court, and contacts. The District Court entered summary judgment for the City, but the Ninth Circuit required detailed notice of California’s return procedures and the information needed to invoke them. The adequacy and practical accessibility of those remedies are outside the question presented; the supplied material does not independently develop that issue.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property*, confronted and distinguished. Its holding requires pre-seizure process for civil forfeiture of real property absent proved exigency and expressly leaves readily movable personal property unresolved; it does not itself establish the post-seizure legal-instruction rule attributed to it in the old export.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed reversal and remand, holding that notice of the identified seizure and custodian sufficed without personalized instruction in accessible published law, while preserving challenges involving an inadequate or inaccessible remedy, concealment, misleading instructions, obstruction, or unreasonable retention. The old Court adopted the position in substance, but Stone had no effect because the same ground independently entered elsewhere.

**Stone innovation.** None. This is ordinary application of existing notice doctrine, an honest distinction of *Good*, and issue reservation.

**Stone's position now.** Reverse and remand with directions to reject the federal individualized-instruction theory. Rung 5, application of received doctrine unchanged, resolves the case: *Mullane* requires notice of the governmental deprivation sufficient to permit response, while *Memphis Light* requires procedural instructions when the operative route is unpublished or otherwise inaccessible, not when state statutes and cases publicly prescribe it. On the presented record, an additional personalized legal guide adds little protection against error after the owner knows what was seized and who holds it; simulated *Good* is materially different because it addressed whether process must precede governmental occupation of real property. Stone reserves the adequacy and actual availability of California’s procedure rather than converting that unpresented issue into a finding.

## 71. *Cable News Network, Inc. v. Berger*, No. 97-1914

**Case** *Cable News Network, Inc. v. Berger*, petition considered June 1, 1999.

**Question presented.** The petition asks whether CNN’s written cooperation with and presence alongside federal officers during a warrant execution made the media defendants federal actors subject to constitutional damages liability and whether a *Bivens*-type remedy may run against them. Stone would not reach either merits question.

**What the case is about.** The Ninth Circuit interlocutorily reversed summary judgment for CNN and other media defendants on the constitutional joint-action theory and material state-law claims, then remanded. Factual questions about joint participation and causation, state tort claims, and the federal remedial channel remain unresolved. Real *Wilson v. Layne* announced the officer-side Fourth Amendment rule seven days earlier, and real *Hanlon v. Berger* applied it to the federal agents and remanded, but neither decided private-media attribution or a cause of action against the press.

**Precedent applied.** *FDIC v. Meyer* is confronted but not extended: its supplied simulated holding refuses a *Bivens* remedy against a federal agency and confirms that a constitutional duty, proper defendant, and damages cause of action remain distinct. It does not answer private-media liability.

**Old sim.** Stone voted to deny because the private-action and implied-remedy questions remained embedded in an interlocutory record. The old Court denied certiorari; Stone caused no rule on state action, *Bivens*, recording, publication, or trespass.

**Stone innovation.** None. This is petition-stage reservation based on interlocutory posture and an undeveloped remedial question.

**Stone's position now.** Deny certiorari. The relation between press activity and governmental search power is serious, but attribution and remedy are separate, fact-sensitive questions, and the remand may narrow or eliminate them after the real *Wilson–Hanlon* officer-side dispositions. Rungs 1 and 3 control. Stone’s opposition to unrelated media entry does not authorize him to imply a damages action or decide private state action prematurely.

**Doctrinal intent.** Deny interlocutory review; distinguish the *Wilson–Hanlon* officer-side dispositions; reserve attribution, cause of action, causation, and state-law liability.

**Proposed holding.** Deny the petition for a writ of certiorari. The denial creates no merits rule concerning media state action or an implied constitutional damages remedy against private journalists.

**Limit.** The position does not immunize CNN, approve joint media-police entry, foreclose a properly authorized remedy, or decide the remaining federal or state claims.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No. Interlocutory posture and unresolved attribution and remedial predicates still require denial without a merits view.
