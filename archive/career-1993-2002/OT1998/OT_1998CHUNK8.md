- **October Term:** 1998
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued; merits decision pending.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 23, 1999
- **Question Presented:** Whether Congress validly subjected nonconsenting States to private patent-infringement damages actions through the Patent and Plant Variety Protection Remedy Clarification Act.
- **Question Granted:** Whether Congress validly subjected nonconsenting States to private patent-infringement damages actions through the Patent and Plant Variety Protection Remedy Clarification Act.
- **Legal Field:** Federalism, patent law, and state sovereign immunity
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Federal Circuit affirmed denial of Florida Prepaid’s sovereign-immunity defense and allowed College Savings Bank’s patent action to proceed.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Reverse and dismiss the damages action as barred by state sovereign immunity.
- **Material Facts and Record:** College Savings alleged that Florida Prepaid, a state entity marketing prepaid tuition plans, infringed its patent for an investment method. Congress had amended the Patent Act to define States as infringers, expressly remove immunity, and make the ordinary patent remedies available against them.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The statutory clarity is undisputed. The Court must identify the source of congressional abrogation before reaching patent liability. The complaint’s patent merits and damages remain for the lower courts.

- **Historical Holding:** The historical Court held that Article I could not abrogate immunity and that the Patent Remedy Act was not valid § 5 legislation under its congruence-and-proportionality analysis.

#### Issue 1: Claim-specific Fourteenth Amendment abrogation for patent infringement

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Simulated *City of Boerne v. Flores* requires claim-specific § 5 analysis. When the conduct supporting the statutory claim independently violates a Fourteenth Amendment guarantee, the statute is valid enforcement as applied and the remedy Congress supplied attaches to that claim. When the constitutional violation includes culpability or remedial inadequacy, the plaintiff must plead and prove those predicates.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Claim-specific enforcement under Fourteenth Amendment § 5.
- **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause and § 5; *Fitzpatrick v. Bitzer*; Patent Remedy Act; Patent Act.
- **Other Authority Used:** *Parratt v. Taylor*, *Hudson v. Palmer*, and *Zinermon v. Burch* make culpability and the availability of constitutionally adequate state process or relief material to a property-deprivation claim.
- **Open Point:** Whether College Savings can prove intentional or reckless infringement and the absence or inadequacy of Florida remedies for the resulting deprivation.

#### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Federal Circuit's judgment and remand for claim-specific proceedings.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The Federal Circuit shall remand to permit College Savings to plead and prove that Florida Prepaid intentionally or recklessly infringed the patent and that Florida supplied no constitutionally adequate process or remedy. If College Savings proves both predicates together with the statutory patent claim, the federal remedy Congress supplied may proceed against Florida Prepaid. If either constitutional predicate fails, the retrospective direct-State claim must be dismissed. Patent validity, infringement, causation, damages, and remedial adequacy remain for lawful proceedings below.

#### Issue 1: Claim-specific Fourteenth Amendment abrogation for patent infringement

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the validity of abrogation as applied to the willful-infringement claim. The complaint alleges willfulness, but the constitutional culpability and remedial-adequacy predicates have not been finally adjudicated.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Blanket immunity and blanket abrogation both disregard simulated *Boerne*'s claim-specific rule. Patent infringement that is merely innocent or negligent, or that is accompanied by an adequate state remedy, does not itself establish a Due Process violation. Intentional or reckless infringement that deprives the owner of patent property without adequate state process or relief does. Congress unmistakably supplied the federal remedy, and valid § 5 enforcement permits that remedy when both constitutional predicates are proved.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Patent Remedy Act validly abrogates state sovereign immunity as applied to a claim proving intentional or reckless patent infringement and the absence or inadequacy of constitutionally sufficient state remedies. A plaintiff alleging such conduct must receive an opportunity to prove those predicates.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** College Savings alleges willful infringement, which is sufficient to prevent the Court from treating this as an innocent-conduct case at the threshold. The present record does not establish whether Florida offered an adequate remedy, so neither categorical dismissal nor categorical enforcement is justified. Simulated *Boerne* directs a claim-by-claim inquiry, and *Fitzpatrick* permits Congress to subject a State to the remedy validly enacted under § 5 for independently unconstitutional conduct.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not authorize federal recovery for innocent or negligent infringement, for conduct accompanied by constitutionally adequate state relief, or on proof of the Patent Act alone. It decides no patent merits, culpability finding, remedial-adequacy finding, damages amount, or equitable entitlement.

## College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board

### Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board
- **Citation or Docket:** 527 U.S. 666; No. 98-149; 131 F.3d 353 (3d Cir. 1997)
- **October Term:** 1998
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued; merits decision pending.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 23, 1999
- **Question Presented:** Whether Congress validly removed state sovereign immunity for a private Lanham Act false-advertising action and whether a State waives immunity by engaging in interstate commercial activity regulated by federal law.
- **Question Granted:** Whether Congress validly removed state sovereign immunity for a private Lanham Act false-advertising action and whether a State waives immunity by engaging in interstate commercial activity regulated by federal law.
- **Legal Field:** Federalism and unfair competition, Commerce Clause abrogation and waiver
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit affirmed dismissal of College Savings Bank’s Lanham Act action on sovereign-immunity grounds.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Reverse and permit the federal false-advertising claim to proceed.
- **Material Facts and Record:** College Savings sold college-savings products and alleged that Florida Prepaid made false statements about its competing state program. The Trademark Remedy Clarification Act expressly subjected States to Lanham Act suits and remedies. Florida Prepaid entered interstate commerce but did not expressly consent to suit.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The express statutory abrogation ground precedes constructive waiver. If abrogation is valid, no waiver theory is necessary. The false-advertising merits remain unresolved.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argued April 20, 1999; simulated opinion-delivery date June 23, 1999.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank, decided the same day, concerns a separate patent-infringement action.

- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Scalia delivered the Court’s opinion; Breyer dissented, joined by Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg.
- **Historical Holding:** The historical Court held the Commerce Clause insufficient for abrogation and rejected constructive waiver based on commercial activity.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court at the stated scope; any fractured issue is described above.
- **Historical Sources:** Official U.S. Reports at 527 U.S. 666; No. 98-149; 131 F.3d 353 (3d Cir. 1997) and the court judgment identified above.

#### Issue 1: Retrospective Lanham Act damages against the State

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Simulated *City of Boerne v. Flores* requires claim-specific correspondence between a § 5 remedy and conduct that independently violates the Fourteenth Amendment. Simulated *Seminole Tribe v. Florida* permits an unmistakably enacted prospective direct-State action under an enumerated commerce power but expressly does not authorize damages.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Claim-specific enforcement under Fourteenth Amendment § 5, and Article I abrogation of state sovereign immunity under IGRA, supply the operative boundaries.
- **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause and § 5; Commerce Clause; Lanham Act; Trademark Remedy Clarification Act; state sovereign immunity.
- **Other Authority Used:** Competitive injury from a rival's statements about its own product is not deprivation of the claimant's property absent appropriation or destruction of an independently protected asset.

#### Issue 2: Constructive waiver through commercial activity

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied simulated holding makes ordinary regulated market participation an unequivocal consent to private monetary suit. *Parden v. Terminal Railway* supplies the contrary constructive-waiver rule Stone confronts directly.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No separately registered constructive-waiver test is identified. Ordinary waiver requires a clear and voluntary manifestation of the State's consent.
- **Other Governing Law:** State sovereign immunity; waiver doctrine; Commerce Clause; Lanham Act.
- **Other Authority Used:** Express consent, voluntary invocation of federal jurisdiction, and acceptance of a separately valid condition with clear notice remain distinct waiver routes.

#### Issue 3: Expressly authorized prospective Lanham Act relief

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Simulated *Seminole Tribe v. Florida* holds that Congress may authorize a prospective federal action directly against a State when it unmistakably identifies the State as defendant, acts within an enumerated commerce power, and expressly supplies the cause of action and prospective remedy.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Article I abrogation of state sovereign immunity under IGRA.
- **Other Governing Law:** Commerce Clause; Lanham Act; Trademark Remedy Clarification Act; Article III and equitable-remedy requirements.
- **Other Authority Used:** Simulated *Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife* governs injury, recurrence, and redressability. False advertising, materiality, causation, and equitable entitlement remain ordinary merits and remedy questions.
- **Open Point:** Whether the challenged statements continued or were likely to recur and whether College Savings can establish standing and entitlement to prospective relief.

#### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Third Circuit's dismissal of damages and other retrospective monetary relief, reverse its dismissal of the expressly authorized prospective Lanham Act claim, and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** No damages or other retrospective monetary relief may be awarded against Florida Prepaid on this claim. The prospective claim may proceed directly against Florida Prepaid under simulated *Seminole*, subject to proof of Article III standing, ongoing or imminently threatened false advertising, the Lanham Act elements, causation, and equitable entitlement. The lower courts shall resolve those matters in the first instance.

#### Issue 1: Retrospective Lanham Act damages against the State

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches both asserted sources of retrospective liability because affirmance of the damages dismissal requires rejecting § 5 and Article I monetary abrogation on this claim.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Competitive harm from Florida Prepaid's statements about its own program is not a Fourteenth Amendment property deprivation absent appropriation or destruction of an independently protected asset, so simulated *Boerne* supplies no § 5 basis for damages. Simulated *Seminole* independently limits Article I abrogation to the prospective remedy Congress expressly supplied and does not authorize retrospective monetary liability.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Trademark Remedy Clarification Act does not authorize retrospective damages against Florida Prepaid on this false-advertising claim because the alleged competitive injury is not an independent Fourteenth Amendment property deprivation and simulated *Seminole* does not permit Article I damages against a nonconsenting State.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** College Savings alleges that Florida Prepaid's representations diverted business, not that Florida appropriated or destroyed an asset belonging to the Bank. The § 5 route therefore fails on the claim alleged. Congress's commerce power supports the separate prospective route described below, but practical enforcement need and commercial competition cannot enlarge simulated *Seminole* beyond its no-damages boundary.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide a materially different claim involving appropriation or destruction of an independently protected asset, valid Reconstruction-amendment abrogation, genuine consent, or federal public enforcement.

#### Issue 2: Constructive waiver through commercial activity

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches constructive waiver because College Savings presents it as an independent basis for every requested remedy.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone candidly overrules *Parden* to the extent it treats ordinary participation in federally regulated commerce as consent to private suit. Market participation may create regulatory obligations, but it does not reliably communicate knowing and voluntary consent to every monetary or judicial remedy. Waiver requires the State's unequivocal legal act.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State does not waive sovereign immunity merely by engaging in interstate commercial activity subject to federal regulation. Waiver requires an unequivocal and voluntary manifestation of consent to the suit or remedy at issue.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Florida Prepaid enacted no consent, voluntarily invoked no federal forum, and made no litigation commitment. Treating the decision to sell a state program as consent would replace waiver with constructive federal compulsion and would make the State's immunity turn on regulatory coverage rather than its own legal choice.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding leaves intact express consent, voluntary invocation of federal jurisdiction, and acceptance of a separately valid condition that gives clear notice of the suit and remedy.

#### Issue 3: Expressly authorized prospective Lanham Act relief

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the immunity question for prospective relief but leaves standing, recurrence, the statutory merits, and equitable entitlement for remand.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies simulated *Seminole* without modification. Congress unmistakably named States as defendants, acted through the Commerce Clause, and expressly made Lanham Act causes of action and prospective remedies available. Sovereign immunity therefore does not bar a direct-State action limited to stopping an ongoing or imminently threatened violation.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Trademark Remedy Clarification Act validly authorizes a prospective Lanham Act action directly against a State under simulated *Seminole*, although it does not authorize damages against the nonconsenting State.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The enacted statute supplies the defendant, cause of action, and prospective remedy that simulated *Seminole* requires. That statutory route is independent of constructive waiver. Because the record does not conclusively establish continuing or imminent false advertising, College Savings must prove standing, recurrence, the Lanham Act elements, and equitable entitlement on remand before relief may issue.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding authorizes no damages, restitution, or retrospective monetary relief and decides no falsity, materiality, injury, causation, recurrence, standing, or equitable-remedy fact.

## Alden v. Maine

### Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** Alden v. Maine
- **Citation or Docket:** 527 U.S. 706; No. 98-436; 715 A.2d 172 (Me. 1998)
- **October Term:** 1998
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued; merits decision pending.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 23, 1999
- **Question Presented:** Whether Congress may require a nonconsenting State to answer in its own courts to a private damages action under the Fair Labor Standards Act enacted pursuant to the Commerce Clause.
- **Question Granted:** Whether Congress may require a nonconsenting State to answer in its own courts to a private damages action under the Fair Labor Standards Act enacted pursuant to the Commerce Clause.
- **Legal Field:** Federalism, state-court sovereign immunity, and federal wage law
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Supreme Judicial Court of Maine affirmed dismissal of state probation officers’ FLSA overtime action on state sovereign-immunity grounds.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Reverse and require Maine’s courts to hear the federal claim.
- **Material Facts and Record:** State probation officers alleged that Maine denied overtime compensation required by the FLSA. After their federal action encountered immunity, they filed in state court. The FLSA expressly included public agencies and authorized employee actions against state employers in a court of competent jurisdiction.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The federal statute clearly covers Maine and supplies a private damages action. The question is whether state sovereign immunity can defeat that valid federal command in a state forum otherwise competent to hear comparable claims.

- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Kennedy delivered the Court’s opinion; Souter dissented, joined by Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer.
- **Historical Holding:** The historical Court recognized constitutional state sovereign immunity in state court beyond the Eleventh Amendment and held Article I insufficient to subject a nonconsenting State to the FLSA action.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court at the stated scope; any fractured issue is described above.

#### Issue 1: Private Article I damages action against a State in state court

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Simulated *Seminole Tribe v. Florida* governs an unmistakably authorized prospective Article I action against a State in federal court and expressly denies damages; it does not decide private state-court treasury actions. No supplied simulated precedent establishes an earned-compensation or specific-restitution exception.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Article I abrogation of state sovereign immunity under IGRA, under Federalism, supplies the current direct-State and remedy boundary requiring extension to the state-court setting.
- **Other Governing Law:** Commerce Clause; Supremacy Clause; FLSA public-employer coverage and private action; structural state sovereign immunity.
- **Other Authority Used:** *Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority* confirms the substantive FLSA power; *Testa v. Katt* and *Howlett v. Rose* prevent discriminatory closure to federal law but do not erase a defendant's generally applicable constitutional immunity.

#### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Supreme Judicial Court of Maine's judgment dismissing the private action against Maine.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** No separate remedial instruction is required. The dismissal does not bar federal enforcement, unequivocal state waiver, valid Reconstruction-amendment abrogation, prospective relief against responsible officers, or relief Maine itself lawfully makes available.

#### Issue 1: Private Article I damages action against a State in state court

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the structural-immunity question. Employee-specific coverage, hours, rates, defenses, and amounts are unnecessary once the direct-State treasury action is unavailable.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The FLSA validly regulates Maine as an employer under *Garcia*, but Article I does not authorize a private direct-State treasury action in the State's own court. Even accepting that private enforcement is more reliable than centralized federal enforcement and that the alternative channels may leave some wage violations unremedied, enforcement necessity cannot create damages authority that the constitutional source and simulated *Seminole* withhold. Reversal would require a candid modification of simulated *Seminole* to create an earned-compensation exception, and Stone declines to make that change.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may not use Article I to subject a nonconsenting State to a private damages action in its own courts. Characterizing accrued FLSA liability as earned compensation or make-whole restitution does not alter the direct State defendant or retrospective treasury remedy.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The practical conflict is real: private claims may enforce earned-wage duties more completely, while federal public enforcement can leave individual violations without compensation. But *Testa* and *Howlett* require nondiscriminatory enforcement only where the defendant is otherwise suable, and they do not remove constitutional immunity. The seriousness and compensatory character of the unpaid wages cannot create a remedial channel. Federal supremacy remains enforceable through the United States, lawful prospective officer relief, waiver, valid Reconstruction legislation, and any relief Maine itself provides.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The position does not deny the substantive FLSA duty, authorize Maine to violate federal law, or bar unequivocal waiver, valid Reconstruction-amendment abrogation, suit by the United States, prospective relief against responsible officers, or relief the State itself makes available.

- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Settlement-only class certification under Rule 23, under Civil Procedure, supplies the operative requirement and permits narrower subclasses and individual resolution.
- **Other Governing Law:** Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(a) and 23(b)(1)(B); Due Process Clause.

#### Issue 1: Rule 23(b)(1)(B) limited fund and adequate representation

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits; no dispositive threshold issue is identified.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the traditional limited-fund category narrowly because mandatory aggregation eliminates individual control and opt-out. The parties may not manufacture the fund’s limit through negotiation, reserve value outside the class, or use one representation structure for materially opposed claimant groups.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A mandatory settlement class under Rule 23(b)(1)(B) requires a demonstrated fund fixed independently at its maximum practical value, insufficiency to satisfy the aggregate claims, commitment of substantially the whole fund, equitable distribution, and adequate conflict-free representation, including separate subclasses where present and future claimants materially diverge.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The record did not establish Fibreboard’s total available value independently of the agreement, the settlement preserved disputed value and preferential arrangements, and the single class structure did not adequately represent present injuries, future disease, and varying insurance interests. Certification therefore fails.
