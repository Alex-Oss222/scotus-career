#### Issue 1: Continued availability of § 2241 habeas review

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves jurisdiction first. The petition presents pure statutory and constitutional questions, and no other threshold defect defeats the historic habeas channel.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the clear-statement and meaningful-review principles already operating in the simulation. AEDPA and IIRIRA altered immigration review extensively, but the provisions invoked do not clearly eliminate § 2241 for the legal claims presented, and the Government may not derive withdrawal of the writ from implication alone.

- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Jurisdiction is not assumed and is not created by the seriousness of the claim. It exists because § 2241 supplied a historic channel and Congress did not clearly close it in the provisions governing this pending proceeding. The alternative review structure did not furnish an equally clear route for the specific legal challenge when Goncalves filed. The First Circuit therefore properly reached the statutory issue.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not establish district-court review of every discretionary immigration determination, reopen factual findings, or displace an exclusive review channel Congress states clearly and preserves meaningfully.

#### Issue 2: Temporal application of AEDPA § 440(d)

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the temporal question because jurisdiction survives and the Board applied § 440(d) to a pending application.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies *Landgraf*, *Rivers*, and the enacted structure. Congress did not clearly direct § 440(d) to govern already-filed applications, while neighboring provisions contained express temporal instructions. Applying the new categorical bar to a request already filed and adjudicated under former § 212(c) would attach a new legal disability to the pending proceeding.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** AEDPA § 440(d) does not apply to a former § 212(c) application that was pending when AEDPA took effect, absent a clear congressional direction making the new categorical restriction applicable to such pending applications.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Goncalves had invoked the discretionary process, obtained an immigration-judge decision on its merits, and pursued an administrative appeal before enactment. The new law did not merely change a tribunal's procedure; it removed eligibility for the discretionary judgment already underway. The First Circuit correctly required the Board to decide the pending request under the former eligibility rule.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not govern applications first filed after AEDPA's effective date, guarantee discretionary relief, or decide the distinct temporal operation of IIRIRA's later repeal in another posture.

#### Issue 3: Interpretive weight owed the Attorney General

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the question because the Board relied on the Attorney General's decision in *Matter of Soriano*.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone works within the then-operative statutory-deference framework and does not silently replace it. The Attorney General cannot receive controlling weight on district-court jurisdiction, and no deference is due when ordinary interpretive tools resolve the temporal question against the agency's construction. At most, *Soriano* receives persuasive consideration for its reasoning, which does not overcome the text, structure, and nonretroactivity principles.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Attorney General's conclusion that AEDPA § 440(d) applies to pending applications receives no controlling weight because the statute's temporal operation is resolved by ordinary interpretive tools and the agency's contrary reasoning is unpersuasive.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The agency's immigration expertise is real within delegated policy and factual domains, but the issue here is Congress's temporal command. *Soriano* treated statutory silence as authorization to extinguish pending eligibility and gave insufficient effect to the enactment's express temporal contrasts. Deference cannot make silence carry a legal consequence the statutory structure does not support.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not deny appropriate weight to authorized immigration judgments on genuinely unresolved statutory details, policy choices, or technical administration within the agency's lawful domain.

## Neder v. United States

- **Case Name:** Neder v. United States
- **Citation or Docket:** 527 U.S. 1; No. 97-1985; 136 F.3d 1459 (11th Cir. 1998)
- **October Term:** 1998
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued; merits decision pending.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 10, 1999
- **Question Presented:** Whether a trial court’s failure to submit an offense element to the jury is structural error or may be harmless beyond a reasonable doubt when the element was uncontested and supported by overwhelming evidence.
- **Question Granted:** Whether a trial court’s failure to submit an offense element to the jury is structural error or may be harmless beyond a reasonable doubt when the element was uncontested and supported by overwhelming evidence.
- **Legal Field:** Criminal procedure, omitted elements and harmless error
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit affirmed Neder’s fraud convictions after finding the omission of materiality from the jury instructions harmless.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Reverse and require automatic reversal for removal of an element from the jury.
- **Material Facts and Record:** The trial judge instructed the jury on multiple fraud offenses but decided materiality himself. Neder did not contest that the false statements were material; his defense disputed other issues, and the evidence of materiality was overwhelming.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The Court reaches harmlessness only after confirming constitutional error. The relevant question is whether the omission affected the jury function in this case, not whether the element can generally be assigned to a judge.

- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Rehnquist delivered the Court’s opinion; Scalia dissented, joined by Souter and Ginsburg.

#### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** Complete rendered October Term 1997 and admitted immediate judicial consequences effective before this event. Later entries in the continuity registers are excluded unless independently effective before the simulated event date.

#### Issue 1: Materiality as a fraud element

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Simulated *United States v. Gaudin* requires the jury to determine every enacted element beyond a reasonable doubt but does not itself identify materiality under the fraud statutes.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Charging and jury determination of offense elements.
- **Other Governing Law:** Federal mail, wire, bank-fraud, and tax-fraud statutes; traditional legal meaning of fraud.
- **Other Authority Used:** Congress's use of the settled term "fraud" incorporates materiality unless the enacted text displaces it.

#### Issue 2: Complete removal of an element from the jury

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Simulated *United States v. Gaudin* establishes that a criminal conviction must rest on a jury determination of every offense element beyond a reasonable doubt. It does not classify complete removal of an element as structural or harmless.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Charging and jury determination of offense elements.
- **Other Governing Law:** Fifth and Sixth Amendments; *Chapman v. California*; *Sullivan v. Louisiana*.
- **Other Authority Used:** *Sullivan* treats the absence of a constitutionally valid jury verdict as structural because a reviewing court cannot hypothesize the verdict the Constitution required the jury to return.
- **Open Point:** Whether complete withholding of one offense element from the jury leaves a valid verdict that can be reviewed for harmlessness.

#### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Eleventh Circuit's judgment as to the affected convictions and remand for a new trial.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The convictions and sentences on counts for which the judge removed materiality from the jury shall be vacated. The Government may retry those counts under instructions submitting every element, including materiality, to the jury. Any unaffected count remains governed by ordinary law.

#### Issue 1: Materiality as a fraud element

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches statutory element identification before deciding the constitutional consequence of the trial court's allocation.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The settled legal meaning of fraud includes materiality unless Congress displaces it. The federal mail, wire, and bank-fraud statutes contain no contrary command, and materiality likewise remained an element of the affected tax offenses.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Materiality is an element of the affected federal fraud offenses and must be submitted to the jury.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress used legal terms carrying an established materiality component. Treating materiality as a question the judge may decide removes part of the enacted offense from the jury contrary to simulated *Gaudin*.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide materiality under every differently worded federal offense.

#### Issue 2: Complete removal of an element from the jury

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the error's classification because it determines whether the convictions may stand without a jury finding on materiality.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone resolves the open point by extending simulated *Gaudin* and *Sullivan*. Complete removal of an element not admitted by the defendant leaves no constitutionally valid jury verdict on the charged offense. The error is structural and cannot be cured by appellate findings that the element was uncontested or supported by overwhelming evidence.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When the trial court completely withholds an offense element from the jury and the defendant has not formally admitted that element, the resulting conviction lacks the jury verdict required by the Fifth and Sixth Amendments and must be reversed without ordinary harmless-error review.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Neder did not plead guilty to or formally stipulate materiality. The jury therefore returned findings only on the remaining components, while the judge supplied an indispensable part of guilt. *Chapman* permits review of trial error within a constitutionally valid adjudicative structure; it does not authorize an appellate court to create the missing jury verdict by deciding what a properly instructed jury would have found. The strength or absence of contest over materiality cannot substitute for the institution the Constitution assigns the decision.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not make every element-related instructional error structural. It is confined to complete removal of an element not formally admitted, and it does not decide a misdescription of an element, an evidentiary error, or the effect of a valid stipulation or plea.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Eleventh Circuit's judgment and enforce the FLRA order as to recognition of the representation right.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** NASA shall comply with the FLRA order recognizing the § 7114(a)(2)(B) right. The representation right does not authorize obstruction of questioning, compromise of protected information, or direction of the investigation.

#### Issue 1: Representation right in an Inspector General examination

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory coverage question.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** An investigator employed by NASA's Office of Inspector General remains a representative of NASA when examining a NASA employee about agency-related conduct. Statutory insulation from ordinary management does not sever the employment and agency relationship for § 7114.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An investigator employed by an agency's Office of Inspector General is a 'representative of the agency' when examining an agency employee within § 7114(a)(2)(B). The office's independence from ordinary management does not erase that statutory relationship.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The investigator is employed within NASA's Inspector General office and conducts an agency-related examination capable of producing discipline. The Inspector General Act and § 7114 operate together because representation need not compromise investigative control or protected information.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A union representative may not obstruct questioning, compromise protected information, or direct the investigation; the holding prescribes no universal interview protocol.
