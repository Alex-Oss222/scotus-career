## Article III and Federal Courts

### Specialized statutory review-channel preclusion

**Governing question:** When a comprehensive federal administrative-review scheme displaces initial district-court jurisdiction over a claim connected to that scheme.

**Current rule:** A specialized statutory scheme precludes initial district-court review when congressional intent to channel the dispute is fairly discernible from the enacted structure and the designated route preserves meaningful judicial review. A closely connected claim ordinarily must use that route.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** Examine the enacted review structure first, then determine whether the asserted claim can receive meaningful consideration through the designated administrative and appellate sequence.

**Exceptions and limits:** Initial district-court review remains available when the statutory route cannot provide meaningful judicial review, the claim is genuinely collateral to the scheme, or the process imposes an irreparable deprivation the designated review cannot address.

**First controlling authority:** Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich, 510 U.S. 200 (1994), decided January 19, 1994, Statutory review-channel preclusion; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich, 510 U.S. 200 (1994), decided January 19, 1994, Statutory review-channel preclusion; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich, 510 U.S. 200 (1994), January 19, 1994.

### Federal constitutional claims under the FTCA and agency waivers

**Governing question:** Whether a federal constitutional claim falls within the Federal Tort Claims Act and whether a federal agency’s sue-and-be-sued clause removes immunity.

**Current rule:** A federal constitutional claim is not cognizable under 28 U.S.C. § 1346(b) because it arises under federal rather than state law and lacks the required private-person state-law analogue. The FTCA therefore does not make the United States the exclusive defendant on that claim. A broad agency sue-and-be-sued clause may waive the agency’s sovereign immunity, but the waiver does not itself create a substantive cause of action.

**Controlling construction:** Sections 1346(b) and 2679(a) do not convert a federal constitutional claim into an FTCA claim or make the FTCA exclusive when § 1346(b) is not satisfied.

**Exceptions and limits:** A separate cause of action and authorized remedy remain necessary even when immunity is waived.

**First controlling authority:** FDIC v. Meyer, 510 U.S. 471 (1994), decided February 23, 1994, FTCA exclusivity and the sue-and-be-sued waiver; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** FDIC v. Meyer, 510 U.S. 471 (1994), decided February 23, 1994, FTCA exclusivity and the sue-and-be-sued waiver; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** FDIC v. Meyer, 510 U.S. 471 (1994), February 23, 1994.

### Constitutional damages actions against federal agencies

**Governing question:** Whether the officer damages remedy recognized in Bivens extends directly to a federal agency.

**Current rule:** Bivens does not imply a damages cause of action directly against a federal agency, even when the agency’s sovereign immunity has been waived. The judicially implied remedy remains directed to responsible federal officers rather than the employing agency.

**Consequence or recurring remedy:** A constitutional damages claim brought directly against the federal agency must be dismissed absent an independent statutory cause of action.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not narrow existing Bivens actions against individual officers or foreclose an otherwise authorized statutory or prospective remedy.

**First controlling authority:** FDIC v. Meyer, 510 U.S. 471 (1994), decided February 23, 1994, Bivens liability of a federal agency; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** FDIC v. Meyer, 510 U.S. 471 (1994), decided February 23, 1994, Bivens liability of a federal agency; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** FDIC v. Meyer, 510 U.S. 471 (1994), February 23, 1994.

### Qualified-immunity review uses the complete body of law

**Governing question:** What legal authorities an appellate court must consider when deciding whether a right was clearly established.

**Current rule:** Because clearly established law is an objective legal question reviewed de novo, a court must consider all materially relevant precedent available at the time of the challenged conduct, including authority not cited to the district court. A party’s incomplete legal research does not bind the court to an incomplete statement of governing law.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not excuse failure to preserve the claim itself or the factual premises on which it depends.

**First controlling authority:** Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510 (1994), decided February 23, 1994, Legal authorities considered on qualified-immunity review; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510 (1994), decided February 23, 1994, Legal authorities considered on qualified-immunity review; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510 (1994), February 23, 1994.

### Ancillary jurisdiction to enforce settlements after dismissal

**Governing question:** When a federal court may enforce a settlement after a Rule 41(a)(1)(ii) dismissal.

**Current rule:** A federal district court lacks ancillary jurisdiction to enforce a settlement after a Rule 41(a)(1)(ii) dismissal when the dismissal neither incorporates the settlement terms nor expressly retains enforcement jurisdiction. Enforcement remains ancillary when the federal dismissal makes compliance part of the decree or expressly retains jurisdiction; otherwise an independent jurisdictional basis is required.

**Consequence or recurring remedy:** Without incorporation, retained jurisdiction, or an independent federal jurisdictional ground, the enforcement proceeding must be dismissed.

**Exceptions and limits:** The parties may pursue an independent state contract action or a separate federal action supported by diversity or another jurisdictional grant.

**First controlling authority:** Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America, 511 U.S. 375 (1994), decided May 16, 1994, Ancillary jurisdiction to enforce a settlement after dismissal; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America, 511 U.S. 375 (1994), decided May 16, 1994, Ancillary jurisdiction to enforce a settlement after dismissal; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America, 511 U.S. 375 (1994), May 16, 1994.

### Collateral-order review of settlement-enforcement rulings

**Governing question:** Whether an order vacating a settlement-based dismissal or refusing to enforce a settlement is immediately appealable.

**Current rule:** An order vacating a settlement-based dismissal or refusing to enforce a settlement is not immediately appealable under the collateral-order doctrine. The private contractual interest in avoiding continued litigation lacks the exceptional public and legal importance required to overcome the final-judgment rule, and the ruling can be reviewed after final judgment.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not govern constitutional or statutory immunities whose value would be destroyed by trial. Section 1292(b), final review, and extraordinary mandamus remain available when their independent requirements are met.

**First controlling authority:** Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc., 511 U.S. 863 (1994), decided June 6, 1994, Collateral-order appeal from refusal to enforce a settlement; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc., 511 U.S. 863 (1994), decided June 6, 1994, Collateral-order appeal from refusal to enforce a settlement; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc., 511 U.S. 863 (1994), June 6, 1994.

### Habeas channeling of conviction-invalidating § 1983 damages claims

**Governing question:** When a claimant in custody may pursue § 1983 damages on a theory that would necessarily invalidate an outstanding conviction or sentence.

**Current rule:** While a claimant remains in custody and a habeas remedy is legally available, a § 1983 damages claim whose success would necessarily establish the invalidity of an outstanding conviction or sentence may not proceed while that judgment remains outstanding. The restriction is procedural and temporary, not jurisdictional, and a genuinely separable claim that would not imply invalidity remains outside it.

**Consequence or recurring remedy:** The conviction-invalidating damages claim is dismissed without prejudice while the outstanding judgment and legally available habeas channel remain.

**Open question:** The Court left open the treatment of a claimant for whom habeas is no longer legally available through no fault of the claimant.

**First controlling authority:** Heck v. Humphrey, 512 U.S. 477 (1994), decided June 24, 1994, Coordination of § 1983 damages with an outstanding conviction; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Heck v. Humphrey, 512 U.S. 477 (1994), decided June 24, 1994, Coordination of § 1983 damages with an outstanding conviction; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Heck v. Humphrey, 512 U.S. 477 (1994), June 24, 1994.

### Capital habeas counsel and prepetition stay authority

**Governing question:** When a capital federal postconviction proceeding begins for appointment of counsel and related stay authority.

**Current rule:** A capital prisoner’s motion requesting appointment of federal habeas counsel commences the represented postconviction proceeding for purposes of 21 U.S.C. § 848(q)(4)(B). Once that right is invoked, the same proceeding is pending for purposes of 28 U.S.C. § 2251, and the district court may stay execution in its sound discretion when reasonably necessary to make the representation effective.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** The court appoints qualified counsel, then determines whether and for how long a stay is reasonably necessary to permit investigation, preparation, and filing of a meaningful first federal petition.

**Exceptions and limits:** A stay is not automatic or indefinite. Inexcusable delay or disregard of available procedures may justify denial, and the decision excuses no later procedural default.

**First controlling authority:** McFarland v. Scott, 512 U.S. 849 (1994), decided June 30, 1994, Commencement of the represented capital proceeding; established the commencement rule; the companion stay component is supplied by the same decision’s stay-authority holding.

**Controlling authorities:** McFarland v. Scott, 512 U.S. 849 (1994), decided June 30, 1994, Commencement of the represented capital proceeding; supplies the commencement rule; the companion stay component is supplied by the same decision’s stay-authority holding.

**Present operation effective:** McFarland v. Scott, 512 U.S. 849 (1994), June 30, 1994.

### FEC authority to seek certiorari and untimely Solicitor General authorization

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs fEC authority to seek certiorari and untimely Solicitor General authorization?

**Current rule:** Section 437d(a)(6) does not independently authorize the FEC to petition this Court for certiorari, and the Solicitor General’s authorization after expiration of § 2101(c)’s filing period cannot relate back to validate the petition.

**Exceptions and limits:** The Court does not decide whether the nonvoting congressional officers violated separation of powers, whether the ex officio provision was severable, or whether later Commission ratification could cure a structural defect.

**First controlling authority:** Federal Election Commission v. NRA Political Victory Fund, 513 U.S. 88, decided December 6, 1994, Threshold question; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Federal Election Commission v. NRA Political Victory Fund, 513 U.S. 88, decided December 6, 1994, Threshold question; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Federal Election Commission v. NRA Political Victory Fund, 513 U.S. 88, December 6, 1994.

### Jurisdictional characterization

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs jurisdictional characterization?

**Current rule:** The ministerial protection is a constitutional limitation on applying Title VII to the merits, not a withdrawal of federal-question jurisdiction. A federal court with statutory jurisdiction over a substantial federal employment claim resolves the church-autonomy defense on the merits. It does not dismiss under Rule 12(b)(1) merely because the Constitution forecloses application of the statute.

**Exceptions and limits:** The Court does not address a federal claim that is immaterial, wholly insubstantial, or pleaded solely to manufacture jurisdiction.

**First controlling authority:** Young v. Northern Illinois Conference of United Methodist Church, 21 F.3d 184, decided January 17, 1995, Jurisdictional characterization; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Young v. Northern Illinois Conference of United Methodist Church, 21 F.3d 184, decided January 17, 1995, Jurisdictional characterization; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Young v. Northern Illinois Conference of United Methodist Church, 21 F.3d 184, January 17, 1995.

### Preservation of the governmental-entity argument

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs preservation of the governmental-entity argument?

**Current rule:** A party may present a new legal argument supporting a preserved claim when the argument was passed upon below, falls within the question presented, and seeks no new claim or form of relief.

**First controlling authority:** Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp., 513 U.S. 374, decided February 21, 1995, Preservation of the governmental-entity argument; established the current rule.

### Federal review when state and federal grounds are interwoven

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs federal review when state and federal grounds are interwoven?

**Current rule:** The Supreme Court may review a state judgment that fairly appears to rest on federal law or on state law interwoven with the federal question when no adequate and independent state ground is clearly stated.

**First controlling authority:** Arizona v. Evans, 514 U.S. 1, decided March 1, 1995, Threshold question; established the current rule.

### Interlocutory review of municipal liability

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs interlocutory review of municipal liability?

**Current rule:** A court of appeals reviewing an interlocutory denial of qualified immunity to individual officers may not also review a nonappealable municipal-liability ruling merely because the matters arise from the same litigation.

**Exceptions and limits:** Section 1292(b), mandamus, and any genuinely inseparable issue necessary to meaningful review remain governed by their own requirements. The Court does not create general pendent party appellate jurisdiction.

**First controlling authority:** Swint v. Chambers County Commission, 514 U.S. 35, decided March 1, 1995, Interlocutory review of municipal liability; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Swint v. Chambers County Commission, 514 U.S. 35, decided March 1, 1995, Interlocutory review of municipal liability; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Swint v. Chambers County Commission, 514 U.S. 35, March 1, 1995.

### Obedience to a bankruptcy injunction pending authorized review

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs obedience to a bankruptcy injunction pending authorized review?

**Current rule:** Parties subject to a bankruptcy injunction issued by a court with at least a colorable basis for jurisdiction must obey the order until it is stayed, modified, or reversed through authorized review. They may not obtain collateral permission from another court to act in defiance of it. The duty to comply is distinct from the ultimate validity of the injunction. A party with notice must challenge the order in the issuing court and through the prescribed appellate route unless the order is transparently beyond any arguable jurisdictional basis.

**Exceptions and limits:** The Court does not approve every injunction protecting a nondebtor surety, decide the ultimate permissible breadth of relief under § 105, or require obedience to an order entered without even an arguable jurisdictional foundation.

**First controlling authority:** Celotex Corp. v. Edwards, 514 U.S. 300, decided April 19, 1995, Obedience to a bankruptcy injunction pending authorized review; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Celotex Corp. v. Edwards, 514 U.S. 300, decided April 19, 1995, Obedience to a bankruptcy injunction pending authorized review; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Celotex Corp. v. Edwards, 514 U.S. 300, April 19, 1995.

### Refund jurisdiction for a compelled third-party payment

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs refund jurisdiction for a compelled third-party payment?

**Current rule:** Section 1346(a)(1) authorizes a refund action by a person who was not the assessed taxpayer but paid the tax under protest to remove a federal tax lien from her own property. A claimant from whom the Government compelled payment through a tax lien on the claimant’s property may invoke § 1346(a)(1), provided the claimant satisfies the applicable administrative and procedural requirements. The waiver is applied through its enacted reference to a tax erroneously or illegally collected, not limited categorically to the person named in the assessment.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not determine when a volunteer or stranger who pays another person’s tax may sue. It is confined to a compelled payment tied to a concrete lien against the claimant’s property.

**First controlling authority:** United States v. Williams, 514 U.S. 527, decided April 25, 1995, Refund jurisdiction for a compelled third-party payment; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** United States v. Williams, 514 U.S. 527, decided April 25, 1995, Refund jurisdiction for a compelled third-party payment; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Williams, 514 U.S. 527, April 25, 1995.

### Evidence-sufficiency disputes in qualified-immunity appeals

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs evidence-sufficiency disputes in qualified-immunity appeals?

**Current rule:** A denial of qualified immunity is not immediately appealable insofar as it determines only that the pretrial record contains sufficient evidence to create a genuine factual dispute. An interlocutory qualified-immunity appeal may present the legal question whether conduct, taken under an assumed or properly supported version of the facts, violated clearly established law. It does not authorize appellate reconsideration of whose account the evidence supports.

**Exceptions and limits:** Immediate review remains available for a separable legal immunity question. Section 1292(b), mandamus, and final appellate review remain governed by their own requirements.

**First controlling authority:** Johnson v. Jones, 515 U.S. 304, decided June 12, 1995, Evidence-sufficiency disputes in qualified-immunity appeals; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Johnson v. Jones, 515 U.S. 304, decided June 12, 1995, Evidence-sufficiency disputes in qualified-immunity appeals; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Johnson v. Jones, 515 U.S. 304, June 12, 1995.

### Judicial review of Westfall Act certification

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs judicial review of Westfall Act certification?

**Current rule:** An Attorney General certification that a federal employee acted within the scope of employment is judicially reviewable before substitution of the United States becomes final. Certification initiates substitution and is conclusive for removal where the statute expressly says so. It does not conclusively determine the scope-of-employment question for final substitution. The district court may receive evidence and resolve disputed facts under ordinary procedures.

**Exceptions and limits:** The Court does not determine whether Lamagno acted within the scope of employment, waive federal immunity, create a cause of action against the United States, or decide the federal jurisdiction that would remain after a court rejects certification in a case lacking another jurisdictional basis.

**First controlling authority:** Gutierrez de Martinez v. Lamagno, 515 U.S. 417, decided June 14, 1995, Judicial review of Westfall Act certification; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Gutierrez de Martinez v. Lamagno, 515 U.S. 417, decided June 14, 1995, Judicial review of Westfall Act certification; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Gutierrez de Martinez v. Lamagno, 515 U.S. 417, June 14, 1995.

### Standing for racial-gerrymandering claims

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs standing for racial-gerrymandering claims?

**Current rule:** Voters who reside outside the district alleged to embody an unconstitutional racial classification lack standing absent evidence that the legislature personally classified or otherwise concretely injured them.

**Exceptions and limits:** The Court does not decide whether District 4 is unconstitutional, whether residents of that district would have standing, or what equal-protection standard would govern a properly presented claim.

**First controlling authority:** United States v. Hays / Louisiana v. Hays, 515 U.S. 737, decided June 29, 1995, Threshold question; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** United States v. Hays / Louisiana v. Hays, 515 U.S. 737, decided June 29, 1995, Threshold question; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Hays / Louisiana v. Hays, 515 U.S. 737, June 29, 1995.

## Due Process

### Pre-seizure process for civil forfeiture of real property

**Governing question:** What process is required before the Government seizes real property for civil forfeiture.

**Current rule:** Except upon particularized proof of exigent circumstances, the Fifth Amendment requires notice and a meaningful opportunity to be heard before the Government seizes real property for civil forfeiture.

**Burdens and proof:** The Government bears the obligation to establish an actual exigency justifying immediate seizure.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule concerns real property and permits immediate action on proof of a genuine exigency. It does not decide the treatment of readily movable personal property or contraband.

**First controlling authority:** United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, 510 U.S. 43 (1993), decided December 13, 1993, Pre-seizure process for real property; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, 510 U.S. 43 (1993), decided December 13, 1993, Pre-seizure process for real property; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, 510 U.S. 43 (1993), December 13, 1993.

### Specific constitutional source and substantive-due-process nonduplication

**Governing question:** Whether substantive due process supplies a parallel claim when a specific constitutional provision directly governs the alleged arrest or personal restraint.

**Current rule:** A claimant may not use substantive due process to duplicate Fourth Amendment protection for injuries alleged to arise from arrest and prosecution-related restraints on the person. When settled Fourth Amendment law supplies the directly relevant source, substantive due process does not create a parallel federal malicious-prosecution tort.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule leaves open a materially distinct nonseizure injury and does not decide a properly pleaded Fourth Amendment, fabricated-evidence, procedural-due-process, or state-law claim.

**First controlling authority:** Albright v. Oliver, 510 U.S. 266 (1994), decided January 24, 1994, Substantive due process as the asserted source; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Albright v. Oliver, 510 U.S. 266 (1994), decided January 24, 1994, Substantive due process as the asserted source; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Albright v. Oliver, 510 U.S. 266 (1994), January 24, 1994.

### Whole-charge review of reasonable-doubt instructions

**Governing question:** How a reviewing court determines whether a reasonable-doubt instruction lowered the prosecution’s burden.

**Current rule:** The Constitution prescribes no fixed wording for a reasonable-doubt instruction. A reviewing court asks whether there is a reasonable likelihood that the jury understood the complete charge to permit conviction on proof below reasonable doubt, evaluating disputed phrases in the context of the directions concerning evidence, the prosecution’s burden, and the required abiding conviction of guilt.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not endorse archaic moral-certainty language; similar wording may be unconstitutional when context fails to anchor the required certainty in the evidence or combines phrases that materially lower the burden.

**First controlling authority:** Victor v. Nebraska / Sandoval v. California, 511 U.S. 1 (1994), decided March 22, 1994, Constitutional standard for reasonable-doubt instructions; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Victor v. Nebraska / Sandoval v. California, 511 U.S. 1 (1994), decided March 22, 1994, Constitutional standard for reasonable-doubt instructions; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Victor v. Nebraska / Sandoval v. California, 511 U.S. 1 (1994), March 22, 1994.

### Meaningful judicial review of punitive-damages amounts

**Governing question:** What postverdict procedure a State must provide when it authorizes punitive damages.

**Current rule:** A State that authorizes punitive damages must provide meaningful judicial review of the amount, including review of whether the award rests on legally permissible punitive considerations and remains within the range a rational jury could derive from the evidence. Review need not be de novo or use a fixed verbal formula, but it must be capable of correcting an amount outside the rational range supported by the record.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule creates no numerical ratio, proof burden, or mandatory remittitur formula and leaves the State discretion to select a constitutionally adequate corrective procedure.

**First controlling authority:** Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg, 512 U.S. 415 (1994), decided June 24, 1994, Procedural safeguard against arbitrary punitive amounts; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg, 512 U.S. 415 (1994), decided June 24, 1994, Procedural safeguard against arbitrary punitive amounts; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg, 512 U.S. 415 (1994), June 24, 1994.

### Withdrawal of an apparent postdeprivation tax remedy

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs withdrawal of an apparent postdeprivation tax remedy?

**Current rule:** When a State holds out a clear postdeprivation refund remedy for unlawfully collected taxes, due process bars it from withdrawing that remedy after taxpayers have paid under the apparent remedial scheme. A State may choose an adequate predeprivation system, an adequate postdeprivation system, or a combination. It may change that system prospectively with fair notice. It may not require payment under an apparently available refund route and then reinterpret that route away after payment.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not dictate the design of Georgia’s future tax procedures. The State may adopt prospective protest, prepayment, limitations, and proof requirements that provide constitutionally adequate notice and opportunity.

**First controlling authority:** Reich v. Collins, 513 U.S. 106, decided December 6, 1994, Withdrawal of an apparent postdeprivation tax remedy; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Reich v. Collins, 513 U.S. 106, decided December 6, 1994, Withdrawal of an apparent postdeprivation tax remedy; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Reich v. Collins, 513 U.S. 106, December 6, 1994.

### Facial review of previability abortion regulations

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs facial review of previability abortion regulations?

**Current rule:** *Casey*, not *Salerno*’s no-set-of-circumstances formulation, governs a facial challenge to a regulation of previability abortion. A provision is invalid when, in a large fraction of the cases in which it operates as a restriction, its purpose or predictable effect is to place a substantial obstacle in the path of a person seeking a previability abortion. Persons for whom the provision is irrelevant do not enlarge the denominator.

**First controlling authority:** Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer, 18 F.3d 526, decided February 13, 1995, Facial review under Casey; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer, 18 F.3d 526, decided February 13, 1995, Facial review under Casey; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer, 18 F.3d 526, February 13, 1995.

### Operational and cumulative undue-burden review

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs operational and cumulative undue-burden review?

**Current rule:** Courts must measure the additional burden attributable to the challenged provision as it operates against actual access conditions, and must consider linked requirements together when their combined operation determines whether a substantial obstacle exists. Geographic distance, provider availability, scheduling limitations, cost, and comparable conditions are not automatically attributable to the State. They remain relevant to identifying what the enactment adds to the claimant’s circumstances. Requirements may not be insulated from review through artificial isolation when they operate as an integrated restriction.

**Exceptions and limits:** The Court does not hold that a twenty-four-hour period is always valid or always invalid. Nor does cumulative analysis justify relief against a provision that contributes nothing to the combined constitutional burden.

**First controlling authority:** Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer, 18 F.3d 526, decided February 13, 1995, Operational burden and the remand record; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer, 18 F.3d 526, decided February 13, 1995, Operational burden and the remand record; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer, 18 F.3d 526, February 13, 1995.

### Usable medical-emergency routes in penal abortion regulation

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs usable medical-emergency routes in penal abortion regulation?

**Current rule:** A penal abortion regulation must preserve a practically usable emergency route permitting prompt action in the physician’s good-faith clinical judgment when delay creates a material risk of death, serious physical injury, substantial irreversible impairment, or comparable grave medically established harm. A court may adopt a saving construction only when the enacted text and controlling state law fairly bear it. Applicable state scienter rules must be included in the notice analysis.

**Exceptions and limits:** The emergency route does not immunize negligent practice or an unsupported invocation of medical necessity.

**First controlling authority:** Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer, 18 F.3d 526, decided February 13, 1995, Medical emergency, statutory definitions, and penal notice; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer, 18 F.3d 526, decided February 13, 1995, Medical emergency, statutory definitions, and penal notice; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer, 18 F.3d 526, February 13, 1995.

### State-created liberty in prison discipline

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs state-created liberty in prison discipline?

**Current rule:** A State creates a protected prison liberty interest when substantive law meaningfully limits officials’ authority to impose a significant deprivation. Mandatory wording alone is insufficient, but a court may not disregard a genuine legal constraint on punitive segregation. The court examines both the substance of state law and the deprivation at issue. State law must genuinely constrain official authority rather than merely prescribe procedures or use mandatory drafting. The restraint must concern a meaningful loss of liberty rather than a routine transfer, brief inconvenience, or ordinary discretionary condition of confinement.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not constitutionalize every prison regulation, routine transfer, brief inconvenience, or discretionary management choice. Institutional security and lawful correctional judgment retain their ordinary weight.

**First controlling authority:** Sandin v. Conner, 515 U.S. 472, decided June 19, 1995, State-created liberty in prison discipline; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Sandin v. Conner, 515 U.S. 472, decided June 19, 1995, State-created liberty in prison discipline; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Sandin v. Conner, 515 U.S. 472, June 19, 1995.

## Equal Protection

### Sex discrimination in peremptory challenges

**Governing question:** Whether a party may use peremptory challenges to exclude prospective jurors because of sex.

**Current rule:** The Equal Protection Clause prohibits purposeful use of peremptory challenges to exclude prospective jurors because of sex, including in civil litigation conducted through state judicial process.

**Burdens and proof:** The ultimate burden of persuasion remains on the challenger.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** The challenger must establish circumstances supporting an inference of purposeful sex discrimination. The striking party must then articulate a genuine sex-neutral explanation, and the trial court determines whether purposeful discrimination occurred.

**Exceptions and limits:** Disproportionate impact alone does not prove purpose, and the rule does not bar strikes based on individualized answers, relationships, experiences, or demeanor that are not proxies for sex.

**First controlling authority:** J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., 511 U.S. 127 (1994), decided April 19, 1994, Sex discrimination in peremptory challenges; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., 511 U.S. 127 (1994), decided April 19, 1994, Sex discrimination in peremptory challenges; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., 511 U.S. 127 (1994), April 19, 1994.

### Equal protection and sex-conscious restructuring

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs equal protection and sex-conscious restructuring?

**Current rule:** A public university may consider sex when restructuring a financially constrained athletic program to avoid perpetuating a demonstrated inequality in participation opportunities, when the choice is substantially related to that remedial objective and does not rest on stereotype or pretext. Correcting a demonstrated inequality in athletic opportunity is an important governmental objective. The university must establish a meaningful disparity, a genuine fiscal or programmatic restructuring, and a substantial relationship between the sex-conscious choice and preservation or correction of the underrepresented sex’s opportunities. Numerical imbalance alone is insufficient.

**Exceptions and limits:** The principle applies symmetrically to whichever sex is demonstrably underrepresented. It does not decide classifications involving sexual orientation, gender identity, transgender status, or eligibility for an individual sex-designated team.

**First controlling authority:** Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois, 35 F.3d 265, decided May 22, 1995, Equal protection and sex-conscious restructuring; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois, 35 F.3d 265, decided May 22, 1995, Equal protection and sex-conscious restructuring; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois, 35 F.3d 265, May 22, 1995.

### Violation-remedy nexus and interdistrict attraction

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs violation-remedy nexus and interdistrict attraction?

**Current rule:** A school-desegregation remedy may continue only insofar as it remains reasonably directed to eliminating an identified vestige of the constitutional violation to the extent practicable. An intradistrict violation does not authorize interdistrict attraction or reversal of demographic movement as an independent constitutional objective. For each challenged component, the district court must identify the constitutional violation or vestige addressed, determine whether the measure remains reasonably connected to eliminating it, and assess whether continued federal control is still necessary. Federal remedial authority is corrective rather than a general mandate to optimize a school system.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not categorically forbid salary measures, educational improvements, or other relief genuinely necessary to eliminate a proven vestige. It does not require premature termination or impose a fixed administrative checklist.

**First controlling authority:** Missouri v. Jenkins, 515 U.S. 70, decided June 12, 1995, Violation-remedy nexus and interdistrict attraction; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Missouri v. Jenkins, 515 U.S. 70, decided June 12, 1995, Violation-remedy nexus and interdistrict attraction; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Missouri v. Jenkins, 515 U.S. 70, June 12, 1995.

### Achievement benchmarks and incremental withdrawal of control

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs achievement benchmarks and incremental withdrawal of control?

**Current rule:** National achievement levels are not freestanding constitutional benchmarks for determining whether desegregation remedies must continue. Federal control must be withdrawn incrementally from separable areas in which compliance is complete and the relevant vestige has been eliminated to the extent practicable. Academic performance may be relevant evidence when prior de jure segregation caused an identified educational deficit, but the constitutional question is whether that attributable vestige has been remedied to the practicable extent, not whether students have reached national averages or the district’s maximum potential.

**Exceptions and limits:** Test scores and other educational evidence remain relevant when tied to a proven vestige. The Court does not determine on the present record which particular programs must end or continue.

**First controlling authority:** Missouri v. Jenkins, 515 U.S. 70, decided June 12, 1995, Achievement benchmarks and incremental withdrawal of control; established the current rule.

### Federal nonexclusive racial access measures

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs federal nonexclusive racial access measures?

**Current rule:** A genuinely remedial federal measure that expands access without making race decisive for a discrete contract, payment, or comparable benefit may be reviewed for an important remedial objective and a substantial relationship between the measure and that objective. The measure must address identified exclusion rather than generalized societal discrimination, racial balance for its own sake, or permanent racial administration. Formal labels and nominal percentages do not control; the measure’s actual legal operation does.

**Exceptions and limits:** Every adverse racial exclusion and every race-determinative allocation remains outside this proposition. The Court does not establish a fixed multi-part gateway.

**Related current rules:** Scrutiny of race-predominant districts, under Equal Protection, applies this remedial-access principle to district-specific redistricting remedies after racial predominance is established.

### Race-determinative federal governmental benefits

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs race-determinative federal governmental benefits?

**Current rule:** Strict scrutiny governs when race determines or effectively determines who receives a particular government contract, payment, or comparable discrete benefit. Government must establish a compelling interest and narrow tailoring. The inquiry includes the identified remedial predicate, necessity, the operation and duration of the classification, workable race-neutral alternatives, and whether the racial rule reaches materially beyond the demonstrated injury.

**Exceptions and limits:** The Court does not decide whether the compensation clause operated in this manner or whether it could survive strict scrutiny on a developed record.

**Related current rules:** Scrutiny of race-predominant districts, under Equal Protection, applies the unsupported-allocation side of this distinction to racial maximization, unnecessary packing or separation, and other unsupported racial sorting.

**First controlling authority:** Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña, 515 U.S. 200, decided June 12, 1995, Race-determinative governmental benefits; established the current rule.

### Racial predominance in districting

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs racial predominance in districting?

**Current rule:** Race is predominant when racial considerations materially subordinate traditional race-neutral districting principles in the particular district. Courts examine the actual design process and boundaries, including who was included or excluded, boundary movement, splitting or combining communities and political subdivisions, compactness, contiguity, communities of interest, ordinary political geography, and whether a racial target displaced those ordinary considerations. Geometric bizarreness is relevant evidence but not a threshold element.

**Exceptions and limits:** Racial awareness, demographic forecasting, or compliance analysis does not alone establish predominance. The inquiry remains district-specific and turns on whether race materially subordinated the principles that otherwise would have shaped the district.

**Related current rules:** Scrutiny of race-predominant districts, under Equal Protection, supplies the next analytical stage after predominance is established.

**First controlling authority:** Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson, 515 U.S. 900, decided June 29, 1995, Predominance threshold; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson, 515 U.S. 900, decided June 29, 1995, Predominance threshold; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson, 515 U.S. 900, June 29, 1995.

### Scrutiny of race-predominant districts

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs scrutiny of race-predominant districts?

**Current rule:** Once race predominates, a genuinely fitted, district-specific remedy for a demonstrated constitutional or Voting Rights Act violation receives intermediate scrutiny. Under that lane, the State must establish an important remedial objective grounded in the demonstrated violation and a substantial, district-specific fit between the racial design and the injury. A race-predominant district lacking that remedial predicate, or driven by racial maximization, unnecessary packing or separation, or unsupported racial allocation, receives strict scrutiny, requiring a compelling interest and narrow tailoring.

**Burdens and proof:** After predominance is shown, the State bears the burden of establishing the qualifying remedial lane and satisfying the corresponding scrutiny.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** First determine racial predominance. If predominance is established, classify the district as a genuinely fitted remedial measure or unsupported racial sorting, then apply intermediate or strict scrutiny accordingly.

**Exceptions and limits:** The Court did not decide whether Georgia’s Eleventh District was constitutional or unconstitutional. The classification and applicable scrutiny remained for remand. Racial awareness, demographic forecasting, and compliance analysis alone do not establish predominance.

**Related current rules:** Racial predominance in districting supplies the threshold. Federal nonexclusive racial access measures and Race-determinative federal governmental benefits, under Equal Protection, supply the Adarand operational distinction applied in the districting setting.

**First controlling authority:** Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson, 515 U.S. 900, decided June 29, 1995, Two scrutiny lanes after race predominates; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson, 515 U.S. 900, decided June 29, 1995, Two scrutiny lanes after race predominates; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson, 515 U.S. 900, June 29, 1995.

## Elections and Voting

### Objective comparator for governing-body-size claims under Voting Rights Act § 2

**Governing question:** When a challenge to the size of a governing body is cognizable under Voting Rights Act § 2.

**Current rule:** A governing-body-size claim is cognizable under § 2 when the plaintiff identifies a concrete and workable alternative structure grounded in objective law, governmental practice, history, or a closely comparable institution. Comparator sufficiency permits the statutory comparison but does not itself prove vote dilution.

**Burdens and proof:** The plaintiff retains the existing burden to establish the Gingles predicates and liability under the statutory totality.

**Exceptions and limits:** The comparator requirement is a comparative merits prerequisite, not an Article III bar, and it creates no right to proportional representation.

**Unresolved interaction or authority boundary:** The judgment vacating the particular liability finding had no controlling rationale under Marks; the majority-supported comparator rule remains controlling.

### Voting Rights Act § 2 totality, proportionality, and nonmaximization

**Governing question:** How Gingles preconditions, proportionality, and the ability to draw additional majority-minority districts bear on § 2 vote-dilution liability.

**Current rule:** The Gingles preconditions are necessary but not invariably sufficient to establish § 2 vote dilution. The court must determine under the full statutory totality whether protected voters have less opportunity than other members of the electorate to participate effectively and elect representatives of choice. Rough proportionality is relevant evidence but neither dispositive nor a safe harbor, and the ability to draw additional majority-minority districts does not by itself establish dilution because § 2 does not require maximization.

**Exceptions and limits:** Districts counted in a proportionality comparison must operate as genuine electoral opportunities. The rule creates no proportional-representation entitlement and leaves influence-district claims unresolved.

### Anonymous political leaflets

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs anonymous political leaflets?

**Current rule:** Government may not require every person distributing independent campaign literature to identify herself on the face of the communication when the rule broadly prohibits anonymous core political advocacy and is not narrowly fitted to fraud or corruption. A content-defined identification mandate imposed directly on independent political advocacy receives exacting review, requiring an overriding interest and narrow fit to the activity producing the asserted harm.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not invalidate disclosure rules governing candidates, political committees, coordinated expenditures, substantial campaign financing, or fraudulent impersonation. Those settings implicate interests and institutional relationships not present in McIntyre’s independent leafletting.

### Responsive financing and the independent speaker

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs responsive financing and the independent speaker?

**Current rule:** A public-financing system does not burden an independent expenditure merely because that expenditure triggers additional public funds or a higher voluntary spending limit for the candidate opposed, where the independent speaker remains legally free to spend and incurs no restriction, charge, delay, reporting duty, or other legal disability. The First Amendment protects an independent speaker against governmental suppression of the speaker’s own advocacy. It does not provide a right to prevent another electoral participant from receiving lawful resources with which to respond, or to preserve the original expenditure’s strategic value against responsive speech.

**Exceptions and limits:** A different question would arise if a trigger reduced the independent speaker’s rights, imposed a fee or new disclosure obligation because of the message, discriminated according to identity or viewpoint, or provided support so disproportionate that the program operated as a device for suppressing disfavored advocacy.

**First controlling authority:** Day v. Holahan, 34 F.3d 1356, decided May 15, 1995, Responsive financing and the independent speaker; established the current rule.

### State-imposed qualifications for Congress

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs state-imposed qualifications for Congress?

**Current rule:** A State may not condition ordinary congressional ballot access on prior congressional service when the restriction operates as an additional substantive qualification for an otherwise constitutionally eligible candidate. ## First Amendment

### Public-employee discipline based on a reasonable factual understanding

**Governing question:** Which factual version of disputed employee speech a public employer may use when applying the public-employee speech doctrine.

**Current rule:** A public employer may act on a version of disputed speech it genuinely believed when that factual understanding was objectively reasonable in light of information reasonably available to the decisionmaker. The inquiry into how the employer investigated is evidence of actual belief, reasonableness, and pretext, not an independent First Amendment hearing code. Pickering and Connick then apply to the speech as permissibly understood.

**Burdens and proof:** The employer must establish actual reliance on a genuinely held and objectively reasonable factual understanding.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not protect a fabricated reason, an asserted belief the employer did not hold, an objectively unsupported factual premise, or pretext.

**First controlling authority:** Waters v. Churchill, 511 U.S. 661 (1994), decided May 31, 1994, Permissible factual basis for public-employee discipline; established the current rule.

### Near-total elimination of the residential-sign medium

**Governing question:** When a municipality may broadly prohibit signs displayed at a resident’s own home.

**Current rule:** A municipality violates the First Amendment when it nearly eliminates residential signs and leaves no realistic equivalent for the inexpensive, location-linked expression conveyed by a sign at one’s own home. Other media are not adequate substitutes when they cannot communicate the same personal message from the residence itself.

**Exceptions and limits:** A municipality may impose reasonable content-neutral rules governing size, number, placement, duration, illumination, and safety, and no one has a right to place a sign on another person’s property.

### Cable must-carry classification and intermediate scrutiny

**Governing question:** What First Amendment scrutiny governs content-neutral cable must-carry obligations and how legislative predictions and editorial burdens are reviewed.

**Current rule:** Cable must-carry obligations keyed to broadcast status and transmission method rather than viewpoint or program subject are content neutral and receive intermediate scrutiny. The government must establish an important or substantial interest unrelated to suppressing expression and a reasonable fit that does not burden substantially more protected editorial discretion than necessary; least-restrictive-means analysis is not required. Courts may respect adequately supported legislative predictions in complex markets while independently reviewing the actual magnitude and distribution of compelled editorial displacement and the fit between that burden and the established interests.

**Burdens and proof:** The government bears the burden of establishing the substantial interest and reasonable fit.

### Provision-specific review of content-neutral speech injunctions

**Governing question:** What fit is required for a content-neutral injunction directed at identified speakers and enforced by contempt.

**Current rule:** A content-neutral injunction directed at identified speakers must be reviewed provision by provision. Each restraint must be connected to demonstrated unlawful conduct and burden no more speech than necessary to remedy that conduct and protect the significant interests affected by it. The injunction need not use the least restrictive conceivable wording, but contempt-backed relief may not exceed what is necessary to provide complete lawful protection.

### Truthful professional credentials and potentially misleading advertising

### Constitutional access to executive criminal-justice records

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs constitutional access to executive criminal-justice records?

**Current rule:** The First Amendment does not create a general right to inspect executive-branch criminal-justice records merely because the information could facilitate protected speech. Ordinary access to executive records begins with enacted disclosure law. A constitutional access right attaches only within a separately established category, such as a judicial proceeding or record satisfying the governing historical and structural requirements. A desire to speak using government-held information does not itself compel disclosure.

### Selective denial based on intended commercial speech

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs selective denial based on intended commercial speech?

**Current rule:** Once a State establishes an access regime, it may not categorically deny otherwise available records solely because the requester intends truthful, lawful commercial solicitation unless the use-based restriction satisfies the commercial-speech standard. The absence of a freestanding access entitlement does not remove a discriminatory access condition from First Amendment review.

### Alcohol-content information on beer labels

### Compelled inclusion in a private parade

### Truthful targeted direct-mail solicitation

**Current rule:** Government may not categorically prohibit truthful, nonmisleading targeted direct-mail solicitation for a fixed post-event period merely because recipients or the public may regard prompt solicitation as offensive or undignified. ### Equal civic access and direct financing of devotional activity

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs equal civic access and direct financing of devotional activity?

**Current rule:** The Free Speech Clause protects religious speakers against exclusion from genuinely opened civic and expressive channels, but it does not require a public university to use mandatory student-fee funds to pay directly for the production of devotional instruction, evangelism, or religious formation. ## Fourth Amendment

### Deadly force and asserted less-deadly alternatives

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs deadly force and asserted less-deadly alternatives?

**Current rule:** The Fourth Amendment does not require an officer to exhaust every feasible less-deadly tactic before using otherwise reasonable deadly force. Earlier police conduct may bear on the totality when officers unreasonably create or materially intensify the need for force, but the record here permits no reasonable finding that Drinski did so. Reasonableness is assessed without hindsight from the circumstances confronting the officer. Availability of another tactic is relevant evidence only insofar as it bears on whether the force actually used was objectively unreasonable. It does not create a least-deadly-means requirement or authorize courts to direct tactical sequencing after the event.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding does not protect an officer who needlessly manufactures a confrontation, uses force after the threat has ended, or relies on earlier unlawful conduct that proximately creates the asserted necessity.

**First controlling authority:** Plakas v. Drinski, 19 F.3d 1143, decided January 9, 1995, Deadly force and asserted less-deadly alternatives; established the current rule.

### Objectively reasonable reliance on a court-maintained warrant record

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs objectively reasonable reliance on a court-maintained warrant record?

**Current rule:** The exclusionary rule does not require suppression when an officer makes an arrest in objectively reasonable reliance on a court-maintained record that incorrectly reports an outstanding warrant, where the record identifies an isolated court-clerical failure and shows no police participation or recurring systemic unreliability. The court considers whether exclusion could materially deter the governmental actors responsible for the error and whether the officer executing the apparent warrant acted objectively reasonably. The holding does not create a general exception for inaccurate databases.

**Exceptions and limits:** The Court does not decide errors generated or maintained by police, recurring inaccuracies known to law enforcement, reckless recordkeeping, or a system whose unreliability makes officer reliance objectively unreasonable.

**First controlling authority:** Arizona v. Evans, 514 U.S. 1, decided March 1, 1995, Objectively reasonable reliance on a court-maintained warrant record; established the current rule.

### Technological acquisition of information from inside a home

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs technological acquisition of information from inside a home?

**Current rule:** Law-enforcement use of sense-enhancing technology from outside a home to obtain information about activity within the home that ordinary public observation could not otherwise acquire constitutes a Fourth Amendment search. The thermal scan of Pinson’s residence was a search. Physical entry is unnecessary when officers deliberately use technological means to obtain otherwise unavailable information concerning activity behind the walls of a home. The constitutional inquiry does not depend on whether the instrument detects light, sound, heat, or another physical signal.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary visual observation from a lawful vantage remains unaffected. The decision does not address smoke, odor, or other conditions perceptible without specialized technology; utility records obtained through lawful process; every technological measurement near private property; or surveillance that reveals no information concerning activity inside a home. The Court expresses no view on the remaining affidavit, probable cause, suppression, or *United States v. Leon*.

### Knock and announce as a component of reasonableness

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs knock and announce as a component of reasonableness?

**Current rule:** The common-law requirement that officers announce their identity and authority before forcibly entering a home forms part of the Fourth Amendment’s reasonableness inquiry. Announcement is ordinarily required before forcible residential entry. It may be excused when case-specific circumstances give officers adequate reason to believe that announcement would create danger, be futile, permit escape, or lead to destruction of evidence.

### Privacy in a rental vehicle

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs privacy in a rental vehicle?

**Current rule:** A driver’s absence from a rental agreement does not by itself defeat a legitimate expectation of privacy when the driver possesses and controls the vehicle with the authorized renter’s permission and without theft, fraud, or comparable wrongful possession. ## Commerce Clause

### Origin-based charges and compensatory-burden defense

**Governing question:** When a state charge triggered by out-of-state origin survives dormant Commerce Clause review as compensatory.

**Current rule:** A charge triggered expressly by interstate origin is presumptively invalid. A compensatory defense requires identification of an actual intrastate tax or burden imposed on a substantially equivalent in-state event for the same governmental purpose; generalized estimates of additional public costs do not substitute for that corresponding burden.

**Burdens and proof:** The State bears the demanding burden of justifying the facial discrimination and establishing the substantially equivalent intrastate burden.

**Exceptions and limits:** The State may impose uniform charges, regulate genuine health or environmental risks evenhandedly, and recover demonstrable costs through nondiscriminatory measures.

**First controlling authority:** Oregon Waste Systems, Inc. v. Department of Environmental Quality, 511 U.S. 93 (1994), decided April 4, 1994, Origin-based waste surcharge; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Oregon Waste Systems, Inc. v. Department of Environmental Quality, 511 U.S. 93 (1994), decided April 4, 1994, Origin-based waste surcharge; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Oregon Waste Systems, Inc. v. Department of Environmental Quality, 511 U.S. 93 (1994), April 4, 1994.

### Operational discrimination favoring a local private enterprise

**Governing question:** When a formally local routing requirement discriminates against interstate commerce by reserving a market for a favored private enterprise.

**Current rule:** A law is discriminatory when its practical operation directs commerce to a favored local private enterprise, even if disfavored local firms are excluded alongside out-of-state firms. Such a law is invalid unless the government establishes a legitimate local objective that cannot adequately be served through reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives.

**Burdens and proof:** The government bears the burden of showing both the legitimate local objective and the inadequacy of reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not condemn evenhanded health and safety regulation or decide a materially different arrangement involving a genuinely public facility and distinct governmental-market circumstances.

**First controlling authority:** C & A Carbone, Inc. v. Town of Clarkstown, 511 U.S. 383 (1994), decided May 16, 1994, Exclusive local processing requirement; established the current rule.

### Linked market assessment and local-producer subsidy

**Governing question:** When a facially evenhanded market assessment becomes discriminatory because of a formally linked local-only distribution.

**Current rule:** A State discriminates against interstate commerce when it links an otherwise evenhanded market assessment to distribution of the entire resulting fund exclusively to in-state producers competing in the assessed market, so that the integrated program shifts the economic burden away from local producers and onto their interstate competitors. Formally linked components are evaluated as one economic arrangement.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not invalidate an evenhanded tax merely because revenue enters the general treasury, prohibit local subsidies financed from general revenues, or constitutionalize ordinary public-spending choices.

### Sales tax on an interstate transportation ticket

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs sales tax on an interstate transportation ticket?

**Current rule:** A conventional sales tax may be measured by the full price of an interstate transportation service when the taxable event is a discrete local sale, the tax is internally consistent, the State’s claim to the sale is externally consistent, interstate and intrastate transactions receive equal treatment, and the tax is fairly related to state-provided services. Apportionment is not required merely because performance of the purchased service later crosses state lines.

### Federal regulation of noneconomic local possession

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs federal regulation of noneconomic local possession?

**Current rule:** The Commerce Clause does not authorize a stand-alone federal prohibition of noneconomic local firearm possession near schools based only on an attenuated chain from violence, to education, to productivity, and finally to the national economy. Congress may regulate the channels of interstate commerce; instrumentalities, persons, and things in interstate commerce, including intrastate threats to them; and economic activity whose properly defined class substantially affects an actual interstate market. Congress may also reach local conduct whose inclusion is reasonably necessary to a comprehensive and otherwise valid regulation of interstate economic activity. It may not aggregate noneconomic local conduct through a chain of generalized social and economic consequences that would erase the distinction between enumerated commerce authority and a general police power.

**Exceptions and limits:** The judgment creates no constitutional right to possess a firearm near a school. States retain general police authority over weapons, schools, and local crime. ### Direct protection of an interstate transportation instrumentality

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs direct protection of an interstate transportation instrumentality?

**Current rule:** The Commerce Clause permits Congress to protect a motor vehicle against a direct intrastate forcible taking when the federal offense requires proof of the particular vehicle’s interstate connection. ## Separation of Powers

### Legislative reopening of final Article III judgments

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs legislative reopening of final Article III judgments?

**Current rule:** Congress violates Article III when it commands federal courts to reopen final judgments in private civil actions and replace them with dispositions required by subsequently enacted law. Congress may change substantive law and apply it to pending or nonfinal cases, regulate future conduct, alter the prospective operation of continuing equitable relief, and enact lawful rules governing judicial relief. Once a private judgment has become final within the Article III hierarchy, Congress may not direct a federal court to reopen that judgment and decide the completed case differently.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding does not prevent new law from governing pending cases, lawful prospective legislation, modification of continuing injunctions when governing law changes, waiver of federal claims or defenses, or generally applicable reopening rules that operate consistently with Article III.

**First controlling authority:** Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc., 514 U.S. 211, decided April 18, 1995, Legislative reopening of final Article III judgments; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc., 514 U.S. 211, decided April 18, 1995, Legislative reopening of final Article III judgments; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc., 514 U.S. 211, April 18, 1995.

### Congressional influence and legally operative control

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs congressional influence and legally operative control?

**Current rule:** Congressional officers or agents do not violate separation of powers merely by receiving information, attending meetings, advising, observing, reporting, or attempting to persuade an executive or operational decisionmaker. Congress crosses the constitutional line when it gives itself or its agents legally operative authority to approve, disapprove, suspend, delay, alter, or condition execution outside the constitutional legislative process. The inquiry turns on the legal consequence of the asserted authority, not solely on the identity of the participant, the presence or absence of a formal vote, or the practical likelihood that political influence will be effective.

**Exceptions and limits:** Congressional affiliation, consultation, advice, observation, political pressure, and ordinary oversight are not independently unconstitutional. Congress may regulate the airports through enacted law.

**First controlling authority:** Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority v. Hechinger, 36 F.3d 97, decided June 5, 1995, Congressional influence and legally operative control; established the current rule.

### Report-and-wait mechanisms and bicameralism

### Timely Appointments Clause challenge

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs timely Appointments Clause challenge?

**Current rule:** A litigant who timely challenges the unconstitutional appointment of officers adjudicating his own case is entitled to a fresh adjudication by lawfully appointed officers. The de facto officer doctrine does not validate the defective panel’s judgment against that litigant. ## Constitutional Attribution

### Government-created and controlled corporations

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs government-created and controlled corporations?

**Current rule:** A corporation created by special federal law to advance governmental objectives and permanently controlled through federal appointment of a majority of its directors is part of the Government for purposes of constitutional rights, notwithstanding a statutory declaration that it is not an agency. Governmental creation, governmental objectives, and permanent governmental control establish constitutional responsibility. Funding, regulation, chartering, or temporary participation alone does not.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not convert every federally chartered, subsidized, or heavily regulated corporation into a governmental actor. Nor does it decide whether the billboard was a public forum or whether Amtrak’s particular restriction survives First Amendment review.

## Property and Takings

### Essential nexus and rough proportionality for adjudicative property exactions

**Governing question:** What relationship must exist between an adjudicative permit exaction of a property interest and a proposed development’s impacts.

**Current rule:** A permit condition compelling dedication of a property interest must bear an essential nexus and rough proportionality, in nature and extent, to impacts attributable to the proposed development. ## Bankruptcy

### Reasonably equivalent value after mortgage foreclosure

**Governing question:** How a bankruptcy court determines reasonably equivalent value after a regularly conducted mortgage foreclosure.

**Current rule:** A regularly conducted, noncollusive foreclosure sale is substantial evidence of value under forced-sale conditions, but the sale price and compliance with state foreclosure law do not conclusively establish reasonably equivalent value under 11 U.S.C. § 548(a)(2). ## Arbitration

### Choice of law and arbitral punitive damages

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs choice of law and arbitral punitive damages?

**Current rule:** A general clause selecting New York law does not clearly withdraw punitive damages from a broad arbitration agreement that otherwise authorizes the arbitrators to resolve the controversy and award available relief. A general choice-of-law clause ordinarily selects the substantive rules governing the parties’ rights. A state rule specially allocating decisionmaking authority between courts and arbitrators is incorporated only when the agreement expresses that limitation clearly. The contract must be read as a whole, and unresolved ambiguity in a standard form is construed against the drafter.

**Exceptions and limits:** An express exclusion of punitive damages or a clear reservation of that remedy to courts would control. The decision does not authorize a remedy forbidden by the governing substantive law.

### Commerce coverage under FAA § 2

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs commerce coverage under FAA § 2?

**Current rule:** Section 2 reaches contracts evidencing transactions within the full extent of Congress’s lawful commerce authority, and coverage depends on the transaction’s actual commercial operation rather than whether the parties contemplated an interstate connection. The phrase involving commerce invokes the breadth of Congress’s commerce power. A court determines coverage from commerce in fact, while preserving generally applicable grounds for revoking the contract under § 2’s saving clause.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision defines the statutory reach of § 2 but does not define the outer constitutional boundary of the Commerce Clause. It does not hold that an otherwise local transaction becomes federal merely because an incidental object crossed a state line at some earlier point.

### Prior consistent statements and the premotive requirement

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs prior consistent statements and the premotive requirement?

**Current rule:** Rule 801(d)(1)(B) admits a prior consistent statement to rebut recent fabrication or improper influence or motive only when the statement was made before the alleged fabrication, influence, or motive arose. ## Criminal Law and Sentencing

### Willfulness in the federal anti-structuring offense

**Governing question:** What mental state the willfulness requirement adds to the federal anti-structuring offense.

**Current rule:** Conviction under 31 U.S.C. §§ 5322 and 5324 requires proof that the defendant knew the structuring was unlawful, in addition to proof that the defendant intended to evade the financial institution’s reporting duty. The Government need not prove knowledge of the precise statutory citation or every detail of the reporting scheme.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule is tied to the text and context of the currency-reporting subchapter and creates no general ignorance-of-law defense.

### Mens rea for operative facts distinguishing lawful conduct from serious crime

**Governing question:** When statutory silence leaves uncertain whether mens rea reaches the physical characteristic or operative fact that separates familiar or potentially lawful conduct from a serious crime.

**Current rule:** When otherwise familiar or potentially lawful conduct becomes seriously criminal only because of a particular physical characteristic or operative fact, statutory silence does not displace the traditional mens rea presumption. The Government must prove knowledge of the characteristic or fact that makes the conduct unlawful, though not knowledge of the statute, legal classification, or precise legal boundary unless the enacted text separately requires it.

**Burdens and proof:** The Government must prove the required knowledge beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not govern unmistakable contraband or conduct whose unlawful character is evident from possession or commission. It does not create a general ignorance-of-law defense.

**First controlling authority:** Staples v. United States, 511 U.S. 600 (1994), decided May 23, 1994, Mens rea for the regulated characteristic; established knowledge of the physical characteristic making an otherwise familiar firearm a machinegun.

**Controlling authorities:** Staples v. United States, 511 U.S. 600 (1994), decided May 23, 1994, supplies the physical-characteristic component. United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., 513 U.S. 64, decided November 29, 1994, Scienter for the facts making a depiction unlawful, extends the presumption across statutory clauses to operative facts distinguishing lawful material from a serious crime.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., 513 U.S. 64, November 29, 1994.

**Development:**
- May 23, 1994, Staples v. United States: The Court required knowledge of the physical characteristic that made an otherwise familiar firearm a machinegun, while leaving broader operative-fact applications unresolved.
- November 29, 1994, United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc.: The Court extended the same mens-rea principle across intervening statutory clauses to require knowledge that a depiction was sexually explicit and involved a minor. The rule remains limited to facts that distinguish familiar or potentially lawful conduct from serious crime.

### Collateral attack on Armed Career Criminal Act predicates

**Governing question:** Which constitutional defects in an outstanding state conviction may be litigated during federal ACCA sentencing.

**Current rule:** A defendant may challenge an ACCA predicate during federal sentencing when the conviction was obtained after a complete denial of the Gideon right to counsel. Other constitutional challenges to an outstanding state conviction, including ineffective assistance and involuntary-plea claims, must first succeed through an authorized direct or collateral proceeding.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** The federal sentencing court determines whether an existing conviction qualifies; fact-intensive collateral challenges proceed in the convicting jurisdiction unless Congress supplies a broader sentencing-stage review mechanism.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule leaves open the procedure and disposition when a predicate is later vacated.

### Use of valid uncounseled fine-only misdemeanors in later sentencing

**Governing question:** When a valid uncounseled misdemeanor that resulted in no imprisonment may affect a later custodial sentence.

**Current rule:** A sentencing scheme may consider a valid fine-only uncounseled misdemeanor when the defendant has a meaningful opportunity to challenge the information’s accuracy and sentencing significance and the court has authority to correct a criminal-history category that materially overstates the defendant’s conduct or risk. ### Overt acts under 21 U.S.C. § 846

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs overt acts under 21 U.S.C. § 846?

**Current rule:** Section 846 does not require the Government to allege or prove an overt act in furtherance of the conspiracy. A narcotics conspiracy under § 846 is complete upon formation of the unlawful agreement. The Government must prove the agreement and the defendant’s knowing participation beyond a reasonable doubt, but no additional overt act is an element.

### Mens rea and fair notice under the then-operative § 2119

### Federal courts under 18 U.S.C. § 1001

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs federal courts under 18 U.S.C. § 1001?

**Current rule:** A federal court is neither a department nor an agency within § 1001, and the statute therefore does not criminalize false statements on the theory that a judicial proceeding is a matter within the jurisdiction of a federal department or agency. The terms department and agency in § 1001 are governed by their ordinary executive-branch meaning and the definitions applicable throughout Title 18. A federal court does not change character depending on whether it performs adjudicative, administrative, or housekeeping work.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not protect false statements made to executive agencies merely because litigation is pending. Congress may enact a different rule expressly covering the Judicial and Legislative Branches and may define exceptions appropriate to adversarial proceedings.

### Nexus between an obstructive act and a pending proceeding

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs nexus between an obstructive act and a pending proceeding?

**Current rule:** False statements to investigators do not violate § 1503 absent proof that the defendant knew the statements were likely to reach and obstruct a pending judicial or grand-jury proceeding. The alleged endeavor must have a relationship in time, causation, or logic to the proceeding. The defendant must know that the conduct is likely to affect that proceeding, and the conduct must possess the natural and probable effect of interference. An intent to mislead an investigation that remains independent of the court or grand jury is insufficient.

**Exceptions and limits:** Direct lies to a grand jury, destruction of subpoenaed material, witness tampering, or statements knowingly routed to a pending proceeding remain within § 1503 when the other elements are proved.

**First controlling authority:** United States v. Aguilar, 515 U.S. 593, decided June 21, 1995, Nexus between an obstructive act and a pending proceeding; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** United States v. Aguilar, 515 U.S. 593, decided June 21, 1995, Nexus between an obstructive act and a pending proceeding; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Aguilar, 515 U.S. 593, June 21, 1995.

### Disclosure after expiration of a wiretap authorization

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs disclosure after expiration of a wiretap authorization?

**Current rule:** Section 2232(c) reaches a disclosure made after the identified authorization expires when the defendant knew of the application or authorization, gave notice of the possible interception, and acted with the specific purpose of obstructing the interception. The statute does not require the application or authorization to remain formally pending at the moment of disclosure. “Such interception” identifies the interception associated with the application or authorization known to the defendant. “Possible interception” accounts for uncertainty about whether interception will occur.

**Exceptions and limits:** The Government must prove both knowledge and the specific obstructive purpose. Disclosure unrelated to an application, authorization, or possible interception is not covered.

## Criminal Procedure

### Objective custody under Miranda

**Governing question:** What role an officer’s undisclosed suspicions or communicated views play in determining Miranda custody.

**Current rule:** Miranda custody turns on the objective circumstances manifested to the person questioned and whether those circumstances impose restraint comparable to formal arrest. An officer’s undisclosed belief that the person is or is not a suspect is legally irrelevant. Communicated suspicion is relevant only insofar as it would affect a reasonable person’s understanding of the freedom to end the encounter and leave, and it is not automatically dispositive.

**Exceptions and limits:** An undisclosed view may be used to test credibility concerning what occurred, but it does not itself enter the custody standard.

**First controlling authority:** Stansbury v. California, 511 U.S. 318 (1994), decided April 26, 1994, Objective custody under Miranda; established the current rule.

### Neutral clarification of an ambiguous request for counsel

**Governing question:** What officers must do when a reasonable officer would understand a suspect’s words as a genuine but ambiguous possible request for counsel after a Miranda waiver.

**Current rule:** Substantive interrogation must pause while officers ask neutral questions directed solely to whether counsel is being requested. A clear invocation ends interrogation under Edwards; a clear disclaimer permits substantive questioning to resume. **Exceptions and limits:** A passing reference that no reasonable officer would understand as a possible request does not activate the clarification requirement.

### Civil and criminal classification of contempt fines

**Governing question:** When a contempt fine is civil rather than criminal and what process a serious criminal fine requires.

**Current rule:** A contempt fine is civil when it compensates proved loss or coerces present compliance while leaving the contemnor a meaningful opportunity to avoid or purge the sanction. A fixed, determinate, noncompensatory, nonpurgeable fine for completed misconduct is punitive and therefore criminal. Serious criminal contempt fines may be imposed only through criminal proceedings that include the right to a jury trial.

**Exceptions and limits:** Courts retain summary authority over petty direct contempt necessary to maintain courtroom order and may use genuinely purgeable coercive sanctions or compensation tied to proved loss.

### Waiver of the plea-statement exclusions

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs waiver of the plea-statement exclusions?

**Current rule:** Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) do not categorically prohibit enforcement of a pre-proffer agreement permitting plea-discussion statements to be used to impeach the defendant’s inconsistent testimony. Evidentiary protections are ordinarily waivable. An impeachment waiver is enforceable unless the defendant establishes an affirmative basis for concluding that the agreement resulted from fraud, coercion, lack of understanding, or involuntariness.

### Cumulative Brady materiality and prosecution-team responsibility

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs cumulative Brady materiality and prosecution-team responsibility?

**Current rule:** The State violates *Brady* when favorable evidence suppressed by the prosecution team, considered collectively in the context of the complete record, creates a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict. The suppressed evidence in Kyles’s case satisfies that standard. Materiality does not require proof by a preponderance that disclosure would have produced acquittal, and it is not a sufficiency-of-the-remaining-evidence inquiry. Courts must evaluate the net effect of all suppressed favorable evidence together. The prosecutor bears responsibility for favorable evidence known to police and other government actors participating in the investigation, whether or not those actors communicated it to the trial prosecutor. Once *Brady* materiality is established, no separate harmless-error inquiry follows.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not require open-file discovery, make every nondisclosure structural error, or relieve the defense of ordinary responsibility for evidence already known and available to it.

**First controlling authority:** Kyles v. Whitley, 514 U.S. 419, decided April 19, 1995, Cumulative Brady materiality and prosecution-team responsibility; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Kyles v. Whitley, 514 U.S. 419, decided April 19, 1995, Cumulative Brady materiality and prosecution-team responsibility; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Kyles v. Whitley, 514 U.S. 419, April 19, 1995.

### Jury determination of materiality

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs jury determination of materiality?

**Current rule:** When materiality is an element of a charged false-statement offense, the Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to determine materiality beyond a reasonable doubt. The judge states the governing legal definition of materiality. The jury determines whether the charged statement, under the proved facts, had the natural tendency or capacity to influence the governmental decision.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not assign abstract statutory interpretation or other pure questions of law to juries. It does not decide whether materiality is an element under materially different statutory language.

## Habeas Corpus

### Actual innocence as a gateway to defaulted trial claims

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs actual innocence as a gateway to defaulted trial claims?

**Current rule:** A petitioner asserting innocence of the offense may obtain review of otherwise barred constitutional trial claims by showing, in light of all reliable evidence, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable juror would have found guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. The habeas court considers the complete evidentiary record, including reliable evidence not presented at trial. It assesses how the new material changes the case a reasonable juror would evaluate. Timing, corroboration, credibility, the reason the evidence was unavailable, and any impairment caused by delay are relevant but nonexclusive considerations bearing on reliability and probability.

**Exceptions and limits:** Satisfaction of the gateway authorizes merits review only. It does not establish a freestanding constitutional innocence claim, shift the ultimate burden on the constitutional claims, or require release.

**First controlling authority:** Schlup v. Delo, 513 U.S. 298, decided January 23, 1995, Actual innocence as a gateway to defaulted trial claims; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Schlup v. Delo, 513 U.S. 298, decided January 23, 1995, Actual innocence as a gateway to defaulted trial claims; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Schlup v. Delo, 513 U.S. 298, January 23, 1995.

### Grave doubt under the federal habeas harmless-error standard

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs grave doubt under the federal habeas harmless-error standard?

**Current rule:** When a federal habeas court finds constitutional trial error and remains in grave doubt about whether the error had a substantial and injurious effect or influence on the verdict, the error is not harmless. Grave doubt exists when, after a conscientious examination of the complete record, the reviewing judge is in virtual equipoise about the error’s effect on the verdict. The rule does not permit a court to avoid making a difficult judgment when the evidence supports a reasoned conclusion one way or the other.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not shift the burden on every habeas issue, alter the substantive *Brecht* standard, decide structural error, or establish that O’Neal’s instruction was unconstitutional.

**First controlling authority:** O’Neal v. McAninch, 513 U.S. 432, decided February 21, 1995, Grave doubt under the federal habeas harmless-error standard; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** O’Neal v. McAninch, 513 U.S. 432, decided February 21, 1995, Grave doubt under the federal habeas harmless-error standard; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** O’Neal v. McAninch, 513 U.S. 432, February 21, 1995.

### Custody under consecutive sentences

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs custody under consecutive sentences?

**Current rule:** A prisoner serving consecutive sentences remains in custody under the conviction supporting an earlier sentence already served in sequence when invalidating that conviction would advance the date of release from the aggregate term. Consecutive sentences are treated as a continuous stream of custody for purposes of 28 U.S.C. § 2254. Formal completion of one segment does not defeat jurisdiction while that conviction continues to postpone release from the undischarged aggregate confinement.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not revive a challenge after all custody has fully ended when the petitioner relies solely on collateral consequences insufficient under otherwise governing law.

**First controlling authority:** Garlotte v. Fordice, 515 U.S. 39, decided May 30, 1995, Custody under consecutive sentences; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Garlotte v. Fordice, 515 U.S. 39, decided May 30, 1995, Custody under consecutive sentences; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Garlotte v. Fordice, 515 U.S. 39, May 30, 1995.

## Ex Post Facto

### Retrospective changes to parole-hearing frequency

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs retrospective changes to parole-hearing frequency?

**Current rule:** Applying California’s limited parole-hearing deferral provision to prisoners whose crimes predated its enactment does not violate the Ex Post Facto Clause because the provision creates only a speculative and attenuated possibility of increased punishment. A retrospective change in parole procedure violates the Ex Post Facto Clause when its design or operation creates a sufficient risk of increasing the measure of punishment attached to the covered crime. A merely conceivable or conjectural possibility is insufficient.

**Exceptions and limits:** A law that materially delays eligibility, removes meaningful reconsideration, or predictably lengthens confinement on a demonstrated record may present a different case.

**First controlling authority:** California Department of Corrections v. Morales, 514 U.S. 499, decided April 25, 1995, Retrospective changes to parole-hearing frequency; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** California Department of Corrections v. Morales, 514 U.S. 499, decided April 25, 1995, Retrospective changes to parole-hearing frequency; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** California Department of Corrections v. Morales, 514 U.S. 499, April 25, 1995.

## Double Jeopardy

### Punitive taxes imposed after criminal punishment

**Governing question:** When an assessment denominated a tax constitutes a second punishment for Double Jeopardy purposes.

**Current rule:** A tax designation receives substantial but not conclusive weight. A levy constitutes punishment when its legal design and practical operation depart sharply from ordinary revenue collection and tie the assessment to a completed criminal offense and prosecution. A separate punitive assessment may not follow criminal punishment for the same conduct.

**Exceptions and limits:** No single unusual feature is dispositive. The rule does not regulate ordinary tax rates, bar taxation of unlawful businesses, or classify remedial charges, interest, investigation costs, or conventional civil assessments as punishment merely because they follow misconduct.

**First controlling authority:** Department of Revenue of Montana v. Kurth Ranch, 511 U.S. 767 (1994), decided June 6, 1994, Punitive post-conviction drug tax; established the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Department of Revenue of Montana v. Kurth Ranch, 511 U.S. 767 (1994), June 6, 1994.

### Consecutive punishment under § 924(c)

**Current rule:** Congress authorized a consecutive § 924(c) sentence in addition to the sentence for carjacking under the then-operative § 2119, so the cumulative punishment imposed in this single prosecution does not violate the Double Jeopardy Clause.

## Eighth Amendment

### Deliberate indifference to a substantial custodial risk

**Governing question:** What knowledge and response establish an Eighth Amendment violation based on a serious risk of harm in custody.

**Current rule:** A prison official violates the Eighth Amendment when the official knows that an inmate faces an objectively substantial risk of serious harm and consciously disregards it by failing to take reasonable protective measures. Actual awareness may be proved directly or circumstantially, and a factfinder may infer knowledge from an obvious risk, but obviousness is evidence rather than an irrebuttable presumption.

**Burdens and proof:** The claimant must prove the objectively substantial risk, each defendant’s actual awareness, conscious disregard, causation, and individual role.

**Exceptions and limits:** An official who responds reasonably is not liable merely because the measures fail, and negligence or a classification mistake alone is insufficient.

**First controlling authority:** Farmer v. Brennan, 511 U.S. 825 (1994), decided June 6, 1994, Knowledge and disregard of a serious custodial risk; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Farmer v. Brennan, 511 U.S. 825 (1994), decided June 6, 1994, Knowledge and disregard of a serious custodial risk; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Farmer v. Brennan, 511 U.S. 825 (1994), June 6, 1994.

## Capital Sentencing

### Exclusion of another sentencer’s irrelevant death judgment

**Governing question:** Whether a capital jury independently choosing life or death may be told that another jury already sentenced the defendant to death.

**Current rule:** When a capital jury must independently choose between life and death, the State may not introduce the fact that another jury already imposed a death sentence when that prior sentence is irrelevant to a valid aggravating issue. The prior conviction and admissible underlying conduct may be proved, but another sentencer’s ultimate life-or-death judgment may not be used as aggravating evidence merely to suggest that execution has already been authoritatively approved.

**Consequence or recurring remedy:** Admission over objection requires a new penalty proceeding when it undermines the reliability and independent responsibility of the sentencing decision.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule leaves open a case in which the prior sentence itself bears on a valid statutory issue.

**First controlling authority:** Romano v. Oklahoma, 512 U.S. 1 (1994), decided June 13, 1994, Prior death sentence in an independent capital sentencing; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Romano v. Oklahoma, 512 U.S. 1 (1994), decided June 13, 1994, Prior death sentence in an independent capital sentencing; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Romano v. Oklahoma, 512 U.S. 1 (1994), June 13, 1994.

### Parole-ineligibility instruction when future dangerousness is argued

**Governing question:** When due process requires a capital jury to be instructed that a life sentence carries no possibility of parole.

**Current rule:** When the prosecution places future dangerousness at issue and the only noncapital sentence makes the defendant legally ineligible for parole, due process requires an accurate judicial instruction that a life sentence carries no possibility of parole.

**Consequence or recurring remedy:** Failure to give the instruction requires a new penalty proceeding when the jury was left with a materially false implication about eventual release.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not require predictions about discretionary parole when legal eligibility remains possible or contingent and does not dictate the weight the jury must assign to parole ineligibility.

**First controlling authority:** Simmons v. South Carolina, 512 U.S. 154 (1994), decided June 17, 1994, Parole-ineligibility instruction when future dangerousness is argued; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Simmons v. South Carolina, 512 U.S. 154 (1994), decided June 17, 1994, Parole-ineligibility instruction when future dangerousness is argued; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Simmons v. South Carolina, 512 U.S. 154 (1994), June 17, 1994.

### Eligibility narrowing and understandable selection factors

**Governing question:** What clarity and guidance the Eighth Amendment requires of factors used after a defendant has been made death eligible.

**Current rule:** Eligibility factors must genuinely narrow the class exposed to death and provide a principled boundary. Once eligibility has lawfully been established, selection factors may direct individualized consideration of relevant characteristics of the offense and offender. A selection factor must possess a common-sense core ordinary jurors can understand, but it need not yield a yes-or-no answer or carry predetermined aggravating or mitigating weight.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not approve a nonnarrowing eligibility factor, an instruction that forecloses relevant mitigation, or a label lacking an understandable core. Another sentencer’s irrelevant ultimate judgment remains governed by the separate rule excluding a prior death sentence.

**First controlling authority:** Tuilaepa v. California / Proctor v. California, 512 U.S. 967 (1994), decided June 30, 1994, Clarity and function of capital selection factors; established the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Tuilaepa v. California / Proctor v. California, 512 U.S. 967 (1994), June 30, 1994.

### Weight assigned to an advisory life recommendation

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs weight assigned to an advisory life recommendation?

**Current rule:** The Eighth Amendment does not require a State to prescribe a numerical weight, fixed verbal formula, or Florida-style limitation governing a trial judge’s consideration of an advisory jury recommendation of life. A State may place final capital-sentencing responsibility in the trial judge, require consideration of the jury’s recommendation, and rely on written findings and appellate review to guard against arbitrary sentencing. The Constitution does not make the recommendation binding or dictate the weight it must receive.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not permit refusal to consider relevant mitigation, reliance on an invalid aggravating circumstance, or a sentence unsupported by the findings and record. It does not decide a scheme in which the advisory verdict is disregarded altogether.

**First controlling authority:** Harris v. Alabama, 513 U.S. 504, decided February 22, 1995, Weight assigned to an advisory life recommendation; established the current rule.

## Labor and Employment

### Title VII hostile work environment without serious psychological injury

**Governing question:** What level of discriminatory workplace conduct and injury is required for a Title VII hostile-work-environment claim.

**Current rule:** A plaintiff need not prove serious psychological injury. The discriminatory conduct must be sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an environment that is objectively hostile or abusive and that the plaintiff subjectively perceives as abusive. The inquiry considers the totality of the circumstances, including nonexclusive considerations such as frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation, and interference with work.

**Exceptions and limits:** Psychological harm is relevant evidence but not an element. Ordinary incivility is not actionable merely because it is offensive, and the conduct must be connected to a protected characteristic and attributable to the employer under governing liability rules.

### Professional direction versus statutory supervisory authority

**Governing question:** When skilled direction of other workers makes a professional employee a supervisor under NLRA § 2(11).

**Current rule:** Work does not become supervisory merely because it benefits the enterprise or includes skilled direction of less-experienced employees. The Board may distinguish professional or technical direction incidental to the employee’s own skilled service from genuine labor-management authority aligning the employee with management, including independent authority over hiring, discharge, discipline, promotion, compensation, grievances, or comparable matters.

**Exceptions and limits:** Professionals may qualify as supervisors when they independently exercise genuine management prerogatives; no profession is categorically exempt.

### NLRA preemption of state withdrawal of generally available protections

**Governing question:** Whether a State may withhold an otherwise available, nonwaivable labor protection solely because an employee is covered by a collective-bargaining agreement.

**Current rule:** The NLRA preempts a state policy that categorically withholds otherwise available, nonwaivable wage protections solely because an employee is covered by a collective-bargaining agreement containing an arbitration clause. A State may administer neutral enforcement priorities and decline a claim genuinely displaced by federal law, but it may not make exercise of federally protected collective bargaining the condition for losing a generally available enforcement service.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not preempt every state distinction involving union representation or invalidate a statute that can be administered neutrally.

### Section 301 interpretation-versus-consultation boundary

**Governing question:** When LMRA § 301 preempts an independent state-law claim that refers to a collective-bargaining agreement.

**Current rule:** Section 301 does not preempt an independent state-law claim when liability arises from state law, no contractual term is genuinely disputed, and the agreement is consulted only for an undisputed factual input such as a wage rate. Preemption may apply when liability arises from the agreement or resolution requires interpretation rather than mere reference.

**Exceptions and limits:** The existence of a collective-bargaining agreement alone does not trigger preemption.

### After-acquired evidence and ADEA remedies

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs after-acquired evidence and ADEA remedies?

**Current rule:** After-acquired evidence of employee wrongdoing does not bar ADEA liability for an earlier discriminatory discharge, but misconduct that would independently have caused lawful termination materially limits the available remedy. The employer bears the burden of proving that the wrongdoing was sufficiently serious and that the employee in fact would have been discharged upon its discovery. When that showing is made, reinstatement and front pay are ordinarily unavailable. Backpay generally runs from the unlawful discharge until the employer discovered the misconduct, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances.

**Exceptions and limits:** Minor wrongdoing, newly invented standards, or pretextual assertions do not shorten relief. The district court retains equitable authority to address extraordinary circumstances and other remedies authorized by the statute.

## Housing and Civil Rights

### Family composition and the maximum-occupancy exemption

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs family composition and the maximum-occupancy exemption?

**Current rule:** The Fair Housing Act’s exemption for reasonable maximum-occupancy restrictions does not cover a zoning rule that permits an unlimited number of related occupants while imposing a numerical limit on unrelated occupants. A maximum-occupancy restriction covered by 42 U.S.C. § 3607(b)(1) limits the number of persons who may occupy a dwelling to address crowding, safety, or comparable conditions. **Governing question:** When parents may obtain reimbursement after rejecting an inadequate public special-education program.

### Title IX participation opportunities

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs title IX participation opportunities?

**Current rule:** Title IX does not entitle athletes to preservation of a particular intercollegiate team. Participation opportunities and accommodation of interests and abilities are evaluated across the athletic program rather than through sport-for-sport symmetry. ### EMTALA’s stabilization duty

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs eMTALA’s stabilization duty?

**Current rule:** A participating hospital that determines an individual has an emergency respiratory condition must provide treatment within its capabilities necessary to prevent material deterioration of that condition, or effect an appropriate transfer, even when an underlying condition makes the treatment incapable of producing a long-term cure. ## Securities Regulation

### Private liability for culpable assistance to a § 10(b) violation

**Governing question:** When a private § 10(b) plaintiff may impose liability on a defendant who materially assists another actor’s completed securities fraud.

**Current rule:** A private § 10(b) plaintiff may pursue aiding-and-abetting liability by proving a completed primary violation, the secondary defendant’s knowledge of that violation or conscious recklessness approaching actual knowledge, and substantial assistance that materially facilitated it. An omission qualifies only when the defendant had a defined, transaction-specific responsibility to act.

**Burdens and proof:** The plaintiff retains the ordinary burdens of proving the primary violation, the secondary defendant’s state of mind and material assistance, transaction causation, loss causation, and damages.

**Exceptions and limits:** Professional status, negligence, proximity to the transaction, passive association, or silence unconnected to a defined responsibility is insufficient.

**First controlling authority:** Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A., 511 U.S. 164 (1994), decided April 19, 1994, Limited private liability for culpable assistance; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A., 511 U.S. 164 (1994), decided April 19, 1994, Limited private liability for culpable assistance; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A., 511 U.S. 164 (1994), April 19, 1994.

### Section 12(2) and a bespoke private sale of an operating company

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs section 12(2) and a bespoke private sale of an operating company?

**Current rule:** Section 12(2) does not apply to this individually negotiated sale of substantially an entire business through a contract allocating disclosure and valuation risks between parties with direct access to the enterprise.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not restrict § 17(a), § 10(b), ordinary fraud, contract remedies, or another provision whose text reaches private transactions. It leaves standardized offerings to multiple institutional investors unresolved.

**First controlling authority:** Gustafson v. Alloyd Co., 513 U.S. 561, decided February 28, 1995, Section 12(2) and a bespoke private sale of an operating company; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Gustafson v. Alloyd Co., 513 U.S. 561, decided February 28, 1995, Section 12(2) and a bespoke private sale of an operating company; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Gustafson v. Alloyd Co., 513 U.S. 561, February 28, 1995.

## Trademark

### Color as a protectable trademark

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs color as a protectable trademark?

**Current rule:** The Lanham Act permits a color, standing alone, to serve as a trademark when the color has acquired source-identifying significance and is not functional. A claimant seeking protection for color alone must establish that consumers understand the color to identify the source of the goods. Protection is unavailable when the color is essential to the product’s use or purpose, affects cost or quality, or is competitively necessary. Ordinary likelihood-of-confusion requirements remain applicable.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not protect color in the abstract, functional or competitively necessary colors, or colors lacking secondary meaning. It does not eliminate ordinary proof of likely confusion.

**First controlling authority:** Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products Co., 514 U.S. 159, decided March 28, 1995, Color as a protectable trademark; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products Co., 514 U.S. 159, decided March 28, 1995, Color as a protectable trademark; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products Co., 514 U.S. 159, March 28, 1995.

## Copyright

### Commercial parody under the fair-use factors

**Governing question:** How the four statutory fair-use factors apply to a commercial work that comments on the copyrighted original.

**Current rule:** A commercial parody may qualify as fair use. The four factors must be weighed together in light of copyright’s purposes. Transformative use reduces the force of commerciality; the amount copied is judged in relation to the need to make the target recognizable; and market injury concerns substitution in a market copyright law protects, not lost demand caused by criticism or ridicule. Commerciality and market harm are not presumptively dispositive.

**Exceptions and limits:** Humor alone does not establish parody. The new work must reasonably be perceived as commenting on the original and remains subject to the complete statutory inquiry.

**First controlling authority:** Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc., 510 U.S. 569 (1994), decided March 7, 1994, Commercial parody under the statutory fair-use inquiry; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc., 510 U.S. 569 (1994), decided March 7, 1994, Commercial parody under the statutory fair-use inquiry; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc., 510 U.S. 569 (1994), March 7, 1994.

### Evenhanded discretionary attorney-fee awards under § 505

**Governing question:** What legal standard governs a Copyright Act attorney-fee request by a prevailing plaintiff or defendant.

**Current rule:** Prevailing copyright plaintiffs and defendants are evaluated under the same discretionary standard. Neither side is presumptively entitled to fees, and a prevailing defendant need not prove frivolousness or bad faith as a categorical prerequisite. Courts may consider objective unreasonableness, frivolousness, motivation, compensation, deterrence, and other circumstances faithful to copyright’s purposes, applying all considerations evenhandedly.

**Exceptions and limits:** The considerations are nonexclusive, no single one is mandatory, and § 505 does not establish automatic prevailing-party fee shifting.

**First controlling authority:** Fogerty v. Fantasy, Inc., 510 U.S. 517 (1994), decided March 1, 1994, Evenhanded discretionary awards under § 505; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Fogerty v. Fantasy, Inc., 510 U.S. 517 (1994), decided March 1, 1994, Evenhanded discretionary awards under § 505; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Fogerty v. Fantasy, Inc., 510 U.S. 517 (1994), March 1, 1994.

## Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act

### No economic-motive element in RICO

**Governing question:** Whether RICO requires the enterprise or alleged predicate acts to pursue profit or another economic objective.

**Current rule:** RICO contains no economic-motive element. Ideological purpose neither establishes nor defeats liability; a civil plaintiff must prove the enacted enterprise, pattern, qualifying predicates, injury to business or property, and required causal connection.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not decide whether particular conduct satisfies the predicates or pattern, and protected advocacy, association, picketing, and persuasion do not become racketeering merely because they accompany a political movement.

**First controlling authority:** National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler, 510 U.S. 249 (1994), decided January 24, 1994, Economic motive under RICO; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler, 510 U.S. 249 (1994), decided January 24, 1994, Economic motive under RICO; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler, 510 U.S. 249 (1994), January 24, 1994.

### A RICO enterprise’s own interstate operations

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs a RICO enterprise’s own interstate operations?

**Current rule:** An enterprise is engaged in interstate commerce under 18 U.S.C. § 1962(a) when its own continuing operations include interstate purchasing, interstate recruitment or movement of labor, interstate shipment of output, or comparable direct interstate activity. When that direct commerce is proved, no separate substantial-effects analysis is necessary.

**Exceptions and limits:** The Court does not decide whether a purely intrastate enterprise with only remote economic consequences satisfies § 1962(a).

**First controlling authority:** United States v. Robertson, 514 U.S. 669, decided May 1, 1995, A RICO enterprise’s own interstate operations; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** United States v. Robertson, 514 U.S. 669, decided May 1, 1995, A RICO enterprise’s own interstate operations; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Robertson, 514 U.S. 669, May 1, 1995.

## Taxation

### Income from repurchase agreements involving federal securities

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs income from repurchase agreements involving federal securities?

**Current rule:** Section 3124(a) does not exempt the income because the mutual funds receive interest on private loans to seller-borrowers, not interest on obligations of the United States. Federal securities used as collateral do not transform the return on an independent private lending obligation into federally exempt interest. The transaction is classified according to its legal and economic operation rather than the parties’ sale-and-repurchase labels.

**Exceptions and limits:** A transaction conveying genuine beneficial ownership of federal securities and the accompanying federal coupon or discount income may present a different question.

**First controlling authority:** Nebraska Department of Revenue v. Loewenstein, 513 U.S. 123, decided December 12, 1994, Income from repurchase agreements involving federal securities; established the current rule.

### ADEA settlements under § 104(a)(2)

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs aDEA settlements under § 104(a)(2)?

**Current rule:** ADEA backpay and liquidated damages are not excluded from gross income under § 104(a)(2). Exclusion requires both a cause of action based on tort or tort-type rights and damages received on account of personal injuries or sickness. **Exceptions and limits:** The Court does not decide damages expressly compensating physical injury, medical injury, or an emotional injury under a statute and record satisfying the enacted exclusion.

## Federal Indian Law

### Legal incidence of the motor-fuels tax

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs legal incidence of the motor-fuels tax?

**Current rule:** When the legal incidence of a state tax falls directly on a Tribe or tribal member inside Indian country, the tax is invalid absent clear congressional authorization. Courts determine legal incidence from the enacted liability, payment, reporting, and pass-through provisions rather than from economic burden alone.

**Exceptions and limits:** Oklahoma may prospectively redesign the tax within federal limits. The Court does not decide a tax whose incidence is lawfully placed on a nontribal distributor or consumer.

**First controlling authority:** Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Chickasaw Nation, 515 U.S. 450, decided June 14, 1995, Legal incidence of the motor-fuels tax; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Chickasaw Nation, 515 U.S. 450, decided June 14, 1995, Legal incidence of the motor-fuels tax; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Chickasaw Nation, 515 U.S. 450, June 14, 1995.

### Income earned from the Tribe

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs income earned from the Tribe?

**Current rule:** A State may not tax wages paid by a Tribe to a tribal member residing in Indian country. It may tax the wages of a member residing outside Indian country absent federal law providing an exemption. Reservation residence and tribal-source income trigger the territorial protection; tribal employment alone does not carry that protection to an off-reservation residence.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not govern nonmember employees, income from nontribal sources, or an express federal alteration of state taxing authority.

**First controlling authority:** Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Chickasaw Nation, 515 U.S. 450, decided June 14, 1995, Income earned from the Tribe; established the current rule.

## Environmental Law

### Scope of Clean Water Act § 401 certification conditions

**Governing question:** What project conditions a State may impose once an activity that may result in a discharge triggers Clean Water Act § 401 certification.

**Current rule:** A potential discharge determines whether certification is required. Once triggered, § 401(d) authorizes conditions on the applicant or licensed activity that are necessary to assure compliance with applicable federal and state water-quality requirements, including adopted designated uses supported by the administrative record.

**Exceptions and limits:** Certification is not a general license to regulate conduct unrelated to water quality or the licensed activity. A flow condition must remain tied to an adopted designated use or other applicable water-quality requirement and have record support.

**First controlling authority:** PUD No. 1 of Jefferson County v. Washington Department of Ecology, 511 U.S. 700 (1994), decided May 31, 1994, Scope of conditions under § 401(d); established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** PUD No. 1 of Jefferson County v. Washington Department of Ecology, 511 U.S. 700 (1994), decided May 31, 1994, Scope of conditions under § 401(d); supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** PUD No. 1 of Jefferson County v. Washington Department of Ecology, 511 U.S. 700 (1994), May 31, 1994.

### Habitat modification that actually kills or injures wildlife

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs habitat modification that actually kills or injures wildlife?

**Current rule:** The ESA permits the Secretary to define “harm” as significant habitat modification or degradation that actually kills or injures protected wildlife, including injury caused by material impairment of essential behavioral patterns. Habitat alteration alone is insufficient. The Government must establish actual death or injury to protected wildlife and the ordinary causal connection between the modification and that injury. Speculative, conjectural, or merely potential ecological effects do not constitute a completed take.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not reach habitat modification causing no actual injury, eliminate ordinary causation, or authorize regulation outside the ESA’s protected-species and permit structure.

**First controlling authority:** Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon, 515 U.S. 687, decided June 29, 1995, Habitat modification that actually kills or injures wildlife; established the current rule.

## Administrative Law

### Delegated power to modify does not authorize elimination

**Governing question:** How far an agency may alter a statutory requirement under a delegated power to modify it.

**Current rule:** A delegated power to modify permits bounded alteration, including adjustments to form, content, timing, and genuinely limited exceptions. It does not authorize an agency to eliminate a central statutory requirement for nearly an entire regulated sector or replace the enacted regime with a fundamentally different model. Deference does not extend authority beyond a meaning the statutory text can bear.

**Exceptions and limits:** The agency may make incremental adjustments and grant genuinely limited waivers consistent with the statutory delegation.

**First controlling authority:** MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218 (1994), decided June 17, 1994, Scope of the power to modify tariff-filing requirements; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218 (1994), decided June 17, 1994, Scope of the power to modify tariff-filing requirements; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218 (1994), June 17, 1994.

### Provider Reimbursement Manual § 233

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs provider Reimbursement Manual § 233?

**Current rule:** Section 233 is an interpretive rule that explains the application of existing reasonable-cost principles to advance refunding and did not require notice-and-comment rulemaking. An agency guideline is interpretive when it explains how existing statutory and regulatory standards apply to a recurring transaction without creating a new binding obligation inconsistent with those standards. A manual may not be used to amend the governing law or impose an obligation outside the statute and regulations.

**First controlling authority:** Shalala v. Guernsey Memorial Hospital, 514 U.S. 87, decided March 6, 1995, Provider Reimbursement Manual § 233; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Shalala v. Guernsey Memorial Hospital, 514 U.S. 87, decided March 6, 1995, Provider Reimbursement Manual § 233; supplies the current rule.

## Statutory Interpretation

### No implied claim-ending sanction for missed statutory timing directives

**Governing question:** When a court may attach dismissal or loss of governmental authority to violation of a statutory timing command.

**Current rule:** Mandatory timing language imposes duties on responsible officials, but a court does not create a claim-ending dismissal sanction or loss of governmental authority that Congress omitted, particularly when Congress separately enacted an express limitations period. The absence of that sanction does not make the timing directives optional or foreclose independently preserved constitutional objections to delay.

**Exceptions and limits:** A different result follows when Congress expressly supplies dismissal, loss of authority, or another consequence.

**First controlling authority:** United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, 510 U.S. 43 (1993), decided December 13, 1993, Effect of the statutory timing directives; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, 510 U.S. 43 (1993), decided December 13, 1993, Effect of the statutory timing directives; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, 510 U.S. 43 (1993), December 13, 1993.

### Presumption against retroactive legal consequences

**Governing question:** How a court determines the temporal reach of a statute enacted while a case is pending.

**Current rule:** A court first asks whether Congress clearly prescribed the statute’s temporal reach. If not, the court determines whether applying the enactment would attach new legal consequences to completed conduct, including increased liability, impairment of an existing right, or imposition of a new duty. A provision with such retroactive effect does not govern absent clear congressional intent. A genuinely procedural provision may apply to a pending matter when it does not alter substantive liability or completed legal consequences.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** First identify a controlling temporal command; only if none exists evaluate retroactive effect.

**Exceptions and limits:** Congress may prescribe retroactive operation through clear language.

**First controlling authority:** Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244 (1994), decided April 26, 1994, Temporal reach of newly enacted remedies; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244 (1994), decided April 26, 1994, Temporal reach of newly enacted remedies; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244 (1994), April 26, 1994.

## Remedies

### Section 1983 remedy for state interference with a concrete federal labor right

**Governing question:** When state interference with a federal labor right is actionable under 42 U.S.C. § 1983.

**Current rule:** Section 1983 is available against an appropriate state official when federal labor law protects a concrete individual interest against state abridgment, the governmental duty is judicially administrable, and Congress has not displaced the remedy with an exclusive alternative enforcement scheme. Federal preemption by itself does not automatically create an individual § 1983 cause of action.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not waive state sovereign immunity, authorize damages against the State, or predetermine the proper defendant and form of relief.

**First controlling authority:** Livadas v. Bradshaw, 512 U.S. 107 (1994), decided June 13, 1994, Section 1983 remedy for state interference with the federal labor right; established the current rule.

### Reliance and retroactive application of a federal constitutional rule

**Governing question:** What recurring rule governs reliance and retroactive application of a federal constitutional rule?

**Current rule:** A state court may not deny the ordinary retroactive application of a controlling federal constitutional rule to a nonfinal case merely because a party relied on the invalidated state statute. Once this Court applies a federal rule to the parties before it, that rule governs other cases still open on direct review. A different outcome remains possible when an independently valid legal doctrine supplies it, such as an alternative cure, a preexisting remedial rule, qualified immunity, res judicata, or a doctrine limiting retroactivity itself. Simple reliance on the former law does not create a free-standing exception.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary rules concerning final judgments, statutes of repose, waiver, harmless error, qualified immunity, and other independently valid remedial doctrines remain intact.

**First controlling authority:** Reynoldsville Casket Co. v. Hyde, 514 U.S. 749, decided May 15, 1995, Reliance and retroactive application of a federal constitutional rule; established the current rule.

**Controlling authorities:** Reynoldsville Casket Co. v. Hyde, 514 U.S. 749, decided May 15, 1995, Reliance and retroactive application of a federal constitutional rule; supplies the current rule.

**Present operation effective:** Reynoldsville Casket Co. v. Hyde, 514 U.S. 749, May 15, 1995.
