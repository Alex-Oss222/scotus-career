## United States v. Shabani

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Citation or Docket:** 513 U.S. 10; No. 93-981
- **October Term:** 1994
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits case; argued or submitted for decision on the simulated historical calendar.
- **Simulated Event Date:** November 1, 1994
- **Question Presented:** Whether 21 U.S.C. § 846 requires proof of an overt act in furtherance of the charged narcotics conspiracy.
- **Question Granted:** Whether 21 U.S.C. § 846 requires proof of an overt act in furtherance of the charged narcotics conspiracy.
- **Legal Field:** Criminal law — narcotics conspiracy and statutory elements
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** United States Court Of Appeals For The Ninth Circuit. Respondent Shabani was convicted of conspiracy to distribute cocaine in violation of 21 U. S. C. § 846 after the District Court refused to instruct the jury that proof of an overt act in furtherance of a narcotics conspiracy is required for conviction under § 846. - **Material Facts and Record:** Respondent Shabani was convicted of conspiracy to distribute cocaine in violation of 21 U. S. Supreme Court Database release 2025_01 (http://scdb.wustl.edu/) supplies docket and vote metadata.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** 993 F. 2d 1419, reversed. Vote: 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** O’Connor, J., delivered the opinion for a unanimous Court.
- **Historical Holding:** In order to establish a violation of § 846, the Government need not prove the commission of any overt acts in furtherance of the conspiracy. The statute’s plain language does not require an overt act, and such a requirement has not been inferred from congressional silence in other conspiracy statutes, see, e. g., Nash v. United States, 229 U. S. 373. Thus, absent contrary indications, it is presumed that Congress intended to adopt the common law definition of conspiracy, which “does not make the doing of any act other than the act of conspiring a condition of liability,” id., at 378. Moreover, since the general conspiracy statute and the conspiracy provision of the Organized Crime Control Act of 1970 both require an overt act, it appears that Congress’ choice in § 846 was quite deliberate.

- **Legal Field:** Criminal law and speech — scienter in child-pornography offenses

#### Issue 1: Religious Freedom Restoration Act

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches RFRA first because it became governing law while this prospective challenge remained pending. Section 6 expressly reaches implementation of preexisting state and local law, and applying it to prospective enforcement does not attach new liability to completed conduct. The Church’s failure to invoke a statute not yet enacted when the court of appeals entered judgment does not forfeit the intervening governing rule. Stone applies RFRA where the existing record permits and remands any materially different application requiring additional factfinding.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The Church bears the threshold obligation to identify a substantial burden on religious exercise. Once that showing is made, RFRA places on Clearwater both the burden of going forward and the burden of persuasion to establish that application of the particular burden furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. Preventing fraud in charitable solicitation is compelling, but the interest must justify the particular regulation rather than religious supervision in general. On this record, the broad compulsory disclosure and recordkeeping regime substantially burdens religious exercise and association by requiring extensive disclosure of internal financial operation, use of funds, member-directed solicitation information, and supporting records under criminal sanction. The mandatory sixty-day refund provision also substantially burdens exercise by displacing the Church’s own religiously connected refund process. Clearwater has not shown those broad measures to be the least restrictive means because targeted anti-fraud law, limited identifying disclosures, transaction-specific investigation, and accurate disclosure of promised refund terms address the demonstrated secular harms more narrowly. The limited identifying registration requirements do not establish a substantial burden on this record. The contemporaneous written statement of the terms of a refund policy, when a refund is voluntarily promised, survives even assuming a substantial burden because the record identifies concrete deception concerning the content and availability of refunds and the provision requires disclosure of the Church’s own policy rather than a policy dictated by government.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** RFRA governs Clearwater’s prospective implementation of the challenged ordinance. Broad compulsory disclosure and recordkeeping of internal religious financial and organizational affairs, and a municipal rule mandating refund within sixty days whenever a church offers refunds, substantially burden religious exercise and fail because Clearwater has not shown those requirements to be the least restrictive means of preventing fraud. Limited identifying disclosures do not establish a substantial burden on this record, and a requirement that a solicitor accurately state in writing the terms of a voluntarily offered refund policy survives RFRA on the established record.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** RFRA restores a statutory compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means inquiry even for burdens imposed through generally applicable law. Clearwater has legitimate authority to prevent affirmative deception. But the statute does not permit the City to substitute a broad continuing inspection and disclosure regime for narrower tools capable of addressing the demonstrated wrong. The record already identifies ordinary fraud prohibitions, limited identification rules, complaint-triggered investigation, and written disclosure of promised refund terms as materially narrower means. The sixty-day mandate goes further still by changing the substance of the Church’s policy rather than requiring truth about the policy it chose. By contrast, the written-policy provision responds directly to evidence that contributors may have been misled about refund conditions and does not require civil officials to determine religious doctrine or the merits of a request.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** RFRA does not exempt a church from generally applicable fraud, contract, tax, restitution, or criminal law; does not bar a targeted subpoena or audit supported by a concrete secular enforcement need and lawful process; and does not authorize government to determine whether religious beliefs are true. Stone reserves materially different investigative applications not established by this record.

#### Issue 2: Purpose, neutrality, and targeting

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional purpose question because RFRA does not resolve whether Clearwater enacted or administers the ordinance for a forbidden sectarian purpose, a question that could affect the ordinance as a whole. He resolves only the summary-judgment issue and leaves the disputed factual determination to the district court.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Government may regulate charitable solicitation through religion-neutral law, but it may not design or administer an ostensibly general ordinance to burden a named religion. Evidence of sustained official focus on Scientology is legally material. A city cannot convert concern about religion into authority to supervise or disadvantage a disfavored church.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Summary judgment for Clearwater cannot stand on the sectarian-purpose claim where the record contains evidence from which a factfinder could conclude that religious hostility or denominational targeting materially shaped the ordinance’s design or enforcement.

#### Issue 1: EMTALA stabilization duty

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory question because the hospital seeks a declaration defining its obligations during recurring emergency presentations.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The respiratory distress is the emergency medical condition relevant to the statute. Once the hospital detects that condition, EMTALA requires treatment necessary to prevent material deterioration within the hospital’s capability, or an appropriate transfer. The court may not add an exception based on the permanence of the infant’s underlying anencephaly or the physicians’ conclusion that treatment is medically futile in a broader sense.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A participating hospital that determines an individual has an emergency respiratory condition must provide the treatment necessary to stabilize that condition within its capabilities, or effect an appropriate transfer, even when the patient has an underlying condition for which the treatment offers no long-term cure.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress wrote an episode-specific emergency duty. Mechanical ventilation did not cure anencephaly, but the record establishes that it stabilized the acute respiratory distress and that no accepting facility was available. The hospital’s ethical objection cannot replace the statutory condition Congress enacted.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require indefinite intensive treatment after stabilization, decide nonemergency care, or prevent Congress from creating a defined medical-futility exception.

#### Issue 2: State law and preemption

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches preemption only because the hospital invokes state law as authority to withhold treatment EMTALA requires.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** State law remains operative except to the extent it permits refusal of the stabilizing treatment required by federal law. On this record, any direct conflict must yield to EMTALA.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A state rule cannot authorize a participating hospital to withhold stabilization that § 1395dd requires; otherwise compatible state medical law remains untouched.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The federal duty is conditional on hospital participation and the presentation of an emergency condition. The State remains free to regulate medical practice outside that conflict.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not recognize a general federal right to every medically requested treatment.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the Seventh Circuit.

#### Issue 1: Individual officer liability

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits on the summary-judgment record and evaluates the encounter as a whole.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The Fourth Amendment does not require an officer to select the least dangerous feasible tactic. Earlier tactical choices remain relevant when officers unreasonably manufacture the necessity for force, but the record here shows pursuit, warning, retreat, an explicit threat, and an armed close-range advance rather than an avoidable escalation created by Drinski.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An officer may use deadly force when the record permits no reasonable finding other than that an armed person was advancing at close range after an explicit lethal threat, even though less-deadly tools or different tactics can be imagined after the event.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone includes the officers’ conduct before the shot in the frame. That wider frame does not help the estate here. Plakas repeatedly resisted, armed himself, struck an officer, threatened death, and closed the remaining distance while Drinski retreated. A constitutional rule requiring use of spray, a dog, or one more step of retreat would convert reasonableness into judicial tactical command.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not insulate an officer who needlessly creates a confrontation, fires after a threat has ended, or ignores a clearly safe and available means when the governing circumstances make deadly force unreasonable.

#### Issue 2: Municipal liability

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the municipal claim because it depends on the same alleged use-of-force violation.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The estate has not established an underlying constitutional violation. The county therefore cannot be held liable on the theory presented.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A municipal policy claim resting solely on an officer’s assertedly excessive force fails when the force was constitutionally reasonable on the undisputed record.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Monell does not supply liability without a constitutional injury caused by the policy. Stone does not reach any independent municipal act producing a separate injury because none is established here.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone reserves a case in which municipal training or policy itself causes a distinct constitutional violation.
