### Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., 510 U.S. 17 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** 92-1168.
**Decided:** November 9, 1993.

#### Judgment

Reversed and remanded, 9-0. Supporting: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.

The lower courts shall reconsider the established record under the correct hostile-environment standard. The Court makes no finding of liability.

#### Opinions

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O’Connor | Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Supports reversal and remand | Complete opinion |
| Concurrence | Scalia | No other Justice | Supports reversal and remand | Joins the Court while noting the unavoidable judgment involved in applying the statutory standard |
| Concurrence | Ginsburg | No other Justice | Supports reversal and remand | Emphasizes discriminatory alteration of working conditions rather than clinical or economic injury |

#### Holding

**Question decided:** Threshold for an actionable hostile work environment.

**Holding:** A Title VII hostile-work-environment plaintiff need not prove serious psychological injury. The discriminatory conduct must be sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an environment that is objectively hostile or abusive and that the plaintiff subjectively perceives as abusive.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by O’Connor, joined as stated in the Opinions table above; Complete opinion. The proposition is controlling at that stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The inquiry considers the totality of the circumstances. Relevant considerations may include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation, and interference with work, but the list is nonexclusive and no single consideration is indispensable. Psychological harm may be relevant evidence, but it is not an element.

**Application:** The district court found repeated sex-based humiliation but attached dispositive weight to the lack of serious psychological harm. Because that erroneous requirement may have affected its ultimate totality assessment, remand is necessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary incivility is not actionable merely because it is offensive. The conduct must be connected to a protected characteristic, meet the objective and subjective requirements, and be attributable to the employer under the governing liability rules.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor is clarified. Serious psychological injury is not required, and the complete circumstances determine whether discriminatory conduct altered the conditions of employment by creating an objectively and subjectively abusive environment.

### Florence County School District Four v. Carter, 510 U.S. 7 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** 92-1523.
**Decided:** November 9, 1993.

#### Judgment

Affirmed, 9-0. Supporting: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg. No opposing Justice.

The reimbursement judgment remains in force, subject to the district court’s continuing equitable authority to ensure that the amount represents an appropriate and reasonable private placement.

Justice O’Connor delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg.

**Opinion of the Court:** O’Connor, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg.

#### Holding

**Question decided:** Reimbursement for a parent-selected private placement.

**Holding:** IDEA permits reimbursement when a public agency failed to offer a free appropriate public education and the parents obtained an education otherwise appropriate under the Act. The private school’s lack of state approval and failure to comply with procedures written for public-agency placements do not categorically bar relief.

**Authority:** O’Connor, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; the proposition is controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** Parents who unilaterally reject a public placement bear the financial risk of their decision. Reimbursement becomes available only if they establish that the public offer violated IDEA and that the private education was appropriate to the child. Even then, § 1415(e)(2) leaves the court with equitable discretion to reduce or deny an unreasonable amount.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reimbursement is not available for a placement that is merely preferred, educationally inappropriate, or unreasonably expensive. The decision does not prevent States from regulating private schools through otherwise valid laws.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Burlington is clarified. Equitable reimbursement does not require parents to recreate every procedural and approval feature governing the public program they lawfully rejected. Rowley’s substantive appropriateness requirement remains applicable to the private education.

### United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, 510 U.S. 43 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** 92-1180.
**Decided:** December 13, 1993.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Relief or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-seizure due process | Affirmed, 6-3 | Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg | O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas | The warrant-based occupation of Good’s home without prior process was unconstitutional absent a demonstrated exigency. |
| Statutory timing | Reversed, 9-0 | Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | No opposing vote | Noncompliance with §§ 1602 through 1604 does not require dismissal of an action timely under § 1621. |

**Judgment, mandate, and remand:**

The judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded. The forfeiture action may continue because it was commenced within § 1621. The lower courts must separately determine the lawful consequences of the unconstitutional pre-hearing seizure.

#### Opinions

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg in full; O’Connor, Scalia, and Thomas as to the statutory-timing holding | Supports both judgment components | Full due-process and timing holdings for the six-Justice coalition; timing holding for all nine |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | O’Connor | Scalia, Thomas | Agrees with the timing reversal and opposes the due-process affirmance | Would treat the ex parte warrant procedure as constitutionally adequate |

#### Question 1: Pre-seizure process for real property

**Question decided:** Pre-seizure process for real property.

**Holding:** Except upon particularized proof of exigent circumstances, the Fifth Amendment requires notice and a meaningful opportunity to be heard before the Government seizes real property for civil forfeiture.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Kennedy, joined as stated in the Opinions table above; Full due-process and timing holdings for the six-Justice coalition; timing holding for all nine. The proposition is controlling at that stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The Government bears the obligation to establish an actual exigency justifying immediate seizure. The forfeiture label alone is insufficient. A lis pendens, restraining order, posting of process, or other less intrusive measure ordinarily preserves the Government’s interests while an adversarial hearing occurs.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule concerns real property. It does not determine the treatment of readily movable personal property or contraband, and it permits immediate action when the Government proves a genuine exigency.

#### Question 2: Effect of the statutory timing directives

**Question decided:** Effect of the statutory timing directives.

**Holding:** A forfeiture action filed within § 1621’s five-year limitation period may not be dismissed solely because officials failed to comply with §§ 1602 through 1604, where Congress did not make dismissal or loss of authority the consequence of noncompliance.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Kennedy, joined as stated in the Opinions table above; Full due-process and timing holdings for the six-Justice coalition; timing holding for all nine. The proposition is controlling at that stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Mandatory timing language imposes duties on responsible officials, but a court does not create a forfeiture-ending sanction that Congress omitted. The rule does not make the directives optional and does not foreclose independently preserved constitutional objections to delay.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Calero-Toledo’s allowance for immediate action against movable property does not extend categorically to an immovable home. Montalvo-Murillo and Brock are applied to reject an implied dismissal sanction for the internal forfeiture timing directives. The decision supplies the first simulated rule governing pre-seizure process for real property.

### John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank, 510 U.S. 86 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** 92-1074.
**Decided:** December 13, 1993.

#### Judgment

Affirmed, 6-3. Supporting: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg. Opposing: O’Connor, Kennedy, Thomas.

The genuinely guaranteed components remain outside plan-asset treatment. Proceedings may continue concerning Hancock’s conduct toward the free funds, including breach and any relief authorized by ERISA.

#### Opinions

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter | Supports affirmance | Complete opinion |
| Dissent | Thomas | O’Connor, Kennedy | Would reverse | Would classify the contract more broadly as a guaranteed-benefit policy and reject fiduciary treatment of the free funds |

#### Holding

**Question decided:** Guaranteed-benefit exclusion and fiduciary control over free funds.

**Holding:** ERISA’s guaranteed-benefit-policy exclusion covers only those components of an insurance contract that genuinely guarantee plan benefits and allocate the corresponding investment risk to the insurer. GAC 50’s unconverted free funds do not qualify, and Hancock is a fiduciary to the extent it exercises authority or control over them.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Ginsburg, joined as stated in the Opinions table above; Complete opinion. The proposition is controlling at that stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Each materially distinct component of a mixed insurance contract must be examined separately. A component falls within the exclusion when the insurer guarantees an aggregate amount of benefits and assumes the relevant investment risk. For an accumulation pool of free funds, that ordinarily requires a guaranteed reasonable return and a contractual mechanism for conversion into defined guaranteed benefits. Corporate status as an insurer and commingling in a general account do not alone establish the exclusion.

**Application:** Hancock’s status as fiduciary follows only with respect to the authority or control it exercised over the free funds. The Court does not determine whether that authority was exercised loyally or prudently.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not make every insurer an ERISA fiduciary, include assets backing genuinely fixed benefits within plan assets, or govern a materially different annuity that guarantees returns and conversion terms during its accumulation phase.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** ERISA’s “to the extent” language now requires component-specific classification of mixed annuity contracts. Functional fiduciary status follows control over nonexcluded plan assets and does not attach categorically to every aspect of an insurer’s contract.

### Ratzlaf v. United States, 510 U.S. 135 (1994)

**Docket or dockets:** 92-1196.
**Decided:** January 11, 1994.

#### Judgment

Reversed and remanded, 6-3. Supporting: Stone, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg. Opposing: Blackmun, O’Connor, Thomas.

Ratzlaf’s conviction under 31 U.S.C. §§ 5322 and 5324 is vacated. The Government may retry him under a correct instruction if otherwise lawful and supported by admissible evidence.

#### Opinions

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stone, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter | Supports reversal and remand | Complete opinion |
| Dissent | Blackmun | O’Connor, Thomas | Would affirm | Would require knowledge of the reporting requirement and an intent to evade it, but not knowledge that structuring is unlawful |

#### Holding

**Question decided:** Willfulness in the anti-structuring offense.

**Holding:** Conviction under §§ 5322 and 5324 requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the structuring was unlawful, in addition to proof that the defendant intended to evade the financial institution’s reporting duty.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Ginsburg, joined as stated in the Opinions table above; Complete opinion. The proposition is controlling at that stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 5324’s purpose-to-evade language establishes the required object of the transaction pattern. Section 5322’s separate willfulness requirement adds knowledge that the chosen structuring conduct violates law. The Government need not prove knowledge of the precise statutory citation or every detail of the reporting scheme.

**Application:** The jury was affirmatively instructed that knowledge of unlawfulness was unnecessary. It therefore did not find every element required by the statute.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding creates no general ignorance-of-law defense, does not alter civil reporting obligations, and does not prevent Congress from changing the offense’s mental state through clear enacted language.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cheek and Liparota are applied to the distinctive text and context of the currency-reporting subchapter. The decision establishes knowledge of unlawfulness as an element of the willful anti-structuring offense, without creating a general rule for every statute using the word willfully.

### Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich, 510 U.S. 200 (1994)

**Docket or dockets:** 92-896.
**Decided:** January 19, 1994.

#### Judgment

Affirmed, 9-0. Supporting: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.

Thunder Basin must use the Mine Act’s administrative-review route. The Court does not decide the underlying labor-law or representative-designation issues.

#### Opinions

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Blackmun | Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg in full; Scalia and Thomas except the legislative-history and portions of the constitutional-review discussion | Supports affirmance | Complete preclusion and meaningful-review rationale for seven Justices; core statutory-preclusion holding for all nine |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Scalia | Thomas | Supports affirmance | Relies on statutory text and structure, rejects reliance on legislative history, and treats the limited constitutional objection separately |

#### Holding

**Question decided:** Statutory review-channel preclusion.

**Holding:** The Mine Act’s comprehensive enforcement and review scheme precludes district-court jurisdiction over Thunder Basin’s pre-enforcement challenge because Congress’s intent to channel the dispute is fairly discernible from the statutory structure and the designated route preserves meaningful judicial review.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Blackmun, joined as stated in the Opinions table above; Complete preclusion and meaningful-review rationale for seven Justices; core statutory-preclusion holding for all nine. The proposition is controlling at that stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** In determining whether a specialized statutory scheme displaces initial district-court review, a court examines the enacted review structure and asks whether the asserted claims can receive meaningful consideration through that structure. A claim closely connected to the statutory scheme ordinarily must use the designated route. District-court review remains open when the statutory route cannot provide meaningful judicial review, when the claim is genuinely collateral to the scheme, or when the process imposes an irreparable deprivation that the designated review cannot address.

**Application:** The Mine Act assigns challenges to enforcement measures to the Commission and then to a federal court of appeals, while expressly granting district-court authority in limited proceedings initiated by the Secretary. Thunder Basin’s labor-law arguments depend on interpretation of Mine Act representative provisions and can be preserved before the Commission and decided by the reviewing court. Civil penalties do not become payable before review is available, and the record shows no constitutionally intolerable prehearing deprivation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether the representative designations violate the National Labor Relations Act, whether the Department’s regulation is valid on the merits, and whether Thunder Basin ultimately must permit every activity attributed to the representatives.

**Application:** The Tenth Circuit’s jurisdictional judgment is affirmed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Block and Whitney National Bank are applied and clarified. A comprehensive administrative and appellate route may impliedly preclude initial district-court review even when the statute does not expressly mention pre-enforcement suits, provided congressional channeling is evident from the structure and meaningful judicial review remains available.

### Albright v. Oliver, 510 U.S. 266 (1994)

**Docket or dockets:** 92-833.
**Decided:** January 24, 1994.

#### Judgment

Affirmed, 7-2. Supporting: Stone, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg. Opposing: Blackmun, Stevens.

No Fourth Amendment damages claim is revived or remanded.

#### Opinions

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Souter | Stone, O’Connor, Scalia, Ginsburg | Supports affirmance | Substantive-due-process holding and reservation of materially distinct nonseizure injuries |
| Concurrence | Stone | No other Justice | Supports affirmance | Would reach the limited Fourth Amendment source-and-seizure question |
| Concurrence | Ginsburg | No other Justice | Supports affirmance | Explains the possible duration of a Fourth Amendment seizure while agreeing that liability is not presented |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Kennedy | Thomas | Supports affirmance on a separate due-process and state-remedy ground | Does not join the Court’s full source-specific rationale |
| Dissent | Stevens | Blackmun | Opposes affirmance | Would recognize a due-process restriction on prosecution without probable cause |

#### Holding

**Question decided:** Substantive due process as the asserted source.

**Holding:** A claimant may not use substantive due process to duplicate Fourth Amendment protection for injuries alleged to arise from arrest and prosecution-related restraints on the person. Albright’s pleaded substantive-due-process claim therefore fails.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Souter, joined as stated in the Opinions table above; Substantive-due-process holding and reservation of materially distinct nonseizure injuries. The proposition is controlling at that stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** When the alleged injury is the arrest or restraint of a person and settled Fourth Amendment law supplies the directly relevant source, substantive due process does not create a parallel federal malicious-prosecution tort. The Court reserves an exceptional case involving a substantial injury distinct from any seizure or other specific constitutional protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether probable cause existed, whether Oliver supplied false information, whether the travel condition constituted a continuing seizure, or whether a properly pleaded Fourth Amendment, fabricated-evidence, procedural-due-process, or state-law claim would succeed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Good remains intact: independently applicable constitutional guarantees may coexist. Albright establishes only that substantive due process does not duplicate Fourth Amendment protection for the arrest and pretrial restraints alleged here. No simulated rule is created concerning whether post-release bond conditions constitute a continuing seizure.

### National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler, 510 U.S. 249 (1994)

**Docket or dockets:** 92-780.
**Decided:** January 24, 1994.

#### Judgment

Reversed and remanded, 9-0. Supporting: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.

The clinics’ RICO claims may proceed past the economic-motive dismissal. Every remaining statutory, causal, injury, and constitutional issue remains open.

#### Opinions

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone | Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Supports reversal and remand | Complete opinion |
| Concurrence | Souter | Kennedy | Supports reversal and remand | Emphasizes that particular RICO applications and remedies remain subject to the First Amendment |

#### Question 1: Standing of the clinic plaintiffs

**Question decided:** Standing of the clinic plaintiffs.

**Holding:** DWHO and SWHO have Article III standing at the pleading stage because each alleges injury to its own business or property fairly traceable to respondents’ asserted campaign and redressable through the relief sought.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Stone, joined as stated in the Opinions table above; Complete opinion. The proposition is controlling at that stated scope.

#### Question 2: Economic motive under RICO

**Question decided:** Economic motive under RICO.

**Holding:** RICO does not require proof that the enterprise or the alleged predicate acts were motivated by profit or another economic objective.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Stone, joined as stated in the Opinions table above; Complete opinion. The proposition is controlling at that stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Ideological purpose neither establishes nor defeats liability. A civil plaintiff must prove the statutory enterprise, pattern, qualifying predicates, injury to business or property, and the required causal connection. No additional economic-motive element may be inserted into those requirements.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether respondents committed qualifying predicates, whether a pattern or enterprise can be proved, whether the clinics satisfy § 1964(c), or whether any particular protest activity is constitutionally protected. Peaceful advocacy, association, picketing, and persuasion cannot become racketeering merely because they accompany a political movement. The Court does not decide any concrete First Amendment defense on this record.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Turkette and Sedima are applied without adding an economic-purpose limitation. RICO liability continues to depend on the enacted elements, injury, and causation rather than the ideological or commercial objective of the enterprise.

**Question decided:** Diminishment of the Uintah Reservation.

### FDIC v. Meyer, 510 U.S. 471 (1994)

**Docket or dockets:** 92-741.
**Decided:** February 23, 1994.

#### Judgment

Reversed, 9-0. Supporting: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.

**Judgment, mandate, and remand:**

The agency damages award shall be vacated, and judgment shall be entered for the FDIC on the claim against FSLIC. The Court’s disposition does not reopen the unreviewed judgment concerning the individual official.

Justice Thomas delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg.

**Opinion of the Court:** Thomas, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg.

#### Question 1: FTCA exclusivity and the sue-and-be-sued waiver

**Question decided:** FTCA exclusivity and the sue-and-be-sued waiver.

**Holding:** Meyer’s federal constitutional claim is not cognizable under 28 U.S.C. § 1346(b), so the FTCA does not make the United States the exclusive defendant. FSLIC’s broad sue-and-be-sued clause waives the agency’s sovereign immunity from suit on the claim.

**Authority:** Thomas, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; the proposition is controlling.

#### Question 2: Bivens liability of a federal agency

**Question decided:** Bivens liability of a federal agency.

**Holding:** Bivens does not imply a damages cause of action directly against a federal agency, even when sovereign immunity has been waived.

**Authority:** Thomas, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; the proposition is controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** A waiver of immunity determines whether an entity may be sued. It does not create a constitutional cause of action or authorize damages. The officer remedy recognized in Bivens does not extend automatically to the employing agency.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Existing Bivens actions against individual officers are not narrowed. The Court does not decide any statutory cause of action, prospective remedy, or the due-process merits of Meyer’s discharge.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Albright’s source-specific reasoning is reinforced but not extended. Meyer establishes that constitutional right, sovereign-immunity waiver, cause of action, and remedy are separate legal questions. Bivens remains an individual-officer remedy and does not authorize direct agency damages.

### Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority, 510 U.S. 487 (1994)

**Docket or dockets:** 92-1223.
**Decided:** February 23, 1994.

#### Judgment

Reversed, 9-0. Supporting: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.

The Fifth Circuit’s judgment is reversed. The home-address disclosure orders may not be enforced through FOIA’s exception to the Privacy Act.

#### Opinions

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter | Supports reversal | Complete opinion |
| Concurrence | Souter | No other Justice | Supports reversal | Joins the Court and adds observations concerning the privacy interest |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Ginsburg | No other Justice | Supports reversal | Treats Reporters Committee as controlling while reserving disagreement with parts of its account of FOIA’s text |

#### Holding

**Question decided:** FOIA public interest and employee home addresses.

**Holding:** FOIA does not require public disclosure of federal employees’ home addresses because the addresses reveal virtually nothing about agency operations and their disclosure would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy. The Privacy Act’s FOIA exception therefore does not authorize release.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Thomas, joined as stated in the Opinions table above; Complete opinion. The proposition is controlling at that stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Under Exemption 6, the legally relevant public interest is the contribution disclosure makes to public understanding of governmental activity. The identity, purpose, and private benefit of the requester do not enlarge that interest. Residential information implicates privacy through unwanted contact, loss of control over further dissemination, and the connection of named individuals to their homes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not diminish collective-bargaining rights, bar disclosure with employee consent, decide every possible Privacy Act routine use, or prevent Congress from creating a separate disclosure mechanism with defined safeguards.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Reporters Committee is applied without alteration. A requester’s beneficial use of personal information does not count as a FOIA public interest unless disclosure itself illuminates government operations.

### Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510 (1994)

**Docket or dockets:** 92-857.
**Decided:** February 23, 1994.

#### Judgment

Reversed and remanded, 9-0. Supporting: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.

The Ninth Circuit shall reconsider qualified immunity using all materially relevant precedent, including Al-Azzawy. The Court does not decide whether the officers ultimately receive immunity.

Justice Ginsburg delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas.

**Opinion of the Court:** Ginsburg, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas.

#### Holding

**Question decided:** Legal authorities considered on qualified-immunity review.

**Holding:** A court reviewing qualified immunity must consider all materially relevant precedent available at the time of the challenged conduct, including authority not cited to the district court.

**Authority:** Ginsburg, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; the proposition is controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** Whether governing law clearly established the asserted right is a question of law reviewed de novo. **Application:** The Ninth Circuit identified Al-Azzawy as potentially relevant but declined to evaluate its legal effect. It must now determine what that decision and the rest of the governing law established concerning the asserted warrantless in-home arrest.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether Al-Azzawy clearly established a violation, whether the officers’ conduct amounted to an in-home arrest, whether exigent circumstances existed, or whether immunity ultimately applies.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow and Anderson are clarified as requiring courts to determine clearly established law from the complete body of materially relevant authority. Failure to cite a case below does not operate as forfeiture of the law itself.

### Fogerty v. Fantasy, Inc., 510 U.S. 517 (1994)

**Docket or dockets:** 92-1750.
**Decided:** March 1, 1994.

#### Judgment

Reversed and remanded, 9-0. Supporting: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg. No opposing Justice.

The district court shall reconsider Fogerty’s application without a plaintiff-favoring presumption and without requiring frivolousness or bad faith as a categorical condition for a prevailing defendant.

#### Opinions

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone | Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg | Supports reversal and remand | Complete opinion |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Thomas | No other Justice | Supports reversal and remand | Agrees with the evenhanded reading of § 505 but objects to its inconsistency with Christiansburg’s treatment of nearly identical language |

#### Holding

**Question decided:** Evenhanded discretionary awards under § 505.

**Holding:** Prevailing copyright plaintiffs and defendants must be evaluated under the same discretionary legal standard. Neither side is presumptively entitled to fees, and a prevailing defendant need not prove frivolousness or bad faith as a categorical prerequisite.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Stone, joined as stated in the Opinions table above; Complete opinion. The proposition is controlling at that stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** A court may consider objective unreasonableness, frivolousness, motivation, compensation, deterrence, and other circumstances faithful to the Copyright Act’s purposes. These considerations are nonexclusive, no one is mandatory, and they must be applied evenhandedly. Section 505 does not establish automatic prevailing-party fee shifting.

**Application:** Fogerty prevailed but was denied fees under the legally erroneous asymmetric threshold. The trial court must exercise its own discretion under the corrected rule rather than treat reversal as an instruction to award fees.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court adopts no presumption for defendants, makes fee awards neither routine nor automatic, and does not turn the illustrative considerations into a mandatory checklist.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The Ninth Circuit’s dual copyright-fee standard is rejected. Christiansburg is not extended to § 505, and Hensley remains relevant to reasoned discretionary fee adjudication. The decision concerns fee shifting and does not alter Campbell’s later fair-use analysis.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |

**Controlling explanation:** The instruction requirement is triggered by the combination of a prosecution argument placing future dangerousness at issue and legally fixed parole ineligibility under the available life sentence. **Application:** The jury specifically expressed concern about the meaning of life imprisonment, and the trial court’s response did not disclose parole ineligibility. The defect requires a new penalty proceeding but supplies no basis for disturbing the conviction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule does not require predictions about discretionary parole when eligibility remains legally possible or contingent. **Treatment of earlier authority:** Gardner and Skipper are extended from a general right to answer material sentencing assertions to a specific instruction requirement when future dangerousness and fixed parole ineligibility coincide. **Question decided:** RFRA and prospective implementation of the ordinance.

**Holding:** RFRA governs Clearwater’s prospective implementation of its preexisting ordinance. The broad compulsory disclosure and recordkeeping regime and the mandatory sixty-day refund rule violate RFRA as applied here. Limited identifying registration does not establish a substantial burden on this record, and written disclosure of the terms of a voluntarily offered refund policy survives.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by O’Connor, joined by Stone, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; controlling.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not find that every provision was enacted for a forbidden purpose and does not disable a neutral replacement ordinance aimed at fraud rather than religious identity.

**Question decided:** Federal regulation of noneconomic local possession.

### United States v. Harris, 25 F.3d 1275

**Decided:** April 27, 1995.

#### Judgment

Affirmed, 9-0. Supporting: Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

#### Question 1: Direct protection of an interstate transportation instrumentality

**Question decided:** Direct protection of an interstate transportation instrumentality.

**Holding:** The Commerce Clause permits Congress to protect a motor vehicle against a direct intrastate forcible taking when the federal offense requires proof of the particular vehicle’s interstate connection. Section 2119 is constitutional as applied to the taking of the Buick proved here.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Kennedy, joined by Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** An offense-specific commerce element must perform real constitutional work rather than add a remote interstate fact to an ordinary local crime. A direct attack on possession and control of a transportation instrumentality, combined with proof that the particular vehicle moved through interstate commerce, falls within Congress’s authority over instrumentalities and things in commerce. The Fifth Circuit’s theory that carjacking is federal because violent crime affects travel, insurance, and the national economy cannot survive *Lopez*. The judgment is sustained on a narrower ground. The object of the offense is the motor vehicle itself, an instrumentality of transportation, and § 2119 requires proof that the vehicle was transported, shipped, or received in interstate or foreign commerce. The Buick’s manufacture and multistate title history established that enacted connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not hold that every local offense involving an object that once crossed a state line is federal, that every crime occurring in or near a car is subject to federal jurisdiction, or that generalized insurance and productivity effects are sufficient. The holding rests on the direct forcible seizure of the transportation instrumentality and the statute’s vehicle-specific interstate requirement.

#### Question 2: Mens rea and fair notice under the then-operative § 2119

**Question decided:** Mens rea and fair notice under the then-operative § 2119.

**Holding:** Section 2119 requires knowing participation in the taking of a covered vehicle by force, violence, or intimidation. It does not require knowledge that the vehicle moved through interstate commerce or a separate intent permanently to deprive the owner.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Kennedy, joined by Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** The traditional mens rea presumption attaches to the conduct that makes the act wrongful. Forcibly removing occupants at gunpoint is not apparently innocent behavior whose criminality turns on an obscure characteristic. The interstate history is jurisdictional, and the statute contains no willfulness language requiring knowledge of federal law. The trial evidence established knowing participation in the forcible taking.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not construe materially different statutory language or remove mens rea from a fact Congress makes part of the wrongful conduct itself.

#### Question 3: Consecutive punishment under § 924(c)

**Question decided:** Consecutive punishment under § 924(c).

**Holding:** Congress authorized a consecutive § 924(c) sentence in addition to the sentence for carjacking under the then-operative § 2119, so the cumulative punishment imposed in this single prosecution does not violate the Double Jeopardy Clause.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Kennedy, joined by Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** *Blockburger* supplies a rule for determining legislative intent when that intent is uncertain. Section 924(c)’s additional-and-consecutive language resolves the issue directly, even though the same firearm conduct also satisfied an element of the carjacking offense.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide a charging pair for which Congress supplied no clear cumulative-punishment instruction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Lopez* is applied and clarified. A jurisdictional element is not talismanic; it must identify a constitutionally relevant connection. Section 2119 satisfies that requirement here because the offense directly attacks the covered transportation instrumentality itself. *X-Citement Video* and *Staples* are applied to the facts constituting wrongful conduct but are not extended to require knowledge of a jurisdictional interstate fact. *Ratzlaf* remains confined to its separate willfulness language.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Powell v. McCormack* is applied and extended from congressional exclusion to state-imposed candidate restrictions. *Storer v. Brown* remains applicable to neutral election-administration rules that do not operate as additional qualifications.

### United States v. Wellons, 32 F.3d 117

**Docket or dockets:** Not supplied in the completed render.
**Decided:** May 30, 1995.

#### Judgment

Vacated and remanded, 9-0. Supporting: Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The lower courts shall reconsider Wellons’s personal Fourth Amendment interests without treating omission from the rental agreement as categorically dispositive and shall determine any preserved warrant exception before ordering suppression.

Justice Kennedy delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

#### Question 1: Privacy in a rental vehicle

**Question decided:** Privacy in a rental vehicle.

**Holding:** A driver’s absence from a rental agreement does not by itself defeat a legitimate expectation of privacy when the driver possesses and controls the vehicle with the authorized renter’s permission and without theft, fraud, or comparable wrongful possession.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Kennedy, joined by Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** The inquiry considers the defendant’s actual relationship to the vehicle, including permission, possession of the keys, practical control, ability to exclude strangers, the owner’s retained rights, and whether possession was obtained or continued wrongfully. A private contractual driver restriction is relevant but not conclusive. The Fourth Circuit converted one contractual term into a universal constitutional forfeiture. Fourth Amendment protection turns on the individual’s own legitimate relationship to the place searched. Permission and practical control can support that relationship even when use breaches a private rental condition. Conversely, effective repossession, theft, fraud, or another wrongful basis for possession may defeat it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Permission from the renter does not automatically establish privacy under every circumstance. The Court does not protect a thief, carjacker, fraudulent renter, or person continuing possession after an effective repossession.

#### Question 2: Independent privacy in closed luggage

**Question decided:** Independent privacy in closed luggage.

**Holding:** A person’s lack of a legitimate expectation of privacy in a vehicle does not by itself eliminate a legitimate expectation of privacy in that person’s closed luggage found within the vehicle.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Kennedy, joined by Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** Privacy in a container depends on ownership, possession, control, and steps taken to preserve its contents as private. The Government may nevertheless search the container when the automobile exception, consent, a valid inventory, or another lawful authority reaches it. Privacy interests in a place and in a closed container do not merge. A person may lack lawful control of the surrounding vehicle while retaining a personal privacy interest in a bag. That interest permits a challenge to the legal authority for opening the container but does not determine whether a warrant exception applies.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide ownership of each bag, disclaimer, abandonment, the scope of probable cause, or the validity of any inventory procedure.

#### Question 3: Warrant exceptions and remedy

**Question decided:** Warrant exceptions and remedy.

**Holding:** When a lower court rejects a Fourth Amendment claim solely under an erroneous categorical privacy rule, preserved warrant exceptions and the resulting suppression question ordinarily remain for remand.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Kennedy, joined by Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** Hertz’s request, the lawful basis and timing of impoundment, the dog alert, the scope of probable cause, consent, container ownership, and standardized inventory procedures may independently determine admissibility. This Court does not convert an incomplete appellate record into trial findings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** This decision supplies the first controlling simulated rule concerning an unlisted rental-car driver and confirms that a privacy determination concerning a vehicle does not categorically determine privacy in every container within it. The ordinary *Rakas* inquiry remains individualized.

### Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority v. Hechinger, 36 F.3d 97

**Docket or dockets:** Not supplied in the completed render.
**Decided:** June 5, 1995.

#### Judgment

Affirmed, 9-0. Supporting: Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

##### Judgment, Mandate, and Remand

The revised Board-of-Review provisions granting legally operative control over Authority decisions, together with any dependent provision that would disable airport operations because the unconstitutional Board cannot act, are unenforceable and severed.

The Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority may continue ordinary airport administration under the remaining transfer statute, lease, compact, and valid operational provisions. No prior transaction is invalidated automatically. Transaction-specific reliance, finality, or remedial questions not necessary to this facial judgment remain for ordinary adjudication.

Justice Stevens delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

#### Question 1: Congressional influence and legally operative control

**Question decided:** Congressional influence and legally operative control.

**Holding:** Congressional officers or agents do not violate separation of powers merely by receiving information, attending meetings, advising, observing, reporting, or attempting to persuade an executive or operational decisionmaker. Congress crosses the constitutional line when it gives itself or its agents legally operative authority to approve, disapprove, suspend, delay, alter, or condition execution outside the constitutional legislative process.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Stevens, joined by Stone, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** The inquiry turns on the legal consequence of the asserted authority, not solely on the identity of the participant, the presence or absence of a formal vote, or the practical likelihood that political influence will be effective. Political communication among the branches is an ordinary part of constitutional government. Oversight, appropriations, hearings, reports, recommendations, and persuasion leave the lawful decisionmaker with authority to act under existing law. The revised Board possesses something different. It can determine whether specified Authority decisions become effective promptly or are subjected to a prolonged congressional-review period. That statutory consequence gives the Board legal leverage over execution, not merely a platform from which to express views.

**Application:** The Board’s ability to select between immediate effectiveness and a 60-legislative-day delay materially controls the implementation of contracts, budgets, bonds, designs, and other operational decisions. Its members’ nonvoting participation and recommendations would not alone establish a violation, but the delay authority does.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congressional affiliation, consultation, advice, observation, political pressure, and ordinary oversight are not independently unconstitutional. Congress may regulate the airports through enacted law.

#### Question 2: Report-and-wait mechanisms and bicameralism

**Question decided:** Report-and-wait mechanisms and bicameralism.

**Holding:** Congress may require advance reporting and may enact a fixed waiting period that expires automatically. It may not make the legal effectiveness of an operational decision depend on approval, disapproval, suspension, extension, or another intervention by Congress or its agent outside bicameralism and presentment.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Stevens, joined by Stone, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** A fixed statutory period leaves the legal rule established by the law already enacted. During that period Congress may investigate and enact a new law. The revised Board instead has discretion to determine whether a particular Authority action must undergo additional congressional review. That discretionary legal intervention performs legislative work without passage by both Houses and presentment to the President.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purely informational reports, hearings, recommendations, and an automatic waiting period remain permissible.

#### Question 3: Severability and continuity of airport operations

**Question decided:** Severability and continuity of airport operations.

**Holding:** The unconstitutional control mechanism is severable from the remaining legal framework governing the Airports Authority.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Stevens, joined by Stone, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** The constitutional defect lies in Congress’s retention of operational control, not in the transfer of airport administration, the interstate compact, the lease, or the Authority’s existence. The Authority can administer the airports under enacted standards without the Board. Enforcing a dependent disabling provision would recreate the unconstitutional control by making ordinary airport operations contingent on maintenance of the invalid mechanism.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not validate a transaction whose only legal authorization depended on the Board or decide a retrospective claim not necessary to the facial judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The prior Airports Authority decision is clarified. Congressional influence is not itself execution of law, but a congressional agent may not possess legal authority over whether execution proceeds. *Chadha* is applied to a discretionary congressional-control mechanism rather than extended to a purely automatic report-and-wait provision. *Federal Election Commission v. NRA Political Victory Fund* remains solely a jurisdictional decision and supplies no merits or severability holding.

#### Question 1: Violation-remedy nexus and interdistrict attraction

**Question decided:** Violation-remedy nexus and interdistrict attraction.

**Holding:** A school-desegregation remedy may continue only insofar as it remains reasonably directed to eliminating an identified vestige of the constitutional violation to the extent practicable. An intradistrict violation does not authorize interdistrict attraction or reversal of demographic movement as an independent constitutional objective.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Stone, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; controlling at the scope stated in the Opinions table.

Salary measures may be lawful when genuinely necessary to cure a proven intradistrict vestige, but they cannot be sustained solely as a means to make the district attractive to suburban students or to reverse white flight not legally attributable to an interdistrict violation. That objective introduces demographic and policy considerations too remote from the judicial task of dismantling the identified dual system.

**Application:** The lower courts relied materially on desegregative attractiveness without adequately identifying which intradistrict vestige each salary component still remedied. Component-specific reconsideration is therefore required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not categorically forbid salary measures, educational improvements, or other relief genuinely necessary to eliminate a proven vestige. It does not require premature termination or impose a fixed administrative checklist.

#### Question 2: Achievement benchmarks and incremental withdrawal of control

**Question decided:** Achievement benchmarks and incremental withdrawal of control.

**Holding:** National achievement levels are not freestanding constitutional benchmarks for determining whether desegregation remedies must continue. Federal control must be withdrawn incrementally from separable areas in which compliance is complete and the relevant vestige has been eliminated to the extent practicable.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Stone, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; controlling at the scope stated in the Opinions table.

The Equal Protection Clause requires eradication of the consequences of state-imposed segregation. Remand permits those findings without presuming either immediate termination or indefinite continuation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Test scores and other educational evidence remain relevant when tied to a proven vestige. The Court does not determine on the present record which particular programs must end or continue.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Milliken v. Bradley* is clarified. Its violation-remedy requirement applies throughout the life of a structural decree rather than only when relief is first imposed. *Freeman v. Pitts* is applied to require component-specific consideration of partial unitary status and incremental withdrawal of federal supervision. **Question decided:** Nexus between an obstructive act and a pending proceeding.

**Holding:** False statements to investigators do not violate § 1503 absent proof that the defendant knew the statements were likely to reach and obstruct a pending judicial or grand-jury proceeding.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Stone, joined by O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer in full; Stevens as to the § 1503 holding; Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas as to the § 2232(c) construction except the official-confidentiality discussion; controlling at the scope stated in the Opinions table.

**Question decided:** Disclosure after expiration of a wiretap authorization.

**Holding:** Section 2232(c) reaches a disclosure made after the identified authorization expires when the defendant knew of the application or authorization, gave notice of the possible interception, and acted with the specific purpose of obstructing the interception.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Stone, joined by O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer in full; Stevens as to the § 1503 holding; Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas as to the § 2232(c) construction except the official-confidentiality discussion; controlling at the scope stated in the Opinions table.

#### Question 1: Habitat modification that actually kills or injures wildlife

**Question decided:** Habitat modification that actually kills or injures wildlife.

**Holding:** The ESA permits the Secretary to define “harm” as significant habitat modification or degradation that actually kills or injures protected wildlife, including injury caused by material impairment of essential behavioral patterns.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by Stevens, joined by O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer; controlling at the scope stated in the Opinions table.

**Controlling explanation:** Habitat alteration alone is insufficient. The Government must establish actual death or injury to protected wildlife and the ordinary causal connection between the modification and that injury. Speculative, conjectural, or merely potential ecological effects do not constitute a completed take. “Harm” performs work beyond the neighboring verbs describing direct pursuit, capture, wounding, or killing. The Act’s incidental-take permit provision presupposes that otherwise lawful activities may cause prohibited injury indirectly, while the distinction between habitat-acquisition authority and § 9 enforcement confirms that § 9 operates only after actual injury or death. The Secretary’s narrowing definition remains within the meaning the statute can bear.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not reach habitat modification causing no actual injury, eliminate ordinary causation, or authorize regulation outside the ESA’s protected-species and permit structure.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *MCI* is distinguished. The Secretary has not eliminated or replaced an enacted requirement, but has defined an operative statutory term within the ESA’s text and structure. The Court’s six-Justice opinion applies ordinary deference within that delegated domain. Stone’s independent-construction position remains noncontrolling. The actual-injury and causation limits are controlling features of the statutory rule.

#### Question 1: Standing for voters outside the challenged district

**Question decided:** Standing for voters outside the challenged district.

**Holding:** Voters who reside outside the district alleged to embody an unconstitutional racial classification lack standing absent evidence that the legislature personally classified or otherwise concretely injured them.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by O’Connor, joined by Stone, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer; controlling at the scope stated in the Opinions table.

**Application:** A generalized objection to allegedly unlawful statewide line drawing is insufficient. The appellees reside in District 5 and identified no evidence that race controlled their placement there or that the asserted classification embodied in District 4 personally injured them. Assignment of every voter to some congressional district does not give every voter standing to challenge every district. The judgment invalidating Louisiana Act 1 is vacated, and the present action must be dismissed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether District 4 is unconstitutional, whether residents of that district would have standing, or what equal-protection standard would govern a properly presented claim.

Its objective-comparator requirement is a merits prerequisite under Voting Rights Act § 2 and does not itself supply Article III injury. This decision creates the first controlling simulated standing rule for a racial-gerrymandering claim.

### Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson, 515 U.S. 900

**Docket or dockets:** Not supplied in the completed render.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

#### Judgment

Vacated and remanded in all three cases, 5-4. Supporting: Stone, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Opposing: O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.

The district court shall determine whether Georgia’s race-predominant Eleventh District was a genuinely fitted, district-specific remedy for a demonstrated constitutional or Voting Rights Act violation or instead reflected unsupported racial allocation, maximization, packing, or separation. It shall then apply the corresponding scrutiny stated by the Court. The Court does not decide whether the Eleventh District is constitutional or unconstitutional.

#### Opinions

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Supports vacatur and remand in all three cases | Complete opinion |
| Dissent | O’Connor | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Would affirm | Would apply strict scrutiny categorically once race predominates and sustain the district court’s legal sequence |

#### Question 1: Predominance threshold

**Question decided:** Predominance threshold.

**Holding:** Race is predominant when racial considerations materially subordinate traditional race-neutral districting principles in the particular district.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone’s Opinion of the Court is joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, producing a five-Justice majority.

**Controlling explanation:** Courts examine the actual design process and the district’s boundaries, including who was included or excluded, boundary movement, the splitting or combining of communities and political subdivisions, compactness, contiguity, communities of interest, ordinary political geography, and whether a racial target displaced those ordinary considerations. Geometric bizarreness is relevant evidence but is not a threshold element.

**Application:** Georgia’s Eleventh District met the predominance threshold. The record showed that racial targets drove the inclusion of separated population centers, the exclusion of nearby areas, extensive boundary movement, and the division of counties, municipalities, and communities in ways ordinary districting principles did not explain.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Racial awareness, demographic forecasting, or compliance analysis does not alone establish predominance. The inquiry remains district-specific and turns on whether race materially subordinated the principles that otherwise would have shaped the district.

#### Question 2: Two scrutiny lanes after race predominates

**Question decided:** Two scrutiny lanes after race predominates.

**Holding:** Once race predominates, a district that is a genuinely fitted, district-specific remedy for a demonstrated constitutional or Voting Rights Act violation receives intermediate scrutiny. A race-predominant district lacking that remedial predicate, or driven by racial maximization, unnecessary packing or separation, or unsupported racial allocation, receives strict scrutiny.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone’s Opinion of the Court is joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, producing a five-Justice majority.

**Controlling explanation:** After predominance is shown, the State bears the burden of establishing the qualifying remedial lane and satisfying the corresponding scrutiny. Under intermediate scrutiny, the State must establish an important remedial objective grounded in a demonstrated violation and a substantial, district-specific fit between the racial design and that injury. Under strict scrutiny, the State must establish a compelling interest and narrow tailoring, including necessity, workable race-neutral alternatives, duration and reach, and correspondence between the classification and the identified injury. *Adarand* distinguishes structural or access-expanding remedies fitted to identified exclusion from racial rules that determine or maximize discrete allocations without an adequate remedial predicate. Redistricting requires the same operational distinction. A bounded district-specific remedy for proved vote dilution does not operate like unsupported racial sorting, but remedial language cannot justify maximization, unnecessary packing or separation, or racial allocation untethered to an established violation.

**Application:** The district court applied categorical strict scrutiny immediately after finding racial predominance. That was the wrong legal sequence. It did not determine whether Georgia’s Eleventh District qualified for the remedial intermediate-scrutiny lane or the unsupported-sorting strict-scrutiny lane, and it did not apply the State’s burden under the correct classification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not hold that Georgia’s Eleventh District is constitutional or unconstitutional. It does not decide whether Georgia can establish a demonstrated constitutional or Voting Rights Act violation, whether the district was genuinely fitted to that violation, or whether the plan satisfies the scrutiny applicable after classification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Shaw v. Reno* is clarified. Geometric bizarreness is not a threshold element, and racial predominance may be established through the complete districting process and boundary record. *Adarand* is applied and clarified in the districting setting. A genuinely fitted, district-specific remedy for a demonstrated constitutional or Voting Rights Act violation receives intermediate scrutiny, while unsupported racial allocation, maximization, or unnecessary packing or separation receives strict scrutiny. The State bears the burden of establishing the remedial lane after predominance. *Holder* and *De Grandy* remain controlling on the need for an actual statutory injury and the rejection of maximization as an independent entitlement.

### Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette, 515 U.S. 753

**Docket or dockets:** Not supplied in the completed render.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

#### Judgment

Affirmed, 7-2. Supporting: Stone, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer. Opposing: Stevens and Ginsburg.

The permit and injunction remain in force. Ohio may apply neutral identification or disclaimer requirements governing comparable private displays.

#### Opinions

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court in part and plurality opinion in part | Scalia | Stone, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer as to Parts I, II, and III; Kennedy and Thomas as to Part IV | Supports affirmance | Seven-Justice case-specific Court opinion; three-Justice categorical plurality |
| Concurrence | Thomas | No other Justice | Supports affirmance | States a broader original-meaning account |

| Dissent | Ginsburg | No other Justice | Would reverse | Regards the offered disclaimer and setting as insufficient |

#### Question 1: Private religious expression in an open public forum

**Question decided:** Private religious expression in an open public forum.

**Holding:** Ohio does not establish religion by neutrally permitting this privately owned and sponsored religious display in Capitol Square, and it may not exclude the display because its viewpoint is religious.

**Authority:** Parts I, II, and III of Scalia’s opinion are joined by seven Justices and establish the case-specific holding. No majority adopts Part IV’s categorical rule that private religious speech in a public forum can never be attributed to government.

**Controlling explanation:** When government has genuinely opened a forum on equal terms, private religious expression remains private where the forum’s history, neutral access rules, ownership and sponsorship of the display, absence of public financing or editorial control, and available identification measures make private attribution clear. A neutral identification requirement may be used, but religious expression may not alone be required to bear a special disclaimer. The cross entered the square through the same permit process available to other private speakers. Ohio did not select, finance, design, or control the message. A reasonable observer acquainted with the square’s longstanding use by diverse private groups and able to read the offered identification would not attribute the expression to the State. The Establishment Clause therefore supplies no compelling justification for the viewpoint exclusion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not approve a government-sponsored religious display, selective religious favoritism, captive-audience worship, public financing of the religious message, or circumstances in which the forum’s operation reasonably attributes the expression to the State.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Rosenberger* is distinguished. That decision concerns direct payment from a public fund for devotional activity; *Pinette* concerns private expression admitted to an existing forum without public financing. *Kiryas Joel* is applied without alteration. Neutral forum access neither specially allocates governmental authority to religion nor establishes the private speaker’s faith.

### Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller, 5 F.3d 1383

**Docket or dockets:** Not supplied in the completed render.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

#### Judgment

Affirmed, 7-2. Supporting: Stone, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer. Opposing: Stevens and Ginsburg.

Georgia shall admit Chabad’s display on the same neutral terms governing comparable private expression. The State may apply neutral rules concerning size, duration, scheduling, safety, identification, and unattended displays, or may prospectively close or redefine the forum through viewpoint-neutral rules.

#### Opinions

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O’Connor | Stone, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer | Supports affirmance | Contextual forum and attribution analysis |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Scalia | Thomas | Supports affirmance | Would apply a broader categorical rule for private religious speech in a genuinely open forum |
| Dissent | Stevens | Ginsburg | Would reverse | Regards the display’s scale, duration, unattended character, and interior Capitol location as creating an impermissible attribution risk |

#### Question 1: Exclusion of a religious viewpoint from the Rotunda forum

**Question decided:** Exclusion of a religious viewpoint from the Rotunda forum.

**Holding:** A State that opens a government-building forum to private displays on neutral terms violates the Free Speech Clause when it excludes an otherwise eligible display solely because the private speaker’s viewpoint is religious.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by O’Connor, joined by Stone, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer; controlling at the scope stated in the Opinions table.

**Controlling explanation:** Government may enforce neutral subject-matter, physical, scheduling, safety, identification, and time, place, and manner rules. It may also close or redefine a designated forum prospectively on viewpoint-neutral terms. It may not maintain the forum for private secular expression while excluding a religious perspective on an otherwise eligible subject. The menorah is privately owned, financed, and sponsored. Georgia’s policy admits diverse private displays to the Rotunda and does not select or pay for their messages. Equal access removes a religious disability; it does not confer preferential placement or governmental assistance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Georgia need not maintain the Rotunda forum, disregard neutral operational limits, or grant Chabad preferential duration or location.

#### Question 2: Establishment Clause attribution inside the Capitol

**Question decided:** Establishment Clause attribution inside the Capitol.

**Holding:** Neutral access for this clearly identified private religious display does not violate the Establishment Clause merely because the forum is located inside the State Capitol.

**Authority:** Opinion of the Court by O’Connor, joined by Stone, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer; controlling at the scope stated in the Opinions table.

**Controlling explanation:** Attribution depends on the forum’s established character, its neutral access rules, private ownership and sponsorship, governmental control or financing, and contextual measures that make private authorship clear. Location in a government building is relevant but not dispositive. The Rotunda had been used for numerous private political, cultural, educational, and religious programs and unattended exhibits. Chabad’s accompanying material identifies the private sponsor, and no public funds produce the menorah or its religious message. An informed observer would understand that Georgia permits the display rather than adopts its doctrine.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A display selected, financed, designed, controlled, or presented by the State as its own may be attributed to government. The Court also reserves a pretextual forum, obscured sponsorship, or circumstances in which the State affirmatively adopts the message.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Rosenberger* is applied. Religious speakers receive equal access to genuine civic channels, while government remains free from any duty to finance the religious message. *Pinette* is applied and clarified for a designated forum inside a government building. Interior location increases the relevance of attribution safeguards but does not override neutral access, private sponsorship, and clear identification.
