## United States v. Aguilar

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** United States v. Aguilar
- **Citation or Docket:** 515 U.S. 593; No. 94-270
- **October Term:** 1994
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits case; argued or submitted for decision on the simulated historical calendar.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 21, 1995
- **Question Presented:** Whether false statements to investigating agents establish an endeavor to obstruct a pending grand-jury proceeding. Whether disclosure intended to obstruct a wiretap violates § 2232(c) after the formal authorization has expired.
- **Question Granted:** Whether false statements to investigating agents establish an endeavor to obstruct a pending grand-jury proceeding. Whether disclosure intended to obstruct a wiretap violates § 2232(c) after the formal authorization has expired.
- **Legal Field:** Criminal law — obstruction of justice and wiretap disclosure
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** United States Court Of Appeals For The Ninth Circuit. Because he lied to Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) agents during a grand jury investigation, he also was convicted of endeavoring to obstruct the due administration of justice under § 1503. The Court of Appeals reversed both convictions, reasoning that Aguilar’s conduct in each instance was not covered by the statutory language.

- **Material Facts and Record:** Respondent Aguilar, a United States District Judge, was convicted of illegally disclosing a wiretap in violation of 18 U. S. Because he lied to Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) agents during a grand jury investigation, he also was convicted of endeavoring to obstruct the due administration of justice under § 1503. The Court of Appeals reversed both convictions, reasoning that Aguilar’s conduct in each instance was not covered by the statutory language.

### A. Law entering the case

#### Issue 1: Section 1503 obstruction nexus

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Hubbard preserves independently enacted obstruction offenses after narrowing § 1001, and Gaudin requires every element of § 1503 to be submitted and proved. Neither case defines the required obstruction nexus.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No registered reusable doctrine directly resolves this issue.

#### Issue 2: Section 2232(c) wiretap disclosure

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No simulated holding specifically construes § 2232(c).
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No registered reusable doctrine directly resolves this issue.
- **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. § 2232(c); First Amendment principles governing official confidentiality.
- **Other Authority Used:** The enacted elements and the Government’s legitimate protection of confidential interceptions control without an artificial saving construction.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM in part, REVERSE in part, and REMAND.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The reversal of the § 1503 conviction is affirmed. The § 2232(c) conviction may be reinstated, subject to remaining preserved claims and ordinary sentencing proceedings.

#### Issue 1: Section 1503 obstruction nexus

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory sufficiency question.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 1503 requires a nexus between the act and the judicial or grand-jury proceeding: the defendant must know his conduct is likely to affect that proceeding, and the conduct must have the natural and probable effect of interference. The proof here showed only false statements to investigators who might later testify.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** False statements to investigators do not violate § 1503 absent proof that the defendant knew they were likely to reach and obstruct a pending judicial or grand-jury proceeding.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The omnibus clause cannot become a general false-statement offense. Pettibone’s knowledge and nexus principles provide a text-grounded boundary. The agents were not shown to act as an arm of the grand jury, and transmission was speculative.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Direct lies to the grand jury, destruction of subpoenaed evidence, witness tampering, and statements knowingly channeled to the proceeding remain within the statute.

#### Issue 2: Section 2232(c) wiretap disclosure

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved statutory construction question.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The statute requires knowledge of an application or authorization, intent to obstruct the interception, and notice of the possible interception. It does not require the authorization to remain formally pending at the instant of disclosure.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 2232(c) reaches a disclosure made with the required knowledge and obstructive intent even after the identified authorization expires, so long as the notice concerns the possible interception authorized or sought.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** “Such interception” links intent to the known application or authorization; “possible interception” anticipates uncertainty about whether interception will occur. Adding an in-force requirement would narrow text Congress did not write.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Government must still prove the defendant’s knowledge and specific obstructive purpose; ordinary disclosure unrelated to an interception is not covered.

- **Other Authority Used:** Skinner and Von Raab recognize special-needs testing; T.L.O. supplies the school context while preserving reasonableness review.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the Ninth Circuit.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The judgment invalidating the suspicionless athlete-testing policy remains in force. The District may continue ordinary disciplinary rules, testing based on individualized reasonable suspicion, and any narrower search practice that independently satisfies the Fourth Amendment.

#### Issue 1: Suspicionless urinalysis of student athletes

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Collection and chemical analysis of urine are searches. The school setting and a genuine non-law-enforcement safety need permit departures from the warrant and probable-cause defaults, but they do not eliminate the requirement of reasonableness. Student athletes accept coaching, physical examinations, communal changing, and sport-specific safety rules; they do not thereby waive privacy in excretory functions or medical information. A documented student drug problem and the possibility of athletic injury establish a serious concern, but not that suspicionless testing of every athlete is necessary or proportionate when individualized suspicion and ordinary school tools remain workable.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A public school may not condition participation in interscholastic athletics on random urinalysis of every athlete when the record does not establish that individualized reasonable-suspicion testing or materially less intrusive measures are impracticable and the program subjects a broad class of students to recurring bodily and medical searches without individualized cause.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The program is not ordinary law enforcement, uses random selection, and limits some disclosure, which reduces its burden. Those features do not answer whether suspicionless testing is justified. The District’s evidence establishes drug use and disciplinary difficulty, but it does not show that athletes as a class caused the problem, that sport-specific risks could not be addressed through observation and reasonable-suspicion testing, or that every athlete must surrender bodily privacy to participate. Voluntary participation in a public-school activity is not a blanket waiver of the Fourth Amendment.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not bar testing based on individualized reasonable suspicion, testing after a specific safety incident, ordinary physical examinations directed to athletic fitness, or a materially narrower safety-sensitive program supported by evidence that individualized methods cannot address the risk. He does not authorize law-enforcement use or testing for unrelated medical information.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's approved position

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** October 5, 1994, including all completed OT1993 simulated holdings and registered immediate consequences.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Church of Scientology Flag Service Organization, Inc. v. City of Clearwater, released December 5, 1994, holds that private religious solicitation and private donations are not public financing; government may apply neutral anti-fraud and transaction rules but may not use church-state separation as authority to supervise religious doctrine or internal religious affairs. That distinction informs, but does not decide, whether mandatory student-fee money must pay for devotional publication.

Church of Scientology confirms that separation is not a license for governmental supervision or civil disability based on religion.

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling simulated holding specifically requires or forbids payment of mandatory student-fee money for the printing of devotional and evangelizing material.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No registered reusable doctrine directly resolves the public-financing question.
- **Other Governing Law:** Establishment Clause; Free Speech Clause; Everson v. Board of Education; Committee for Public Education & Religious Liberty v. Nyquist; Lemon v. Kurtzman; Regan v. Taxation With Representation of Washington.
- **Other Authority Used:** The religion-funding precedents distinguish neutral civic services from public support directed to religious instruction and propagation. Regan confirms that refusal to subsidize an activity does not automatically prohibit the activity.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the Fourth Circuit.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** UVA need not reimburse the printing expense from the Student Activities Fund. No further remedial instruction is required on that payment claim. UVA remains constitutionally bound to administer recognition, facilities, distribution access, security, and other genuinely neutral civic services without excluding an otherwise eligible organization because it is religious.

#### Issue 1: Religious status and access to civic channels

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the speech question because petitioners claim that the University’s refusal is religious viewpoint discrimination.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Religious speakers are equal participants in public civic life. When a public university opens recognition, meeting rooms, distribution channels, security, or another genuine private-speech forum, it may not exclude an eligible speaker because the speaker or viewpoint is religious. But equality of civic access does not make every public expenditure a speech forum and does not convert a right to participate into a right to have government pay for the religious exercise itself.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Free Speech Clause forbids religious-status and religious-viewpoint exclusion from genuinely opened civic and expressive channels, but it does not classify direct payment for devotional religious activity as mere forum access.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Wide Awake is not barred from organizing, speaking, distributing its publication, using ordinary university facilities, or participating in student life. The disputed benefit is payment from a mandatory-fee fund to produce the publication. The constitutional analysis therefore must distinguish equal access to a channel from transfer of public resources for the religious use.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not permit UVA to relabel ordinary forum access or neutral civic protection as religious funding in order to exclude religious speakers.

#### Issue 2: Mandatory-fee financing of devotional publication

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the funding question because it supplies the University’s defense and determines whether the Free Speech Clause requires reimbursement.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Government must not exclude religion from public civic life, but government has no constitutional duty to finance religious exercise, worship, evangelism, or religious formation. The Student Activities Fund consists of mandatory fees collected and allocated by a public university. Payment to an outside printer remains a transfer of public resources, and the record characterizes Wide Awake as a publication devoted to Christian doctrine, evangelism, and religious formation. UVA may decline that religious use without disqualifying the organization from neutral civic services or from a secular program for a genuinely severable secular activity.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The First Amendment requires government to treat religious speakers equally in generally available civic and expressive channels, but it does not require government to finance religious exercise. A public university therefore may not exclude a student organization from recognition, facilities, distribution, or other neutral services because of its religious character, while declining to use mandatory student-fee funds to pay directly for worship, devotional instruction, theological formation, evangelism, or proselytization. Equal citizenship for religious speakers does not entail governmental financing of religion.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The payment is not a generally available police, fire, sanitation, legal, transportation, or comparable civic service supplied directly to every eligible person. It reimburses the production of the devotional message itself. The private editorial control and disclaimer make the words private speech, but they do not make the public money private. Refusing that payment leaves petitioners free to publish with private funds and leaves their ordinary civic access intact. The University therefore may preserve the church-state boundary without imposing a civil disability based on religious status.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that religious organizations are ineligible for every public program. A religious organization may receive neutral civic services and may compete for a genuinely secular benefit on terms that do not finance religious exercise. The holding concerns the designated religious use of public funds, not religious identity alone.

## United States v. Hays / Louisiana v. Hays

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Citation or Docket:** 515 U.S. 737; No. 94-558

- **Question Presented:** Whether voters residing outside the challenged majority-minority district have standing to attack the statewide plan as a racial classification.

- **Legal Field:** Article III and equal protection — standing to challenge racial districting
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** A three-judge United States District Court invalidated Louisiana Act 1, and Louisiana and the United States appealed directly.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Petitioner seeks reversal or vacatur of the judgment below and remand for the relief authorized by the asserted rule.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Appellees claim in this litigation that Louisiana’s congressional redistricting plan (Act 1) is a racial gerrymander that violates the Fourteenth Amendment’s Equal Protection Clause. The District Court invalidated Act 1, and the State and the United States, which had precleared Act 1 pursuant to its authority under the Voting Rights Act of 1965, appealed directly to this Court.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the district court’s invalidation and REMAND for lawful redistricting.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Georgia shall adopt a lawful congressional plan. The remedial court may consider race to comply with a demonstrated constitutional or statutory obligation, but it may not pursue racial maximization, proportional allocation, or a racial configuration unsupported by the identified violation.

#### Issue 1: Racial predominance and the remedial standard

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the equal-protection question.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Racial awareness is not racial classification. Legislatures inevitably know demographic facts, may examine racial voting conditions, and may consider what the Voting Rights Act lawfully requires. Heightened scrutiny begins when race predominates, meaning it displaces the race-neutral districting principles that otherwise explain the district. Adarand then requires attention to what the racial rule does and why. Race-predominant districting that maximizes racial representation, pursues proportionality, sorts citizens without an identified remedial duty, or otherwise lacks a demonstrated legal predicate receives strict scrutiny. A race-predominant district genuinely directed to curing demonstrated vote dilution or satisfying another valid voting-rights obligation may fall within the structural, access-expanding remedial lane and receive intermediate scrutiny when the State establishes an important remedial objective and a substantial, nonstereotyped, bounded relationship between the district and the identified injury. Georgia has not established that predicate here.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Awareness of race in redistricting does not itself trigger heightened scrutiny. When race predominates without a demonstrated constitutional or statutory remedial necessity, or when the district pursues racial maximization beyond correction of an identified voting-rights injury, strict scrutiny applies. Georgia’s Eleventh District was race predominant, was not shown necessary to remedy an established violation, and fails strict scrutiny.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The Eleventh District’s shape and construction cannot be explained by compactness, contiguity, political subdivisions, communities of interest, or ordinary political geography. The record instead shows racial targets and federal pressure for an additional majority-minority district displacing those criteria. Georgia did not prove a § 2 violation, a constitutional violation, or another federal obligation requiring this configuration. The Department of Justice’s demand for maximization cannot itself create the compelling interest or tailoring that strict scrutiny requires. Because the remedial predicate is absent, the Court need not decide whether a materially different district satisfying Adarand’s structural remedial requirements would survive intermediate scrutiny.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone reserves the application of intermediate scrutiny to a race-predominant district that is genuinely necessary to correct demonstrated vote dilution or comply with another valid obligation. Any such district must be tied to the identified injury, substantially related rather than stereotyped, limited in scope and duration, reviewable, and nonmaximizing. Those propositions perform the work of the governing scrutiny and do not create a separate checklist.
