## Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña
- **Citation or Docket:** 515 U.S. 200; No. 93-1841
- **October Term:** 1994
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits case; argued or submitted for decision on the simulated historical calendar.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 12, 1995 (released after Missouri v. Jenkins)
- **Question Presented:** What scrutiny applies to the federal subcontractor compensation program and whether the present record permits final judgment.
- **Question Granted:** What scrutiny applies to the federal subcontractor compensation program and whether the present record permits final judgment.
- **Legal Field:** Equal protection — federal race-conscious contracting
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** United States Court Of Appeals For The Tenth Circuit. The District Court granted respondents summary judgment. In affirming, the Court of Appeals assessed the constitutionality of the federal race-based action under a lenient standard, resembling intermediate scrutiny, which it determined was required by Fullilove v. Klutznick, 448 U. S. 448, and Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U. S. 547.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Petitioner seeks reversal or vacatur of the judgment below and remand for the relief authorized by the asserted rule.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Most federal agency contracts must contain a subcontractor compensation clause, which gives a prime contractor a financial incentive to hire subcontractors certified as small businesses controlled by socially and economically disadvantaged individuals, and requires the contractor to presume that such individuals include minorities or any other individuals found to be disadvantaged by the Small Business Administration (SBA). The prime contractor under a federal highway construction contract containing such a clause awarded a subcontract to a company that was certified as a small disadvantaged business. The record does not reveal how the company obtained its certification, but it could have been by any one of three routes: under one of two SBA programs—known as the 8(a) and 8(d) programs—or by a state agency under relevant Department of Transportation regulations.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argued January 17, 1995; historical decision and simulated event June 12, 1995.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified in the controlled simulation record.

The syllabus reports the lower-court citation and Supreme Court disposition as 16 F. 3d 1537, vacated and remanded. - **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O’Connor announced the judgment and wrote the principal opinion; Justices Scalia and Thomas concurred separately; Justices Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg wrote or joined dissents.

- **Other Authority Used:** Metro supports federal remedial latitude; Croson requires strict scrutiny for race-determinative contracting. Stone limits each by the measure’s operation.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** VACATE the Tenth Circuit and REMAND.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The lower courts shall determine how race operated in the program and then apply the governing level of review stated here. They may conduct lawful factfinding concerning certification, rebuttability, decisional weight, alternatives, and the identified remedial predicate.

#### Issue 1: Merits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone openly reconciles and limits Metro Broadcasting and Croson. A genuinely remedial, nonexclusive federal measure that expands access without making race decisive for a discrete award is reviewed for an important remedial objective and substantial relationship. When race decides or effectively decides who receives a particular contract, payment, or comparable benefit, strict scrutiny governs. The record does not reveal which operation occurred here.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A federal race-conscious measure that expands access without determining a discrete award may receive intermediate scrutiny under Metro Broadcasting, while a classification that determines or effectively determines a particular governmental benefit receives strict scrutiny under Croson; this record requires remand to identify the program’s operation.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The precedents entering the case point in different directions. Stone preserves Congress’s capacity to dismantle identified exclusion while requiring the most searching review when the racial rule allocates a zero-sum benefit to identified persons. Formal labels and nominal percentages do not control; actual legal effect does.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Every adverse racial exclusion or race-determinative allocation remains subject to strict scrutiny. General societal discrimination, racial balance for its own sake, and permanent racial administration do not qualify as remedial predicates. Stone does not create a six-part gateway.

## Ryder v. United States

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** Ryder v. United States
- **Citation or Docket:** 515 U.S. 177; No. 94-431
- **October Term:** 1994
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits case; argued or submitted for decision on the simulated historical calendar.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 12, 1995
- **Question Presented:** Whether the de facto officer doctrine validates a military appellate judgment rendered by improperly appointed civilian judges after a timely Appointments Clause objection.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the de facto officer doctrine validates a military appellate judgment rendered by improperly appointed civilian judges after a timely Appointments Clause objection.
- **Legal Field:** Appointments Clause, timely structural challenge, and de facto officer doctrine
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The United States Court of Military Appeals held that civilian judges of the Coast Guard Court of Military Review were inferior officers whose appointments by the General Counsel of the Department of Transportation did not satisfy the Appointments Clause, but it affirmed Ryder’s conviction under the de facto officer doctrine.

- **Material Facts and Record:** Ryder, an enlisted member of the Coast Guard, was convicted by court-martial of drug offenses. Two civilian judges on the three-judge Coast Guard Court of Military Review panel had been appointed by the Department of Transportation’s General Counsel rather than by the President, a court of law, or the head of a department. Ryder raised the Appointments Clause objection during his direct military appeal and continued to press it through the available review process.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenge was timely and preserved. Stone finds no jurisdictional or vehicle bar and reaches the remedy for the conceded appointment defect.

#### Issue 1: Timely Appointments Clause challenge and de facto officer doctrine

- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No registered reusable doctrine directly resolves this issue.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Court of Military Appeals and REMAND.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Ryder is entitled to a new review before a lawfully appointed military appellate panel. The conviction is not automatically dismissed.

#### Issue 1: Timely Appointments Clause challenge and de facto officer doctrine

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The defect concerns the constitutional source of the judges’ adjudicative authority, not a technical assignment error. The de facto officer doctrine may protect institutional continuity and settled acts in appropriate circumstances, but it cannot deny relief to a litigant who timely challenged the authority of the officers deciding his own case and pursued that objection through the available process.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A party who timely challenges the unconstitutional appointment of officers adjudicating his case is entitled to a fresh adjudication by lawfully appointed officers; the de facto officer doctrine does not validate the defective panel’s judgment against that party.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The civilian judges were inferior officers appointed by an official who was neither the President, a court of law, nor the head of a department. Ryder objected before the defective adjudication became final. Preserving the Coast Guard appellate system does not require preserving the judgment entered against the timely challenger by officers lacking lawful appointment. A new appellate review provides complete relief without dismissing the underlying conviction or unsettling unrelated judgments.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not reopen final judgments lacking a timely challenge, invalidate routine ministerial acts, or decide the treatment of officers whose authority was not contested through an available channel.

## Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Chickasaw Nation

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM in part, REVERSE in part, and REMAND.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Oklahoma may not enforce the motor-fuels tax against the Tribe under the current statute and may not tax income of tribal members residing in Indian country. It may tax wages of members residing outside Indian country, subject to ordinary law.

#### Issue 1: Motor-fuels tax

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved legal-incidence question and declines to decide the Hayden-Cartwright Act argument first raised on the merits.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Absent clear congressional authorization, a State may not impose a tax whose legal incidence falls on a Tribe or tribal members in Indian country. The statute’s language and structure place legal liability on the tribal retailer.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Oklahoma’s motor-fuels tax, as currently written, may not be enforced against the Tribe because its legal incidence falls on the tribal retailer in Indian country and Congress has not clearly authorized that tax.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The State does not require pass-through to consumers and imposes reporting, payment, and liability on the retailer. Legal incidence supplies the administrable first question and respects Congress’s primary role. Oklahoma remains free to amend the tax prospectively within federal limits.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Court does not decide the late-raised Hayden-Cartwright argument or a tax whose legal incidence is lawfully shifted to nontribal distributors or consumers.

#### Issue 2: Income tax on tribal members

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved income-tax question.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A State lacks authority to tax a tribal member’s income earned from the Tribe when the member resides in Indian country. Residence outside Indian country changes the territorial and sovereign basis, and the ordinary state income tax may then apply absent federal law to the contrary.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Oklahoma may not tax the tribal wages of members residing in Indian country, but may tax members residing outside Indian country under its generally applicable income tax.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** McClanahan protects reservation Indians within Indian country from state income taxation. The categorical territorial rule does not follow members to off-reservation residences where Oklahoma provides ordinary governmental services and no federal enactment supplies exemption.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide nonmember employees, income from nontribal sources, or a federal statute expressly altering state authority.
