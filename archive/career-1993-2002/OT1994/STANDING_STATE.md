**Current stage:** Certiorari granted; plenary merits briefing and oral argument completed; merits adjudication paused before judgment. No Supreme Court judgment, vote, opinion, or holding has been entered.

**Operative grant or order:** Certiorari was granted for the OT1994 merits docket by the supplied controlled instruction. No separate grant date, Supreme Court docket number, limiting order, or reformulated question was supplied.

**Last Supreme Court action or material milestone:** Oral argument occurred January 10, 1995. The scheduled March 20, 1995 merits event was paused under the Engine’s source-conflict rule after revalidation disclosed a material conflict concerning the trial posture and governing standard of review.

**Court below:** United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit; the judgment affirmed Little Rock’s adult-business zoning ordinance and its amortization provisions.

**Question before the Court:** Whether the adult-business zoning ordinance is a valid content-neutral regulation of secondary effects that leaves reasonable alternative avenues of communication, and whether applying its amortization provisions to existing businesses violates the First Amendment, Due Process Clause, Takings Clause, or Bill of Attainder Clause.

**Grant or order scope:** No limiting order, issue exclusion, consolidation instruction, or separate grant text was supplied. The matter therefore remains open on the lower-court questions carried in the controlled grant.

**Argument or submission history:** Argued January 10, 1995, during OT1994, before Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

**Next nonroutine act or controlling condition:** A corrected or superseding controlled case brief, or another authoritative reconciliation of the trial posture, must establish whether the district court ruled on summary judgment or after a bench trial and what findings and standard of review govern. The Engine must then revalidate the matter before a merits decision may be rendered.

**Procedural uncertainty:** The controlled brief states that the facial and as-applied claims were resolved on a developed summary-judgment record. The published Eighth Circuit opinion states that the district court ruled after a bench trial, made findings concerning the City’s predominant purpose, and received clear-error review. The difference is material to the standard of review and the legal significance of the purpose finding. Until corrected, no merits disposition is authorized.
