## Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products Co.

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products Co.
- **Citation or Docket:** 514 U.S. 159; No. 93-1577
- **October Term:** 1994
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits case; argued or submitted for decision on the simulated historical calendar.
- **Simulated Event Date:** March 28, 1995
- **Question Presented:** Whether color alone can serve as a protectable trademark under the Lanham Act.
- **Question Granted:** Whether color alone can serve as a protectable trademark under the Lanham Act.
- **Legal Field:** Trademark law — color marks and functionality
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** United States Court Of Appeals For The Ninth Circuit. Qualitex won in the District Court, but the Ninth Circuit set aside the judgment on the infringement claim because, in its view, the Trademark Act of 1946 (Lanham Act) does not permit registration of color alone as a trademark.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Petitioner seeks reversal or vacatur of the judgment below and remand for the relief authorized by the asserted rule.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Petitioner Qualitex Company has for years colored the dry cleaning press pads it manufactures with a special shade of green gold. After respondent Jacobson Products (a Qualitex rival) began to use a similar shade on its own press pads, Qualitex registered its color as a trademark and added a trademark infringement count to the suit it had previously filed challenging Jacobson’s use of the green-gold color. Qualitex won in the District Court, but the Ninth Circuit set aside the judgment on the infringement claim because, in its view, the Trademark Act of 1946 (Lanham Act) does not permit registration of color alone as a trademark.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argued January 9, 1995; historical decision and simulated event March 28, 1995.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified in the controlled simulation record.
- **Public Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 514 U.S. 159, bound volume 514 (https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/514bv.pdf), supplies the caption, record summary, lower-court posture, argument and decision dates, historical judgment, and opinion structure. The syllabus reports the lower-court citation and Supreme Court disposition as 13 F. 3d 1297, reversed. Supreme Court Database release 2025_01 (http://scdb.wustl.edu/) supplies docket and vote metadata.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** 13 F. 3d 1297, reversed. Vote: 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Breyer, J., delivered the opinion for a unanimous Court.
- **Historical Holding:** The Lanham Act permits the registration of a trademark that consists, purely and simply, of a color. (a) That color alone can meet the basic legal requirements for use as a trademark is demonstrated both by the language of the Act, which describes the universe of things that can qualify as a trademark in the broadest of terms, 15 U. S. C. § 1127, and by the underlying principles of trademark law, including the requirements that the mark “identify and distinguish [the seller’s] goods . . . from those manufactured or sold by others and to indicate [their] source,” ibid., and that it not be “functional,” see, e. g., Inwood Laboratories, Inc. v. Ives Laboratories, Inc., 456 U. S. 844, 850, n. 10. The District Court’s findings (accepted by the Ninth Circuit and here undisputed) show Qualitex’s green-gold color has met these requirements.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Ninth Circuit and REMAND.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The lower courts shall recognize color as potentially registrable and determine infringement, secondary meaning, and functionality under ordinary Lanham Act rules.

#### Issue 1: Merits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the statute’s broad definition of a mark. A color can identify source after acquiring secondary meaning, and trademark protection then serves the same informational function as words or symbols. The functionality doctrine prevents control of useful or competitively necessary color features.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A color may be protected as a trademark when it has acquired source-identifying significance and is not functional.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The green-gold dry-cleaning press-pad color can distinguish Qualitex’s goods without performing a utilitarian function. Existing doctrines address shade confusion, depletion, and competitive need rather than imposing a categorical ban absent from the Act.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not protect color in the abstract, functional colors, or colors lacking secondary meaning, and it leaves ordinary likelihood-of-confusion proof intact.

## Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Jefferson Lines, Inc.

- **Citation or Docket:** 514 U.S. 175; No. 93-1677

- **Question Granted:** Whether Oklahoma may impose a sales tax on the full price of an interstate bus ticket sold within the State.

- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Petitioner seeks reversal or vacatur of the judgment below and remand for the relief authorized by the asserted rule.

After Jefferson filed for bankruptcy, petitioner, Oklahoma Tax Commission, filed proof of claims for the uncollected taxes, but the Bankruptcy Court found that the tax was inconsistent with the Commerce Clause in that it imposed an undue burden on interstate commerce and presented a danger of multiple taxation. Apportionment is not required merely because the service later crosses state lines.

- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The tax attaches to the completed sale, not to mileage or gross receipts earned in several States. Oklahoma treats local and interstate tickets alike, and the purchaser receives the protections and market infrastructure associated with the in-state transaction.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not authorize multiple taxation of interstate receipts, origin-based discrimination, or taxes failing external or internal consistency.

## Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc.

#### Issue 1: Legislative reopening of a final Article III judgment

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Landgraf v. USI Film Products and Rivers v. Roadway Express govern retroactive legal consequences but do not authorize legislative reopening of final judgments.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Presumption against retroactive legal consequences addresses statutory temporal reach; the Article III finality limit is independently constitutional.
- **Other Governing Law:** U.S. Const. art. III; Hayburn’s Case; United States v. Klein; Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co.
- **Other Authority Used:** Klein and the early separation-of-powers cases preserve the judiciary’s conclusive role. Wheeling Bridge permits new law to alter the future operation of continuing equitable relief and does not authorize legislative revision of a completed private judgment.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the judgment of the Sixth Circuit.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The final dismissal remains binding. Section 27A(b) may not compel its reopening. No separate remand instruction is required.

#### Issue 1: Legislative reopening of a final Article III judgment

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Article III’s allocation of judicial power. Congress may change substantive law, apply new law to pending cases, alter the rules governing prospective relief, create generally applicable judicial remedies, and waive federal claims or defenses when otherwise constitutionally permissible. It may not direct an Article III court to reopen and replace a final judgment in a private case after the judicial process for revising that judgment has ended. The defect is not principally unfairness or disappointed reliance. It is that another department has asserted legal authority to revise the judiciary’s conclusive disposition of a case.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress violates Article III when it commands federal courts to reopen final judgments in private civil actions and enter new dispositions under a subsequently enacted rule.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 27A(b) does not merely change the law applicable to pending litigation. It identifies cases already dismissed by final judgment and orders their restoration. If Congress may require a completed Article III judgment to be replaced, the original judgment was not a conclusive exercise of judicial power. Wheeling Bridge is different because new law changed what continuing prospective relief could lawfully regulate going forward; it did not legislate a substitute disposition for a completed damages or dismissal judgment.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not bar prospective legislation, new law governing nonfinal cases, lawful alteration of continuing equitable relief, waiver of a federal claim or defense, or generally applicable judicial reopening rules administered by courts consistently with Article III.
