### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** VACATE the jurisdictional characterization, AFFIRM the dismissal of the ministerial-employment claims on the merits, and REMAND for entry of a judgment consistent with this disposition.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court shall dismiss with prejudice the Title VII claims insofar as they challenge ordination, ministerial promotion, discipline, assignment, or continued ministerial employment. A genuinely severable salary, property, tort, contract, or other claim may proceed only if independently pleaded and capable of resolution without deciding religious doctrine, ministerial fitness, or the church’s selection of its religious leaders.

#### Issue 1: Church autonomy and ministerial employment

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits because jurisdiction exists to determine whether applying Title VII to the pleaded claims is constitutionally permissible.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A religious body must retain authority to select, promote, supervise, discipline, and remove persons performing genuine ministerial functions. That protection follows function rather than title. It does not depend on whether a court finds the church’s reasons admirable, fair, or consistent with civil equality norms. Even a substantial evidentiary showing that sex entered the decision cannot authorize a civil court to decide whether the church’s doctrinal or ministerial reasons were valid or pretextual, because that inquiry itself would transfer the choice of religious leadership to the State.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Religion Clauses bar application of Title VII to a claim whose adjudication or requested relief would require a civil court to determine whether a religious body lawfully ordained, promoted, assigned, disciplined, retained, or removed a minister.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Young’s claims challenge decisions concerning her ministerial status and continued service. The prohibition against sex discrimination is a serious civil norm, and moral disapproval of women would not justify discrimination in ordinary secular employment. Ministerial selection is different because civil adjudication of motive, qualification, and remedy would require the court to review the church’s criteria for religious authority. Neutral civil law remains applicable to severable secular conduct that can be resolved without deciding who should preach, lead worship, teach doctrine, or represent the faith.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Receipt of public money, tax benefits, licensure, or other contact with government does not by itself waive ministerial autonomy. Government may attach lawful and clearly stated conditions to the secular use of public funds and may recover diverted funds, but it may not purchase authority to select clergy. The holding does not exempt every employee of a religious institution or every church transaction from civil law.

#### Issue 2: Jurisdictional characterization

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches and resolves the characterization because it affects the form and preclusive effect of the judgment.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The ministerial protection is a constitutional limitation on applying Title VII to the merits, not a withdrawal of federal-question jurisdiction. The court had statutory power to decide the defense.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A federal court possessing statutory jurisdiction over a pleaded Title VII claim should enter a merits judgment when the Religion Clauses prohibit applying the statute to the ministerial decision, rather than dismissing for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Jurisdiction, statutory coverage, constitutional defense, and remedy are separate questions. The constitutional defense defeats these claims, but it does not retroactively eliminate the court’s authority to say so.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide the treatment of a claim that is wholly insubstantial or pleaded solely to manufacture federal jurisdiction.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Ninth Circuit and REMAND.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court may enforce the agreement only as an impeachment waiver if the Government proves that the represented defendant knowingly and voluntarily accepted that use before the proffer. #### Issue 1: Impeachment waiver

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Rule 410 and Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 11(e)(6) protect plea discussions, but neither contains an express antiwaiver command. A represented defendant may knowingly and voluntarily agree that his own proffer statements may be used to impeach him if he later testifies inconsistently. The Government, as the party invoking the waiver and controlling the proffer condition, bears the burden of proving knowing and voluntary agreement.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A represented defendant’s pre-proffer agreement permitting use of his plea-discussion statements solely to impeach his own inconsistent testimony is enforceable when the Government proves the waiver was knowing and voluntary.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Impeachment use does not place the proffer before the jury before the defendant acts. It prevents the defendant from using the exclusion rule as a license to give materially inconsistent testimony. That limited consequence is different from allowing the Government to present the statements as substantive proof in its case in chief. Voluntariness remains a real inquiry, including representation, clarity of the term, prosecutorial threats or deception, and the circumstances in which the agreement was demanded.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not approve case-in-chief use, sentencing use, or a waiver that effectively eliminates the right to trial. Defense counsel’s cross-examination, introduction of independent evidence, or argument of an alternative theory does not ordinarily trigger this personal-testimony waiver. A broader trigger would require a separate, explicit agreement and a distinct validity analysis not presented here. Existing rule-based exceptions for perjury, false statements, and comparable abuse remain intact.
