## California Department of Corrections v. Morales

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** California Department of Corrections v. Morales
- **Citation or Docket:** 514 U.S. 499; No. 93-1462
- **October Term:** 1994
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits case; argued or submitted for decision on the simulated historical calendar.
- **Simulated Event Date:** April 25, 1995
- **Question Presented:** Whether applying California’s amendment allowing deferral of some parole suitability hearings increases punishment for prisoners convicted before enactment.
- **Question Granted:** Whether applying California’s amendment allowing deferral of some parole suitability hearings increases punishment for prisoners convicted before enactment.
- **Legal Field:** Ex Post Facto Clause — parole-hearing frequency
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** United States Court Of Appeals For The Ninth Circuit. The District Court denied the petition, but the Court of Appeals reversed, holding that the retrospective law made a parole hearing less accessible to respondent and thus effectively increased his sentence in violation of the Ex Post Facto Clause.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Petitioner seeks reversal or vacatur of the judgment below and remand for the relief authorized by the asserted rule.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Respondent was sentenced to 15 years to life for the 1980 murder of his wife and became eligible for parole in 1990. As required by California law, the Board of Prison Terms (Board) held a hearing in 1989, at which time it found respondent unsuitable for parole for numerous reasons, including the fact that he had committed his crime while on parole for an earlier murder. Respondent would have been entitled to subsequent suitability hearings annually under the law in place when he murdered his wife. The law was amended in 1981, however, to allow the Board to defer subsequent hearings for up to three years for a prisoner convicted of more than one offense involving the taking of a life, if the Board finds that it is not reasonable to expect that parole would be granted at a hearing during the intervening years and states the bases for the finding.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argued January 9, 1995; historical decision and simulated event April 25, 1995.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.

The syllabus reports the lower-court citation and Supreme Court disposition as 16 F. 3d 1001, reversed. ### Historical Supreme Court comparator

## United States v. Williams

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** United States v. Williams
- **Citation or Docket:** 514 U.S. 527; No. 94-395
- **October Term:** 1994
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits case; argued or submitted for decision on the simulated historical calendar.
- **Simulated Event Date:** April 25, 1995
- **Question Presented:** Whether 28 U.S.C. § 1346(a)(1) authorizes a refund action by a person who paid another’s tax under protest to remove a federal lien from her property.
- **Question Granted:** Whether 28 U.S.C. § 1346(a)(1) authorizes a refund action by a person who paid another’s tax under protest to remove a federal lien from her property.
- **Legal Field:** Federal courts and taxation — third-party refund jurisdiction
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** United States Court Of Appeals For The Ninth Circuit. The District Court accepted this jurisdictional argument, but the Court of Appeals reversed.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Petitioner seeks reversal or vacatur of the judgment below and remand for the relief authorized by the asserted rule.

## Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson

- **Question Presented:** Whether FAA § 2 covers the termite-treatment contract only if the parties contemplated substantial interstate activity, or whenever the transaction actually involved commerce within Congress’s lawful power.
- **Question Granted:** Whether FAA § 2 covers the termite-treatment contract only if the parties contemplated substantial interstate activity, or whenever the transaction actually involved commerce within Congress’s lawful power.
- **Legal Field:** Arbitration and federalism — FAA commerce coverage
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Supreme Court Of Alabama. After respondents Dobson, who had purchased Gwin’s home, sued in state court following a termite infestation, petitioners asked for, but were denied, a stay to allow for arbitration under the contract and § 2 of the Federal Arbitration Act, which makes enforceable a written arbitration provision in “a contract evidencing a transaction involving commerce.” The Alabama Supreme Court affirmed on the basis of a state statute invalidating predispute arbitration agreements, ruling that the federal Act applies only if, at the time the parties entered into the contract and accepted the arbitration clause, they “contemplated” substantial interstate activity.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Petitioner seeks reversal or vacatur of the judgment below and remand for the relief authorized by the asserted rule.
- **Material Facts and Record:** The termite prevention contract between petitioner exterminators and respondent Gwin, a homeowner, specified that any controversy thereunder would be settled exclusively by arbitration. After respondents Dobson, who had purchased Gwin’s home, sued in state court following a termite infestation, petitioners asked for, but were denied, a stay to allow for arbitration under the contract and § 2 of the Federal Arbitration Act, which makes enforceable a written arbitration provision in “a contract evidencing a transaction involving commerce.” The Alabama Supreme Court affirmed on the basis of a state statute invalidating predispute arbitration agreements, ruling that the federal Act applies only if, at the time the parties entered into the contract and accepted the arbitration clause, they “contemplated” substantial interstate activity. Despite some such activities, the court found that these parties “contemplated” a transaction that was primarily local and not “substantially” interstate.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** October 5, 1994, including all completed OT1993 simulated holdings and registered immediate consequences.

#### Issue 1: Merits

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling simulated holding specifically resolves this question.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No registered reusable doctrine directly resolves this question.
- **Other Governing Law:** U.S. Const. art. I, § 8, cls. 3 and 18; Gibbons v. Ogden; Wickard v. Filburn; Perez v. United States; Maryland v. Wirtz.
- **Other Authority Used:** Wickard preserves aggregation for genuinely economic classes; Perez concerns an economic class; neither supplies a general police power.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the Fifth Circuit.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Lopez’s federal conviction under 18 U.S.C. § 922(q) may not be reinstated. The judgment does not affect state authority to prohibit the same conduct.

#### Issue 1: Merits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone holds that federal commerce authority is broad but enumerated. Congress may regulate channels; instrumentalities, persons, and things in commerce; economic activity whose properly defined class substantially affects an actual interstate market; and local conduct whose inclusion is reasonably necessary to a comprehensive valid interstate economic regime. Section 922(q) regulates bare local possession without a commercial element, a genuine interstate nexus, or a necessary relationship to a broader economic scheme. The Court cannot insert the missing jurisdictional element.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Commerce Clause does not authorize a stand-alone federal prohibition of noneconomic local firearm possession near schools based only on an attenuated chain from violence to education, productivity, and the national economy.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The Government’s theory converts every serious local problem into commerce because nearly every activity has eventual economic consequences. That would erase enumeration. #### Issue 1: Merits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone finds no jurisdictional, preservation, or vehicle bar and reaches the question presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Harper and separates retroactivity from remedy. Once a federal rule governs cases still open on direct review, state reliance on the invalid statute does not authorize a special substantive exception preserving the defeated judgment. Ordinary procedural doctrines may still operate if independently valid and evenhanded.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A state court may not deny retroactive application of a controlling federal constitutional rule to a nonfinal case merely because a party relied on the invalidated statute.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Creating a reliance exception would make the federal rule prospective in substance while pretending to apply it. Concrete reliance can matter under an existing remedial doctrine, but no such doctrine supports the judgment here.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding leaves ordinary res judicata, statutes of repose, waiver, harmless error, and other independently valid remedial rules intact.
