### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Second Circuit judgment and remand for a new trial limited to defendant-specific causation, lawful same-decision grounds, immunity, and relief.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court shall treat Jeffries’s Albany speech as citizen speech as a matter of law because the speech was not assigned, commissioned, controlled, authorized, or delivered as part of his teaching or chair duties. The new trial shall determine which defendants caused the shortening of his chair term, whether any defendant proves that the same action would have occurred for an independently lawful reason, and any remaining immunity, damages, or other relief questions. CUNY may rely on independently established failure to perform assigned duties or other lawful conduct-based grounds, but not on the protected private speech itself or on hostility and reputational consequences generated by its viewpoint.

#### Issue 1: Capacity and protection of the Albany speech

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the First Amendment merits because the speech’s legal capacity can be resolved from the undisputed nature of the Albany event and the institutional role established in the record.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Albany speech was CUNY-controlled official speech or Jeffries’s own citizen speech, and what consequence follows from that classification for the challenged adverse action.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the received distinction between employee speech and citizen speech but openly narrows the received disruption rule at this boundary. A public university may direct and discipline speech that a professor is assigned, authorized, or required to deliver as part of teaching, administration, or delegated chair functions. It may not convert off-duty citizen speech into institutional speech merely because the speaker holds an academic title, speaks about education, or is publicly associated with the university. When the speech is the employee’s own citizen speech on a matter of public concern, offense, controversy, audience hostility, institutional embarrassment, reputational pressure, and disruption consisting of those reactions may not themselves supply the lawful ground for punishment. The university remains free to act on independently proved failure to perform assigned duties, threats, harassment, insubordination in official work, misuse of delegated authority, or other independently regulable conduct. To the extent existing public-employee doctrine permits punishment of this kind of private citizen speech based only on predicted disruption generated by reaction to its viewpoint, Stone would narrow that proposition accordingly.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A public university may control speech delivered pursuant to a professor’s assigned teaching or administrative duties, but it may not attribute off-duty citizen speech to the university or punish the professor for that private speech merely because of controversy, audience hostility, reputational harm, or predicted disruption generated by reaction to the viewpoint expressed. Jeffries’s Albany speech was citizen speech because it was not part of his assigned teaching or chair duties.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Jeffries spoke off campus at an event unaffiliated with CUNY and addressed public-school curriculum and racial history on his own behalf. Nothing in the record makes the speech an assigned lecture, curricular act, departmental communication, or exercise of delegated chair authority. The chair was described as ministerial rather than a policymaking office. Those facts make the Albany speech Jeffries’s speech, not CUNY’s. CUNY could evaluate whether he actually performed his university responsibilities, but it could not shorten his chair term merely because outsiders objected to protected private expression or officials anticipated reputational or political consequences from that expression.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** This holding does not prevent a public university from controlling assigned classroom instruction, official curricular decisions, departmental communications, or speech delivered through delegated chair authority. It does not protect threats, harassment, unlawful discrimination in performance of official duties, refusal to perform assigned work, or other independently regulable misconduct. Merely holding the title of professor or chair does not make private speech official.

#### Issue 2: Defendant-specific causation and lawful same-decision grounds

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches causation far enough to reject the Second Circuit’s judgment as a matter of law. The conflicting special verdicts do not permit the appellate court to select one factual theory and conclusively treat the nine trustees’ votes as lawful superseding causes.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Mt. Healthy defendant by defendant. Jeffries must establish that protected speech substantially motivated each defendant’s legally relevant conduct and that the conduct caused the adverse action. A defendant may avoid liability by proving that the same action would have occurred for an independently lawful reason. A vote cannot be labeled a lawful superseding cause merely by assuming that reaction-generated disruption was constitutionally sufficient under Issue 1, and nonvoting conduct that materially caused the Board to consider or adopt the challenged action remains subject to ordinary causation principles.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The irreconcilable special verdicts and differentiated defendant roles require a limited new trial on defendant-specific causation and lawful same-decision grounds; the Second Circuit could not enter judgment for all defendants by treating the nine trustees’ votes as automatically lawful superseding causes.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The verdicts simultaneously credited a disruption rationale and found retaliatory motive and but-for causation as to identified defendants. Some defendants voted, while others allegedly played different causal roles in placing or shaping the one-year-chair action before the Board. Once the lawful-ground premise is corrected under Issue 1, the verdicts cannot support a single appellate conclusion resolving every defendant. The proper remedy is a focused retrial rather than reinstatement of inconsistent findings or appellate factfinding.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not determine which individual defendant caused the adverse action, whether any defendant satisfies the same-decision defense, qualified immunity, damages, punitive damages, or the precise form of any prospective relief.
