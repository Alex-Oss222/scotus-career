#### Issue 1: Decency and respect considerations in arts grants

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Simulated speech law permits government to define lawful subsidy scope but forbids laundering viewpoint censorship through funding. Government speech and subsidy choices remain distinct from open private-expression forums.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Government may speak and choose what to subsidize, but may not reject an eligible private speaker for viewpoint in a program for independent expression.
- **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment; NEA governing statutes including 20 U.S.C. § 954(d)(1).
- **Other Authority Used:** The competitive and discretionary character of arts grants does not erase the enacted command. The statutory phrase “taking into consideration” requires the criteria to influence grant decisions and cannot fairly be narrowed into legal irrelevance.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the First Amendment invalidation of the decency-and-respect proviso.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The decency-and-respect proviso may not govern NEA grants supporting independent private expression. The remaining viewpoint-neutral program criteria remain available, and vagueness and unrelated remedial questions remain unresolved.

#### Issue 1: Decency and respect considerations in arts grants

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the First Amendment facial challenge and does not decide the separate Fifth Amendment vagueness theory.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The phrase “taking into consideration” requires conscious use of decency and respect for prevailing public beliefs. Treating those criteria as legally inconsequential would delete the enacted command. Because the criteria favor conventional or deferential expression over offensive, irreverent, or challenging viewpoints, they cannot govern a grant program for independent private expression.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 954(d)(1) requires viewpoint-sensitive considerations to influence grants supporting independent private expression, and the Court cannot avoid that command by treating the considerations as optional. The decency-and-respect proviso is therefore facially invalid under the First Amendment.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Government may define an arts program and use neutral judgments of merit and administration, but it may not make conformity with prevailing beliefs a criterion for selecting independent expression. The mandatory text cannot be saved through a construction that makes it perform no legal work.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Congress may continue using viewpoint-neutral criteria concerning artistic merit, subject, audience, educational suitability, finances, and program administration. This position neither requires funding every applicant nor decides the settled individual claims, Fifth Amendment vagueness, or public financing of religious exercise.

- **Material Facts and Record:** Abbott disclosed asymptomatic HIV infection and sought routine dental treatment in Bragdon’s office. #### Issue 1: Mootness and continuing damages controversy

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Spencer v. Kemna* requires a concrete continuing stake after exposure ends, while an otherwise valid damages claim for a completed constitutional injury ordinarily preserves a live controversy.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Article III mootness and redressability.

- **Other Authority Used:** Graduation, the forms of relief requested, and the existence of a damages claim are the material posture facts.

#### Issue 2: Religious music and religious venues under the Establishment Clause

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Existing establishment doctrine distinguishes government instruction about or performance of religious art for genuine secular pedagogical purposes from government-conducted worship or devotional exercise.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Government attribution, secular curricular purpose, and nondevotional use of religious material.
- **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments.
- **Other Authority Used:** Repertoire, curricular explanation, musical significance, venue selection, and the school's asserted pedagogical purposes are event-specific facts.

#### Issue 3: Free Exercise and compelled expression

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Neutral curricular requirements do not ordinarily target religion, and a genuine no-penalty opt-out can eliminate compelled participation in an asserted religious exercise or message.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Neutral and generally applicable school rules; compelled-expression principles.
- **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments.
- **Other Authority Used:** The grading policy, scope of the opt-out, whether the student was required to affirm a religious message, and any practical penalty for declining participation.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The dismissal of prospective relief as moot is affirmed. The damages claim preserves a live controversy sufficient to decide the granted merits questions, but those claims fail on the supplied record. No ruling is made on teacher-led devotional allegations outside the simulated grant.

#### Issue 1: Mootness and continuing damages controversy

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone holds the student-specific prospective claims moot after graduation but reaches the granted constitutional merits because the damages claim concerns completed conduct and remains live.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Graduation eliminates a personal stake in prospective regulation of the choir absent continuing exposure or a certified class. A damages claim for completed alleged constitutional injury, however, prevents the entire controversy from becoming moot.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Bauchman’s graduation moots her individual prospective claims, but the remaining damages claim preserves a live controversy concerning the completed conduct challenged in the grant.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** An injunction would no longer alter Bauchman’s school experience, but a valid damages judgment could redress a completed constitutional injury if one were proved. The Court therefore may resolve the granted merits questions while dismissing prospective relief.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Court does not decide a certified class, a continuing school consequence, or prospective claims by current students.

#### Issue 2: Religious music and religious venues under the Establishment Clause

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the Establishment Clause challenge to the selection, rehearsal, and performance of religious music, including performances in religious buildings.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Public-school music education may include religious works and may use religious buildings for genuine artistic or pedagogical reasons when the school neither conducts worship nor uses the repertoire or venue to advance devotional observance.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Establishment Clause does not prohibit a public-school choir from selecting, rehearsing, and performing religious music, including in religious buildings, when the choices serve genuine secular artistic and pedagogical purposes and the school does not conduct or sponsor worship.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Sacred music forms a substantial part of the choral canon, and teaching it as music does not itself establish religion. The supplied record identifies educational reasons for the repertoire and venues and does not establish that the school converted the performances into official worship.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Government-led prayer, devotional instruction, denominational observance, or venue selection adopted for a religious purpose would present a different question and is not decided here.

#### Issue 3: Free Exercise and compelled expression

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the challenge to required rehearsal or performance subject to the grade-neutral opt-out.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A neutral curricular assignment involving religious music does not compel religious exercise or ideological affirmation when the student has a genuine, grade-neutral option not to participate in the challenged performance and is not required to profess belief or engage in worship.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Requiring study or performance of religious music as part of a neutral choir curriculum, subject to a genuine grade-neutral opt-out, does not violate the Free Exercise Clause or compelled-expression principles on this record.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The school required musical study rather than religious profession, and the opt-out removed the claimed forced participation without academic penalty. The Constitution protects Bauchman from compelled worship or affirmation, not from encountering religious works in a secular curriculum.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A sham opt-out, lost credit, stigma, exclusion from the program, compelled profession of belief, or actual worship requirement would present a different case.
