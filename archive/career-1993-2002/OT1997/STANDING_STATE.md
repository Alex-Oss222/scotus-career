## 3. Open Certiorari, Merits, and Original Matters

### Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education, 136 F.3d 364 (4th Cir. 1998) (en banc), No. 97-1835

**Current stage:** Post-grant merits stage. No merits briefing completion, argument date, or disposition is supplied.

**Operative grant or order:** Certiorari granted October 5, 1998, in simulated No. 97-1835, limited to the two supplied First Amendment questions.

**Last Supreme Court action or material milestone:** Simulated grant of certiorari on October 5, 1998.

**Court below:** The United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, sitting en banc, affirmed the Western District of North Carolina's Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal of the teacher's First Amendment claim. The controlling judgment is *Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education*, 136 F.3d 364 (4th Cir. 1998), as amended March 18, 1998. The earlier panel decision was vacated on rehearing en banc and is not the judgment under review.

**Question before the Court:**

1. Whether a public-school teacher's selection, production, and direction of curricular material pursuant to assigned teaching responsibilities constitutes the teacher's protected First Amendment speech or curricular expression subject to the school system's institutional control.
2. If a teacher retains a First Amendment interest in curricular expression, what constitutional standard governs school officials' decision to alter the curriculum or impose an adverse employment action because of that curricular choice.

**Grant or order scope:** Review is confined to the federal First Amendment claim arising from Boring's selection, production, and direction of a play through an assigned high-school drama class. Federal and state due-process, liberty, and North Carolina constitutional claims are outside the simulated grant.

**Next nonroutine act or controlling condition:** No established next nonroutine act or controlling condition is supplied.

**State source:** Supplied simulated grant of October 5, 1998, No. 97-1835; *Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education*, 136 F.3d 364 (4th Cir. 1998) (en banc).

### Jackson v. Benson, 218 Wis. 2d 835, 578 N.W.2d 602 (Wis. 1998), No. 98-376

**Current stage:** Post-grant merits stage. No merits briefing completion, argument date, or disposition is supplied.

**Operative grant or order:** Certiorari granted November 9, 1998, in simulated No. 98-376, limited to the two supplied federal Establishment Clause questions.

**Last Supreme Court action or material milestone:** Simulated grant of certiorari on November 9, 1998.

**Court below:** The Supreme Court of Wisconsin reversed the Wisconsin Court of Appeals and remanded with directions to grant the State's summary-judgment motion, dismiss the challengers' remaining facial claim, and dissolve the injunction against the amended Milwaukee Parental Choice Program. The court held that the program did not violate the federal Establishment Clause or the state-law provisions resolved below.

**Question before the Court:**

1. Whether a religiously neutral school-choice program violates the Establishment Clause when public funds may be used at religious schools only because eligible parents independently select those schools.
2. What distinguishes constitutionally permissible aid resulting from genuinely independent private choice from governmental financing attributable to the State itself.

**Grant or order scope:** Review is limited to the federal Establishment Clause. The simulated grant does not reopen the Wisconsin religious-benefit, private-or-local-bill, uniformity, public-purpose, or facial equal-protection rulings.

**Next nonroutine act or controlling condition:** No established next nonroutine act or controlling condition is supplied.

### Russell v. Burris, 146 F.3d 563 (8th Cir. 1998), Nos. 98-397 and 98-399

**Current stage:** Post-grant merits stage in two companion petitions. No merits briefing completion, argument date, consolidation order, or disposition is supplied.

**Operative grant or order:** Certiorari granted November 16, 1998, in simulated Nos. 98-397 and 98-399, limited to the two supplied campaign-contribution questions.

**Last Supreme Court action or material milestone:** Simulated grants of certiorari on November 16, 1998.

**Court below:** The United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded for declaratory and injunctive relief. As relevant here, it held unconstitutional Arkansas Initiated Act I's limits of $300 per election for specified statewide offices and $100 per election for other state offices. The appeal followed a bench-trial judgment reported at 978 F. Supp. 1211 (E.D. Ark. 1997).

**Question before the Court:**

1. Whether candidate-contribution limits of $100 and $300 per election are so low that they materially impair protected political association and a candidate's ability to amass sufficient resources for effective advocacy, notwithstanding the State's anticorruption interest.
2. What showing of actual or apparent corruption and constitutional fit must the government make when defending contribution limits dramatically below the limits previously sustained by the Court.

**Grant or order scope:** Review is limited to the constitutionality of the $100 and $300 direct candidate-contribution limits and the evidentiary and tailoring requirements governing those limits. Separate limits and classifications concerning political action committees, the standing and ripeness rulings, and other severability questions resolved below are outside the simulated grants.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** No. 98-397, *Citizens for Clean Government v. Russell*, and No. 98-399, *Burris v. Russell*, arise from the same Eighth Circuit judgment and are companion petitions. No consolidation order is supplied. Ron Russell's later petition, No. 98-1030, is not part of the operative grant.

**Next nonroutine act or controlling condition:** No established next nonroutine act or controlling condition is supplied.

**State source:** Supplied simulated grants of November 16, 1998, Nos. 98-397 and 98-399; *Russell v. Burris*, 146 F.3d 563 (8th Cir. 1998).

### Redmon v. United States, 138 F.3d 1109 (7th Cir. 1998) (en banc), No. 98-243

**Current stage:** Post-grant merits stage. No merits briefing completion, argument date, or disposition is supplied.

**Operative grant or order:** Certiorari granted January 11, 1999, in simulated No. 98-243, limited to the two supplied Fourth Amendment questions.

**Last Supreme Court action or material milestone:** Simulated grant of certiorari on January 11, 1999.

**Court below:** The United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, sitting en banc, affirmed the judgment of the Central District of Illinois. The district court had denied Redmon's suppression motion, after which he entered a conditional guilty plea preserving the issue. The court of appeals held on the particular record that Redmon lacked an objectively reasonable expectation of privacy in garbage placed for collection in closed containers on a common driveway immediately outside his attached garage.

**Question before the Court:**

1. Whether *California v. Greenwood*'s rule governing garbage exposed for collection extends to garbage kept in closed containers immediately beside an attached garage within, or at the boundary of, the home's curtilage.
2. Whether an intent eventually to discard the garbage, or its accessibility to collectors or other private persons, eliminates Fourth Amendment protection when police must enter or exploit constitutionally protected residential space to inspect it.

**Grant or order scope:** Review is limited to the warrantless inspections of the garbage containers and their use to establish probable cause for the later residential search warrant. Redmon's separate career-offender sentencing challenge is excluded. Whether the containers were within the curtilage, and whether their location was constitutionally protected residential space, remain disputed questions rather than established facts.

**Next nonroutine act or controlling condition:** No established next nonroutine act or controlling condition is supplied.

**State source:** Supplied simulated grant of January 11, 1999, No. 98-243; *United States v. Redmon*, 138 F.3d 1109 (7th Cir. 1998) (en banc).

### Reno v. Goncalves, 144 F.3d 110 (1st Cir. 1998), No. 98-835

**Current stage:** Post-grant merits stage. No merits briefing completion, argument date, or disposition is supplied.

**Operative grant or order:** Certiorari granted March 8, 1999, in simulated No. 98-835, limited to the three supplied immigration and habeas questions.

**Last Supreme Court action or material milestone:** Simulated grant of certiorari on March 8, 1999.

**Court below:** The United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit reversed the District of Massachusetts's dismissal of Goncalves's 28 U.S.C. § 2241 petition. The court held that § 2241 jurisdiction remained available for the pure statutory and constitutional questions presented, held that AEDPA § 440(d) did not apply to former § 212(c) applications pending when AEDPA took effect, granted the writ to that extent, and remanded to the Board of Immigration Appeals for a discretionary determination.

**Question before the Court:**

1. Whether AEDPA and IIRIRA clearly withdrew district-court jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 2241 to hear statutory or constitutional habeas challenges to deportation by covered criminal aliens.
2. If habeas jurisdiction survives, whether AEDPA § 440(d)'s restrictions on former § 212(c) discretionary relief apply to applications or proceedings already pending when AEDPA took effect.
3. What interpretive weight, if any, is owed to the Attorney General's contrary determination that § 440(d) applies to pending applications.

**Grant or order scope:** Review is limited to the continued availability and relevant scope of § 2241 habeas review, the applicability of AEDPA § 440(d) to pending former § 212(c) applications, and the interpretive weight due the Attorney General's determination. The First Circuit did not decide Goncalves's equal-protection claim, which is outside the simulated grant.

**Next nonroutine act or controlling condition:** No established next nonroutine act or controlling condition is supplied.

**State source:** Supplied simulated grant of March 8, 1999, No. 98-835; *Goncalves v. Reno*, 144 F.3d 110 (1st Cir. 1998).

### Schleifer v. City of Charlottesville, 159 F.3d 843 (4th Cir. 1998), No. 98-1146

**Current stage:** Post-grant merits stage. No merits briefing completion, argument date, or disposition is supplied.

**Operative grant or order:** Certiorari granted March 22, 1999, in simulated No. 98-1146, limited to the three supplied constitutional questions.

**Last Supreme Court action or material milestone:** Simulated grant of certiorari on March 22, 1999.

**Court below:** A divided panel of the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit affirmed the Western District of Virginia's final merits judgment rejecting the constitutional challenges and upholding Charlottesville's juvenile nocturnal-curfew ordinance. The reported preliminary-injunction decision at 963 F. Supp. 534 is not the final judgment under review.

**Question before the Court:**

1. Whether, and to what extent, minors possess a constitutionally protected liberty to move about and remain in public places at night, and what constitutional review governs a juvenile curfew burdening that liberty.
2. Whether a juvenile curfew impermissibly interferes with a fit parent's constitutional authority to permit and direct a minor child's otherwise lawful activities.
3. Whether the ordinance's exceptions and enforcement standards provide adequate notice, constrain official discretion, and sufficiently preserve protected First Amendment activity.

**Grant or order scope:** Review encompasses the distinct claims concerning minors' liberty and the applicable level of review, parental authority, and the facial adequacy of the ordinance's exceptions and enforcement standards under the First and Fourteenth Amendments. It does not encompass an as-applied prosecution or a claim based on enforcement facts arising after the judgment below.

**Next nonroutine act or controlling condition:** No established next nonroutine act or controlling condition is supplied.

**State source:** Supplied simulated grant of March 22, 1999, No. 98-1146; *Schleifer v. City of Charlottesville*, 159 F.3d 843 (4th Cir. 1998).

### City of Dallas v. Dallas Fire Fighters Association, 150 F.3d 438 (5th Cir. 1998), No. 98-966

**Current stage:** Post-grant merits stage. No merits briefing completion, argument date, or disposition is supplied.

**Operative grant or order:** Certiorari granted March 29, 1999, in simulated No. 98-966, limited to the two supplied questions concerning the Dallas Fire Department's out-of-rank promotions.

**Last Supreme Court action or material milestone:** Simulated grant of certiorari on March 29, 1999.

**Court below:** The United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit affirmed the judgment holding the race-conscious and sex-conscious out-of-rank promotions unconstitutional. It separately reversed and rendered judgment for the City concerning the deputy-chief appointment, which is outside the simulated grant. The appeal arose from consolidated actions in the Northern District of Texas.

**Question before the Court:**

1. What showing of the government's own past discrimination is constitutionally sufficient to justify race-conscious or sex-conscious remedial promotions in public employment.
2. Once a sufficient remedial predicate exists, whether out-of-rank promotions that bypass higher-scoring qualified candidates are sufficiently fitted to the identified discrimination under the distinct constitutional standards governing race and sex.

**Grant or order scope:** Review is confined to the federal constitutional validity of the out-of-rank promotions to driver-engineer, lieutenant, and captain. It does not place the City's affirmative-action plan as a whole, the separate deputy-chief appointment, or the Title VII and Texas-law claims before the Court.

**Next nonroutine act or controlling condition:** No established next nonroutine act or controlling condition is supplied.

**State source:** Supplied simulated grant of March 29, 1999, No. 98-966; *Dallas Fire Fighters Association v. City of Dallas*, 150 F.3d 438 (5th Cir. 1998).
