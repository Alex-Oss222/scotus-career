### 28. [United States v. Scheffer, 523 U.S. 303](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep523/usrep523303/usrep523303.pdf)

**Case.** *United States v. Scheffer*.

**Proposed holding.** Rule 707 does not violate the right to present a defense when excluding disputed polygraph evidence serves legitimate evidentiary interests and leaves the accused able to testify and present the underlying exculpatory facts.

**Limit.** This does not authorize suppression of underlying facts, selective government use of polygraphs, exclusion of independently admissible statements merely because they arose during testing, or exclusion of constitutionally indispensable defense evidence.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The disposition survives as an application of existing constitutional doctrine, not a new evidentiary framework.

**Novelty.** Issue reservation or minimalist disposition; application disagreement only secondary.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes. The direct due-process-standing ground fully reverses the no-standing judgment, so rung 5 requires Stone to reserve the broader third-party equal-protection route.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty; secondarily, statutory or constitutional interpretation and remedial or dispositional difference.

## 52. [Eastern Enterprises v. Apfel](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep524/usrep524498/usrep524498.pdf)

**Question presented.** Does §9706(a)(3) of the Coal Act’s assignment of substantial retroactive premium liability to Eastern violate the Fifth Amendment’s Takings Clause or Due Process Clause?

**What the case is about.** Eastern signed the industry benefit agreements from 1947 through 1964, left coal mining in 1965 before later lifetime-benefit promises arose, and was assigned premium responsibility for more than 1,000 former workers. **Precedent applied.** Simulated *Landgraf*. It confirms that the Act attaches new consequences to completed conduct, but Congress clearly prescribed that temporal reach, so the statutory presumption does not answer the constitutional questions.

**Old sim.** Stone rejected the takings theory but proposed reversing under a new severe-retroactivity due-process formulation based on unexpected burden, remoteness, reliance, and attenuated responsibility. The old Court accepted his takings classification and as-applied result but treated the due-process theory only as an alternative rationale already independently advanced; Stone caused no doctrinal change.

**Stone innovation.** None now. Stone applies the established property categories and received rational-basis standard instead of creating a heightened retroactivity rule.

**Stone’s position now.** Affirm. Rung 3 requires application of *Turner Elkhorn*, *R.A. Gray*, *Connolly*, and *Concrete Pipe* unchanged: a general payment obligation is not an appropriation of identified property, and Congress could rationally charge an employer whose former miners receive the benefits and that participated in the industry benefit arrangements. The liability’s severity, unexpectedness, and remoteness make the policy harsh but do not establish animus, forbidden purpose, sham reasoning, or the absence of every rational connection.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply and clarify *Landgraf*; reject the takings extension; apply received rational-basis review; reject the old heightened substantive-due-process rule.

**Proposed holding.** Section 9706(a)(3) imposes a general payment obligation and does not take an identified property interest on this record. Its application to Eastern survives due process because assigning former miners’ benefit costs to an employer that employed them and participated in the industry benefit arrangements rationally serves a legitimate legislative purpose.

**Limit.** The position does not decide an appropriation of a specified fund, lien, or asset; unclear statutory retroactivity; punitive liability; or an assignment whose connection to the regulated party is affirmatively nonexistent.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty; secondarily, application disagreement only.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes. Stone’s current commitment to ordinary economic regulation and received rational-basis review cannot support the old heightened severe-retroactivity rule, so reversal becomes affirmance.

## 53. [National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep524/usrep524569/usrep524569.pdf)

**Question presented.** Is §954(d)(1)’s requirement that the NEA take “general standards of decency and respect” into consideration facially invalid under the First and Fifth Amendments? Stone would resolve the First Amendment issue and not reach the Fifth Amendment vagueness claim.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *Rosenberger*. Its viewpoint-neutrality rule governs a program supporting independent private expression; its separate restriction on direct financing of devotional religious use is not presented here.

**Stone’s position now.** Affirm the First Amendment invalidation of the decency-and-respect proviso. Because decency and respect for prevailing public beliefs favor conventional or deferential expression over offensive, irreverent, or challenging viewpoints, they cannot govern an independent-expression grant program; rung 5 leaves vagueness unresolved.

**Doctrinal intent.** Interpret text; apply and extend viewpoint-neutrality doctrine; affirm facial invalidation; reserve vagueness.

**Proposed holding.** Section 954(d)(1) requires viewpoint-sensitive considerations to influence grants supporting independent private expression, and the Court cannot avoid that command by treating the considerations as optional. **Limit.** Congress may continue using viewpoint-neutral criteria concerning artistic merit, subject, audience, educational suitability, finances, and program administration. **Novelty.** Extension of existing doctrine; secondarily, statutory or constitutional interpretation.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes. The old narrowing construction made mandatory statutory language functionally meaningless and conflicts with Stone’s current prohibition against laundering viewpoint censorship through subsidies for independent expression.

## 54. [Clinton v. City of New York](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep524/usrep524417/usrep524417.pdf)

**Question presented.** Do the New York and Snake River plaintiffs have Article III standing to challenge completed presidential cancellations? If so, may Congress authorize the President to cancel operative provisions after bicameral passage and presentment?

**What the case is about.** After signing the relevant statutes, the President invoked the Line Item Veto Act to cancel a Medicaid recoupment provision and a tax provision supporting a cooperative’s pending acquisition. **Stone innovation.** None. This is ordinary constitutional application and extension of the existing bicameralism-and-presentment rule.

**Stone’s position now.** Affirm. Rung 1 requires resolving standing first: each cancellation produced a concrete legal or financial disadvantage redressable by treating the provision as operative. **Proposed holding.** The plaintiffs have standing because the completed cancellations altered their concrete legal and financial positions. After a provision becomes law through bicameralism and presentment, the President may not use independent policy judgment to cancel its legal effect.

**Limit.** The position does not restrict executive factfinding, selection within enacted ranges, genuine spending discretion, administrative prioritization, or contingent legislation. It does not decide separate discretionary-budget applications or unnecessary severability questions.

**Novelty.** Extension of existing doctrine.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The disposition and constitutional boundary survive, although the old named cancellation framework and unnecessary severability discussion are omitted.

## 55. [Swidler & Berlin v. United States](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep524/usrep524399/usrep524399.pdf)

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty; secondarily, issue reservation or minimalist disposition.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old privilege rule and disposition remain consistent with supplied *Jaffee* and Stone’s current source-based evidentiary jurisprudence.
