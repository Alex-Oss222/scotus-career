See Hormel v. Helvering, 312 U. S. 552, 556. Where that proceeding is not adversarial, the reasons for a court to require issue exhaustion are much weaker than where the parties are expected to develop the issues themselves.

- **Open Point:** Genuine doctrinal innovation or modification: physical attachment of a tracking device to a protected effect for the purpose of obtaining information is itself a search. The seizure rule and short-term public-road monitoring rule remain separate.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse in part, affirm in part, and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The receiving court shall implement the controlled disposition and the following source-preserved instruction: Modify the search trigger; distinguish search from seizure; apply *Knotts* to public-road monitoring; remand for warrant, exception, causation, and harmless-error questions.

#### Issue 1: Physical attachment of tracking devices

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The deliberate physical attachment of a government tracking device to a person's vehicle, an “effect,” to obtain information is a search and requires a warrant or recognized exception. Attachment is not necessarily a seizure unless it meaningfully interferes with possession, control, or use. Short-term monitoring of movements exposed on public roads is not independently a search under *Knotts* on this record, but information obtained through the unlawful attachment may still require ordinary causal and remedial analysis.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Government physically searches an effect when it attaches a tracking device to that effect for the purpose of obtaining information. The attachment is a seizure only if it meaningfully interferes with possessory control, and short-term public-road monitoring remains separately governed by *Knotts*.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Reverse in part, affirm in part, and remand. The application is confined to the record summarized in Section I and makes no factual finding the reviewing record does not support.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide prolonged aggregate monitoring, attachment with valid consent or a warrant, or suppression without the ordinary causal and remedial inquiry.
