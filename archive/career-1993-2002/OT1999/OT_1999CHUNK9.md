#### Issue 1: Section 2 coverage of election-to-appointment change

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 2 reaches, in a narrow category, a State's change from an existing elected governing body to an appointed body because the change removes a previously existing electoral opportunity. Section 2 does not require every school board to remain elective and does not automatically invalidate universal elimination of the vote.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A change from a lawful elected body to an appointed body may be tested under Section 2 when the challenged restructuring removes an existing electoral opportunity, but liability still requires proof that protected voters have less political opportunity than other citizens.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The case concerns a change in the machinery through which voting opportunity previously existed, not an office that has always been appointive. That makes the restructuring cognizable without converting Section 2 into a permanent command that the office remain elected. The application is confined to the record summarized in Section I and makes no factual finding the reviewing record does not support.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Universal loss of a direct election is not by itself racial vote dilution, and Section 2 does not create a general right to elect school boards.

#### Issue 2: Section 2 benchmark

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The benchmark is the lawful electoral system immediately preceding the restructuring. No hypothetical governing-body design is required because the State itself supplied a concrete, operative comparator before eliminating the election.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** For a Section 2 challenge to replacement of an elected body with an appointed body, the lawful immediately preceding electoral system supplies the benchmark, subject to proof of unequal political opportunity under the statutory totality.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The prior system provides an objective and administrable comparator consistent with the simulation's benchmark requirement. The comparison does not end the case, because plaintiffs must still establish racial inequality rather than merely the universal disappearance of the vote. The application is confined to the record summarized in Section I and makes no factual finding the reviewing record does not support.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The benchmark rule is confined to a direct restructuring from an existing lawful election and does not create a benchmark for an office that has always been appointive.

#### Issue 3: Racially discriminatory restructuring

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone does not reach the direct Equal Protection claim against Ohio because sovereign immunity disposes of that defendant. He reaches the merits of the preserved claim against Cleveland’s mayor, a municipal defendant not protected by Ohio’s immunity.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A State ordinarily may choose an appointive school board, but a proper nonimmune defendant may not implement or maintain an otherwise permissible structure adopted for the purpose of disadvantaging citizens because of race. The absence of a freestanding right to elect the board changes what a neutral State may do; it does not authorize purposeful racial discrimination.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Equal Protection Clause forbids a proper nonimmune defendant from implementing replacement of an elected body with an appointed body when racial discrimination materially motivated the restructuring, even though a racially neutral appointment system would be constitutional.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The direct Equal Protection claim against Ohio remains dismissed on sovereign-immunity grounds. The claim against Cleveland’s mayor is different. Governmental authority to choose appointment rather than election does not include authority to choose that structure for a racially discriminatory purpose. Because the historical courts rejected the purpose theory rather than making findings under Stone’s rule, the proper course is remand for application of the governing purpose and causation framework. No finding of discriminatory purpose is made by this Court.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Purpose must be proved under governing equal-protection doctrine; disparate impact or awareness alone is insufficient.

#### Issue 4: One-person-one-vote and appointment power

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The appointment arrangement does not violate one-person-one-vote merely because Cleveland's mayor is elected only by Cleveland voters while the appointed board serves residents of additional municipalities. One-person-one-vote principally regulates elected representation, and residents do not gain a constitutional vote for every official who appoints an entity exercising authority over them.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A genuinely appointive multi-jurisdictional board does not violate one-person-one-vote solely because the appointing mayor is elected by only one municipality's voters.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Once the board is genuinely appointive, the Constitution does not automatically require all persons subject to the board's authority to vote for the appointing official. A proven racially discriminatory design remains separately actionable under the preceding issue. The application is confined to the record summarized in Section I and makes no factual finding the reviewing record does not support.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not protect a nominally appointive arrangement that is actually an electoral device or a structure adopted for a prohibited racial purpose.

#### Issue 5: Effect of no independent right to elect the office

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The absence of an independent constitutional right to elect the school board defeats only the claim that appointment is inherently unconstitutional. It does not defeat a claim that the State abolished the election for a racially discriminatory purpose.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A neutral decision to use appointment rather than election is ordinarily constitutional, but the lack of a right to elect the office does not immunize racially discriminatory restructuring.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Equal protection regulates why government chose the structure when discriminatory purpose is proved. The law does not transform the office into a constitutionally required elective office; it forbids use of structural discretion as an instrument of racial disadvantage. The application is confined to the record summarized in Section I and makes no factual finding the reviewing record does not support.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The statutory Section 2 claim, the discriminatory-purpose claim, and the one-person-one-vote claim remain separate and retain their own elements and remedies.
