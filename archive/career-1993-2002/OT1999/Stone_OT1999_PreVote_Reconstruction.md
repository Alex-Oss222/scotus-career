# Stone Pre-Vote Reconstruction, October Term 1999

## Run-wide correction after confirmation

Stone's constant orientation is equality, realistic operation, and meaningful access to courts and justice. That orientation applies throughout the confirmed ladder, especially when filing restrictions, review channels, procedural defaults, standing, remedy, or practical availability determine whether a right can actually be used. It does not create jurisdiction, a cause of action, liability, governmental power, an immunity exception, or relief that the governing source withholds. When clear controlling law points the other way, Stone applies it or identifies the precise change under rung 12 rather than disguising preference as application.

The supplied Holdings and Standards registers are the complete new-simulation precedent set for this run. The OT1999 attachment is used only as historical evidence of Stone's earlier objective and the old Court's treatment. No old OT1999 disposition and no pre-vote position reconstructed here becomes precedent for another case.

## Material changes and expanded docket

This file contains 88 matters: 70 merits or summary matters and 18 simulation-granted lower-court cases. The simulation grants are integrated as merits matters and include grant dates, complete questions presented, simulated argument dates, and simulation-assigned opinion dates.

User-supplied positions control the revisions to *Kimel*, *Bossier Parish*, *Fraternal Order of Police*, *Gillespie*, and *Valdespino*, and the requested refinements to *Morrison*, *Olech*, *Adarand*, and *Jones*. The latest reasoning supplied for *Morrison*, *Bossier Parish II*, *Kimel*, *Olech*, and *Adarand* has been rechecked and incorporated into the case-specific fields below. Four newly granted matters remain deliberately unanswered because the user reserved voting-rights, Fourteenth Amendment, and equal-protection analysis for a separate run: *Reform Party of Allegheny County*, *Eisenberg*, *Mixon*, and *Bradley*. Their question sets are now complete and expanded to include unequal-treatment scrutiny in *Reform Party*, the K-12 diversity-interest issue in *Eisenberg*, the benchmark and extraterritorial voting issues in *Mixon*, and funding-conditioned waiver plus prospective enforcement in *Bradley*.

## Merits cases and summary dispositions

### 1. *Dempsey v. Martin*

**Case.** *Dempsey v. Martin*

**Question presented.** Whether persistent abuse of subsidized noncriminal filing process permits denial of in forma pauperis status and a tailored prospective filing condition?

**What the case is about.** Dempsey submitted another pro se certiorari petition with an IFP motion after a documented pattern of frivolous noncriminal filings. The underlying merits are not before the Court; only filing administration is.

**Precedent applied.** None supplied.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed to deny IFP status and impose a prospective restriction limited to noncriminal filings. His proposed rule was: Persistent frivolous use of subsidized noncriminal process permits a fee-and-compliance condition while criminal and habeas access remains open. The old Court adopted the position in substance. Attribution: Stone caused no change; he aligned with or supported a ground that existed independently.

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary remedial move: replace a wealth-based future bar with a litigant-specific prefiling screen tied to documented abuse.

**Stone's position now.** Deny IFP status for the present filing. For later noncriminal certiorari or extraordinary-writ submissions, require leave to file or a short certification identifying a nonfrivolous ground, rather than an unconditional fee-only bar. Rung 8 controls because docket protection can be achieved without making poverty itself a permanent barrier to a future meritorious civil filing.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply Rule 39.8; narrow the prospective restriction; preserve a usable route to court.

**Proposed holding.** Repeated frivolous use of subsidized Supreme Court process permits denial of the current IFP motion and a litigant-specific prefiling screen, but an indigent litigant must retain a route for a future nonfrivolous submission.

**Limit.** Criminal-sanction and habeas filings remain unrestricted, and the order neither adjudicates the current petition's merits nor excuses ordinary filing requirements.

**Novelty.** Remedial or dispositional difference; narrowing or limiting existing doctrine.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes. The old fee-and-compliance condition could make indigence an absolute bar to a later meritorious civil filing; screening addresses abuse without foreclosing justice by wealth.

### 3. *Flippo v. West Virginia*

**Case.** *Flippo v. West Virginia*

**Question presented.** Whether the Fourth Amendment recognizes a general crime-scene exception permitting an extended warrantless evidentiary search and opening of closed containers at a homicide scene?

**What the case is about.** After a homicide, officers secured the scene but then conducted an approximately sixteen-hour evidentiary search and opened a closed briefcase without a warrant. The Fayette County trial court denied suppression and entered the murder judgment; the state high court denied discretionary review.

**Precedent applied.** None supplied.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed to reverse the operative state judgment and remand. His proposed rule was: Officers may secure a homicide scene and address emergencies, but an extended evidentiary search or opening of a closed container requires a warrant or recognized exception. The old Court adopted the position in substance. Attribution: Stone caused no change; he aligned with or supported a ground that existed independently.

**Stone innovation.** None. Ordinary move: apply *Mincey*; reject a categorical crime-scene exception; remand preserved exception and harmless-error issues.

**Stone's position now.** Reverse the operative state judgment and remand. Officers may secure a homicide scene and address emergencies, but an extended evidentiary search or opening of a closed container requires a warrant or recognized exception. The commitment driving the result is that a warrant exception extends only as far as its emergency purpose; the seriousness of a homicide is not freestanding search authority. Leave consent, exigency, and harmless error for the state courts. Rungs 2, 3, and 8 control. Prompt emergency entry, protective activity, and evidence preservation while a warrant is sought remain lawful.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply *Mincey*; reject a categorical crime-scene exception; remand preserved exception and harmless-error issues.

**Proposed holding.** Officers may secure a homicide scene and address emergencies, but an extended evidentiary search or opening of a closed container requires a warrant or recognized exception.

**Limit.** Prompt emergency entry, protective activity, and evidence preservation while a warrant is sought remain lawful.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old disposition remains supported by the controlling law and Stone's current jurisprudence; no additional doctrinal change is required.

### 4. *Texas v. Lesage*

**Case.** *Texas v. Lesage*

**Question presented.** Whether a plaintiff may obtain compensatory or nominal relief for a discrete admissions denial when the government conclusively proves it would have made the same decision under a lawful process?

**What the case is about.** Lesage challenged the use of race in a graduate-admissions process, but the State's undisputed evidence showed that he would have been rejected under a lawful race-neutral process. The Fifth Circuit nevertheless allowed the § 1983 claim tied to the 1996-1997 denial to proceed.

**Precedent applied.** The supplied *Mt. Healthy* same-decision principle and the supplied *Lujan* standing rule.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed to reverse as to retrospective damages and remand for dismissal of that claim; preserve only an independently live prospective challenge. His proposed rule was: Conclusive same-decision proof defeats relief tied to the adverse decision but does not validate the challenged policy or foreclose forward-looking relief supported by standing. The old Court adopted the position in substance. Attribution: Stone caused no change; he aligned with or supported a ground that existed independently.

**Stone innovation.** Precise remedial modification: same-decision proof defeats outcome-based compensation but not nominal relief for a separately proved unconstitutional classification in the decisional process.

**Stone's position now.** Reverse insofar as the claim seeks compensatory relief for the rejection, because the lawful same decision breaks causation as to that loss. Remand, however, for nominal damages if Lesage proves that an impermissible racial classification actually governed his consideration, and preserve any independently live prospective equal-competition claim. Rungs 4, 8, and 12 control: remedy must track injury, but a completed constitutional wrong is not erased merely because it did not alter the ultimate outcome.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply same-decision causation to compensatory relief; distinguish process injury; preserve nominal and prospective remedies.

**Proposed holding.** Conclusive proof that the same admissions decision would have occurred lawfully bars compensatory damages for the denial, but it does not bar nominal damages for a proven unconstitutional use of race in the decision process or an independently live prospective claim.

**Limit.** The position does not validate the policy, establish that discrimination occurred, award admission, or permit compensation for an outcome the lawful process would also have produced.

**Novelty.** Modification of existing doctrine; remedial or dispositional difference.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes. The old simulation extended same-decision proof to nominal relief; Stone now separates loss causation from the completed injury of being subjected to an unconstitutional classification.

### 5. *Fiore v. White*

**Case.** *Fiore v. White*

**Doctrinal intent.** Certify an antecedent state-law question; retain jurisdiction; reserve due process.

**Proposed holding.** The Court should obtain the authoritative historical meaning of the Pennsylvania statute before deciding whether Fiore was convicted without proof of an element the statute required.

**Limit.** No federal merits judgment, habeas disposition, or view of the statute's meaning issues at this stage.

**Novelty.** Issue reservation or minimalist disposition.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old simulation correctly used certification before federal constitutional adjudication.

### 8. *Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents; United States v. Florida Board of Regents*

**Case.** *Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents; United States v. Florida Board of Regents*

**Question presented.** (1) Whether the ADEA unmistakably expresses Congress's intent to authorize private suits against nonconsenting state employers. (2) Whether the ADEA's extension to state employers is valid legislation under § 5 of the Fourteenth Amendment sufficient to support private retrospective monetary relief against the States.

**What the case is about.** State employees brought ADEA claims against state institutions and agencies. The Eleventh Circuit dismissed or directed dismissal on sovereign-immunity grounds; arm-of-state status is assumed, and no age-discrimination merits were adjudicated.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *Seminole Tribe*, *City of Boerne*, *Florida Prepaid*, *College Savings Bank*, and the rewritten *Alden* rule governing valid Article I duties, adequate state remedial channels, and a federal fallback when no adequate equivalent forum exists.

**Old sim.** Stone previously would have affirmed dismissal of accrued private monetary claims and preserved only qualifying prospective compliance. The old Court adopted that split disposition. Attribution: Stone caused no independent doctrinal change.

**Stone innovation.** None. The changed result follows from applying the supplied rewritten *Alden* remedial-channel rule to a valid Article I employment duty; Stone does not create a new immunity exception in this case.

**Stone's position now.** Vacate or reverse the categorical dismissal and remand. Stone agrees that the ADEA is not congruent and proportional § 5 legislation for retrospective state-treasury liability because age classifications ordinarily receive rational-basis review and the statute reaches substantially more conduct than equal protection forbids. The ADEA nevertheless remains valid Commerce Clause regulation of the labor market, clearly binds state employers, and clearly authorizes monetary enforcement. Under rewritten *Alden*, the State may channel the retrospective claim into an adequate equivalent state remedial forum; if no such forum exists, the supplied federal fallback opens. Proper prospective officer relief remains separately available, and no ADEA merits issue is decided. Rungs 2, 3, and 8 control.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply § 5 congruence and proportionality; apply the supplied Article I remedial-channel rule; vacate the categorical immunity dismissal; reserve statutory liability.

**Proposed holding.** The ADEA does not validly abrogate state immunity through § 5, but failure of that route does not extinguish the valid Article I duty. A State may channel retrospective enforcement into an adequate equivalent state forum; if none exists, the supplied federal fallback governs, while proper prospective officer relief remains separate.

**Limit.** Stone makes no finding of age discrimination, does not validate § 5 abrogation, and does not authorize recovery without proof of the ADEA's elements and satisfaction of the governing remedial channel.

**Novelty.** Remedial or dispositional difference; application of controlling simulated precedent.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes. The old simulation treated invalid § 5 abrogation as ending retrospective enforcement; the supplied rewritten *Alden* rule now preserves the valid Article I obligation through an adequate state channel or the federal fallback.

### 9. *Illinois v. Wardlow*

**Case.** *Illinois v. Wardlow*

**Question presented.** Whether the supported record supplied reasonable suspicion for the initial stop after Wardlow fled upon seeing police in an area described as high crime?

**What the case is about.** Wardlow fled after seeing police in an area the record described as associated with narcotics trafficking. Officers stopped and frisked him and recovered a firearm; the Illinois Supreme Court sustained suppression.

**Precedent applied.** The supplied *Ornelas* appellate-review assignment. No supplied simulated holding changes *Terry*'s substantive totality inquiry.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed to affirm the Illinois Supreme Court. His proposed rule was: Flight and neighborhood context are relevant, but without an objective individualized indicator connecting the person to crime they do not alone establish reasonable suspicion. The old Court adopted a materially narrower formulation. Attribution: Stone caused no change; he aligned with or supported a ground that existed independently.

**Stone innovation.** None. This is an application disagreement under *Terry*'s existing totality standard, not a new categorical rule.

**Stone's position now.** Reverse and remand. Under *Terry* and *Brown*, unprovoked headlong flight is individualized evasive conduct, and the specifically supported narcotics-area context may give it additional objective weight; together they supplied reasonable suspicion for a brief stop on this record. Stone rejects both a rule that flight is always sufficient and the old requirement of a third independently incriminating fact, because the received totality standard contains no such element. Rungs 3, 7, and 9 control; the frisk remains outside the grant.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply *Terry*'s totality standard and the supplied appellate-review assignment; reverse the suppression judgment; reserve the frisk.

**Proposed holding.** Unprovoked headlong flight upon noticing police, considered with a specifically supported high-crime context, can establish reasonable suspicion for a brief stop without an additional categorical prerequisite.

**Limit.** Presence in a disadvantaged neighborhood, refusal to cooperate, ordinary departure, or a conclusory high-crime label does not alone justify a stop. The separate justification and scope of the frisk are not decided.

**Novelty.** Application disagreement only.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes. The old position silently added a third-factor requirement that the received totality standard does not contain.

**Case.** *Henerey v. City of St. Charles, School District*, 200 F.3d 1128 (8th Cir. 1999)
