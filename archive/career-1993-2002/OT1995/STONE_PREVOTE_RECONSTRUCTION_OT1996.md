# Stone Pre-Vote Reconstruction — October Term 1996

## Scope and source control

This reconstruction covers the 47 merits matters and the separate *Doe v. SEPTA* petition-stage matter contained in the supplied old-simulation record. The current *Master Jurisprudence* governs Stone; the *Standards and Tests* and *Holdings* registers are the exclusive supplied new-simulation precedent; the general tests explainer supplies vocabulary only, not precedent. The attached full-court export supplies both the old-simulation evidence and, as the user confirmed, the case record for this run. Real Supreme Court law is fixed at each case’s decision date, while simulated precedent is used exactly as supplied; no pre-vote position reconstructed here is treated as precedent in any later case.

## Step 0: Stone’s jurisprudence ladder

1. **Fix the controlling frame before choosing a route.** Identify the preserved question, posture, jurisdiction and justiciability, governing source and legal field, standard of review, native test, and assigned burdens. Supplied new-simulation precedent governs this run, with nonconflicting real law filling the remainder; Stone starts from that law even if he may ultimately propose changing it.

2. **Take a dispositive threshold first.** Jurisdiction, standing, preservation, ripeness, mootness, and posture precede the merits. If one disposes of the case, Stone states no merits position.

3. **Decide only what the disposition requires.** Stone decides preserved questions and necessary antecedents only to the extent required. He reserves issues that are adjacent, unnecessary, or unpreserved; reservation yields only when the issue is a logically necessary antecedent.

4. **Prefer the narrowest adequate lawful ground.** A sufficient statutory or other nonconstitutional ground precedes a constitutional one; a textually permissible narrowing or saving construction precedes invalidation; genuine avoidance precedes an unnecessary constitutional ruling; and an as-applied holding precedes an unnecessary facial holding. Narrowness cannot support a construction the text will not bear.

5. **Text controls purpose.** In statutory cases, the order is enacted text and definitions, grammar and context, statutory scheme, applicable presumptions, and then enacted purpose and practical function within the range the text can bear; legislative history follows only if genuine uncertainty remains. Purpose selects among plausible readings and never overrides text. Constitutional analysis likewise begins with the text at the level of generality its words chose, while precedent remains controlling unless changed candidly under the departure rule below.

6. **Apply received doctrine before creating doctrine.** The ordinary route is to apply controlling precedent and its existing legal form unchanged, including its standard and burdens, or to disagree about its application to the evidence. Assessing the record differently is application; adding a prerequisite, shifting a burden, increasing the proof demand, changing scrutiny, or creating a test is doctrinal change. Stone begins with the inherited constitutional test and updates it only when the case squarely shows that the test no longer implements its source under present conditions.

7. **Use an existing record, evidentiary, procedural, remedial, or dispositional route when it fully resolves the case.** If a necessary finding lacks record support, Stone applies the governing burden, remands, or resolves the case more narrowly; he never supplies the missing fact or invents a record requirement. Relief is separately sourced, complete for the proven injury, and no broader than necessity permits. This preference does not permit evasion of a doctrinal question the case necessarily presents or use of a narrow remedy to conceal that the law changed.

8. **If change is necessary, use the least law-changing honest operation that fully addresses the defect.** A genuine distinction comes first where precedent’s holding does not reach the materially different facts or question; clarification or narrowing follows where the holding can remain materially intact; modification or overruling is required where the defect reaches the holding itself. This is not a mechanical hierarchy: the defect selects the correction. Stone will not distinguish a collapsed factual premise or pretend that a precedent narrowed to nothing remains good law.

9. **Depart from controlling simulated precedent only on the required candid showing.** The case must squarely present the precedent’s validity, scope, or continued authority, and Stone must identify a legal reason: conflict with superior text or structure, internal contradiction, administrative unworkability, a collapsed factual premise, systematic defeat of the doctrine’s own protection, or another source-grounded defect. He examines the holding, source, factual premise, subsequent treatment, administrability, and concrete reliance; statutory precedent receives added weight. Any change states the prior rule, reason, new boundary, what remains untouched, and treatment of reliance. Without that showing, Stone follows controlling simulated precedent despite conflict with his own jurisprudence.

10. **Use the final tiebreak only after ordinary sources are exhausted.** If two lawful readings remain in genuine equipoise, a rule admits two constructions and one makes it defeat its own protection, or a nominal right admits two lawful routes to effective exercise, Stone selects the reading that widens equal standing and reduces subordination. That tiebreak cannot create jurisdiction, power, a cause of action, or a remedy, displace a native test, or overcome clear precedent.

The jurisprudence does not mechanically rank every equally adequate distinction, narrowing, remand, or remedial disposition. Throughout this run, the first tiebreak is the least law-changing honest route that fully disposes of the presented question; only if two lawful readings remain genuinely equal does the equal-standing tiebreak govern.

The user’s confirmed gloss is applied throughout: in bodily-autonomy and abortion cases Stone improves practical protection incrementally when a lawful opening exists; qualified immunity does not protect materially obvious or plainly unreasonable wrongdoing; criminal and evidence questions begin with enacted text, definitions, grammar, context, and received doctrine rather than ideological alignment; and inherited constitutional tests are maintained for current operation only when the case actually demonstrates a source-grounded need.

## 1. Salt Lake County and Michael George v. J. Gary Sheets

**Case.** *Salt Lake County and Michael George v. J. Gary Sheets*, No. 94-2017.

**Question presented.** (1) Does the Fourteenth Amendment protect against intentional public disclosure of highly intimate information entrusted to police for a limited investigation? (2) Which Utah limitations period governs the § 1983 claim, with Stone reaching neither question because the supplied Holdings Register records a final simulated decision in this same case on April 29, 1996?

**What the case is about.** The Tenth Circuit had left in place a civil-rights judgment based on George’s intentional disclosure to an author of intimate information about Sheets and his marriage that police had obtained for a murder investigation, and it had rejected Utah’s special two-year limitations period. The supplied materials identify no later judgment, rehearing order, recalled mandate, or other event capable of placing that same case before the Court for a second merits decision.

**Precedent applied.** *Sheets v. Salt Lake County*, 45 F.3d 1383, as entered in the supplied Holdings Register. That controlling simulated decision already affirmed the judgment and held that the Fourteenth Amendment protects against deliberate, functionless public dissemination of highly intimate information entrusted to police for a limited investigation.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed affirmance on both privacy and limitations, using a broad intimate-information rule with a narrower governmental-custody fallback. The old Court adopted the fallback in substance and adopted the residual-personal-injury limitations rule; the old record attributes no causal effect to Stone.

**Stone innovation.** None. The required move is procedural application of an already rendered controlling simulated judgment.

**Stone’s position now.** Dismiss the purported second writ as improvidently granted and remove the duplicate merits proceeding without reaching either stated question. The threshold rung controls: before merits or doctrinal choice, Stone must identify an undecided judgment within the Court’s appellate authority, and none is supplied. He would not silently duplicate, enlarge, or revise the April 29 holding.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply controlling simulated precedent, dismiss the second writ as improvidently granted, and reserve everything not decided by the April 29 judgment.

**Proposed holding.** The purported second writ is dismissed as improvidently granted because the supplied record shows that this same case was finally decided on April 29, 1996. Absent a rehearing order or recalled mandate, no second merits adjudication is available.

**Limit.** This position neither reopens the April 29 privacy holding nor creates a new ruling on limitations, immunity, municipal responsibility, causation, damages, or fees.

**Novelty.** Remedial or dispositional difference; no meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes. The old simulation conducted a second merits adjudication, but the supplied controlling register makes that proceeding a procedural nullity.

## 2. Lopez v. Monterey County

**Case.** [*Lopez v. Monterey County*, 519 U.S. 9](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep519/usrep519009/usrep519009.pdf).

**Question presented.** May a three-judge § 5 enforcement court authorize elections under an unprecleared plan embodying a covered jurisdiction’s policy choices, and what temporary remedial authority remains while preclearance is unresolved? Stone would not reach the underlying coverage, designation, laches, retrogression, purpose, or substantive preclearance questions.

**What the case is about.** Hispanic voters challenged Monterey County’s use of a countywide judicial-election arrangement that had not received § 5 preclearance. The three-judge District Court nevertheless authorized the March 1996 election under that arrangement while treating permanent implementation as still requiring preclearance; the supplied record leaves California’s state-plan theory, laches defense, challenge to coverage, and contention that no covered change occurred unresolved.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia*, including its Section 5 Voting-Change Coverage Inquiry and Private Prospective-Enforcement Channel. Stone also applies the surviving real rules of *Allen*, *Clark*, and *McDaniel* to the extent they do not conflict with *Morse*.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed reversal, nonuse of the unprecleared county plan, and prompt resort to a designated preclearance forum while reserving the threshold defenses. The old Court adopted that position in substance; the old record attributes no causal Stone effect.

**Stone innovation.** None. This is ordinary application of the statutory separation between a § 5 enforcement court and the designated substantive preclearance forums.

**Stone’s position now.** Reverse and remand on the narrow ground that an enforcement court cannot put a covered jurisdiction’s unprecleared policy choice into operation while proceeding on the premise that § 5 applies and preclearance remains outstanding. The narrow-disposition and issue-reservation rungs control: the court below may decide coverage and the preserved threshold defenses, but it may not decide substantive preclearance or evade the statutory sequence through a temporary label.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply *Morse*, interpret § 5’s allocation of authority, choose nonuse and remand as the remedy, and reserve the unresolved threshold and substantive-preclearance questions.

**Proposed holding.** A § 5 enforcement court may determine coverage and preclearance status and may preserve lawful election administration temporarily. It may not authorize a covered jurisdiction’s unprecleared policy choice or decide substantive preclearance itself.

**Limit.** The position does not decide whether California supplied an independent state plan, whether Monterey County remains covered, whether laches applies, whether a covered change occurred, or whether a genuinely independent emergency judicial plan would require preclearance.

**Novelty.** Statutory or constitutional interpretation; issue reservation or minimalist disposition. No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old disposition, statutory channel, remedy, and reservations remain supported by *Morse* and surviving § 5 law.

## 3. Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis

**Case.** [*Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis*, 519 U.S. 61](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep519/usrep519061/usrep519061.pdf).

**Question presented.** Must a completed federal diversity judgment be vacated because complete diversity was absent at removal, although a lawful dismissal created complete diversity before trial and jurisdiction remained complete through judgment?

**What the case is about.** Lewis timely sought remand after Caterpillar removed while a nondiverse defendant remained, and the District Court wrongly denied that motion. The nondiverse party later left through an ordinary settlement before trial, the case was then tried to judgment for Caterpillar with complete diversity, and the Sixth Circuit vacated solely because diversity had been absent at removal; the supplied record identifies no collusion or manipulation.

**Precedent applied.** None. The real law then available includes 28 U.S.C. §§ 1332, 1441, 1446, and 1447, together with *American Fire & Casualty Co. v. Finn* and *Grubbs v. General Electric Credit Corp.*

**Old sim.** Stone proposed reinstating the District Court judgment because the removal defect was lawfully and noncollusively cured before trial, while preserving any independently authorized fee or sanction remedy. The old Court adopted that source-separated position in substance; the old record attributes no causal Stone effect.

**Stone innovation.** None. The move is ordinary statutory interpretation and clarification of the difference between the remand duty while a defect exists and adjudicatory jurisdiction after the party configuration lawfully changes.

**Stone’s position now.** Reverse and direct reinstatement of the federal judgment. Section 1447 required remand while nondiversity persisted, but § 1332 supplied subject-matter jurisdiction before any merits trial and through judgment; *Finn* and *Grubbs* do not require destruction of a judgment rendered with jurisdiction. The least law-changing adequate ground preserves the completed trial while leaving the earlier statutory violation and any authorized sanction separate.

**Doctrinal intent.** Clarify the interaction of §§ 1332 and 1447, apply the final-judgment principle indicated by *Finn* and *Grubbs*, and choose reinstatement as the disposition.

**Proposed holding.** An erroneous refusal to remand does not invalidate a later diversity judgment when complete diversity arises lawfully and noncollusively before trial and remains through judgment. The district court must still remand while the defect exists.

**Limit.** Finality does not cure absent subject-matter jurisdiction, collusive manufacture of diversity, or improper manipulation, and it does not extinguish any separately authorized fee or sanction request.

**Novelty.** Statutory or constitutional interpretation; remedial or dispositional difference. No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old result and source-separated rationale remain the narrowest lawful resolution.

## 4. M.L.B. v. S.L.J.

**Case.** [*M.L.B. v. S.L.J.*, 519 U.S. 102](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep519/usrep519102/usrep519102.pdf).

**Question presented.** May a State condition the first appeal it already provides from a permanent termination of parental rights on prepayment of record fees that an indigent parent cannot afford?

**What the case is about.** Mississippi permanently terminated M.L.B.’s parental rights, then dismissed her first appeal because she could not prepay more than $2,300 for the record needed to challenge the sufficiency of the termination findings. The supplied record treats her indigency and the record’s necessity as established and leaves the termination merits for the state appellate court.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *A. St. P. C. v. B. C.*, which requires heightened reliability when government imposes a complete, indefinite, or partly permanent parent-child separation. Stone also applies the surviving real foundations in *Griffin v. Illinois*, *Mayer v. Chicago*, and *Santosky v. Kramer*.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed a broader rule for an appeal from governmental extinction of any protected civil status, with a parent-specific fallback. The old Court rejected the broader formulation as unnecessary and adopted the parent-specific fallback in substance; the old record attributes no causal Stone effect.

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary judicial move: extend the existing first-appeal access principle to permanent parental termination in light of *Santosky* and simulated *A. St. P. C.* No separate Stone framework is needed.

**Stone’s position now.** Reverse and remand for meaningful review with a record of sufficient completeness or a functionally adequate substitute. Family integrity, the permanence of the state-created deprivation, and the impossibility of reviewing factual sufficiency without the record make wealth an impermissible gate to the first appeal Mississippi chose to provide. The narrow-disposition rung selects the parent-specific rule because it fully resolves this case; no cross-status rule is necessary.

**Doctrinal intent.** Extend *Griffin* and *Mayer* to this termination setting, apply *Santosky* and *A. St. P. C.* to the error stakes, and select record access rather than disturbing the termination merits directly.

**Proposed holding.** When a State provides a first appeal from permanent termination of an existing parent-child relationship, it may not make meaningful factual review depend on an indigent parent’s ability to purchase the necessary record. It must furnish that record or a functionally adequate substitute.

**Limit.** The position does not require a State to create an appeal, subsidize ordinary civil litigation, furnish every requested expense, excuse neutral appellate rules, or decide the termination merits.

**Novelty.** Extension of existing doctrine.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes, in scope only. The disposition survives, but Stone makes the parent-specific fallback his sole ground and abandons the old protected-status generalization.

## 5. United States v. Watts; United States v. Putra

**Case.** [*United States v. Watts; United States v. Putra*, 519 U.S. 148](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep519/usrep519148/usrep519148.pdf).

**Question presented.** May a sentencing court consider conduct underlying an acquitted count, proved under the governing sentencing burden, when selecting a sentence within the range legally authorized by the conviction?

**What the case is about.** The Ninth Circuit vacated Watts’s and Putra’s sentences solely because their sentencing judges used conduct underlying acquitted counts in calculating the then-mandatory Guidelines ranges. The supplied record establishes general acquittals and sentencing findings under a lower burden but does not supply instructions or verdict forms establishing that either jury returned a special factual finding.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *United States v. Gaudin*, which reserves offense elements to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt. Stone must distinguish it from sentencing facts that do not alter the offense of conviction or exceed the punishment legally authorized by that conviction.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed reversal with a new requirement that the sentencing court identify the conduct, evidence, burden, and sentencing effect, plus a bar on contradicting an exact fact necessarily resolved by the acquittal. The old Court adopted those added safeguards in substance; the old record attributes no causal Stone effect.

**Stone innovation.** None now. Stone applies § 3661, the then-operative Guidelines, *Witte v. United States*, and *Dowling v. United States* without adding a special findings code or a new acquittal-preclusion rule.

**Stone’s position now.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit’s categorical prohibition and remand under ordinary sentencing law. A general acquittal establishes failure of proof beyond a reasonable doubt, not factual nonoccurrence under the lower sentencing burden; *Gaudin* remains fully operative for elements, while § 3661 and the Guidelines permit reliable relevant-conduct findings within the conviction-authorized range. The received-doctrine rung controls, so Stone enforces existing Rule 32 and Guidelines procedures but adds no special constitutional findings mandate or acquittal-preclusion rule.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply § 3661 and the Guidelines, distinguish *Gaudin*, follow *Witte* and *Dowling*, and reserve any sentence exceeding the conviction-authorized range or using a sentencing label to replace an element.

**Proposed holding.** An acquittal does not bar a sentencing court from considering the underlying conduct proved by a preponderance when the resulting sentence remains within the punishment legally authorized by the conviction. The ordinary evidentiary and sentencing procedures continue to govern the finding.

**Limit.** The position does not permit accusation to substitute for evidence, authorize punishment beyond the conviction-authorized range, remove an element from the jury, or excuse noncompliance with existing Rule 32 and Guidelines requirements.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes. The judgment remains reversal and remand, but Stone drops the old simulation’s additional findings mandate and acquittal-preclusion rule because the question, enacted scheme, and received doctrine do not require them.

## 6. Old Chief v. United States

**Case.** [*Old Chief v. United States*, 519 U.S. 172](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep519/usrep519172/usrep519172.pdf).

**Question presented.** Does Rule 403 require exclusion of the name and violent nature of a prior felony when a binding admission conclusively establishes the formal felon-status element and the details have no independent relevance?

**What the case is about.** Old Chief offered to admit the qualifying prior-conviction element of the firearm charge, but the prosecution introduced the judgment naming his prior violent assault and the Ninth Circuit affirmed. The dispute is confined to proof of a legal status collateral to the charged conduct, not an offer to concede the act, intent, identity, causation, or narrative facts of the current offense.

**Precedent applied.** None. The real law then available includes Federal Rules of Evidence 401, 403, and 404(b), together with surviving *Michelson v. United States*.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed reversal because the binding status admission supplied equally complete proof without the predicate offense’s propensity risk. The old Court adopted the position in substance; the old record attributes no causal Stone effect.

**Stone innovation.** None. This is an ordinary, text-and-structure application of Rule 403 to the concrete evidentiary alternative in the record.

**Stone’s position now.** Reverse and remand. Once the admission proves the formal status with equal completeness, the conviction’s name and violent nature add little legitimate probative value and carry substantial unfair-prejudice risk through forbidden propensity reasoning. The narrowest-ground rung confines the comparison to formal status and leaves the prosecution’s ordinary control of narrative proof intact elsewhere.

**Doctrinal intent.** Interpret and apply Rules 403 and 404(b), distinguish *Michelson* and ordinary narrative evidence, and choose exclusion of the inflammatory details if the complete admission is renewed.

**Proposed holding.** When a defendant offers a binding admission that completely proves a formal prior-conviction status, Rule 403 ordinarily requires exclusion of the predicate offense’s name and nature unless those details have independent legitimate relevance.

**Limit.** The prosecution need not accept a substitute that leaves a genuine gap concerning charged conduct, intent, identity, causation, context, or another disputed proposition, and ordinary harmless-error rules remain available.

**Novelty.** Statutory or constitutional interpretation; no meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old result follows from the enacted evidence rules without ideological or doctrinal supplementation.

## 7. Atherton v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation

**Case.** [*Atherton v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation*, 519 U.S. 213](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep519/usrep519213/usrep519213.pdf).

**Question presented.** Does federal common law supply a uniform gross-negligence rule for officers and directors of federally chartered insured institutions, or does 12 U.S.C. § 1821(k) establish a federal gross-negligence floor while preserving stricter state-law duties?

**What the case is about.** The FDIC, as receiver, pursued negligence, gross-negligence, and fiduciary-duty claims against former officers and directors of a federally chartered institution. The Third Circuit treated § 1821(k) as a floor but invoked federal common law for federally chartered institutions; choice of state law, breach, defenses, causation, and damages remain unresolved.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *O’Melveny & Myers v. FDIC*, which permits displacement of state law only upon an enacted federal rule or a specific and significant conflict with identified federal policy.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed vacatur and remand under the statutory floor and savings clause, rejecting a federal common-law ceiling. The old Court adopted that position; its circulation record specifically attributes removal of reliance on legislative history to Stone’s source-based objection.

**Stone innovation.** None. Stone applies *O’Melveny* and reads the enacted floor, savings clause, and statutory structure without relying on legislative history.

**Stone’s position now.** Vacate and remand for application of governing state law subject to § 1821(k)’s gross-negligence minimum. “Other applicable law” preserves stricter duties, while *O’Melveny* forecloses creating federal common law from charter status, uniformity, or receivership convenience alone. The text-before-purpose and apply-before-change rungs resolve the case.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply *O’Melveny*, interpret § 1821(k) as a floor rather than a ceiling, and reject federal common-law displacement on this record.

**Proposed holding.** Section 1821(k) prevents applicable law from insulating officers and directors from gross negligence or more culpable conduct, but its savings clause preserves stricter state-law standards. Federal chartering and receivership do not independently create a federal common-law standard.

**Limit.** State law cannot immunize conduct Congress made actionable, and express displacement or a specific significant federal conflict remains possible; choice of law and claim-specific issues remain below.

**Novelty.** Statutory or constitutional interpretation; no meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old position is compelled by the supplied *O’Melveny* rule and § 1821(k)’s enacted structure.

## 8. Ingalls Shipbuilding, Inc. v. Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs

**Case.** [*Ingalls Shipbuilding, Inc. v. Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs*, 519 U.S. 248](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep519/usrep519248/usrep519248.pdf).

**Question presented.** Was a spouse who settled a third-party claim before the worker’s death then a “person entitled to compensation” under LHWCA § 33(g)? After another party properly invokes appellate review, may the Director participate as a respondent?

**What the case is about.** Spouses joined third-party settlements while the injured workers were alive and later sought survivor death benefits after the workers died; Ingalls asserted forfeiture because it had not approved the earlier settlements. The Fifth Circuit rejected forfeiture and allowed the Director to appear as respondent in review of the Benefits Review Board’s awards; the case does not involve a post-death settlement.

**Precedent applied.** None. The real law then available includes *Estate of Cowart v. Nicklos Drilling Co.* and *Director, OWCP v. Newport News Shipbuilding & Dry Dock Co.*

**Old sim.** Stone proposed affirmance on both questions, first through an independent-survivor-entitlement account and alternatively through the narrower timing ground, and he supported the Director’s limited respondent participation. The old Court adopted the timing fallback and respondent rule; the old record attributes no causal Stone effect.

**Stone innovation.** None. Both conclusions follow from ordinary interpretation of § 33(g), § 21(c), and Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 15(a).

**Stone’s position now.** Affirm on both questions. Section 33(g)’s present-tense entitlement language cannot attach forfeiture before death creates and identifies the survivor’s statutory claim; separately, Rule 15(a) permits the Department’s designated representative to participate as respondent once an aggrieved private party has lawfully invoked review. The separate-questions and text-first rungs control, and *Newport News* is distinguished because it addressed initiation of review.

**Doctrinal intent.** Interpret statutory and rule text, apply *Cowart*’s timing principle, distinguish *Newport News*, and reserve issues not necessary to either answer.

**Proposed holding.** A spouse is not a “person entitled to compensation” for death benefits before the worker dies, so a predeath settlement does not trigger § 33(g) forfeiture. When another party properly invokes review, the Director may participate as the Department’s respondent without acquiring independent petitioning authority.

**Limit.** The position does not govern post-death settlements, offsets, amounts, causation, the Director’s authority to initiate review, or jurisdiction when no proper private controversy exists.

**Novelty.** Statutory or constitutional interpretation; no meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes, in rationale only. Stone keeps the same judgment but uses the old timing fallback as his sole § 33(g) ground rather than the broader independent-survivor theory.

## 9. Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network of Western New York

**Case.** [*Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network of Western New York*, 519 U.S. 357](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep519/usrep519357/usrep519357.pdf).

**Question presented.** Do the fixed fifteen-foot entrance and driveway zones, the floating fifteen-foot person and vehicle zones, and the linked cease-and-desist condition burden more speech than necessary under the injunction-specific First Amendment standard? Stone would not recognize a freestanding right to personal space or prescribe a universal post-refusal distance.

**What the case is about.** The en banc Second Circuit sustained a party-specific injunction imposed after repeated obstruction, crowding, close pursuit, face-to-face yelling, grabbing, pushing, and shoving at abortion clinics. Stable fixed zones protected entrances and driveways; an exception allowed two counselors to make an initial nonthreatening approach but required them to cease after a clear verbal or nonverbal refusal. The injunction also imposed moving fifteen-foot zones around every entering or departing person or vehicle and made a refusal trigger an automatic fifteen-foot retreat.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *Madsen v. Women’s Health Center, Inc.*, which requires provision-specific connection to demonstrated misconduct and no greater speech burden than necessary to provide lawful relief.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed sustaining the fixed zones, invalidating the unconditional floating zones, and authorizing on remand a new listener-triggered disengagement rule. The old Court adopted all three components in substance; the old record attributes no causal Stone effect.

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary judicial move: apply *Madsen* provision by provision, sever and narrow the listener-triggered condition, and remand for precise relief. Stone does not create a named test or freestanding personal-space doctrine.

**Stone’s position now.** Affirm the fixed entrance and driveway zones and the cease-and-desist condition governing counselors whom the injunction permits to enter those zones; reverse the unconditional person and vehicle floating zones; vacate the automatic fifteen-foot retreat outside the fixed zones; and remand for a narrower post-refusal provision. The record of crowding and persistent close pursuit permits a clear rule that preserves an initial peaceful approach but, after an objectively clear verbal or nonverbal refusal, requires the enjoined defendants to stop following and maintain a modest, specifically defined separation supported by the issuing court's findings. The ordinary-remedial-fix rung controls: refusal may end targeted pursuit by these adjudicated parties, but it does not silence stationary or generally addressed advocacy.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply *Madsen*, distinguish fixed from floating provisions on record and fit, sever and narrow the cease-and-desist condition, and remand for a precise record-supported post-refusal remedy without establishing a new categorical rule.

**Proposed holding.** The fixed entrance and driveway zones and the cease-and-desist limit on counselors admitted within them satisfy *Madsen*, but the unconditional floating zones and automatic fifteen-foot retreat do not. On remand, the injunction may preserve an initial peaceful approach yet require the enjoined defendants, after an objectively clear refusal, to stop following and maintain a modest, precisely defined separation supported by the record.

**Limit.** The position creates no general listener's veto, automatic fifteen-foot distance, or undefined right to reasonable personal space. It requires clarity rather than politeness in the refusal and restricts neither nonparties, willing conversation, an initial peaceful approach, stationary advocacy, nor speech addressed generally instead of pursued at a particular unwilling person.

**Novelty.** Remedial or dispositional difference; application disagreement only.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old disposition and listener-triggered objective survive, but the position is correctly characterized as severance, narrowing, and record-based relief under *Madsen*, not an independent personal-space doctrine.

## 10. Maryland v. Wilson

**Case.** [*Maryland v. Wilson*, 519 U.S. 408](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep519/usrep519408/usrep519408.pdf).

**Question presented.** May police categorically order a passenger out of a lawfully stopped vehicle without individualized suspicion or a specific protective or operational need?

**What the case is about.** A trooper observed a rented car traveling 64 miles per hour in a 55-mile-per-hour zone without a regular license tag. After he activated his lights and siren, the car continued for about a mile and a half; the passengers repeatedly ducked below sight, and Wilson later appeared extremely nervous and sweaty. The trooper ordered Wilson, a passenger, to exit, and crack cocaine fell as he complied. The trial court suppressed and the Maryland Court of Special Appeals affirmed because *Pennsylvania v. Mimms* governs drivers, not passengers; Maryland did not preserve or press the record-specific facts as an alternative ground, and no frisk, search, unrelated interrogation, relocation, prolongation, or remain-at-the-scene command is presented.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *Whren v. United States* governs initiation of the stop: speeding supplied objective probable cause, while *Whren* does not authorize a stop lacking objective legal support and leaves discriminatory enforcement to Equal Protection. Real *Terry v. Ohio*, *Brown v. Texas*, and *United States v. Cortez* require a particularized objective basis for an investigative stop and make presence in a high-crime neighborhood, standing alone, insufficient, but that initiation question is not presented here. No supplied simulated holding extends *Mimms* to passengers.

**Old sim.** Stone’s controlled pre-vote position was to affirm and require specific, articulable protective or operational need; he offered a sharply limited categorical rule only as a fallback. The old Court rejected his primary position and adopted the fallback’s exit-command component, after which Stone accepted that ground; the old record attributes no change by Stone. The old material contains no Stone position or Court ruling on neighborhood-based suspicion for initiating a stop because speeding made that issue absent.

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary judicial move: distinguish *Mimms* and decline to extend its categorical driver rule. No multi-part test is required.

**Stone’s position now.** Affirm the Maryland judgment. A passenger ordinarily committed no traffic offense, so the lawful stop does not itself justify an additional command imposed on that person. The government must identify objective facts showing a reasonable protective or operational need: passenger-specific weapons information, threatening movement, or attempted concealment may suffice, and so may stop-specific circumstances such as reckless flight, a lawful custodial arrest, vehicle impoundment, or an immediate roadside hazard that genuinely requires control of the occupants. Severe speeding is relevant only through those objective safety or operational consequences; routine citation-level speeding and passenger presence alone are insufficient. A generalized crime-rate label or the racial composition of a neighborhood likewise cannot substitute for passenger-specific facts or concrete operational demands of this stop. **Limit.** No numerical speeding threshold controls, and an officer’s unsupported characterization of a stop as dangerous cannot substitute for objective circumstances. The position leaves *Mimms*’s driver rule intact and decides no frisk, search, interrogation, relocation, prolongation, remain-in authority, or rule governing initiation of an investigative stop. **Changed from old sim.** No. The clarification identifies when the old protective-or-operational-need rule permits passenger movement without changing the rule or judgment.

**Proposed holding.** Equal protection permits a State to allow every competent person to refuse unwanted treatment while prohibiting every person from intentionally assisting suicide. The distinction concerns conduct, causation, and intent and is rationally related to legitimate protective interests.

**Limit.** No authority for forced treatment, unequal palliation, punishment of good-faith symptom relief, discriminatory enforcement, or a pretextual distinction contradicted by the State’s actual medical rules.

**Novelty.** Application disagreement only; no meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The same independent conduct-and-intent analysis controls, without treating the *Glucksberg* pre-vote position as law.

## 46. Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union, 521 U.S. 844

**Case.** [*Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union*, 521 U.S. 844 (1997)](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep521/usrep521844/usrep521844.pdf).

**Question presented.** Do the Communications Decency Act provisions criminalizing “indecent” transmissions and “patently offensive” online displays accessible to minors violate the First Amendment?

**What the case is about.** A three-judge District Court enjoined the challenged CDA provisions on direct review. The supplied record shows broad criminal language, uncertainty about covered expression and community standards, substantial protected adult communication, and age-verification methods unavailable or prohibitively burdensome for many speakers. Obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, threats, fraud, solicitation, and other independently unlawful material are outside the injunction.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *Turner Broadcasting System v. FCC*, 512 U.S. 622, supplies evidence-based classification and fit analysis for a different communications structure. Simulated *Action for Children’s Television v. FCC* and *Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium v. FCC* protect realistic adult access and confine broadcast-specific rationales. The current run’s pre-vote position in *Turner II* is not precedent.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed affirming the injunction because the content-based criminal categories were indeterminate, substantially suppressed protected adult speech, and lacked practical narrow tailoring. The old Court adopted the position through an existing speech rationale; Stone caused no distinctive change.

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary judicial move: apply settled content discrimination, vagueness, overbreadth, and strict-scrutiny doctrine to a new communications medium.

**Stone’s position now.** Affirm the injunction. Internet speech does not enter a lower constitutional tier merely because the medium is new; unlike scarce licensed broadcasting, the network is decentralized and users ordinarily seek content affirmatively. The CDA’s undefined criminal categories, severe penalties, national reach, and impractical defenses suppress substantial protected adult speech and fail strict scrutiny, while scienter does not tell speakers what “indecent” or “patently offensive” means. Existing doctrine fully resolves the case, so Stone creates no Internet-specific test.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply existing First Amendment doctrine to changed technology; invalidate the challenged provisions; preserve severable lawful regulation.

**Proposed holding.** Internet speech receives full First Amendment protection. The CDA’s challenged indecency provisions are facially invalid because their indeterminate criminal categories suppress substantial protected adult expression and are not narrowly tailored to protecting minors.

**Limit.** No protection for obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, true threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation, or direct harmful conduct; no bar on user-controlled filtering or a materially narrower law with fair notice and realistic adult access.

**Novelty.** Extension of existing doctrine.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The outcome and method survive; the corrected precedent set excludes the current run’s *Turner II* position.

## 47. Printz v. United States; Mack v. United States, 521 U.S. 898

**Case.** [*Printz v. United States; Mack v. United States*, 521 U.S. 898 (1997)](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep521/usrep521898/usrep521898.pdf).

**Question presented.** May Congress require state and local executive officers to conduct background checks and perform related administrative tasks for the interim federal Brady Act program?

**What the case is about.** The Brady Act directs designated local chief law-enforcement officers to receive forms, search records, and perform related duties until a federal system becomes operational. The Ninth Circuit sustained those commands. The case concerns compulsory use of state executive machinery, not direct federal regulation of dealers or purchasers, preemption, voluntary cooperation, spending conditions, or state-court enforcement of supreme federal law.

**Precedent applied.** Simulated *Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson* permits federal duties administered through state offices only because the Elections Clause expressly authorizes Congress to alter the machinery of federal elections; it identifies rather than defeats the need for a particular constitutional source. Simulated *Seminole Tribe* reinforces separation of federal objectives from the constitutional means used against States. Real *New York v. United States* supplies the anti-commandeering baseline.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed reversing and extending *New York* from legislative commands to compulsory state-executive administration while preserving severable federal regulation. The old Court adopted that position independently; Stone caused no movement.

**Stone innovation.** Extension of existing doctrine: extend the anti-commandeering rule from state legislatures to state executive officers.

**Stone’s position now.** Reverse both judgments. Congress may regulate firearms transactions directly, preempt conflicting state law, create a federal system, seek voluntary cooperation, and offer lawful spending bargains, but the commerce power supplies no authority to conscript state officers into administering the program. *Voting Rights Coalition* confirms the exception for an express constitutional assignment and is distinguishable because the Brady duties have no comparable source. The existing-doctrine and narrowly matched extension rungs control.

**Doctrinal intent.** Extend *New York*; distinguish the Elections Clause holding in *Voting Rights Coalition*; sever the compulsory duties.

**Proposed holding.** Absent a particular constitutional authorization, Congress may not require state executive officers acting in their official capacities to administer or enforce a federal regulatory program. The Brady Act’s compulsory interim duties are unenforceable against Printz and Mack and are severable from the federal regulation of dealers, purchasers, and the national system.

**Limit.** No immunity for States from generally applicable federal law, no restriction on valid preemption, state-court duties, information rules resting on a different source, consensual cooperation, lawful spending conditions, or Congress’s express Elections Clause authority.

**Novelty.** Extension of existing doctrine.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The same extension, disposition, severability, and limits remain supported.

## 48. John Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority, No. 95-1742

**Case.** *John Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority*, No. 95-1742.

**Question presented.** Does a public employer violate the Fourteenth Amendment by obtaining, using, or internally disclosing an employee’s identified prescription and HIV-related information beyond legitimate administrative need, and what standard governs that claim under §1983? No merits question has yet been accepted, so Stone decides only whether to grant review.

**What the case is about.** A jury returned a $125,000 verdict for Doe, but the Third Circuit applied its general medical-privacy balance, reversed, and directed judgment for SEPTA and an individual official. The supplied petition record concerns identified prescription information, asserted benefit-plan monitoring, confidentiality safeguards, alleged need-to-know disclosures, and evidence that employee names were unnecessary. The judgment is final and the constitutional question preserved, but the old source packet contains no controlled Stone merits position.

**Precedent applied.** The supplied precedent set contains a final simulated holding in *Sheets v. Salt Lake County*. It protects against deliberate public dissemination of highly intimate information entrusted to police for a limited official purpose when disclosure serves no connected governmental function; it does not decide internal public-employer acquisition, use, or limited official disclosure. Simulated *Hodge v. Jones* addresses confidential retention of an accurate, nonconsequential investigative record and likewise does not resolve Doe’s employment-use claim.

**Old sim.** Stone initially supported holding the petition for the old simulation’s still-pending *Sheets* case and later supported a GVR after that old *Sheets* decision. The old simulation expressly supplied no Stone merits position and created no merits holding in Doe.

Doe cleanly presents the next source-bound question on a final judgment and developed jury record. Stone takes no view yet on liability, immunity, causation, damages, or the correct merits standard.

**Doctrinal intent.** Grant plenary review; reserve every merits issue.

**Proposed holding.** None at petition stage. Grant review limited to the constitutional standard governing a public employer’s acquisition, use, or disclosure of identified medical information beyond asserted administrative need.

**Limit.** The grant does not establish a constitutional violation, validate the jury verdict, decide municipal or individual liability, remove qualified immunity, approve a general privacy balance, or select a remedy.

**Novelty.** Issue reservation or minimalist disposition; no meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes. The supplied precedent set contains a finalized but materially narrower *Sheets* holding, so neither the old hold nor a mere GVR fits; Stone would grant plenary review.
