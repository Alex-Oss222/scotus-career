# Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21*
- **Citation or Docket:** 980 F.2d 437 (7th Cir. 1992), No. 91-1684.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Granted merits case concerning school-led recitation of the Pledge of Allegiance.
- **Simulated Event Date:** February 12, 1996, simulation-assigned opinion-delivery date.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a public school violates the Establishment Clause by conducting voluntary recitation of the Pledge of Allegiance containing the words “under God” when students remain free not to participate.
- **Question Granted:** Whether a public school violates the Establishment Clause by conducting voluntary recitation of the Pledge of Allegiance containing the words “under God” when students remain free not to participate.
- **Legal Field:** Establishment Clause, public schools, government religious expression, and ceremonial civic exercises.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Seventh Circuit affirmed summary judgment for the School District and other defendants, holding that a public school may lead the Pledge of Allegiance, including “under God,” so long as students remain free not to participate.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The Shermans seek reversal and prospective relief against the challenged school-led practice; the School District seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Illinois law directed that the Pledge of Allegiance be recited each school day in publicly supported elementary schools. Richard Sherman attended an elementary school in the District and objected to the Pledge’s words “under God.” The record accepted below showed that students were not compelled to recite the Pledge, stand, place a hand over the heart, leave the room, or suffer punishment for remaining silent and seated. The challenged religious words were nevertheless part of the official daily Pledge led in the public-school setting.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The controlled grant presents the Establishment Clause challenge to the school-led Pledge. Stone reaches that claim against the School District and responsible school officials. He does not decide ungranted Free Exercise, compelled-speech, damages, or Attorney General immunity questions except to recognize that *Barnette* independently protects a student from compelled participation.
- **Relevant Dates:** Seventh Circuit decision, November 20, 1992; historical certiorari denied at 508 U.S. 950 (1993); simulated decision, February 12, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** 980 F.2d 437 supplies the Seventh Circuit record, question, summary-judgment posture, and judgment; 758 F. Supp. 1244 supplies the district-court merits judgment; Standing State supplies the controlled OT1995 grant and question.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits decision. Certiorari was denied, and the Seventh Circuit judgment remained in force.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** No historical Supreme Court merits opinion; Seventh Circuit opinion by Judge Easterbrook.
- **Historical Holding:** The Seventh Circuit held that schools may lead the Pledge of Allegiance, including “under God,” when students are free not to participate.
- **Historical Authority:** Lower-court historical anchor only.
- **Historical Sources:** 980 F.2d 437; historical denial at 508 U.S. 950.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to chronological revalidation through February 12, 1996.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** *Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue*, scheduled earlier in the term, may further clarify the distinction between private religious speech and government religious expression if it becomes effective before this event. It does not control unless completed before February 12, 1996, and its private-forum setting is materially different from the government-led Pledge here.

#### Issue 1: School-led Pledge containing “under God”

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision directly resolves a government-authored religious phrase in a daily public-school civic exercise. *Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet* requires religion-neutral civil government. *Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette*, *Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia*, and *Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller* protect clearly private religious expression admitted on neutral terms, while expressly reserving government-sponsored or government-attributed religious expression.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No registered school-Pledge or ceremonial-acknowledgment standard controls. The current private-religious-expression and attribution rules are relevant principally by contrast because the Pledge here is selected and led by government rather than admitted as private speech.
- **Other Governing Law:** Establishment Clause; *West Virginia State Board of Education v. Barnette*; *Engel v. Vitale*; *School District of Abington Township v. Schempp*; *Marsh v. Chambers*; *Lynch v. Donnelly*; *County of Allegheny v. ACLU*; *Lee v. Weisman*.
- **Other Authority Used:** The government authorship and daily school setting of the Pledge, the expressly theistic phrase, the students’ freedom to remain silent and seated, the absence of punishment, and the distinction between a patriotic civic exercise and devotional prayer.
- **Open Point:** Whether a school-led patriotic exercise becomes an unconstitutional governmental religious affirmation when the State includes a theistic proposition in the official text even though no student is compelled to join.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Seventh Circuit and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The lower court shall enter appropriately limited prospective relief barring the District from conducting the official daily Pledge in a manner that makes “under God” part of the government-led affirmation. The District remains free to conduct a secular patriotic Pledge and to protect voluntary private religious expression by students. No damages or broader relief is decided.

#### Issue 1: School-led Pledge containing “under God”

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the granted Establishment Clause question. *Barnette* independently establishes that Richard may not be compelled to recite or salute, but the absence of compelled participation does not dispose of the separate question whether the school itself may lead the religious proposition.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A patriotic civic exercise is not worship merely because it is solemn or traditional, and government may recognize history and national occasion. But a public school may not place a religious proposition inside the official daily affirmation of civic allegiance when the government selects the words, leads the exercise, and addresses children in a compulsory educational setting. “Under God” is not private speech and is not made secular by repetition. Formal permission to remain silent cures compelled profession under *Barnette* but does not cure the State’s own adoption of a theistic proposition. In Stone’s view, ceremonial acknowledgment remains permissible only where it does not conduct worship, prefer religious belief, demand participation, or mark nonadherents as outsiders in the civic community.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A public school may lead a patriotic Pledge and may not compel any student to participate, but the Establishment Clause does not permit the school itself to make the theistic affirmation “under God” part of the official daily Pledge. A student’s right to remain silent prevents compelled profession; it does not transform the government’s religious affirmation into private speech.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The District controls the exercise, the setting, and the official text used by teachers and pupils. The challenged words state a religious proposition about the Nation rather than merely teaching that religion has influenced American history. Richard’s ability to remain silent is constitutionally important, but the school still presents the proposition to a captive elementary-school audience as part of the shared civic pledge. The patriotic purpose of the Pledge is fully available without requiring government to attach allegiance to a belief in God, and historical repetition cannot by itself cure a governmental religious affirmation.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not prohibit private students from reciting “under God,” classroom teaching about the Pledge or the Nation’s religious history, voluntary religious clubs, neutral forum access, legislative prayer under its own historical doctrine, or genuinely ceremonial references that neither prescribe religious belief nor place nonadherents outside a shared civic affirmation.

# Behrens v. Pelletier

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Behrens v. Pelletier*
- **Citation or Docket:** 516 U.S. 299; No. 94-1244.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review of appellate jurisdiction over a second qualified-immunity appeal.
- **Simulated Event Date:** February 21, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a defendant may take a second interlocutory appeal from denial of qualified immunity after discovery, and which issues remain immediately appealable after *Johnson v. Jones*.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Ninth Circuit had jurisdiction to consider the legal qualified-immunity question following the summary-judgment denial.
- **Legal Field:** Federal appellate jurisdiction, collateral orders, and qualified immunity.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit dismissed Behrens’s second interlocutory appeal, treating an earlier appeal from the pleading-stage denial as exhausting the right to immediate review.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Behrens seeks reversal and appellate consideration of the legal immunity question; Pelletier seeks affirmance or dismissal to the extent the appeal disputes facts.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Behrens first appealed denial of immunity at an early stage and later moved for summary judgment after discovery. The second order rested on the developed record and included disputes about what conduct occurred and whether the assumed conduct violated clearly established law.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The collateral-order doctrine permits immediate review only of a separable legal immunity issue. Under *Johnson*, the appellate court may not reconsider whether the evidence is sufficient to support the plaintiff’s factual account.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, November 7, 1995; simulated decision, February 21, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 516 U.S. 299; Ninth Circuit proceedings described there.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 7-2.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Opinion of the Court by Justice Scalia; Justice Breyer, joined by Justice Stevens, dissented.
- **Historical Holding:** A defendant may appeal a later denial of qualified immunity after discovery, but immediate review excludes evidence-sufficiency disputes barred by *Johnson*.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 516 U.S. 299.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, including *Johnson v. Jones*, *Elder v. Holloway*, and *Swint v. Chambers County Commission*.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Successive collateral-order appeal

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Johnson v. Jones* bars immediate appeal of a denial that only finds sufficient evidence for a factual dispute, while preserving a separable legal question on assumed facts. *Swint* rejects expansion of interlocutory jurisdiction to nonappealable matters merely associated with immunity.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Evidence-sufficiency disputes in qualified-immunity appeals** and **Interlocutory review of municipal liability.**
- **Other Governing Law:** *Mitchell v. Forsyth*, *Cohen*, and 28 U.S.C. § 1291.
- **Other Authority Used:** The procedural difference between a pleading-stage record and a developed summary-judgment record.
- **Open Point:** Whether an earlier immunity appeal categorically precludes a later appeal from a materially different order.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The Ninth Circuit shall exercise jurisdiction over the separable legal immunity question using the facts the district court assumed or the plaintiff-supported version of disputed facts. It shall dismiss any attempt to reweigh evidentiary sufficiency.

#### Issue 1: Successive collateral-order appeal

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves appellate jurisdiction and does not decide whether Behrens ultimately receives immunity.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Each order denying qualified immunity is assessed by what it decides. An earlier appeal from a pleading-stage order does not exhaust review of a later legal denial based on a developed record. But the reason for immediate appeal remains protection from trial on a legally barred claim, not appellate resolution of factual disputes.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A second interlocutory appeal is available from a later denial of qualified immunity insofar as it presents a separable legal question; *Johnson* forecloses review insofar as the appellant contests only whether the record supports the plaintiff’s facts.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Discovery can narrow the conduct alleged and change the legal immunity question. Treating the first appeal as the only possible one would require defendants to litigate through trial even when the later assumed facts show no clearly established violation. The jurisdiction must nevertheless stop at the legal boundary fixed by *Johnson*.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not create a right to repeated appeals from unchanged orders or permit delay through repackaged factual disputes.

# Peacock v. Thomas

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Peacock v. Thomas*
- **Citation or Docket:** 516 U.S. 349; No. 94-1453.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review.
- **Simulated Event Date:** February 21, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a federal court has ancillary jurisdiction in a new action to impose an existing ERISA judgment on a nonparty alleged to have diverted the judgment debtor’s assets.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the enforcement suit against Peacock required an independent basis of federal jurisdiction.
- **Legal Field:** Article III, ancillary jurisdiction, judgment enforcement, and ERISA.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit affirmed a judgment imposing personal liability on Peacock in an ancillary enforcement action.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Peacock seeks reversal and dismissal for lack of jurisdiction; Thomas seeks affirmance as necessary to enforce the prior federal judgment.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Thomas obtained an ERISA judgment against his former employer. He later sued Peacock, an officer or shareholder not liable in the original judgment, alleging that Peacock had dissipated or concealed corporate assets and should personally answer for the judgment. The new action required proof of new conduct and a new basis of liability.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Subject-matter jurisdiction is mandatory. The Court distinguishes proceedings executing a judgment against the judgment debtor’s assets from a new action imposing liability on a different person.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, November 27, 1995; simulated decision, February 21, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 516 U.S. 349; Fourth Circuit judgment identified there.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, 8-1.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Opinion of the Court by Justice Thomas; Justice Stevens dissented.
- **Historical Holding:** Ancillary enforcement jurisdiction does not authorize a new action to impose a judgment on a third party absent an independent jurisdictional basis.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 516 U.S. 349.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, including *Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co.*
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Ancillary jurisdiction over third-party liability

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Kokkonen* holds that ancillary jurisdiction after dismissal exists only where enforcement is part of the decree, jurisdiction is expressly retained, or an independent jurisdictional basis exists. Its source-bound treatment of ancillary power applies by analogy.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Ancillary jurisdiction to enforce settlements after dismissal.**
- **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§ 1331 and 1367, ERISA’s jurisdictional provisions, and traditional judgment-execution practice.
- **Other Authority Used:** The distinction between reaching assets of an existing debtor and creating liability for a new defendant.
- **Open Point:** Whether any pleaded federal ERISA claim independently reaches Peacock; the present judgment rests on ancillary power alone.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and direct dismissal of the action for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction, without prejudice to a suit in a court possessing an independent basis.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The personal judgment against Peacock shall be vacated. Ordinary execution against the original debtor and any independent state or federal action remain available.

#### Issue 1: Ancillary jurisdiction over third-party liability

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone decides jurisdiction before any veil-piercing or fraudulent-transfer merits.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A federal court may protect and execute its judgment by reaching property of the judgment debtor, resolving ownership necessary to execution, and using authorized supplementary process. It may not use ancillary jurisdiction to create personal liability against a new party based on new facts and legal theories. That controversy requires its own jurisdictional grant.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The action against Peacock is not ancillary enforcement because it seeks a new judgment against a nonparty rather than execution of the existing judgment against the debtor or its property.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The new complaint requires adjudication of Peacock’s conduct, corporate separateness, causation, and defenses. Serious allegations of asset diversion do not create jurisdiction. Thomas may pursue them under state fraudulent-transfer law, diversity, or another federal source if available, but the importance of collecting a federal judgment does not enlarge Article III or statutory jurisdiction.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not limit contempt, receivership, garnishment, or other proceedings directed to assets legally belonging to the original debtor.

# Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson*
- **Citation or Docket:** 60 F.3d 1411 (9th Cir. 1995), No. 95-15449.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Granted merits case concerning the National Voter Registration Act.
- **Simulated Event Date:** February 26, 1996, simulation-assigned opinion-delivery date.

- **Material Facts and Record:** The NVRA requires covered States to offer registration for federal elections through driver-license, mail, and designated public-assistance channels, and to maintain related procedures. - **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Private plaintiffs have standing as eligible voters denied statutory registration opportunities; the United States has an express enforcement role. - **Historical Opinion Structure:** Opinion by Judge Sneed affirming the compliance injunction.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, including *U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton* and *United States v. Lopez*.

#### Issue 1: Federal registration duties for federal elections

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *U.S. Term Limits* confirms that States may regulate election administration but may not alter substantive federal qualifications. *Lopez* requires an identified constitutional source; the Elections Clause independently grants Congress power over the times, places, and manner of congressional elections.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **State-imposed qualifications for Congress** supplies the administration-versus-qualification distinction. No registered anti-commandeering rule displaces the specific Elections Clause allocation.
- **Other Governing Law:** U.S. Const. art. I, § 4; Necessary and Proper Clause; NVRA text and enforcement provisions.
- **Other Authority Used:** *Ex parte Siebold* and surviving Elections Clause precedent.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm, with a limiting construction of the injunction.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** California officials shall implement the NVRA for federal elections. The district court shall ensure that relief reaches state-election registration only to the extent California law integrates the rolls or federal compliance cannot otherwise be administered, and shall not treat the Act as a free-standing federal command governing state-only elections.

#### Issue 1: Federal registration duties for federal elections

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the federal-power and remedial questions.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The Elections Clause specifically authorizes Congress to alter state regulations governing the manner of congressional elections. Registration is part of that manner. Congress may require state election machinery to perform federal-election duties because the constitutional allocation itself contemplates state administration subject to congressional alteration. The Necessary and Proper Clause permits related record and agency procedures reasonably tied to that federal function.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The NVRA validly requires California to provide and administer specified registration opportunities for federal elections, and state officials may not nullify that federal law by executive directive.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The Act regulates who is placed on the rolls and how applications are received for federal elections; it does not add candidate qualifications or claim a general police power. California already uses the designated offices and election system. Federal law may attach federal-election duties to that machinery. Accountability remains visible because Congress enacted the requirement and the injunction enforces that enacted choice.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide a federal mandate unrelated to federal elections, authorize Congress to control state-only voter qualifications, or require consolidation of rolls where severable administration is practical.

# Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein

- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review.

- **Legal Field:** Full faith and credit, class actions, preclusion, and federal securities jurisdiction.

- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Matsushita seeks reversal and enforcement of the release; Epstein seeks affirmance and federal adjudication of the securities claims.
- **Material Facts and Record:** A Delaware shareholder class action challenging a corporate transaction settled and released state and federal claims, including claims that could be litigated only in federal court. **Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to revalidation.

#### Issue 1: Preclusive effect of the Delaware settlement

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision directly addresses release of exclusively federal claims by a state class settlement.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No exact registered test. Statutory full-faith-and-credit law and class-action due process govern.
- **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. § 1738; *Marrese v. American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons*; *Phillips Petroleum Co. v. Shutts*; federal securities jurisdiction provisions.
- **Other Authority Used:** Delaware law concerning judgment finality and settlement releases.
- **Open Point:** Whether Congress created an express or implied exception to § 1738 for these federal claims and whether the class process bound absent members lawfully.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The Ninth Circuit shall determine the preclusive effect Delaware law gives the judgment, then decide any preserved due-process objection to notice, representation, opt-out opportunity, or settlement fairness. #### Issue 1: Preclusive effect of the Delaware settlement

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches statutory preclusion and the necessary due-process boundary, not the underlying securities merits.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 1738 begins with the effect the rendering State gives its judgment. Exclusive federal jurisdiction means the state court could not adjudicate the federal claim on the merits; it does not necessarily prevent parties from releasing that claim as part of a settlement of a controversy properly before the state court. A federal exception requires congressional direction or an irreconcilable conflict, not judicial preference. Binding absent class members remains conditioned on constitutionally adequate representation and process.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A Delaware class judgment releasing transaction-related federal securities claims receives the preclusive effect Delaware law assigns unless Congress displaced § 1738 or the class judgment fails due process.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** A settlement release resolves the parties’ claims and litigation risk; it does not purport to exercise federal adjudicatory jurisdiction over the securities merits. Congress knew how to create exclusive jurisdiction but did not state that related state judgments lack preclusive effect. The judgment therefore travels under § 1738, while *Shutts* protects absent members from being bound by defective process.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not approve a state court’s merits adjudication of an exclusively federal claim or hold that a settlement can bind persons who lacked adequate notice, representation, or an effective opportunity to protect their interests.

# Norfolk & Western Railway Co. v. Hiles

- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Opinion of the Court by Justice Thomas; unanimous judgment.
- **Historical Holding:** Ordinary drawbar misalignment is not itself a statutory defect when the coupler functions automatically after alignment.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 516 U.S. 400.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to revalidation.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Statutory failure to couple

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision construes this Safety Appliance Act requirement.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No exact registered test. Enacted text, statutory function, and surviving railroad-safety precedent govern.
- **Other Governing Law:** 45 U.S.C. § 2 as then codified; *Affolder v. New York, Chicago & St. Louis Railroad Co.* and related cases.
- **Other Authority Used:** Undisputed mechanical evidence concerning normal lateral drawbar movement.
- **Open Point:** The line between ordinary operational misalignment and a coupler that fails because of defect or improper condition.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The statutory liability judgment is reversed. No separate remand instruction is required. Any independently preserved negligence or defect claim remains governed by ordinary lower-court procedures without direction from this Court.

#### Issue 1: Statutory failure to couple

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory merits.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The Act imposes an absolute duty to maintain couplers that couple automatically by impact when properly set for coupling. It does not guarantee that drawbars will never shift laterally through ordinary operations or eliminate every need to align equipment. Failure after proper alignment may establish violation; ordinary misalignment alone does not.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Ordinary drawbar misalignment is not a violation of the Safety Appliance Act absent failure of a properly set coupler to perform its statutorily required automatic function.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory device is the automatic coupler, and strict liability attaches when that appliance fails to perform its required function. Treating natural lateral movement as a defect would convert the provision into a guarantee against an operational condition the mechanism was not designed to prevent. The record permits no finding that the coupler itself malfunctioned, so the statutory claim fails without a Supreme Court directive concerning a different negligence theory.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone reserves a case involving recurrent misalignment caused by defective maintenance, improper setup, or a coupler that fails after the drawbars are properly positioned.

# Action for Children's Television v. FCC

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Action for Children’s Television v. FCC*
- **Citation or Docket:** 58 F.3d 654 (D.C. Cir. 1995) (en banc), consolidated petitions for review.
- **October Term:** 1995.

- **Simulated Event Date:** March 4, 1996, simulation-assigned opinion-delivery date.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a federal rule barring indecent broadcast programming from 6 a.m. to midnight for most stations, while permitting certain public stations to begin at 10 p.m., is narrowly tailored and sufficiently precise under the First Amendment.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the safe-harbor structure and disparate treatment of broadcasters may be enforced on the record before Congress and the FCC.
- **Legal Field:** First Amendment, broadcast regulation, indecency, administrative record, and severability.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The en banc D.C. Circuit sustained a channeling rule in principle but set aside the more restrictive midnight boundary because Congress had not justified the 10 p.m. exception for selected public stations, directing a uniform 10 p.m.-to-6 a.m. safe harbor.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Broadcaster petitioners seek broader invalidation; the FCC seeks restoration of the statutory midnight boundary or affirmance of the channeling authority.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Congress directed the FCC to bar indecent broadcasts during specified hours. Most stations received a midnight-to-6 a.m. safe harbor, while some public stations signing off by midnight could broadcast after 10 p.m. The record contained audience data showing declining child audiences late at night but did not connect the broadcaster classification to differential risk.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The petitions properly challenge a final FCC rule. The Court may decide the time-channeling and classification issues without adjudicating every future application of the indecency definition. Any remedy must respect statutory severability and avoid judicially designing a broadcasting code beyond what the lawful text and record permit.
- **Relevant Dates:** En banc decision, June 30, 1995; simulated decision, March 4, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** Multiple broadcaster, civil-liberties, and public-media petitions were consolidated.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** 58 F.3d 654; FCC Enforcement Order, 8 F.C.C.R. 704; Public Telecommunications Act of 1992, § 16(a).

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits decision. The en banc D.C. Circuit judgment is the historical anchor.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** En banc majority sustained channeling but required a uniform 10 p.m. safe harbor; several judges dissented and would have invalidated the restriction more broadly.
- **Historical Holding:** Broadcast indecency could be channeled, but the unexplained differential safe harbors made the more restrictive midnight rule unconstitutional.
- **Historical Authority:** Lower-court historical anchor.
- **Historical Sources:** 58 F.3d 654.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, including *Turner Broadcasting*, *City of Ladue*, and the speech-injunction rules in *Madsen*.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Channeling protected broadcast indecency

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Turner Broadcasting* requires government to establish a substantial interest and a reasonable fit for content-neutral carriage burdens, with independent review of burden and fit. The present rule is content based and therefore requires the more demanding protection established by *Sable Communications* and *FCC v. Pacifica*’s narrow broadcast holding.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Cable must-carry classification and intermediate scrutiny** supplies record discipline by analogy but not the tier. No registered rule authorizes a broad content-based ban without a realistically available channel.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Pacifica*, *Sable*, *Ginsberg*, 18 U.S.C. § 1464, and the First Amendment.
- **Other Authority Used:** Audience data, the size of adult and child audiences by hour, alternative technologies, and the statutory public-station exception.
- **Open Point:** The earliest uniform boundary the record can justify without turning channeling into practical suppression.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment insofar as it sets aside the midnight distinction and requires a uniform 10 p.m.-to-6 a.m. safe harbor; vacate any broader rationale treating the midnight restriction as independently valid.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The FCC may enforce a uniform prohibition from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. under the present statute and record. Any more restrictive boundary requires new lawful authority and a record establishing why less speech-restrictive channeling is inadequate.

#### Issue 1: Channeling protected broadcast indecency

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the facial time-channeling and classification issues. He does not decide every as-applied vagueness or indecency dispute.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Indecent but nonobscene speech is protected. Broadcast characteristics and the protection of children permit time channeling only when adults retain a realistic opportunity to receive the material and government establishes that the selected hours materially reduce children’s exposure with no substantially less restrictive workable boundary. Government may not impose different burdens on similarly situated speakers without a reason connected to the asserted harm. Congress’s own 10 p.m. exception is strong evidence that a broader midnight ban was not necessary on this record.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The 6 a.m.-to-midnight ban is not narrowly tailored because the statute permits comparable public stations to broadcast after 10 p.m. without showing a different risk; a uniform 6 a.m.-to-10 p.m. restriction is the maximum channeling supported by the present record and severable text.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Child audiences decline as the evening progresses, while substantial adult audiences remain. The Government’s asserted interest does not explain why identical content becomes tolerable at 10 p.m. on one station but remains forbidden on another until midnight. Rather than extend the harsher rule, remedial narrowness requires removing the unsupported two-hour burden. The FCC may continue to enforce settled cores of indecency, subject to as-applied notice review.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not freeze the safe harbor permanently. New technology, audience evidence, or a differently structured parental-control system may support another rule, but the agency must explain and substantiate it at the time.

- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The Court must identify whether Varity acted in a fiduciary capacity, not merely as an employer making a business decision. - **Historical Holding:** Varity acted as a fiduciary when misleading beneficiaries about plan benefits, and § 502(a)(3) authorized appropriate individualized equitable relief.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *John Hancock v. Harris Trust* applies ERISA’s functional “to the extent” fiduciary rule: status follows the authority or control exercised over plan assets or administration, not corporate title.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No separate registered Varity test. Functional fiduciary status and source-separated remedies govern.
- **Other Governing Law:** ERISA §§ 404 and 502(a)(3); *Mertens v. Hewitt Associates*; trust-law principles used within enacted terms.
- **Other Authority Used:** The meeting’s content, speaker capacity, purpose, and employees’ reliance.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The equitable relief ordered may stand to the extent it restores the employees to the benefits position lost through the fiduciary breach and remains within traditional equitable authority. The district court shall avoid duplicative recovery.

#### Issue 1: Fiduciary communication and equitable relief

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches fiduciary status, breach, and statutory remedy. Article III injury and causation are established by the loss of benefits following reliance.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** An employer is not a fiduciary merely because employment decisions affect a plan. It acts as a fiduciary when it speaks in an authorized plan-administration capacity about benefits and uses that authority to guide beneficiaries’ choices. Deliberate material deception breaches loyalty. Section 502(a)(3) provides appropriate equitable relief for violations when no more specific ERISA remedy adequately reaches the injury; it is not displaced by provisions that do not authorize relief for these plaintiffs.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Varity acted as an ERISA fiduciary when it intentionally misrepresented plan security to induce beneficiaries to surrender existing coverage, breached its duty of loyalty, and is subject to individualized equitable relief under § 502(a)(3).

Varity explained benefits, answered questions as plan administrator, and invoked participant trust while withholding known risk. The employees changed position and lost coverage. Denying relief because the statute contains other remedies that do not fit would leave the express catchall without work.

# Wisconsin v. City of New York; Oklahoma v. City of New York; Department of Commerce v. City of New York

- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Consolidated plenary merits review of the Secretary of Commerce’s decision not to statistically adjust the 1990 census.
- **Simulated Event Date:** March 20, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether the Secretary of Commerce’s decision not to use a post-enumeration survey statistical adjustment to correct the differential undercount in the 1990 census violated the Census Clause, and whether the Second Circuit applied an impermissibly heightened standard of constitutional review.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Secretary’s decision not to use the post-enumeration survey adjustment was within the constitutional discretion governing the actual enumeration, rather than subject to the heightened scrutiny applied by the Second Circuit.
- **Legal Field:** Census Clause, apportionment, constitutional review of federal census methodology, and delegated administrative judgment.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit, 34 F.3d 1114, reversed the district court’s judgment upholding the Secretary’s no-adjustment decision and held that the constitutional challenge required heightened scrutiny.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Wisconsin, Oklahoma, and the Department of Commerce seek reversal of the Second Circuit and restoration of the judgment sustaining the Secretary’s decision not to adjust the 1990 census figures; New York and allied respondents seek affirmance of the appellate judgment.
- **Material Facts and Record:** The initial 1990 enumeration undercounted some racial and socioeconomic groups at higher rates than others. The Census Bureau developed a post-enumeration survey and statistical adjustment intended to correct that differential undercount. After reviewing extensive technical work and competing recommendations, the Secretary declined to substitute the PES-adjusted figures for the initial enumeration, relying on concerns including distributive accuracy, historical census practice, and technical uncertainty about whether adjustment would improve the apportionment count. The district court upheld that decision; the Second Circuit reversed on the ground that a more searching constitutional standard applied.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The merits question before Stone is the preserved constitutional challenge to the Secretary’s no-adjustment decision and the standard governing that review. Stone does not decide statutory or Administrative Procedure Act theories not properly carried to the Court and does not decide whether statistical adjustment is categorically required or forbidden in a different census or record.

- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** *Wisconsin v. City of New York*, No. 94-1614; *Oklahoma v. City of New York*, No. 94-1631; and *Department of Commerce v. City of New York*, No. 94-1985, consolidated for decision.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 517 U.S. 1, supplies the consolidated posture, no-adjustment controversy, standard-of-review dispute, argument and decision dates, and historical Supreme Court disposition; 34 F.3d 1114 supplies the Second Circuit judgment; 822 F. Supp. 906 supplies the district-court judgment.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Opinion of the Court by Chief Justice Rehnquist for a unanimous Court.
- **Historical Holding:** The Secretary’s decision not to use the PES-based statistical adjustment was not subject to heightened scrutiny and was within the constitutional bounds of discretion because it bore a reasonable relationship to accomplishing an actual enumeration for apportionment.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 517 U.S. 1.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to chronological revalidation through March 20, 1996.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Constitutional review of the Secretary's no-adjustment decision

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision directly governs the Secretary’s choice whether to use the challenged post-enumeration statistical adjustment.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No census-specific registered standard controls. The Census Clause, the enacted delegation, and surviving *Department of Commerce v. Montana* and *Franklin v. Massachusetts* principles govern the constitutional allocation of methodological discretion.
- **Other Governing Law:** U.S. Const. art. I, § 2; Fourteenth Amendment, § 2; 13 U.S.C. § 141(a); surviving precedent recognizing broad congressional and delegated executive discretion over the census within the constitutional goal of equal representation.
- **Other Authority Used:** The acknowledged differential undercount, the PES research, the Secretary’s concern with distributive accuracy, historical census practice, technical disagreement, and the absence of a preserved finding of intentional discrimination.
- **Open Point:** Whether the Secretary’s decision not to adjust remained within the enacted delegation and bore a reasonable relationship to accomplishing the constitutionally required enumeration for apportionment.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Second Circuit in all consolidated cases.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Reverse the appellate judgment and leave in force the district court’s judgment sustaining the Secretary’s decision not to use the PES-based statistical adjustment. Stone expresses no view on statutory or Administrative Procedure Act claims not properly before the Court.

#### Issue 1: Constitutional review of the Secretary's no-adjustment decision

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the preserved constitutional challenge to the Secretary’s no-adjustment decision and the standard governing that review. He does not decide unappealed statutory or Administrative Procedure Act theories.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Article I requires an actual enumeration but leaves substantial methodological judgment to Congress and to the Secretary acting within the enacted delegation. The Secretary’s decision is not subjected to heightened scrutiny merely because census error affects voting power or falls unevenly across racial groups absent proof of discriminatory purpose. The constitutional question is whether the chosen census methodology remains authorized and bears a reasonable relationship to accomplishing the actual enumeration and its apportionment purpose.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Secretary’s decision not to use the PES-based statistical adjustment satisfies the Census Clause because it remains within the enacted delegation and bears a reasonable relationship to accomplishing the actual enumeration for apportionment; the disproportionate undercount does not, without discriminatory purpose, trigger heightened scrutiny or constitutionally compel adjustment.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The Secretary confronted competing forms of accuracy and a technically disputed adjustment methodology. He gave priority to distributive accuracy for apportionment, relied on historical census practice, reviewed extensive statistical work, and concluded that the PES adjustment had not been shown to improve the interstate distribution of the population count. The Constitution fixes the enumeration duty, not a judicially preferred statistical technique. The Second Circuit therefore erred by importing heightened voting-rights scrutiny into the constitutionally delegated census methodology decision.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not immunize intentional discrimination, disregard of material evidence, use of a method outside delegated authority, or a properly presented statutory or Administrative Procedure Act violation. He also does not hold that statistical adjustment is constitutionally forbidden in a future census supported by a materially different record.
