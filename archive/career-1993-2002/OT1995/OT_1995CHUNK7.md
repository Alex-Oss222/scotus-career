# Degen v. United States

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Degen v. United States*
- **Citation or Docket:** 517 U.S. 820; No. 95-173.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review of a civil-forfeiture judgment entered through fugitive disentitlement.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 10, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a district court may strike a claimant’s answer and enter summary judgment in a civil forfeiture action because the claimant remains abroad and does not appear to face related criminal charges.
- **Question Granted:** Whether inherent judicial authority permits complete disentitlement from the property action on these facts.
- **Legal Field:** Federal courts, inherent authority, civil forfeiture, due process, and fugitive disentitlement.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed an order striking Degen’s filings and forfeiting the property to the United States.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Degen seeks reversal and adjudication of the forfeiture merits; the United States seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** A federal indictment charged Degen with drug and money-laundering offenses. He remained in Switzerland, beyond compulsory return under the applicable extradition arrangements. In a parallel in rem action, the United States sought forfeiture of properties allegedly connected to the crimes. Degen appeared through counsel to contest the forfeiture, but the district court struck his claims solely because he did not submit to the criminal prosecution.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The court possessed jurisdiction over the property and could enforce a forfeiture judgment. Discovery management, adverse inferences, stays, and issue-specific sanctions remain distinct from categorical loss of the right to defend.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, April 22, 1996; decision, June 10, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** Related criminal charges remain pending but are not adjudicated here.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 517 U.S. 820; 21 U.S.C. § 881; forfeiture record summarized there.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Kennedy wrote for a unanimous Court.
- **Historical Holding:** Inherent authority did not permit striking the claim because jurisdiction over the property was secure and narrower measures addressed the asserted problems.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 517 U.S. 820.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to chronological revalidation.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Inherent power to extinguish a forfeiture defense

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision authorizes fugitive disentitlement in a separate civil action. Existing procedural law confines inherent powers to measures necessary and proportionate to protection of the court’s lawful proceedings.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Pre-seizure process for civil forfeiture of real property** recognizes that forfeiture does not suspend due process and that less intrusive measures ordinarily protect governmental interests. **Civil and criminal classification of contempt fines** likewise requires attention to function and lawful process rather than labels.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Ortega-Rodriguez v. United States*, *Chambers v. NASCO, Inc.*, in rem jurisdiction, and ordinary discovery and sanctions rules.
- **Other Authority Used:** Secure control over the res, availability of protective orders and stays, and absence of demonstrated prejudice requiring default.
- **Open Point:** Case-specific sanctions remain available when a claimant disobeys an order or uses the civil process abusively.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Restore Degen’s answer and permit adjudication of the forfeiture. The district court may use lawful, issue-specific measures to prevent discovery abuse, protect the criminal case, or sanction actual noncompliance.

#### Issue 1: Inherent power to extinguish a forfeiture defense

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the inherent-authority question. He does not decide ownership, traceability, or the criminal charges.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Inherent authority may preserve jurisdiction, enforce orders, protect evidence, and prevent abuse. It may not create a merits forfeiture unrelated to proof when narrower lawful measures fully protect the proceeding. A claimant’s absence from a related criminal case does not by itself waive property defenses in an in rem action the Government chose to initiate.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A court with secure jurisdiction over the property may not strike a claimant’s filings and forfeit the property solely because the claimant remains beyond reach of a related criminal prosecution.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Degen’s absence did not prevent adjudication or enforcement against the property. The Government’s discovery concerns can be addressed by sequencing, protective orders, stays, and ordinary sanctions tied to actual misconduct. Judicial dignity is not enhanced by refusing to hear a defense and entering a property judgment without proof. The sanction therefore exceeds any demonstrated necessity.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not protect disobedience of a discovery order, require unrestricted civil discovery, or prevent Congress from enacting a constitutionally adequate rule governing a materially defined form of fugitive abuse.

# United States v. International Business Machines Corp.

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *United States v. International Business Machines Corp.*
- **Citation or Docket:** 517 U.S. 843; No. 95-591.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review of an Export Clause tax-refund judgment.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 10, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether the Export Clause permits a generally applicable federal excise tax on premiums paid to foreign insurers for policies covering goods in export transit.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the insurance-premium tax is a prohibited tax on exports and whether *Thames & Mersey Marine Insurance Co. v. United States* should be retained.
- **Legal Field:** Constitutional taxation, Export Clause, stare decisis, and international shipping insurance.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Federal Circuit affirmed a refund judgment for IBM, holding the tax unconstitutional as applied to insurance of exported goods.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The United States seeks reversal; IBM seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** IBM shipped domestic products to foreign subsidiaries and insured the shipments against loss. When foreign subsidiaries obtained insurance from foreign carriers, IBM was a policy beneficiary. The Internal Revenue Service assessed the federal premium tax under 26 U.S.C. § 4371. IBM paid and sought refunds, invoking the Export Clause and the longstanding *Thames & Mersey* rule.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The constitutional tax question and stare decisis issue are preserved. Justice Stevens historically did not participate; no separate simulated participation fact is established beyond the historical comparator.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, March 18, 1996; decision, June 10, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** Multiple tax years are resolved in the same refund action.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified for the simulation.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 517 U.S. 843; U.S. Const. art. I, § 9, cl. 5; 26 U.S.C. § 4371.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 6-2; Justice Stevens did not participate.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Thomas wrote for the Court; Justice Kennedy dissented, joined by Justice Ginsburg.
- **Historical Holding:** The nondiscriminatory premium tax violated the Export Clause as applied to export insurance, and the Court retained *Thames & Mersey*.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 517 U.S. 843.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to chronological revalidation.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Export Clause and tax on export insurance

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision construes the Export Clause. Surviving Supreme Court authority includes *Thames & Mersey Marine Insurance Co. v. United States*, which treats a tax on insurance of export transit as a prohibited export tax.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No registered Export Clause test. **Precedent and candid change** from Master Jurisprudence informs Stone’s treatment but supplies no governing law; the governing inquiry remains constitutional text and binding precedent.
- **Other Governing Law:** U.S. Const. art. I, § 9, cl. 5; *Fairbank v. United States*; *Richfield Oil Corp. v. State Board of Equalization*; Import-Export Clause cases as limited analogies.
- **Other Authority Used:** Textual difference between “Tax or Duty” and “Imposts or Duties,” settled commercial reliance, and the Government’s litigated theory.
- **Open Point:** Whether *Thames & Mersey* should be reconsidered on a directly presented functional theory remains open because the Government does not supply an adequate basis here.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The refund judgment remains in force. No separate remedial instruction is required.

#### Issue 1: Export Clause and tax on export insurance

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional question because the statute otherwise applies and no narrower construction removes the assessment.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The Export Clause categorically denies Congress authority to lay a tax or duty on exports. A tax imposed on a transaction inseparably incident to export transit falls within the settled *Thames & Mersey* rule. Nondiscrimination cannot save a tax the text forbids categorically. Departure from that century-old construction would require a squarely presented legal defect and treatment of concrete reliance, not analogy to differently worded dormant Commerce or Import-Export doctrine.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 4371 may not be applied to premiums paid for insurance covering goods in export transit because that application imposes a tax within the Export Clause’s prohibition.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The insurance protects the goods only as they move through export. The Government asks the Court to narrow express constitutional text through doctrines developed under distinct clauses. The text, longstanding precedent, and stable commercial understanding point the other way. Stone therefore applies the existing rule rather than using this record to redesign it.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not exempt exporters from ordinary income, property, payroll, or generally applicable taxes imposed on independent events unrelated to export transit.

# Lockheed Corp. v. Spink

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Lockheed Corp. v. Spink*
- **Citation or Docket:** 517 U.S. 882; No. 95-809.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review of ERISA fiduciary and retroactivity rulings.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 10, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether an employer acts as an ERISA fiduciary when amending a pension plan to offer enhanced early-retirement benefits in exchange for releases, whether the arrangement is a prohibited transaction, and whether 1986 statutory amendments require credit for earlier service.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Ninth Circuit correctly reinstated Spink’s ERISA claims and applied the statutory amendments retroactively.
- **Legal Field:** ERISA, fiduciary status, prohibited transactions, plan amendments, releases, and retroactivity.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed dismissal in relevant part, treating the release program as a prohibited use of plan assets and requiring credit for pre-1988 service.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Lockheed seeks reversal and dismissal; Spink seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Spink was excluded from Lockheed’s plan when rehired at age 61 under then-lawful rules. After 1986 amendments, Lockheed admitted previously excluded older employees but did not credit pre-1988 service. It later offered enhanced early-retirement benefits to employees who waived employment claims. Spink declined the release and sued, challenging both the amendment and the service-credit decision.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Fiduciary status must be established before § 406 prohibited-transaction liability. Plan-sponsor amendment conduct and plan administration are distinct. Statutory temporal reach must be addressed separately.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, April 22, 1996; decision, June 10, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** Claims against Lockheed, directors, and retirement-committee members arise from the same plan.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 517 U.S. 882; ERISA §§ 3(21), 404, and 406; OBRA 1986 provisions.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Thomas wrote for a unanimous Court; separate opinions addressed aspects of the release.
- **Historical Holding:** Plan amendment was settlor conduct, the release was not a prohibited transaction on the pleaded theory, and the 1986 provisions did not require retroactive service credit.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 517 U.S. 882.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, including *John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank*.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Those cases do not control until effective before June 10, 1996.

#### Issue 1: Fiduciary status and the release program

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *John Hancock* makes fiduciary status functional and attaches it only “to the extent” a person exercises authority or control over plan assets or administration. Status does not arise categorically from corporate form.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No separate registered ERISA amendment rule. The functional fiduciary principle and source separation between status, duty, transaction, cause of action, and remedy govern.
- **Other Governing Law:** ERISA §§ 3(21), 404, and 406; *Curtiss-Wright Corp. v. Schoonejongen*.
- **Other Authority Used:** Employer’s role as plan sponsor when adopting the amendment and committee’s later implementation.
- **Open Point:** A release procured through fraud, coercion, or administration inconsistent with plan terms remains separately actionable if proved.

#### Issue 2: Temporal reach of the service-credit amendments

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Landgraf v. USI Film Products* and *Rivers v. Roadway Express* require a clear temporal command before new legal consequences attach to completed conduct.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The registered statutory-interpretation rules on retroactivity and completed legal consequences.
- **Other Governing Law:** OBRA 1986 §§ 9201 through 9203; ERISA and ADEA provisions.
- **Other Authority Used:** Effective-date language and Lockheed’s pre-amendment exclusion.
- **Open Point:** None.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Dismiss the § 406 claim insofar as it rests on plan-sponsor amendment conduct and dismiss the demand for pre-1988 service credit under the 1986 amendments. The lower courts may consider any independently preserved claim that implementation violated plan terms or another applicable law.

#### Issue 1: Fiduciary status and the release program

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches status before prohibited-transaction liability.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** An employer acts as settlor, not fiduciary, when deciding whether and how to amend a benefit plan. Section 406 applies only if a fiduciary causes the plan to enter the challenged transaction. Offering additional benefits in exchange for a release is not, without more, a transfer of plan assets for the employer’s benefit forbidden by § 406; the participant exchanges a claim release for an optional enhancement.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Lockheed and its directors did not act as ERISA fiduciaries in adopting the early-retirement amendment, and the release condition does not state a § 406(a)(1)(D) violation on the pleaded facts.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** ERISA deliberately makes status function specific. The Ninth Circuit skipped the predicate and converted every employer benefit-design decision into fiduciary administration. Spink remained free to reject the offer and retain his claims, as he did. That does not establish misuse of assets by a fiduciary.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not validate an unlawful waiver, misrepresentation, coercive implementation, or administration contrary to the plan.

#### Issue 2: Temporal reach of the service-credit amendments

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory question.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The 1986 amendments prohibit specified age-based rules prospectively. They do not clearly require plans to recreate benefit accrual for service completed under a lawful prior exclusion.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The amendments do not require Lockheed to credit Spink’s pre-effective-date service years.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Retroactive credit would attach a new benefit obligation to completed service and plan administration. Congress supplied effective dates rather than a clear command to recalculate all prior accrual. *Landgraf* therefore bars the proposed reach.

# United States v. Myers

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *United States v. Myers*
- **Citation or Docket:** 46 F.3d 668 (7th Cir. 1995), No. 94-2222.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Granted merits review after a conditional guilty plea and denial of suppression.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 11, 1996, simulation-assigned opinion-delivery date.
- **Question Presented:** Whether warrantless thermal imaging of a home to infer activities inside is a Fourth Amendment search and, if so, what follows for the later warrant.
- **Question Granted:** Does warrantless use of thermal-imaging technology to detect heat patterns emanating from a private home constitute a search within the meaning of the Fourth Amendment?
- **Legal Field:** Fourth Amendment, home, sense-enhancing technology, warrants, and suppression.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Seventh Circuit affirmed the conviction, holding that the Agema 210 scan of heat emitted from Myers’s home was not a search.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Myers seeks reversal of the suppression ruling and vacatur of his conviction; the United States seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Police suspected indoor marijuana cultivation from surveillance, purchases, electric use, and the absence of curbside garbage. Without a warrant they aimed an Agema 210 thermal imager at Myers’s residence and detected unusual heat patterns associated with grow lights. The scan and other information supported a search-warrant affidavit; execution uncovered marijuana and equipment. Myers entered a conditional plea preserving suppression.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The controlled grant reaches whether the thermal scan was a Fourth Amendment search. Suppression, independent source, attenuation, good-faith reliance, and the consequences for the conditional plea are downstream remedial questions for remand and receive no independent merits holding here.
- **Relevant Dates:** Thermal scan, March 12, 1992; Seventh Circuit decision, February 2, 1995; simulated decision, June 11, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** 46 F.3d 668; warrant and technology described in the opinion.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits decision. The Seventh Circuit affirmance is the historical anchor.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Published panel opinion affirming denial of suppression.
- **Historical Holding:** Thermal imaging of emitted heat was not a search because no protected expectation of privacy existed in the heat.
- **Historical Authority:** Lower-court historical anchor.
- **Historical Sources:** 46 F.3d 668.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, including the simulated decision in *United States v. Pinson*.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** *Ornelas v. United States*, if effective, governs appellate review of probable cause but not whether the thermal scan is a search.

#### Issue 1: Thermal imaging of a home

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *United States v. Pinson*, 24 F.3d 1056, decided May 8, 1995, holds that law-enforcement use of sense-enhancing technology from outside a home to obtain information about activity within that ordinary public observation could not otherwise acquire is a search.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Technological acquisition of information from inside a home**.
- **Other Governing Law:** Fourth Amendment warrant requirement; *Katz v. United States*; *Payton v. New York*; *United States v. Leon* as to remedy.
- **Other Authority Used:** The scan’s purpose, information obtained, relation to interior grow lights, and use in the affidavit.
- **Open Point:** The remaining affidavit, probable cause, good faith, and causal consequences were not decided in *Pinson* and require record-specific adjudication.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Seventh Circuit’s no-search holding, vacate the conviction, and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court shall treat the thermal scan as a warrantless search, excise or otherwise account for the unlawfully obtained information, and determine whether the warrant and evidence survive under independent-source, attenuation, or objectively reasonable reliance principles. If suppression is required, Myers may withdraw or obtain relief from the conditional plea as governing procedure provides.

#### Issue 1: Thermal imaging of a home

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the search question and remands remedy.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The constitutional inquiry turns on what officers deliberately learned about activity inside the home, not whether the device physically emitted energy or whether the signal was described as “waste heat.” Technology used to infer otherwise unavailable interior activity crosses the Fourth Amendment threshold and ordinarily requires a warrant.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The warrantless Agema 210 scan of Myers’s residence was a search because officers used sense-enhancing technology to obtain information about activity inside the home unavailable through ordinary public observation.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The police did not measure heat for an environmental or safety purpose. They used a pattern image to infer operation of grow lights behind the walls. Calling the carrier heat rather than light or sound cannot determine constitutional protection. *Pinson* controls and forecloses the Seventh Circuit’s contrary premise.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Ordinary observation from a lawful vantage, perceptible smoke or odor, utility records obtained through lawful process, and technology revealing no interior activity remain outside this holding.

# Shaw v. Hunt; Pope v. Hunt

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Shaw v. Hunt; Pope v. Hunt*
- **Citation or Docket:** 517 U.S. 899; Nos. 94-923 and 94-924.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Direct appeals after remand in a congressional racial-gerrymandering challenge.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 13, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether North Carolina’s Twelfth Congressional District, drawn with race predominant, is justified as a remedy for an identified Voting Rights Act or constitutional violation.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the district survives the scrutiny required by the Stone Court’s predominance-and-remedy framework.
- **Legal Field:** Equal protection, racial districting, Voting Rights Act compliance, standing, and remedial design.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** A three-judge district court upheld District 12 after trial, concluding that the State’s asserted voting-rights interests justified the district.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The Shaw and Pope appellants seek reversal; North Carolina and intervenors seek affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** North Carolina’s plan created a long, narrow majority-Black district connecting separated population concentrations along an interstate corridor. The record showed substantial use of racial data and subordination of ordinary districting criteria. The State invoked remediation of discrimination and compliance with §§ 2 and 5 of the Voting Rights Act, but the trial court found that eradication of past discrimination did not actually precipitate the design, and the asserted § 2 injury was not tied to a geographically compact minority population corresponding to District 12.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The resident plaintiffs have standing under the district-specific rule. Racial predominance and district-specific justification are preserved. The Court must apply simulated *Miller*, not substitute the historical strict-scrutiny-only framework.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, December 5, 1995; decision, June 13, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** *Shaw* and *Pope* challenge the same district and judgment.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 517 U.S. 899; district-court judgment, 861 F. Supp. 408; plan record summarized in the opinions.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, 5-4.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Chief Justice Rehnquist wrote for five Justices; Justices Stevens and Souter dissented in separate writings with additional joins.
- **Historical Holding:** District 12 failed strict scrutiny because the asserted remedial and VRA interests did not justify its design.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 517 U.S. 899.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, including *United States v. Hays* and simulated *Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson*.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Standing and racial predominance

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *United States v. Hays / Louisiana v. Hays* requires residence in the challenged district or another concrete personal classification. Simulated *Miller* defines predominance as material subordination of traditional race-neutral districting principles.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Standing for racial-gerrymandering claims** and **Racial predominance in districting**.
- **Other Governing Law:** The Equal Protection Clause and the district-specific record.
- **Other Authority Used:** Shape, boundary choices, racial targets, compactness, political geography, and the design process.
- **Open Point:** None on standing or predominance.

#### Issue 2: Scrutiny and remedial classification

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Simulated *Miller* requires a race-predominant district to be classified as a genuinely fitted district-specific remedy for a demonstrated constitutional or Voting Rights Act violation, or as unsupported racial sorting. The corresponding intermediate or strict scrutiny then applies, and the State bears the remedial showing.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Scrutiny of race-predominant districts** and **Voting Rights Act § 2 totality, proportionality, and nonmaximization**.
- **Other Governing Law:** Voting Rights Act §§ 2 and 5; *Thornburg v. Gingles*; *Johnson v. De Grandy*.
- **Other Authority Used:** The claimed district-specific remedial predicate, compactness evidence, bloc voting, boundary fit, and alternative designs.
- **Open Point:** The lower court did not determine whether District 12 qualifies for the fitted remedial lane or instead constitutes unsupported racial sorting.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand; prohibit use of the existing district in another election unless it is sustained under the controlling simulated *Miller* sequence.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The lower court shall determine whether District 12 is a genuinely fitted, district-specific remedy for a demonstrated violation or unsupported racial sorting, then apply the corresponding scrutiny. The existing district may not be used in another election unless the State satisfies that sequence. Stone does not direct a particular replacement map.

#### Issue 1: Standing and racial predominance

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the claims of residents of District 12 and rejects claims by nonresidents lacking another concrete personal classification.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Racial awareness is insufficient, but race predominates where it materially subordinates the criteria that otherwise would have shaped the district. The district-specific record establishes predominance in District 12.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Residents of District 12 have standing, and race predominated in the district's design.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The boundary choices, racial targets, connectors, and departures from ordinary political geography show that race materially controlled the design. Shape is evidence rather than an element, and the conclusion rests on the full design record.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not treat racial data, Voting Rights Act compliance analysis, or irregular shape alone as unconstitutional.

#### Issue 2: Scrutiny and remedial classification

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the governing sequence but remands final classification and application because the lower court moved directly from predominance to strict scrutiny.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** After predominance, North Carolina must identify an actual district-specific constitutional or § 2 violation and establish a substantial fit between that injury and the racial design to qualify for simulated *Miller*'s remedial lane. Unsupported racial sorting, maximization, unnecessary packing, or separation receives strict scrutiny. The Court may not supply missing findings from appellate inference.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** After race predominates in a district, the State must establish whether the design is a genuinely fitted, district-specific remedy for a demonstrated violation or unsupported racial sorting, and the corresponding scrutiny under simulated *Miller* then applies.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The district court used the wrong legal sequence and did not resolve the predicates controlling the scrutiny assignment. The record contains evidence bearing on compactness, bloc voting, and remedial fit, but those matters require district-specific findings. Remand preserves lawful repair without validating a remedial label or presuming invalidity.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not presume Voting Rights Act compliance from invocation alone, authorize maximization, decide District 12's ultimate validity, or create a third districting framework.

# Bush v. Vera; Lawson v. Vera; United States v. Vera

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Bush v. Vera; Lawson v. Vera; United States v. Vera*
- **Citation or Docket:** 517 U.S. 952; Nos. 94-805, 94-806, and 94-988.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Direct appeals from a three-judge district court.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 13, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether Texas Congressional Districts 18, 29, and 30 were drawn with race predominant and, if so, whether each is a genuinely fitted voting-rights remedy or unsupported racial sorting.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the judgment invalidating the three districts may stand under the Stone Court’s district-specific two-lane scrutiny.
- **Legal Field:** Equal protection, racial districting, standing, political considerations, and Voting Rights Act remediation.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The three-judge Southern District of Texas invalidated Districts 18, 29, and 30 and ordered remedial proceedings.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Texas, private intervenors, and the United States seek reversal; resident plaintiffs seek affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** After receiving three additional seats, Texas created a new majority-Black Dallas district, a new majority-Hispanic Houston district, and reconfigured an existing majority-Black Houston district. Legislators used detailed racial data and pursued minority-opportunity goals while also protecting incumbents and political interests. The district court found that race predominated and that the challenged boundaries substantially departed from compactness, subdivision lines, and communities of interest.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Only residents of each challenged district possess ordinary standing under *Hays*; a plaintiff residing elsewhere requires a separate personal injury. The district court applied a strict-scrutiny framework that must be reconciled with simulated *Miller*’s remedial lane. The record is district specific but contains mixed motives and disputed remedial predicates.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, December 5, 1995; decision, June 13, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** Three appeals from the same judgment; each challenged district is a separable merits component.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 517 U.S. 952; district-court judgment, 861 F. Supp. 1304; Texas plan record summarized there.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 5-4, through a fractured set of opinions.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O’Connor announced the judgment and wrote principal portions; separate concurrences and dissents produced different coalitions by district and rationale.
