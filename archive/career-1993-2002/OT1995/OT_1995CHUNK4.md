# National Association for the Advancement of Colored People v. Fordice

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *National Association for the Advancement of Colored People v. Fordice*
- **Citation or Docket:** Supplied caption for the Mississippi higher-education desegregation remedial proceeding; *Ayers v. Fordice*, 879 F. Supp. 1419 (N.D. Miss. 1995), supplies the identified judgment and record anchor.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Granted merits review of a remedial decree following *United States v. Fordice*.
- **Simulated Event Date:** March 25, 1996, simulation-assigned opinion-delivery date.
- **Question Presented:** Whether Mississippi’s proposed and court-ordered policies adequately dismantle traceable vestiges of its de jure university system, and whether each component of the decree is tied to an identified violation and educationally practicable remedy.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the admissions, mission, program, funding, scholarship, and institutional remedies satisfy the State’s constitutional duty without converting desegregation into racial balancing or indefinite federal administration.
- **Legal Field:** Equal protection, higher-education desegregation, structural remedies, and federal equitable power.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The United States District Court for the Northern District of Mississippi found continuing vestiges in several parts of Mississippi’s public university system and entered a detailed remedial decree, including admissions changes, program and funding measures, and monitoring.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Private plaintiffs seek broader correction of traceable policies; state defendants seek narrowing and greater deference to educational judgments; the United States supports effective dismantling tied to proved vestiges.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Mississippi historically operated separate white and Black public universities. After the earlier Supreme Court remand, the district court found that some admissions standards, mission assignments, duplicated programs, funding consequences, and scholarship practices remained traceable to the former system and continued to shape racial identifiability and student choice. The decree adopted some proposals, rejected others as educationally unsound, and ordered targeted investments and policy changes.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The appeal presents final or appealable remedial rulings. Each challenged provision must be assessed separately. The Court may not infer present discriminatory effect, practicability, or educational necessity beyond the district court’s supported findings, and it may not require racial proportionality as the constitutional endpoint.
- **Relevant Dates:** Remedial decree, March 7, 1995; published findings, 879 F. Supp. 1419; simulated decision, March 25, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** The private-plaintiff and United States proceedings arise from the same statewide system and remedial decree.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** 879 F. Supp. 1419; *United States v. Fordice*, 505 U.S. 717; the district-court findings and remedial proposals described in the record.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits decision at this stage. The district-court decree and later Fifth Circuit review are historical anchors.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** District-court findings and remedial decree from the United States District Court for the Northern District of Mississippi; later appellate review addressed individual remedial components.
- **Historical Holding:** Mississippi remained obliged to eliminate policies traceable to de jure segregation that continued to have segregative effects and could practicably be eliminated without undermining sound educational policy.
- **Historical Authority:** Lower-court historical anchor under the earlier Supreme Court mandate.
- **Historical Sources:** 879 F. Supp. 1419; 505 U.S. 717.

### A. Law entering the case

#### Issue 1: Undergraduate admissions and related admissions policies

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Missouri v. Jenkins* requires each remedial component to be tied to an identified vestige of the constitutional violation and requires withdrawal of federal control from a separable component once the attributable vestige has been eliminated to the extent practicable.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Violation-remedy nexus and interdistrict attraction** and **Achievement benchmarks and incremental withdrawal of control.**
- **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause; *Brown v. Board of Education*; *Green v. County School Board*; *Bazemore v. Friday*; *United States v. Fordice*, 505 U.S. 717 (1992). *Fordice* specifically requires Mississippi to reform policies traceable to the de jure system that continue to foster segregation when practicable, educationally sound alternatives exist.
- **Other Authority Used:** The district court's findings that the former differential undergraduate standards were traceable to the de jure system and continued to influence student choice; its separate findings that the challenged ACT-based scholarship practices and graduate-admissions practices were not proved to be traceable vestiges.
- **Open Point:** None that prevents a component-specific judgment on the admissions provisions of the decree.

#### Issue 2: Missions, academic programs, duplication, and number of institutions

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Missouri v. Jenkins* requires a specific causal and remedial connection for each structural or programmatic measure and forbids use of general institutional improvement, demographic attraction, or racial balance as independent constitutional objectives.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Violation-remedy nexus and interdistrict attraction** and **Achievement benchmarks and incremental withdrawal of control.**
- **Other Governing Law:** *United States v. Fordice* identifies mission assignments, unnecessary program duplication, and continued operation of the eight-university system as constitutionally suspect when they remain traceable, continue to foster segregation, and can practicably be reformed consistent with sound educational practices.
- **Other Authority Used:** The district court's findings concerning the arrested development and limited professional programs of JSU and ASU; the segregative interaction of differential admissions and duplication in the Delta; the educational uncertainty surrounding actual consolidation; and the lack of a demonstrated desegregative justification for the proposed MUW/MSU merger.
- **Open Point:** A final DSU/MVSU consolidation decision remains premature because the district court ordered further study rather than making the findings necessary to compel merger.

#### Issue 3: Funding, facilities, land-grant measures, endowments, and race-specific scholarships

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Missouri v. Jenkins* bars remedial expenditures that are justified only by general institutional improvement, prestige, racial balance, or historical inequality without a present violation-remedy nexus. A remedial expenditure may remain when it is reasonably directed to eliminating a current, identified vestige to the extent practicable.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Violation-remedy nexus and interdistrict attraction** and **Achievement benchmarks and incremental withdrawal of control.**
- **Other Governing Law:** *United States v. Fordice*; *City of Richmond v. J.A. Croson Co.* for an express state racial classification allocating an individualized benefit. The district court found no current traceable defect in the general funding formula or capital-facilities allocation, found the existing land-grant allocation educationally sound and not presently shown to deter enrollment, but separately found mission-related underdevelopment at JSU and ASU.
- **Other Authority Used:** The decree's $15 million JSU capital package, the JSU and ASU endowments, the white-applicant scholarship provisions, the ASU Small Farm Development Center funding, and the ASU MBA program.
- **Open Point:** The present record does not supply sufficiently specific findings tying the general JSU capital package or the unrestricted portions of the JSU and ASU endowments to a particular current vestige and demonstrating the necessity and duration of those expenditures.

#### Issue 4: Monitoring, studies, and continuing federal supervision

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Missouri v. Jenkins* requires incremental withdrawal of federal control from separable components in which the State has complied and the relevant vestige has been eliminated to the extent practicable.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Achievement benchmarks and incremental withdrawal of control.**
- **Other Governing Law:** *Freeman v. Pitts*; *Green*; *Fordice*; ordinary federal equitable-remedy principles.
- **Other Authority Used:** The Monitoring Committee, the decree's reporting and study requirements, and the retained-jurisdiction provision.
- **Open Point:** Monitoring may continue only for surviving remedial obligations and may not become continuing supervision of areas for which no traceable vestige was found.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm in part, vacate in part, and remand. More specifically: affirm the decree's uniform undergraduate admissions remedy; affirm the identified JSU program additions, JSU institutional/program-duplication study, JSU articulation and Universities Center measures, the ASU MBA program and related necessary capital support, and the DSU/MVSU study without authorizing consolidation; affirm the district court's refusal to require a general funding-formula rewrite, wholesale land-grant restructuring, a MUW/MSU merger, or systemwide elimination of nonproximate program duplication. Vacate the decree's general JSU capital-enhancement package, the JSU and ASU endowment provisions, the ASU Small Farm Development Center matching-fund mandate, the facility-maintenance study, the graduate-catalog monitoring requirement, and the systemwide community-college coordination study to the extent specified below. The portions of the JSU and ASU endowments authorizing scholarships exclusively for white applicants are vacated. The decree's general injunction and retained supervision are affirmed only as narrowed to the surviving *Fordice* violations and remedies.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court shall revise the decree component by component. It may retain the provisions Stone affirms. It shall remove the provisions Stone vacates because the existing findings establish no remediable *Fordice* predicate. It may reconsider the non-race-specific portions of the JSU capital and JSU/ASU endowment provisions only if it identifies the particular current vestige each expenditure remedies, explains why the expenditure is necessary and educationally sound, and fixes meaningful limits on duration and supervision. Any future race-specific scholarship or other individualized racial allocation must satisfy the independently applicable constitutional standard. The Monitoring Committee and retained jurisdiction may continue only for surviving remedial obligations, and control shall terminate incrementally as each obligation is satisfied.

#### Issue 1: Undergraduate admissions and related admissions policies

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the admissions components because they form part of the decree and the parties' requested relief.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The former differential undergraduate admissions standards are the clearest *Fordice* vestige in the record: they were traceable to the de jure system, had discriminatory origins, and continued to influence student choice. A uniform statewide standard that is educationally sound and eliminates the HBI/HWI differential is directly fitted to that violation. By contrast, the record did not establish that the challenged ACT-based alumni or academic scholarship rules were traceable to the de jure system or presently fostered segregation, and the district court expressly found graduate-admissions policies not to be vestiges even while directing voluntary catalog reform.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The decree's uniform 1995 undergraduate admissions standards are affirmed because they directly eliminate a traceable differential admissions policy that continued to foster segregation. No additional *Fordice* remedy is authorized for the preexisting ACT-based scholarship rules or graduate-admissions practices on the findings made here.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** *Fordice* itself identified Mississippi's differential admissions structure as a continuing remnant requiring reform. The district court found the Board's uniform standards educationally sound and reasonably capable of eliminating that channeling effect. That is a direct violation-remedy fit. The scholarship and graduate-admissions claims stand differently because the findings do not establish the required traceability and continuing segregative effect. A disparate effect or misleading catalog, without the *Fordice* predicate, does not authorize desegregation relief.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not constitutionalize open admissions, prohibit educationally sound standardized testing, or prevent ordinary statutory or equal-protection challenges to a scholarship or graduate-admissions rule on a different record.

#### Issue 2: Missions, academic programs, duplication, and number of institutions

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches each separable mission, program, duplication, and institutional-structure component of the decree.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The limited missions and underdevelopment of the HBIs are traceable to the de jure system and, in conjunction with admissions and program structure, can continue to influence student choice. Specific program additions and studies may therefore be used when the record identifies the affected institution, the missing or duplicated programmatic opportunity, the present segregative mechanism, and an educationally sound corrective measure. That principle does not authorize program transfer, closure, or merger merely to change racial composition or institutional prestige.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The identified JSU program additions, JSU institutional and duplication study, JSU articulation and Universities Center measures, and the ASU MBA program are affirmed. The DSU/MVSU study is affirmed only as a study designed to determine whether a practicable, educationally sound remedy exists; no merger is authorized by this judgment. The district court's refusal to require a MUW/MSU merger and its refusal to order broad elimination or transfer of nonproximate duplicative programs are affirmed.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The district court found that JSU's arrested development and absence of professional and advanced programs were products of the former system and affected its ability to attract other-race students. It also found the specified JSU additions and ASU MBA educationally sound. Those targeted measures fit the current mission-related vestiges more closely than wholesale reorganization. Program duplication between DSU and MVSU is traceable and can foster segregation because the institutions are proximate, but the record did not establish that consolidation is presently the only educationally sound practicable remedy. The study requirement therefore survives while merger remains reserved. The proposed MUW/MSU merger, by contrast, lacked a demonstrated desegregative necessity and may not be constitutionally compelled for fiscal or symbolic reasons.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that every HBI must receive a unique professional school, that every duplicated program is unconstitutional, that racial identifiability is itself a violation, or that closure or merger is forbidden when later findings establish that it is necessary, practicable, and educationally sound to eliminate a proven vestige.

#### Issue 3: Funding, facilities, land-grant measures, endowments, and race-specific scholarships

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the challenged funding and expenditure components because they are separate parts of the decree and may independently impose continuing remedial obligations.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** *Fordice* and *Jenkins* do not authorize compensatory equalization of institutions for its own sake. The constitutional question is whether a present policy traceable to the dual system continues to foster segregation and whether the expenditure ordered is reasonably necessary to reform that policy. The district court found the current general funding formula educationally sound and not itself a traceable discriminatory policy, found capital-facilities allocation not traceable, and found the current land-grant structure educationally sound with no present policy shown to prevent or discourage cross-racial enrollment. Those findings cannot support a compulsory constitutional expenditure merely to compensate for historical underinvestment. At the same time, a specifically identified mission-related program may receive the funding necessary to make the otherwise valid remedial program real. An express scholarship preference for white applicants is a discrete racial allocation and cannot be sustained without the independent constitutional justification applicable to an express state racial classification.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The district court's refusal to rewrite the general funding formula, require wholesale land-grant restructuring, or order general facilities equalization is affirmed. The ASU MBA's necessary program-specific funding is affirmed. Decree paragraph 8's Small Farm Development Center matching-fund mandate and paragraph 11's facility-maintenance study are vacated because the findings do not establish the current *Fordice* predicate necessary for those compulsory remedies. Decree paragraph 6 and the non-race-specific portions of paragraphs 7 and 9 are vacated and remanded for more specific violation-remedy findings before those general capital and endowment expenditures may be constitutionally compelled. The white-applicant scholarship portions of paragraphs 7 and 9 are vacated; they may not be reimposed without satisfying the independently applicable review for an individualized racial benefit.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The record establishes profound historical underinvestment in the HBIs, but historical inequality and institutional image alone are not the current constitutional violation. *Jenkins* requires the remedial court to identify the surviving vestige being corrected and to connect the money ordered to elimination of that vestige. The JSU $15 million physical-enhancement package and general endowment funds were justified in significant part by image, prestige, and racial-diversity objectives without sufficiently precise findings showing why the particular expenditures are necessary to eliminate a present traceable policy. They therefore require reconsideration rather than automatic affirmance. The land-grant findings are stronger on historical causation but weaker on current segregative effect: the court expressly found the existing allocation educationally sound and did not identify a current state policy preventing cross-racial access. That defeats a compulsory equalization remedy under *Fordice*. The ASU MBA stands differently because it is a concrete mission-related program found educationally sound and directed to the underdeveloped programmatic structure.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not prevent Mississippi from voluntarily funding JSU or ASU above the constitutional minimum, expanding land-grant programs, improving facilities, establishing endowments, or conducting race-neutral recruitment. The holding addresses only what a federal desegregation decree may compel.

#### Issue 4: Monitoring, studies, and continuing federal supervision

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches oversight because the Monitoring Committee and retained jurisdiction are operative parts of the decree.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A monitoring mechanism may administer a valid structural remedy, receive studies necessary to determine whether an identified vestige can be eliminated practicably, and report compliance to the court. It may not supervise subjects for which the court found no traceable vestige or continue after the relevant remedial obligation has been satisfied. The district court's continuing jurisdiction is therefore valid only at the scope necessary to enforce surviving components and must contract as compliance occurs.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Decree paragraph 1 and paragraph 16 are affirmed as narrowed to the surviving remedial obligations. Paragraph 15 is affirmed only as an administrative funding and accountability provision for valid remedies. Paragraph 13's graduate-catalog monitoring requirement and paragraph 14's systemwide community-college coordination study are vacated because the district court found graduate admissions not to be a de jure vestige and identified no community-college policy traceable to the university system's prior de jure segregation. The JSU study and DSU/MVSU study remain permissible because they investigate remedies for identified mission and duplication vestiges.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** *Jenkins* and *Freeman* require federal control to follow the violation and to end when the constitutional work is complete. A monitoring committee is a means of administering a lawful decree, not an independent source of judicial authority. The graduate-catalog and systemwide community-college requirements exceed that boundary on the findings made here. The community-college system is the subject of separate litigation, and the district court expressly found no university-case traceability for its current policies. The remaining studies are tied to identified mission and program-duplication questions and therefore may continue until those questions are resolved and any valid remedy is implemented.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not impose a fixed termination date. The district court may retain enough jurisdiction to ensure compliance with each surviving obligation, but it must relinquish supervision of a separable component once the relevant vestige has been eliminated to the extent practicable.

# Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida*
- **Citation or Docket:** 517 U.S. 44; No. 94-12.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review.
- **Simulated Event Date:** March 27, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether Congress validly authorized an Indian tribe to sue a State in federal court under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act for failure to negotiate a compact in good faith.
- **Question Granted:** Whether IGRA unmistakably abrogates state sovereign immunity, whether Congress possesses that authority under the Indian Commerce Clause, and whether prospective officer relief remains available.
- **Legal Field:** State sovereign immunity, Indian commerce, federal jurisdiction, and prospective relief.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit held that Florida possessed immunity and barred the statutory suit.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The Tribe seeks reversal and enforcement of IGRA’s remedial process; Florida seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** IGRA directs States and tribes to negotiate gaming compacts in good faith and expressly authorizes tribes to sue States in federal court when that duty is breached. The Seminole Tribe alleged that Florida failed to negotiate as required. The action sought statutory prospective relief through the sequence Congress enacted, not retrospective damages.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The Court must decide immunity before the merits of good faith. Clear abrogation, constitutional authority, cause of action, and remedy remain separate. If the statutory suit is unavailable, the Court must determine whether an officer suit is consistent with the remedial structure without manufacturing a substitute Congress excluded.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, October 11, 1995; simulated decision, March 27, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 517 U.S. 44; IGRA, 25 U.S.C. § 2710(d); Eleventh Circuit proceedings.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 5-4.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Opinion of the Court by Chief Justice Rehnquist; Justice Stevens dissented; Justice Souter dissented, joined by Justices Ginsburg and Breyer.
- **Historical Holding:** Congress may not abrogate state sovereign immunity under the Indian Commerce Clause, and IGRA’s detailed remedy displaced an *Ex parte Young* action.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 517 U.S. 44.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, including *Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp.*
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Article I abrogation of state sovereign immunity

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** The surviving rule in *Pennsylvania v. Union Gas Co.* permits Congress, when acting under the Interstate Commerce Clause and speaking unmistakably, to subject a State to private federal suit. No simulated holding has overruled that proposition. IGRA unmistakably authorizes a Tribe to sue a State for failure to negotiate in good faith.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No Standards and Tests entry directly resolves Article I abrogation. **Eleventh Amendment status of a bistate compact entity** concerns whether an entity shares a State's immunity and does not decide Congress's power to abrogate immunity.
- **Other Governing Law:** The Eleventh Amendment, the Indian Commerce Clause, Article III, *Union Gas*, *Fitzpatrick v. Bitzer*, and IGRA, 25 U.S.C. § 2710(d).
- **Other Authority Used:** IGRA's express cause of action and remedial sequence; *Union Gas*'s treatment of congressional abrogation through an Article I commerce power.
- **Open Point:** Whether the Indian Commerce Clause is materially different from the Interstate Commerce Clause for the abrogation proposition recognized in *Union Gas*.

#### Issue 2: Alternative officer action

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision determines whether IGRA's detailed compacting remedy leaves a parallel *Ex parte Young* route available.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Existing source-separation rules require the substantive duty, proper defendant, cause of action, immunity rule, and remedy to be established independently.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Ex parte Young* and IGRA's negotiation, bad-faith, mediation, and Secretarial fallback provisions.
- **Other Authority Used:** The lower court's separate disposition of the officer claim.
- **Open Point:** The officer question becomes unnecessary if Florida is a proper statutory defendant.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** **REVERSE the sovereign-immunity dismissal; VACATE the officer-action ruling; REMAND for adjudication under IGRA.**
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court shall determine whether Florida failed to negotiate in good faith and, if so, administer the prospective negotiation, mediation, and Secretarial sequence Congress enacted. Stone authorizes no damages, judicially imposed compact, or remedy outside IGRA.

#### Issue 1: Article I abrogation of state sovereign immunity

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches Florida's immunity defense before any determination whether the State violated IGRA. The statutory violation remains for the district court.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies rather than overrules *Union Gas*. Article I does not itself create a cause of action or remedy, but federalism does not categorically prevent Congress from authorizing a federal action against a State when Congress speaks unmistakably, legislates within an enumerated power, and supplies the cause and remedy by statute. IGRA unmistakably authorizes this prospective action, and *Union Gas* supplies the controlling answer to the abrogation question. Stone finds no sufficient stare decisis reason to discard that precedent here, and its Article I rationale is not materially confined to the Interstate Commerce Clause.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** IGRA unmistakably authorizes a Tribe to sue a State for failure to negotiate a gaming compact in good faith. Under controlling *Union Gas*, Congress may make that prospective federal enforcement action available as an incident of its Indian Commerce authority.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress identified the regulated field, the State defendant, the actionable duty, and the remedial sequence. The statutory clarity is undisputed. *Union Gas* remains controlling Supreme Court authority at this point in the simulation, and its rationale does not support withholding the same abrogation consequence from Congress's Indian Commerce power. That conclusion establishes the lawful forum and defendant, not Florida's liability, which must be proved on remand under IGRA.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that Article I automatically creates damages, that every federal statute abrogates immunity, that Congress may act outside an enumerated power, or that a statutory violation generates an implied remedy. The holding concerns the express, prospective IGRA action and its enacted relief.

#### Issue 2: Alternative officer action

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone does not reach whether *Ex parte Young* independently authorizes an officer action. Because Florida is a proper statutory defendant, that alternative question is unnecessary to the judgment.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone supplies no advisory position on whether IGRA displaces or preserves a parallel officer route.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The separate officer-action ruling should be vacated as unnecessary once the statutory action against Florida is restored.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The Tribe can obtain adjudication through the express action Congress enacted. Deciding a parallel remedial route would not affect the present judgment and could unnecessarily alter prospective-relief doctrine.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The availability of an officer action under IGRA or a materially different statute remains open.

# Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia*
- **Citation or Docket:** 517 U.S. 186; No. 94-203; judgment below reported at 853 F. Supp. 212 (W.D. Va. 1994).
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review on direct appeal from a three-judge district court.
- **Simulated Event Date:** March 27, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** **Issue 1: Section 5 coverage.** Whether a registration fee imposed as a condition of participating as a delegate in a state-authorized party convention selecting a nominee for automatic placement on the general-election ballot is a change affecting voting that must be precleared under § 5 of the Voting Rights Act. **Issue 2: Private enforcement and statutory route.** Whether the voters may obtain judicial relief against use of the unprecleared fee and whether the Court must also decide their separate § 10 poll-tax theory.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Party's convention fee was subject to § 5 preclearance and whether appellants possessed an enforceable statutory claim.
- **Legal Field:** **Issue 1:** Elections and voting, Voting Rights Act § 5, party nomination. **Issue 2:** Federal statutory enforcement, private action, and remedial sequencing.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The three-judge United States District Court for the Western District of Virginia dismissed the § 5 and § 10 claims, concluding that the convention fee was outside § 5 and that the asserted poll-tax provision was enforceable only by the Attorney General.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Appellants request reversal, an injunction against enforcement of the unprecleared fee, return of the fee paid by Morse, and further proceedings on any remaining statutory relief. The Party requests affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** **Issue 1:** Virginia law allowed the two major parties to choose nominees by primary, convention, or another authorized method and placed the selected nominees automatically on the general-election ballot. The Republican Party conditioned delegate participation in its 1994 senatorial nominating convention on payment of a $35 or $45 registration fee; two otherwise qualified voters declined to pay, and Morse paid with funds advanced by supporters. **Issue 2:** Appellants filed before the convention and alleged that the unprecleared fee altered who could participate in the legally recognized nomination process; the district court dismissed without adjudicating discriminatory purpose or effect.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The excluded and fee-paying voters allege direct injury and possess standing. Because § 5 preclearance is an antecedent prohibition on enforcing a covered change, the Court may decide coverage without determining whether the fee is discriminatory. A ruling for appellants under § 5 may make it unnecessary to decide whether § 10 independently creates a private cause of action or authorizes repayment.
- **Relevant Dates:** Party convention call, December 16, 1993; convention, June 3, 1994; district-court judgment, 1994; argument, October 2, 1995; simulated decision, March 27, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 517 U.S. 186; 853 F. Supp. 212; Virginia election provisions and party rules reproduced and described in the official opinions; 28 C.F.R. § 51.7 as then in force.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 5-4.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens announced the judgment and wrote an opinion joined by Justice Ginsburg. Justice Breyer concurred in the judgment, joined by Justices O'Connor and Souter, supplying the additional votes for § 5 coverage. The dissenting Justices would have affirmed.
- **Historical Holding:** The fee imposed for participation in the state-authorized nominating convention was a change affecting voting subject to § 5 preclearance. No five-Justice rationale was necessary to establish a broader private action under § 10.
- **Historical Authority:** Majority-supported judgment and § 5 coverage proposition; fragmented reasoning beyond that common ground.
- **Historical Sources:** 517 U.S. 186.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** *Southern Christian Leadership Conference of Alabama v. Sessions* is scheduled earlier and could affect a distinct § 2 or constitutional voting issue if rendered. It does not control this § 5 coverage question unless its completed holding expressly reaches preclearance or party nomination.

#### Issue 1: Section 5 coverage

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings entry directly addresses a political party's state-authorized nominating convention or a participation fee under § 5. Surviving authority, including *Allen v. State Board of Elections*, treats § 5 as reaching changes that bear on who may participate in processes necessary to make a vote effective, while *Smith v. Allwright* and *Terry v. Adams* reject evasion through formally private control of an official nomination stage.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** None identified in Standards and Tests for this precise § 5 question.
- **Other Governing Law:** Voting Rights Act §§ 5 and 14(c)(1), Virginia's election code, and the Attorney General's then-operative regulation covering party changes made under authority granted by a covered jurisdiction.
- **Other Authority Used:** *Whitley v. Williams* and the statutory structure linking nomination processes to voting; the Party's automatic-ballot-access role under Virginia law.
- **Open Point:** Whether the Party's convention fee is sufficiently connected to a state-authorized nomination process to constitute a covered voting change rather than an internal associational rule.

#### Issue 2: Private enforcement and statutory route

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings entry changes *Allen*'s recognition that private voters may seek an injunction against enforcement of an unprecleared change. No controlling simulated decision establishes a private action under § 10.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** None identified.
- **Other Governing Law:** Voting Rights Act §§ 5 and 10; ordinary rules of statutory cause of action and equitable relief; constitutional avoidance and decisional-necessity principles.
- **Other Authority Used:** *Allen* for private § 5 enforcement.
- **Open Point:** The availability and scope of a private § 10 action and any restitutionary relief for a fee already paid.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and enjoin use of the fee pending preclearance.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court shall treat the convention fee as a covered change that may not be enforced unless and until precleared. It shall enter appropriate prospective relief and address any remaining claim for repayment or other relief under a lawful remedial source. # Cooper v. Oklahoma

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Cooper v. Oklahoma*
- **Citation or Docket:** 517 U.S. 348; No. 95-5207; judgment below reported at 889 P.2d 293 (Okla. Crim. App. 1995).
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review.
- **Simulated Event Date:** April 16, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether due process permits a State to try a criminal defendant unless he proves incompetence by clear and convincing evidence.
- **Question Granted:** Whether Oklahoma's heightened burden of proof adequately protects the constitutional prohibition against trying an incompetent defendant.
- **Legal Field:** Criminal procedure, competency to stand trial, due process, and burden of proof.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Oklahoma Court of Criminal Appeals affirmed Cooper's murder conviction and death sentence after trial courts applied a presumption of competence and required clear and convincing proof of incompetence.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Cooper requests reversal, vacation of the conviction and sentence, and a new trial only after a lawful competency determination. Oklahoma requests affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Cooper displayed bizarre behavior before and during trial, and experts gave conflicting competency opinions. The trial judge expressed uncertainty but ruled Cooper competent because the evidence did not satisfy Oklahoma's clear-and-convincing standard. Cooper was convicted of first-degree murder and sentenced to death.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The burden-of-proof challenge is preserved and outcome material. The Supreme Court need not determine Cooper's historical competence as an appellate fact. The remedy must account for whether a reliable retrospective determination is possible and for the rule that no retrial may occur while Cooper is incompetent.

- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Unanimous Opinion of the Court by Justice Stevens.
- **Historical Holding:** Oklahoma's clear-and-convincing burden violated due process because it allowed trial of a defendant who was more likely than not incompetent.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Burden for proving incompetence

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings entry establishes the permissible competency burden. Surviving *Dusky v. United States*, *Pate v. Robinson*, and *Drope v. Missouri* prohibit trial unless the defendant has present capacity to understand the proceedings and assist counsel. *Medina v. California* permits a State to presume competence and place a preponderance burden on the defendant.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** None identified for competency. The general record discipline in the simulated jurisprudence forbids an appellate court from supplying the missing competence finding.
- **Other Governing Law:** Due Process Clause; Oklahoma's clear-and-convincing rule; historical practice and the allocation analysis recognized in *Medina*.
- **Other Authority Used:** The conflicting expert evidence, trial judge's stated uncertainty, and the near-uniform use of a lower burden in other jurisdictions.
- **Open Point:** Whether a burden higher than preponderance permits an unacceptable risk of trying a person who is probably incompetent.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The Oklahoma Court of Criminal Appeals shall determine the lawful consequence of the unconstitutional burden. If state law permits and a reliable retrospective inquiry remains possible, the state courts may determine Cooper's competence at the original trial under a constitutionally valid burden. If a reliable retrospective determination is not possible, or if the resulting determination does not sustain the original trial, the conviction and death sentence must be set aside and Oklahoma may retry Cooper only after he is competent under a constitutionally valid procedure. Stone does not himself find historical competence or incompetence.

#### Issue 1: Burden for proving incompetence

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the due process question but does not decide Cooper's competence at the original trial on the existing appellate record.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A State may presume competence and require the defendant to prove incompetence by a preponderance. It may not require clear and convincing proof because that rule affirmatively permits trial when the evidence shows the defendant is more likely than not unable to understand the proceedings or assist counsel. The State's interest in efficient adjudication does not justify that risk to the integrity of the trial itself.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Due process forbids a clear-and-convincing burden for incompetence because it allows criminal trial and punishment of a defendant who has shown that incompetence is more probable than competence; a preponderance allocation remains permissible.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Competence is the condition that makes counsel, confrontation, waiver, testimony, and defense choice meaningful. Error cannot be treated as an ordinary evidentiary mistake because an incompetent person cannot reliably participate in correcting it. Historical practice and the dominant modern rule support preponderance, and Oklahoma identifies no operational necessity for the additional burden. The trial judge's uncertainty shows that the heightened standard may have controlled the result, so the conviction cannot be affirmed by assuming competence.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not require the prosecution to prove competence beyond a reasonable doubt, prohibit a rebuttable presumption of competence, or resolve the standards for civil commitment.

#### Issue 1: Allocation of patent-claim construction

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings entry addresses patent-claim construction. Surviving Seventh Amendment doctrine preserves the jury function existing at common law and allocates a modern issue by historical analogy, precedent, and the nature of the task when no exact eighteenth-century counterpart exists. Patent precedent has long treated construction of the patent instrument as a legal question for the court while leaving infringement under the construed claim to the factfinder.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** None identified; ordinary patent and Seventh Amendment law governs without a Stone-specific overlay.
- **Other Governing Law:** Seventh Amendment; Patent Act, including 35 U.S.C. § 112; *Winans v. Denmead*, *Bischoff v. Wethered*, and related claim-construction precedent.
- **Other Authority Used:** Historical practice concerning patent specifications, the interpretive character of claim construction, and the statutory need for consistent public notice of a patent's scope.
- **Open Point:** Whether technical evidentiary disputes embedded in construing a claim require jury resolution even though the ultimate meaning of the patent instrument is legal.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Federal Circuit.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** No separate remedial instruction is required. The judgment for Westview remains in force.

#### Issue 1: Allocation of patent-claim construction

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved Seventh Amendment allocation question. He does not reweigh the infringement evidence under a different claim construction.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the received judge-jury allocation. The court construes the patent claim as a legal instrument, including technical terms whose meaning may be informed by expert evidence; the jury decides whether the accused product infringes the claim as construed and resolves genuinely factual matters assigned to it. Expert testimony aids interpretation but does not transfer the ultimate construction to the jury.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Construction of a patent claim, including the meaning of terms of art within the claim, is a question for the court; the Seventh Amendment preserves the jury's role in deciding infringement and other factual issues under the court's construction.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** No clear founding-era practice assigns modern claim construction to juries. The closest historical practice placed construction of patent specifications with judges, and precedent treats the patent as an instrument whose legal scope must be stated consistently. Judicial construction also serves the public-notice function of claims and avoids different meanings for the same patent across juries. The district court therefore could construe “inventory” independently and enter judgment when Westview's system fell outside that construction.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that every dispute involving expert testimony is legal, remove infringement from the jury, or decide the standard of appellate review for every subsidiary factual determination not necessary here.
