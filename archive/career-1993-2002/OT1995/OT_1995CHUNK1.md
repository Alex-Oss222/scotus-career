# Wood v. Bartholomew

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Wood v. Bartholomew*
- **Citation or Docket:** 516 U.S. 1; 34 F.3d 870 (9th Cir. 1994).
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Certiorari granted; merits disposition by summary reversal.
- **Simulated Event Date:** October 10, 1995.
- **Question Presented:** Whether the prosecution's failure to disclose inadmissible polygraph results of two witnesses was material under *Brady v. Maryland* because disclosure might have caused the defense to investigate further.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Ninth Circuit misapplied *Brady* by setting aside the conviction on a speculative chain from undisclosed, inadmissible polygraph results to possible additional impeachment evidence.
- **Legal Field:** Criminal procedure, disclosure of favorable evidence and federal habeas review.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit reversed denial of habeas relief and ordered relief from Bartholomew's Washington murder conviction.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The State requests reversal of the Ninth Circuit and reinstatement of the denial of habeas relief.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Bartholomew admitted the laundromat robbery but claimed that the victim's shooting was accidental. His brother and the brother's girlfriend testified that he had announced a plan to rob the laundromat and leave no witnesses. The prosecution did not disclose pretrial polygraph results suggesting deception in parts of their accounts. The results were inadmissible under Washington law, defense counsel did not plan to depose the brother, and counsel acknowledged that the results would not have changed the scope of cross-examination.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The Brady claim is properly before the Court on federal habeas review. The record permits resolution because the parties accept the inadmissibility of the polygraph results and the alleged downstream benefit is identified only through speculation.
- **Relevant Dates:** Ninth Circuit judgment, August 25, 1994; simulated Supreme Court disposition, October 10, 1995.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 516 U.S. 1; Ninth Circuit opinion, 34 F.3d 870; GovInfo, *United States Reports*, volume 516.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 5-4.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Per curiam disposition; Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer dissented from summary disposition.
- **Historical Holding:** Inadmissible polygraph results were not Brady material where their asserted value depended on speculation that disclosure might have produced other evidence and the record did not support a reasonable probability of a different result.
- **Historical Authority:** Per curiam judgment and opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 516 U.S. 1; official bound-volume syllabus and opinions.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** Holdings Register and Standards and Tests processed through June 29, 1995, including *Kyles v. Whitley*.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Brady materiality

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Kyles v. Whitley*, 514 U.S. 419, Question decided: cumulative Brady materiality and prosecution-team responsibility. Suppressed favorable material must be considered collectively in the context of the complete record; materiality exists when the net effect creates a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Cumulative Brady materiality and prosecution-team responsibility.** Materiality is not a sufficiency test, does not require proof by a preponderance that acquittal would have followed, and requires evaluation of all suppressed favorable information together.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Brady v. Maryland*, *United States v. Bagley*, and federal habeas principles surviving the simulation's cutoff.
- **Other Authority Used:** The Ninth Circuit opinion supplies the record concerning the suppressed examinations, the witnesses' testimony, the defense theory, and the conditional habeas remedy. Washington evidence law supplies the accepted premise that the polygraph results themselves were inadmissible.
- **Open Point:** Whether the suppressed information's cumulative relationship to the defense theory and to the only direct proof of premeditation undermines confidence in the aggravated verdict notwithstanding the information's inadmissibility.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment of the Ninth Circuit.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Leave in force the conditional writ requiring Washington, within a reasonable period, either to provide a new trial on the premeditated-murder charge or to reduce the conviction to ordinary first-degree murder.

#### Issue 1: Brady materiality

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved *Brady* merits. No jurisdictional, exhaustion, or procedural-default issue disposes of the claim.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies *Kyles* without adding an admissibility threshold or a requirement that the defense identify the final evidence disclosure would have produced. Suppressed inadmissible information may be material when it directly corroborates the defense's existing account, identifies a specific avenue for admissible investigation or impeachment, and bears on the only direct proof of a disputed element. Trial counsel's retrospective assessment is relevant contrary evidence, but it is not conclusive because counsel never possessed the information and could not conduct the investigation timely disclosure would have permitted.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When suppressed information directly corroborates the defense's existing account that a principal prosecution witness concealed his participation in the offense, identifies a specific avenue for admissible impeachment, and bears on the only direct proof of the element distinguishing the greater conviction, its cumulative effect may satisfy *Brady* even though the suppressed item itself is inadmissible. On this record, nondisclosure undermines confidence in the premeditation verdict.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Premeditation was the genuinely disputed guilt issue. Rodney and Tracy supplied the only direct evidence that Bartholomew announced an intent to leave no witnesses, while the defense maintained that Rodney participated in the robbery and minimized his own role. The undisclosed examination indicated deception when Rodney denied assisting the robbery and denied being inside the laundromat with Bartholomew. The prosecution initially denied that the examinations existed, and later inconsistencies in Rodney's accounts confirmed that the investigative route was concrete rather than imaginary. Because the suppressed information bore directly on the witnesses whose testimony distinguished aggravated murder from ordinary first-degree murder, its cumulative effect leaves the premeditation verdict unworthy of confidence.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that every undisclosed polygraph result is material, that inadmissible information automatically establishes a *Brady* violation, or that an unspecified hope of finding useful evidence warrants habeas relief. The result depends on the direct connection among the suppressed information, the key witness's possible participation, the defense theory, and the disputed element.

# Doe v. Taylor Independent School District

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Doe v. Taylor Independent School District*
- **Citation or Docket:** 15 F.3d 443 (5th Cir. 1994) (en banc); district-court docket not supplied.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Granted merits case on supervisory liability and qualified immunity.
- **Simulated Event Date:** October 16, 1995, simulation-assigned opinion-delivery date.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a public-school student has a Fourteenth Amendment right to bodily integrity against sexual abuse by a teacher, and when supervisory officials may be individually liable for deliberate indifference to a known pattern pointing toward that abuse.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the principal and superintendent are entitled to qualified immunity on the record accepted at summary judgment.
- **Legal Field:** Substantive due process, bodily integrity, supervisory liability, and qualified immunity.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The en banc Fifth Circuit held that the student's bodily-integrity right was clearly established, denied qualified immunity to the principal, and granted qualified immunity to the superintendent because the record did not show comparable notice and deliberate indifference.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The parties seek review of the Fifth Circuit's divided immunity rulings; the principal seeks judgment in his favor, while Doe seeks restoration of the claim against the superintendent.
- **Material Facts and Record:** A teacher cultivated and then sexually abused a minor student during the school year. Evidence attributed to the principal included complaints, rumors, favoritism, inappropriate contact, and earlier concerns about the teacher's relationships with girls. The record against the superintendent was materially thinner and did not show the same direct notice of facts plainly pointing to ongoing abuse. The teacher's direct liability is not the contested supervisory issue.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The appeal concerns qualified immunity at the summary-judgment stage. Factual disputes must be viewed in the nonmovant's favor; the Court may decide the legal sufficiency of the assumed facts but may not resolve credibility.
- **Relevant Dates:** Fifth Circuit en banc decision, March 3, 1994; rehearing proceedings completed in 1994; simulated event, October 16, 1995.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** Claims against the teacher, district, principal, and superintendent arose from the same action; only the individual supervisory immunity questions are controlled here.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Published en banc opinion, 15 F.3d 443; district-court record as described by the Fifth Circuit; later official citations confirming the three-part supervisory formulation.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits decision. The Fifth Circuit's en banc judgment is the historical anchor.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Divided en banc decision; the controlling Fifth Circuit opinions recognized the right and adopted a notice, deliberate-indifference, and causation framework, with separate disagreements over the administrators' liability.
- **Historical Holding:** A student has a clearly established bodily-integrity right against sexual abuse by a public-school teacher. A supervisor may be liable when the official had notice of a pattern plainly pointing to abuse, responded with deliberate indifference by failing to take obviously necessary action, and thereby caused the injury.
- **Historical Authority:** Lower-court historical anchor only.
- **Historical Sources:** 15 F.3d 443.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Bodily-integrity right and supervisory liability

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision directly governs sexual abuse of a student by a public employee or individual supervisory liability for failure to stop it.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No exact registered test. The source-separation rules preserved in the governing materials require the right, individual responsibility, causation, and remedy to remain distinct.
- **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment; *Ingraham v. Wright*, *Youngberg v. Romeo*, *DeShaney v. Winnebago County*, *City of Canton v. Harris*, and *Monell* principles surviving October 4, 1993.
- **Other Authority Used:** The Fifth Circuit's framework is persuasive insofar as it requires specific notice, deliberate indifference, and causation rather than respondeat superior.
- **Open Point:** The simulated Court has not yet stated a reusable individual-supervisor rule for a subordinate's direct bodily-integrity violation.

#### Issue 2: Qualified immunity

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Elder v. Holloway*, 510 U.S. 510, Question decided: legal authorities considered on qualified-immunity review. The court must consider the complete body of materially relevant law in deciding whether the right was clearly established.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Qualified-immunity review uses the complete body of law.** Preservation of the claim and facts remains required, but incomplete briefing does not narrow the governing law.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Harlow v. Fitzgerald*, *Anderson v. Creighton*, and *Mitchell v. Forsyth*.
- **Other Authority Used:** The assumed summary-judgment record determines what each official knew and did.
- **Open Point:** Whether the right was clearly established at the level of specificity presented by the two different administrators.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Fifth Circuit's judgment: deny qualified immunity to Principal Lankford and grant qualified immunity to Superintendent Mike Caplinger.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The claim against the principal may proceed on the assumed facts; the individual-capacity claim against Superintendent Caplinger shall be dismissed. No liability finding is made against the principal, and district or teacher claims remain governed by their own elements.

#### Issue 1: Bodily-integrity right and supervisory liability

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional question because the immunity inquiry requires identification of the asserted right. The summary-judgment posture permits only a legal ruling on assumed facts.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone recognizes a student's Fourteenth Amendment right not to be intentionally sexually assaulted by a public-school teacher acting through school authority. A supervisor is not liable by title. Individual liability requires proof that the supervisor had actual or inferable notice of facts or a pattern plainly pointing to a substantial risk that the subordinate was sexually abusing students, consciously failed to take an obviously necessary protective step within the supervisor's authority, and caused the plaintiff's injury.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The teacher's alleged sexual abuse violated Doe's bodily-integrity right, and the stated notice, deliberate-indifference, and causation rule lawfully defines the limited path to individual supervisory liability.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Sexual assault is an intentional physical invasion without pedagogical or disciplinary justification. The direct constitutional defect comes from the teacher's misuse of public authority, not from a general constitutional duty to protect against private harm. The supervisor rule tracks personal responsibility and avoids respondeat superior. A pattern of warnings that plainly points to abuse can establish actual awareness circumstantially; generalized knowledge that an employee is unconventional or unpopular cannot.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not create a general federal negligence code for schools, constitutionalize every failure to investigate, or impose liability for abuse by a private actor absent attribution.

#### Issue 2: Defendant-specific merits and qualified immunity

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone decides direct supervisory liability before qualified immunity and evaluates each official on the assumed summary-judgment facts.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The assumed record permits a finding that Principal Lankford received sufficiently specific warnings, consciously failed to take an obviously necessary protective step, and thereby allowed the abuse to continue. The record does not establish comparable notice or deliberate indifference by Superintendent Mike Caplinger. Caplinger therefore is not personally liable on the assumed facts; qualified immunity independently protects him because no broader supervisory duty was clearly established.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The assumed facts permit direct supervisory liability against Principal Lankford but not Superintendent Caplinger. Lankford is not entitled to qualified immunity, while Caplinger is entitled to judgment and, alternatively, qualified immunity.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The principal allegedly received direct complaints and observed a continuing pattern close to the school and teacher. If proved, those facts place the case beyond a bad guess in a gray area. Caplinger's more remote and generalized knowledge does not satisfy the notice and deliberate-indifference elements, and it did not give fair notice of a broader constitutional duty. The difference is defendant-specific notice, response, and causation, not official rank.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone leaves municipal liability, damages, and the truth of the disputed facts for ordinary proceedings.

# Hodge v. Jones

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Hodge v. Jones*
- **Citation or Docket:** 31 F.3d 157 (4th Cir. 1994); 812 F. Supp. 593 (D. Md. 1992).
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Granted merits case arising from an interlocutory qualified-immunity appeal.
- **Simulated Event Date:** October 23, 1995, simulation-assigned opinion-delivery date.
- **Question Presented:** Whether confidential retention of a child-abuse investigation file after allegations were classified as unsubstantiated and ruled out violated familial privacy or procedural due process, and whether the responsible officials were entitled to qualified immunity.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Fourth Circuit correctly reversed the district court's liability ruling and denial of qualified immunity.
- **Legal Field:** Due process, informational and familial privacy, state-created interests, and qualified immunity.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit vacated in part, reversed in part, and remanded with instructions after holding that the officials violated no clearly established federal right and were entitled to immunity.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The Hodges seek reversal and restoration of the district court's interlocutory liability ruling; the officials seek affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** A physician reported suspected abuse after initially diagnosing an unexplained fracture in the Hodges' infant. Specialists soon diagnosed osteomyelitis, and the investigation was closed as unsubstantiated and ruled out. Maryland retained a confidential investigation report and coded database entry, refused immediate expungement, and limited disclosure by statute. The Hodges showed no separation of the family, public disclosure, change in legal status, or present denial of employment, custody, or benefit.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The qualified-immunity denial was immediately appealable on the legal issues. Claims for prospective expungement became moot when the retention period expired; the live dispute concerns individual-capacity damages and fees.
- **Relevant Dates:** Fourth Circuit decision, July 19, 1994; simulated event, October 23, 1995.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Fourth Circuit opinion, 31 F.3d 157; district-court opinion, 812 F. Supp. 593.

- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Williams wrote for the court, joined by Judge Widener; retired Justice Powell concurred in the judgment.
- **Historical Holding:** Confidential retention of the cleared investigation record did not directly interfere with the family, alter legal status, or create actionable stigma, and any broader asserted right was not clearly established.
- **Historical Authority:** Lower-court historical anchor only.

#### Issue 1: Familial and informational privacy

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone dismisses the expired prospective claim as moot and reaches the live damages merits only as necessary to resolve qualified immunity.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The Constitution protects family integrity against direct governmental alteration or coercive interference and protects confidential personal information in settings recognized by law. - **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The legal landscape approved confidentiality systems and did not establish the proposed right. Officials were not required to predict a constitutional rule broader than the sources then supplied.

# Tuggle v. Netherland

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Tuggle v. Netherland*
- **Citation or Docket:** 516 U.S. 10; No. 95-6016; 79 F.3d 1386 and earlier Fourth Circuit proceedings.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits disposition on a granted petition; submitted on the papers and decided per curiam.
- **Simulated Event Date:** October 30, 1995.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a death sentence may stand after an *Ake* violation prevented the defense from answering the prosecution's psychiatric future-dangerousness evidence, merely because a separate aggravating circumstance remained valid.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Fourth Circuit applied the correct constitutional and habeas harmless-error analysis to the capital sentence.
- **Legal Field:** Capital sentencing, psychiatric assistance, due process, and federal habeas harmless error.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit denied federal habeas relief from Tuggle's Virginia death sentence.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Tuggle asks the Court to vacate the judgment and require a new penalty proceeding; the warden seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Virginia sought death on future-dangerousness and vileness grounds. The prosecution presented unrebutted psychiatric testimony supporting future dangerousness, while the State denied Tuggle the independent psychiatric assistance required by *Ake v. Oklahoma*. On remand from an earlier Supreme Court order, the Virginia Supreme Court invalidated the future-dangerousness aggravator but left the death sentence in place because the vileness aggravator remained.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The federal habeas vehicle is proper, and the psychiatric-assistance claim was preserved through the prior remand. The conviction is not before the Court. The Court must distinguish invalidation of an aggravating label from constitutional error that altered the evidence and mitigation available to the sentencer.
- **Relevant Dates:** Earlier Supreme Court remand, 1985; simulated disposition, October 30, 1995.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and per curiam opinion, 516 U.S. 10; cited Virginia and Fourth Circuit proceedings.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Unanimous per curiam opinion.
- **Historical Holding:** *Zant v. Stephens* did not permit the courts below to disregard the effect of the *Ake* error merely because one valid aggravating circumstance remained. The psychiatric error could have affected the jury's selection between life and death.
- **Historical Authority:** Per curiam opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 516 U.S. 10.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Prejudice from denial of psychiatric assistance at capital sentencing

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *O'Neal v. McAninch* applies the federal habeas harmless-error standard and requires relief when the reviewing court remains in grave doubt whether constitutional error had a substantial and injurious effect. No simulated holding treats one surviving aggravator as an automatic cure for an independent sentencing error.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Grave doubt under the federal habeas harmless-error standard.** The reviewing court must examine the complete sentencing record and the actual function of the denied safeguard.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Ake v. Oklahoma*, *Brecht v. Abrahamson*, *Zant v. Stephens*, *Satterwhite v. Texas*, and capital-sentencing reliability principles.
- **Other Authority Used:** The unrebutted future-dangerousness testimony, foreclosed expert rebuttal, lost mitigation development, counsel preparation, and the surviving vileness aggravator.
- **Open Point:** The lower courts did not perform the complete *Brecht* inquiry into how the denied assistance affected the jury's selection of death.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand for *Brecht* review, with a conditional writ requiring a new penalty proceeding if the error had a substantial and injurious effect.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Tuggle's conviction remains undisturbed. The lower court shall evaluate the complete penalty record, including the expert assistance foreclosed in rebuttal, mitigation, and preparation. If the error had a substantial and injurious effect, or leaves the court in grave doubt under *O'Neal*, it shall grant a conditional writ requiring a new penalty proceeding within a reasonable period.

#### Issue 1: Prejudice from denial of psychiatric assistance at capital sentencing

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved habeas claim concerning the death sentence. He does not decide the prejudice question in the first instance on an undeveloped lower-court analysis.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The *Ake* violation is conceded. *Brecht* supplies the prejudice standard. The inquiry must account for the denied expert's possible role in testing the State's future-dangerousness evidence, developing mitigation, and assisting counsel, considered with the complete penalty record. The continued validity of another aggravator establishes eligibility only and does not answer whether the constitutional error substantially influenced selection.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A conceded denial of *Ake* assistance in a capital sentencing is reviewed under *Brecht* on the complete penalty record, including the expert assistance foreclosed in rebuttal, mitigation, and preparation.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The prosecution used psychiatric future-dangerousness testimony to support death, and the defense lacked the independent assistance needed to test that evidence and develop its own penalty case. Later removal of the formal future-dangerousness aggravator did not erase the testimony from the jury's deliberations or recreate the defense never presented. A valid vileness aggravator therefore cannot substitute for the required prejudice inquiry.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not presume what a defense expert would have said, declare every *Ake* error structural, or disturb the conviction.

# Citizens Bank of Maryland v. Strumpf

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Citizens Bank of Maryland v. Strumpf*
- **Citation or Docket:** 516 U.S. 16; No. 94-1340; 37 F.3d 155 (4th Cir. 1994).
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review.
- **Simulated Event Date:** October 31, 1995.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a bank violates the Bankruptcy Code's automatic stay by placing a temporary administrative hold on funds subject to a claimed setoff while promptly seeking relief from the stay.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the temporary hold constituted a prohibited setoff or another stayed act under 11 U.S.C. § 362(a).
- **Legal Field:** Bankruptcy, automatic stay, bank deposits, and setoff.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit reversed the district court and sustained a contempt judgment against Citizens Bank for violating the automatic stay.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The bank seeks reversal of the contempt judgment; Strumpf seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Strumpf owed the bank on a defaulted loan and also maintained a deposit account at the bank. After he filed bankruptcy, the bank temporarily refused withdrawals that would reduce the account below the amount of its asserted setoff and promptly moved for relief from the automatic stay. It did not make a permanent accounting entry extinguishing mutual debts before seeking judicial authorization.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The statutory issues were preserved. The Court reviews the legal characterization of the undisputed hold. The validity and amount of the bank's ultimate setoff claim remain distinct from whether the temporary hold itself violated the stay.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, October 3, 1995; decision, October 31, 1995.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinion, 516 U.S. 16; Fourth Circuit judgment, 37 F.3d 155.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Opinion of the Court by Justice Scalia for a unanimous Court.
- **Historical Holding:** A temporary refusal to pay while the bank sought stay relief was not a completed setoff and did not violate §§ 362(a)(3), (6), or (7).
- **Historical Authority:** Unanimous Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 516 U.S. 16.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Temporary hold and the automatic stay

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision directly construes §§ 362(a)(3), 362(a)(6), 362(a)(7), 542(b), or 553 in this setting.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No exact registered bankruptcy test. Ordinary statutory interpretation and the separation of substantive setoff rights from stay procedure control.
- **Other Governing Law:** 11 U.S.C. §§ 362, 542(b), and 553; the traditional legal meaning of setoff.
- **Other Authority Used:** *United States v. Whiting Pools, Inc.* for turnover structure; established setoff law requiring a decision and act that actually settle mutual accounts.
- **Open Point:** The Code does not expressly name a short administrative hold used solely to preserve the status quo while stay relief is sought.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit's judgment.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The contempt judgment based on the administrative hold shall be vacated. Any unresolved dispute over the bank's entitlement to setoff or other relief shall proceed in the bankruptcy court under the Code.

#### Issue 1: Temporary hold and the automatic stay

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved statutory merits.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A setoff occurs when the creditor decides to settle mutual obligations and takes action giving that settlement legal and accounting effect. A temporary, amount-limited refusal to pay that preserves the account while the creditor promptly asks the bankruptcy court for permission is not itself the completed setoff prohibited by § 362(a)(7).
