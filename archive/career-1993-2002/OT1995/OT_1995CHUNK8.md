# Leavitt v. Jane L.

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Leavitt v. Jane L.*
- **Citation or Docket:** 518 U.S. 137; No. 95-1242.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Granted merits matter concerning severability of Utah abortion provisions.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 17, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether Utah’s post-20-week abortion restrictions are severable from invalid pre-20-week restrictions in light of an express statutory severability command.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Tenth Circuit could disregard Utah Code § 76-7-317 and invalidate § 76-7-302(3) solely because § 302(2) was unconstitutional.
- **Legal Field:** Severability, abortion regulation, statutory interpretation, and federal-state remedial allocation.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Tenth Circuit held the post-20-week provision inseverable and invalidated it without independently deciding its federal constitutionality.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Utah seeks reversal of the inseverability ruling; respondents seek affirmance or consideration of their constitutional challenge to the surviving provision.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Section 302(2) restricted abortions at or before 20 weeks to five circumstances; § 302(3) imposed a distinct set of restrictions after 20 weeks. Section 317 expressly stated that the legislature would have enacted every provision regardless of invalidity of any other provision. The district court invalidated § 302(2) but upheld and severed § 302(3); the Tenth Circuit treated the two as inseparable based on its prediction of legislative preference.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Severability is the only question necessary to correct the judgment. The merits of § 302(3), its emergency route, gestational operation, and any facial record remain for the lower courts under current simulated law. The Court should not adjudicate those questions without the lower-court analysis the Tenth Circuit displaced.
- **Relevant Dates:** Tenth Circuit judgment, 1995; decision, June 17, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official per curiam opinion, 518 U.S. 137; Utah Code §§ 76-7-302 and 76-7-317.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Certiorari granted, reversed, and remanded per curiam.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Per curiam opinion; four Justices dissented from the summary disposition.
- **Historical Holding:** The express severability clause foreclosed the Tenth Circuit’s speculation that the legislature would not have enacted § 302(3) independently.
- **Historical Authority:** Per curiam Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 518 U.S. 137.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, including simulated *Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer*.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Revalidation must account for any earlier abortion decision, but the express severability command remains the threshold issue.

#### Issue 1: Severability of the post-20-week provision

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register case construes Utah’s clause. Ordinary severability gives controlling weight to enacted state-law direction unless the remainder cannot function or the severance would create a scheme the legislature did not enact.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Facial review of previability abortion regulations**, **Operational and cumulative undue-burden review**, and **Usable medical-emergency routes in penal abortion regulation** govern the surviving provision on remand, not the severability question itself.
- **Other Governing Law:** Utah Code § 76-7-317; state severability principles; federal remedial restraint.
- **Other Authority Used:** Separate gestational categories and independent operation of § 302(3).
- **Open Point:** The constitutionality of § 302(3) under current simulated abortion law remains open.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Reinstate § 302(3) as severed for purposes of further adjudication. The Tenth Circuit shall decide preserved constitutional challenges under the law effective at the time of its decision. No enforcement may proceed contrary to any existing injunction until lawful review is complete.

#### Issue 1: Severability of the post-20-week provision

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone decides severability and does not adjudicate the surviving provision’s constitutional merits.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A federal court must respect a State’s express severability rule when the surviving provision can operate independently and severance does not rewrite its text. Judicial speculation about the legislature’s preferred overall bargain cannot override an enacted statement that each provision is independently desired.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Sections 302(2) and 302(3) are severable because § 317 expressly directs independent operation and the post-20-week rule can function without the invalid earlier-term rule.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The two subsections govern distinct gestational periods and contain complete operative commands. The Tenth Circuit’s prediction directly contradicts the legislature’s enacted instruction. Respect for state law requires enforcement of that instruction, while federal constitutional review remains fully available.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone expresses no view on whether § 302(3) contains a usable medical-emergency route, imposes an undue burden, or may be enforced in any particular application.

# SSC Corp. v. Town of Smithtown; Town of Smithtown v. SSC Corp.

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *SSC Corp. v. Town of Smithtown; Town of Smithtown v. SSC Corp.*
- **Citation or Docket:** 66 F.3d 502 (2d Cir. 1995), consolidated cross-appeals.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Granted merits review of a dormant Commerce Clause judgment.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 18, 1996, simulation-assigned opinion-delivery date.
- **Question Presented:** Whether Smithtown’s criminally enforceable flow-control ordinance and its competitively bid residential collection contracts unlawfully reserve waste disposal for a designated local incinerator.
- **Question Granted:** When does a municipality act as a market participant rather than a market regulator under the dormant Commerce Clause, and may it contractually require waste collected on its behalf to be delivered to a designated facility while separately imposing a compulsory flow-control ordinance?
- **Legal Field:** Dormant Commerce Clause, waste flow control, market participation, municipal contracting, and local financing.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit invalidated the ordinance but upheld the residential improvement contracts, affirming in part and reversing in part the district court.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Smithtown seeks validation of the ordinance; SSC challenges the contract requirement; each side seeks a favorable judgment on its component.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Smithtown and Huntington financed access to a privately owned incinerator and relied on tipping fees. Smithtown’s ordinance required all residential and commercial waste generated in town to go to the designated facility and imposed criminal penalties for violation. Separately, Smithtown created residential collection districts, solicited bids, paid haulers under municipal service contracts, and required its contractors to deliver the municipality-purchased waste service to the same facility.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The ordinance and contract are legally separable. Government regulation backed by criminal law differs from the terms on which government purchases collection and disposal services. Ownership and control of the incinerator are relevant but not dispositive.
- **Relevant Dates:** Second Circuit decision, September 27, 1995; simulated decision, June 18, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** Cross-appeals concerning the same waste program.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** 66 F.3d 502; Smithtown Code and contracts described there; *C & A Carbone, Inc. v. Town of Clarkstown*.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits decision; certiorari was denied. The Second Circuit’s mixed judgment is the historical anchor.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Published panel opinion.
- **Historical Holding:** The ordinance violated *Carbone*, while the improvement contract was municipal market participation.
- **Historical Authority:** Lower-court historical anchor.
- **Historical Sources:** 66 F.3d 502; denial of certiorari, 516 U.S. 1112.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, including *Oregon Waste Systems*, *Carbone*, and *West Lynn Creamery*.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Criminally enforceable flow-control ordinance

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Carbone* holds that a law discriminates when its practical operation directs commerce to a favored local private enterprise, even if some local firms are also excluded. The government must show a legitimate objective unavailable through reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Operational discrimination favoring a local private enterprise**.
- **Other Governing Law:** Dormant Commerce Clause; municipal police power.
- **Other Authority Used:** Mandatory routing, criminal penalties, private facility ownership, and above-market tipping fees.
- **Open Point:** A genuinely public facility under materially different governmental-market circumstances remains reserved by *Carbone*.

#### Issue 2: Residential collection and disposal contracts

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Carbone* does not condemn a government’s own market purchases. Market-participant authority permits government to select suppliers and contractual performance for the service it buys, but not to regulate downstream private commerce beyond the transaction.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No separate registered market-participant test. Source separation between coercive law and contractual privity governs.
- **Other Governing Law:** *White v. Massachusetts Council of Construction Employers*; *South-Central Timber Development v. Wunnicke*.
- **Other Authority Used:** Competitive bidding, municipal payment, defined residential service, and contractual privity.
- **Open Point:** Contract provisions reaching commercial waste or independent transactions outside the purchased service would require separate review.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Second Circuit in both cross-appeals.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The ordinance may not be enforced. The residential improvement contracts remain valid to the extent they govern waste collected and disposed of as the service Smithtown purchased.

#### Issue 1: Criminally enforceable flow-control ordinance

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the Commerce Clause merits.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A municipality regulates rather than participates when it uses criminal law to command every private hauler and waste generator to patronize a designated facility. Financial exposure and local planning do not convert coercion into a market transaction.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Smithtown’s ordinance discriminates in operation by reserving the waste-disposal market for a designated local private incinerator and fails because nondiscriminatory financing and disposal alternatives are available.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The ordinance imposes fines and imprisonment on private routing choices. Its object is to secure a waste stream and tipping revenue for the favored facility. Uniform user fees, general taxation, municipal purchasing, or competitive disposal contracts could pursue the local objectives without excluding interstate processors by law.

#### Issue 2: Residential collection and disposal contracts

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone separately reaches the contract component.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** When a municipality purchases residential collection and disposal as an integrated service, it may specify the facility used to perform that service within the contractual transaction. The exception ends where the government uses the contract to control independent commerce beyond the purchased service.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Smithtown acts as a market participant when it competitively purchases residential collection and disposal and requires its contractors to use the designated facility for that municipally purchased service.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The residents receive a municipal service for which Smithtown contracts and pays. The routing term defines what the contractors must deliver to the buyer. It does not criminally command noncontracting commercial haulers or private generators. The distinction follows the source of the obligation, not a preference for municipal finance.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not approve contractual control over waste outside the improvement districts, coercive conditions imposed on unrelated private transactions, or sham contracting used to reproduce the ordinance.

# Gray v. Netherland

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Gray v. Netherland*
- **Citation or Docket:** 518 U.S. 152; No. 95-6510.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary federal habeas review limited to penalty-phase notice and Brady issues.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 20, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether Gray may obtain federal habeas relief based on late disclosure and alleged prosecutorial misrepresentation concerning evidence of other murders introduced at his capital sentencing.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the notice claim is barred as a new rule, whether the Brady claim is procedurally defaulted, and whether a distinct misrepresentation claim was preserved and exhausted.
- **Legal Field:** Habeas corpus, capital sentencing, due process, prosecutorial disclosure, exhaustion, default, and retroactivity.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit reversed a district-court grant of habeas relief on the notice theory and rejected the Brady claim.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Gray seeks a new penalty proceeding; the warden seeks affirmance of the denial of relief.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Before trial, Virginia stated that it would use Gray’s admissions to inmates concerning the Sorrell murders if the case reached sentencing. On the day of conviction, the prosecution disclosed that it would also present photographs, an investigating detective, and a medical examiner. Defense counsel objected to surprise and sought exclusion but did not request a continuance. In habeas, Gray separately alleged inadequate notice, suppression of favorable Sorrell evidence, and prosecutorial misrepresentation about the evidence to be used.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The three theories are distinct. Exhaustion and procedural default must be resolved claim by claim. A new-rule bar cannot substitute for deciding whether an older, separately preserved misrepresentation principle governs. Cause and prejudice, if asserted, require record support.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, April 15, 1996; decision, June 20, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 518 U.S. 152; state and federal habeas records summarized there.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded, 5-4.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Chief Justice Rehnquist wrote for the Court; Justice Ginsburg dissented with three Justices.
- **Historical Holding:** The Brady claim was defaulted; the misrepresentation claim required preservation analysis; the notice claim was separately characterized.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 518 U.S. 152.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, including *Kyles v. Whitley* and current habeas gateway rules.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: New advance-notice theory

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling authority at the time Gray's conviction became final established a general constitutional right to item-by-item advance notice of all nonstatutory capital-sentencing evidence.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No exact registered standard. *Teague v. Lane* bars federal habeas relief resting on a new rule unless a recognized exception applies.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Gardner v. Florida*, capital due process, and habeas nonretroactivity.
- **Other Authority Used:** The notice theory as presented, the finality date, and the absence of controlling authority establishing the requested general rule.
- **Open Point:** None for the notice-only theory.

#### Issue 2: Suppression and affirmative prosecutorial deception

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Kyles v. Whitley* governs cumulative materiality of suppressed favorable evidence. Existing due process prohibits knowing prosecutorial deception. Procedural default, cause, prejudice, and exhaustion remain claim-specific thresholds.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Cumulative Brady materiality and prosecution-team responsibility** and **Actual innocence as a gateway to defaulted trial claims** when properly invoked.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Brady v. Maryland*, *Mooney v. Holohan*, *Raley v. Ohio*, *Wainwright v. Sykes*, and *Murray v. Carrier*.
- **Other Authority Used:** The prosecution's statements about the Sorrell evidence, the timing and content of disclosure, Gray's state and federal pleadings, and any connection between alleged deception and failure to present the claim earlier.
- **Open Point:** Whether Gray fairly preserved distinct suppression and affirmative-deception claims, and whether any default is excused, remain unresolved.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm as to the notice-only theory; vacate and remand as to the suppression and deception claims.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The notice-only theory may not support habeas relief. The Fourth Circuit shall classify the separately pleaded suppression and affirmative-deception claims, determine preservation, exhaustion, default, cause, prejudice, and materiality under their own doctrines, and order a new penalty proceeding only if a surviving claim satisfies its governing standard.

#### Issue 1: New advance-notice theory

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves the habeas nonretroactivity threshold and does not supply an advisory merits rule.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A general right to advance notice of every item of nonstatutory penalty evidence was not dictated by precedent when Gray's judgment became final. Federal habeas therefore may not create that rule on this record.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The notice-only theory is unavailable on federal habeas under *Teague*.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Existing cases protected against secret evidence, suppression, and deception, but they did not establish the broad item-by-item notice rule Gray seeks. The novelty bar applies to that new proposition and not automatically to separate old-law claims arising from some of the same events.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone creates no universal witness-list or criminal-discovery right and expresses no view on a prospectively enacted notice requirement.

#### Issue 2: Suppression and affirmative prosecutorial deception

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone does not decide the underlying merits until the lower court determines which distinct claims were fairly presented and whether any default is excused.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A suppression claim and a claim that the prosecution affirmatively misrepresented the evidence it intended to present are not converted into a new notice theory merely because they concern late-disclosed penalty evidence. Each must be adjudicated under its existing source, elements, and habeas thresholds. State concealment or deception may establish cause only if it actually prevented timely presentation.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Separately preserved suppression and affirmative-deception claims must be classified and adjudicated under their own existing doctrines rather than rejected as a new advance-notice rule.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The novelty bar attaches to the new notice proposition, not to *Brady* or *Mooney* claims that require different proof and legal consequences. The record does not permit the Court to decide preservation, cause, cumulative materiality, or the truth and effect of the alleged representation in the first instance.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone orders a new penalty proceeding only if a surviving claim satisfies its governing prejudice or materiality standard.

# Lane v. Peña

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Lane v. Peña*
- **Citation or Docket:** 518 U.S. 187; No. 95-365.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review of a sovereign-immunity ruling after a statutory discrimination finding.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 20, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether Congress unequivocally waived the Federal Government’s sovereign immunity from monetary damages for violations of § 504(a) of the Rehabilitation Act in federally conducted programs.
- **Question Granted:** Whether Lane may recover compensatory damages from the federal defendants after obtaining reinstatement and a finding of unlawful disability discrimination.
- **Legal Field:** Sovereign immunity, disability discrimination, statutory remedies, and federal programs.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The D.C. Circuit denied monetary relief while leaving equitable relief and the statutory violation otherwise undisturbed.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Lane seeks damages; the federal defendants seek affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Lane entered the United States Merchant Marine Academy and later was diagnosed with insulin-dependent diabetes. The Academy separated him as medically disqualified. The district court found a § 504 violation and ordered reinstatement. The remaining dispute concerns whether the United States consented to compensatory damages.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Right, violation, defendant, immunity, cause of action, and remedy are separate. The Court assumes the statutory violation for purposes of the damages question. A waiver must be expressed unambiguously in enacted text.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, April 15, 1996; decision, June 20, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 518 U.S. 187; 29 U.S.C. § 794; 42 U.S.C. § 2000d-7.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 7-2.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O’Connor wrote for seven Justices; Justice Stevens dissented, joined by Justice Breyer.
- **Historical Holding:** Congress had not unequivocally waived federal sovereign immunity for compensatory damages under § 504(a).
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 518 U.S. 187.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, including *FDIC v. Meyer* and current waiver rules.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Federal waiver of monetary liability

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *FDIC v. Meyer* separates sovereign-immunity waiver from cause of action and remedy. A broad sue-and-be-sued clause may waive immunity but does not create substantive damages; conversely, a statutory right does not waive the United States’ immunity without clear text.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Federal constitutional claims under the FTCA and agency waivers** and **Constitutional damages actions against federal agencies** by source-separation analogy.
- **Other Governing Law:** 29 U.S.C. § 794; 42 U.S.C. § 2000d-7; clear-statement rule for waivers.
- **Other Authority Used:** Text distinguishing States, federal agencies, recipients, and available remedies.
- **Open Point:** Prospective and other nonmonetary relief remain available under their own sources.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The denial of compensatory damages remains in force. Lane’s reinstatement and any otherwise authorized equitable relief are unaffected.

#### Issue 1: Federal waiver of monetary liability

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches immunity before damages calculation and assumes the statutory violation.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Waiver of federal sovereign immunity from money damages must be unequivocal. Section 2000d-7 clearly addresses state immunity and equalizes specified remedies in suits against States; it does not unambiguously subject federal agencies to compensatory damages for federally conducted programs. Ambiguous remedial cross-references cannot supply consent to money judgments.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress did not unequivocally waive the Federal Government’s sovereign immunity from compensatory damages for a § 504(a) violation in a federally conducted program.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The discrimination finding does not answer whether the Treasury may be charged. Congress knew how to identify sovereign defendants and did so expressly for States. The competing plausible readings of the remedial cross-reference defeat the required clarity. Stone therefore preserves the proven right and equitable remedy while denying relief the waiver does not authorize.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not diminish § 504’s substantive command, foreclose prospective relief, or decide damages against a nonfederal recipient under an authorized cause of action.

# Brown v. Pro Football, Inc.

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Brown v. Pro Football, Inc.*
- **Citation or Docket:** 518 U.S. 231; No. 95-388.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review at the intersection of antitrust and labor law.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 20, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether the nonstatutory labor exemption shields a multiemployer bargaining unit’s postimpasse implementation of a proposed employment term from federal antitrust liability.
- **Question Granted:** Whether professional football clubs may jointly implement the developmental-squad salary term after bargaining impasse without treble-damages antitrust exposure.
- **Legal Field:** Antitrust, labor law, collective bargaining, multiemployer units, and nonstatutory exemption.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The D.C. Circuit held the challenged conduct exempt and reversed a large antitrust judgment for the players.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The players seek reversal and restoration of antitrust liability; the clubs seek affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** NFL clubs bargained through a lawful multiemployer unit with the players’ union over developmental-squad terms. After impasse, the clubs jointly implemented the last proposed weekly salary. Developmental players sued under the Sherman Act and won treble damages, arguing that the labor exemption ended at impasse and did not cover a nonconsensual employer agreement.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The existence of a lawful collective-bargaining relationship, mandatory subject, and impasse are established. The question concerns the boundary between labor regulation and antitrust, not whether the implementation complied with every NLRA duty.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, March 27, 1996; decision, June 20, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** Claims by multiple developmental players arise from the same employer conduct.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 518 U.S. 231; Sherman Act; NLRA bargaining rules.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 8-1.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Breyer wrote for eight Justices; Justice Stevens dissented.
- **Historical Holding:** The nonstatutory labor exemption covered the postimpasse implementation because it grew directly from lawful collective bargaining over a mandatory subject.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 518 U.S. 231.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, including current NLRA and antitrust coordination principles.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material beyond revalidation.

#### Issue 1: Postimpasse labor exemption

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register case fixes the exemption’s termination. Surviving labor and antitrust precedent recognizes an implied exemption necessary to permit collective bargaining over mandatory subjects without antitrust courts displacing the labor-law process.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No exact registered test. Existing labor-law source allocation gives the NLRB primary responsibility for bargaining conduct and distinguishes federal labor rights from independent antitrust rules.
- **Other Governing Law:** Sherman Act; NLRA; *Jewel Tea*, *Pennington*, and *Connell Construction*.
- **Other Authority Used:** Multiemployer bargaining, mandatory subject, timing, parties, and direct relation to the negotiation.
- **Open Point:** Conduct remote in time or circumstance from bargaining remains subject to antitrust law.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The antitrust judgment remains vacated. Any preserved labor-law challenge proceeds through the NLRA’s own channels.

#### Issue 1: Postimpasse labor exemption

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the exemption.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The nonstatutory exemption extends to coordinated employer conduct that concerns a mandatory bargaining subject, involves the bargaining parties, and grows directly from the lawful operation of the collective-bargaining process, including immediate postimpasse implementation permitted by labor law. Impasse is not an automatic switch to antitrust because labor law continues to regulate the relationship after impasse.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The clubs’ joint implementation of the developmental-squad salary is exempt because it followed multiemployer bargaining over a mandatory term and remained directly within the labor relationship.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Applying antitrust at the moment of impasse would make conduct labor law permits a treble-damages offense and would force antitrust judges to supervise bargaining tactics and timing. The same parties negotiated the same term in the same unit, and the challenged action immediately implemented the proposal. Labor law, not antitrust, owns the legality of that bargaining conduct.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The exemption does not cover employer combinations involving nonlabor markets, nonparties, terms unrelated to bargaining, or conduct sufficiently remote from the process that antitrust review no longer interferes with labor policy.

# Doe v. Kirchner

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Doe v. Kirchner*
- **Citation or Docket:** Adoption judgment reported at *In re Petition of Doe*, 159 Ill. 2d 347, 638 N.E.2d 181 (1994); related U.S. Supreme Court docket No. 94-1644.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Supplied merits review of the adoptive parents’ federal constitutional claims.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 21, 1996, simulation-assigned opinion-delivery date.
- **Question Presented:** Whether John and Jane Doe possess a federal liberty interest that permits them to retain custody against a fit biological father after the adoption was invalidated because his parental rights were never lawfully terminated.
- **Question Granted:** Do adoptive parents have a constitutionally protected liberty interest in an established parent-child relationship that requires a hearing before the State orders the child transferred to a biological parent after the adoption has been invalidated?
- **Legal Field:** Family integrity, adoption, parental rights, procedural due process, and child custody.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Illinois Supreme Court invalidated the adoption and recognized Otakar Kirchner’s right to custody after concluding that he had been deceived about the child’s birth and promptly asserted paternity when he learned the truth.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The Does seek reversal and continued custody or a new custody proceeding; Kirchner seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Richard was placed with the Does four days after birth with his mother’s consent. Kirchner, the unmarried biological father, was falsely told that the child had died and learned of the adoption when Richard was 57 days old. He promptly intervened and pursued custody. The Does developed a deep parent-child relationship during years of litigation, but the Illinois Supreme Court found Kirchner fit and held that his consent or a lawful unfitness finding was required before adoption.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The Does’ interests must be distinguished from Richard’s independent interest in a safe transition. The Court reviews the validity of their claimed right to defeat Kirchner’s status, not the later execution of the transfer. State-law adoption validity is accepted unless it independently violates federal law.
- **Relevant Dates:** Child’s birth, March 16, 1991; Illinois adoption judgment, June 16, 1994; related certiorari denial, November 7, 1994; simulated decision, June 21, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** *Baby Richard* separately presents the child’s own procedural claim arising from the custody-transfer order.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** 159 Ill. 2d 347, 638 N.E.2d 181; related orders and federal filings; *O’Connell v. Kirchner*, 513 U.S. 1303.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical merits decision; certiorari was denied.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Illinois Supreme Court judgment is the historical merits anchor; later federal stay and certiorari requests were denied.
- **Historical Holding:** Kirchner’s rights had not been lawfully terminated, the adoption was invalid, and the Does could not retain custody on a best-interests theory without a finding of his unfitness.
- **Historical Authority:** State-court historical anchor.
- **Historical Sources:** 159 Ill. 2d 347; 513 U.S. 994; 513 U.S. 1303.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

# United States v. Ursery; United States v. $405,089.23 in United States Currency

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Legal Field:** Double jeopardy, civil forfeiture, punishment classification, property, and sequential proceedings.

In the companion matter, the Government forfeited large sums of currency and other property alleged to be drug proceeds or instrumentalities and pursued related criminal charges. The lower courts treated the civil labels as noncontrolling and found punishment.

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

#### Issue 1: Double Jeopardy classification of civil in rem forfeiture

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Kurth Ranch* establishes that a civil or tax label is not conclusive when legal design and practical operation make an exaction punishment. The lower courts therefore erred by treating the forfeitures as second criminal punishment.
