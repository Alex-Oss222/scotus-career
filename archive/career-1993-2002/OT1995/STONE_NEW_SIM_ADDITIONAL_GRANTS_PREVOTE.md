# Justice Stone Pre-Vote Positions: Additional New-Simulation Grants

## 1. [Tyler Murray v. Montrose County School District RE-1J, 51 F.3d 921](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/51/921/618583/)

**Stone’s answers.** (1) IDEA establishes the second alternative in the
granted question: no freestanding legal presumption that the child must
stay in the neighborhood school while the district exhausts
supplementary aids there. Former 20 U.S.C. § 1412(5)(B) commands
education with nondisabled children to the maximum appropriate extent
and consideration of supplementary aids before removal from regular
classes; it does not select a building. Former 34 C.F.R. § 300.552 gives
concrete content to a neighborhood-placement preference—the school the
child would attend if nondisabled unless the IEP requires another
arrangement, and otherwise placement as close to home as possible—but
makes individual educational need expressed through the IEP controlling.

**Disposition.** Affirm the Tenth Circuit’s judgment on the granted
question, while clarifying that a “preference” is an operative placement
instruction rather than permission to ignore geography or centralize
children for administrative convenience. Do not decide Tyler’s
now-superseded placement, the adequacy of his later IEP, or a general
LRE test.

**Precedent applied.** No supplied simulated holding squarely controls.
Supplied *Florence County School District Four v. Carter*, 510 U.S. 7
(1993), is consistent with child-specific appropriateness but decides
reimbursement, not neighborhood placement; controlling real law is
former 20 U.S.C. §§ 1401, 1412(5)(B), 1414–1415, former 34 C.F.R. §
300.552, and *Board of Education v. Rowley*, 458 U.S. 176, 181, 202–07
(1982).

**Stone’s reasoning and exact legal move.** Separate integration from
location. The phrases “regular educational environment,” “regular
classes,” and “supplementary aids and services” regulate separation from
nondisabled peers; § 300.552 separately directs where an otherwise
appropriate placement should occur and yields only when a valid,
individualized IEP requires another arrangement—not merely when
officials write a different school into the document. Stone therefore
refuses both to import 20 U.S.C. § 1701’s general
neighborhood-assignment policy into § 1412 and to drain the implementing
regulation of effect: neighborhood placement matters, but it does not
create a burden to exhaust every conceivable aid in a particular
building.

**Proposed holding.** IDEA does not presume that every disabled child
must remain in the neighborhood school with supplementary aids and
services. It requires education with nondisabled peers to the maximum
appropriate extent and establishes an IEP-conditioned preference for the
school the child would attend if nondisabled, or otherwise a placement
as close to home as possible.

**Limit.** The holding neither adopts a general LRE test nor decides
when an IEP validly requires another arrangement. It does not authorize
cost- or convenience-driven segregation, weaken the supplementary-aids
duty before removal from regular classes, or decide any live application
to Tyler.

**Novelty.** Statutory or constitutional interpretation; secondary:
issue reservation or minimalist disposition.

## 2. [In re Askin, 47 F.3d 100](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/47/100/472728/)

**Stone’s answers.** (1) Former Title III left acquisition of the
expressly excluded cordless radio segment outside its prohibition, and
that segment carried both parties’ words; the exclusion did not
affirmatively authorize interception at another point or answer the
Constitution. (2) Yes, if the caller neither knew of the cordless
retransmission nor otherwise exposed the call. Absent participant
consent or a willing informant, a landline or cellular caller does not
lose a reasonable expectation in the call’s contents merely because
unknown agents acquire them through the other endpoint; whether Askin
knew Brumbaugh used a cordless handset is not established in the
supplied record. (3) A Title III violation supplies “just cause” through
§ 2515’s express evidentiary prohibition and *Gelbard*. Stone would
extend the Fourth Amendment exclusionary rule narrowly to direct-fruit
questions in this criminal-trial contempt proceeding against the
intercepted speaker himself; *Calandra* remains confined to the distinct
grand-jury balance, and independently sourced questions remain
compulsory.

**Disposition.** Affirm the former-Title-III construction, reverse the
Fourth Amendment categorical ruling, vacate the civil-contempt judgment,
and remand to determine Askin’s knowledge or knowing exposure,
derivation question by question, and any independent source,
attenuation, good-faith reliance, or other lawful basis. If the grant
establishes lack of knowledge and direct derivation as premises, direct
that the contempt judgment be set aside.

**Precedent applied.** Supplied simulated *Landgraf v. USI Film
Products*, 511 U.S. 244 (1994), bars retroactive application of the 1994
amendment deleting the cordless exclusions. Supplied simulated *United
States v. Pinson*, 24 F.3d 1056 (May 8, 1995), and *United States v.
Wellons*, 32 F.3d 117 (May 30, 1995), are limited
analogies—respectively, technological acquisition at the home and the
independence of personal privacy interests—not controlling
telephone-interception rules. Controlling unchanged real law is former
18 U.S.C. §§ 2510(1), 2510(12)(A), 2511, and 2515; 28 U.S.C. § 1826(a);
*Katz v. United States*, 389 U.S. 347 (1967); *United States v. White*,
401 U.S. 745, 749–53 (1971); *Alderman v. United States*, 394 U.S. 165,
171–76 (1969); *Gelbard v. United States*, 408 U.S. 41, 46–52 (1972);
*United States v. Calandra*, 414 U.S. 338, 347–55 (1974); *Rakas v.
Illinois*, 439 U.S. 128, 133–38 (1978); *United States v. Leon*, 468
U.S. 897, 906–25 (1984); and *Illinois v. Krull*, 480 U.S. 340, 349–60
(1987).

**Stone’s reasoning and exact legal move.** Decide statutory coverage,
constitutional right, and remedy separately. The place of acquisition
controls former Title III, so the later amendment cannot govern
completed surveillance; noncoverage, however, is not constitutional
permission. *White* assigns the risk that a willing interlocutor will
reveal or transmit a conversation, not the different risk that unknown
agents will independently intercept two unconsenting speakers. On
remedy, § 1826 is the contempt channel, not an automatic exclusionary
source: § 2515 controls the Title III branch, while Stone openly extends
Fourth Amendment exclusion because the Government proposes to use the
direct fruits of intercepting this witness’s own calls to imprison him
at trial; that core deterrent benefit outweighs the bounded cost.
*Krull* does not control merely because Title III omitted a
prohibition—the former exclusion did not affirmatively authorize a
constitutional search—and *Calandra*, third-party suppression,
objectively reasonable reliance on a warrant or specifically controlling
precedent, attenuation, and independent source remain intact.

**Proposed holding.** Former Title III excluded the cordless radio
segment intercepted here, including both speakers’ words, but that
exclusion did not displace the Fourth Amendment. A caller using a
landline or cellular telephone does not lose Fourth Amendment protection
merely because the other participant uses a vulnerable cordless handset
without the caller’s knowledge. When trial questions are direct fruits
of that unlawful interception of the witness’s own calls, the Fourth
Amendment exclusionary rule makes refusal “just cause” under § 1826(a).

**Limit.** The holding does not decide the cordless user’s own
expectation, protect a speaker who knew the call was publicly exposed,
disturb participant-consent cases, apply the 1994 amendment
retroactively, extend the trial rule to grand-jury questioning governed
by *Calandra*, or excuse questions supported by independent source,
attenuation, or another lawful acquisition.

**Novelty.** Extension of existing doctrine; secondary: statutory or
constitutional interpretation; remedial or dispositional difference.

## 3. [United States v. Sahhar, 56 F.3d 1026](https://openjurist.org/56/f3d/1026/united-states-v-sahhar)

**Stone’s answers.** (1) Yes. Section 4246 expressly reaches a person
committed under § 4241(d), a person whose sentence is about to expire,
or a person whose charges were dismissed solely because of his mental
condition; no maximum-sentence clock appears in the civil-commitment
text. The court initially commits the person to the Attorney General;
the Attorney General must make and periodically renew reasonable
state-placement efforts, and transfer becomes mandatory if an eligible
State accepts. (2) Yes, but only while current mental disease causes the
statutorily specified danger and meaningful review and a practical route
to unconditional or conditional release remain available; the expired
hypothetical sentence is not the measure of a nonpunitive commitment. (3)
Yes. The custody-defined federal class and the federal interests in
safely winding up lawful custody, treatment, and public protection are
sufficiently related. Section 4246 requires a neutral judicial hearing,
counsel, clear-and-convincing proof of illness-caused danger, and
recurring review; unlike *Jackson* it does not permit indefinite custody
on incompetence alone, and unlike *Baxstrom* it does not deny a state
prisoner access to the State’s generally applicable end-of-sentence
commitment process. Stone would reserve the ultimate tier because the
scheme survives intermediate scrutiny. (4) Not reached because the supplied materials do not establish
preservation; Stone therefore states no position on whether *Greenwood*
extends beyond an extant prosecution.

**Disposition.** Affirm the Ninth Circuit’s judgment reversing the
district court’s release order and remanding for a contemporaneous
inquiry into Arizona’s response to the Attorney General’s reasonable
efforts; do not disturb the unappealed dismissal of the indictment.

**Precedent applied.** No supplied simulated holding directly resolves §
4246. The litigation baseline is *United States v. Sahhar (Sahhar I)*,
917 F.2d 1197, 1201–08 (9th Cir. 1990), which sustained the scheme’s
procedures and interests while leaving the ultimate scrutiny tier open.
Controlling unchanged real law is 18 U.S.C. §§ 4246–4247; *Greenwood v.
United States*, 350 U.S. 366, 375–76 (1956), which upheld commitment
while prosecution remained unexhausted and expressly decided no more;
*Jackson v. Indiana*, 406 U.S. 715 (1972); *O’Connor v. Donaldson*, 422
U.S. 563 (1975); *Addington v. Texas*, 441 U.S. 418 (1979); *Jones v.
United States*, 463 U.S. 354, 368–69 (1983); *Foucha v. Louisiana*, 504
U.S. 71 (1992); and *Baxstrom v. Herold*, 383 U.S. 107 (1966).

**Stone’s reasoning and exact legal move.** Begin with the enacted civil
scheme, not a punishment analogy: Congress made present illness-caused
dangerousness, proved clearly and convincingly, the custody ground,
while §§ 4246(e) and 4247 provide discharge, periodic reporting,
counsel, hearings, and renewed petitions. Apply *Jones*, *Jackson*, and
*Foucha* together—sentence length does not measure recovery, but the
nature and duration of custody must continuously track illness and
danger—and give independent force to § 4246(d)’s repeated “shall”
commands rather than treating state placement as precatory. Resolve due
process and equal protection on those grounds, assume the more
protective equality standard without choosing it, and reserve the
unpreserved enumerated-power issue rather than silently extending
*Greenwood*.

**Proposed holding.** Section 4246 permits confinement beyond the
maximum possible sentence because the confinement is civil and rests on
current mental illness causing current dangerousness, not punishment for
the charge. Continued custody requires the statute’s meaningful review
and release routes and reasonable, periodically renewed efforts to
transfer responsibility to a willing State. The Court does not decide an
unpreserved enumerated-power question.

**Limit.** The holding does not permit confinement based on incompetence
alone, dangerousness without qualifying mental disease, a stale finding,
absence of meaningful review, or federal retention after a State accepts
responsibility. It does not make a one-time state inquiry sufficient,
establish a freestanding federal civil-commitment power, or decide the
constitutional result if the statutory safeguards become nominal in
operation.

**Novelty.** Statutory or constitutional interpretation; secondary:
issue reservation or minimalist disposition.

## 4. [DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers’ Association, 51 F.3d 591](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/51/591/618202/)

**Stone’s answers.** (1) Yes as a statutory matter. A small number of
written communications can be severe or pervasive, and, viewing the
proof favorably to the verdict, a rational jury could rely on ten
sex-derogatory columns distributed to more than 700 colleagues, four
links to DeAngelis, repeated “dingy woman” comments, two insubordination
incidents, humiliation and lost confidence, promotion deterrence, and
the Association membership’s refusal to unmask the columnist; *Harris*
makes neither lost performance, physical threat, supervisor status, nor
medium indispensable. (2) No. The complaint-related publications and
conditional statement that the Association might sue if her action
proved groundless did not deny membership, representation, pay,
promotion, assignment, authority, or another material employment or
associational advantage. (3) Because Question 1 otherwise permits
liability, reached: imposing damages on a labor organization’s editorial
publication solely because protected opinion or satire expresses a
sex-derogatory viewpoint is content based and cannot survive the most
searching review absent a threat, an unprotected category, or
independently discriminatory employment conduct to which the expression
is integral or evidentiary.

**Disposition.** Affirm the Fifth Circuit’s judgment for the Association
on the alternative First Amendment ground as to hostile environment and
on statutory insufficiency as to retaliation. Reject its reweighing of
the hostile-environment verdict and any indispensable work-performance
requirement.

**Precedent applied.** Supplied simulated *Harris v. Forklift Systems,
Inc.*, 510 U.S. 17 (1993), controls the statutory branch: severe **or**
pervasive, objective and subjective abuse, totality, and no
indispensable listed consideration. Supplied simulated *Hurley v.
Irish-American Gay, Lesbian & Bisexual Group of Boston*, 515 U.S. 557
(1995), protects an association’s control of its own expressive
presentation and supplies the closest simulated analogy, not a
workplace-harassment holding. Controlling real law is Title VII, 42
U.S.C. §§ 2000e-2(c), 2000e-3(a); *Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson*, 477
U.S. 57 (1986); *Hustler Magazine, Inc. v. Falwell*, 485 U.S. 46, 50–57
(1988); *R.A.V. v. City of St. Paul*, 505 U.S. 377, 383–90 (1992); and
*Wisconsin v. Mitchell*, 508 U.S. 476, 487–90 (1993).

**Stone’s reasoning and exact legal move.** Stone keeps statute and
Constitution separate. Under the verdict-review posture, the proof
clears *Harris* without turning every insult into Title VII liability,
while the retaliation proof lacks a material employment or membership
consequence. That statutory result makes Question 3 necessary: Title VII
may regulate discriminatory decisions, quid pro quo demands, threats,
and speech integral to or used as evidence of independently unlawful
conduct, but an as-applied damages judgment triggered solely by the
viewpoint of protected newsletter opinion and satire directly penalizes
editorial content. Eradicating sex discrimination is compelling, but on
this record liability is not narrowly tailored because the publication
itself is the only independently asserted wrong.

**Proposed holding.** A rational jury could find these publications
severe or pervasive under *Harris*, but Title VII damages may not rest
solely on protected opinion or satire in an association newsletter
without an unprotected category or independently discriminatory
employment conduct. The complaint-related references imposed no material
employment or membership disadvantage and therefore did not establish
retaliation. The judgment for the Association is affirmed.

**Limit.** The result does not immunize discriminatory employment
decisions, quid pro quo demands, true threats, speech integral to
unlawful conduct, authority-backed directives, or evidentiary use of
expression to prove discrimination; nor does it require measurable
performance decline under *Harris*. It decides only civil liability
resting on the protected viewpoint of editorial opinion and satire, and
it does not define every actionable retaliation as an ultimate
employment decision.

**Novelty.** Extension of existing doctrine.

## 5. [Walmer v. Department of Defense, 52 F.3d 851](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/52/851/573233/)

**Stone’s answers.** (1) Intermediate scrutiny governs. A rule mandating
separation specifically for homosexual acts intentionally sorts by
sexual orientation, whether described as conduct or as a status proxy;
the Government must identify an actual important military objective and
establish a substantial relationship without relying on moral
disapproval, private bias, or stereotype. Professional military
judgments and reasonable contemporaneous predictions receive substantial
evidentiary weight within that standard, especially on discipline,
cohesion, readiness, and command, but military deference neither lowers
the tier nor carries the Government’s burden by assertion. Because the
courts below used rational basis and the supplied appellate record
contains no developed current military showing, application
must be remanded. (2) No. Article I’s Bill of Attainder Clause restrains
legislative punishment, not an executive or military regulation;
executive abuse remains reviewable under the Fifth Amendment, statute,
and ordinary administrative law. (3) Not reached because the Clause
does not apply to an executive military regulation, making its
punishment element unnecessary to the disposition.

**Disposition.** Affirm as to the Bill of Attainder claim; vacate as to
the equal-protection denial of preliminary relief and remand for
application of intermediate scrutiny and the ordinary
preliminary-injunction factors. The interlocutory posture and missing
substantiation do not permit a final merits judgment that AR 635-100 is
valid or invalid.

**Precedent applied.** No supplied simulated holding decides the
sexual-orientation tier; supplied simulated *Kelley v. Board of Trustees
of the University of Illinois*, 35 F.3d 265 (May 22, 1995), expressly
reserved it, so Stone makes the assignment openly. Supplied simulated
*Ambassador Books & Video, Inc. v. City of Little Rock*, 20 F.3d 858
(Mar. 20, 1995), supplies the identification-and-punishment framework
for a legislative enactment but does not extend the Clause to executive
regulation. Controlling unchanged real law is the Fifth Amendment
equality rule of *Bolling v. Sharpe*, 347 U.S. 497 (1954); *City of
Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center*, 473 U.S. 432 (1985); *Rostker v.
Goldberg*, 453 U.S. 57, 64–83 (1981), *Parker v. Levy*, 417 U.S. 733,
743–44 (1974), and *Goldman v. Weinberger*, 475 U.S. 503, 506–10 (1986)
on military weight; *Bowers v. Hardwick*, 478 U.S. 186 (1986), which
forecloses the asserted fundamental-conduct right at this date but not
the separate equality tier; and *Nixon v. Administrator of General
Services*, 433 U.S. 425, 468–84 (1977), and *Selective Service System v.
MPIRG*, 468 U.S. 841, 846–56 (1984), defining a bill of attainder as
legislative identification and punishment without judicial trial.

**Stone’s reasoning and exact legal move.** Stone openly assigns sexual
orientation the elevated baseline and treats an act rule selected to
identify that class as the classification it operationally uses; this is
a different scrutiny assignment, not “active rational basis.” He keeps
*Bowers*’s substantive-due-process holding separate from equal
protection and keeps military competence inside the evidentiary
application of intermediate scrutiny: courts do not second-guess
reasonable military expertise, but the Army must connect the categorical
discharge rule to an actual functional need. Because the lower court
placed the practical burden on Walmer to disprove old circuit precedent
and the full Army record is not supplied, Stone remands rather than
manufactures the missing fit finding; he resolves the Bill of Attainder
threshold by Article I’s allocation and never reaches punishment.

**Proposed holding.** A military rule mandating separation for
homosexual acts classifies by sexual orientation and must be
substantially related to an actual important military objective, with
substantial but nonconclusive weight given to professional military
judgment. Because that standard was not applied and its factual
predicate was not resolved, the equal-protection component of the
preliminary-injunction judgment is vacated and remanded. The Bill of
Attainder Clause does not govern an executive military regulation, so
its punishment element is not reached.

**Limit.** Stone does not decide that all regulation of sexual conduct
in the military is invalid, overrule *Bowers*, constitutionalize a right
to serve, substitute judicial command judgment for supported military
prediction, or finally adjudicate AR 635-100 on an incomplete
preliminary record. The Bill of Attainder ruling does not insulate an
executive regulation from equal protection, due process, statutory, or
administrative review.

**Novelty.** Different scrutiny assignment; secondary: remedial or
dispositional difference.

## 6. [Elizabeth G. Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth, 420 Mass. 749](https://law.justia.com/cases/massachusetts/supreme-court/volumes/420/420mass749.html)

**Stone’s answers.** (1) No. A voluntary condom-availability program
does not directly or coercively displace parental direction or invade
familial privacy when a student need not request, receive, read about,
be counseled concerning, or use a condom and suffers no penalty for
declining. (2) No; absent a deprivation, the Fourteenth Amendment
requires neither notice nor an opt-out merely because the optional
program is located in a compulsory-attendance school. (3) No; the
program neither regulates religious exercise, compels religiously
forbidden conduct, penalizes belief, nor conditions a benefit on
abandoning belief, and offense or peer pressure is not a substantial
Free Exercise burden.

**Disposition.** Affirm.

**Precedent applied.** No supplied simulated holding squarely controls;
*Meyer v. Nebraska*, *Pierce v. Society of Sisters*, *Prince v.
Massachusetts*, *Wisconsin v. Yoder*, *Bowen v. Roy*, *Lyng v. Northwest
Indian Cemetery Protective Ass’n*, and *Employment Division v. Smith*.

**Stone’s reasoning and exact legal move.** Apply the received
deprivation/coercion threshold and Stone’s strong parental presumption
without converting parental authority into a veto over optional school
services; compulsory attendance does not make every choice available on
school grounds compulsory.

**Proposed holding.** A public school does not substantially burden
parental liberty, family privacy, or religious exercise by making
condoms voluntarily available without penalty for nonparticipation. The
Constitution therefore does not require parental notice or an opt-out on
this record.

**Limit.** No decision on compulsory instruction or counseling,
compelled receipt, a medical procedure, a penalty for refusal, or school
interference with a parent’s instruction to the child.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

## 7. [Uhlrig v. Harder, 64 F.3d 567](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/64/567/631313/)

**Stone’s answers.** (1) Yes in principle: outside custody, Due Process
may reach an official’s affirmative creation or material intensification
of a private danger, but it creates no general duty to protect or
guarantee of a safe public workplace. (2) Under supplied simulated
*Pinder*, the claimant must prove a specific imminent threat,
affirmative official inducement, reasonable reliance that changes
available self-protection, deliberate use of governmental authority,
material increased vulnerability, deliberate indifference, and
causation; particularization and foreseeability are served by the
specific-imminent-threat requirement, not by a free-standing “limited
class” box. Uhlrig cannot satisfy that chain because the administrators
gave no protective assurance inducing reliance, she was warned generally
and specifically, clinical personnel—not these defendants—made Waddell’s
placements, and the phase-out procedure negates deliberate indifference
on this record. (3) A considered institutional choice can shock the
conscience upon deliberate indifference to a known, specific, grave, and
imminent danger coupled with the affirmative causal sequence above;
negligence, gross negligence, or “recklessness” that means less than
conscious disregard is insufficient. (4) **Not reached** because no
constitutional violation is established.

**Disposition.** Affirm summary judgment.

**Precedent applied.** Supplied simulated *Pinder v. Johnson*, 54 F.3d
1169; controlling real law *DeShaney v. Winnebago County*, *Collins v.
City of Harker Heights*, *Daniels v. Williams*, and *Davidson v.
Cannon*.

**Stone’s reasoning and exact legal move.** Apply *Pinder*’s bounded
affirmative-act/reliance/causation chain and resolve the case on the
merits before immunity.

**Proposed holding.** State-created-danger liability outside custody
requires deliberate official action that affirmatively and causally
makes the claimant materially more vulnerable to a specific imminent
private threat. Closing the AWL unit through a delegated clinical
placement process, without inducement, changed reliance, or deliberate
indifference by these defendants, does not meet that rule.

**Limit.** No ruling on an official who conceals a known attacker’s
danger, gives a concrete assurance, induces an employee to forgo
protection, or personally orders the dangerous placement.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

## 8. [Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc., 68 F.3d 525](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/68/525/537667/)

**Stone’s answers.** (1) No. The students possess no freestanding constitutional privacy right against offensive curricular speech, and compulsory attendance at this one school-selected AIDS-awareness presentation did not compel either named student to participate in a skit, affirm a message, engage in conduct, or undergo a bodily intrusion. *Meyer* and *Pierce* protect parental choice among lawful educational paths and parental instruction outside school; they do not give each parent authority to prescribe the lessons taught in a chosen public school. (2) **Not reached** as a substantive constitutional condition because no protected parental liberty was impaired. Independently, assuming the written positive-subscription policy created a state-law interest, the procedural claim fails because the plaintiffs did not preserve an argument that Massachusetts postdeprivation remedies were inadequate; Stone does not decide whether the planned departure was random and unauthorized. (3) No Free Exercise violation: exposure without compelled profession, worship, affirmation, or religiously forbidden conduct is not a substantial exercise burden, the attendance rule was neutral and generally applicable under *Smith*, and no independent parental-liberty claim exists with which to form a hybrid claim. (4) No Title IX claim; neither the presentation nor the alleged later ridicule was because of sex, so severity or pervasiveness is **not reached**. (5) **Not reached** because no constitutional violation is established.

**Disposition.** Affirm dismissal of the federal claims. Any remedy for violation of state law or the School Committee’s positive-subscription policy remains outside the granted federal questions.

**Precedent applied.** Supplied simulated *Settle v. Dickson County School Board* recognizes educators’ authority to apply genuine curricular requirements reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical objectives; simulated *Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21* is distinguished because its practical-nonparticipation condition belongs to a government-led religious civic observance, not nonreligious curriculum generally. Controlling real law includes *Meyer*, *Pierce*, *Prince*, *Yoder*, *Parratt v. Taylor*, *Hudson v. Palmer*, *Zinermon v. Burch*, *Smith*, *Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye v. Hialeah*, *Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson*, *Harris v. Forklift Systems*, and *Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools*.

**Stone’s reasoning and exact legal move.** Apply the received distinction between protecting a parent’s authority to choose and supplement a child’s education and giving the parent a line-item veto over public-school curriculum. Apply simulated *Settle* to the school’s selection of curricular content, distinguish *Sherman* through source separation, reject the student-privacy, Free Exercise, and Title IX theories under their own elements, and reserve the unnecessary *Parratt-Hudson* classification while resolving procedural due process on preservation. Because no constitutional violation exists, the narrow-disposition rung makes qualified immunity unnecessary.

**Proposed holding.** Parental liberty secures parents’ choice among lawful educational paths and their own instruction; it does not confer a constitutional right to prescribe or opt out of each public-school curricular presentation reasonably related to a legitimate pedagogical objective. Compulsory exposure to ideas, without compelled participation, affirmation, religious conduct, or bodily intrusion, does not violate student privacy or Free Exercise, and sexual content is not Title IX discrimination absent hostility because of sex. Assuming a school policy created a state-law interest, failure to preserve a challenge to available postdeprivation remedies defeats the procedural-due-process claim.

**Limit.** The position does not give schools unlimited authority or make professional approval a constitutional safe harbor. It does not authorize compelled profession or participation, bodily intrusion, religious establishment or targeting, discrimination because of sex, pretextual curricular action, or disregard of state law and valid local policy without the remedies those sources provide. Parents remain free to supplement instruction and pursue administrative, political, and state-law relief.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

## 9. [Wright v. MetroHealth Medical Center, 58 F.3d 1130](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/58/1130/574869/)

**Stone’s answers.** (1) No. The rule neither forbids, annuls,
criminalizes, nor conditions legal permission to marry; it requires one
spouse to leave a particular LifeFlight assignment and promises
reasonable efforts toward comparable responsibility and pay, an
incidental employment consequence rather than a direct and substantial
marital obstacle. (2) Rational-basis review governs. (3) Yes; avoiding
conflicts, favoritism or its appearance, impaired judgment in a
high-risk unit, and morale or administrative problems are legitimate
interests, and a same-unit transfer rule is rationally related to them
despite imperfect, case-specific, or underinclusive enforcement.

**Disposition.** Affirm the federal constitutional judgment.

**Precedent applied.** No supplied simulated holding squarely controls;
*Loving v. Virginia*, *Califano v. Jobst*, *Zablocki v. Redhail*, and
*Williamson v. Lee Optical*.

**Stone’s reasoning and exact legal move.** Apply *Zablocki*’s
direct-and-substantial-interference trigger and keep the protected right
to marry separate from an unprotected entitlement to a preferred public
workplace assignment; no evidence of marital animus or deliberate proxy
moves the rule out of ordinary review.

**Proposed holding.** A governmental anti-nepotism policy that leaves
marriage legally and practically available and transfers one spouse from
a particular assignment does not significantly interfere with the right
to marry. The policy survives rational-basis review when reasonably
directed to conflicts, favoritism, safety, or workplace administration.

**Limit.** No ruling on a marriage ban, automatic discharge, severe
penalty making marriage practically impossible, discriminatory
enforcement proved to rest on a forbidden purpose, or the ungranted
state-law claims.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

## 10. [Brown v. Polk County, 61 F.3d 650 (en banc)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/61/650/492754/)

**Stone’s answers.** (1) Under Title VII as then authoritatively
construed, the employer must reasonably accommodate a known sincere
practice unless accommodation would impose more than a de minimis, real
operational burden. Polk County need not let Brown divert a secretary or
other public resources to Bible-study work, open a workspace otherwise
closed to all personal use, or use supervisory authority for official
