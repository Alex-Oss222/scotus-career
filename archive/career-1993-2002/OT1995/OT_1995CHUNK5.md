# Sheets v. Salt Lake County

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Sheets v. Salt Lake County*
- **Citation or Docket:** 45 F.3d 1383 (10th Cir. 1995), Nos. 93-4128 and 93-4134.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Granted merits case on governmental disclosure of intimate information.
- **Simulated Event Date:** April 29, 1996, simulation-assigned opinion-delivery date.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a county investigator violated a constitutional confidentiality interest by disclosing intimate portions of a murder victim’s diary concerning her surviving husband, and whether the County may be liable for the disclosure policy.
- **Question Granted:** Does the Fourteenth Amendment protect intimate personal information obtained by government through a criminal investigation from intentional disclosure for purposes unrelated to a legitimate governmental function?
- **Legal Field:** Informational privacy, § 1983, municipal liability, and damages.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Tenth Circuit affirmed a $650,000 jury verdict and attorney-fee award against Salt Lake County and investigator Michael George.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Defendants seek reversal or a new trial; Sheets seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** After Kathy Sheets was killed by a bomb, Gary Sheets voluntarily supplied her diary to investigators for the murder inquiry under evidence supporting an assurance of confidentiality. The diary contained intimate observations about his marriage, finances, character, and business. After the investigation closed, George allowed a favored author access to notes and copies, and the resulting book quoted the diary. The County maintained an unwritten policy permitting post-investigation press access and discussion, and the jury found causation and liability.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The claims were tried to a jury. Review must respect supported verdict findings while independently resolving whether the identified interest and rule are constitutional. The Court must distinguish Sheets’s own intimate information from his deceased wife’s independent privacy and distinguish individual from municipal liability.
- **Relevant Dates:** Tenth Circuit decision, January 10, 1995; simulated decision, April 29, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** Consolidated appeals from merits and fee judgments.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** 45 F.3d 1383; trial record and diary description summarized there; 42 U.S.C. § 1983.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits decision. The Tenth Circuit judgment is the historical anchor.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Opinion by Judge Kelly affirming the verdict and fee award.
- **Historical Holding:** Sheets possessed a legitimate confidentiality interest in intimate information about himself contained in the diary, and sufficient evidence supported disclosure, causation, and liability.
- **Historical Authority:** Lower-court historical anchor.
- **Historical Sources:** 45 F.3d 1383.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to chronological revalidation.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** *Hodge v. Jones*, if effective before this event, distinguishes confidential retention from public disclosure and does not foreclose this claim.

#### Issue 1: Constitutional confidentiality

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision establishes a general informational-privacy cause. Existing due-process authority leaves open protection against deliberate governmental disclosure of intimate information, while **Specific constitutional source and substantive-due-process nonduplication** requires use of a more specific source when one directly governs. No other constitutional provision directly governs the deliberate publication alleged here.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Specific constitutional source and substantive-due-process nonduplication.**
- **Other Governing Law:** *Whalen v. Roe*, *Nixon v. Administrator of General Services*, *Paul v. Davis*, and pre-cutoff confidentiality cases.
- **Other Authority Used:** The confidentiality assurance, intimate marital and financial content, limited investigative purpose, intentional publication, and the established trial record.
- **Open Point:** The precise boundary of constitutional informational privacy remains narrow and context dependent.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment. Stone's merits holding is limited to the granted constitutional-confidentiality question; the municipal-liability, compensatory-damages, and fee rulings remain undisturbed without separate merits adjudication.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** No separate remedial instruction is required. The existing judgment remains in force. Simulated *Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg* concerns punitive-damages review and supplies no basis to reopen the compensatory award in a case where that issue is outside the controlled grant.

#### Issue 1: Constitutional confidentiality

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the granted constitutional question. Sheets asserts his own interest in intimate information about his marriage and finances, not a derivative right belonging only to his wife.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Government may obtain intimate personal information for a lawful investigation and retain it under safeguards. It may not deliberately disclose that information for public dissemination after the investigative purpose ends when no law-enforcement purpose, public-record duty, or comparably sufficient governmental justification supports the disclosure. The present grant does not require a separate ruling on municipal policy, damages amount, or fees.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects against deliberate governmental publication of intimate marital and financial information entrusted to police for a limited criminal investigation when the disclosure serves no law-enforcement purpose, public-record duty, or comparably sufficient governmental function.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Turning the diary over to investigators did not invite publication. Limited disclosure for a murder inquiry is materially different from access by a private author for public dissemination. The information concerned Sheets directly and was comparable in intimacy to medical and financial records. On the established record, the publication was outside the purpose for which official access was obtained and lacked a sufficient governmental justification.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not create a general constitutional right to secrecy, bar lawful public-record access, protect already public facts, or impose liability for negligent disclosure without the state of mind required by the governing claim. He gives no separate merits holding on municipal liability, the compensatory amount, or fees.

# Holly Farms Corp. v. NLRB

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Holly Farms Corp. v. NLRB*
- **Citation or Docket:** 517 U.S. 392; No. 95-210.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review.
- **Simulated Event Date:** May 6, 1996, simulation-assigned opinion-delivery date.
- **Question Presented:** Whether “live-haul” workers who catch chickens raised by independent growers and transport them to a processor are excluded agricultural laborers or covered employees under the NLRA.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Board reasonably classified the workers as employees aligned with processing rather than agriculture.
- **Legal Field:** Labor law, NLRA coverage, agricultural exemption, and agency interpretation.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit enforced the Board’s order approving a bargaining unit that included the live-haul crews.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Holly Farms seeks reversal; the Board and union seek affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Holly Farms was a vertically integrated poultry processor. Independent growers raised broilers under contract. Company-employed catchers, forklift operators, and drivers entered the farms, loaded mature chickens, and transported them to the processing plant. Their employer, supervision, equipment, and operational integration were tied to Holly Farms’s processing business rather than the growers’ farm enterprises.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The statutory coverage issue is preserved. The Court independently fixes the statute’s permissible meaning and then determines whether the Board’s reasoned classification lies within it.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, February 21, 1996; supplied simulated decision, May 6, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** The bargaining-unit and unfair-labor-practice proceedings are part of the same Board order.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 517 U.S. 392; Board and Fourth Circuit decisions described there.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 5-4.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Opinion of the Court by Justice Ginsburg; Justice O’Connor dissented, joined by the Chief Justice and Justices Scalia and Thomas.
- **Historical Holding:** The Board reasonably classified the live-haul workers as covered employees rather than exempt agricultural laborers.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 517 U.S. 392.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, including *NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America*.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** *Town & Country* may clarify the breadth of “employee” and the Board’s classification role.

#### Issue 1: Agricultural-laborer exclusion

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp.* sustains the Board’s distinction between ordinary skilled work and genuine management authority and recognizes its role in classifying employment relationships within statutory bounds.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Professional direction versus statutory supervisory authority** supplies the functional classification principle, not the agricultural result.
- **Other Governing Law:** NLRA § 2(3), Fair Labor Standards Act § 3(f) incorporated through appropriations practice, and *Farmers Reservoir & Irrigation Co. v. McComb*.
- **Other Authority Used:** Employer identity, task sequence, relation to growers, and integration with processing.
- **Open Point:** Whether catching and loading remain agriculture when performed by the processor’s employees after the growers’ raising work is complete.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The Board’s order may be enforced. No separate remedial instruction is required.

#### Issue 1: Agricultural-laborer exclusion

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory coverage question.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The secondary-agriculture clause covers practices performed by a farmer or on a farm as an incident to or in conjunction with that farming operation. Work does not become agricultural merely because it physically occurs on a farm or concerns an agricultural product. The actual employer, whose operation the task serves, the stage of production, and the relation to the farmer’s enterprise are legally relevant.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Holly Farms’s live-haul crews are covered employees because their catching, loading, and transportation work is performed by the processor after the independent growers complete the raising operation and is integrated with slaughter and processing.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The crews work for Holly Farms, use its equipment, follow its schedules, and move finished broilers into the plant’s production stream. The independent growers do not employ or control them. The Board’s line reflects the enacted distinction and is reasonably explained; formal location on farm property does not compel the exemption.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that every contract harvester or transporter is covered; employees of the farmer or work genuinely incidental to the farmer’s own operation may remain exempt.

# United States v. Armstrong

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *United States v. Armstrong*
- **Citation or Docket:** 517 U.S. 456; No. 95-157.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review of a selective-prosecution discovery order.
- **Simulated Event Date:** May 13, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** What showing a criminal defendant must make to obtain limited discovery on a claim that federal prosecutors selected Black defendants for crack-cocaine prosecution because of race.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the defense evidence was sufficient to justify discovery and whether the required threshold may depend on information held exclusively by the prosecution.
- **Legal Field:** Equal protection, selective prosecution, criminal discovery, and prosecutorial discretion.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit sustained a district-court discovery order after the defense presented evidence that all identified federal crack defendants in the relevant office were Black and offered comparative state-prosecution information.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The United States seeks reversal of the discovery order; respondents seek affirmance and limited access to charging information.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Respondents presented an affidavit from the federal public defender identifying twenty-four crack cases handled in one year, all involving Black defendants, along with materials indicating crack offenses and state prosecutions involving persons of other races. The defense lacked access to the full universe of federal referrals, declinations, charging criteria, and similarly situated suspects. The district court ordered limited discovery rather than finding discrimination.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The order is reviewable through the Government’s appeal of dismissal or sanctions associated with noncompliance. Discovery, prima facie merits proof, ultimate discriminatory purpose, and remedy remain distinct. The Court must protect legitimate prosecutorial confidentiality while avoiding a threshold that can be met only with withheld information.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, February 26, 1996; simulated decision, May 13, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** Multiple defendants are respondents in the same prosecution.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 517 U.S. 456; defense affidavits and district-court order described there.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 8-1.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Opinion of the Court by Chief Justice Rehnquist; Justice Stevens dissented.
- **Historical Holding:** Discovery required credible evidence that similarly situated persons of another race were not prosecuted; the defense showing was insufficient.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 517 U.S. 456.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Discovery threshold for selective prosecution

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision sets the discovery threshold. Equal-protection purpose doctrine requires discriminatory selection and impermissible purpose, with ultimate persuasion remaining on the defendant. Existing criminal-discovery rules permit focused disclosure on a substantial constitutional claim.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The registered equality rules distinguish impact from purpose and preserve ordinary burdens. No current reusable rule requires a defendant to identify by name uncharged comparators before discovery.
- **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause, *Wayte v. United States*, *Oyler v. Boles*, Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 16 to the extent applicable, and inherent authority for constitutional discovery.
- **Other Authority Used:** Statistical concentration, charging geography, comparative state cases, and the Government’s exclusive control of referral and declination data.
- **Open Point:** The minimum showing must balance access to proof against the costs and sensitivity of prosecutorial files.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the limited discovery order, subject to protective narrowing by the district court.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Discovery shall be confined to objective charging criteria, referrals, declinations, and anonymized comparator information for materially similar crack cases in the relevant period and office. Privileged work product, unrelated investigations, and sensitive identities shall be protected unless independently necessary.

#### Issue 1: Discovery threshold for selective prosecution

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone decides only discovery. He makes no finding of discriminatory selection or purpose.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A defendant seeking selective-prosecution discovery must present credible evidence that race may have materially influenced charging, including a substantial unexplained disparity and some basis to believe comparable conduct by others entered another enforcement channel or escaped federal prosecution. The showing must be stronger than speculation but cannot require the defendant to produce the Government’s confidential comparator file before obtaining access to it. The ultimate burden of proving discriminatory effect, purpose, and causation remains unchanged.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Respondents’ office-specific evidence of an all-Black federal crack docket, joined with indications of comparable non-Black offending handled outside federal court, is sufficient for narrowly tailored discovery even though respondents cannot yet identify every uncharged comparator.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Prosecutorial discretion is broad and presumptively regular, but it is not immunity from equal protection. The requested data are uniquely controlled by the Government. Requiring names and charging details from that undisclosed file would make the discovery standard self-defeating. The defense showing is not proof of liability, yet it is concrete enough to justify a limited test of whether neutral criteria explain the pattern.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not authorize open-file discovery, presume discrimination from disparity alone, shift the ultimate burden of persuasion, or require disclosure outside a relevant office, offense class, and time period without additional proof.

# 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island*
- **Citation or Docket:** 517 U.S. 484; No. 94-1140.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review.
- **Simulated Event Date:** May 13, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether Rhode Island may prohibit truthful advertising of retail liquor prices in order to promote temperance.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the ban satisfies the First Amendment and whether the Twenty-first Amendment alters the applicable protection.
- **Legal Field:** Commercial speech, alcohol regulation, and the Twenty-first Amendment.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Rhode Island Supreme Court upheld the advertising prohibition.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Retailers seek reversal and an injunction; the State seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Rhode Island prohibited retailers and media from publishing liquor prices except on premises in limited form. The State asserted that suppressing price information would reduce price competition and thereby lower consumption. The record did not establish that the ban materially advanced temperance or that direct regulatory alternatives were inadequate.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The speech and enforcement claims are preserved. The communication is truthful and concerns a lawful product. The Court need not decide false or misleading claims.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, November 1, 1995; simulated decision, May 13, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 517 U.S. 484; Rhode Island statutes and trial record described there.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens announced the judgment and wrote for a plurality; multiple concurrences agreed the ban was unconstitutional but differed on commercial-speech doctrine.
- **Historical Holding:** The blanket prohibition on truthful liquor-price advertising violated the First Amendment, and the Twenty-first Amendment did not save it.
- **Historical Authority:** Unanimous judgment with majority-supported application of meaningful commercial-speech review.
- **Historical Sources:** 517 U.S. 484.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, including *Rubin v. Coors Brewing Co.* and *Florida Bar v. Went For It, Inc.*
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Suppression of truthful price information

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Rubin v. Coors* holds that a restriction on truthful commercial information must materially advance a substantial interest and have a reasonable fit; internal contradictions are evidence of failure. *Florida Bar v. Went For It* protects truthful targeted solicitation absent evidence of concrete harm and reasonable fit.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Alcohol-content information on beer labels** and **Truthful targeted direct-mail solicitation.**
- **Other Governing Law:** *Central Hudson*, *Virginia Pharmacy Board*, *Edenfield*, and the Twenty-first Amendment.
- **Other Authority Used:** The State’s evidence of consumption, price competition, and available direct regulation.
- **Open Point:** Whether the Court should alter the general commercial-speech framework; the case can be decided by full application of current law.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand for entry of judgment barring enforcement of the advertising ban.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Rhode Island may regulate alcohol prices, taxes, sales conditions, misleading advertisements, and harmful marketing directly, but may not enforce this blanket suppression of truthful prices.

#### Issue 1: Suppression of truthful price information

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the First Amendment merits.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Truthful price information about a lawful product is protected for speakers and consumers. A State may pursue temperance, but it must establish that suppressing the information materially advances that interest and that the fit is reasonable. The Twenty-first Amendment grants regulatory authority over alcohol; it does not displace an independently applicable speech guarantee.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Rhode Island’s blanket ban on truthful liquor-price advertising violates the First Amendment because the State has not shown that information suppression materially reduces consumption or is reasonably fitted compared with direct regulation.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The policy seeks to keep consumers ignorant so prices remain high. That paternalistic mechanism conflicts with the premise that lawful choices are ordinarily made with information. The record supplies no persuasive causal showing, and the State may regulate taxes, minimum prices, hours, quantity, and sales directly. As in *Coors*, statutory inconsistencies and alternative channels weaken the asserted fit.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not protect false or misleading advertising or foreclose supported restrictions on promotion directed to unlawful sales or minors.

# United States v. Noland

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *United States v. Noland*
- **Citation or Docket:** 517 U.S. 535; No. 95-323.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review.
- **Simulated Event Date:** May 13, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a bankruptcy court may categorically subordinate an otherwise valid federal tax-penalty claim solely because it is a penalty.
- **Question Granted:** Whether equitable subordination may reorder the statutory priority scheme without creditor misconduct or a case-specific equitable basis.
- **Legal Field:** Bankruptcy, federal claims, priority, and equitable subordination.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit approved categorical subordination of the Internal Revenue Service’s penalty claim.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The United States seeks reversal; the trustee seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** The IRS filed claims including penalties in a corporate bankruptcy. The bankruptcy court subordinated the penalty claim as a class, not because the Government engaged in inequitable conduct in this case, but because the claim was punitive and would reduce distributions to innocent creditors.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The statutory and equitable-authority questions are preserved. Validity, priority, subordination, and allowance are separate.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, February 20, 1996; simulated decision, May 13, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 517 U.S. 535; Bankruptcy Code provisions and lower-court rulings described there.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Opinion of the Court by Justice Souter; unanimous judgment.
- **Historical Holding:** Bankruptcy courts may not use equitable subordination to adopt a categorical rule displacing Congress’s priority treatment of tax penalties.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 517 U.S. 535.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to revalidation.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Categorical equitable subordination

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision governs tax-penalty subordination. The source-bound remedial rules require equity to operate within enacted priority and authority.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No exact registered test. Ordinary bankruptcy law governs.
- **Other Governing Law:** 11 U.S.C. §§ 510(c), 726, and applicable tax-priority provisions; *Pepper v. Litton* and *Mobile Steel* principles.
- **Other Authority Used:** The absence of creditor misconduct and Congress’s treatment of penalty claims.
- **Open Point:** Case-specific subordination for inequitable conduct remains available but is not presented by this categorical rationale.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The penalty claim shall not be subordinated solely because it is punitive. The bankruptcy court may consider any preserved case-specific ground authorized by § 510(c).

#### Issue 1: Categorical equitable subordination

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches statutory authority.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Equitable subordination permits case-specific adjustment where a claimant’s inequitable conduct or another recognized equitable circumstance makes ordinary priority unjust. It does not authorize courts to create a categorical priority rule Congress omitted. The fact that a penalty burdens innocent creditors is a policy consequence common to the entire statutory class and therefore for Congress to address.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 510(c) does not permit categorical subordination of federal tax-penalty claims absent case-specific inequitable conduct or another recognized equitable basis.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The lower rule rewrites priority for every penalty claim and therefore functions as legislation rather than equity. Congress expressly addressed penalties in the Code. The IRS did nothing inequitable in asserting the lawful claim. A bankruptcy court may respond to misconduct, but it cannot subordinate a class because it considers the enacted distribution unwise.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide subordination based on creditor misconduct, insider abuse, fraud, or another supported case-specific ground.

# United Food & Commercial Workers Union Local 751 v. Brown Group, Inc.

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *United Food & Commercial Workers Union Local 751 v. Brown Group, Inc.*
- **Citation or Docket:** 517 U.S. 544; No. 95-340.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review.
- **Simulated Event Date:** May 13, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a union may sue in a representative capacity for members’ WARN Act damages when individual participation may be needed to calculate relief.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the third prong of associational standing is constitutional or prudential and whether Congress displaced it for WARN Act suits by unions.
- **Legal Field:** Article III standing, associational representation, labor law, and statutory enforcement.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eighth Circuit held that the union lacked representational standing because damages required individualized participation.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The union seeks reversal and adjudication of the WARN Act claim; Brown Group seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** The union alleged that Brown Group closed a facility without the sixty days’ notice required by the WARN Act and sought backpay and benefits for affected members. The members shared the alleged statutory violation, although individual payroll information would affect the amount due.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The members’ injury, causation, and redressability satisfy Article III if at least one member has a claim. The question is whether individual participation is constitutionally indispensable or a prudential limit Congress may alter.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, March 19, 1996; simulated decision, May 13, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 517 U.S. 544; WARN Act text and Eighth Circuit proceedings.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Opinion of the Court by Justice Souter; unanimous judgment.
- **Historical Holding:** The individual-participation prong is prudential, and Congress authorized unions to sue for members under the WARN Act.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 517 U.S. 544.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to revalidation.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Representative standing for statutory damages

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision alters the *Hunt* associational-standing framework.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No exact registered test. Article III standing and statutory authorization govern.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission*, *Warth v. Seldin*, and the WARN Act’s union-enforcement language.
- **Other Authority Used:** The common liability issue and payroll-based damages method.
- **Open Point:** Whether Congress clearly authorized union representation; the statutory text and structure indicate that it did.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The union may litigate liability and seek member relief. The district court may require individual proofs, subclasses, claims procedures, or direct distribution safeguards as necessary to calculate and deliver damages.

#### Issue 1: Representative standing for statutory damages

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves standing and statutory authorization, not WARN Act liability.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The first *Hunt* requirement, member injury, causation, and redressability, is constitutional. Germaneness supports genuine adverseness. The third requirement, that neither claim nor relief require individual participation, is an administrability rule and may yield to congressional authorization. Individual damages calculations do not destroy Article III when the association represents members who possess the claims.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The union has representative standing because its members have concrete WARN Act injuries, the claim is germane to union purposes, and Congress authorized union enforcement notwithstanding the need for individualized damages calculations.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Liability turns on a common closure and notice question. Payroll records can calculate relief through ordinary procedures. Requiring every worker to file a separate action would frustrate the enforcement structure Congress selected without serving an Article III function. Courts retain authority to ensure proof and distribution are accurate.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not authorize an association to recover for persons who are not members or injured, waive individual defenses, or dispense with proof of each claimant’s compensable loss.

# BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore*
- **Citation or Docket:** 517 U.S. 559; No. 94-896.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review.
- **Simulated Event Date:** May 20, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a $2 million punitive-damages award for nondisclosure of minor pre-sale repainting is so grossly excessive that it violates due process.
- **Question Granted:** What constitutional review applies to the amount and whether the Alabama judgment may punish conduct lawful in other States.
- **Legal Field:** Due process, punitive damages, interstate regulatory authority, and judicial review.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Alabama Supreme Court reduced a $4 million award to $2 million but otherwise affirmed.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** BMW seeks reversal or further reduction; Gore seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** BMW sold Gore a new automobile that had been repainted after minor transit damage. The economic loss was approximately $4,000. BMW followed a national nondisclosure policy for repair costs below a percentage of retail value; much of the conduct used to multiply punishment occurred in States where the nondisclosure was lawful. The jury imposed $4 million, later reduced to $2 million.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The federal due-process challenge is preserved. The Court reviews the constitutional ceiling, not Alabama tort liability or entitlement to some punitive award. Any comparison uses the record and lawful state interests, not a fixed national formula.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, October 11, 1995; simulated decision, May 20, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 517 U.S. 559; Alabama judgments and trial record described there.

#### Issue 1: Gross excessiveness and state punitive authority

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg* requires meaningful judicial review of punitive amounts and a procedure capable of correcting an award outside the rational range supported by the evidence; it creates no numerical ratio.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Meaningful judicial review of punitive-damages amounts.**
- **Other Governing Law:** The Due Process Clause, *Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Haslip*, *TXO Production Corp. v. Alliance Resources Corp.*, and principles limiting one State's punishment of lawful out-of-state conduct.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment of the Supreme Court of Alabama, leaving the remitted $2 million punitive award in force.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** No separate remedial instruction is required. The original $4 million verdict is not before the Court for restoration.

#### Issue 1: Gross excessiveness and state punitive authority

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the due-process amount and territorial questions.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies simulated *Honda*. Due process requires meaningful review capable of removing punishment based on legally impermissible considerations and correcting an amount outside the rational range supported by culpability and deterrence. Alabama supplied that review: its courts applied established excessiveness principles, reduced the verdict by half, and disclaimed punishment for lawful out-of-state transactions. The Constitution imposes no fixed punitive-to-compensatory ratio and does not require a mandatory three-guidepost formula. Evidence of BMW's nationwide policy may show deliberateness, knowledge, profitability, and the need for deterrence, even though Alabama may punish only conduct within its lawful territorial interest.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Due process is satisfied where a State provides meaningful judicial review, removes any punishment based on lawful out-of-state transactions, and sustains a punitive amount within the rational range supported by deliberate, profitable, hard-to-detect in-state fraud and the State's legitimate interest in punishment and deterrence. The Constitution imposes no fixed punitive-to-compensatory ratio, and the remitted $2 million award should stand.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** BMW maintained a deliberate nondisclosure policy, profited by selling repaired vehicles as new, and imposed modest individual losses that purchasers were unlikely to discover or litigate for compensatory damages alone. Those features permit a high ratio because compensation by itself could leave the scheme profitable. The Alabama Supreme Court performed the review simulated *Honda* requires and reduced the amount after rejecting punishment of transactions beyond Alabama's authority. The remaining award is severe, as punitive damages are intended to be, but the record does not show financial annihilation, punishment based solely on wealth, or continued use of lawful out-of-state sales as independent offenses. Federal due process does not authorize this Court to substitute its preferred number for the maximum a properly functioning state reviewing court found rational.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not authorize confiscatory or bankruptcy-producing punishment, treat wealth alone as culpability, permit a State to punish lawful conduct elsewhere, immunize an unsupported award, or revive the original $4 million verdict. An award based on forbidden conduct or outside any rational relation to culpability and deterrence remains subject to simulated *Honda* review.
