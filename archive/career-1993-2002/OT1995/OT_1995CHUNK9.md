# Settle v. Dickson County School Board

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Settle v. Dickson County School Board*
- **Citation or Docket:** 53 F.3d 152 (6th Cir. 1995).
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Granted merits review of a public-school curricular speech judgment.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 25, 1996, simulation-assigned opinion-delivery date.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a ninth-grade English teacher violated the First Amendment by refusing to approve a student research-paper topic concerning the life of Jesus and assigning no credit when the student did not submit a paper on an approved topic.
- **Question Granted:** May a public-school teacher reject a student’s proposed research-paper topic concerning the life of Jesus as an exercise of curricular and pedagogical authority, or does doing so violate the student’s First Amendment rights?
- **Legal Field:** Student speech, curricular authority, religious viewpoint discrimination, equal protection, municipal liability, and qualified immunity.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for the teacher, principal, superintendent, and School Board.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Settle seeks reversal and damages or other relief on her speech and equal-protection claims; respondents seek affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Dana Ramsey assigned a ninth-grade research paper designed to teach source gathering, synthesis, and objective exposition. Students had to obtain topic approval and use at least four sources. Settle first proposed a secular topic, then changed without permission to “The Life of Jesus Christ” and later proposed “A Scientific and Historical Approach to Jesus Christ.” Ramsey rejected the topic, citing its departure from the approved subject, concern that Settle intended an opinion paper rather than the assigned research exercise, grading and researchability concerns, and an erroneous view that public schools should avoid personal religious topics. Other students were permitted to research subjects including spiritualism, reincarnation, and magic.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The case arrives after summary judgment. Genuine factual disputes and reasonable inferences must be resolved for Settle, but unsupported assertions of pretext do not create a dispute. Individual liability, School Board liability, and qualified immunity require separate analysis. Any prospective claim may be affected by completion of the assignment and Settle's progression through school.
- **Relevant Dates:** Assignment and disputed grading, March 1991; Sixth Circuit decision, May 8, 1995; simulated decision, June 25, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Published Sixth Circuit opinions, 53 F.3d 152; district-court record as described there; *Tinker v. Des Moines Independent Community School District*, *Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier*, and *Epperson v. Arkansas*.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits decision. The Sixth Circuit's affirmance is the historical anchor.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Chief Judge Merritt wrote for the panel; Judge Batchelder concurred in the result and rejected several statements suggesting that religious subject matter is categorically unsuitable for public-school classrooms.
- **Historical Holding:** A teacher may control a curricular research assignment and reject a topic for legitimate pedagogical reasons without violating the student's First Amendment rights.
- **Historical Authority:** Lower-court historical anchor only.
- **Historical Sources:** 53 F.3d 152.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to revalidation through June 25, 1996.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any earlier rendered student-speech or school-religion decision must be incorporated before adjudication.

#### Issue 1: Curricular topic selection and religious viewpoint

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia* distinguishes neutral access from government sponsorship or financing. *Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette* and *Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller* protect private religious expression in genuinely open government forums. No Holdings Register decision governs a teacher's administration of a graded classroom assignment.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Equal civic access and direct financing of devotional activity** and **Private religious expression in an open government forum** establish that religion is not a disfavored viewpoint where government has opened an expressive channel. They do not remove educators' authority to define a curriculum and evaluate whether work satisfies an assignment.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Tinker*, *Hazelwood*, *Epperson*, *Board of Education v. Pico*, and the First Amendment.
- **Other Authority Used:** The assignment's stated research objectives, topic-approval procedure, source requirement, treatment of comparable topics, and the reasons actually relied upon by the teacher.
- **Open Point:** The simulated Court has not stated a reusable rule for religious-topic exclusions within a broad but graded curricular assignment.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** No separate remedial instruction is required. Because Stone finds no First Amendment violation on the granted question, the existing judgment for respondents remains in force. Stone gives no separate merits holding on municipal liability or qualified immunity.

#### Issue 1: Curricular topic selection and religious viewpoint

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the First Amendment question carried in the controlled grant and does not expand the case into a separate municipal-liability or qualified-immunity decision.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A graded classroom assignment is part of the curriculum rather than an open speech forum. A teacher may define its subject, method, source requirements, and evaluative criteria when the restrictions are reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical objectives. Religious expression may not be rejected merely because it is religious, and a teacher's mistaken belief that religious topics are categorically forbidden supplies no lawful justification. The judgment nevertheless may rest on independent, genuinely applied curricular reasons that would produce the same result for comparable secular work.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A public-school teacher does not violate the First Amendment by rejecting a proposed research-paper topic when the undisputed record shows that the student changed topics without approval and the teacher reasonably applied neutral curricular requirements concerning the assignment's research method and objectives, rather than rejecting the topic because it concerned Jesus or expressed a religious viewpoint.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The assignment required source-based synthesis, not personal profession or an opinion essay. Ramsey had approved a different topic and applied a preexisting approval rule. The record supplies no evidence that a compliant, objectively researched paper was submitted and rejected, or that Settle was denied credit while a materially comparable secular departure received credit. Ramsey's categorical statements about avoiding religion were legally wrong, but the record supports independent pedagogical grounds actually applied and does not establish that those grounds were pretextual.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A school may not exclude a religious perspective from an assignment that otherwise invites comparable student viewpoints, and it may not invoke curricular authority to conceal hostility toward religion. Stone does not approve Ramsey's categorical statements about religion in public education and does not announce a separate rule on municipal liability or qualified immunity.

# Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr; Lohr v. Medtronic, Inc.

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr; Lohr v. Medtronic, Inc.*
- **Citation or Docket:** 518 U.S. 470; Nos. 95-754 and 95-886; 56 F.3d 1335 (11th Cir. 1995).
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Consolidated granted merits cases concerning express federal preemption of medical-device tort claims.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 26, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether 21 U.S.C. § 360k(a) preempts Florida common-law design, manufacturing, labeling, warning, and strict-liability claims involving a Class III pacemaker lead marketed through the federal § 510(k) substantial-equivalence process.
- **Question Granted:** Whether common-law duties are state “requirements,” whether § 510(k) or general FDA regulations create device-specific federal requirements, and whether claims paralleling federal obligations survive.
- **Legal Field:** Express preemption, implied conflict preemption, medical-device regulation, agency implementation, negligence, and products liability.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit held the negligent-design claims not preempted but held manufacturing and failure-to-warn claims preempted by general FDA manufacturing and labeling rules.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Medtronic seeks preemption of the remaining design and strict-liability claims; the Lohrs seek restoration of their manufacturing and warning claims and remand for merits proceedings.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Lora Lohr depended on a pacemaker fitted with Medtronic's Model 4011 lead. The device entered the market after FDA found it substantially equivalent to a pre-1976 device through § 510(k), a process directed principally to equivalence rather than full premarket safety approval. The lead allegedly failed in 1990, causing complete heart block and emergency surgery. The Lohrs alleged defective design and manufacture, inadequate warning, negligence, and strict liability. The federal requirements relied upon below were general manufacturing and labeling provisions rather than device-specific conditions imposed through full premarket approval.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Both sides preserved the claim-specific preemption questions in petitions and cross-petitions. The Court should decide preemption by duty and federal counterpart, not determine tort liability or factual breach. Agency regulations interpreting § 360k are relevant only within delegated authority and according to their persuasiveness.
- **Relevant Dates:** Device implantation, 1987; alleged failure, December 30, 1990; Eleventh Circuit decision, 1995; argument, April 23, 1996; decision, June 26, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** No. 95-754, Medtronic's petition; No. 95-886, the Lohrs' cross-petition. The cases concern the same judgment and are consolidated for decision.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official opinions, 518 U.S. 470; 56 F.3d 1335; Medical Device Amendments of 1976; 21 U.S.C. § 360k; 21 C.F.R. §§ 808.1, 801.109, and 820.20 through 820.198 as then effective.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed in part, affirmed in part, and remanded, 5-4 overall.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens announced the Court's judgment and wrote the controlling opinion on the rejection of broad preemption; Justice Breyer concurred in part and in the judgment; Justice O'Connor concurred in part and dissented in part, joined by the Chief Justice and Justices Scalia and Thomas.
- **Historical Holding:** Section 360k(a) did not preempt the Lohrs' claims because § 510(k) and the general federal rules relied upon did not impose the specific device requirements necessary to displace the asserted state duties, and genuinely parallel claims were not “different from, or in addition to” federal law.
- **Historical Authority:** Majority holdings and judgment, with fractured reasoning on portions of the preemption analysis.
- **Historical Sources:** 518 U.S. 470.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to revalidation through June 26, 1996.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Scope of § 360k(a) express preemption

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick*, Questions decided: express preemption without a federal standard in force and implied conflict preemption. An express clause does not displace state requirements concerning an aspect of performance when no operative federal standard governs the same aspect, and nonregulation does not itself establish a federal policy requiring freedom from state law.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Express preemption without a federal standard in force** and **Implied conflict preemption**.
- **Other Governing Law:** 21 U.S.C. § 360k(a); the MDA's device-classification, premarket-approval, and § 510(k) provisions; 21 C.F.R. § 808.1; *Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc.* and *Rice v. Santa Fe Elevator Corp.*.
- **Other Authority Used:** The distinction between full premarket approval and substantial-equivalence review, and the specificity of the federal requirement asserted as the preemptive counterpart.
- **Open Point:** The exact degree of specificity required when a device is subject to a binding federal condition short of full premarket approval.

#### Issue 2: Parallel state duties and conflict preemption

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Myrick* rejects implied conflict preemption where compliance with both systems is possible and no operative federal policy requires the absence of state liability.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Implied conflict preemption**.
- **Other Governing Law:** Section 360k(a)'s “different from, or in addition to” language; ordinary impossibility and obstacle-preemption principles.
- **Other Authority Used:** The Lohrs' allegation that Medtronic violated duties equivalent to federal manufacturing and labeling obligations.
- **Open Point:** Whether each pleaded state duty genuinely parallels an enforceable federal requirement is a claim- and record-specific question for remand.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** In No. 95-754, affirm the rejection of preemption for the design claims. In No. 95-886, reverse the preemption judgment concerning manufacturing, labeling, warning, and parallel-duty claims. Remand both dockets.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Restore the Lohrs' complaint for claim-specific proceedings. The district court may dismiss a particular theory only if Medtronic identifies a binding federal requirement applicable to this device and the same aspect of performance, and demonstrates that the asserted state duty is different from, additional to, or impossible to satisfy together with that federal requirement. No tort liability is determined.

#### Issue 1: Scope of § 360k(a) express preemption

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches statutory preemption and does not decide breach, causation, defect, or damages.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 360k(a) preempts a state requirement only when a federal requirement actually applies to the particular device or relevant aspect of its design, manufacture, or labeling, the state rule concerns that same subject, and the state rule is different from or additional to the federal rule. Common-law duties may qualify as requirements, but their remedial form does not broaden the federal counterpart. A § 510(k) finding of substantial equivalence is not, without more, a device-specific safety approval or a federal command preserving the submitted design.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The § 510(k) process and the general manufacturing and labeling provisions identified here do not preempt the Lohrs' claims because they do not impose specific federal requirements governing the Model 4011 lead and the precise duties pleaded.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory text links preemption to a federal requirement “applicable” to the device and to a state requirement concerning the same regulated matter. Section 510(k) maintained market access based on equivalence and expressly did not endorse safety. General rules governing broad classes of manufacturers do not establish that federal law selected this lead's design, warning, or manufacturing details as an exclusive standard. Reading them as categorical preemption would turn transitional review into immunity Congress did not enact.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Full premarket approval or another binding, device-specific federal condition may preempt a genuinely conflicting state requirement. Stone does not decide that materially different setting.

#### Issue 2: Parallel state duties and conflict preemption

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches whether parallel duties are categorically barred and leaves pleading sufficiency and proof for remand.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A state duty that requires compliance with the same operative federal obligation is not “different from, or in addition to” federal law merely because state law supplies damages. Conflict preemption remains available when the plaintiff's rule would require conduct federal law forbids or would obstruct an identified federal objective, but the defendant must identify the conflict rather than rely on agency inaction or regulatory generality.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Genuinely parallel manufacturing and labeling claims survive § 360k(a), and the present record establishes no impossibility or federal policy requiring manufacturers to be free from those claims.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** A damages remedy can enforce rather than contradict the federal rule. The Lohrs may not use a federal violation as a label for a materially different state obligation, but neither may Medtronic transform compliance review into a shield against ordinary state duties. Claim-specific comparison is required.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not imply a federal private cause of action, alter state tort elements, or authorize a State to impose a requirement inconsistent with a device-specific federal command.

# United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States*
- **Citation or Docket:** 518 U.S. 515; Nos. 94-1941 and 94-2107.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Consolidated merits review after remand in an equal-protection action concerning Virginia Military Institute.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 26, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether Virginia's exclusion of women from VMI violates equal protection and, if so, whether the separate Virginia Women's Institute for Leadership at Mary Baldwin College supplies a constitutionally adequate remedy.
- **Question Granted:** Whether Virginia has established an exceedingly persuasive justification for the sex classification and whether VMI and VWIL provide genuinely equal opportunities and benefits.
- **Legal Field:** Equal protection, sex classification, public higher education, institutional design, and constitutional remedies.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** After an earlier finding of a violation and remedial remand, the Fourth Circuit affirmed approval of VWIL as a substantively comparable separate program while allowing VMI to remain male only.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The United States seeks reversal and a remedy ending the exclusion; Virginia seeks affirmance of its separate-program solution and preservation of VMI's male-only admissions policy.
- **Material Facts and Record:** VMI is Virginia's only public single-sex institution and uses an intensive adversative system to train citizen-soldiers. It offers unusual resources, prestige, alumni support, military structure, and professional networks. Virginia created VWIL at a private women's college after litigation began. VWIL did not replicate VMI's adversative method, military environment, faculty profile, facilities, course breadth, endowment, degree reputation, or alumni network. The record showed that some women were qualified for and desired VMI's program, even though average preferences and physical characteristics differed between groups.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Liability and remedy are both properly before the Court. The Court must evaluate Virginia's actual justification rather than a post hoc rationale and must compare the opportunities as they operate. Justice Thomas did not participate historically because his son attended VMI.
- **Relevant Dates:** Initial district-court judgment, 1991; first Fourth Circuit decision, 1992; VWIL remedial proceedings, 1993 through 1995; argument, January 17, 1996; decision, June 26, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** No. 94-1941, the United States' petition; No. 94-2107, Virginia's cross-petition.
- **Known Participation Matter:** Justice Thomas historically did not participate. The simulation should apply its current participation state at adjudication.
- **Public Sources:** Official opinions, 518 U.S. 515; Fourth Circuit and district-court opinions described there; VMI and VWIL records; Equal Protection Clause.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 7-1; Justice Thomas did not participate.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Ginsburg wrote for the Court; the Chief Justice concurred in the judgment; Justice Scalia dissented.
- **Historical Holding:** Virginia's categorical exclusion of women from VMI lacked an exceedingly persuasive justification, and VWIL did not provide an equal remedy.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 518 U.S. 515.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to revalidation through June 26, 1996.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Any rendered *Klinger* holding concerning comparison, burdens, or institutional differences must be incorporated.

#### Issue 1: Constitutionality of VMI's male-only admissions policy

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B.* prohibits purposeful sex classification based on stereotype. *Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois* permits sex-conscious restructuring only when government establishes an actual important objective and a substantial, nonstereotyped relationship between the choice and that objective.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Sex discrimination in peremptory challenges** and **Equal protection and sex-conscious restructuring**. Government bears the justification for an express sex classification; intermediate scrutiny requires an actual important objective and a substantial relationship.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Craig v. Boren*, *Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan*, *Weinberger v. Wiesenfeld*, and the Fourteenth Amendment.
- **Other Authority Used:** VMI's purpose and history, the fit between exclusion and the adversative method, evidence concerning individual capacity, and experience at military academies.
- **Open Point:** Whether the Court should describe the existing intermediate-scrutiny demand as requiring an “exceedingly persuasive justification” without converting it into a new tier.

#### Issue 2: Adequacy of VWIL as a remedy

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Missouri v. Jenkins*, Questions decided: violation-remedy nexus and incremental withdrawal of control, requires a remedy directed to the identified violation and no broader than necessary. The remedy must eliminate the legally cognizable consequence of exclusion to the practicable extent.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Violation-remedy nexus and interdistrict attraction** and **Achievement benchmarks and incremental withdrawal of control**, applied for remedial fit rather than school-desegregation subject matter.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Milliken v. Bradley*, *Green v. County School Board*, *Sweatt v. Painter*, and ordinary equitable principles.
- **Other Authority Used:** Tangible and intangible differences in curriculum, method, resources, prestige, networks, and degree value.
- **Open Point:** The exact institutional means Virginia may choose on remand if it does not admit women to VMI.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm liability, reverse approval of VWIL, and remand for a constitutionally adequate remedy.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Virginia must end the constitutional violation. It may admit qualified women to VMI or adopt another lawful system that provides genuinely equal public educational opportunities and benefits without preserving the challenged sex-based exclusion. The receiving court shall assess the remedy in operation and retain only the supervision necessary to secure compliance.

#### Issue 1: Constitutionality of VMI's male-only admissions policy

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the equal-protection merits. The express sex classification and the completed remedial record present no threshold obstacle.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Sex classifications receive intermediate scrutiny. Virginia bears persuasion that the exclusion serves an actual important objective and is substantially related to that objective. The justification must be genuine, must address the classification actually used, and may not rest on overbroad averages or predictions that deny qualified individuals opportunity because of their sex. The phrase “exceedingly persuasive justification” describes the rigor of this received standard; it does not create a separate tier.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Virginia's categorical exclusion of women from VMI violates equal protection because the asserted diversity and pedagogical interests do not explain or justify denying every qualified woman access to the Commonwealth's unique program.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Educational diversity can be important, and single-sex education is not unconstitutional as a category. Virginia did not establish that VMI's male-only design was adopted or maintained as one evenhanded component of a genuine range of public options. The adversative method may require sex-aware accommodations in housing or physical standards, but the record does not show that the method is inherently male or that admitting qualified women would defeat its objective. Group tendencies cannot establish the incapacity of every individual excluded.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that every single-sex public program is invalid. A State may maintain such programs when the classification satisfies intermediate scrutiny and equal opportunity is real rather than nominal.

#### Issue 2: Adequacy of VWIL as a remedy

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches remedy because the separate program is the basis of the judgment under review.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A remedy must place the excluded class, as nearly as practicable, in the position it would have occupied absent the violation. Formal availability of a different program is insufficient when the government preserves the challenged institution and offers materially inferior educational, professional, and civic benefits to the excluded group.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** VWIL does not cure VMI's exclusion because it is unequal in method, resources, institutional stature, degree value, and alumni opportunity, and it withholds the specific public program denied on account of sex.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The violation was not the absence of any leadership education for women. It was exclusion from the distinctive opportunity Virginia supplied through VMI. VWIL's different pedagogy may have independent merit, but it does not reproduce the experience, resources, and networks the State continued to reserve for men. A remedial comparison must include those operational and intangible features, not only a list of course titles.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not prescribe every implementation detail or require identical treatment where genuine sex-related physical differences make tailored arrangements necessary. He requires equality of opportunity and benefit under a lawful design.

# Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. Federal Election Commission

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. Federal Election Commission*
- **Citation or Docket:** 518 U.S. 604; No. 95-489; 59 F.3d 1015 (10th Cir. 1995).
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Granted merits review of a federal campaign-expenditure limitation.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 26, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether the Party Expenditure Provision of the Federal Election Campaign Act may constitutionally limit a political party's expenditure made independently of any candidate.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Colorado Party's pre-nomination advertising was independent or coordinated, whether § 441a(d)(3) may be applied to it, and whether the broader facial challenge should be decided.
- **Legal Field:** First Amendment, political-party speech and association, campaign finance, independent expenditures, coordination, and facial review.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Tenth Circuit held that the expenditure fell within the statutory limit and that the limit was constitutional, reversing summary judgment for the Colorado Party.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The Colorado Party seeks reversal and facial or as-applied invalidation; the FEC seeks affirmance and enforcement of the limit.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Before the Republican Party selected its 1986 Colorado Senate nominee, the state party's federal campaign committee purchased radio advertisements criticizing the likely Democratic nominee. The summary-judgment record contained uncontroverted evidence that the party developed and placed the advertisements without consultation, coordination, or agreement with a candidate. The FEC treated the spending as subject to the party expenditure ceiling and argued more broadly that party spending is inherently coordinated.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The as-applied challenge is squarely presented on a developed independence record. The facial challenge to limits on coordinated party expenditures was pleaded, but the lower courts did not develop the factual, statutory, or anticorruption questions needed to resolve it. The Court should not equate party identity with candidate coordination as a jurisdictional or categorical matter.
- **Relevant Dates:** Expenditure, 1986; Tenth Circuit decision, 1995; argument, April 15, 1996; decision, June 26, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official opinions, 518 U.S. 604; 59 F.3d 1015; 2 U.S.C. §§ 441a(a)(7)(B)(i) and 441a(d)(3) as then effective; FEC regulations and advisory materials in the record.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded, 7-2.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Breyer announced the judgment in a plurality opinion joined by Justices O'Connor and Souter; Justices Kennedy and Thomas wrote opinions supporting broader invalidation, with the Chief Justice and Justice Scalia joining material portions; Justices Stevens and Ginsburg dissented.
- **Historical Holding:** The party's expenditure was independent and could not constitutionally be limited as a contribution; the broader facial question concerning coordinated party spending was left for remand.
- **Historical Authority:** Majority-supported as-applied holding; no single opinion controlled every rationale.
- **Historical Sources:** 518 U.S. 604.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to revalidation through June 26, 1996.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Limit on an independent political-party expenditure

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission* protects independent core political advocacy against broad identification burdens not fitted to fraud or corruption. *Day v. Holahan* holds that the First Amendment protects an independent speaker against governmental suppression of that speaker's advocacy, while allowing lawful responsive public financing that imposes no disability on the speaker.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Anonymous political leaflets** and **Responsive financing and the independent speaker**. Independent advocacy may not be burdened without a constitutionally sufficient interest and fit; corruption analysis must track actual coordination or another legally relevant mechanism.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Buckley v. Valeo*, *Federal Election Commission v. National Conservative Political Action Committee*, *Federal Election Commission v. Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee*, FECA, and the First Amendment.
- **Other Authority Used:** The uncontroverted independence evidence and the absence of findings that independent party spending creates quid pro quo corruption.
- **Open Point:** The standard governing coordinated party expenditures is not necessary to decide the as-applied claim.

#### Issue 2: Facial challenge to coordinated party-spending limits

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register case decides whether a political party's coordinated spending is constitutionally identical to the candidate's own expenditure or may be limited as an in-kind contribution.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Responsive financing and the independent speaker** confirms that the legal disability imposed on the speaker matters. It does not decide coordination.
- **Other Governing Law:** FECA's coordination provisions, *Buckley*, and ordinary facial-challenge principles.
- **Other Authority Used:** The underdeveloped record concerning party-candidate relationships, circumvention, contribution limits, and administrable definitions of coordination.
- **Open Point:** Whether and to what extent Congress may limit truly coordinated party expenditures remains open.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand with judgment that the challenged expenditure may not be treated as coordinated on party status alone.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Enter judgment for the Colorado Party on the FEC's claim concerning the challenged independent advertisements. Restore the facial counterclaim for lower-court consideration of coordinated expenditures under a developed record and a precise construction of the statute.

#### Issue 1: Limit on an independent political-party expenditure

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the as-applied First Amendment claim. The undisputed summary-judgment record permits classification of this expenditure as independent.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Independent political advocacy by a party is core political speech and association. It may not be treated as a contribution merely because the speaker is a political party. Government must prove coordination, agreement, or another legally sufficient connection that makes the expenditure a candidate-controlled transfer or a vehicle for circumvention. Organizational sympathy and common electoral goals do not establish that connection.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 441a(d)(3) is unconstitutional as applied to the Colorado Party's independently developed, pre-nomination advertisements because the expenditure was not coordinated and the Government established no corruption interest sufficient to limit it.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The advertisements expressed the party's own view before it had a nominee. The FEC identified no communication, agreement, candidate control, or empirical basis for treating all party advocacy as coordinated. Converting identity of political interest into legal coordination would erase the independent-expenditure category precisely for the association organized to speak about elections.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Disclosure and reporting rules, precise anti-coordination rules, and limits on actual contributions remain governed by their own standards.

#### Issue 2: Facial challenge to coordinated party-spending limits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone declines to decide the broader facial claim because the as-applied judgment is sufficient and the lower courts did not develop the necessary record or statutory construction.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The Court should not assume that every coordinated party expenditure is constitutionally regulable as a contribution, but it also should not decide the question without determining what conduct counts as coordination and what corruption or circumvention interest the limit serves.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The facial challenge remains open for adjudication on remand and receives no merits holding at this stage.

National Railroad Passenger Corp.* treats government-created and controlled institutions as governmental for constitutional rights. *Capitol Square* and *Chabad-Lubavitch* make attribution depend on forum structure, control, sponsorship, and actual design.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Government-created and controlled corporations**, **Private religious expression in an open government forum**, and **Attribution of private religious displays inside government buildings**, used for attribution and forum operation.
- **Other Governing Law:** Public-forum doctrine, *Sable*, *Perry Education Association*, local franchise law, and the First Amendment.
- **Other Authority Used:** The government's creation of public-access channels, historic absence of operator editorial control, and the delegation of content-based veto power.
- **Open Point:** The exact public-forum classification may vary with a franchise agreement, but § 10(c)'s categorical delegation fails under any materially protective standard on this record.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment upholding § 10(a). Reverse the judgment upholding §§ 10(b) and 10(c). Remand for entry of provision-specific relief.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The FCC may implement § 10(a) consistent with this holding and as-applied constitutional review. Enforcement of §§ 10(b) and 10(c) shall be enjoined. The lower court may sever the invalid provisions and preserve lawful subscriber-controlled blocking, identification, and channel-management rules.

#### Issue 1: Section 10(a), leased-access operator discretion

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the facial challenge and does not decide every operator policy or program-specific exclusion.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 10(a) withdraws part of a statutory access entitlement and restores limited editorial choice to private cable operators. A programmer has no freestanding constitutional right to compel a private operator to carry content on a leased channel. The operator's choice is not attributable to government merely because federal law permits it, provided government does not command, coerce, jointly administer, or selectively enforce the decision. The written-policy and reasonable-belief requirements constrain the statutory permission and provide a basis for ordinary review.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 10(a) is facially constitutional because it permits private operators to exercise limited editorial discretion over federally created leased access and does not itself prohibit protected programming.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The First Amendment protects both programmers and operators, but the programmer's access here exists because Congress restricted the operator's ordinary editorial control. Allowing the operator to decline a narrow category does not convert a private editorial decision into federal censorship. Child protection reinforces the permissibility of the limited restoration but is not the source of operator authority.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A policy adopted under government pressure, administered as a joint public-private censorship program, or used to suppress viewpoints outside the statutory category may present a different case.

#### Issue 2: Section 10(b), mandatory segregation and blocking

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the facial burden because the statute directly commands the segregation and access procedure.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Protected indecent speech may be channeled to protect children only when adults retain realistic access and the restriction is fitted to the harm. A requirement that all covered programming be placed on a stigmatized blocked channel and made available only after advance written request and delay burdens adult viewing, programmer reach, and household privacy substantially more than necessary where prompt subscriber-controlled blocking is available.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 10(b) violates the First Amendment because its mandatory segregation and written-request regime is not narrowly fitted to child protection and imposes avoidable barriers on adults' access to protected programming.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** A viewer cannot obtain an occasional program without planning, disclosure, and delay. Grouping diverse protected material on one channel predictably reduces audience and labels the subscriber. Existing lockboxes and related technology allow households to block unwanted programming without requiring all adults to identify themselves before receiving it. The Government did not establish why those measures were inadequate.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not invalidate prompt, confidential, user-controlled blocking or neutral program information that permits household choice.

#### Issue 3: Section 10(c), public-access operator veto

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the facial challenge. Whether every public-access channel has identical forum status is unnecessary because the federal delegation authorizes content exclusion without sufficient fit or safeguards.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** When local government creates a public-access channel as an expressive forum and federal law delegates to the private operator a content-based power to override that access, the operator exercises legally operative authority attributable to government for that decision. A broad veto over protected programming requires a compelling, record-supported justification and narrow tailoring. Child protection is compelling, but the Government must use less restrictive household controls when they can address exposure without eliminating speech from the forum.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 10(c) violates the First Amendment because it delegates an insufficiently constrained content-based veto over public-access programming and is not narrowly tailored to the demonstrated risk to children.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Public-access channels were created to carry community expression outside the operator's ordinary editorial selection. Section 10(c) does not merely restore a private baseline; it authorizes the operator to defeat the public access government established. The record did not show a problem on public-access channels sufficient to justify the veto, and subscriber blocking provides a more direct response to household exposure.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Local authorities may adopt lawful, precise channeling rules and may address obscenity, true threats, unlawful exploitation, or other unprotected material under the governing doctrines.

# United States v. Winstar Corp.

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *United States v. Winstar Corp.*
- **Citation or Docket:** 518 U.S. 839; No. 95-865; 64 F.3d 1531 (Fed. Cir. 1995) (en banc).
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Granted merits review of federal contractual liability following a statutory change in thrift-capital rules.
- **Simulated Event Date:** July 1, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether the United States is liable for breach when federal banking agencies induced private parties to acquire failing thrifts by promising specified treatment of supervisory goodwill and capital credits, and Congress later enacted FIRREA, which made that treatment unavailable.
- **Question Granted:** Whether enforceable contracts existed, whether the Government allocated the risk of regulatory change, and whether the unmistakability, reserved-powers, sovereign-acts, or impossibility doctrines bar damages.
- **Legal Field:** Government contracts, sovereign acts, regulatory change, statutory authority, unmistakability, impossibility, and damages.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The en banc Federal Circuit affirmed liability to Winstar, Statesman, and Glendale, holding that the Government had contracted for favorable accounting treatment and breached when FIRREA eliminated it.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The United States seeks reversal and judgment against contractual liability; respondents seek affirmance and damages proceedings.
- **Material Facts and Record:** During the savings-and-loan crisis, FSLIC and the Federal Home Loan Bank Board encouraged healthy institutions and investors to acquire failing thrifts. Written agreements and contemporaneous approvals allowed acquiring institutions to count supervisory goodwill and certain capital credits toward regulatory capital over defined periods. The acquisitions would have produced immediate regulatory insolvency without that treatment. FIRREA later imposed new capital rules that disallowed or rapidly phased out the promised treatment. Respondents sought damages rather than an injunction preventing Congress or regulators from applying the new law.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Liability was decided on stipulated or adjudicated contracts; damages remained for further proceedings. The Court must distinguish interpretation of the promises, agency authority to contract, legislative power to change law, and the financial consequence of breach. No party seeks to invalidate FIRREA or compel the Government to continue the superseded regulatory regime.
- **Relevant Dates:** Supervisory mergers and assistance agreements, 1980s; FIRREA enacted August 9, 1989; Federal Circuit en banc judgment, 1995; argument, April 24, 1996; decision, July 1, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** Winstar, Statesman, and Glendale claims were consolidated for the liability judgment.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official opinions, 518 U.S. 839; 64 F.3d 1531; transaction agreements and regulatory approvals described there; FIRREA.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 7-2.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Souter announced the judgment in a plurality opinion joined in substantial part by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, and Breyer; Justice Breyer concurred; Justice Scalia concurred in the judgment, joined by Justices Kennedy and Thomas; the Chief Justice dissented, joined in material parts by Justice Ginsburg.
- **Historical Holding:** The Government expressly promised the accounting treatment and was liable in damages when FIRREA prevented performance; sovereign defenses did not excuse the breach.
- **Historical Authority:** Majority judgment with multiple rationales supporting contractual liability.
- **Historical Sources:** 518 U.S. 839.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to revalidation through July 1, 1996.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Contract formation, authority, and allocation of regulatory-change risk

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register case decides supervisory-goodwill contracts. *MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T* holds that an agency may not transform bounded statutory authority into power to eliminate a central statutory regime; that authority principle requires identification of the agencies' enacted contracting and assistance powers here.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No exact government-contract title. **Scope of the power to modify tariff-filing requirements** is relevant only for the separate proposition that agency authority must come from enacted law and cannot be manufactured by necessity.
- **Other Governing Law:** The Financial Institutions Reform, Recovery, and Enforcement Act; statutes authorizing FSLIC assistance and Bank Board approvals; ordinary federal contract law; *United States v. Utah, Nevada & California Stage Co.*; *Perry v. United States*.
- **Other Authority Used:** The assistance agreements, resolutions, forbearance letters, accounting schedules, transaction necessity, and contemporaneous agency representations.
- **Open Point:** The precise contractual document or integrated set of documents establishing each respondent's promise is transaction specific.

#### Issue 2: Unmistakability, sovereign acts, and impossibility

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc.* confirms that Congress may change substantive law for pending and future matters while respecting independent constitutional limits. No register case decides whether legal change excuses the Government's own contractual liability.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Legislative reopening of final Article III judgments**, relevant only to separation of legislative power from the consequences of prior government commitments.
- **Other Governing Law:** The unmistakability doctrine, sovereign-acts doctrine, reserved-powers principles, *Horowitz v. United States*, *Omnia Commercial Co. v. United States*, and ordinary impossibility and risk-allocation rules.
- **Other Authority Used:** Respondents' request for damages rather than continued regulatory treatment and the foreseeability of capital-rule change.
- **Open Point:** The opinions historically differed over whether unmistakability applied and why the sovereign-acts defense failed, but the common liability proposition can be stated narrowly.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm liability and remand for contract-specific causation and damages.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Return the cases for contract-specific damages proceedings. FIRREA remains fully effective. Respondents may recover only losses proved under ordinary contract causation, foreseeability, mitigation, and damages rules.

#### Issue 1: Contract formation, authority, and allocation of regulatory-change risk

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches liability and leaves damages amount for further proceedings.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The United States is bound when authorized officials enter an express contract supported by consideration and the integrated agreement reasonably commits the Government to specified regulatory accounting treatment for a defined period. A promise may allocate the financial risk of future legal change without disabling Congress from changing the law. Authority to provide assistance and approve merger terms includes authority to make the accounting treatment part of the bargain when that treatment is lawful at formation and essential to the transaction.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The transaction documents and circumstances establish enforceable promises that respondents could count supervisory goodwill and capital credits as specified, and those promises allocated to the Government the risk that later law would prevent the promised treatment.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The agencies sought acquisitions they could not otherwise obtain and understood that the merged institutions would fail capital requirements immediately without the promised accounting. Respondents assumed failing-thrift liabilities in exchange. Treating the documents as merely describing current law would deprive the central inducement of contractual work. The statutory assistance powers supplied authority; the agreements did not purport to bind Congress or prohibit regulation.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Government officials cannot create authority by promise, and not every regulatory statement or forbearance is contractual. The holding depends on authorized, bargained-for commitments proved in these transactions.

#### Issue 2: Unmistakability, sovereign acts, and impossibility

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the sovereign defenses because they are necessary to the Government's requested judgment.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The unmistakability doctrine protects sovereign authority against an inferred surrender. It does not require special words before the Government may assume damages liability for nonperformance while retaining full power to legislate. The sovereign-acts doctrine places the Government as contractor on comparable footing with a private party when a public and general act prevents performance; it does not excuse a risk the contract assigned to the Government. Impossibility fails when the supervening legal change was foreseeable and the agreement's function was to allocate that very risk.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** FIRREA lawfully changed capital regulation, but it did not erase the Government's contractual duty to compensate respondents for breach; the unmistakability, reserved-powers, sovereign-acts, and impossibility doctrines do not bar damages on these agreements.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Respondents seek no injunction against sovereign action. Damages acknowledge that Congress remained free to legislate while preserving the legal consequence of an authorized bargain. Regulatory change in the thrift field was foreseeable, and the promised multi-year treatment addressed that uncertainty. The Government cannot accept the acquisition benefit and then use its own legal change as an automatic release from the allocated risk.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that every government contract insures against later legislation or that an agency may bargain away a constitutional or nondelegable sovereign power.

# Pennsylvania v. Labron; Pennsylvania v. Kilgore

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Pennsylvania v. Labron; Pennsylvania v. Kilgore*
- **Citation or Docket:** 518 U.S. 938; Nos. 95-1691 and 95-1738.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Consolidated summary merits disposition of state suppression judgments.
- **Simulated Event Date:** July 1, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether the federal automobile exception requires a separate showing of exigent circumstances beyond probable cause and a vehicle's ready mobility.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Pennsylvania Supreme Court applied an erroneous Fourth Amendment rule in suppressing cocaine found in Labron's car and Kilgore's truck, and whether the judgments rest on adequate and independent state grounds.
- **Legal Field:** Fourth Amendment, automobile exception, probable cause, state-ground jurisdiction, suppression, and remand.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Pennsylvania Supreme Court suppressed evidence in both cases after requiring exigent circumstances beyond probable cause for a warrantless vehicle search.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Pennsylvania seeks reversal and reinstatement of the prosecutions with the evidence available under federal law; respondents seek affirmance or dismissal for lack of jurisdiction.
- **Material Facts and Record:** In Labron, officers observed street drug transactions and found cocaine in the trunk of his car. In Kilgore, officers conducting a drug raid at his home searched his truck and found cocaine. The state court accepted that probable cause existed in both cases but treated a separate exigency as constitutionally necessary. The Labron opinion referred to federal and state automobile cases without clearly stating an independent state-law ground; Kilgore expressly rested on the Fourth Amendment.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Supreme Court jurisdiction must be resolved docket by docket. A clearly independent state constitutional ground would bar review, but an interwoven or unclear judgment is reviewable under *Michigan v. Long*. Reversal on the federal question leaves the state court free to decide any properly preserved independent state-law claim and any other suppression issue.
- **Relevant Dates:** Pennsylvania Supreme Court judgments, 1995; decision, July 1, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** No. 95-1691, Labron; No. 95-1738, Kilgore. The cases share the federal rule but retain separate state records and judgments.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official per curiam opinion and dissent, 518 U.S. 938; Pennsylvania Supreme Court opinions; search records described there.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Certiorari granted, judgments reversed, and cases remanded, 7-2.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Per curiam Opinion of the Court; Justice Stevens dissented, joined by Justice Ginsburg.
- **Historical Holding:** The federal automobile exception requires probable cause and ready mobility, not a separate exigency; the Court had jurisdiction because the state grounds were interwoven or expressly federal.
- **Historical Authority:** Per curiam Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 518 U.S. 938.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to revalidation through July 1, 1996.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Incorporate any effective *Ornelas* or *Whren* proposition relevant to probable cause, search scope, or pretext.

#### Issue 1: Supreme Court jurisdiction over the state judgments

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Arizona v. Evans*, Question decided: federal review when state and federal grounds are interwoven. The Court may review a state judgment that fairly appears to rest on federal law or state law interwoven with the federal question when no adequate and independent state ground is clearly stated.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Federal review when state and federal grounds are interwoven**.
- **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. § 1257(a); *Michigan v. Long*; adequate-and-independent-state-ground principles.
- **Other Authority Used:** The text and citations of the two Pennsylvania opinions.
- **Open Point:** Any independent state constitutional disposition on remand remains for the Pennsylvania courts.

#### Issue 2: Federal automobile exception

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *United States v. Wellons* recognizes privacy interests in vehicles and luggage while stating that the automobile exception may authorize container searches when its own requirements are met. No Holdings Register case adds a separate exigency requirement.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Privacy in a rental vehicle** and **Independent privacy in closed luggage**, limited to the proposition that standing and container privacy do not displace lawful automobile-exception authority.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Carroll v. United States*, *Chambers v. Maroney*, *United States v. Ross*, *California v. Carney*, *California v. Acevedo*, and the Fourth Amendment.
- **Other Authority Used:** The conceded probable cause, vehicle mobility, and scope of the searches in each record.
- **Open Point:** Any distinct issue concerning ownership, scope, or state-law protection remains open on remand.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand in Nos. 95-1691 and 95-1738.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The Pennsylvania Supreme Court shall reconsider both suppression judgments without requiring a separate exigency beyond probable cause and ready mobility under the Fourth Amendment. It may decide any preserved independent state constitutional ground, scope issue, or other suppression question not resolved here.

#### Issue 1: Supreme Court jurisdiction over the state judgments

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves jurisdiction first and finds review available in both dockets.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A state court may provide greater protection under its own constitution, but it must clearly identify an adequate and independent state ground if its judgment is to be insulated from federal review. A decision that relies on federal automobile doctrine or expressly finds a Fourth Amendment violation is reviewable when the independence of state law is not clear.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Court has jurisdiction because Labron's state and federal analysis is interwoven and Kilgore's judgment expressly rests on the Fourth Amendment.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Labron cites and applies federal cases as part of the asserted rule without a clear statement that state law alone controls. Kilgore directly declares the federal search unconstitutional. The Court therefore may correct the federal rule while leaving state courts sovereign over a distinctly stated state-law ground.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone expresses no view on the proper construction of the Pennsylvania Constitution.

#### Issue 2: Federal automobile exception

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the federal warrant question. Probable cause is accepted for present purposes; he does not make new factual findings.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** When officers have probable cause to believe a readily mobile vehicle contains contraband or evidence, the Fourth Amendment permits a warrantless search of the vehicle and containers within the probable-cause scope. Ready mobility supplies the exigency recognized by the exception; the Constitution does not require a second case-specific showing that the vehicle was about to depart or evidence was about to disappear.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Pennsylvania Supreme Court applied an erroneous federal rule by requiring exigent circumstances in addition to probable cause for the searches of Labron's car and Kilgore's truck.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The exception rests on the practical mobility of vehicles and the reduced expectation associated with their pervasive regulation. Once probable cause exists, seizing and holding the vehicle pending a warrant is not constitutionally less intrusive than searching it promptly. The search remains limited by the object of probable cause; the exception is not a general evidence-gathering license.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not authorize searches of unrelated digital accounts, property outside the vehicle, or containers beyond the probable-cause scope. State law may impose a stricter warrant rule if clearly and independently grounded.
