# Pinder v. Johnson

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Pinder v. Johnson*
- **Citation or Docket:** 54 F.3d 1169 (4th Cir. 1995) (en banc), No. 93-2125.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Granted merits review of an interlocutory qualified-immunity judgment.
- **Simulated Event Date:** May 21, 1996, simulation-assigned opinion-delivery date.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a police officer may be held liable under § 1983 for failing to protect a woman and her children from a private assailant after arresting the assailant, assuring the woman that he would be held overnight, charging only minor offenses, and permitting his prompt release.
- **Question Granted:** Does the Due Process Clause impose an affirmative duty to protect when a police officer, through affirmative conduct or assurances, leaves an identified person more vulnerable to a known private danger, and what distinguishes such state-created danger from the no-duty rule of *DeShaney*?
- **Legal Field:** Substantive due process, state-created danger, third-party violence, and qualified immunity.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The en banc Fourth Circuit reversed the district court’s denial of summary judgment and held Johnson entitled to qualified immunity because no asserted affirmative duty was clearly established in March 1989.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Pinder seeks reversal and continuation of her damages action; Johnson seeks affirmance of the immunity judgment.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Pinder’s former boyfriend Pittman broke into her home, assaulted her, threatened to kill her and her children, and had recently been released after serving time for attempting to burn the same home. Johnson arrested Pittman and told Pinder that he would be held overnight, inducing her to return to work. Johnson nevertheless charged only misdemeanor trespass and property damage; Pittman was released on recognizance, returned to the house, set it afire, and killed Pinder’s three sleeping children.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The appeal is interlocutory but properly presents the legal qualified-immunity question on Pinder’s version of the facts. Under the governing collateral-order limits, factual sufficiency is not independently reweighed. The Court may resolve whether the asserted right was clearly established without announcing a broader affirmative-duty rule unnecessary to the immunity judgment.
- **Relevant Dates:** Conduct, March 10, 1989; en banc decision, May 30, 1995; simulated decision, May 21, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** Claims against the municipality and state-law claims are not within this immunity appeal.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** 54 F.3d 1169; district and panel proceedings described in the en banc opinion; 42 U.S.C. § 1983.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits decision; certiorari was denied. The en banc Fourth Circuit judgment is the historical anchor.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Wilkinson wrote the principal en banc opinion; several judges concurred separately; Judge Russell dissented for four judges.
- **Historical Holding:** No clearly established due-process rule imposed the asserted affirmative duty, so Johnson received qualified immunity.
- **Historical Authority:** Lower-court historical anchor.
- **Historical Sources:** 54 F.3d 1169; denial of certiorari, 516 U.S. 994.

#### Issue 1: Affirmative state-created danger and qualified immunity

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Elder v. Holloway* requires consideration of the complete body of materially relevant law when determining whether a right was clearly established. *DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services* remains surviving authority that the Due Process Clause ordinarily imposes no affirmative duty to protect a person from private violence absent custody or another recognized source.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Qualified-immunity review uses the complete body of law**; **Evidence-sufficiency disputes in qualified-immunity appeals** confines interlocutory review to separable legal questions on assumed facts; **Specific constitutional source and substantive-due-process nonduplication** requires the asserted injury to be assigned to its proper constitutional source.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Harlow v. Fitzgerald*, *Anderson v. Creighton*, *DeShaney*, and pre-March 1989 affirmative-duty cases.
- **Other Authority Used:** Johnson's knowledge of Pittman's specific threat, the assurance of overnight detention, Pinder's alleged reliance, Johnson's release decision, and the causal sequence assumed at summary judgment.
- **Open Point:** Current law does not resolve whether an official's affirmative inducement and use of state authority to increase a specific danger falls outside *DeShaney*'s no-general-duty rule.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Fourth Circuit's judgment granting Officer Johnson qualified immunity.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The federal damages claim against Johnson ends. The judgment does not dispose of independently viable state-law claims or claims against another defendant outside the appeal.

#### Issue 1: Affirmative state-created danger and qualified immunity

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone decides the constitutional merits before qualified immunity on the assumed facts, then resolves Johnson's personal immunity separately.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** *DeShaney* bars a general due-process duty to rescue a person from private violence. Stone would recognize a narrow exception where an official knows of a specific and imminent violent threat, gives a concrete assurance that foreseeably induces the threatened person to abandon available self-protection, then affirmatively uses official authority in deliberate indifference to make that person materially more vulnerable, and thereby causes the threatened harm. This rule addresses the official's affirmative creation or intensification of vulnerability, not the private violence standing alone. The rule was not sufficiently apparent in March 1989 to support personal damages against Johnson.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Due process is violated when an official with knowledge of a specific imminent threat affirmatively induces a person to forego self-protection through a concrete assurance, then uses official authority in deliberate indifference to make that person materially more vulnerable to the threatened private violence, and the action causes the harm. That rule was not sufficiently established in March 1989 to defeat Officer Johnson's qualified immunity.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** On the assumed facts, Johnson knew of Pittman's specific threat, assured Pinder that Pittman would remain detained overnight, and then took official action resulting in release after that assurance allegedly caused Pinder to forego other protective steps. That affirmative sequence materially differs from a failure to rescue. It states a narrow constitutional violation if proved. The materially relevant law in March 1989, however, had not clearly marked that boundary, and *DeShaney* had just emphasized the no-duty baseline. Johnson therefore receives qualified immunity.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone creates no general police duty to protect, no liability for a broken promise alone, and no constitutional tort for negligence. The rule requires a specific imminent threat, affirmative inducement, deliberate use of official authority, material increase in vulnerability, deliberate indifference, reliance, and causation.

# Ornelas v. United States

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Ornelas v. United States*
- **Citation or Docket:** 517 U.S. 690; No. 95-5257.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review after denial of a suppression motion.
- **Simulated Event Date:** May 28, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** What standard of appellate review governs determinations of reasonable suspicion and probable cause under the Fourth Amendment.
- **Question Granted:** Whether appellate courts review the historical facts for clear error but independently determine whether those facts amount to reasonable suspicion or probable cause.
- **Legal Field:** Fourth Amendment, suppression, and appellate standards of review.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Seventh Circuit affirmed the convictions after reviewing both the stop and search determinations deferentially for clear error.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Ornelas and Ornelas-Ledesma seek vacatur and independent appellate review; the United States seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Milwaukee officers investigating drug trafficking encountered petitioners at a motel, examined identification and a vehicle, and observed circumstances they associated with hidden-compartment transportation. After obtaining consent to inspect parts of the car and probing a loose interior panel, officers found cocaine. The district court found reasonable suspicion for the encounter and probable cause for the warrantless automobile search.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The standard-of-review issue is preserved. Historical facts and credibility findings remain for clear-error review. The ultimate constitutional characterization is separable and reviewable without new factfinding.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, March 26, 1996; decision, May 28, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** The two petitioners’ convictions arise from the same stop and search.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 517 U.S. 690; suppression record summarized there.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded, 8-1.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Chief Justice Rehnquist wrote for the Court; Justice Scalia dissented.
- **Historical Holding:** Historical facts receive clear-error review, but ultimate reasonable-suspicion and probable-cause determinations receive de novo appellate review with due weight to trial-court inferences and local officers’ experience.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 517 U.S. 690.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to chronological revalidation.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Appellate review of suspicion and probable cause

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision fixes the appellate standard for these mixed Fourth Amendment questions. Existing Fourth Amendment holdings require objective application to the intrusion actually performed.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No registered standard directly controls appellate review. The Fourth Amendment’s objective-reasonableness rules and ordinary clear-error review of historical findings supply the components.
- **Other Governing Law:** Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 52(a) by analogy; *Brinegar v. United States*, *Illinois v. Gates*, *Terry v. Ohio*, and *United States v. Cortez*.
- **Other Authority Used:** Uniformity, trial-court competence in factfinding, and appellate responsibility for constitutional boundaries.
- **Open Point:** The degree of weight owed to inferences drawn from local police experience must not become disguised deference on the ultimate legal question.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The Seventh Circuit shall review historical facts for clear error and determine de novo whether the facts establish reasonable suspicion and probable cause. It may affirm or reverse the suppression ruling under that standard without reopening supported historical findings.

#### Issue 1: Appellate review of suspicion and probable cause

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the granted standard-of-review question and does not finally decide suppression on a record the court of appeals reviewed under the wrong legal standard.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The trial court finds what happened and resolves credibility, subject to clear-error review. Whether those facts satisfy the constitutional minimum is a mixed question requiring independent appellate judgment. Appellate courts should give practical weight to reasonable inferences drawn by trial judges and experienced officers, but that weight does not transfer final authority over the Fourth Amendment’s boundary.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Historical facts underlying a stop or search are reviewed for clear error; the ultimate determinations of reasonable suspicion and probable cause are reviewed de novo, with respectful consideration of record-supported local and experiential inferences.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** A uniform constitutional rule cannot depend on the reviewing circuit’s willingness to defer to materially different legal characterizations of similar facts. Trial courts remain superior at credibility and immediate factual context. Appellate courts remain responsible for keeping suspicion and probable cause within administrable nationwide bounds. Because the Seventh Circuit treated the ultimate determinations as clear-error questions, its judgment cannot establish that the correct review was performed.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Independent review does not authorize appellate factfinding, disregard of credibility determinations, or a mechanical checklist of suspicious circumstances.

# Love v. Pepersack

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Love v. Pepersack*
- **Citation or Docket:** 47 F.3d 120 (4th Cir. 1995), No. 94-1745.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Granted merits review of dismissal of a § 1983 action.
- **Simulated Event Date:** May 29, 1996, simulation-assigned opinion-delivery date.
- **Question Presented:** Whether state officers violated substantive due process, a constitutional freedom to contract, or the right to keep and bear arms by denying a handgun-purchase application solely because an incomplete records check disclosed prior arrests, where arrest history was not a statutory disqualification and a state court later ordered approval.
- **Question Granted:** Does the Second Amendment protect an individual right to acquire and possess a handgun against state interference, does that right apply to the States through the Fourteenth Amendment, and does arbitrary denial of a handgun application on grounds not authorized by state law violate Due Process?
- **Legal Field:** Arms, due process, licensing, state-law entitlement, and qualified immunity.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit affirmed dismissal of Love’s § 1983 claims.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Love seeks reversal and damages proceedings; the officers seek affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Love truthfully completed Maryland’s handgun application. A computer printout showed several arrests but did not reveal that only one minor conviction resulted. Officers followed an asserted practice of denying applications when arrest dispositions were unknown, although arrest alone was not a statutory ground for denial. Love exhausted administrative review, obtained a state-court order requiring approval, and then sued the officers for damages.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Love pleaded substantive, not procedural, due process. The state court already supplied the requested license. The remaining federal case seeks personal damages, making qualified immunity and the clearly established status of any arms right material. The Court may affirm without adopting the Fourth Circuit’s collective-right characterization.
- **Relevant Dates:** Application and denial, September 1990; Fourth Circuit decision, February 3, 1995; simulated decision, May 29, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** 47 F.3d 120; Maryland handgun statute described in the opinion; 42 U.S.C. § 1983.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits decision. The Fourth Circuit affirmance is the historical anchor.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Hall wrote for the court; Judge Luttig concurred in the judgment.
- **Historical Holding:** The corrected licensing error did not establish substantive due process, and the court rejected an incorporated individual Second Amendment right.
- **Historical Authority:** Lower-court historical anchor.
- **Historical Sources:** 47 F.3d 120.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to chronological revalidation.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Substantive due process and corrected licensing error

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Albright v. Oliver* requires use of the specific constitutional source that directly governs an asserted injury and rejects substantive due process as a duplicate or generalized tort remedy.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Specific constitutional source and substantive-due-process nonduplication**.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Board of Regents v. Roth*, *Parratt v. Taylor*, *Hudson v. Palmer*, and state-law licensing procedures.
- **Other Authority Used:** The established state review and the order granting the application.
- **Open Point:** Whether a deliberately maintained policy that denies statutory entitlements without predeprivation process could support a properly pleaded procedural-due-process claim is not before the Court.

#### Issue 2: Individual arms right, incorporation, and qualified immunity

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision resolves the Second Amendment's individual scope or incorporation. The received nonincorporation rule is associated with *United States v. Cruikshank* and *Presser v. Illinois*; *United States v. Miller* did not establish the licensing rule presented here.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No current registered Arms standard controls. **Qualified-immunity review uses the complete body of law** governs the separate damages question.
- **Other Governing Law:** The Second and Fourteenth Amendments; selective-incorporation precedent; *Cruikshank*, *Presser*, *Miller*, *Harlow*, and *Anderson*.
- **Other Authority Used:** Love's status as a law-abiding applicant, the absence of a disqualifying conviction or adjudicated dangerousness finding, the arrest-record practice, and the later state-court correction.
- **Open Point:** Whether the received nonincorporation rule should be retained and whether a bare, unadjudicated arrest record may constitutionally bar acquisition of a common handgun for home self-defense.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm dismissal of the federal damages action.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** No separate remedial instruction is required. The state judgment ordering approval remains unaffected.

#### Issue 1: Substantive due process and corrected licensing error

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the pleaded substantive-due-process theory and rejects it. He does not convert the complaint into an unpleaded procedural claim.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A state officer's mistaken or unlawful application of a licensing statute does not become substantive due process merely because the decision is arbitrary under state law. The specific Second Amendment source governs the asserted denial, while any procedural-due-process claim must be pleaded and proved on its own elements.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Love's corrected denial does not state a substantive-due-process claim, and any procedural-due-process claim was not pleaded and is not resolved.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The state court performed the corrective function state law assigned and ordered approval. The federal Constitution does not transform every erroneous licensing decision into a generalized substantive tort. The officers' use of an arrest record must instead be assessed under the constitutional source that directly governs the claimed firearm acquisition.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A systematic policy denying an entitlement without meaningful review, retaliation for protected conduct, or a forbidden classification would present a different claim under its own source.

#### Issue 2: Individual arms right, incorporation, and qualified immunity

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone decides the necessary Second Amendment merits before qualified immunity because the specific constitutional source governs the injury and his immunity sequence requires identification of the right.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The received nonincorporation rule of *Cruikshank* and *Presser* rests on an account under which the Bill of Rights generally did not constrain the States, an account later selective-incorporation doctrine displaced. Stone would not retain that rule. The Second and Fourteenth Amendments protect a law-abiding person's individual right to keep a common handgun in the home for self-defense, and lawful acquisition is an incident of that core protection. A State may disqualify persons through convictions or adjudicated findings that bear on danger, but an unadjudicated arrest record is an accusation rather than a finding and cannot alone bar acquisition. The defendant officers nevertheless receive qualified immunity because none of these propositions was sufficiently established in September 1990.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Second and Fourteenth Amendments protect a law-abiding person's acquisition of a common handgun for home self-defense, and a State may not deny that acquisition solely because of an unadjudicated arrest record bearing no established relationship to danger. The defendant officers are nevertheless entitled to qualified immunity because that rule was not sufficiently established in September 1990.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Love had no disqualifying conviction or adjudicated dangerousness finding, and Maryland's own court concluded that the arrest-record practice was unauthorized. The practice therefore burdened the acquisition incident of the core right without a danger-based predicate. Personal damages remain a separate question. In September 1990, materially relevant Supreme Court and circuit authority pointed against the individual and incorporated right Stone recognizes, so the officers lacked fair notice and are immune.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not invalidate objective licensing, background checks, disqualification after a serious conviction or adequate dangerousness finding, or regulation of public carriage. He decides no broader weapon-category or carry question.

# Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co.

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co.*
- **Citation or Docket:** 517 U.S. 706; No. 95-244.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Plenary merits review of a federal remand order based on abstention.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 3, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a remand order based on Burford abstention is appealable and whether a federal court may dismiss or remand a damages action on that abstention ground.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Ninth Circuit properly reviewed and reversed the remand of the California Insurance Commissioner’s damages suit.
- **Legal Field:** Federal jurisdiction, abstention, remand, collateral-order review, and insurance insolvency.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit exercised appellate jurisdiction and vacated the district court’s order remanding the removed diversity action to state court.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The Commissioner seeks reversal and restoration of the remand; Allstate seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Acting as trustee for insolvent Mission Insurance entities, the Commissioner sued Allstate in state court for contract and tort damages arising from reinsurance agreements. Allstate removed on diversity grounds and sought arbitration. The Commissioner urged Burford abstention because related setoff questions implicated California’s centralized insurance-insolvency regime. The district court remanded the action.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Appellate jurisdiction over the remand order precedes the abstention merits. The action seeks damages rather than purely equitable relief. A stay and a dismissal or remand have different jurisdictional consequences.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argument, February 20, 1996; decision, June 3, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 517 U.S. 706; 28 U.S.C. §§ 1291 and 1447; underlying complaint and remand order summarized there.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O’Connor wrote for the Court; separate opinions addressed portions of the abstention analysis.
- **Historical Holding:** The abstention-based remand was appealable, and federal courts may not dismiss or remand damages actions under Burford, though a stay may be available.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court at the controlling scope.
- **Historical Sources:** 517 U.S. 706.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, subject to chronological revalidation.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Appealability of the abstention remand

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc.* construes collateral-order review narrowly but preserves immediate review where an order conclusively surrenders federal adjudication and cannot be effectively reviewed after final judgment. *Kokkonen* reinforces that federal jurisdiction must rest on an identified source.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Collateral-order review of settlement-enforcement rulings** supplies the general finality discipline, while § 1447(d) and the source of the remand determine reviewability here.
- **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§ 1291, 1447(c), and 1447(d); *Thermtron Products v. Hermansdorfer*.
- **Other Authority Used:** The conclusive return of the case to state court on a ground outside § 1447(c).
- **Open Point:** None.

#### Issue 2: Burford abstention in a damages action

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision governs Burford relief in a damages action.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No registered abstention standard directly controls. General remedial discipline distinguishes postponing adjudication from surrendering jurisdiction.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Burford v. Sun Oil Co.*, *Alabama Public Service Commission v. Southern Railway Co.*, and ordinary equitable principles.
- **Other Authority Used:** California’s insurance regime, the nature of the requested damages, and the availability of a stay.
- **Open Point:** The precise circumstances supporting a temporary stay remain case specific.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm appellate jurisdiction, reverse the abstention remand, vacate any unnecessary arbitration directive, and remand for further federal proceedings.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The federal action shall return to the district court. Any appellate direction compelling arbitration is vacated because arbitration is outside the granted abstention questions. The district court may adjudicate the action or, upon supported findings, enter a bounded stay while a material state-law issue is resolved. It may not dismiss or remand merely to avoid exercising damages jurisdiction.

#### Issue 1: Appealability of the abstention remand

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone holds the order reviewable before reaching abstention.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 1447(d) bars review only of remands authorized by § 1447(c)’s jurisdictional and procedural grounds. An abstention remand rests on a discretionary adjudicative ground. Because it conclusively terminates the federal proceeding and cannot be reviewed after state litigation, it is a final appealable order.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A remand based on Burford abstention, rather than a § 1447(c) defect, is reviewable under § 1291.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The district court did not find removal jurisdiction absent or procedure defective. It chose not to exercise concededly existing jurisdiction. That choice ends the federal case and satisfies finality without enlarging collateral-order doctrine.

#### Issue 2: Burford abstention in a damages action

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the permissible form of abstention.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Abstention is an exceptional coordination doctrine, not authority to renounce jurisdiction. Where a plaintiff seeks equitable or discretionary relief, a court may sometimes decline that relief. Where the action seeks legal damages within jurisdiction, the court may postpone adjudication through a necessary, proportionate stay, but ordinarily may not dismiss or remand the claim.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Burford concerns may support a bounded stay of a federal damages action, but they do not authorize dismissal or remand of that action.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** California’s interest in coherent insurer liquidation may justify waiting for a controlling state resolution of a discrete setoff issue. It does not erase Congress’s diversity jurisdiction or transfer Allstate’s federal forum entitlement. A stay preserves both interests; remand surrenders one entirely.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not require a stay, decide arbitrability, or prevent remand on an independently valid statutory ground.

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. Shalala* independently construed the regulation before considering agency persuasion.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No general Chevron module is registered, but current law requires independent identification of statutory range and reasoned agency interpretation within delegated authority.
- **Other Governing Law:** 12 U.S.C. §§ 85 and 86; National Bank Act structure; OCC’s banking-regulation authority.

- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** No separate remedial instruction is required.

#### Issue 1: Meaning of “interest” and agency weight

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches statutory construction and preemption.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 85 uses “interest” broadly enough to include a charge imposed as compensation for a borrower’s delayed payment. The Comptroller acted within the banking domain Congress assigned and supplied a reasonable, authoritative interpretation consistent with statutory text and function. The OCC’s interpretation fits the enacted term and therefore merits substantial weight.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that every penalty or collateral service charge is interest, authorize deceptive fees, or foreclose neutral laws outside the field § 85 occupies.

# Loving v. United States

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Loving v. United States*
