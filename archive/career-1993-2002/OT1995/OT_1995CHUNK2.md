# Bailey v. United States; Robinson v. United States

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Bailey v. United States; Robinson v. United States*
- **Citation or Docket:** 516 U.S. 137; Nos. 94-7448 and 94-7492.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Consolidated merits cases after plenary review.
- **Simulated Event Date:** December 6, 1995.
- **Question Presented:** Whether “uses” a firearm in 18 U.S.C. § 924(c)(1) reaches possession, storage, or proximity that facilitates a drug offense, or requires active employment of the firearm.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the evidence in each prosecution was legally sufficient to establish “use” of a firearm during and in relation to a drug-trafficking offense.
- **Legal Field:** Federal criminal law, statutory elements, and firearm sentencing.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The District of Columbia Circuit affirmed Bailey’s and Robinson’s § 924(c)(1) convictions under a broad facilitation standard.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Petitioners seek reversal of the firearm convictions and remand for proceedings on any independently valid charge or sentence.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Police found a loaded pistol in the locked trunk of Bailey’s car after stopping him with cocaine in the passenger compartment. Officers found an unloaded handgun in a locked trunk in Robinson’s bedroom together with drugs and cash. The Government proved possession and availability but no brandishing, display, reference, barter, firing, attempted firing, or other active employment during either predicate offense.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The statutory-sufficiency issue is preserved. A narrower construction does not prevent the Government from pursuing a distinct “carry” theory only if that theory was charged, preserved, and supported under ordinary procedural law; the Court may not affirm on an untried element.
- **Relevant Dates:** Arguments, November 1, 1995; simulated decision, December 6, 1995.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** The two judgments are consolidated only for construction of § 924(c)(1); each conviction and remand remains docket-specific.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official syllabus and opinions, 516 U.S. 137; D.C. Circuit proceedings identified in the official report; statutory text in force at the time of the offenses.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded in both cases, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Opinion of the Court by Justice O’Connor; unanimous judgment.
- **Historical Holding:** “Use” under § 924(c)(1) requires active employment of a firearm and is not established by mere possession or storage near drugs or proceeds.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 516 U.S. 137 and the official bound-volume opinions.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** Holdings Register and Standards and Tests processed through June 29, 1995.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** *Libretti v. United States* is scheduled earlier and could affect only forfeiture or plea questions, not the meaning of “use.”

#### Issue 1: Meaning and proof of firearm “use”

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision construes “uses” in § 924(c)(1). *Staples v. United States* and *United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc.* require courts to respect enacted offense elements and traditional mens rea, but neither defines the conduct element here.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Mens rea for operative facts distinguishing lawful conduct from serious crime** confirms that courts may not dilute a criminal element through assumption; it does not itself supply the meaning of “use.”
- **Other Governing Law:** Section 924(c)(1), ordinary usage, statutory structure distinguishing use from carrying, and surviving principles of strict criminal construction.
- **Other Authority Used:** Dictionaries and pre-cutoff federal decisions are used only to identify the ordinary active sense of “use” and the conflict among circuits.
- **Open Point:** Whether accessibility intended to embolden or protect a drug operation is enough without active employment.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse both § 924(c)(1) judgments and remand for proceedings consistent with the narrowed statutory construction.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The courts below shall vacate the “use” convictions. The Government may seek any lawful disposition on a separately charged and preserved “carry” theory, but it may not obtain affirmance by converting the jury’s broad-use verdict into a finding on an element the jury was not required to decide.

#### Issue 1: Meaning and proof of firearm “use”

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory merits. No constitutional question is necessary.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies enacted text and structure. “Use” means active employment of the firearm as an operative instrument of the offense, including brandishing, displaying, firing, attempting to fire, referring to the weapon to affect another person, striking with it, or bartering it. Possession, concealment, storage, and strategic proximity may prove availability or a purpose, but they do not themselves constitute use. Treating every accessible firearm as “used” would collapse Congress’s separate verbs and make the conduct element turn on an unbounded facilitation inference.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A firearm is “used” under the then-operative § 924(c)(1) only when the defendant actively employs it during and in relation to the predicate crime; neither Bailey’s locked-trunk pistol nor Robinson’s stored bedroom handgun satisfies that element on this record.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The ordinary sentence “he used a firearm” describes employment of the weapon, not merely its availability. Congress separately spoke of carrying, and the statute’s severe consecutive punishment warrants fidelity to that distinction. The Government’s emboldenment theory proves at most why a defendant possessed the gun. Because no evidence shows either petitioner made the weapon operative in the transaction, the convictions cannot stand.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide the full meaning of “carry,” constructive possession rules under other statutes, or a later congressional amendment using broader language.

# Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue*
- **Citation or Docket:** 28 F.3d 1501 (8th Cir. 1994), No. 93-2148.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Granted merits case on private religious speech in school facilities.
- **Simulated Event Date:** December 11, 1995, simulation-assigned opinion-delivery date.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a school district that permits Scouts and athletic groups to use facilities immediately after classes may exclude a parent-run Christian youth club, or require the permitted Scouts to omit religious speech, because the viewpoint is religious.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the amended access policy violates the Free Speech Clause and whether equal access would violate the Establishment Clause.
- **Legal Field:** First Amendment, limited or nonpublic forums, viewpoint discrimination, and religious attribution.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eighth Circuit reversed a district-court judgment for the School District, holding that the amended policy discriminated against a religious viewpoint on moral and character development.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The School District seeks reversal and restoration of the district-court judgment; the Club seeks affirmance and prospective equal access.
- **Material Facts and Record:** The parent-run Club met monthly after school, required parental consent, disclaimed district sponsorship, and used skits, songs, Bible reading, prayer, and discussion to address friendship, morality, and youth development. The District later closed the 3-to-6 p.m. period to most community groups but retained Scouts and athletics, while expressly forbidding religious speech in Scout meetings. Club members remained free to meet after 6 p.m., but lost the late-bus period available to the favored groups.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The viewpoint and Establishment Clause defenses are preserved. Prospective relief remains live because the access policy continues to govern; no damages question is necessary.
- **Relevant Dates:** Eighth Circuit decision, July 7, 1994; simulated decision, December 11, 1995.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** 28 F.3d 1501; district-court record described there; *Lamb’s Chapel* and the public-forum cases cited in the opinions.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits decision. The Eighth Circuit judgment is the historical anchor.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Court of appeals majority reversing the district court; dissent would have sustained the after-school distinction.
- **Historical Holding:** Excluding the Club while permitting Scouts to address moral development constituted religious-viewpoint discrimination, and equal access would not establish religion.
- **Historical Authority:** Lower-court historical anchor.
- **Historical Sources:** 28 F.3d 1501.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995, including *Rosenberger*, *Capitol Square v. Pinette*, and *Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller*.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** *Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21* is scheduled later and does not control unless rendered first after calendar reconciliation.

#### Issue 1: Religious viewpoint and attribution

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia* distinguishes equal civic access from direct public financing of devotional activity. *Capitol Square v. Pinette* and *Chabad-Lubavitch v. Miller* hold that neutral access for clearly private religious expression does not become government endorsement when forum history, sponsorship, control, and identification make private attribution clear.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Private religious expression in an open government forum** and **Attribution of private religious displays inside government buildings.**
- **Other Governing Law:** *Lamb’s Chapel*, *Widmar*, *Perry Education Association*, and the Free Speech and Establishment Clauses.
- **Other Authority Used:** The district’s access policy and actual use data identify the forum and comparative speakers.
- **Open Point:** Whether the district may define the after-school forum around Scouts and athletics while excluding religious treatment of the same moral-development subject.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Eighth Circuit and remand for entry of appropriately limited prospective relief.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court shall enjoin exclusion of the Club from the 3-to-6 p.m. access program on account of its religious viewpoint. The District may apply neutral scheduling, supervision, safety, and disclaimer rules equally to all community groups.

#### Issue 1: Religious viewpoint and attribution

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches both the Free Speech claim and the District’s necessary Establishment Clause defense.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Once the District admits private groups to address youth character and development, it may not identify religious treatment of that subject as the one forbidden perspective. Equal access is private speech, not governmental worship, when meetings are voluntary, parent-run, outside instructional time, unsupported by public funds, and clearly disclaimed. The age of participants permits careful attribution and consent safeguards, but not categorical viewpoint exclusion.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The policy violates the Free Speech Clause because it allows private Scouts to address moral development while excluding the Club’s religious perspective, and admitting the Club on neutral terms would not violate the Establishment Clause on this record.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The District’s own policy reveals the discrimination: permitted Scouts may discuss the approved subject only if religion is omitted. The Club receives no curricular authority, public payment, mandatory audience, or school sponsorship. Meetings occur after classes, parents consent, and private sponsorship can be stated plainly. Avoiding a constitutional violation is compelling only when the feared violation is real; neutral access does not attribute the Club’s devotional content to the District.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not require access during instruction, permit recruitment through teachers acting in official roles, or bar neutral rules preventing coercion, disruption, or misuse of school authority.

# Southern Christian Leadership Conference of Alabama v. Sessions

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Southern Christian Leadership Conference of Alabama v. Sessions*
- **Citation or Docket:** 56 F.3d 1281 (11th Cir. 1995) (en banc), No. 92-6798.
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Granted merits case concerning Voting Rights Act § 2 challenges to judicial elections.
- **Simulated Event Date:** December 18, 1995, simulation-assigned opinion-delivery date.
- **Question Presented:** Whether Alabama’s at-large, numbered-place, majority-vote judicial election systems dilute Black voting strength in violation of § 2 and whether plaintiffs identified a workable benchmark and proved less opportunity under the statutory totality.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the en banc Eleventh Circuit applied the correct § 2 framework to the challenged judicial circuits and districts.
- **Legal Field:** Voting Rights Act § 2, judicial elections, comparator, electoral opportunity, and remedy.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The en banc Eleventh Circuit affirmed judgment for Alabama after concluding that plaintiffs had not established vote dilution warranting the requested restructuring.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Plaintiffs request reversal and remand for liability and remedial proceedings; the State seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Black voters challenged several Alabama judicial election structures, relying on racially polarized voting, electoral history, numbered posts, majority-vote requirements, and proposed alternatives. The claims concerned offices with different geographic units and institutional functions. The lower courts disputed whether the proposed subdistrict and alternative-election structures supplied lawful benchmarks and whether the totality showed less electoral opportunity rather than a demand for proportional officeholding.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The plaintiffs have Article III standing as voters in the challenged units. The comparator and Gingles questions are merits prerequisites, not jurisdictional bars. Any remedy must respect the State’s judicial structure while correcting a proved statutory violation.
- **Relevant Dates:** En banc decision, June 21, 1995; simulated decision, December 18, 1995.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** Claims involve multiple circuits and districts; each unit requires separate liability and remedial analysis.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** 56 F.3d 1281; district-court record and opinions described there; Voting Rights Act § 2.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits decision. The en banc Eleventh Circuit is the historical anchor.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** En banc majority for the State with multiple separate opinions.
- **Historical Holding:** The plaintiffs did not establish § 2 liability requiring restructuring of Alabama’s judicial election systems.
- **Historical Authority:** Lower-court historical anchor.
- **Historical Sources:** 56 F.3d 1281.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 29, 1995.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None completed at drafting.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No earlier OT1995 case is presently identified as controlling.

#### Issue 1: Comparator and § 2 totality

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** *Holder v. Hall* holds that a governing-structure claim is cognizable when plaintiffs identify a concrete, workable comparator grounded in objective law, practice, history, or a closely comparable institution; comparator sufficiency does not prove liability. *Johnson v. De Grandy* requires the Gingles predicates and full statutory totality, treats proportionality as relevant but not dispositive, and rejects maximization.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Objective comparator for governing-body-size claims under Voting Rights Act § 2** and **Voting Rights Act § 2 totality, proportionality, and nonmaximization.**
- **Other Governing Law:** 52 U.S.C. § 10301 as then codified; *Thornburg v. Gingles*; surviving judicial-election applications of § 2.
- **Other Authority Used:** Alabama’s judicial-election statutes and record evidence concerning cohesion, bloc voting, elections, geography, and proposed alternatives.
- **Open Point:** How the statutory opportunity inquiry applies to multiple judicial offices where a remedy must preserve adjudicative function and cannot guarantee racial proportionality.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the en banc judgment and remand for district-by-district application of the comparator, Gingles, and totality rules.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court shall identify each challenged electoral unit, determine whether a concrete and lawful alternative exists, apply the Gingles predicates and full totality, and fashion relief only for units in which liability is proved. The Court makes no present finding that any particular alternative is required.

#### Issue 1: Comparator and § 2 totality

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory merits. He rejects treatment of the benchmark question as an Article III defect.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Judicial elections are covered by § 2 when State law uses popular election, but the statute protects equal electoral opportunity rather than racial ownership of judgeships. Plaintiffs must identify a workable alternative that permits a meaningful comparison, prove the Gingles predicates where applicable, and establish under the totality that the challenged design leaves protected voters with less opportunity. The State may defend institutional and geographic features, but may not treat the judicial label as categorical immunity.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Eleventh Circuit erred insofar as it treated the absence of an existing alternative or the distinctive character of judges as a categorical bar; each claim must be tested against a concrete lawful comparator and the full § 2 totality without a presumption of proportional representation.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** *Holder* supplies a comparative prerequisite, not a method for ending the case before the evidence is assessed. Some proposed subdistricts or alternative election methods may prove unworkable or inconsistent with state structure; others may permit a valid comparison. The record must then establish cohesion, bloc voting, and unequal opportunity, not merely underrepresentation. Because the en banc analysis did not keep those steps separate across the challenged units, a unit-specific remand is required.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that § 2 requires election of judges of a particular race, authorize maximization, or approve any remedy before liability and institutional fit are established.

# Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. Calhoun

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** *Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. Calhoun*
- **Citation or Docket:** 516 U.S. 199 (1996); *Calhoun v. Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A.*, 40 F.3d 622 (3d Cir. 1994).
- **October Term:** 1995.
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued October 30, 1995; merits decision scheduled January 9, 1996.
- **Simulated Event Date:** January 9, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a court of appeals exercising interlocutory jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1292(b) may decide an issue fairly included within the certified order even though the district court did not separately formulate that issue, and whether federal maritime law displaces state wrongful-death and survival remedies for the death of a nonseafarer in territorial waters.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Third Circuit had authority under § 1292(b) to resolve the state-remedies issue as part of the certified order, and, if so, whether state wrongful-death and survival remedies remain available for a recreational nonseafarer's death in territorial waters.
- **Legal Field:** Federal courts, interlocutory appellate jurisdiction under § 1292(b); admiralty and maritime remedies.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit exercised § 1292(b) jurisdiction over the certified interlocutory order and held that state wrongful-death and survival remedies were not categorically displaced for the death of a recreational nonseafarer in territorial waters.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Yamaha seeks reversal of the Third Circuit's jurisdictional and maritime-remedies rulings; the Calhouns seek affirmance and continuation of the action with otherwise applicable state remedies available.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Twelve-year-old Natalie Calhoun died in a recreational jet-ski accident in Puerto Rico's territorial waters. She was neither a seaman nor a maritime worker. The district court certified an interlocutory order under § 1292(b); the certified order rested on the premise that federal maritime law exclusively controlled the available wrongful-death remedies. The Third Circuit addressed the antecedent question whether state remedies remained available even though the district court had not separately phrased that issue as the controlling question.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The § 1292(b) issue is antecedent. Stone must determine whether the Third Circuit lawfully reached the state-remedies issue before deciding the maritime merits. No broader interlocutory review of separate orders is presented.
- **Relevant Dates:** Third Circuit judgment, 1994; argued October 30, 1995; simulated decision January 9, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** 516 U.S. 199; 40 F.3d 622; 28 U.S.C. § 1292(b).

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Opinion of the Court by Justice Stevens.
- **Historical Holding:** A court of appeals accepting a § 1292(b) appeal may address any issue fairly included within the certified order, and state remedies remain available for the death of a nonseafarer in territorial waters where no federal maritime statute or uniform rule supplies an exclusive remedy.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** 516 U.S. 199.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's position

**Approval Status:** Draft for express approval; this Section II is not controlling or frozen until approved.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** Law effective immediately before January 9, 1996.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified that materially changes the § 1292(b) or maritime-remedies questions.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

#### Issue 1: Scope of interlocutory appellate jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1292(b)

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision resolves whether a court of appeals accepting a § 1292(b) appeal is limited to the particular question formulated by the district court or may resolve another issue fairly included within the certified order.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No exact Standards and Tests entry governs this question. Ordinary statutory interpretation and the jurisdictional limits governing interlocutory review control.
- **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. § 1292(b); the distinction between an appeal from a certified order and review of a separate, uncertified order.
- **Other Authority Used:** *United States v. Stanley*, 483 U.S. 669 (1987), for the boundary against using an interlocutory appeal to reach unrelated orders.
- **Open Point:** Whether the Third Circuit could resolve the state-remedies issue because it was fairly included within and antecedent to the certified order.

#### Issue 2: Maritime displacement of state wrongful-death and survival remedies

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No Holdings Register decision resolves the availability of state remedies for the territorial-water death of a recreational nonseafarer.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No exact registered test. Existing source-separation principles require distinction among the general maritime cause of action, status-specific federal statutes, location-specific federal statutes, and supplementary state remedies.
- **Other Governing Law:** *Moragne v. States Marine Lines, Inc.*, 398 U.S. 375 (1970); *Miles v. Apex Marine Corp.*, 498 U.S. 19 (1990); the Jones Act; the Death on the High Seas Act; traditional maritime principles governing coexistence of federal and state remedies.
- **Other Authority Used:** The decedent's nonseafarer status and the territorial location of the accident determine whether an exclusive federal remedial regime applies.
- **Open Point:** Whether *Moragne* and *Miles* displace state remedies outside the maritime-worker and high-seas categories that generated the federal uniformity concerns.

### B. Stone's position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment of the Third Circuit.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The case may proceed with otherwise applicable state wrongful-death and survival remedies available. Liability, choice of law, recoverable categories of damages, and other unresolved matters remain for ordinary proceedings below.

#### Issue 1: Scope of interlocutory appellate jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1292(b)

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the § 1292(b) threshold first. He concludes that the Third Circuit lawfully possessed authority to decide the antecedent state-remedies question and therefore reaches the maritime-remedies merits.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a court of appeals that accepts an interlocutory appeal under § 1292(b) may resolve an issue fairly included within the certified order even though the district court did not separately formulate that issue as the controlling question.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 1292(b) authorizes an appeal from the certified order, not merely from the particular question of law identified in the district court's certification. Once the court of appeals lawfully accepts the appeal, it may decide an issue fairly included within that order when resolution of the issue is necessary or antecedent to the questions the order presents. It may not use § 1292(b) to review separate orders or unrelated matters outside the certified order.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A court of appeals exercising jurisdiction under § 1292(b) may resolve an issue fairly included within the certified order and is not confined to the particular controlling question formulated by the district court; it may not reach separate orders or unrelated matters outside that order.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The certified order proceeded from the premise that federal maritime law exclusively controlled the Calhouns' remedies and then presented damages questions flowing from that premise. Whether state remedies remained available was therefore part of the same order and logically antecedent to the certified damages questions. The Third Circuit did not reach a separate order or unrelated controversy. The jurisdictional threshold is satisfied.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not authorize plenary interlocutory review of the entire case whenever one order is certified. Issues outside the certified order require their own lawful appellate basis.

#### Issue 2: Maritime displacement of state wrongful-death and survival remedies

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Because the Third Circuit lawfully reached the antecedent issue under § 1292(b), Stone reaches the maritime-remedies merits. He does not decide product-defect liability, the amount of damages, or particular choice-of-law questions not necessary to the judgment.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** *Moragne* created a general maritime wrongful-death cause of action but did not silently displace every state remedy historically available in territorial waters. *Miles* coordinates remedies in the congressionally regulated seaman setting, and the Death on the High Seas Act supplies a federal regime for deaths within its territorial scope. Neither establishes an exclusive remedial measure for the death of a recreational nonseafarer in territorial waters. Federal uniformity does not itself authorize displacement when no statute or controlling maritime rule makes the federal remedy exclusive.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** State wrongful-death and survival remedies remain available for the death of a nonseafarer in territorial waters because no federal statute or controlling uniform maritime rule supplies an exclusive remedial scheme for that category.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Natalie Calhoun was neither a seaman nor a longshore worker, and her death did not occur on the high seas. The categories that drove the uniform remedial treatment in *Miles* and the statutory high-seas regime therefore do not control. Creation of a general maritime wrongful-death action in *Moragne* did not itself extinguish state remedies in this residual territorial-water category.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide remedies for seamen, longshore workers, deaths governed by the Death on the High Seas Act, or a state remedy that actually conflicts with a specific federal maritime rule.

# Allridge v. Scott

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **October Term:** 1995.

#### Issue 1: Parole information when future dangerousness is argued

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the controlled due-process question because future dangerousness was placed at issue.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether due process requires a capital jury to receive evidence or instruction concerning delayed or practically unlikely parole when state law leaves the defendant legally eligible for parole.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies *Simmons* at its controlling boundary and declines to extend it to predictions about discretionary parole. The constitutional problem in *Simmons* arose because the prosecution invited a future-dangerousness inference while the jury was left with a materially false understanding that the defendant could return to society even though the only life sentence legally foreclosed parole. When state law instead leaves parole legally possible, a court cannot convert estimates concerning future parole-board decisions into a constitutionally required sentencing fact. States may provide additional accurate information, but due process does not require a judicial prediction of discretionary release.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When future dangerousness is argued, due process requires the *Simmons* instruction where the available life sentence legally forecloses parole; it does not require a jury instruction or evidentiary presentation predicting the timing or likelihood of discretionary parole when the defendant remains legally parole-eligible.

The requested information therefore concerned how parole law and future discretionary decisions might operate, not correction of the false legal premise that triggered *Simmons*. Such predictions are contingent, may change over time, and are not necessary to prevent the jury from misunderstanding the legal sentence imposed. The Fifth Circuit's denial of relief on this controlled question should be affirmed.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not weaken *Simmons* where life imprisonment legally eliminates parole, and he does not prevent a State from voluntarily giving accurate, nonmisleading information about a legally fixed minimum period of confinement.

#### Issue 2: Special instruction for mitigating evidence under Penry

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches each preserved *Penry* theory encompassed by the controlled mitigating-instruction question because any valid theory could independently require a new penalty proceeding.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** When the Texas capital special issues require a supplemental instruction so the jury can give constitutionally meaningful effect to mitigating evidence actually presented.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A supplemental mitigation instruction is constitutionally required when relevant mitigating evidence actually presented to the jury possesses substantial mitigating force that the statutory special issues do not provide a meaningful vehicle to express, creating a reasonable likelihood that jurors cannot give the evidence full effect in the life-or-death decision. Stone does not require a formal diagnosis, a uniquely severe permanent disability, or a rigid causal nexus between mitigation and the offense as prerequisites. But *Penry* does not require an additional instruction when the mitigating force of the evidence can be meaningfully considered through the existing questions, and it does not require an instruction designed around evidence never introduced at trial.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Texas special issues require an additional mitigation instruction only when mitigating evidence actually presented has constitutionally relevant force that the statutory questions do not permit the jury meaningfully to express. Allridge's preserved mitigation theories do not satisfy that requirement on this record.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The father's lay testimony concerning alleged mental illness and abuse during a prior incarceration did not establish the kind of impairment or two-edged mitigation whose mitigating force necessarily fell outside the Texas questions. The jury could consider that testimony in assessing Allridge and future dangerousness, and the record does not establish that the statutory framework converted its mitigating significance solely into aggravation. The separate theory concerning medical evidence fails because the medical evidence was not introduced. Without the evidence, a court cannot determine its content, reliability, mitigating significance, aggravating implications, or whether the special issues prevented the jury from giving it effect. To the extent Allridge characterizes practical parole consequences themselves as mitigation, that theory is governed by the parole-information analysis above and cannot be used to circumvent the boundary of *Simmons*.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not approve a sentencing structure that allows genuinely two-edged mitigating evidence to operate only as aggravation. He reserves a case in which reliable mitigation is actually introduced and the statutory questions provide no coherent lawful mechanism for the jury to give its mitigating force effect.

- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The academy required training officers to rotate through assignments so recruits received varied instruction, and the police department operated continuously. A permanent exception would constrain scheduling and redistribute recurring assignments rather than solve a single conflict. Those proved operational effects satisfy the then-governing *Hardison* standard. Stone does not rely on an unsupported finding that an ordinary shift-swap mechanism was available.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone creates no categorical public-safety exception. Speculative inconvenience, coworker hostility, or a nominal accommodation that is predictably unavailable would not establish undue hardship.
