## Merits cases

### 1. United States v. Wheat

**Question presented.** May an officer stop a specifically described vehicle on a contemporaneous anonymous report of dangerous driving without personally observing a traffic violation?

**What the case is about.** An anonymous caller described repeated dangerous driving, the vehicle, part of its plate, and its route. An officer promptly located the matching vehicle. The Eighth Circuit upheld the stop and denied suppression; the supplied record establishes neither independent observation of unlawful driving nor the caller’s traceability. 

**Precedent applied.** Actual baseline: *Terry*, *Alabama v. White*, and *Florida v. J. L.* Supplied *J. L.* requires circumstances supporting source reliability or a reliable basis for the asserted illegality, not corroboration of identity alone.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed affirmance without a categorical dangerous-driver exception. The old Court adopted a narrower, report-specific rationale through independent alignment; Stone caused no identified change. 

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary judicial move: application of the existing reasonable-suspicion totality.

**Stone’s position now.** Affirm on the narrow supplied facts. The detailed, immediate account of openly observable driving supplies a basis of knowledge beyond a bare accusation attached to a vehicle description. Rungs 4 and 6 select that application over an emergency exception; urgency does not independently establish reliability.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply *Terry* and distinguish *J. L.* without weakening its reliability requirement.

**Proposed holding.** This report’s contemporaneity, description of observed driving, and prompt vehicle-and-route confirmation together supplied reasonable suspicion for the initial stop.

**Limit.** No authority for a frisk, search, prolonged detention, or a stop based merely on the label “drunk driver.”

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The old narrow fallback remains legally available without creating an offense-based exception.

### 2. Sprietsma v. Mercury Marine

**Question presented.** Does the Federal Boat Safety Act, or the Coast Guard’s decision not to require propeller guards, preempt the asserted state common-law claims?

**What the case is about.** Sprietsma brought design-defect and warning claims concerning an outboard motor without a propeller guard. Illinois’s highest court upheld dismissal on implied-preemption grounds. Defect, causation, and damages are not before the Court. 

**Precedent applied.** Actual and supplied *Freightliner* distinguish regulatory absence from an operative federal restriction. Supplied *Geier* permits conflict preemption where a tort duty obstructs a deliberately adopted federal objective, not merely a preference for uniformity.

**Old sim.** Stone sought reversal and restoration of the claims. The old Court adopted the substance independently; no Stone-caused change was recorded. 

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary statutory interpretation and application of existing preemption law.

**Stone’s position now.** Reverse and remand. The Act’s text and saving clause preserve the relevant common-law channel, and the Coast Guard’s nonregulation supplies no demonstrated federal prohibition on guards or tort liability. Rungs 2 and 3 resolve the case without broader constitutional intervention.

**Doctrinal intent.** Interpret text, apply *Freightliner*, and distinguish *Geier*.

**Proposed holding.** Neither the Act’s express clause nor the identified Coast Guard action preempts these common-law claims. They may proceed under otherwise applicable law.

**Limit.** Reinstatement establishes no product defect or entitlement to compensation.

**Novelty.** Statutory or constitutional interpretation.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The supplied precedent supports the same statutory disposition.

### 3. American Amusement Machine Association v. Kendrick

**Question presented.** May Indianapolis restrict minors’ access to violent video games through content-triggered warnings, shielding, and adult-accompaniment requirements by analogizing violent expression to obscenity?

**What the case is about.** The Seventh Circuit directed preliminary relief against an ordinance regulating coin-operated games depicting graphic violence. The case arrives at the preliminary-injunction stage, not after a final liability trial. 

**Precedent applied.** Actual baseline includes *Ginsberg*, *Reno*, and the established content-based-speech rules. Supplied *Reno* and *Free Speech Coalition* protect expression outside established unprotected categories and require genuine justification for suppression.

**Old sim.** Stone supported the injunction and ordinary First Amendment protection. The old Court adopted those grounds independently. 

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary classification and scrutiny application, not creation of a video-game-specific right.

**Stone’s position now.** Affirm preliminary relief. Violence and interactivity do not themselves establish obscenity, while the supplied evidence does not adequately connect this ordinance’s coverage to the asserted injury or justify its restrictions. Rungs 4 and 6 preserve protection without deciding every future minors’ access law.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply existing speech categories and strict scrutiny; reserve unnecessary vagueness questions.

**Proposed holding.** The challenged games remain protected expression, and the challengers have established the required likelihood that this content-based ordinance fails strict scrutiny.

**Limit.** No immunity for independently unlawful conduct, obscenity, true threats, or genuinely neutral arcade regulation.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The same protective result follows through existing doctrine, with the preliminary posture kept explicit.

### 4. United States v. Hambrick

**Question presented.** Was acquisition of the specified subscriber and connection information a Fourth Amendment search, and did that acquisition taint the later residential warrant?

**What the case is about.** An Internet provider disclosed basic account, billing, and discrete connection information. The Fourth Circuit upheld denial of suppression. The supplied grant separates those records from communications content, comprehensive activity histories, and statutory compliance. 

**Precedent applied.** Actual *Katz*, *Miller*, and *Smith* govern the limited records question. Supplied *Bond* and *Kyllo* require attention to the examination actually performed but do not establish that every provider-held record is constitutionally protected.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed narrow affirmance, rejecting a blanket abandonment rule for digital information. The old Court independently adopted that distinction. 

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary application and issue reservation.

**Stone’s position now.** Affirm the Fourth Amendment disposition concerning these limited records. Their demonstrated function and revealing capacity do not warrant extending protection beyond the inherited transactional-record cases here. Rungs 4 and 6 prevent both a new universal digital warrant rule and a universal third-party exception.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply the limited transactional-record precedents and reserve materially different information.

**Proposed holding.** Acquisition of the limited subscriber and discrete connection records described here was not a search under the governing law. The derivative taint argument therefore fails.

**Limit.** No approval of unlawful statutory process, content acquisition, comprehensive histories, or an independently defective home warrant.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The narrow disposition survives without treating intermediary possession as automatic abandonment.

### 5. Peavy v. WFAA-TV, Inc.

**Question presented.** Does publication protection shield defendants alleged to have participated in unlawful interception, and how must the separate interception, use, and disclosure claims be treated?

**What the case is about.** The Fifth Circuit ordered claim-specific proceedings concerning a broadcaster’s alleged involvement in continued interception of telephone conversations. Participation, culpability, causation, and independent sourcing remain disputed. 

**Precedent applied.** Actual and supplied *Bartnicki* protect truthful public-concern publication by a nonparticipating recipient. Knowledge that the source acted unlawfully is not itself procurement or participation.

**Old sim.** Stone supported the claim-specific remand. The old Court adopted the distinction independently, without an attributable Stone change. 

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary application, statutory claim separation, and remand.

**Stone’s position now.** Affirm the remand. Protect passive receipt and publication while permitting claims supported by the defendants’ own legally relevant acquisition conduct. Rungs 2, 4, and 5 require proof of the enacted elements rather than either blanket press immunity or liability by association.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply *Bartnicki*, distinguish participation, and reserve disputed liability.

**Proposed holding.** *Bartnicki* does not immunize a defendant’s own unlawful procurement or participation. Each statutory claim must nevertheless satisfy its own elements, and mere knowledge of a source’s illegality does not establish participation.

**Limit.** No implied aiding-and-abetting action, automatic liability for knowing receipt, or present damages determination.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The distinction remains sound when knowledge and participation are kept genuinely separate.

### 6. Eldred v. Ashcroft

**Question presented.** Does the Copyright Term Extension Act exceed the Copyright Clause’s limited-times authority or violate the First Amendment?

**What the case is about.** The D.C. Circuit rejected a facial challenge to a twenty-year extension covering existing and future copyrights. Particular fair-use disputes and a future sequence of extensions are outside the supplied case. 

**Precedent applied.** Actual copyright precedents, including *Sony* and *Harper & Row*, supply the historical framework. Supplied fair-use precedent does not establish a constitutional maximum term or decide serial extension.

**Old sim.** Stone supported affirmance and proposed an outer boundary against practical perpetuity. The old Court accepted the present-law result but reserved that boundary; the alignment was independent. 

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary constitutional interpretation and reservation.

**Stone’s position now.** Affirm this facial disposition without announcing a separate practical-perpetuity rule. Congress’s finite adjustment, considered with the retained idea-expression distinction and fair use, does not establish the asserted facial defect. Rung 6 excludes adjudication of hypothetical future extensions.

**Doctrinal intent.** Interpret the Copyright Clause, apply existing speech safeguards, and reserve serial extension.

**Proposed holding.** The challenged finite extension does not establish either constitutional violation asserted in this facial case.

**Limit.** No holding that any nominally finite term is necessarily valid, that repeated extensions are immune from review, or that copyright defeats every as-applied speech claim.

**Novelty.** Issue reservation or minimalist disposition.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes. Stone retains affirmance but withdraws the unnecessary announcement concerning future practical perpetuity.

### 7. Urofsky v. Gilmore

**Question presented.** May Virginia impose its content-defined prior-approval requirement on public-university research and teaching conducted using state computers?

**What the case is about.** The old export presents the Fourth Circuit’s categorical rejection of the professors’ claim. The supplied new register already contains *Urofsky*, decided May 7, 2001, reversing that categorical approach and remanding the approval process for examination; no subsequent return record is supplied. 

**Precedent applied.** Actual *Pickering*, *Connick*, and *NTEU* provide the background. Supplied *Urofsky* directly controls and preserves institutional authority over genuine curriculum, resources, security, and operations.

**Old sim.** Stone sought reversal and an academic-licensing rule. The old Court instead used existing employee-speech doctrine through independent alignment. 

**Stone innovation.** None. The pertinent extension has already been supplied as controlling law.

**Stone’s position now.** Adhere to the registered reversal and remand, not a newly invented second merits adjudication. Rungs 1, 4, and 5 require application of the existing scholarship holding and assessment of the approval mechanism on a proper record.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply supplied *Urofsky* and preserve its record-based remand.

**Proposed holding.** Public-university scholarship is not categorically unprotected employment activity. The challenged prospective restriction must receive the review already required by supplied *Urofsky*.

**Limit.** No faculty veto over curriculum, no new academic-freedom test, and no assumed post-remand findings.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes. This is now application of an existing same-case holding, not a new academic-licensing proposal.

### 8. LaVine v. Blaine School District

**Question presented.** Could the school temporarily remove LaVine because of the supplied safety concerns, and could it retain disciplinary consequences unsupported by an established student-speech ground?

No supplied *Kia P.* holding is available.

**Old sim.** Stone proposed a mixed disposition separating protection from punishment. The old Court adopted that distinction independently. 

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary application and correction of the disposition’s scope.

**Stone’s position now.** Affirm the described initial-response and expungement portions; remand only genuinely unresolved continuing-exclusion or liability questions. Rungs 4 and 5 reject reversal of an unidentified lower-court ruling and reject automatic destruction of every accurate safety record.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply existing school-speech and procedural law; distinguish emergency justification from continuing punishment.

**Proposed holding.** The specific contemporaneous concerns permitted a bounded safety response. They did not, without an independently sufficient legal basis, establish disciplinary guilt or justify indefinite exclusion.

**Limit.** A negative commitment assessment does not automatically resolve every school-safety question; damages and immunity remain separately governed.

**Novelty.** Remedial or dispositional difference.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes. The operative disposition is confined to rulings and unresolved issues actually identified in the supplied record.

### 9. Boroff v. Van Wert City Board of Education

**Question presented.** May a public school prohibit a student’s personal band shirt because administrators consider its message offensive or contrary to the school’s educational mission?

**What the case is about.** The Sixth Circuit sustained restrictions on Boroff’s Marilyn Manson shirts. The supplied justification concerns the band’s perceived values rather than a developed showing of material disruption from the apparel. 

**Precedent applied.** Actual *Tinker*, *Fraser*, and *Hazelwood* distinguish personal expression, school-context vulgarity, and sponsored speech. Supplied *Boring* and *Henerey* concern institutional curricular or sponsored expression, not every student’s clothing.  

**Old sim.** Stone sought reversal under *Tinker*. The old Court adopted that position independently.

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary doctrinal classification and application.

**Stone’s position now.** Reverse and remand. School ownership of the setting does not turn personal apparel into an institutional message, and disapproval of a musician’s worldview does not establish disruption. Rungs 4 and 7 select *Tinker* and distinguish the sponsored-expression cases.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply *Tinker*; distinguish *Fraser*, *Hazelwood*, *Boring*, and *Henerey*.

**Proposed holding.** The school may not suppress this personal expression solely because its perceived message conflicts with the school’s preferred values.

**Limit.** Genuine disruption, independently regulable vulgarity, and properly fitted neutral dress rules remain separate questions.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The current sources support the same rejection of viewpoint-based suppression.

### 10. FCC v. NextWave Personal Communications Inc.

**Question presented.** Does Bankruptcy Code § 525(a) prohibit cancellation of the licenses because of the debtor’s failure to pay a dischargeable debt, notwithstanding the FCC’s regulatory characterization?

**What the case is about.** The FCC canceled licenses following nonpayment during bankruptcy. The D.C. Circuit set aside that action, and the dispute concerns the statutory reason for cancellation rather than every licensing or debt-collection power. 

**Precedent applied.** Section 525(a)’s text controls. Supplied *MCI* and *Mead* do not permit agency administration to eliminate an operative statutory limitation or manufacture deference where the relevant provision does not assign interpretation to that agency.  

**Old sim.** Stone supported affirmance. The old Court adopted the text-based ground independently.

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary statutory interpretation.

**Stone’s position now.** Affirm. A regulatory objective does not change the immediate statutory cause of the prohibited cancellation. Rungs 2 and 3 resolve the question without replacing administrative-deference doctrine.

**Doctrinal intent.** Interpret § 525(a) and reject an unexpressed regulatory-purpose exception.

**Proposed holding.** The FCC may not revoke the licenses solely because NextWave failed to pay the covered debt. Its characterization of cancellation as regulatory does not create an exception to § 525(a).

**Limit.** Independent regulatory violations and otherwise lawful debt-collection methods remain available.

**Novelty.** Statutory or constitutional interpretation.

**Changed from old sim.** No. The same statutory reading carries the result.

### 11. Miller-El v. Cockrell

**Old sim.** Stone sought a certificate and remand. The old Court independently adopted the threshold distinction. 

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary application of the certificate threshold.

**Stone’s position now.** Reverse and direct issuance of the certificate. The claim is reasonably debatable on the whole supplied showing. Rungs 1 and 4 prevent importing the ultimate habeas burden into the gateway and thereby requiring a prisoner to win before receiving an appeal.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply the certificate standard and reserve merits.

**Proposed holding.** Miller-El’s *Batson* claim warrants appellate consideration because reasonable jurists could debate its rejection.

**Novelty.** No meaningful Stone novelty.

**Changed from old sim.** No. Meaningful appellate access follows from the existing threshold, not a lowered habeas merits standard.

### 13. Ewing v. California

**Question presented.** Is Ewing’s twenty-five-years-to-life sentence grossly disproportionate to his current theft considered with his recidivist history?

**What the case is about.** California affirmed the sentence for theft of three golf clubs valued at approximately $1,200, committed while on parole, against a record including serious burglary and robbery offenses. **Old sim.** Stone affirmed by distinguishing the supposed *Durden* precedent. The old Court independently accepted that approach. 

**Stone innovation.** Ordinary proportionality application.

**Stone’s position now.** Affirm, but not because the State called the triggering offense a felony. Considering the present theft, serious predicate history, repeated offending, and actual minimum term, Stone would retain the old conclusion that this sentence does not cross the governing gross-disproportionality boundary. Rung 4 resolves the case.

**Doctrinal intent.** Apply existing proportionality doctrine.

**Proposed holding.** This sentence, assessed against the current offense and the proved serious recidivist history, is not grossly disproportionate under the governing cases.

**Limit.** No categorical approval of three-strikes sentences or rule allowing criminal history to replace consideration of the current offense.

**Novelty.** Application disagreement only.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes in rationale, not disposition: the unsupported *Durden* premise and the felony-label shortcut are removed.

### 14. Lockyer v. Andrade

**What the case is about.** Andrade received two consecutive twenty-five-years-to-life terms for petty thefts. The Ninth Circuit upheld federal habeas relief. His convictions are not challenged through the presented issue.

**Stone innovation.** Application disagreement under the existing AEDPA standard.

**Stone’s position now.** Affirm relief. The practical fifty-year minimum for these petty triggering offenses makes the case materially different from a genuinely available parole regime. **Doctrinal intent.** Apply proportionality through § 2254(d)(1), with sentence-only relief.

**Proposed holding.** Sustaining the supplied fifty-year minimum for these two petty thefts was an objectively unreasonable application of the established gross-disproportionality principle. Relief is limited to resentencing.

**Limit.** No categorical prohibition on consecutive sentences, repeated use of prior convictions, or recidivist enhancement.

**Novelty.** Application disagreement only.

**Changed from old sim.** Yes in its legal basis: the result must stand on the historical proportionality cases and AEDPA, not an unsupplied or backdated simulation rule.

## Revisions to the existing positions

These replace the corresponding pre-vote positions, not the supplied holdings themselves.

### OT2001: Lapides v. Board of Regents

**Stone’s position.** Reject the State’s federal-forum objection and remand for adjudication or another independently lawful disposition. Voluntary removal supplies consent to the selected forum, not consent to liability. The supplied *Lapides* holding already establishes that distinction for removed state-law claims amenable to adjudication in state court. Extending the proposition to every category of removed claim would be a further extension, not something the existing entry has already decided. 

**Proposed holding.** A State that voluntarily removes the claims presented cannot object to their adjudication merely because the forum is federal. Removal does not create a cause of action, enlarge substantive liability or damages, surrender substantive defenses, or displace independently applicable remedial-autonomy rules.

**Legal move.** Apply the existing forum-consent rule. Preserve the distinction between that holding’s established scope and Stone’s broader proposed principle.

### OT2001: Federal Maritime Commission v. South Carolina State Ports Authority

**Stone’s position.** Vacate and remand. Identify the federal shipping obligation, the Commission’s statutory authority, the relief requested, and the Port Authority’s operational and fiscal status. Private initiation neither immunizes the proceeding from review nor turns every requested remedy into permissible relief. This approach is already substantially embodied in the supplied *FMC* holding. 

**Proposed holding.** A privately initiated complaint may invoke valid, congressionally authorized administrative adjudication of prospective compliance with federal shipping law. Retrospective compensation requires separate determination of entity status and an authorized remedial route, including the simulation’s remedial-autonomy, federal-fallback, and commercial-enterprise rules.

**Legal move.** Apply the supplied remedy-specific approach, not create another sovereign-immunity test.

### OT2002: Grutter v. Bollinger

**Stone’s position.** Affirm narrowly under strict scrutiny. Selective admission allocates individual seats, so the supplied structural-access exception does not lower the standard. The university must substantiate the educational function served by meaningful cross-racial participation; “critical mass” cannot establish either the interest or the fit by repetition. Applicant-specific consideration remains potentially permissible, but racial membership cannot automatically establish an experience, viewpoint, qualification, or predetermined advantage. The supplied *Adarand* rule makes actual allocation decisive, not the institution’s preferred description. 

**Proposed holding.** The described individualized admissions process may consider an applicant’s demonstrated race-related experiences under common evaluative criteria in pursuing a substantiated compelling educational interest. It may not reserve seats, assign automatic racial-status value, operate hidden racial targets, or substitute racial stereotypes for individual assessment. Narrow tailoring requires serious consideration of workable race-neutral alternatives and meaningful limits, review, and termination.

**Legal move and record limit.** Apply strict scrutiny, with a narrow educational-interest extension. Auditability concerns whether actual compliance can be examined, not whether prose is constitutionally preferable to scoring; affirmance cannot establish the absence of hidden practices that the supplied record does not address.

### OT2002: Gratz v. Bollinger

**Stone’s position.** Invalidate the automatic racial award. The defect is predetermined decisional value attached to racial status, not the use of numbers. Under this proposed boundary, reducing the award from twenty points to five does not cure the categorical substitution of racial membership for applicant-specific evaluation. A transparent rubric evaluating actual qualifications and experiences remains permissible.

**Proposed holding.** An admissions mechanism defended by educational diversity may not automatically assign decisional credit solely because an applicant belongs to a designated racial category, whether that credit is numerical or concealed in an automatic nonnumerical preference. Numerical evaluation of applicant-specific qualifications and experiences is not prohibited.

**Legal move and limit.** This expressly narrows permissible means within educational-diversity admissions, beyond merely finding the particular twenty-point award excessive. It does not silently invalidate every race-conscious remedy for an independently established constitutional or statutory violation, a separate subject in the supplied precedent. 

### OT2002: Georgia v. Ashcroft

**Stone’s position.** Vacate and remand, replacing the previous reconstruction’s affirmance. The proposed clarification identifies the protected opportunity before assessing retrogression: a practical, reasonably durable ability to elect demonstrated candidates of choice. District-specific findings supply the planwide comparison. Reliable crossover and independently lawful coalition behavior may matter, but influence, responsiveness, party affiliation, incumbency, and officeholding cannot simply be aggregated into an equivalent electoral opportunity. The supplied *Bossier Parish* decisions retain the lawful benchmark, the jurisdiction’s burden, and the distinct purpose inquiry. 

**Proposed holding.** Section 5 protects practical electoral opportunity rather than a fixed racial population percentage. The court must construct its planwide retrogression determination from district-specific evidence of reasonably durable ability to elect, without substituting generalized influence or political access for that ability. The case should be remanded for application of that distinction.

**Legal move and limit.** Clarify the content of effective electoral opportunity and remand under that clarification. No numerical threshold, prescribed statistical method, Section 2 holding, or future *Alabama* result is assumed.

### OT2002: Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs

**Stone’s position.** Affirm abrogation for the family-care provision and permit the suit. The argument concerns the relationship among documented state sex-role stereotyping, the enforcement difficulty, and a limited gender-neutral entitlement addressing that mechanism. It is not a judgment that disability discrimination receives less substantive concern. The supplied *Garrett* itself recognizes bounded accommodation and intentional-discrimination claims. 

**Proposed holding.** Congress may authorize damages enforcement of the limited family-care entitlement against States because that bounded, gender-neutral measure directly prevents and remedies the documented mechanism of unconstitutional sex discrimination in leave administration. The employee must still establish the statutory claim and entitlement to the requested remedy.

**Legal move and limit.** Extend *Garrett*’s bounded preventive-enforcement principle. The supplied *Boerne* formulation contains restrictive, enumerated mechanisms for broader-than-constitutional prohibitions; insofar as their exclusivity would foreclose this family-care measure, Stone must openly modify that exclusivity rather than call legislative history alone sufficient. Self-care, unrelated benefits, unlimited paid leave, and other FMLA applications remain unresolved. 

### OT2002: Lawrence v. Texas

**Stone’s position.** Reverse on substantive liberty and invalidate the prohibition as applied to private, actually consensual intimacy between adults capable of consent. The liberty-first instruction changes the earlier rung-six choice: narrowness now limits the conduct protected rather than avoiding the liberty question through equal protection. Stone’s current character expressly rejects criminalization of consensual adult intimacy through moral disapproval. 

**Proposed holding.** Government may not criminalize private, consensual sexual intimacy between adults capable of consent solely to enforce moral condemnation or conformity. *Bowers v. Hardwick* should be overruled to the extent it authorizes that criminal prohibition.

**Legal move and limit.** Recognize the protected liberty and candidly overrule the contrary precedent, rather than distinguish it away. This decides no question involving minors, coercion, exploitation, abuse of dependency or authority, commercial sex, public conduct, independently cognizable injury, marriage, adoption, benefits, employment, military administration, or public accommodations.

### OT2002: Limon v. Kansas

**Stone’s position.** Reverse and remand under intermediate scrutiny. Generalize the supplied public-employment principle to express sexual-orientation classifications and their operative proxies. The relevant question is not whether protecting minors is important, but whether the additional punishment imposed on otherwise comparable same-sex conduct substantially advances that actual objective without stereotype. The existing employment cases support the extension but do not already establish a universal tier. 

**Proposed holding.** Express sexual-orientation classifications receive intermediate scrutiny: government must establish an actual important objective and a substantial, nonstereotyped relationship between the unequal treatment and that objective. The challenged punishment distinction must be reconsidered under that standard, without treating moral condemnation or conventional sexual roles as sufficient governmental interests.

**Legal move and limit.** Different scrutiny assignment through an express extension beyond public employment, consistent with Stone’s current Elevated classification. This neither establishes a right to sexual activity with minors nor depends on treating the proposed *Lawrence* position as an already adopted holding. 

## New simulation grants

I use your questions as the scope of each grant. Additional record details come from the cited lower-court opinions. Because no simulated merits dates were supplied, the real authorities used below were already available by the respective grant dates; the proposed positions above are not added to the controlling precedent set.

### 1. FA1: Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists

**Stone’s position.** Affirm the First Amendment basis for FACE liability and the targeted injunction, without independently approving every damages determination or RICO theory. The decisive distinction is between public advocacy that somebody fears and a communication deliberately used to place identified people in apprehension of unlawful bodily harm. The reported record connects the particular posters and physician listings to a known pattern of similarly identified physicians being murdered. Stone would examine the complete jury instructions and defendant-specific participation, not substitute the judge’s injunction findings for findings required to support the jury’s verdict. The existing punitive-damages remand remains separate. ([Justia Law][1])

**Precedent and reasoning.** Actual *Watts*, *Claiborne Hardware*, and *Virginia v. Black* supply the relevant distinctions. *Black*, available before the June 27, 2003 grant, permits consideration of threatening context and distinguishes intent to intimidate from intent actually to carry out violence. An actual purpose to induce apprehension of bodily harm, combined with a communication objectively conveying a serious threat, supplies the culpability required here; objective foreseeability alone does not. A claimant need not prove an express first-person promise of violence. Supplied *Rice v. Paladin* reinforces the rejection of liability from association or foreseeable misuse alone, but its criminal-assistance elements do not replace FACE’s separate elements. ([Legal Information Institute][2]) 

**Proposed holding.** Public posters and online identification can constitute true threats when, in context, they seriously communicate unlawful violence against identifiable people and are intentionally used to place those people in apprehension of bodily harm. The intent to intimidate required here, together with that objectively threatening communication, is sufficient without proof that the defendants intended personally to execute the threatened violence. Mere publication of identifying information, foreseeable fear, or agreement with an unpopular movement is insufficient.

**Legal move and limit.** Apply and clarify existing threat doctrine in this civil statutory setting. No general civil-negligence standard for threats, presumption from political viewpoint, or categorical prohibition on naming physicians is created.

### 2. FA2: North Jersey Media Group, Inc. v. Ashcroft

**Stone’s position.** Reverse the categorical rejection of a qualified access right and remand for proceeding-specific closure review. The Executive’s designation cannot itself answer the constitutional question. Stone would examine removal adjudication as the relevant proceeding, rather than define the category circularly as “hearings the Executive has designated secret.” The Government’s historical and national-security arguments deserve consideration, including its argument that separately innocuous disclosures can combine to expose an investigation. They do not make executive designation conclusive. ([Department of Justice][3])

**Precedent and reasoning.** No supplied simulated holding settles access to these hearings. Stone would extend the actual *Richmond Newspapers*, *Globe Newspaper*, and *Press-Enterprise* access principles through their experience-and-logic inquiry. He would not invent an uninterrupted historical tradition of openness; the history is contested, and recognized closure exceptions are part of it. His conclusion would rest on the relevant history of ordinarily accessible adjudication and openness’s contribution to reliable, accountable removal proceedings. Necessary protection of classified information, witnesses, or investigations can justify a properly supported closure, including reliance on sealed evidence where necessary. ([OpenJurist][4])

**Proposed holding.** The press and public possess a qualified First Amendment right of access to the removal hearings presented. Closure must rest on reviewable findings connecting a sufficiently weighty interest to the particular proceeding and explaining why narrower measures would not adequately protect it. An Executive designation covering an entire category is not itself a sufficient substitute.

**Legal move and limit.** Extend existing access doctrine, using its existing closure standard rather than constructing a national-security framework. No absolute entitlement to classified evidence, unrestricted access to every administrative proceeding, or immediate opening of a hearing without evaluating a properly supported closure request.

### 3. R1: Tenafly Eruv Association, Inc. v. Borough of Tenafly

**Stone’s position.** Affirm the preliminary-injunction judgment against selective enforcement. A claimant need not prove that the practice is compulsory under religious doctrine before objecting to discriminatory governmental treatment. Sincerely undertaken religious exercise is protected against selective exclusion even when adherents could decline the practice without abandoning the faith. The reported arrangement involved privately supported attachments, utility-owner permission, and tolerated comparable secular attachments. That is materially different from government financing devotional instruction or requiring unwilling third parties to subsidize religious observance. ([Justia Law][5])

**Precedent and reasoning.** Apply *Smith* and *Lukumi* to the actual enforcement pattern. The relevant defect is the absence of neutral, generally applicable administration, not failure to prove that an otherwise neutral property rule coerced abandonment of a mandatory ritual. The supplied distinction between equal civic access and direct religious financing supports rejecting the Establishment Clause defense. There is no need to characterize the eruv itself as an expressive message to reach the Free Exercise claim. 

**Proposed holding.** Selective governmental exclusion of a sincere religious practice may violate Free Exercise without proof that the practice is religiously mandatory or that government compelled abandonment of the faith. The Establishment Clause does not require removal of privately financed religious attachments, installed with lawful owner permission, merely because comparable secular attachments are tolerated.

**Legal move and limit.** Apply neutrality and equal-access principles; no meaningful distinctive Stone innovation. The ruling concerns preliminary protection and does not authorize trespass, compelled private financing, interference with utility safety, or exemption from genuinely evenhanded attachment rules.

### 4. V1: Save Palisade FruitLands v. Todd

**Stone’s position.** Affirm under ordinary rational-basis review. The constitutional distinction is between distributing governmental powers among genuinely different county structures and excluding otherwise comparable participants from the same existing electoral decision. Colorado’s home-rule and statutory counties have different allocations of governmental authority, and initiative power forms part of the home-rule arrangement. That provides a rational structural explanation without requiring Colorado to prove that initiative would harm statutory counties or that its chosen allocation is necessary. The isolated Logan County episode does not, on the reported record, establish a continuing selectively administered initiative system. ([Justia Law][6])

**Precedent and reasoning.** The constitutional part of supplied *Mixon* supports this limited extension: no freestanding right requires every local body to be elective, while independently unlawful discrimination remains actionable. One qualification to your analysis matters: *Mixon* also recognizes a potential Section 2 claim when government eliminates an existing electoral opportunity. Its protection is not limited to proof of unconstitutional purpose. Nothing in *Save Palisade* should retract that statutory holding.  

**Proposed holding.** Equal protection permits Colorado to allocate county initiative authority to home-rule counties as part of their broader self-government structure without granting identical authority to statutory counties. The distinction survives ordinary rational-basis review because it does not exclude comparable voters from the same existing electoral decision or establish another independently forbidden classification or purpose.

**Legal move and limit.** Extend *Mixon*’s constitutional structural principle while preserving its statutory branch. Creating an initiative mechanism, regulating advocacy within it, and treating votes once submitted are distinct questions governed by existing doctrines, not a necessary new three-stage test. *Meyer*, supplied *Buckley v. ACLF*, *Reform Party*, voting-equality rules, and *Olech* retain their respective work; ordinary advocacy also remains protected where no initiative mechanism exists.

### 5. B1: State v. Oakley

**Stone’s position.** Reverse insofar as Wisconsin sustained the procreation condition, vacate that condition, and remand for lawful sentencing or probation terms. The established willful failure to support existing children remains punishable. It does not itself authorize the State to make further procreation conditional on a judicial assessment of financial capacity and willingness. The condition directly regulates whether Oakley may have another child, rather than merely regulating earnings, payment, employment, or compliance with existing support obligations. The availability of imprisonment as an alternative does not automatically validate every condition offered in its place. ([Justia Law][7])

**Precedent and reasoning.** Actual *Skinner*, *Carey*, and *Zablocki* supply the protected-procreation and direct-interference principles; *Bearden* keeps inability separate from willful refusal. Stone would apply strict scrutiny to this direct reproductive restriction. That is an express extension of the fundamental-right protection into this probation setting, not a claim that the Supreme Court had already decided an identical condition. Probation-search precedents do not transfer a reduced privacy standard into unrestricted governmental control over reproduction. Stone’s character independently treats bodily and intimate choice as protected liberty, while requiring the governing legal move to be identified. ([Justia Law][7]) 

**Proposed holding.** A probation condition directly prohibiting further procreation unless the probationer demonstrates the prescribed financial capacity must satisfy strict scrutiny. The condition presented is not narrowly tailored to enforcing existing support obligations and therefore cannot stand.

**Legal move and limit.** Different scrutiny assignment in the probation context, followed by application. No invalidation of the nonsupport convictions, entitlement to avoid lawful support enforcement, or approval of automatic revocation because a person cannot pay despite genuine efforts.

### 6. D1: Krimstock v. Kelly

**Stone’s position.** Affirm the Second Circuit’s requirement of meaningful interim review and remand for implementation. Initial seizure and continued retention are separate deprivations. An eventual forfeiture judgment does not answer whether government needs possession throughout the intervening period, particularly when loss of the vehicle affects work, medical access, or family obligations. The reported practice allowed vehicles to remain impounded for months or longer without a hearing addressing that possessory deprivation. ([Justia Law][8])

**Precedent and reasoning.** Apply *Mathews* and extend the procedural principle reflected in supplied *James Daniel Good*. A movable vehicle may justify immediate seizure in circumstances unlike real property, but that distinction does not establish indefinite retention authority. The hearing must examine the probable forfeiture basis, the governmental need for continued possession, and effective alternatives. Stone would require government to justify the continued hold, while leaving the hearing’s precise mechanics and any proof quantum not resolved by the question to lawful implementation. This is a retention-specific procedural requirement, not a new merits test for forfeiture.  ([Justia Law][8])

**Proposed holding.** Due process requires a prompt opportunity for neutral review of continued vehicle retention pending civil forfeiture. The review must address the probable validity of forfeiture, the necessity of continued governmental possession, and whether a bond or another practicable arrangement adequately protects the governmental interest.

**Legal move and limit.** Extend existing due-process protection and specify the interim issue to be adjudicated. No automatic return, fixed constitutional deadline, full forfeiture trial at the preliminary hearing, or assumption that an unaffordable bond necessarily supplies meaningful relief; independently justified evidentiary custody remains separately assessable.

### 7. D2: Fairley v. Luman

**Stone’s position.** Affirm the municipal judgment. The reported record involves twelve days of detention on the twin brother’s warrants, repeated identification objections, discrepancies in available identifying information, and accessible means of verification. The municipal claim concerns the absence of adequate procedures for correcting continued mistaken-identity detention, not simply whether the original arrest involved excessive force or whether a particular arresting officer incurred personal liability. ([Justia Law][9])

**Precedent and reasoning.** Actual *Baker v. McCollan* does not make a facially valid warrant conclusive against every later procedural claim. *Mathews*, *Monell*, and *City of Canton* require analysis of the liberty deprivation, feasible safeguards, municipal responsibility, and causation. Supplied *Carter* recognizes deliberate-indifference claims based on a known pattern or obvious recurring constitutional risk; supplied *Plakas* bars municipal liability without a constitutional injury, but does not erase a distinct institutional injury because another claim against individual officers failed.  

**Proposed holding.** A facially valid warrant does not eliminate the duty to provide timely, reasonable identity-verification procedures when credible notice and readily available identifying evidence materially undermine the basis for continuing detention. A municipality may be liable when its deliberately indifferent failure to provide those procedures causes the continued deprivation.

**Legal move and limit.** Apply municipal-liability doctrine and clarify its operation for continued mistaken-identity detention. This creates neither strict liability for every identification error nor a new qualified-immunity exception; the municipal claim requires its own constitutional injury, fault, and causal connection.

### 8. P1: Bechhoefer v. United States Department of Justice, DEA

**Stone’s position.** Affirm on the reported record, while rejecting a categorical “desk-drawer exception.” An informal or unregistered agency collection can qualify when it actually functions as an identifier-based retrieval system. But the Act does not cover every identifying document merely because an agency employee possesses it. Following discovery, the reported evidence described the letters among unrelated papers, without incorporation into an identifier-retrieved collection; the claim that recognizing the author’s letterhead necessarily established a statutory system did not supply the missing organizational or retrieval practice. ([Justia Law][10])

**Precedent and reasoning.** The controlling question is the enacted distinction between a “record” and a record contained in a “system of records.” No supplied simulated holding eliminates the latter requirement. Stone would reject both extremes: formal filing is not indispensable, but a name appearing on a document does not itself establish the required system. Agency labels and an employee’s preferred storage location cannot decide coverage; the actual agency-controlled collection and retrieval method must do that work. The supplied constitutional privacy cases cannot replace this statutory element or manufacture a different action against the DEA. ([Law Resource][11]) 

**Proposed holding.** An agency-controlled collection may be a system of records when it functions to retrieve information through personal identifiers, whether maintained formally or informally. Mere possession of an identifying letter in an otherwise unsystematized assortment of papers does not establish that requirement. The statutory predicate is not established on the record presented.

**Legal move and limit.** Statutory interpretation and narrow application. No categorical immunity for unofficial files, permission to evade an existing system by relocating its records, or revival of the separately dismissed and unappealed First Amendment claim.

### 9. S1: United States v. Bach

**Stone’s position.** Affirm the Eighth Circuit’s rejection of suppression based on the officer’s physical absence. Accept the granted assumption that the stored communications receive Fourth Amendment protection. Having the provider retrieve material under a valid warrant is a method of executing judicial authority, not a reason to declare the communications abandoned or the retrieval a constitutionally irrelevant private search. The reported dispute concerned provider employees performing the electronic retrieval rather than officers entering and searching the provider’s premises. ([Justia Law][12])

**Precedent and reasoning.** Actual *Dalia* rejects a universal requirement that warrants prescribe every execution detail, while *New York Telephone* recognizes lawful technical assistance with execution. Stone would apply those principles without equating assistance with an unrestricted delegation of search authority. The warrant must determine the authorized account and material; the provider may perform the technical work, but officers remain responsible for the constitutionally attributable search they direct. Whether the actual production exceeded the warrant is distinct from whether an officer had to stand beside the technician. ([Justia Law][12])

**Proposed holding.** The Fourth Amendment does not categorically require an officer’s physical presence while a service provider retrieves and delivers communications pursuant to a valid warrant. Provider-assisted execution must remain reasonably confined to the warrant’s authorization and subject to ordinary constitutional accountability for the government-directed search.

**Legal move and limit.** Apply existing warrant-execution principles to electronic retrieval. No third-party abandonment rule, unrestricted provider discretion, automatic approval of every item produced, or new mandatory technical protocol.

### 10. PR1: Westside Mothers v. Haveman

**Stone’s position.** Affirm the judgment allowing the specified childhood screening-and-treatment claims to proceed, and remand for adjudication of eligibility, breach, responsibility, and relief. This grant supersedes the earlier certiorari-denial position because the user has now placed the merits before Stone. The decision should identify the particular childhood-service commands, not pronounce Medicaid enforceable or unenforceable as a whole. The lower-court record was not sufficiently developed to resolve the State’s alternative factual defenses. ([Justia Law][13])

**Precedent and reasoning.** Apply supplied *Blessing* and *Gonzaga*. The relevant provisions identify eligible children and prescribe screening, diagnostic, and necessary treatment duties, rather than merely demand a satisfactory statewide performance percentage. Read the beneficiary and service provisions together, including the applicable parts of §§ 1396a(a)(10), 1396a(a)(43), and 1396d(r); do not convert an aggregate reporting obligation into an individual entitlement. Section 1983 supplies the enforcement mechanism, so this is not invention of a new implied cause of action. General federal funding oversight does not itself demonstrate congressional displacement of that mechanism.  ([Department of Justice][14])

**Proposed holding.** The specified mandatory childhood screening, diagnostic, and treatment provisions confer sufficiently definite individual federal rights on eligible children enforceable through § 1983. Their placement within a federally funded state-plan statute does not reduce them to aggregate funding conditions. The plaintiffs may proceed against proper defendants for independently authorized relief, subject to proof of the particular statutory violations.

**Legal move and limit.** Statutory interpretation under the existing rights-enforcement doctrine. No guarantee of every requested treatment or provider, adjudicated systemwide violation, new reimbursement rate, automatic damages remedy, or assumption that every subsection of Medicaid creates an individual right.

### 11. AD1: Heimmermann v. First Union Mortgage Corp.

**Stone’s position.** Affirm the vacatur of class certification and the remand, but reject the broader proposition that any agency document becomes binding whenever Congress authorizes “interpretations.” Here Stone would read the specific RESPA architecture, including the express interpretive delegation, the statutory treatment of good-faith conformity, and the published, formally identified policy statement, as supporting an authoritative delegated interpretation. He would then determine independently whether it remains within the statute. His personal preference for independent interpretation cannot displace supplied precedent recognizing controlling agency weight within a valid delegation. ([Justia Law][15]) 

**Answer to the two questions.** First, notice-and-comment procedure is not an invariable prerequisite to controlling interpretive weight under the governing *Mead* approach, but express interpretive language is not a universal exemption from procedures required for new legislative duties. Second, the relevant earlier circuit decision relied on its reading of ambiguous HUD guidance; an authorized clarification may remove that interpretive premise without giving HUD power to override a fixed judicial determination of statutory meaning. Stone would therefore avoid announcing a general agency-over-circuit-precedent rule. Supplied *Appalachian Power* continues to prohibit binding duties unsupported by the governing source and required procedure. ([Justia Law][15]) 

**Proposed holding.** The published interpretation presented may receive controlling weight within genuine RESPA ambiguity because it exercises the statute’s specifically delegated interpretive authority; the absence of notice and comment is not alone dispositive. An earlier judicial construction of ambiguous agency guidance does not prevent a lawful agency clarification, although a fixed judicial construction of the statute remains a constraint. Class certification must be reconsidered under the legally correct substantive standard.

**Legal move and limit.** Apply the supplied delegation-and-deference rules and distinguish interpretation of prior guidance from interpretation of statutory meaning. No agency power to erase clear statutory prohibitions, reopen final judgments, deem substantive changes retroactively valid merely by calling them clarifications, declare all yield-spread premiums lawful, or categorically prohibit class treatment under the correct standard.
