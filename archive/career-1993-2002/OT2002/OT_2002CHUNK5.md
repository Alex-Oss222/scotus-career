#### Current reusable doctrine

**Claim-specific enforcement under Fourteenth Amendment § 5**

**Current rule:** A court first determines whether the plaintiff states the statutory claim on the conduct alleged and whether that same conduct independently violates a Fourteenth Amendment guarantee as this Court has construed it. If the conduct independently violates the guarantee, the statute is valid enforcement as applied to that conduct, the remedy Congress provided attaches to that claim, and the § 5 inquiry ends. If the conduct does not independently violate the guarantee, the statute must satisfy the fitted requirements drawn from its enacted terms and the governing constitutional standard. A statutory prohibition that reaches substantially more rational-basis state conduct than the Fourteenth Amendment forbids and lacks a fitted remedial, evidentiary, coverage, reauthorization, or bailout boundary cannot support retrospective state-treasury liability under § 5. Bounded Title I duties validly abrogate immunity for damages claims alleging intentional disability discrimination against an otherwise-qualified state employee and denial of a specific necessary reasonable accommodation when the employee can perform essential functions and the measure creates no fundamental alteration, undue hardship, or direct threat. Section 5 does not authorize untethered constitutional damages liability against a private assailant for private conduct.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** First, the operative command must run against a State, its subdivisions, or persons acting under state authority. Second, the statute must be referable to a specific Fourteenth Amendment guarantee, using this Court’s standard for state action in that field as the baseline. Third, a statute reaching only conduct that baseline forbids is valid without more. A statute that also reaches state conduct the Constitution permits is valid only if it allocates proof under the constitutional standard, supplies remedial or compliance machinery attached to that standard, or imposes a broader prohibition while either preserving a defense of constitutional compliance on the same evidence that would defeat a constitutional claim or using an enacted coverage criterion with a stated reauthorization interval or a bailout keyed to present conditions. Fourth, Congress may not supply the rule of decision for whether the Constitution has been violated, direct the standard of review courts apply to a constitutional claim, or legislate so that the constitutional standard governs for no purpose. When the constitutional violation includes a mental-state or remedial-inadequacy element, the plaintiff must plead and prove that element to proceed on the independently unconstitutional conduct. When retrospective state-treasury liability depends on § 5 abrogation, the same claim-specific validity inquiry determines whether § 5 supplies that authority.

**Exceptions and limits:** The volume of congressional findings neither satisfies nor defeats any requirement. Validity is assessed claim by claim and remedy by remedy, and failure as to one application does not invalidate the statute or provision as to others. Validity is measured against the constitutional standard at enactment; a later narrowing operates prospectively. Congress may prevent and remedy unconstitutional conduct by States and state officials and may adopt fitted measures directed to official obstruction or discriminatory administration. The rule does not decide RFRA’s validity as applied to the Federal Government, authority under another enumerated power or a lawful spending condition, or broader state-law protection of religious exercise. Failure of the § 5 route does not by itself erase a valid substantive duty enacted under another constitutional power.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 8540–8563, “Claim-specific enforcement under Fourteenth Amendment § 5”.

**Article I duties, remedial autonomy, and federal fallback**

**Current rule:** A valid exercise of Article I may impose substantive federal obligations on States. Congress may unmistakably authorize prospective enforcement directly against a State when it acts within an enumerated Article I power, identifies the State as defendant, creates the cause of action, and supplies a constitutionally permissible remedy. For a private retrospective compensatory monetary claim, a nonconsenting State may channel enforcement through an adequate state remedial system but may not extinguish the federal entitlement. When the State supplies no constitutionally and practically adequate substitute and Congress has clearly authorized the monetary remedy, the federal remedial action may proceed.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** The court first identifies a valid Article I duty and the remedy Congress clearly enacted. Prospective direct-State enforcement may proceed within the enacted authorization. For retrospective compensatory relief, the court determines whether the State supplies an adequate remedial forum and remedy. An adequate system controls the remedial channel; the clearly authorized federal action remains available when no adequate substitute exists.

**Exceptions and limits:** Article I does not itself create a cause of action, and statutory clarity remains indispensable. Valid Reconstruction Amendment enforcement, unequivocal state consent, federal public enforcement, lawful prospective officer relief, and commercial-enterprise liability remain independent routes. Seminole Tribe did not decide the treatment of retrospective compensatory relief because IGRA required no treasury award for accrued liability.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 3622–3643, “Article I duties, remedial autonomy, and federal fallback”.

**Waiver of state sovereign immunity**

**Current rule:** Waiver requires an unequivocal and voluntary manifestation of the State’s own consent. A State does not waive immunity merely by engaging in interstate commercial activity after Congress announces that private suit will follow. A State consents to program-specific federal enforcement when, with clear advance notice, it knowingly accepts federal funds subject to a separately valid condition unmistakably identifying private federal suit and the program duties and remedies to be enforced. Removal waiver is forum specific and creates no cause of action, liability, damages, surrender of substantive defenses, or § 1983 personhood.
