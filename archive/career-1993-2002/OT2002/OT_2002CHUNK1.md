#### Current reusable doctrine

**Reliability of anonymous tips for investigatory stops**

**Current rule:** An anonymous or unidentified accusation supports a *Terry* stop only when the complete circumstances contain information beyond innocent identification bearing on source reliability, the asserted illegality, or the suspect’s conduct. Corroboration of readily observable identity and location is insufficient by itself, and no fixed form of predictive corroboration is indispensable. An immediate face-to-face report may carry added reliability when officers can observe the source, the report claims firsthand knowledge, the described person remains nearby, and prompt corroboration confirms details indicating correct identification. There is no categorical firearm exception.

**Exceptions and limits:** Predictive information, a traceable source, an explained firsthand basis, observed demeanor, immediacy, proximity, independently observed conduct, an emergency, or comparable circumstances may supply reliability. If a person does not submit to a show of authority, conduct before physical force or submission may enter the reasonable-suspicion assessment. The rule does not prescribe the response to a materially different immediate emergency.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 3534–3549, “Reliability of anonymous tips for investigatory stops”.

**Appellate review of reasonable suspicion and probable cause**

**Current rule:** The trial court determines what occurred, resolves credibility, and identifies the immediate factual setting. The appellate court independently applies the constitutional standard to those facts. Practical consideration of local knowledge and police experience does not transfer final authority over the constitutional boundary.

**Exceptions and limits:** Independent review does not authorize appellate factfinding, disregard of supported credibility determinations, or use of a mechanical checklist of suspicious circumstances. The Court does not decide whether suppression is ultimately required.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 3320–3334, “Appellate review of reasonable suspicion and probable cause”.

**Objective reasonableness of a traffic stop**

**Current rule:** The constitutional inquiry ordinarily asks whether the facts objectively authorized the seizure actually performed. A court does not suppress evidence because an officer also possessed an unexpressed investigative motive or because another officer might have exercised enforcement discretion differently.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not authorize a stop lacking objective legal support, fabricated violations, prolongation beyond the traffic mission without independent justification, or discriminatory enforcement. A claim that officers selected motorists because of race remains governed by equal protection and its own proof and remedial rules.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 3305–3319, “Objective reasonableness of a traffic stop”.

**Current rule:** State law is impliedly preempted when simultaneous compliance with state and federal law is impossible or when the state duty materially obstructs a concrete objective embedded in an enacted federal statute, an operative regulation, or an affirmative and lawfully adopted federal decision. The court must identify the federal objective from the legal regime itself and compare the state duty’s operation with the method federal law selected. Conflict exists when state law would eliminate a range of options federal law deliberately preserved, expand sanctions beyond Congress’s chosen coverage, remove flexibility Congress assigned to a federal decisionmaker, create private enforcement of a duty arising solely from an exclusively federal agency relationship, or materially obstruct the federal method for carrying out the enacted policy. Regulatory absence, generalized federal preference, or the mere possibility that state law may influence regulated conduct is insufficient.

**Exceptions and limits:** A savings clause does not preserve a state duty that actually conflicts with the governing federal regime, but it confirms that compatible state duties remain available. Foreign-policy context does not create a free-standing judicial foreign-affairs preemption power; displacement must rest on a concrete conflict with enacted federal law or lawfully operative federal action. Traditional state duties existing independently of federal disclosure or agency-process obligations survive when compliance with both systems is possible and the state rule does not frustrate the identified federal method.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 3831–3848, “Implied conflict preemption”.

**Express preemption without a federal standard in force**

**Current rule:** The Safety Act’s express preemption clause does not displace state requirements concerning a vehicle-safety feature when no federal standard governing the same aspect of performance is in effect.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 3820–3830, “Express preemption without a federal standard in force”.

**General manufacturing and labeling rules**

**Current rule:** Express preemption requires a binding federal requirement applicable to the particular device or the relevant aspect of its performance, a state requirement concerning the same subject, and a material difference or addition between them. Regulatory generality, market permission, or agency inaction does not by itself establish that federal law selected an exclusive device standard.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 3864–3874, “General manufacturing and labeling rules”.

**Federal displacement of state tanker standards**

**Current rule:** Title II of the Ports and Waterways Safety Act occupies the subjects of general vessel design, equipment, operation, manning, and personnel qualification. Title I leaves room for genuinely local waterway measures addressing demonstrated local conditions when they do not conflict with federal law or an affirmative federal determination. The Oil Pollution Act’s saving clauses preserve state liability, compensation, and additional cleanup authority, not independent systemic vessel standards within the occupied Title II subjects.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** The court identifies the subject regulated by the state provision, determines whether Title II occupies it, and, for a genuine local-waterway measure under Title I, separately tests conflict with enacted federal law, a valid regulation, or an affirmative federal determination.

**Exceptions and limits:** Agency preference, a generalized interest in uniformity, regulatory absence, or federal nonregulation alone does not preempt. State measures addressing distinctive channel depth, narrowness, visibility, traffic, or similar local-waterway conditions remain potentially valid outside an occupied field and absent conflict.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 8499–8517, “Federal displacement of state tanker standards”.

**Exceptions and limits:** The Court does not create a private federal cause of action, decide tort liability, or determine preemption for a device subjected to full premarket approval or another specific federal condition.

> The Free Speech Clause protects religious speakers against exclusion from genuinely opened civic and expressive channels, but it does not require a public university to use mandatory student-fee funds to pay directly for the production of devotional instruction, evangelism, or religious formation.

**Recorded force:** This proposition remains controlling as stated through the synchronized cutoff.
