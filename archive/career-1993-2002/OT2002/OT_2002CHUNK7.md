#### Current reusable doctrine

**Section 5 retrogression and Section 2**

**Current rule:** A § 2 violation does not independently establish prohibited effect under § 5. Section 5 effect compares the proposed change with the jurisdiction’s lawful operative baseline and asks whether the change is retrogressive. A nonretrogressive plan does not violate the effects prong merely because it preserves dilution, fails to create a majority-minority district, or is less favorable than an available alternative. Section 2 separately uses the statutory totality and an undiluted benchmark. Preclearance does not decide a separate § 2 claim, and possible § 2 liability does not substitute for the § 5 effect inquiry.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule creates no entitlement to proportional representation or maximization. Nonretrogression resolves only the effects prong and does not provide a safe harbor from the separate prohibition on discriminatory purpose.

**Section 5 discriminatory purpose and identical-change proof**

**Current rule:** Section 5’s purpose condition is independent of its effects condition and is not limited to intent to cause retrogression. A covered jurisdiction may not obtain preclearance for a voting change selected with a racial purpose to deny or abridge voting rights merely because the change is nonretrogressive. #### Current reusable doctrine

**Racial predominance in districting**

**Current rule:** Race is predominant when racial considerations actually control district assignments or materially subordinate traditional race-neutral districting principles in the particular district. Courts examine the actual design process and boundaries, including who was included or excluded, boundary movement, splitting or combining communities and political subdivisions, compactness, contiguity, communities of interest, ordinary political geography, supported political objectives and data, and whether a racial target displaced those ordinary considerations. Geometric bizarreness is relevant evidence but not a threshold element. Where race and party closely correlate, correlation alone does not convert a supported political objective into racial predominance, but deliberate use of race as a partisan proxy remains racial sorting.

**Burdens and proof:** At summary judgment, supported evidence permitting both a racial and a political explanation must be viewed in favor of the nonmoving State. Predominance may not be found for the challenger at that stage unless no reasonable factfinder could accept the political account.

**Exceptions and limits:** Racial awareness, demographic forecasting, or compliance analysis does not alone establish predominance. The inquiry remains district-specific and turns on whether race materially subordinated the principles that otherwise would have shaped the district.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 1551–1571, “Racial predominance in districting”.

**First Gingles precondition and demographic proof**

**Current rule:** A protected group need not categorically exceed 50 percent of citizen voting-age population in the proposed district. A geographically compact and politically cohesive protected group satisfies the first precondition by proving that, in a reasonably configured district, it has a practical ability to elect its preferred candidates with dependable crossover support. The crossover support must be reliable and recurring, not speculative, episodic, or sufficient only to influence an election. Citizen voting-age population is the appropriate demographic measure when citizenship is material, and reliable current demographic evidence may displace decennial census figures.

**Burdens and proof:** The plaintiff must establish the compactness, cohesion, practical ability to elect, and any current demographic facts by a preponderance of the evidence. No clear-and-convincing standard applies to displacement of decennial data.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** Apply this first precondition before the remaining *Gingles* predicates and the statutory totality. Satisfaction of this threshold does not establish § 2 liability.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not recognize a free-standing influence-district claim or decide broader multigroup-coalition theories. It creates no right to proportional representation or maximization.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 2144–2163, “First Gingles precondition and demographic proof”.

**Scrutiny of race-predominant districts**

**Current rule:** Once race predominates, a genuinely fitted, district-specific remedy for a demonstrated constitutional or Voting Rights Act violation receives intermediate scrutiny. Under that lane, the State must establish an important remedial objective grounded in the demonstrated violation and a substantial, district-specific fit between the racial design and the injury. A race-predominant district lacking that remedial predicate, or driven by racial maximization, unnecessary packing or separation, or unsupported racial allocation, receives strict scrutiny, requiring a compelling interest and narrow tailoring.

**Burdens and proof:** After predominance is shown, the State bears the burden of establishing the qualifying remedial lane and satisfying the corresponding scrutiny.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** First determine racial predominance. If predominance is established, classify the district as a genuinely fitted remedial measure or unsupported racial sorting, then apply intermediate or strict scrutiny accordingly.

**Exceptions and limits:** The Court did not decide whether Georgia’s Eleventh District was constitutional or unconstitutional. The classification and applicable scrutiny remained for remand. Racial awareness, demographic forecasting, and compliance analysis alone do not establish predominance.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 1572–1590, “Scrutiny of race-predominant districts”.

**Voting Rights Act § 2 totality, proportionality, and nonmaximization**

**Current rule:** The *Gingles* preconditions are necessary but not invariably sufficient to establish § 2 vote dilution. The court must determine under the full statutory totality whether protected voters have less opportunity than other members of the electorate to participate effectively and elect representatives of choice. Rough proportionality is relevant evidence but neither dispositive nor a safe harbor, and the ability to draw additional majority-minority districts does not by itself establish dilution because § 2 does not require maximization.

**Exceptions and limits:** Districts counted in a proportionality comparison must operate as genuine electoral opportunities. The rule creates no proportional-representation entitlement and leaves free-standing influence-district claims unresolved.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 1795–1809, “Voting Rights Act § 2 totality, proportionality, and nonmaximization”.
