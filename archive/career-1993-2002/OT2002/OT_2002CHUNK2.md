#### Current reusable doctrine

**Judicial weight for agency statutory interpretations**

**Current rule:** The court first uses ordinary interpretive tools to identify and enforce any fixed statutory boundary. If genuine ambiguity or evaluative space remains and the agency adopted its interpretation through an exercise of delegated authority carrying the force of law, a reasonable construction controls. An interpretation in an informal opinion letter, litigation position, or other format lacking delegated legal force receives weight only to the extent its reasoning, consistency, expertise, and power to persuade warrant. An agency does not lose delegated authority merely because it reasonably changes an earlier position, but it must acknowledge and justify the change.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** First determine the statute’s fixed meaning. Second determine whether Congress delegated authority to act with the force of law and whether the interpretation was issued through that authority. Third, apply controlling deference within genuine ambiguity when those predicates are met; otherwise evaluate the interpretation for persuasive weight. Review agency reasoning and record support under the otherwise applicable administrative standards.

**Exceptions and limits:** Deference cannot enlarge an agency’s authority beyond enacted text or resolve a question ordinary interpretation settles. Informal guidance lacking the force of law does not receive controlling statutory deference. Deference to an agency’s interpretation of its own genuinely ambiguous regulation remains governed by *Auer v. Robbins* and does not apply to an unambiguous regulation or an interpretation inconsistent with its text.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 7813–7835, “Judicial weight for agency statutory interpretations”.

**Truthful commercial speech and contradictory regulatory exceptions**

**Current rule:** A restriction on truthful, nonmisleading commercial information about lawful activity must materially advance a substantial governmental interest and have a reasonable fit with that interest. Internal statutory or regulatory contradictions and extensive exceptions leaving comparable conduct, incentives, or speech untouched may establish that the restriction does not directly and materially advance its asserted purpose. A proxy such as the height of in-store advertising requires evidence that the proxy materially identifies the targeted harm and that the restriction is proportionate to it. Governmental authority to regulate the underlying activity does not automatically authorize suppression of truthful speech about that activity.

**Exceptions and limits:** Government may prohibit false or misleading claims and may directly regulate lawful products and conduct within its substantive authority. Neutral rules governing product placement, handling, and sale regulate conduct when they directly restrict access while leaving lawful product communication available. A coherent restriction closely implementing a lawful interstate policy remains governed by its own record and fit.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 2549–2567, “Truthful commercial speech and contradictory regulatory exceptions”.

#### Current reusable doctrine

**Certificate of appealability after a procedural dismissal**

**Current rule:** A certificate should issue only if reasonable jurists could debate both whether the petition states a valid constitutional claim and whether the district court’s procedural ruling was correct.

**Burdens and proof:** The applicant must make both threshold showings. Failure on either component defeats the certificate.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** The reviewing court may address first whichever component is more apparent from the record and arguments, but a certificate requires reasonable debatability on both.

**Exceptions and limits:** The inquiry is a threshold certificate determination, not a merits adjudication of either component. A certificate following a merits disposition is governed by the otherwise applicable substantial-showing standard.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 5834–5850, “Certificate of appealability after a procedural dismissal”.

**Temporal reach of AEDPA chapter 153 and the certificate regime**

**Current rule:** AEDPA’s chapter 153 merits amendments do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996; the filing of the federal application supplies that boundary. Section 2253(c), by contrast, governs an appeal initiated after AEDPA’s effective date even when the underlying district-court habeas proceeding began earlier. The district-court merits stage and the later appellate-certificate stage attach to their respective statutory provisions.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** Determine chapter 153’s merits regime from the date the federal application was filed. Determine the governing certificate regime separately from the date the prisoner initiated the appellate proceeding.

**Exceptions and limits:** Chapter 154 follows its own express pending-case instruction. Successive applications and enactments carrying another temporal command remain governed by their specific provisions.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 5702–5719, “Temporal reach of AEDPA chapter 153 and the certificate regime”.

**Strickland prejudice from omitted capital mitigation**

**Current rule:** The court evaluates the totality of the mitigating evidence that competent investigation and presentation would have made available against the aggravating evidence before the sentencer and asks whether there is a reasonable probability of a different sentencing result. Substantial omitted mitigation must be assessed cumulatively and as part of the defendant’s altered sentencing profile.

**Exceptions and limits:** *Lockhart v. Fretwell* does not displace ordinary *Strickland* prejudice when deficient performance deprived the defendant of mitigating evidence the law permitted the sentencer to consider. The rule does not presume prejudice from deficient investigation or require a new sentence whenever additional mitigating evidence exists.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 6168–6181, “Strickland prejudice from omitted capital mitigation”.

**Prisoner-attributable failure to develop facts under § 2254(e)(2)**

**Current rule:** The opening clause of § 2254(e)(2) is triggered only when lack of diligence or greater fault in developing the facts is attributable to the prisoner or counsel. The absence of a completed state-court factual record by itself is insufficient. Diligence ordinarily requires a reasonable and timely effort, in light of the information available, to investigate and present the claim through the state procedures provided.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** Determine first whether prisoner-attributable lack of diligence or greater fault caused the undeveloped factual basis. Only if that threshold is met do § 2254(e)(2)’s further statutory conditions restrict a federal hearing.

**Exceptions and limits:** State prevention of factual development despite reasonable efforts does not activate the heightened bar. When available investigative leads were not reasonably pursued, the failure is attributable to the defense, and a hearing is unavailable unless the statute’s further conditions are satisfied.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 5819–5833, “Prisoner-attributable failure to develop facts under § 2254(e)(2)”.

**Contrary-to and unreasonable-application review under § 2254(d)(1)**

**Current rule:** A state decision is contrary to clearly established Supreme Court law when it applies a rule opposite to the governing rule or reaches a different result on materially indistinguishable facts. A state court unreasonably applies clearly established law when it identifies the correct rule but applies it in an objectively unreasonable manner. An incorrect application alone does not authorize habeas relief.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** Identify the Supreme Court holdings constituting clearly established law at the time of the state adjudication; determine separately whether the state decision is contrary to that law; if not, determine whether its application of the correct rule was objectively unreasonable.

**Exceptions and limits:** Clearly established law consists of holdings, not dicta, of this Court. Objective unreasonableness is not measured by whether every reasonable jurist would agree, and federal habeas review is not ordinary de novo correction of legal error.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 5804–5818, “Contrary-to and unreasonable-application review under § 2254(d)(1)”.

#### Current reusable doctrine

**Qualified-immunity review uses the complete body of law**

**Current rule:** Because clearly established law is an objective legal question reviewed de novo, a court must consider all materially relevant precedent available at the time of the challenged conduct, including authority not cited to the district court. A party’s incomplete legal research does not bind the court to an incomplete statement of governing law. Clearly established law does not categorically require a materially identical prior case; governing principles, obviousness, training, regulations, official warnings, and the official’s own conduct may provide fair notice in context.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not excuse failure to preserve the claim itself or the factual premises on which it depends.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 57–75, “Qualified-immunity review uses the complete body of law”.

**Governmental use and disclosure of sensitive identifying information**

**Current rule:** Identifiable prescription and medical records, sexual orientation, and a governmental compilation linking a named person to nonpublic current residential and identifying information receive limited Fourteenth Amendment protection against unjustified governmental acquisition, examination, use, threatened use, and disclosure. Personally identifying access, coercive use, or dissemination must remain genuinely and proportionately connected to the actual governmental function. Relevant considerations include sensitivity, whether names or addresses rather than aggregate or coded data are necessary, who receives access, the use made of the information, the scope of disclosure, and the safeguards actually maintained. A signature demanded as a condition of public employment does not itself establish constitutionally sufficient consent to unlimited governmental access.

**Exceptions and limits:** The Constitution creates no general right to conceal a public conviction or every fact obtainable from public sources. Aggregate or coded auditing, an identified inquiry prompted by concrete fraud or eligibility concerns, legally required disclosure, confidential retention, and targeted notification to persons with a concrete protective need may present different cases. Perfect prevention of every unauthorized disclosure is not required, but recipient eligibility, authorized use, confidentiality, and redistribution controls must be meaningfully fitted to the governmental function. The rule governs governmental, not private-employer, action.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 1098–1117, “Governmental use and disclosure of sensitive identifying information”.

**Constitutional-merits sequence in qualified-immunity review**

**Current rule:** A court deciding qualified immunity should first determine whether the plaintiff-supported facts establish a constitutional violation and should reach clearly established law only if a violation exists. If no violation occurred, the individual-capacity claim ends without an immunity ruling. If a violation is established, fair notice is assessed separately from the complete body of materially relevant law.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** State the governing constitutional boundary and apply it to the plaintiff-supported facts; only then ask whether that boundary was clearly established when the official acted. The sequence does not merge the questions, alter ordinary summary-judgment treatment of disputed facts, or authorize appellate fact reweighing.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 8015–8029, “Constitutional-merits sequence in qualified-immunity review”.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not authorize repeated appeals from unchanged orders, review of discovery management, reconsideration of witness credibility, or repackaging of an evidence-sufficiency argument as a legal issue.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 284–298, “Successive collateral-order appeal of qualified immunity”.

**Interstate commerce, anti-evasion, and material interstate externalities**

**Current rule:** Congress may regulate interstate transactions and the channels through which interstate commerce moves; instrumentalities, persons, and things in interstate commerce and direct threats to them; economic activity whose properly defined class substantially affects an actual interstate market; intrastate conduct whose inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime; and intrastate conduct that Congress, on a materially adequate evidentiary basis, reasonably identifies as causing a substantial interstate externality that individual States cannot reasonably internalize. Noneconomic character is relevant but not categorically dispositive. Generalized chains through social consequences, productivity, insurance, consumption, or similar downstream effects are insufficient without an identified interstate mechanism. When Congress relies on an offense-specific commerce element, that element must establish a genuine connection in the particular case; a nominal jurisdictional phrase or remote historical movement that performs no present constitutional work is insufficient.

**Burdens and proof:** Courts independently determine whether the asserted category falls within an enumerated commerce route. Congress’s predictive and empirical judgments receive reasonable respect when supported by an identified interstate mechanism and materially adequate evidence, but legislative findings do not create constitutional authority merely by reciting interstate commerce. The Government must genuinely prove any offense-specific commerce element on which federal authority depends.

**Exceptions and limits:** National importance alone is not an interstate mechanism. Generalized interests in biodiversity, scientific research, tourism, development, environmental quality, or possible future economic value do not establish a material interstate externality without evidence connecting the regulated class to a substantial spillover that individual States cannot reasonably internalize. An intrastate species or noneconomic activity is not automatically beyond federal authority when the anti-evasion, anti-impairment, or externality requirements are satisfied.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 3972–3992, “Interstate commerce, anti-evasion, and material interstate externalities”.

**Intrastate actual-child-pornography possession within an interstate-market prohibition**

**Interstate commerce, anti-evasion, and material interstate externalities**

**Current rule:** Congress may regulate interstate transactions and the channels through which interstate commerce moves; instrumentalities, persons, and things in interstate commerce and direct threats to them; economic activity whose properly defined class substantially affects an actual interstate market; intrastate conduct whose inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime; and intrastate conduct that Congress, on a materially adequate evidentiary basis, reasonably identifies as causing a substantial interstate externality that individual States cannot reasonably internalize. Noneconomic character is relevant but not categorically dispositive. Generalized chains through social consequences, productivity, insurance, consumption, or similar downstream effects are insufficient without an identified interstate mechanism. When Congress relies on an offense-specific commerce element, that element must establish a genuine connection in the particular case; a nominal jurisdictional phrase or remote historical movement that performs no present constitutional work is insufficient.

**Burdens and proof:** Courts independently determine whether the asserted category falls within an enumerated commerce route. Congress’s predictive and empirical judgments receive reasonable respect when supported by an identified interstate mechanism and materially adequate evidence, but legislative findings do not create constitutional authority merely by reciting interstate commerce. The Government must genuinely prove any offense-specific commerce element on which federal authority depends.

**Exceptions and limits:** National importance alone is not an interstate mechanism. Generalized interests in biodiversity, scientific research, tourism, development, environmental quality, or possible future economic value do not establish a material interstate externality without evidence connecting the regulated class to a substantial spillover that individual States cannot reasonably internalize. An intrastate species or noneconomic activity is not automatically beyond federal authority when the anti-evasion, anti-impairment, or externality requirements are satisfied.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 3972–3992, “Interstate commerce, anti-evasion, and material interstate externalities”.

**Claim-specific enforcement under Fourteenth Amendment § 5**

**Current rule:** A court first determines whether the plaintiff states the statutory claim on the conduct alleged and whether that same conduct independently violates a Fourteenth Amendment guarantee as this Court has construed it. If the conduct independently violates the guarantee, the statute is valid enforcement as applied to that conduct, the remedy Congress provided attaches to that claim, and the § 5 inquiry ends. If the conduct does not independently violate the guarantee, the statute must satisfy the fitted requirements drawn from its enacted terms and the governing constitutional standard. A statutory prohibition that reaches substantially more rational-basis state conduct than the Fourteenth Amendment forbids and lacks a fitted remedial, evidentiary, coverage, reauthorization, or bailout boundary cannot support retrospective state-treasury liability under § 5. Bounded Title I duties validly abrogate immunity for damages claims alleging intentional disability discrimination against an otherwise-qualified state employee and denial of a specific necessary reasonable accommodation when the employee can perform essential functions and the measure creates no fundamental alteration, undue hardship, or direct threat. Section 5 does not authorize untethered constitutional damages liability against a private assailant for private conduct.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** First, the operative command must run against a State, its subdivisions, or persons acting under state authority. Second, the statute must be referable to a specific Fourteenth Amendment guarantee, using this Court’s standard for state action in that field as the baseline. Third, a statute reaching only conduct that baseline forbids is valid without more. A statute that also reaches state conduct the Constitution permits is valid only if it allocates proof under the constitutional standard, supplies remedial or compliance machinery attached to that standard, or imposes a broader prohibition while either preserving a defense of constitutional compliance on the same evidence that would defeat a constitutional claim or using an enacted coverage criterion with a stated reauthorization interval or a bailout keyed to present conditions. Fourth, Congress may not supply the rule of decision for whether the Constitution has been violated, direct the standard of review courts apply to a constitutional claim, or legislate so that the constitutional standard governs for no purpose. When the constitutional violation includes a mental-state or remedial-inadequacy element, the plaintiff must plead and prove that element to proceed on the independently unconstitutional conduct. When retrospective state-treasury liability depends on § 5 abrogation, the same claim-specific validity inquiry determines whether § 5 supplies that authority.

**Exceptions and limits:** The volume of congressional findings neither satisfies nor defeats any requirement. Validity is assessed claim by claim and remedy by remedy, and failure as to one application does not invalidate the statute or provision as to others. Validity is measured against the constitutional standard at enactment; a later narrowing operates prospectively. Congress may prevent and remedy unconstitutional conduct by States and state officials and may adopt fitted measures directed to official obstruction or discriminatory administration. The rule does not decide RFRA’s validity as applied to the Federal Government, authority under another enumerated power or a lawful spending condition, or broader state-law protection of religious exercise. Failure of the § 5 route does not by itself erase a valid substantive duty enacted under another constitutional power.
