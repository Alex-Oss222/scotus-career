#### Current reusable doctrine

**Judicial weight for agency statutory interpretations**

**Current rule:** The court first uses ordinary interpretive tools to identify and enforce any fixed statutory boundary. If genuine ambiguity or evaluative space remains and the agency adopted its interpretation through an exercise of delegated authority carrying the force of law, a reasonable construction controls. An interpretation in an informal opinion letter, litigation position, or other format lacking delegated legal force receives weight only to the extent its reasoning, consistency, expertise, and power to persuade warrant. An agency does not lose delegated authority merely because it reasonably changes an earlier position, but it must acknowledge and justify the change.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** First determine the statute’s fixed meaning. Second determine whether Congress delegated authority to act with the force of law and whether the interpretation was issued through that authority. Third, apply controlling deference within genuine ambiguity when those predicates are met; otherwise evaluate the interpretation for persuasive weight. Review agency reasoning and record support under the otherwise applicable administrative standards.

**Exceptions and limits:** Deference cannot enlarge an agency’s authority beyond enacted text or resolve a question ordinary interpretation settles. Informal guidance lacking the force of law does not receive controlling statutory deference. Deference to an agency’s interpretation of its own genuinely ambiguous regulation remains governed by *Auer v. Robbins* and does not apply to an unambiguous regulation or an interpretation inconsistent with its text.

**Binding guidance as final agency action**

**Current rule:** A guidance document is final and reviewable when it represents the agency’s settled position and in practical operation determines regulated parties’ or permitting authorities’ obligations, notwithstanding a nonbinding disclaimer. Guidance may explain existing duties and offer technical assistance, but it may not create duties beyond the governing statute and regulation or demand compliance as law without the force-of-law procedure Congress required.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** Determine whether agency decisionmaking is complete and examine practical legal consequences, including instructions to field officials or permitting authorities, compliance expectations, and effects on permits or ongoing operations. If the document operates as binding law, test whether authorized procedure supports those duties; a boilerplate disclaimer does not neutralize practical consequences.

**Exceptions and limits:** Genuinely advisory interpretations remain permissible and receive only the persuasive weight their reasoning earns. Finality does not convert informal guidance into force-of-law authority, and the rule does not require notice and comment for every interpretive explanation that creates no new binding duty.

**Source:** `STANDARDS_AND_TESTS(20260905-075915).md`, lines 7797–7812, “Binding guidance as final agency action”.

**Current rule:** Section 207(o) creates no employee veto over a public employer’s direction to use accrued compensatory time when no statute, valid regulation, or binding agreement prohibits the policy.
