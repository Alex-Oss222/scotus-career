# Church on the Rock v. City of Albuquerque

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** Church on the Rock v. City of Albuquerque
- **Citation or Docket:** 84 F.3d 1273 (10th Cir. 1996); No. 94-2282; simulated Supreme Court merits review
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued merits case on review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit.
- **Simulated Event Date:** May 27, 1997, simulation-assigned.
- **Question Presented:** Whether Albuquerque may exclude private devotional religious instruction and worship from city senior centers while permitting private presentations on religion, philosophy, literature, history, and other subjects.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the City's prohibition on sectarian instruction and religious worship is unconstitutional as applied to the requested film showing and distribution of New Testaments.
- **Legal Field:** First Amendment, religious viewpoint discrimination, public facilities, and Establishment Clause.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Tenth Circuit reversed summary judgment for the City, enjoined the City from barring the film and literature, awarded appellate fees, and remanded for a fee determination.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The City asks the Court to reverse and restore judgment in its favor. Church on the Rock and Pastor Don Kimbro seek affirmance of the injunction and fee award.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Albuquerque operates six voluntary, nonresidential senior centers that host privately organized lectures, classes, movies, crafts, and other activities. The centers permitted programs treating the Bible, religion, and philosophy from nondevotional perspectives. Pastor Kimbro, a qualifying senior-center member, sought to show the film Jesus and give attending adults giant-print New Testaments. The City denied permission under a policy barring sectarian instruction and religious worship, while attendance at all programs remained voluntary and participants could leave freely.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The as-applied challenge and prospective injunction remain live. The City invokes a federal funding condition in the Older Americans Act, but the case does not require adjudication of the United States as a party or invalidation of the statute if the condition can be administered consistently with the First Amendment. The record is adequate to decide the viewpoint and attribution questions.
- **Relevant Dates:** Request March 24, 1994; denial May 18, 1994; Tenth Circuit argued January 25, 1996, and decided May 23, 1996; simulated Supreme Court argument February 18, 1997; simulated decision May 27, 1997.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Church on the Rock v. City of Albuquerque, 84 F.3d 1273 (10th Cir. 1996), for the panel, dates, senior-center policies, funding condition, injunction, fee award, and remand; supplied Holdings and Standards and Tests entries for Good News/Good Sports Club, Pinette, Chabad-Lubavitch, and Rosenberger.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits comparator. The historical anchor is the Tenth Circuit's reversal and injunction.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Tacha wrote for a panel joined by Judge Logan and Judge Reavley of the Fifth Circuit, sitting by designation.
- **Historical Holding:** The City's exclusion was viewpoint discrimination because the centers allowed secular and nondevotional treatment of subjects including religion but excluded an affirmative religious perspective. The asserted Establishment Clause and captive-audience concerns did not justify the exclusion.
- **Historical Authority:** Lower-court judgment only.
- **Historical Sources:** Church on the Rock, 84 F.3d 1273.

#### Issue 1: Religious viewpoint exclusion from the senior-center access program

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue holds that once government opens a private-access channel for a defined subject it may not identify the religious perspective on that subject as the forbidden viewpoint. Pinette and Chabad-Lubavitch require contextual attribution analysis for private religious expression in a government forum. Rosenberger distinguishes equal access from direct public financing of devotional activity.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Religious viewpoint discrimination in an after-school access program applies by analogy to the City's private senior-center program. Private religious expression in an open government forum remains private when neutral access, private sponsorship, voluntary attendance, and clear attribution prevent governmental adoption of the message.
- **Other Governing Law:** The Free Speech and Establishment Clauses; the Older Americans Act funding condition then codified at 42 U.S.C. § 3027(a)(14)(A)(iv); neutral forum-management principles.
- **Other Authority Used:** Lamb's Chapel supports the prohibition on excluding a religious viewpoint from a permitted subject. Lee v. Weisman is used only to distinguish a captive or coercive school setting from this voluntary adult program.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Tenth Circuit's judgment.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The injunction against barring the film and voluntary distribution of New Testaments remains in force. The case returns only for determination of reasonable fees and administration of neutral, generally applicable scheduling, safety, capacity, and identification rules.

#### Issue 1: Religious viewpoint exclusion from the senior-center access program

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the as-applied First Amendment question. The City created the relevant access program and denied these speakers because their treatment of a permitted religious subject was devotional. The record presents no disputed operational fact requiring remand.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies existing simulated law. A city that permits private treatment of religion, morality, history, literature, and philosophy in a voluntary adult program may not exclude an otherwise eligible presentation because it affirms a faith, invites prayer, or distributes religious text. Neutral access does not become governmental worship where attendance is voluntary, the speaker and materials are privately identified, no public funds produce the message, and the City retains no editorial control over its content. The federal funding condition may be read to prevent governmental or center-sponsored sectarian instruction, not to compel unconstitutional viewpoint exclusion of private speakers.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Albuquerque violated the First Amendment by excluding the privately sponsored film and New Testaments because they presented an affirmative religious viewpoint on subjects the senior-center program otherwise allowed, and the voluntary, privately attributed program created no Establishment Clause justification for that exclusion.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The centers invited private speakers and offered broad programming, including nondevotional discussion of the Bible and religion. The line the City drew therefore turned on viewpoint, not subject matter. Adults chose whether to attend and could leave at any time, so the record supplies neither coercion nor a captive audience. A neutral notice identifying Church on the Rock as sponsor would address any residual attribution concern without suppressing the speech. The injunction fits the proven denial and leaves ordinary facility administration intact.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require the City to sponsor worship, finance devotional materials, permit disruption, or open the centers outside their defined program. It does not bar a genuinely subject-neutral rule excluding all private instruction or a neutral rule against conduct that interferes with other users.

# Clinton v. Jones

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** Clinton v. Jones
- **Citation or Docket:** 520 U.S. 681 (1997); No. 95-1853
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued merits case on review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit.
- **Simulated Event Date:** May 27, 1997.
- **Question Presented:** Whether a sitting President is constitutionally entitled to temporary immunity from federal civil litigation based on unofficial conduct alleged to have occurred before taking office, and whether the action must be stayed until the Presidency ends.
- **Question Granted:** Whether separation of powers requires dismissal or postponement of Jones's damages action during President Clinton's term.
- **Legal Field:** Executive power, presidential immunity, separation of powers, and federal civil procedure.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eighth Circuit affirmed denial of dismissal on immunity grounds and reversed the district court's categorical postponement of trial until the President left office.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** President Clinton asks the Court to reverse and require dismissal without prejudice or a stay through the end of his Presidency. Jones seeks affirmance and ordinary progression subject to reasonable case management.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Paula Jones alleges that in 1991, while Clinton was Governor of Arkansas, he engaged in private sexual misconduct and that state employees later retaliated against her. She filed federal and state claims in 1994 after Clinton became President. The district court rejected temporary immunity, allowed discovery, but postponed trial until the end of the presidential term. The Eighth Circuit rejected both constitutional immunity and the categorical trial stay.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The Court does not decide whether a comparable action in state court could be stayed under federal supremacy principles or whether a court may compel a President's attendance at a particular time or place. The federal claims establish jurisdiction, and the immunity and stay orders are properly before the Court.
- **Relevant Dates:** Complaint filed May 6, 1994; Eighth Circuit decision January 9, 1996; argued January 13, 1997; decided May 27, 1997.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** The action also names former Arkansas State Police Officer Danny Ferguson, whose case was not independently stayed on constitutional grounds.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 520 U.S. 681; Jones v. Clinton, 72 F.3d 1354 (8th Cir. 1996); district-court orders reproduced in the official opinion.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by the Chief Justice and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. Justice Breyer concurred in the judgment.
- **Historical Holding:** The Constitution does not grant a sitting President temporary immunity from federal civil litigation concerning unofficial conduct that occurred before office. Courts retain ordinary authority to manage the case sensitively, but a categorical stay until the Presidency ends is not constitutionally required.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** Clinton v. Jones, 520 U.S. 681 (1997).

### A. Law entering the case

- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None. Every listed OT1996 matter remains pending at the time of this draft and therefore supplies no entering law.

#### Issue 1: Temporary immunity for unofficial pre-office conduct

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Nixon v. Fitzgerald recognizes absolute damages immunity for acts within the outer perimeter of presidential official responsibility. United States v. Nixon and Youngstown confirm judicial authority to decide legal claims affecting the President. No controlling simulated holding extends official-act immunity to private or pre-office conduct.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No reusable simulated standard grants temporary presidential immunity for unofficial conduct. The ordinary source-separation rule distinguishes official authority from private conduct and requires the claimed immunity to arise from a governing legal source.
- **Other Governing Law:** Article II, Article III, the separation of powers, 42 U.S.C. §§ 1983 and 1985, and ordinary federal jurisdiction.
- **Other Authority Used:** Fitzgerald supplies the official-function boundary. Nixon v. United States is used only for the principle that generalized burdens of litigation do not eliminate Article III authority.
- **Open Point:** The simulated Court has not previously decided whether the sitting President receives temporary immunity for purely private conduct.

#### Issue 2: Categorical postponement and case-specific accommodation

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Federal courts possess inherent and rule-based authority to control their dockets, but a stay must remain proportionate to demonstrated necessity and cannot become immunity by delay. Landis and ordinary civil procedure govern discretionary stays.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Remedial discipline requires a source and necessity for any postponement. Institutional competence permits accommodation of concrete executive burdens without creating a categorical exemption from adjudication.
- **Other Governing Law:** Federal Rules of Civil Procedure; Article II responsibilities; the district court's case-management authority.
- **Other Authority Used:** The historical comparator is persuasive on the distinction between constitutional immunity and ordinary docket management.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Eighth Circuit's judgment.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The case may proceed in the district court under ordinary procedural law. The district court shall accommodate concrete Article II responsibilities through scheduling, protective orders, sequencing, remote or written procedures where lawful, and reconsideration if a particular demand would materially impair presidential duties. No categorical immunity or term-long stay is authorized.

#### Issue 1: Temporary immunity for unofficial pre-office conduct

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the federal separation-of-powers question. Jones's allegations are assumed for purposes of immunity, and no issue concerning the merits of her claims is decided.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone declines to extend Fitzgerald. Presidential immunity protects the lawful independence of official decisionmaking, not the officeholder's private conduct. A status-based temporary immunity covering all unofficial litigation would create a constitutional privilege unsupported by text, history, or function. The possibility of distraction is real, but it is addressed through concrete case management rather than a categorical withdrawal of judicial authority.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A sitting President has no constitutional temporary immunity from a federal civil action based on unofficial conduct alleged to have occurred before taking office.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The alleged acts were neither presidential nor undertaken in any governmental capacity. Adjudicating them does not permit a court to review or control an exclusive Article II power. Fitzgerald's concern that personal liability could distort official choices is absent. The President remains subject to generally applicable law, and the judiciary retains authority over a properly filed private controversy. The judgment resolves only immunity and expresses no view on liability.

#### Issue 2: Categorical postponement and case-specific accommodation

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the validity of the term-long trial stay because the Eighth Circuit reversed it and the issue is within the granted question. He does not decide any particular subpoena, deposition date, or attendance order.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone holds that the district court may postpone or sequence particular proceedings upon a concrete showing that they materially interfere with presidential duties, but it may not presume that every phase of the action must wait until the term ends. The party seeking delay must identify the burden and the court must use a proportionate accommodation.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A categorical stay of trial through the end of the presidential term is not justified on this record, while case-specific accommodations remain available to prevent material interference with Article II functions.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** An automatic stay would impose substantial risks of lost evidence and delay on Jones without linking those costs to any identified presidential duty. Ordinary tools allow the court to avoid genuine conflict as it arises. That solution respects both the executive office and the claimant's right to timely adjudication without manufacturing either immunity or judicial control of presidential functions.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide litigation against a President in state court, criminal process, or a specific demand that would require the President's presence at a time or place incompatible with an essential official duty.

# De Buono v. NYSA-ILA Medical and Clinical Services Fund

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** De Buono v. NYSA-ILA Medical and Clinical Services Fund
- **Citation or Docket:** 520 U.S. 806 (1997); No. 95-1594
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued merits case on review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 2, 1997.
- **Question Presented:** Whether ERISA preempts New York's generally applicable gross-receipts assessment as applied to treatment centers owned and operated by an ERISA welfare plan.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Health Facility Assessment relates to the Fund's ERISA plan within 29 U.S.C. § 1144(a).
- **Legal Field:** ERISA preemption, state taxation, employee benefits, and federalism.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit reinstated its judgment enjoining the assessment after remand for reconsideration in light of Travelers, reasoning that the tax directly depleted plan assets.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** New York officials ask the Court to reverse and permit assessment of the Fund's New York treatment centers. The trustees seek affirmance and a refund.
- **Material Facts and Record:** The Fund is a self-insured multiemployer welfare plan that owns and operates treatment centers for longshore workers, retirees, and dependents. New York imposed a 0.6 percent assessment on gross receipts for patient services at licensed diagnostic and treatment centers, with proceeds entering general revenues. Most taxed facilities were not ERISA plans. The Fund stopped paying and sued for prospective relief and repayment.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The State did not press the Tax Injunction Act in the court of appeals or this Court, and the Second Circuit had treated New York remedies as not plain in the materially similar Travelers litigation. Stone reaches the preserved ERISA question and makes no independent ruling on an unpressed jurisdictional objection.
- **Relevant Dates:** Second Circuit reinstated judgment January 11, 1996; argued February 24, 1997; decided June 2, 1997.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 520 U.S. 806; NYSA-ILA Medical and Clinical Services Fund v. Axelrod, 74 F.3d 28 (2d Cir. 1996); N.Y. Public Health Law § 2807-d; supplied Travelers holding and standard.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, 7-2.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by the Chief Justice and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice Scalia dissented, joined by Justice Thomas.
- **Historical Holding:** A generally applicable state tax on health-care facilities is not preempted merely because an ERISA plan owns a taxed facility and therefore bears the charge directly. The supposed direct versus indirect economic distinction did not separate the case from Travelers.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** De Buono v. NYSA-ILA Medical and Clinical Services Fund, 520 U.S. 806 (1997).

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's approved position

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 28, 1996, the last completed simulated merits event reflected in the supplied Holdings and Standards and Tests registers. Only law effective before the simulated event date is used.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None. Every listed OT1996 matter remains pending at the time of this draft and therefore supplies no entering law.
- **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified. Any earlier listed matter that is later rendered before this event must be checked at revalidation.

#### Issue 1: ERISA preemption of the generally applicable health-facility tax

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Simulated New York State Conference of Blue Cross & Blue Shield Plans v. Travelers holds that a state law does not relate to an ERISA plan merely because market-price effects influence plan choices. Preemption remains when state law specifically refers to ERISA plans, binds benefit structure or administration, creates an alternative enforcement mechanism, or effectively dictates plan choices through acute indirect effects. Mackey preserves generally applicable state law that incidentally burdens plans.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Indirect economic influence on ERISA plans supplies the current reusable rule. The direct payment of an ordinary state charge by a plan does not itself convert the charge into plan regulation.
- **Other Governing Law:** ERISA § 514(a), 29 U.S.C. § 1144(a); New York Public Health Law § 2807-d; the presumption against superseding traditional state taxation absent clear congressional purpose.
- **Other Authority Used:** Travelers and Fort Halifax support the distinction between plan administration and ordinary economic regulation. The historical De Buono opinion is persuasive on the practical equivalence between direct ownership and purchasing taxed services.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The injunction against the Health Facility Assessment shall be dissolved, and the lower courts shall resolve any remaining refund, state-law, or remedial questions consistent with the holding that ERISA does not preempt this application.

#### Issue 1: ERISA preemption of the generally applicable health-facility tax

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved preemption question. The assessment's coverage, rate, and treatment of the Fund's centers are undisputed.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Travelers. A generally applicable tax does not relate to an ERISA plan merely because the plan chose to own the taxed provider and therefore remits the assessment itself. The relevant inquiry is whether state law governs plan benefits, administration, enforcement, or choices with a coercive force equivalent to regulation. This tax does none of those things.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** ERISA does not preempt New York's Health Facility Assessment as applied to the Fund's treatment centers because the assessment is a generally applicable facility tax that neither refers to ERISA plans nor dictates their benefit structure, administration, or enforcement.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Had the Fund purchased care from an independent treatment center, the facility could pass the same tax cost through its prices. Ownership changes the route of the economic burden, not its legal character. Treating every direct cost paid by a plan as preempted would displace taxes, property law, utility charges, and other ordinary state rules Congress did not make ERISA-specific. The assessment enters general revenues and does not condition benefits or plan procedure.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A tax or charge that singles out ERISA plans, operates as an alternative enforcement device, or is structured to compel a particular benefit or provider arrangement may present a different case.

# Bracy v. Gramley

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** Bracy v. Gramley
- **Citation or Docket:** 520 U.S. 899 (1997); No. 96-6133
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued federal habeas merits case on review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 9, 1997.
- **Question Presented:** Whether Bracy showed good cause under Habeas Corpus Rule 6 for discovery supporting his claim that a corrupt trial judge displayed compensatory bias against defendants who did not bribe him.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the particular allegations and public corruption record require discovery into actual judicial bias in Bracy's capital trial.
- **Legal Field:** Federal habeas corpus, due process, judicial bias, and discovery.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Seventh Circuit affirmed denial of habeas relief and discovery, reasoning that Bracy had not first produced evidence showing bias in his own case.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Bracy asks the Court to reverse and permit focused discovery. The warden seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Bracy was convicted and sentenced to death by Illinois Judge Thomas Maloney, later federally convicted for accepting bribes to fix criminal cases. Bracy alleges that Maloney harshly handled unbribed cases to conceal corruption. He points to counsel's prior association with Maloney, unusually rapid preparation and penalty proceedings, and the placement of Bracy's trial between murder trials identified in the federal proffer as fixed. He has not yet proved that Maloney was biased in this case.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The writ was granted only on discovery, not final habeas relief. Rule 6 requires good cause and leaves scope to the district court. Stone assumes that actual compensatory bias in Bracy's own case would violate due process but does not presume bias from Maloney's corruption in other cases.
- **Relevant Dates:** Seventh Circuit decision April 2, 1996; certiorari granted January 13, 1997; argued April 14, 1997; decided June 9, 1997.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 520 U.S. 899; Bracy v. Gramley, 81 F.3d 684 (7th Cir. 1996); federal corruption proffer and habeas pleadings described in the official opinion; Habeas Corpus Rule 6.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Chief Justice Rehnquist delivered the Opinion of the Court for a unanimous Court.
- **Historical Holding:** Bracy's specific allegations, combined with the established corruption record, showed good cause for discovery into actual bias. The district court retained discretion over the scope of discovery.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** Bracy v. Gramley, 520 U.S. 899 (1997).

#### Issue 1: Good cause for discovery on the judicial-bias claim

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Due process requires a neutral judge and forbids adjudication by a judge with actual bias or a personal interest in the outcome. Habeas Corpus Rule 6 permits discovery for good cause upon specific allegations showing reason to believe that developed facts may entitle the petitioner to relief. Armstrong confirms that a discovery threshold must be matched to the elements of the underlying constitutional claim.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No separate simulated habeas-discovery test appears in Standards and Tests. The existing due-process requirement of a neutral decisionmaker and the source-specific Rule 6 good-cause standard govern.
- **Other Governing Law:** The Due Process Clause; 28 U.S.C. § 2254; Rule 6 of the Rules Governing Section 2254 Cases.
- **Other Authority Used:** Tumey, Ward, and Aetna Life identify disqualifying judicial interest and bias. Harris v. Nelson supports controlled habeas discovery where specific allegations cannot be fairly tested without it.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court shall authorize focused discovery reasonably directed to whether Maloney was actually biased in Bracy's case, including the asserted relationship with counsel, trial scheduling, and relevant corruption evidence. The court shall then adjudicate the habeas claim under the developed record. No finding of bias or entitlement to a new trial is made.

#### Issue 1: Good cause for discovery on the judicial-bias claim

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the discovery ruling. Bracy's allegations were preserved, and the public corruption record is judicially cognizable. Final bias, prejudice, procedural default, and remedy remain for later adjudication.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Rule 6. Good cause does not require the petitioner to prove the concealed fact before receiving the means reasonably necessary to investigate it. The petitioner must identify a legally sufficient bias theory and concrete facts connecting that theory to his own trial. Bracy has done so, while the district court retains authority to confine discovery to the relevant connection.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Bracy established good cause for focused discovery because Maloney's proved corruption, the alleged use of unbribed cases to camouflage fixed cases, counsel's relationship and rapid preparation, and the trial's placement among identified fixed cases provide a concrete basis to investigate actual bias in Bracy's own prosecution.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Maloney's corruption defeats the ordinary assumption of regular judicial conduct, but it does not alone prove Bracy's claim. The additional case-specific allegations supply the missing connection. Requiring admissible proof of the very agreement or motive alleged to be hidden would make Rule 6 circular. Discovery may still fail to substantiate the claim, and the district court may sequence depositions and documents to avoid an unfocused inquiry.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not authorize discovery whenever a judge committed unrelated misconduct or permit merits relief based on appearance alone. Bracy must ultimately prove constitutionally material bias in his own case.

# Gilbert v. Homar

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** Gilbert v. Homar
- **Citation or Docket:** 520 U.S. 924 (1997); No. 96-651
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued merits case on review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 9, 1997.
- **Question Presented:** Whether due process required a hearing before a state university suspended a tenured police officer without pay immediately after his felony arrest, and whether the later hearing was sufficiently prompt.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the pre-suspension process and timing of the post-suspension hearing satisfied the Fourteenth Amendment.
- **Legal Field:** Procedural due process, public employment, emergency suspension, and timing.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit reversed summary judgment for university officials, holding that a tenured employee ordinarily must receive notice and an opportunity to respond before suspension without pay.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The officials ask the Court to reverse the categorical pre-suspension rule. Homar seeks affirmance or a ruling that the delayed hearing was inadequate.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Homar was a police officer at East Stroudsburg University. State police arrested him during a drug raid and charged him with a felony. University officials immediately suspended him without pay pending investigation. The criminal charges were dismissed six days later, but the unpaid suspension continued. Homar first received an opportunity to respond to university officials on September 18, twenty-three days after suspension, and was later demoted after another meeting.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Homar had a protected property interest in his tenured employment and pay. The record supports review of the need for pre-suspension process but does not contain a complete lower-court assessment of the justification for the period after the criminal charges were dismissed. Damages, individual immunity, and the validity of the ultimate demotion remain separate.
- **Relevant Dates:** Suspension August 26, 1992; charges dismissed September 1, 1992; first opportunity to respond September 18, 1992; Third Circuit decision July 2, 1996; argued March 24, 1997; decided June 9, 1997.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 520 U.S. 924; Homar v. Gilbert, 89 F.3d 1009 (3d Cir. 1996); university letters and police report described in the official opinion.

- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Scalia delivered the Opinion of the Court for a unanimous Court.
- **Historical Holding:** The arrest and formal felony charges supplied an objective basis for immediate temporary suspension of a police officer without a prior hearing. The lower court had to determine whether the post-suspension opportunity to respond was sufficiently prompt.

The court explained its use of county lines, precinct integrity, district cores, and communities of interest. The appellants' alternatives depended on racial targets and additional splits. The Constitution does not require a remedial court to reproduce unsupported racial allocation.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not license a court to disregard a valid legislative policy unrelated to the violation or to treat racial proportionality as forbidden. A proved, fitted voting-rights remedy remains available under simulated Miller's remedial lane.

#### Issue 2: Voting Rights Act Section 2

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the § 2 challenge to the remedial plan. He reviews the district court's legal standard independently and its record findings for clear error.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Gingles and De Grandy. The ability to assemble a second numerical majority is not enough. The claimants must prove a sufficiently large and geographically compact population, political cohesion, majority bloc voting that ordinarily defeats preferred candidates, and less opportunity under the totality. The district court found the proposed second district required substantial departures from ordinary geography and found significant crossover voting. Those findings are not clearly erroneous.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The remedial plan does not violate § 2 because appellants failed to establish the compactness and bloc-voting predicates for a second genuine electoral-opportunity district, and § 2 does not require racial maximization.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The proposed alternatives linked separated populations and split counties in service of a racial target. Evidence also showed successful Black candidates and meaningful crossover voting, which the court assessed with the full electoral record rather than treating incumbency as conclusive. The statute protects equal political opportunity, not a fixed number of majority-minority districts. The district court's different treatment of the existing Fifth District and a proposed second district reflects district-specific facts, not inconsistency.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not treat statewide electoral success or rough proportionality as a safe harbor. A compact, cohesive community facing legally sufficient bloc voting could require a different remedy.

#### Issue 3: Voting Rights Act Section 5

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory objections to the court plan but does not conduct preclearance of a future legislative enactment. He assumes for this appeal that § 5 requirements constrain the remedial result to the extent applicable.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone rejects a benchmark built from an unprecleared or unconstitutional racial feature. The last lawful operative plan, the 1982 plan, supplies the effect comparison. The appellants have not shown district-specific retrogression from that benchmark or discriminatory purpose in the court's remedial design. Section 5 cannot freeze an unconstitutional district or require maximization.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The remedial plan does not violate § 5 because the lawful 1982 benchmark contained one majority-Black district, the court plan does not reduce protected voters' opportunity from that baseline, and the record does not establish a racially discriminatory purpose in the court's design.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The 1991 proposal never became operative, and the 1992 plan cannot serve as a benchmark to the extent its racial design was unconstitutional. Comparing percentages of majority-Black districts without examining electoral opportunity would turn retrogression into a mechanical quota. The court restored neutral criteria and maintained a genuine opportunity district. Nothing in the record supports an inference that the court acted to deny or abridge voting rights because of race.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A court plan that reduced actual electoral opportunity from the lawful benchmark or embodied discriminatory purpose would not receive protection from its remedial label.

#### Issue 4: Population equality in the remedial plan

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the population challenge. The numerical deviation and the alternative plans are in the record.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the exacting court-plan standard. The small deviation is justified by Georgia's unusually numerous counties, the consistent effort to avoid county and precinct splits, preservation of district cores and communities, and the need to remedy multiple invalid districts. No alternative plan with materially lower deviation avoided independent constitutional defects.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The slight population deviation in the district court's plan does not violate Article I, § 2 because it is supported by consistently applied, legitimate state and remedial criteria and is lower than the deviation in any otherwise lawful alternative presented.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Population equality remains the baseline, not a factor casually traded away. The court identified each relevant line-drawing constraint and used them consistently. Ordering another statewide redraw late in the decade would produce disruption without a demonstrated representational benefit, while any isolated residual imbalance could be corrected by minor precinct shifts rather than adopting a race-driven alternative.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding creates no numerical safe harbor and does not excuse a deviation adopted for convenience, favoritism, or an unexplained policy.

- **Legal Field:** Civil RICO, statutes of limitations, accrual, and fraudulent concealment.

- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The Klehrs ask the Court to reverse and permit their civil RICO action. Harvestore seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** The Klehrs bought a Harvestore silo in 1974 based on representations that oxygen-limiting storage would prevent mold and improve milk production. They allege the feed instead fermented, cows performed poorly, and Harvestore continued related misrepresentations and sales for years. They filed in August 1993 and claimed that product design, advertising, and dealer statements concealed the defect until 1991. The lower courts found that they should have discovered the injury, source, and alleged pattern before August 1989.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The case permits rejection of the last-predicate-act rule and decision of the diligence requirement. The broader circuit conflict among injury, injury-discovery, and injury-and-pattern discovery rules does not affect this judgment because the Klehrs lose under the most plaintiff-favorable rule applied below. Their fact-specific challenge to the Eighth Circuit's diligence application is outside the granted scope.

#### Issue 1: Separate accrual and no revival

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the last-predicate-act rule but reserves the universal accrual trigger because the thin record does not make that choice necessary.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A later predicate act cannot reopen damages for an older time-barred injury. A genuinely new injury caused by a later act may accrue separately, but the plaintiff may recover only the timely injury and related damages. RICO's pattern definition does not create a rolling limitations period for every prior injury.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A later predicate act does not revive damages for an older time-barred injury, although a genuinely new injury may accrue separately. The universal civil RICO discovery trigger remains unresolved.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Revival would defeat the borrowed four-year period whenever a pattern continued. Separate accrual preserves recovery for a new injury without converting later conduct into a reset for completed harm. The supplied record does not adequately identify each claimed injury or its causal act, so final application belongs below.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not bar recovery for a timely new injury or for damages that could not previously be proved and does not require knowledge of the legal label RICO before accrual.

#### Issue 2: Fraudulent-concealment diligence

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the legal diligence requirement but not the unresolved fact-specific application.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Fraudulent-concealment tolling requires proof that concealment prevented timely discovery despite reasonable diligence. Misleading conduct alone does not suspend the period when reasonable inquiry would have exposed the claim.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Fraudulent-concealment tolling requires reasonable diligence, and the lower court must apply that requirement injury by injury on remand.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Tolling prevents a wrongdoer from profiting from successful concealment, but it does not erase the claimant's responsibility to investigate facts that would alert a reasonable person to injury and source. The present materials do not permit the Court to decide each inquiry itself.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require extraordinary investigation where concealment defeats reasonable inquiry.
