# United States v. O'Hagan

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** United States v. O'Hagan
- **Citation or Docket:** 521 U.S. 642; No. 96-842; useful lower-court citation: 92 F.3d 612 (8th Cir. 1996).
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Certiorari granted; argued April 16, 1997; plenary merits decision pending.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 25, 1997.
- **Question Presented:** Issue 1: Whether a person commits fraud in connection with a securities transaction by secretly using material nonpublic information in breach of a duty of trust or confidence owed to the source of the information. Issue 2: Whether SEC Rule 14e-3(a) is a valid exercise of the Commission's authority under Exchange Act § 14(e).
- **Question Granted:** Whether O'Hagan's deceptive misappropriation and securities trading fall within § 10(b) and Rule 10b-5, and whether Rule 14e-3(a) validly regulates trading on material nonpublic tender-offer information within the circumstances the rule specifies.
- **Legal Field:** Securities regulation, primary fraud, misappropriation of confidential information, tender-offer regulation, and agency authority.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eighth Circuit reversed O'Hagan's securities-fraud and mail-fraud convictions, rejected the misappropriation theory, invalidated Rule 14e-3(a), and also reversed the money-laundering convictions.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The United States asks the Court to reverse the securities-fraud and mail-fraud portions of the Eighth Circuit's judgment and to sustain Rule 14e-3(a). O'Hagan asks the Court to affirm. The United States does not seek review of the separate money-laundering disposition.
- **Material Facts and Record:** O'Hagan was a partner in a law firm representing Grand Metropolitan PLC in a contemplated tender offer for Pillsbury. Although he did not work on the transaction, he obtained confidential information through the firm, purchased Pillsbury stock and call options before public announcement, and realized more than $4 million after the market reacted. The prosecution's theory was that he feigned loyalty to the information source while secretly converting entrusted transaction information into securities-trading gain.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The securities and tender-offer questions were preserved and decided below. Primary liability for O'Hagan's own alleged deception must remain separate from the simulated rule permitting limited private aiding-and-abetting liability. The Court need not recognize a general parity-of-information duty, decide every relationship capable of creating confidentiality, or disturb the unreviewed money-laundering judgment. Any unresolved instruction, sufficiency, or harmless-error matter remains for ordinary proceedings on remand.
- **Relevant Dates:** Indictment returned in 1992; Eighth Circuit judgment entered August 2, 1996; certiorari granted January 13, 1997; argued April 16, 1997; simulated decision June 25, 1997.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 521 U.S. 642, for the record, questions, judgment, dates, and historical comparator; 92 F.3d 612 for the judgment under review; Exchange Act §§ 10(b) and 14(e), Rule 10b-5, and Rule 14e-3(a) for the operative law.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded. The misappropriation holding was 6-3; the Rule 14e-3 and mail-fraud holdings were 7-2.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Ginsburg wrote the Court's principal opinion, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Justice Scalia joined Parts I, III, and IV and wrote separately. Justice Thomas concurred in the judgment in part and dissented in part, joined by Chief Justice Rehnquist.
- **Historical Holding:** The historical Court accepted the misappropriation theory when a trader deceptively breaches a duty to the information source and uses the information in securities trading, and it sustained Rule 14e-3(a) as a prophylactic tender-offer rule.
- **Historical Authority:** Historical Opinion of the Court at the issue-specific scopes reflected in the joins; the historical comparator does not control the simulated disposition.
- **Historical Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 521 U.S. 642.

### A. Law entering the case

- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None. Every listed OT1996 matter remains pending at the time of this draft and therefore supplies no entering law.

#### Issue 1: Primary § 10(b) liability for deceptive misappropriation

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A., 511 U.S. 164 (1994), recognizes limited private liability for culpable assistance but preserves the distinction between secondary assistance and a defendant's own primary deception. No simulated holding squarely resolves deceptive trading against an information source.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Private liability for culpable assistance to a § 10(b) violation. The registered doctrine confirms that a defendant's state of mind and conduct must be established independently; it does not displace primary liability under enacted § 10(b) and Rule 10b-5.
- **Other Governing Law:** Exchange Act § 10(b), 15 U.S.C. § 78j(b); SEC Rule 10b-5; the statutory requirement that the fraud occur in connection with the purchase or sale of a security; surviving Chiarella and Carpenter principles concerning duty, deception, and confidential information.
- **Other Authority Used:** Chiarella is used for the proposition that silence is fraudulent only when a duty makes nondisclosure deceptive. Carpenter is used for the proposition that secret conversion of entrusted information can constitute fraud on its source. Neither supplies a general equal-information rule.
- **Open Point:** The exact connection required between deception of the information source and the securities transaction.

#### Issue 2: SEC authority for Rule 14e-3(a)

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T requires an agency to remain within authority the enacted text fairly grants and forbids using interpretation to eliminate or replace a central statutory command. No simulated holding specifically construes § 14(e).
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No registered securities-specific agency-authority doctrine resolves Rule 14e-3(a). Ordinary independent statutory interpretation controls, with agency reasoning receiving only the weight warranted by the enacted delegation and the rule's fit.
- **Other Governing Law:** Exchange Act § 14(e), including its express authorization for rules reasonably designed to prevent fraudulent, deceptive, or manipulative acts or practices in connection with tender offers; SEC Rule 14e-3(a).
- **Other Authority Used:** The tender-offer provisions are used for their enacted prophylactic delegation and their defined transactional domain. General administrative deference cannot create authority, but no deference is needed when the text itself authorizes preventive rules.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand as to the securities-fraud and related mail-fraud counts; leave the money-laundering disposition outside the mandate.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The Eighth Circuit shall reinstate the securities and mail-fraud judgments to the extent supported by valid instructions and sufficient evidence, or address any preserved instruction, sufficiency, or harmless-error question not already resolved. The money-laundering reversal remains outside the mandate.

#### Issue 1: Primary § 10(b) liability for deceptive misappropriation

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits. The theory concerns O'Hagan's own alleged deception, not imputed primary liability or the private aiding-and-abetting cause of action. The source relationship, transaction connection, scienter, and use of the information are supported sufficiently to resolve the legal rule, while ordinary record challenges remain for remand.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether § 10(b) reaches a trader who secretly monetizes information entrusted by a source participating in an actual or contemplated securities transaction.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies § 10(b)'s antifraud language while supplying a source-bound limitation. Misappropriation is primary fraud when the source entrusted material nonpublic information in connection with an actual or contemplated securities transaction, the trader owed the source a defined duty of fidelity or confidentiality, the trader deceptively withheld the planned use, and the trader used the information to trade securities materially affected by the source's transaction. Disclosure to the source before trading removes the deception required by this theory. A breach involving information unrelated to the source's securities activity is not transformed into § 10(b) fraud merely because the defendant later trades.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A trader commits primary fraud under § 10(b) when, with scienter, the trader secretly uses material nonpublic information entrusted by a source participating in an actual or contemplated securities transaction, in breach of a defined duty to that source, to trade securities materially affected by the source's transaction; the trading must consummate or monetize the deceptive breach.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** O'Hagan's firm represented the contemplated bidder, and the confidential information concerned the very tender offer that changed the value of the target securities he acquired. His trading was not an unrelated use followed by a coincidental securities purchase. It was the means by which the concealed disloyalty produced gain. Requiring a defined source duty, source participation in the securities transaction, scienter, and transaction-linked trading gives independent work to both fraud and in connection with and avoids a general federal law of confidential information.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding creates no parity-of-information duty, reaches no innocent receipt, and reserves relationships involving family, friendship, government service, ordinary employment, or commerce when the source is not participating in the securities transaction. It does not narrow the distinct simulated rule for secondary assistance.

#### Issue 2: SEC authority for Rule 14e-3(a)

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the validity of Rule 14e-3(a). The question is one of statutory authority, not whether every application of the rule is constitutional or whether the SEC possesses a general power to equalize market information.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the express preventive-rule authority in § 14(e). Rule 14e-3(a) addresses the distinctive secrecy, timing, and proof risks of tender offers by regulating trading on material nonpublic tender-offer information obtained from specified transaction participants when the trader knows or has reason to know the source. The bounded rule is reasonably designed to prevent tender-offer fraud and remains within the statutory domain.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 14(e) authorizes Rule 14e-3(a)'s bounded prohibition on trading while possessing material nonpublic tender-offer information obtained from the offeror, issuer, or persons acting for them under the knowledge conditions stated in the rule.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress did more than forbid completed common-law fraud. It expressly authorized preventive rules in the tender-offer field. Tender offers operate under compressed timing and unusual secrecy, and trading before announcement can make source deception difficult to prove after the fact. The rule addresses those risks within a defined transaction, source, information, and knowledge structure. It does not claim authority over all trading or all confidential information.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not authorize SEC regulation outside the tender-offer and antifraud domain, eliminate the rule's source and knowledge limits, or permit an agency to create power from statutory silence.

# Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union
- **Citation or Docket:** 521 U.S. 844; No. 96-511; useful lower-court citation: 929 F. Supp. 824 (E.D. Pa. 1996).
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Direct appeal from a three-judge district court; argued March 19, 1997; plenary merits decision pending.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 26, 1997.
- **Question Presented:** Whether the Communications Decency Act's criminal restrictions on transmitting or displaying indecent or patently offensive material in a manner available to minors violate the First Amendment.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the challenged indecency and patently-offensive provisions are facially invalid under the First Amendment because they are content based, vague, substantially overbroad, and not narrowly fitted to protecting minors.
- **Legal Field:** First Amendment, Internet speech, criminal vagueness, facial overbreadth, adult access, and protection of minors.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** A three-judge district court entered a preliminary injunction against enforcement of the challenged CDA provisions. Its treatment preserved prosecution of obscenity and other unprotected material outside the injunction's scope.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The United States asks the Court to reverse or narrow the injunction. The ACLU and the other plaintiffs ask the Court to affirm the injunction against the challenged content restrictions.
- **Material Facts and Record:** The CDA criminalized knowing transmission to a minor of an obscene or indecent communication and knowing use of an interactive computer service to send or display material described in sexual or excretory terms that was patently offensive under contemporary community standards and available to persons under eighteen. The record showed that the Internet carried a vast range of lawful adult speech and that age verification was unavailable, costly, or practically exclusionary for many speakers, especially noncommercial and small speakers.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The plaintiffs' facial challenge is ripe because the provisions impose severe criminal penalties and predictably alter speech before prosecution. The Court may sever the word indecent from the obscenity provision and may preserve obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, true threats, fraud, and other independently unprotected conduct. The case does not require a categorical forum classification for the Internet or a ruling on every future age-assurance technology.
- **Relevant Dates:** CDA enacted February 8, 1996; district-court judgment entered June 11, 1996; probable jurisdiction noted December 6, 1996; argued March 19, 1997; simulated decision June 26, 1997.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 521 U.S. 844, for the statute, record, posture, dates, and historical comparator; 929 F. Supp. 824 for the injunction and factual findings; 47 U.S.C. § 223 as then in force.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 7-2.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens wrote for the historical majority, joined by Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice O'Connor concurred in the judgment in part and dissented in part, joined by Chief Justice Rehnquist.
- **Historical Holding:** The historical Court held the challenged provisions facially invalid because their content-based, vague, and overbroad terms suppressed substantial protected adult speech and were not narrowly tailored to protecting minors.
- **Historical Authority:** Historical Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 521 U.S. 844.

## Section II: Entering law and Stone's approved position

### A. Law entering the case

- **Current Through:** June 28, 1996, the last completed simulated merits event reflected in the supplied Holdings and Standards and Tests registers. Only law effective before the simulated event date is used.
- **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None. Every listed OT1996 matter remains pending at the time of this draft and therefore supplies no entering law.

The completed OT1995 Denver Area decision already governs practical adult access and less restrictive household controls.

#### Issue 1: Content-based suppression of protected Internet speech

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium, Inc. v. FCC holds that government may protect children through channeling measures only while preserving realistic adult access and invalidates default suppression when confidential subscriber-controlled measures can address household exposure less restrictively. Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC preserves independent review of speech burdens and fit without importing broadcast scarcity into cable.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Mandatory cable segregation and written-request access; Public-access operator vetoes over protected programming; Cable must-carry classification and intermediate scrutiny. These modules supply practical adult-access and fit principles, not a cable-specific classification for the Internet.
- **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment; ordinary content-discrimination, vagueness, and substantial-overbreadth doctrine; the CDA provisions as enacted; surviving Sable principles concerning protected adult speech and minors.
- **Other Authority Used:** Sable is used for the proposition that protecting minors does not permit reducing adult discourse to material suitable for children. Ginsberg is distinguished because it concerned a more precisely defined category and a direct commercial sale to a minor rather than suppression across a general-purpose communications network.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment of the three-judge district court.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Enforcement of the invalid content restrictions remains enjoined. The mandate preserves severable laws and statutory applications governing obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, true threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation, direct harmful contact with minors, and other independently unprotected conduct.

#### Issue 1: Content-based suppression of protected Internet speech

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the facial First Amendment challenge. The severe criminal sanctions, uncertain content categories, and unavoidable effect on third-party speakers create the present chilling injury. Because the First Amendment grounds are sufficient, he does not announce a separate freestanding Fifth Amendment holding.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone classifies the provisions as content based and applies the most searching review. Protecting children is compelling, but the Government must prove narrow tailoring in the actual medium. The terms indecent and patently offensive leave speakers uncertain about a national audience and community standard, reach serious discussion of health, art, sexuality, politics, and personal experience, and force many speakers either to abandon anonymity, incur prohibitive verification costs, reduce their audience, or remain silent. Scienter does not cure uncertainty about what content is criminal. User-controlled filtering, precise regulation of unprotected material, and targeted rules for direct communication with identified minors are materially less speech-restrictive on this record.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A content-based criminal restriction on expression through a general-purpose communications network is facially invalid when vague and substantially overbroad categories suppress a large amount of protected adult speech and the Government fails to prove that the restriction is narrowly tailored in light of practical, materially less speech-restrictive means of limiting minors' access.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute makes the speaker responsible for every minor who might gain access, even when adults are the intended audience and the speaker lacks a feasible age gate. The record shows that many noncommercial speakers cannot use credit-card verification and that available controls either destroy anonymity, exclude lawful adults, or remove the speaker from the medium. The structural defect parallels Denver Area's invalidated default blocking: protected adult speech is suppressed first and restored only through burdensome mechanisms. The Internet's open and non-scarce architecture supplies no broadcast rationale for lesser protection.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not protect obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, true threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation, direct harmful contact with minors, or a future precisely drawn rule using genuinely effective and nonexclusionary age-assurance methods. It does not require one constitutional classification for every Internet service.

# Vacco v. Quill

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** Vacco v. Quill
- **Citation or Docket:** 521 U.S. 793; No. 95-1858; useful lower-court citation: 80 F.3d 716 (2d Cir. 1996) (en banc).
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Certiorari granted; argued January 8, 1997; plenary merits decision pending.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 26, 1997.
- **Question Presented:** Whether New York's prohibition of assisted suicide violates equal protection because the State permits a competent person to refuse life-sustaining medical treatment.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the State may distinguish between refusing unwanted treatment and intentionally assisting suicide without violating the Equal Protection Clause.
- **Legal Field:** Equal protection, end-of-life medical decisions, treatment refusal, assisted suicide, and rational-basis review.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The en banc Second Circuit held New York's distinction unconstitutional as applied to competent, terminally ill persons in the circumstances presented.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** New York asks the Court to reverse. The physicians and patients ask the Court to affirm the equal-protection judgment.
- **Material Facts and Record:** New York criminalized intentionally aiding another person's suicide while recognizing a competent patient's authority to refuse or discontinue unwanted medical treatment and permitting good-faith palliative care even when death might be foreseen. The challenged distinction turned on consent to bodily intervention, the actor's purpose, and the legal cause assigned to death.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The law applies without an express status classification. The Court must analyze the conduct distinction rather than equate acts solely because both may precede death. Treatment refusal, advance directives, pain relief, and good-faith palliation remain independently protected or regulated under their own sources. No evidence establishes that New York enacted or administered the prohibition from animus toward terminally ill or disabled persons.
- **Relevant Dates:** Second Circuit en banc judgment entered April 2, 1996; certiorari granted October 1, 1996; argued January 8, 1997; simulated decision June 26, 1997.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** Washington v. Glucksberg presents the related substantive-due-process question and is decided on the same simulated date, but the cases are not formally consolidated.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 521 U.S. 793, for the statutes, medical distinctions, posture, dates, and historical comparator; 80 F.3d 716 for the judgment under review; New York Penal Law provisions and treatment-refusal law then in force.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, unanimously as to the judgment.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Chief Justice Rehnquist delivered the principal opinion, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas. Justice O'Connor concurred, joined in part by Justices Ginsburg and Breyer. Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer filed opinions concurring in the judgment.
- **Historical Holding:** The historical Court held that New York rationally distinguished treatment refusal from assisted suicide because the acts differ in legal causation and intent and because the prohibition served legitimate protective interests.
- **Historical Authority:** Historical Opinion of the Court on the equal-protection disposition, with separate concurrences reserving narrower end-of-life questions.
- **Historical Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 521 U.S. 793.

#### Issue 1: Equal-protection distinction between treatment refusal and assisted suicide

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Romer v. Evans invalidates a status-based withdrawal of ordinary legal protection when extraordinary breadth bears no rational relationship to an independent legitimate purpose and hostility or civic subordination supplies the remaining explanation. No simulated holding recognizes assisted suicide as a protected status or treats refusal and assistance as identical conduct.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No registered assisted-death equal-protection doctrine controls. Ordinary rational-basis review applies, with Romer's status-based anti-animus floor available only if the record establishes the kind of class-wide disability Romer addressed.
- **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause; New York homicide and medical-practice law; surviving Cruzan principles concerning competent refusal of unwanted treatment.
- **Other Authority Used:** Cruzan is used to distinguish bodily noninterference and informed refusal from an affirmative entitlement to another person's assistance in causing death. Ordinary causation and intent principles are used to identify the law's actual distinction.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Remand with instructions to reject the equal-protection challenge to New York's conduct distinction. The judgment does not disturb independent rights to refuse unwanted treatment, receive lawful pain relief, or obtain good-faith palliative care.

#### Issue 1: Equal-protection distinction between treatment refusal and assisted suicide

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the equal-protection merits. The law does not classify by disability, terminal illness, or another listed status. It distinguishes conduct and legal roles. No threshold defect prevents adjudication, and the record supports no finding of animus.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies ordinary rational-basis review. A competent patient's refusal directs that an unwanted bodily intervention stop or not begin, after which the underlying condition takes its course. Assisted suicide requires another actor intentionally to provide or administer the means of death. The State may recognize those differences in consent, causation, purpose, and professional role. Its interests in preserving life, preventing suicide, protecting vulnerable patients, and maintaining a medical system oriented toward care are legitimate and rationally served by the line.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State does not violate equal protection by permitting a competent person to refuse unwanted medical treatment while prohibiting intentional assistance in suicide, because the distinction rests on legally meaningful differences in bodily consent, causation, intent, and professional role and rationally serves legitimate protective interests.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Equal protection does not require the State to collapse different acts merely because death may follow both. The treatment-refusal rule protects the patient's authority over bodily intervention and does not direct a physician to make death the intended means or object. The assisted-suicide prohibition applies across persons and does not withdraw an ordinary civic protection from a disfavored status group. The asserted protective purposes therefore explain the line without resort to condemnation or civic subordination.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not authorize forced treatment, override a valid advance directive, permit discriminatory denial of pain care, punish good-faith palliation because death is foreseen, or foreclose an as-applied claim supported by a materially different record of discriminatory administration.

# Washington v. Glucksberg

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** Washington v. Glucksberg
- **Citation or Docket:** 521 U.S. 702; No. 96-110; useful lower-court citation: 79 F.3d 790 (9th Cir. 1996) (en banc).
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Certiorari granted; argued January 8, 1997; plenary merits decision pending.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 26, 1997.
- **Question Presented:** Whether the Fourteenth Amendment protects a fundamental right for a competent, terminally ill adult to obtain a physician's assistance in committing suicide.
- **Question Granted:** Whether Washington's prohibition of knowingly causing or aiding suicide violates the Due Process Clause as applied to the asserted physician-assisted-death claim.
- **Legal Field:** Substantive due process, personal liberty, bodily integrity, medical decisionmaking, and assisted suicide.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The en banc Ninth Circuit invalidated Washington's assisted-suicide prohibition as applied to competent, terminally ill adults seeking physician assistance.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Washington asks the Court to reverse. The physicians, patients, and organization ask the Court to affirm recognition of the asserted constitutional right.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Washington criminalized knowingly causing or aiding another person's suicide while permitting competent patients to refuse unwanted treatment, recognizing advance directives, and allowing good-faith palliative care. The plaintiffs sought authority for physicians to prescribe lethal medication for competent, terminally ill adults who wished to end their lives.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The asserted right must be defined as obtaining intentional assistance in suicide, not as autonomy at the highest level of generality. The case does not require approval of forced treatment, interference with treatment refusal, denial of pain relief, or punishment of good-faith palliation. Facial and broad as-applied relief would be improper if the asserted fundamental right does not exist.
- **Relevant Dates:** Ninth Circuit en banc judgment entered March 6, 1996; certiorari granted October 1, 1996; argued January 8, 1997; simulated decision June 26, 1997.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** Vacco v. Quill presents the related equal-protection question and is decided on the same simulated date, but the cases are not formally consolidated.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 521 U.S. 702, for the state law, record, posture, dates, and historical comparator; 79 F.3d 790 for the judgment under review; Washington's homicide, treatment-refusal, and advance-directive law then in force.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 9-0 as to the judgment.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Chief Justice Rehnquist delivered the principal opinion, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas. Justice O'Connor concurred, joined in part by Justices Ginsburg and Breyer. Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer filed opinions concurring in the judgment.
- **Historical Holding:** The historical Court held that the Due Process Clause does not protect a fundamental right to physician-assisted suicide and that Washington's prohibition rationally served legitimate state interests.
- **Historical Authority:** Historical Opinion of the Court on the absence of the claimed fundamental right and the judgment, with separate concurrences reserving materially narrower applications.

### A. Law entering the case

#### Issue 1: Asserted fundamental right to physician-assisted suicide

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No simulated holding recognizes a fundamental right to obtain intentional assistance in death. Surviving Cruzan authority protects competent refusal of unwanted medical treatment. Romer v. Evans addresses status-based political disability and does not establish the asserted substantive liberty.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No registered assisted-suicide doctrine controls. The ordinary substantive-due-process sequence requires precise identification of the asserted liberty, examination of text, structure, precedent, and relevant tradition, and rational-basis review if no fundamental right is established.
- **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause; Washington's homicide and medical-practice law; surviving principles of bodily integrity, treatment refusal, and state authority over medicine.
- **Other Authority Used:** Cruzan is used for bodily noninterference and refusal, not for an affirmative right to another actor's assistance in causing death. - **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The Ninth Circuit shall direct entry of judgment for Washington on the asserted substantive-due-process right to physician-assisted suicide. #### Issue 1: Asserted fundamental right to physician-assisted suicide

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits. The plaintiffs present a concrete challenge to Washington's prohibition, but the right must be stated at the legally relevant level. The record does not require a ruling on forced treatment, palliation, or a patient who lacks practical access to lawful pain care.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Due Process Clause protects an affirmative right to obtain intentional physician assistance in suicide.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone declines to recognize the asserted right as fundamental. Treatment refusal protects bodily integrity by allowing a person to decline intervention. Physician-assisted suicide requires another actor to provide the means of death and asks the Constitution to disable state regulation of that affirmative assistance. Text, structure, precedent, and the legal tradition do not establish that entitlement. Rational-basis review therefore applies. Washington's interests in preserving life, preventing suicide, protecting vulnerable persons from pressure and error, maintaining medical ethics, and permitting cautious democratic experimentation are legitimate and rationally served by the prohibition.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Due Process Clause does not protect a fundamental right to obtain intentional physician assistance in suicide, and Washington's prohibition survives rational-basis review.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The broad language of personal autonomy cannot decide the case without a manageable boundary. Many important choices remain subject to ordinary law, and the Constitution's protection against unwanted bodily intervention does not itself create a right to compel or immunize another person's affirmative lethal assistance. The State may account for diagnostic error, coercion, depression, unequal access to care, and difficulty policing a line once assistance is normalized. Those concerns need not be proved with certainty under rational review, and the prohibition leaves States free to authorize a different policy through legislation.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not permit forced treatment, override competent refusal or valid directives, criminalize good-faith pain relief merely because death is foreseen, or use the assisted-suicide ban to deny lawful palliative care. It leaves States free to authorize and regulate assisted death.

#### Issue 1: Commerce power over an interstate support-payment obligation

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the Commerce Clause question. The different-state element is present on the indictment's allegations, and the issue is legal. - **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone affirms on a narrower rationale than generalized aggregate economic harm.
