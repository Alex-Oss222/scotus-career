# Metropolitan Stevedore Co. v. Rambo

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** Metropolitan Stevedore Co. v. Rambo
- **Citation or Docket:** 521 U.S. 121 (1997); No. 96-272
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued merits case on review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 19, 1997.
- **Question Presented:** Whether the Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act permits a nominal compensation award when a work-related injury causes no present loss of wage-earning capacity but creates a significant potential for future loss.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Act authorizes nominal compensation in those circumstances and whether the court of appeals could direct entry of such an award without further factfinding by the administrative law judge.
- **Legal Field:** Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act, disability compensation, modification, burden of proof, and administrative factfinding.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed the order discontinuing Rambo's compensation and remanded with instructions to enter a nominal award because his permanent injury could reduce his future earning capacity.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Metropolitan Stevedore asks the Court to reverse and sustain termination of compensation. Rambo and the Director of the Office of Workers' Compensation Programs defend the legal availability of nominal compensation, while Rambo seeks preservation of an award.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Rambo injured his back and leg while performing longshore work in 1980 and received an award based on a stipulated 22.5 percent permanent partial disability. After training as a crane operator, he earned about three times his preinjury wages. An administrative law judge discontinued benefits because the new earnings reflected no current wage-earning loss. The record establishes a permanent physical impairment, but the administrative law judge did not apply a distinct inquiry into whether that impairment created a significant potential for future economic loss.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The case presents statutory interpretation and allocation of factfinding authority. The administrative law judge, not an appellate court, is the statutory factfinder. The Court may state the governing standard but may not itself find the probability or magnitude of future wage loss. The proponent of each requested order retains the burden assigned by 5 U.S.C. § 556(d).
- **Relevant Dates:** Initial injury 1980; modification sought 1989; Ninth Circuit decision April 11, 1996; certiorari granted November 4, 1996; argued March 17, 1997; decided June 19, 1997.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** This is the second Supreme Court review arising from the same compensation proceeding; no separately listed companion docket is before the Court.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 521 U.S. 121; Rambo v. Director, Office of Workers' Compensation Programs, 81 F.3d 840 (9th Cir. 1996); 33 U.S.C. §§ 902(10), 908(c)(21), 908(h), 913(a), 921, and 922; 5 U.S.C. § 556(d).

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded, 6-3.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Souter delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by the Chief Justice and Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice O'Connor dissented, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas.
- **Historical Holding:** The Act permits nominal compensation when there is a significant potential that a permanent work injury will reduce future wage-earning capacity, but the Ninth Circuit erred by directing an award rather than remanding for findings by the administrative law judge.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** Metropolitan Stevedore Co. v. Rambo, 521 U.S. 121 (1997).

#### Issue 1: Nominal compensation for a significant potential future loss of wage-earning capacity

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied Holdings entry separately resolves nominal compensation under the Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act. The prior order in this litigation treats statutory disability as economic loss of earning capacity and permits modification when that capacity changes. That case-specific mandate does not answer how § 8(h) and § 22 operate when present earnings mask a significant future risk.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No exact reusable Standards and Tests entry is identified. The statutory burden rule and the requirement that the authorized administrative factfinder make material findings govern.
- **Other Governing Law:** LHWCA §§ 2(10), 8(c)(21), 8(h), 13(a), 21, and 22; Administrative Procedure Act § 7(c), 5 U.S.C. § 556(d). Section 8(h) directs attention to the effect of disability as it may naturally extend into the future, while § 22 limits later modification after an award denial or termination.
- **Other Authority Used:** Director, Office of Workers' Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries supplies the burden-of-persuasion rule. Potomac Electric Power Co. v. Director distinguishes scheduled from unscheduled disability. The historical Rambo comparator is used only to test the statutory operation and not as entering simulated law.
- **Open Point:** Current simulated law does not state whether the statute can preserve a future modification route through nominal compensation when current wages show no present economic loss.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The Ninth Circuit shall remand to the Benefits Review Board with instructions to return the matter to the administrative law judge. The administrative law judge shall determine whether Rambo has proved a significant, nonconjectural potential that his work injury will reduce his future wage-earning capacity. A nominal award may be entered only if that finding is made. No award is directed by this Court.

#### Issue 1: Nominal compensation for a significant potential future loss of wage-earning capacity

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved statutory question. He does not decide whether this record proves the required future risk because the administrative law judge did not make the necessary finding under the governing standard.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone adopts a narrow statutory reconciliation. A nominal award is available when a permanent work injury causes no current wage loss but the claimant proves a significant and nonconjectural potential that the injury, interacting with reasonably foreseeable labor-market conditions, will reduce future wage-earning capacity. The employer seeking termination must first establish the change in conditions supporting termination of actual benefits. Once current earnings fairly represent current capacity, the claimant seeking nominal compensation carries persuasion on the significant future potential. The possibility may not be presumed as an administrative convenience.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The LHWCA permits nominal compensation when a work-related injury creates a significant, proved potential for future diminished wage-earning capacity despite no current loss, and the administrative law judge must decide whether that condition exists.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 8(c)(21) compensates lost earning capacity rather than physical impairment alone. Section 8(h), however, expressly requires attention to the future effect of disability. Reading the Act to require an outright denial whenever current wages equal or exceed preinjury wages would make § 22's one-year limitation prevent later relief even when the anticipated economic consequence materializes. A nominal award gives effect to both provisions without paying present compensation for a merely physical loss. Metropolitan's proof of much higher current market wages supports termination of actual benefits. It does not answer the separate future-risk question, and the Ninth Circuit could not make that factual finding for the administrative law judge.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding creates no nominal award for every permanent impairment, does not assume future unemployment, and does not permit an appellate court to substitute its view of probability for supported administrative findings.

# O'Dell v. Netherland

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** O'Dell v. Netherland
- **Citation or Docket:** 521 U.S. 151 (1997); No. 96-6867
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued federal habeas merits case on review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 19, 1997.
- **Question Presented:** Whether Simmons v. South Carolina announced a new constitutional rule under Teague v. Lane and, if so, whether that rule applies retroactively on federal habeas review to O'Dell's final capital judgment.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the Simmons parole-ineligibility rule is available to a state prisoner whose conviction and sentence became final before Simmons.
- **Legal Field:** Federal habeas corpus, retroactivity, capital sentencing, due process, and parole information.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The en banc Fourth Circuit reversed a grant of habeas relief and held that Simmons announced a new rule unavailable to O'Dell under Teague.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** O'Dell asks the Court to reverse and reinstate penalty-phase habeas relief. The warden seeks affirmance of the denial of relief.
- **Material Facts and Record:** At O'Dell's capital sentencing, Virginia argued future dangerousness and the court refused his requested instruction that a life sentence would make him legally ineligible for parole. The jury found future dangerousness and selected death. O'Dell's judgment became final in 1988. Simmons was decided in 1994 and later established in the simulation that, when future dangerousness is argued and the only noncapital sentence forecloses parole, the jury must receive an accurate parole-ineligibility instruction.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** Teague retroactivity is a threshold limit on federal habeas relief. Stone therefore resolves whether Simmons was dictated by then-existing precedent and whether either recognized exception applies. He does not issue an advisory ruling on harmlessness or the underlying penalty-phase merits if Teague bars relief.
- **Relevant Dates:** Virginia Supreme Court judgment January 15, 1988; certiorari denied October 3, 1988; Fourth Circuit en banc decision September 5, 1996; certiorari granted January 6, 1997; argued March 18, 1997; decided June 19, 1997.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 521 U.S. 151; O'Dell v. Netherland, 95 F.3d 1214 (4th Cir. 1996) (en banc); O'Dell v. Commonwealth, 234 Va. 672, 364 S.E.2d 491 (1988); supplied Simmons and Allridge Holdings and Standards and Tests entries.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 5-4.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Thomas delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by the Chief Justice and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, and Kennedy. Justice Stevens dissented, joined by Justices Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.
- **Historical Holding:** Simmons announced a new rule under Teague and was not retroactive because it was neither substantive nor a watershed rule of criminal procedure.

#### Issue 1: Teague treatment of Simmons

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Simmons v. South Carolina requires an accurate parole-ineligibility instruction when the prosecution places future dangerousness at issue and the only noncapital sentence legally forecloses parole.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No supplied simulated holding alters Teague's new-rule sequence or its existing exceptions.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit's judgment and remand to reinstate the District Court's conditional sentencing relief.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** O'Dell's conviction remains undisturbed. Virginia may conduct a new penalty proceeding that complies with Simmons or impose the lawful noncapital sentence under the District Court's conditional writ.

#### Issue 1: Teague treatment of Simmons

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone applies Teague before reaching ordinary habeas merits. He classifies Simmons as a new procedural rule and then applies the existing watershed exception to the narrow conjunction presented.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Simmons applies retroactively when the prosecution seeks death through future dangerousness, state law makes parole legally impossible, and the jury is left uninformed after the trial court refuses an accurate instruction.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Simmons was not dictated when O'Dell's sentence became final and is therefore new and procedural. In the narrow setting where future dangerousness is the State's reason for death, life without parole is the only lawful alternative, and the court refuses the accurate instruction needed to correct the false release premise, the safeguard is bedrock to a reliable choice between the only lawful penalties. That conjunction brings the rule within Teague's existing watershed exception without creating a general capital-retroactivity rule.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Simmons announced a new procedural rule. When the State seeks death through future dangerousness, the only lawful alternative is life without parole, and the trial court refuses an accurate instruction stating that legal fact, the resulting risk that death rests on a false release premise brings the rule within Teague's watershed exception.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** O'Dell's jury was asked to select death because of future danger while being denied the legally fixed fact that life meant no parole. The error distorted the only choice the jury had to make and could not be corrected through argument or an alternative instruction. The exception is confined to that precise alignment of prosecution theory, legal parole ineligibility, and refused accurate instruction.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not confer watershed status on sentencing rules generally, alter Simmons on direct review, disturb O'Dell's conviction, or govern cases in which future dangerousness was not raised, parole remains legally possible, only practical release predictions are offered, or the jury received an accurate instruction.

# Agostini v. Felton

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** Agostini v. Felton
- **Citation or Docket:** 521 U.S. 203 (1997); Nos. 96-552 and 96-553
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued consolidated merits cases on review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 23, 1997.
- **Question Presented:** Whether parties bound by the Aguilar injunction are entitled to relief under Rule 60(b)(5) and whether New York City's use of public employees to provide secular Title I remedial instruction on religious-school premises violates the Establishment Clause.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the continuing injunction should be dissolved in light of intervening doctrine and whether Aguilar v. Felton should be retained.
- **Legal Field:** Establishment Clause, public educational services, precedent, and Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 60(b)(5).
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit affirmed denial of relief from the permanent injunction implementing Aguilar v. Felton.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The City, Board of Education, and parents ask the Court to reverse, dissolve the injunction, and permit on-premises Title I services under public control. The respondents seek affirmance and continued enforcement of Aguilar.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Title I provides remedial education to educationally deprived children selected by neutral statutory criteria. Before Aguilar, New York City assigned public-school teachers to provide secular instruction to eligible children inside religious schools. The teachers remained public employees, used public materials, and were instructed not to teach religion. The injunction forced the City to use mobile units and off-site arrangements at substantial cost and with inferior practical access. No record finding shows actual religious indoctrination, transfer of public funds to a school, or religious control of the public teachers.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The parties bound by a prospective injunction may seek relief when continued enforcement has become inequitable. Lower courts remain bound by this Court's precedent until it is changed, but this Court may reconsider Aguilar in reviewing the Rule 60(b)(5) judgment. Stone resolves the constitutional merits because the continuing injunction rests entirely on Aguilar's rule.

- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 521 U.S. 203; Felton v. ### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 5-4.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O'Connor delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by the Chief Justice and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas. Justice Souter dissented, joined by Justices Stevens and Ginsburg and in part by Justice Breyer. Justice Ginsburg filed a separate dissent joined by Justices Stevens, Souter, and Breyer.

Later simulated decisions, including Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad-Lubavitch, and Good News/Good Sports, distinguish private religious identity from governmental attribution and separate equal civic services from direct financing of devotional activity. #### Issue 2: Public delivery of secular Title I services on religious-school premises

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Aguilar treated the public teachers' presence and the monitoring thought necessary to keep instruction secular as unconstitutional advancement and entanglement. Pinette, Chabad-Lubavitch, and Good News/Good Sports make attribution turn on program structure and actual control.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Equal civic access and direct financing of devotional activity, together with contextual attribution of private religious expression, require examination of who controls the personnel, curriculum, funds, materials, and message. The religious identity of a site does not itself make a publicly controlled secular service religious.
- **Other Governing Law:** The Establishment Clause; Title I's neutral eligibility and remedial-education requirements.
- **Other Authority Used:** School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball is reconsidered only to the extent it used the same presumptions. Witters and Zobrest support neutral benefits and direct services that reach religious settings without financing religious exercise.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand in both dockets.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The lower courts shall dissolve the permanent injunction. New York City may provide Title I remedial services on religious-school premises under neutral eligibility, public employment and supervision, secular curriculum, separate public materials, and safeguards against diversion. A materially different program involving devotional content, transfer of public resources to religious control, or religious selection of beneficiaries remains subject to challenge.

#### Issue 1: Relief from the continuing Aguilar injunction

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the Rule 60(b)(5) question and the necessary antecedent question whether Aguilar remains valid. The lower courts correctly treated Aguilar as binding, but this Court must decide whether continuing the injunction remains lawful.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Rule 60(b)(5) to a prospective injunction whose sole legal foundation no longer fits the controlling constitutional structure. He openly proposes overruling Aguilar to the stated extent rather than treating the lower courts as free to disregard it. Later doctrine has removed the premises-based and employee-based presumptions that made Aguilar dispositive, and the record shows substantial continuing cost from the decree.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A court must dissolve a prospective injunction when the controlling constitutional rule supporting it is overruled, and the Aguilar injunction cannot continue after the Court rejects Aguilar's categorical treatment of publicly controlled secular instruction on religious-school premises.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The decree governs future public administration and therefore depends on the law now controlling that administration. The lower courts did not err by following Aguilar, but this Court cannot preserve an injunction after concluding that its legal premise is defective. The change is not a request to relitigate a completed judgment; it concerns the continuing force of prospective relief. Concrete costs and reduced access reinforce, but do not create, the legal basis for dissolution.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not permit a district court to anticipate overruling by this Court or reopen completed liability merely because later decisions create tension.

#### Issue 2: Public delivery of secular Title I services on religious-school premises

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional merits because the injunction cannot be dissolved without deciding whether the proposed on-premises service is lawful.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone openly changes the prior rule. A neutral public service does not advance religion merely because public employees deliver it on religious premises. The service is constitutional when eligibility is independent of religion, government retains control of employees, curriculum, materials, and assessment, no public funds or discretionary authority are transferred to the religious institution, instruction is secular and separable from worship, and practical safeguards prevent diversion without requiring religious surveillance. Aguilar and Ball are overruled to the extent they presume indoctrination, attribution, or excessive entanglement from the location and public employment alone.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** New York City's publicly staffed Title I program does not violate the Establishment Clause because it provides secular remedial instruction to neutrally eligible children under public control, transfers no funds or educational authority to the religious schools, and does not attribute religious teaching to the government.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The children receive the same remedial service because of educational need, not because of the school's faith. Public teachers answer to public supervisors and use a secular curriculum and public materials. The school neither chooses the beneficiaries nor controls the instruction. Ordinary professional supervision is less entangling than the elaborate off-site system required by the injunction and does not require inquiry into doctrine. Aguilar's contrary presumptions are inconsistent with the later attribution and neutral-service cases, impose substantial reliance costs on public administration, and systematically deny equal remedial access without evidence of religious diversion.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The decision does not authorize public financing of worship, devotional instruction, theological formation, clergy training, or a program in which religious officials control public employees or content. It leaves Establishment Clause review available for diversion, coercion, or attribution shown in operation.

# Cohen v. Brown University

- **Question Granted:** Whether the First Circuit correctly sustained Title IX liability and vacated the team-specific remedial order for a further institutional compliance plan.
- **Legal Field:** Title IX, intercollegiate athletics, effective accommodation, sex-conscious remediation, and equitable relief.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The First Circuit affirmed the liability judgment and the continuing preliminary injunction, vacated the order requiring Brown to elevate four particular women's teams to university-funded varsity status, and remanded to permit a new compliance plan.

- **Material Facts and Record:** Brown receives federal financial assistance and operates a two-tier varsity program with university-funded and donor-funded teams. Women were materially underrepresented in participation opportunities compared with enrollment, Brown had not maintained a continuing practice of expansion for women, and the record identified existing women's teams with sufficient interest and ability for varsity competition. #### Issue 1: Title IX's alternative compliance routes and prong three

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory framework and its application to liability.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Title IX and § 106.41 permit three alternative compliance routes. Prong three asks whether the actual, competitively viable unmet interests and abilities of the underrepresented sex are fully and effectively accommodated. Aggregate comparisons between men's and women's interest levels cannot excuse proved unmet demand or freeze a disparity that unequal opportunity may itself have produced. Section 1681(b) is respected because numerical imbalance alone creates neither liability nor a mandatory quota.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Title IX permits three alternative routes to effective accommodation, and prong three requires full accommodation of demonstrated, competitively viable unmet interests and abilities of the underrepresented sex rather than a comparison with the other sex's aggregate interest level. Numerical imbalance alone establishes neither liability nor a quota.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Brown materially underrepresented women, showed no continuing practice of responsive expansion, and left proved viable interests unaccommodated. The conclusion rests on program operation and demonstrated unmet demand, not enrollment arithmetic alone.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not preserve any particular team, make proportionality the exclusive route, or require accommodation of unsupported or infeasible interest.

#### Issue 2: Equal protection of prong-three enforcement

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional challenge because federal enforcement expressly treats the underrepresented sex differently.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Correcting demonstrated program-wide inequality in participation opportunities is an important governmental objective. Accommodating proved, competitively viable unmet interests is substantially related to that objective and does not rely on stereotype or numerical imbalance alone. The rule therefore satisfies intermediate scrutiny.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** As applied to prong-three enforcement, the sex-conscious rule survives intermediate scrutiny because it is substantially related to correcting demonstrated program-wide inequality without relying on stereotype or numerical imbalance alone.

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Richards requires genuinely aligned interests and legally accountable representation before absent persons are bound. ### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Third Circuit's judgment.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The certification, settlement approval, and classwide injunction remain vacated. #### Issue 1: Application of Rule 23 to a settlement-only class

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches Rule 23 and does not reach Article III or a broader constitutional notice rule because certification fails on the rule-based ground.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies every Rule 23 requirement to a settlement-only class. Settlement may remove trial-management problems and may be considered in evaluating how the proposed class will operate. It cannot erase differences in claims, governing law, or remedial interests, and a court may not treat the desirability of peace as a substitute for predominance or adequate representation.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A class proposed only for settlement must satisfy Rule 23(a) and (b) in full; settlement may affect manageability but does not relax predominance, typicality, or adequate representation.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Rule 23 protects absent persons precisely when a judgment will bind them without individual litigation. A settlement can make trial logistics irrelevant, but it increases rather than reduces the need to examine who surrendered which claims for whose benefit. Allowing settlement value to supply missing prerequisites would let the agreement define the court's power to bind persons who never consented.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not disfavor settlement classes categorically and does not require litigation of a case that can satisfy Rule 23 through a properly structured agreement.

#### Issue 2: Predominance and adequate representation in the proposed asbestos class

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone applies Rule 23 to the undisputed class structure and settlement terms. No additional factfinding could remove the conflicts built into the proposed undivided class.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone concludes that predominance and adequacy are absent. Exposure, causation, disease, defenses, damages, and state law vary materially. More important, currently injured claimants seek generous immediate payment, while exposure-only claimants need inflation protection, preservation of future diagnostic categories, and protection against annual caps. One set of representatives and counsel cannot trade those interests without separately represented subclasses or another structure that makes the negotiation accountable.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The proposed asbestos settlement class fails Rule 23 because individual and state-law issues overwhelm the common questions and because one undivided group of representatives cannot adequately protect the conflicting interests of currently injured and future claimants.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The common fact of asbestos exposure does not answer product identification, latency, causation, injury, limitations, defenses, or damages across many States. The agreement itself reveals the conflict: present claimants value immediate fixed payments, while future claimants bear inflation, diagnostic uncertainty, annual caps, and surrendered claims that may not yet exist. An opt-out right and generalized notice cannot replace representatives whose incentives match each group. Subclasses with independent counsel are a possible route, but the Court does not design the settlement.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The decision does not hold that mass-tort settlement classes are impossible, determine the validity of particular state-law claims, or prescribe one mandatory subclass structure.

#### Issue 1: Congressional authority to apply RFRA to States and local governments

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the § 5 power question before applying RFRA to the permit dispute. The City is a local government, and the RFRA claim depends on a valid federal command.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 5 permits preventive and remedial legislation, including some coverage of otherwise lawful conduct, when the measure is fitted to demonstrated or seriously threatened Fourteenth Amendment violations. RFRA instead replaces Smith across every state and local law, subject, official, and level of government without a record explaining that universal reach. The conflict with the superior constitutional allocation requires candid treatment of simulated Church of Scientology and Swanner: they are overruled insofar as they treat RFRA as universally enforceable against state and local government under § 5, while their final judgments remain undisturbed.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 5 authorizes preventive and remedial legislation congruent and proportional to Fourteenth Amendment violations Congress may address, but RFRA's universal application to state and local government exceeds that authority by replacing the governing constitutional rule. Insofar as simulated Church of Scientology and Swanner treat RFRA as universally enforceable against state and local government under § 5, they are overruled; their final judgments remain intact.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress may reach beyond completed constitutional violations when the excess is connected to a demonstrated pattern or serious risk and proportionate to prevention. RFRA makes the heightened statutory rule universal without a jurisdictional, subject-matter, temporal, or problem-specific limit. That is substantive replacement, not fitted enforcement. The two prior simulated cases applied RFRA without deciding this antecedent power question, so their judgments retain finality while their prospective universal premise cannot survive the squarely presented conflict.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not reconsider Smith, invalidate RFRA as applied to the Federal Government, decide Congress's power under another enumerated source or a lawful spending condition, or restrict States from protecting religious exercise more broadly under their own law.
