# Lopez v. Monterey County

## Section I: Case, record, and procedural posture

- **Case Name:** Lopez v. Monterey County
- **Citation or Docket:** 519 U.S. 9 (1996); No. 95-1201; review of the United States District Court for the Northern District of California
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued merits case, submitted for decision after plenary briefing and argument.
- **Simulated Event Date:** November 6, 1996.
- **Question Presented:** **Issue 1: State-mandated voting changes.** Whether a covered political subdivision must obtain § 5 preclearance before administering a postbaseline voting change mandated by a noncovered State, even when the subdivision has no independent discretion. **Issue 2: Covered change, designation, and laches.** Whether the district-to-countywide consolidation is a change affecting voting, whether Monterey County may defeat its § 4(b) designation in this enforcement action, and whether laches can bar prospective nonuse. **Issue 3: Enforcement-court authority and temporary remedy.** Whether the three-judge district court may authorize elections under an unprecleared governmental plan and when a wholly court-developed temporary plan is exempt from preclearance.
- **Question Granted:** **Issue 1: State-mandated voting changes.** Whether Monterey County was required to obtain § 5 preclearance before implementing state-mandated changes to its municipal-court election system, including the preserved antecedent coverage defenses. **Issue 3: Enforcement-court authority and temporary remedy.** What remedial authority the district court possessed while preclearance remained absent.
- **Legal Field:** **Issue 1:** Elections and Voting, Voting Rights Act § 5 administration of state-mandated changes. **Issue 2:** Elections and Voting, § 5 coverage, § 4 designation, and equitable defenses. **Issue 3:** Federal courts and Voting Rights Act remedies, enforcement-court authority and interim plans.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** A three-judge district court ordered Monterey County to conduct March 1996 judicial elections under the countywide, at-large system while joining California to permit further litigation over whether later state law had displaced the County ordinances as the source of that system.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The Lopez plaintiffs ask the Court to reverse, prohibit further use of the unprecleared election system, and require the County to obtain preclearance or use a lawful interim arrangement.
- **Material Facts and Record:** **Issue 1:** Monterey County was covered by § 5 and was the governmental body that would conduct the judicial elections. California argued that later state law required the countywide system and left the County no independent choice. **Issue 2:** On the November 1, 1968 coverage date, the County had nine separate inferior-court districts whose judges were elected by the voters of their respective districts. Between 1972 and 1983, the County adopted six ordinances that ultimately produced one countywide municipal court with judges elected at large. The County did not submit those ordinances for preclearance. California's 1983 submission may have precleared the final 1983 ordinance, but it did not identify or describe the earlier consolidation ordinances. **Issue 3:** During remedial proceedings the district court ordered elections under substantially the same countywide, at-large arrangement, with ordinary six-year terms.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The Court has appellate jurisdiction over the three-judge court's order, and no mootness defect is identified. **Issue 1:** California preserved its state-plan and no-local-discretion arguments. **Issue 2:** California preserved laches, designation, and no-covered-change defenses, although the district court had not finally resolved them. **Issue 3:** The enforcement court may decide coverage, preclearance status, and an authorized temporary remedy, but it may not decide substantive preclearance.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argued October 8, 1996; simulated decision November 6, 1996. The challenged consolidation ordinances were adopted from 1972 through 1983. The Court stayed the proposed March 1996 election on February 1, 1996.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
- **Known Participation Matter:** None identified.
- **Public Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 519 U.S. 9, including the 1968 baseline, the six consolidation ordinances, the 1983 submission, the district-court orders, the threshold arguments, the stay, and the historical disposition; the jurisdictional statement and merits briefs for the parties' preserved positions.

### Historical Supreme Court comparator

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O'Connor delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by all participating Justices.
- **Historical Holding:** The historical Court reversed because the district court ordered elections under the County's own unprecleared countywide, at-large arrangement and thereby exceeded the limited role of a § 5 enforcement court. It left unresolved whether later California law had made the arrangement a state plan, whether laches applied, whether the County's coverage designation was constitutionally proper, and whether the consolidations were changes affecting voting.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The order authorizing the March 1996 countywide, at-large election shall be vacated. The district court shall enjoin Monterey County from administering the countywide, at-large system unless and until every operative post-1968 voting change necessary to that system has received § 5 preclearance. It shall determine the exact scope of any preclearance produced by California's 1983 submission, identify the remaining unprecleared changes, and require their expeditious submission through one of the forums Congress designated. If no lawful electoral arrangement can operate in time and an independent source of remedial authority permits judicial action, the court may adopt a wholly court-developed, necessary, and temporary plan. Such a plan need not itself receive § 5 preclearance, but it must comply with the Constitution and the Voting Rights Act, displace state law no further than necessity requires, and terminate when a lawful precleared arrangement becomes available. The enforcement court may not decide whether the County's plan is substantively entitled to preclearance.

#### Issue 1: State-mandated voting changes

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the state-compulsion question because it is expressly within the granted coverage question and presents a legal issue independent of the unresolved state-law characterization. He need not decide whether California law actually displaced the County ordinances because the answer is the same even if the arrangement is now wholly state mandated.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a covered political subdivision "seek[s] to administer" a postbaseline voting change when it gives the change operative effect solely because the superior law of a noncovered State requires it to do so.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Morse's functional account of electoral administration and openly extends it to the discretion question Morse left unresolved. Section 5 separately reaches a covered jurisdiction that enacts a change and one that seeks to administer a change. "Administer" concerns execution and operation, not authorship or freedom to choose. Requiring local discretion would add a condition the enacted text does not contain and would permit a noncovered State to route voting changes around § 5 throughout its covered subdivisions. Stone therefore holds that the County's duty turns on its administration of the changed election system, not on whether California or the County selected the policy. The State is not required to preclear its law in the abstract; the covered County must obtain preclearance before giving that law operative effect within Monterey County. This interpretation changes no burden or substantive preclearance standard and leaves the designated forums' authority intact.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A covered political subdivision seeks to administer a voting change when its officials give that change operative effect in the subdivision. Section 5 therefore requires preclearance before Monterey County implements a postbaseline judicial-election change mandated by California law, even if the County possesses no independent discretion over the change.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 5's paired verbs distinguish lawmaking from implementation. Monterey County conducts the elections, defines the operative electorate through the system it administers, and would put the countywide arrangement into effect. The change experienced by voters is identical whether it originated in a County ordinance or a California statute. Earlier treatment of state reapportionment affecting covered counties and the statute's preventive design reinforce the textual conclusion, but the holding rests on the enacted command. Because the County has not obtained preclearance for all operative changes, it may not administer the countywide, at-large system.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding imposes no statewide preclearance obligation on California and does not reach state laws with no operative effect in a covered jurisdiction. It does not decide whether the countywide system has a discriminatory purpose or retrogressive effect, or whether preclearance should be granted.

#### Issue 2: Covered change, designation, and laches

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the legal sufficiency of the change, designation, and laches defenses because the undisputed baseline and operation permit decision without new factual findings. He remands only the exact scope of the 1983 submission and any backward-looking remedial question.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the consolidation altered a voting standard, practice, or procedure; whether the County may collaterally defeat its published § 4(b) designation in this enforcement action; and whether delay can bar prospective enforcement of § 5's no-use command.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** First, replacing nine district electorates with one countywide electorate changes district boundaries, the constituency entitled to elect each judge, and the method by which votes translate into office. It is a covered voting change under § 5 and § 14(c)(1). Second, Monterey County's published coverage designation remains operative unless the County obtains statutory bailout. The underlying administrative coverage findings may not be collaterally relitigated here, and the facial constitutional objection is foreclosed by South Carolina v. Katzenbach and City of Rome. Third, laches cannot convert an unprecleared change into an enforceable one or bar an injunction against its future use. Delay and reliance may bear on retrospective relief, treatment of completed acts, or the timing and form of a lawful transition, but they do not dispense with preclearance.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The shift from district-based judicial elections to a countywide, at-large system is a change affecting voting. Monterey County remains covered until released through the statutory bailout process, and laches does not authorize prospective administration of an unprecleared change.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The 1968 system allowed voters in each district to elect the judges serving that district. The challenged system places every judgeship before the countywide electorate. That is a direct alteration of electoral boundaries and voting power, not an internal governmental reallocation outside § 5. The County's designation was published and remains legally effective, while Congress supplied bailout rather than collateral relitigation as the route out of coverage. The County's long noncompliance and the plaintiffs' later filing cannot make the statutory precondition disappear. California's 1983 submission may have reached the final 1983 ordinance, but it did not identify the earlier ordinances; the district court must determine its exact scope without treating one submission as blanket approval of the full consolidation sequence.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide a factual bailout claim, damages, relief concerning completed elections, or the precise administrative effect of the 1983 submission. Those matters require their own lawful record and remedial source.

#### Issue 3: Enforcement-court authority and temporary remedy

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the enforcement court's remedial authority because the challenged judgment ordered an election under an unprecleared plan. He does not reach substantive preclearance, discriminatory purpose, or retrogression because Congress assigned those questions to the Attorney General and the United States District Court for the District of Columbia.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the enforcement court may authorize the County's or the State's unprecleared policy choice, and when a genuinely court-developed temporary arrangement falls outside § 5's preclearance requirement.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** An enforcement court may determine coverage, determine whether preclearance occurred, enjoin nonuse, and select a temporary remedy authorized by law. It may not decide substantive preclearance or obtain the same practical result by ordering elections under an unprecleared governmental plan. A narrow exception applies to a plan formulated wholly by the federal court in the first instance, because such a plan is a judicial remedy rather than a voting change enacted or administered as the policy of the covered jurisdiction. The exception does not apply when the court adopts material policy choices proposed, required, or previously selected by the County or the State. A court-developed plan must be supported by necessity and independent remedial authority, must be temporary, and must yield promptly to a lawful precleared governmental plan.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A § 5 enforcement court may not authorize elections under an unprecleared County or State plan. When an independently authorized temporary remedy is indispensable, a plan wholly formulated by the federal court may operate without separate § 5 preclearance, but a court order does not exempt a plan that carries forward governmental policy choices.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The March 1996 order reinstated the same countywide, at-large arrangement Monterey County had adopted and allowed judges elected under it to serve ordinary six-year terms. The plan therefore remained a governmental policy choice, not a wholly judicial stopgap. Years of notice and the County's abandonment of its D.C. preclearance action also defeat any claim that an extreme last-minute circumstance required use of that plan. The district court's administrative difficulty does not enlarge its authority or permit it to adjudicate the plan's discriminatory purpose or effect. Its task is to enforce nonuse, require prompt submission, and employ only a separately authorized temporary remedy if one is truly necessary.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A wholly court-developed plan remains subject to all other constitutional and statutory constraints. Stone does not select the interim arrangement, approve the countywide system on the merits, or state any view on whether the Attorney General or the D.C. District Court should grant preclearance.

#### Issue 2: Fourth Amendment protection of the noncordless caller

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the legal rule but cannot finally apply it because Askin's knowledge or knowing exposure is unresolved.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a landline or cellular caller automatically loses Fourth Amendment protection because the other participant unknowingly uses a vulnerable cordless handset.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Absent participant consent, a willing informant, or knowing exposure, a caller does not assume the risk that unknown government agents will independently intercept both sides of a private conversation through the other endpoint. # Old Chief v. United States

- **Citation or Docket:** 519 U.S. 172 (1997); No. 95-6556

#### Issue 1: Rule 403 treatment of a conceded status element

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied simulated holding directly governs Rule 403 stipulations.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No exact reusable doctrine is identified.
- **Other Governing Law:** Federal Rules of Evidence 401, 402, and 403; the felon-in-possession statute; surviving Michelson and Huddleston principles concerning prior-conviction evidence and unfair prejudice.

### B. Stone's approved position

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Vacate the conviction and remand for a new trial. #### Issue 1: Rule 403 treatment of a conceded status element

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits. The issue was preserved and the judgment is final.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether an equally conclusive admission changes the Rule 403 balance when the only disputed proof concerns legal status.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Rule 403 and holds that available evidentiary alternatives matter when they prove exactly the same discrete proposition. The prosecution ordinarily controls the narrative of its case and need not accept a defendant's preferred stipulation. That principle has limited force when the element is a formal status, the admission conclusively supplies it, and the detailed prior offense adds no narrative link to the charged conduct. In that setting the incremental probative value is negligible and the status-propensity risk is acute.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When a defendant offers an unconditional admission to the complete prior-felony status element and the Government identifies no other permissible purpose, Rule 403 requires exclusion of the prior judgment's name and nature if their incremental probative value is substantially outweighed by the danger of unfair propensity prejudice.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The relevant comparison is not between some evidence and none. It is between two means of proving the same legal status. Old Chief's admission would have allowed an instruction establishing the element beyond dispute. The assault judgment added vivid information likely to invite judgment about character while doing no additional work on possession. The district court therefore exercised discretion under an erroneous conception of probative value. Because the prior offense closely resembled violent conduct and the Government cannot show the error harmless on this record, a new trial is required.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not let defendants stipulate away the narrative of a charged event, require acceptance of partial or qualified admissions, or govern prior acts offered for a genuinely disputed nonpropensity purpose.
