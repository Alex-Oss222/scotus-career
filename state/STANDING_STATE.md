## 3. Standing Practices

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991; signed explanations and certification, effective October 2, 1995.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). From October 2, 1995, referral does not prevent the Circuit Justice, or the substitute the term-effective Rules designate, from taking a necessary temporary action in his or her own name while the Court considers the application. Each action is explained in short signed writings, including the Circuit Justice's own explanation at final disposition, whether prevailing or dissenting, and a statement by each participating Justice in each Court action deciding relief, and is certified for issuance by the Chief Justice or the Chief's substitute, as Engine §10 provides. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. An application not on the Term's inventory remains subject to ordinary Circuit Justice practice. Source: the OT1991 opening practice carried in the opening Standing State and the OT1993 Term Continuity Note. Source of the October 2, 1995 revision: user instruction.

### Questions in the OT1996 sim-granted cases

As supplied with the OT1996 inventory, in case-index order.

**Hopwood v. Texas** (OT1996 case index No. 6, chunk 1)

1. Whether achieving racial and ethnic diversity in higher education constitutes a compelling governmental interest sufficient to justify considering an applicant's race in admissions under the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment.
2. Whether a public university may employ race-conscious admissions preferences to remedy the effects of past discrimination, and what showing of prior discrimination and remedial necessity the Constitution requires.
3. Whether the University of Texas School of Law's admissions system satisfies the constitutional requirements governing racial classifications, and what relief is available to applicants subjected to an unconstitutional admissions process.

**Tyler Murray v. Montrose County School District RE-1J** (OT1996 case index No. 8, chunk 1)

1. Whether the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act's least restrictive environment requirement creates a presumption that a child with disabilities must be educated in the child's neighborhood school when an appropriate educational program could be provided there with supplementary aids and services.
2. Whether a school district may assign a child with disabilities to another school offering specialized services when the district maintains that the alternative placement better satisfies the child's individualized educational needs.
3. To what extent may a reviewing court consider evidence beyond the administrative record when evaluating the appropriateness of an individualized education program and placement decision.

**In re Askin** (OT1996 case index No. 10, chunk 1)

1. Whether the warrantless interception of the radio portion of a cordless telephone conversation violates the Fourth Amendment rights of a participant who communicated through an ordinary wire telephone.
2. Whether the interception and subsequent use of such communications were prohibited by Title III of the Omnibus Crime Control and Safe Streets Act under the statutory provisions governing the interception.
3. Whether a witness may refuse criminal-trial testimony directly derived from unlawful interception of his own communications, and what findings, exclusionary-rule limits, and just-cause principles govern review of the resulting civil-contempt order.

**Humane Society v. Babbitt** (OT1996 case index No. 11, chunk 1)

1. Whether an individual who has regularly observed or studied a particular endangered animal suffers an injury in fact under Article III when government action facilitates the animal's removal from the location where the individual observes it.
2. Whether a plaintiff establishes causation and redressability when the challenged federal authorization permits, but does not independently compel, a private party's conduct allegedly causing the plaintiff's injury.
3. Whether a claimed deprivation of procedures required by the Endangered Species Act can confer standing without a separately established concrete interest threatened by the alleged procedural violation.

**United States v. Sahhar** (OT1996 case index No. 16, chunk 2)

1. Whether 18 U.S.C. §4246 permits the continued civil commitment of a mentally ill and dangerous federal defendant found incompetent to stand trial after the underlying criminal charges have been dismissed.
2. Whether continued federal civil commitment beyond the maximum punishment authorized for the underlying criminal offense violates the Due Process Clause or equal protection principles of the Fifth Amendment.
3. Whether continued federal custody is constitutionally or statutorily permissible when the individual's home state has not assumed responsibility for treatment and supervision.

**DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers' Association** (OT1996 case index No. 17, chunk 2)

1. Whether sex-based insults and derogatory commentary published in an employee association's newsletter may constitute sufficiently severe or pervasive harassment to establish a hostile work environment under Title VII.
2. Whether Title VII's prohibition on workplace harassment may be applied to expressive material published by an independent employee association consistently with the First Amendment.
3. Whether publication of disparaging references to an employee's discrimination complaint constitutes actionable retaliation under Title VII absent a separately established adverse employment action.

**Walmer v. Department of Defense** (OT1996 case index No. 20, chunk 2)

1. Whether military regulations permitting the separation of an officer on the basis of homosexual conduct or related admissions violate the equal protection component of the Fifth Amendment's Due Process Clause.
2. Whether the constitutional deference afforded to military personnel decisions permits classifications that would receive more demanding review in civilian employment.
3. Whether the challenged military separation regulation constitutes a prohibited bill of attainder or otherwise imposes constitutionally impermissible punishment without a judicial proceeding.

**Abebe-Jira v. Negewo** (OT1996 case index No. 23, chunk 2)

1. Whether 28 U.S.C. §1350 merely confers subject-matter jurisdiction over specified international-law torts, or also authorizes federal courts to recognize and enforce private civil causes of action for violations of customary international law.
2. Whether federal courts possess authority to fashion compensatory and punitive remedies for established international-law violations in an action under the Alien Tort Statute.
