## 3. Standing Practices

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991; signed explanations and certification, effective October 2, 1995.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). From October 2, 1995, referral does not prevent the Circuit Justice, or the substitute the term-effective Rules designate, from taking a necessary temporary action in his or her own name while the Court considers the application. Each action is explained in short signed writings, including the Circuit Justice's own explanation at final disposition, whether prevailing or dissenting, and a statement by each participating Justice in each Court action deciding relief, and is certified for issuance by the Chief Justice or the Chief's substitute, as Engine §10 provides. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. An application not on the Term's inventory remains subject to ordinary Circuit Justice practice. Source: the OT1991 opening practice carried in the [opening Standing State](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/13b19ee463d16fb4377d846e5b058599f886bed3/state/STANDING_STATE.md#3-standing-practices) and the [OT1993 Term Continuity Note](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/workspace/continuity.md#standing-practices). Source of the October 2, 1995 revision: user instruction.
