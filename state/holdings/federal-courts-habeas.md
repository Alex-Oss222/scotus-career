## Federal Courts: Habeas and Collateral Review

### Execution stays

#### Coleman v. Thompson, 504 U.S. 188 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. A-877; associated petition No. 91-8336.
**Decided:** May 20, 1992.

##### Whether the renewed habeas presentation satisfies the requirements for a stay pending certiorari review

**Holding:** A stay of execution pending certiorari review requires a reasonable probability that four Justices would find the issue sufficiently meritorious to grant review, a significant possibility of reversal, and likely irreparable harm without a stay. Coleman's combined presentation does not establish the necessary possibility of overcoming the surviving independent claim-default and successive-petition barriers; denial decides neither ultimate innocence nor a general freestanding-innocence or hearing rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: five of nine participating Justices adopt this record-specific application of Barefoot. Stevens concurs in the denial without joining that opinion. Stone, Blackmun and Souter dissent.

**Material application:** The disputed recantation, competing confession accounts, physical-evidence allegations and scientific interpretations are considered together but do not show the needed prospect of overcoming independent claim-default and successive-petition barriers. Irreparable harm alone does not establish the missing likelihood.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no ultimate innocence, freestanding-innocence merits rule, conclusive-exoneration requirement, automatic hearing entitlement, categorical credibility or expert-selection rule, or reversal of the preterm Coleman holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The stay application is denied, 6–3. The denial is an interim disposition and does not finally decide associated petition No. 91-8336.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880 (1983): applied; the ordinary capital-stay prerequisites remain distinct and no automatic stay is created.
- Coleman v. Thompson, 501 U.S. 722 (1991): applied; independent and adequate state claim-default rules and their established exceptions remain in force.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467 (1991): applied to the independent successive-petition and abuse barriers; its cause-and-prejudice rule and innocence exception are not replaced.
- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478 (1986): applied for the extraordinary miscarriage-of-justice exception; the Court does not require conclusive exoneration.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436 (1986): its plurality formulation identifies the lower court's stated factual-innocence inquiry; no broader controlling rule is attributed to that plurality.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, May 4, 1992, No. 90-1859: distinguished and preserved; the simulated holding concerns inadequate factual development of a properly available claim, not automatic removal of an independent claim-default bar.
- Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293 (1963): preserved through Keeney; its material-fact hearing framework is not displaced.

#### Gomez v. United States District Court for the Northern District of California, 503 U.S. 653 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. A-767.
**Decided:** April 21, 1992.

##### Whether unjustified delay independently defeats equitable support for the April 20 execution stays

**Holding:** A court considering an execution stay may weigh the State's interest in enforcing its judgment and an applicant's unjustified delay in presenting a long-available claim independently of any habeas procedural bar. On this record, the unexplained postponement of the cyanide-gas challenge through four prior federal habeas proceedings leaves the April 20 stays without sufficient equitable support; those stays must be vacated even assuming the claim is cognizable under §1983 and not barred by abuse-of-writ doctrine.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Six of nine participating Justices adopt this independent equitable ground. Stone, Blackmun and Stevens dissent. No other ground is necessary to the judgment, and no fractured-opinion rule is invoked.

**Material application:** The recent Harding account adds support but does not explain failure to raise the established cyanide-gas challenge through four prior federal habeas proceedings. Medical submissions and execution’s irreversibility do not outweigh the unexplained delay on this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The §1983 channel, the final Eighth Amendment method claim, the application of every habeas-default element, Younger abstention and any universal alternative-method requirement remain undecided. The decision creates no permanent injunction or direction barring another federal stay.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The application is granted and the April 20, 1992 execution stays in Ninth Circuit No. 92-70237 are vacated, 6–3. The order adds no injunction or general restriction on later federal stays.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Delo v. Stokes, 495 U.S. 320 — applied for full-Court review and vacatur of an unsupported execution stay; its successive-habeas holding remains intact. The equitable assessment here does not treat Kennedy's separate writing as prior Court law.
- Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880 — applied: successive litigation does not itself require a stay, and substantial grounds for relief matter to interim protection.
- In re Blodgett, January 13, 1992 — distinguished: its requirement to seek focused relief below before mandamus neither resolves this application nor supplies a broader rule about capital-litigation delay.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467 — application to this §1983 action is reserved; its cause-and-prejudice and miscarriage-of-justice rules remain governing within their proper habeas domain.
- Preiser v. Rodriguez, 411 U.S. 475; Younger v. Harris, 401 U.S. 37; Huffman v. Pursue, Ltd., 420 U.S. 592; Pennzoil Co. v. Texaco Inc., 481 U.S. 1 — their respective channel and abstention rules are left intact; the Court decides no extension to this execution-method claim.

### Habeas evidentiary hearings

#### Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, 504 U.S. 1 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1859.
**Decided:** May 4, 1992.

##### Whether ordinary counsel negligence in developing state-court facts bars a hearing on a properly presented habeas claim

**Holding:** When a federal habeas claim properly presented to the state courts turns on material facts inadequately developed in state proceedings, Townsend’s fifth mandatory-hearing circumstance remains governed by its deliberate-bypass qualification: ordinary counsel negligence, without deliberate bypass, does not bar the necessary federal evidentiary hearing. Tamayo-Reyes is entitled to a hearing confined to whether the manslaughter mental-state element was adequately explained; the supported findings and denial of a new hearing concerning translation of the plea form and plea proceeding remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, and Kennedy. All five adopt this rule, its application, and the limited hearing remedy; five of nine participating Justices constitute a majority. No independently sufficient alternative holding or fractured-opinion analysis is necessary.

**Current-force modification:** The original hearing rule remains controlling under former law. AEDPA §104 adds §2254(e)(2): when an applicant has failed to develop a claim's factual basis in state proceedings, a federal evidentiary hearing is forbidden unless the claim relies either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive to collateral review by this Court or on a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts would establish by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. Michael Wayne Williams v. Taylor, No. 99-6615, decided April 18, 2000, construes failed to develop as deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel, rather than an incomplete record alone. Reasonable efforts depend on the available information and procedures; diligent development defeated by unavailable information or state process does not trigger the restriction. Section 2254(e)(2) governs that post-enactment application and changes the consequence of deficient diligence where applicable. Keeney's deliberate-bypass rule remains controlling in its former-law domain, without rewriting its original adjudication or deciding the status of its remand. Hearing eligibility, independent state claim default and ultimate entitlement to relief remain distinct.

**Material application:** Tamayo-Reyes presented the claim to the state courts, and the State identifies no independent and adequate procedural bar. Counsel’s deficient factual development was negligent, not deliberate; the missing facts concern explanation of the manslaughter mental-state element.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Supported findings and the denial of a new hearing about translation of the plea form and plea proceeding remain undisturbed. Coleman’s independent state claim-default rule, other Townsend hearing circumstances and §2254(d)’s distinct factual-presumption rules remain operative. No constitutional error or deliberate bypass on another record is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The limited hearing requirement is affirmed and remanded for a federal evidentiary hearing confined to the manslaughter mental-state explanation, followed by adjudication of that claim. No writ or unconditional release issues now. If the claim is proved and establishes an unconstitutional plea, a conditional writ must allow the State a reasonable opportunity for a lawful plea or trial process.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293 (1963): retained and applied; its fifth circumstance and deliberate-bypass qualification continue to govern inadequate factual development. Its other hearing grounds are not displaced.
- Fay v. Noia, 372 U.S. 391 (1963): its deliberate-bypass discussion remains operative through Townsend for this hearing question; the Court does not revive Fay’s displaced claim-default rule.
- Coleman v. Thompson, 501 U.S. 722 (1991): distinguished; its cause-and-prejudice rule for independent and adequate state procedural defaults remains in force and is not extended to this hearing entitlement.
- Vasquez v. Hillery, 474 U.S. 254 (1986): relied on for the distinction between supplementing facts and fundamentally altering an exhausted claim; its deliberate-withholding reservation remains open.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996, Pub. L. 104-132, Title I, enacted April 24, 1996: §104, new §2254(e)(2), limits on evidentiary hearings. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding. Michael Wayne Williams v. Taylor, No. 99-6615, April 18, 2000: applicant-attributed failure under section 2254(e)(2) and its application to the post-enactment habeas filing.

### Habeas procedural gateways

#### Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-6382.
**Decided:** June 22, 1992.

##### What capital-innocence showing permits review of an otherwise barred habeas claim without cause and prejudice

**Holding:** A prisoner seeking review of an otherwise successive, abusive or procedurally defaulted capital habeas claim without the ordinarily required cause and prejudice must establish by clear and convincing evidence that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find the prisoner eligible for death under applicable state law. The inquiry includes the offense, required aggravating facts and every other eligibility condition; additional mitigation affecting only discretionary selection of the punishment does not satisfy this gateway.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt both the eligibility-only scope and clear-and-convincing quantum. Stone, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor concur only in the judgment on broader grounds; their votes do not enlarge the controlling rule. No Marks synthesis is necessary.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the capital-eligibility gateway to otherwise barred claims.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The crime-innocence standard is unchanged. Ordinary cause and prejudice, exhaustion, retroactivity, merits requirements and factual development of a properly available claim remain distinct. Passing the gateway does not itself grant relief. No freestanding innocence claim or general bar to consideration of impeachment evidence is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The precise capital formulation previously left open is established effective June 22, 1992. Sawyer's refusal of further review is affirmed on the application stated below; no new hearing, sentencing proceeding or release is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478, preserves cause and prejudice and its miscarriage exception; only the capital-eligibility branch is specified here.
- Smith v. Murray, 477 U.S. 527, has its open sentencing application specified through eligibility rather than discretionary mitigation reweighing.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467, is applied: abuse remains an independent obstacle unless cause and prejudice or the applicable exceptional gateway is established.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436, supplies relevant plurality discussion without becoming a retrospective majority holding.
- Dugger v. Adams, 489 U.S. 401, is applied: possible sentencing inaccuracy does not itself establish exceptional innocence.
- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862, and Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231, supply the distinction between death eligibility and selection of punishment.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104, preserve mitigation protection on the merits without independently reopening a barred claim.
- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668, and United States v. Bagley, 473 U.S. 667, supply different reasonable-probability merits inquiries; they do not replace the exceptional gateway.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, May 4, 1992, retains Townsend for development of properly available claims without removing successive or abusive-claim barriers. Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992, and Sochor v. Florida, June 8, 1992, preserve correction of available weighing errors without eliminating independent barriers; Wright v. West, June 19, 1992, changes no general habeas-review standard.

##### Whether Sawyer's added evidence removes every reasonable basis for death eligibility

**Holding:** Sawyer's psychiatric and family evidence concerns mitigation, while the impeachment affidavits and caregiver account do not clearly and convincingly establish that no reasonable juror would find the surviving bases of death eligibility. His abusive Brady claim and successive sentencing-ineffectiveness claim therefore remain barred.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by White, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five adopt this gateway application. The remaining four Justices independently support affirmance under broader inquiries but do not join this rationale.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as the stated application to Sawyer's submissions; it is not a categorical exclusion of later impeachment evidence.

**Material application:** Louisiana principal liability permits aggravated-arson eligibility without personal ignition. Sawyer's participation and the lighter-fluid evidence remain material; the preceding beating and scalding independently support the surviving cruelty aggravator. The credibility affidavits do not require every reasonable juror to reject the core account, and the medical and childhood submissions do not negate necessary eligibility facts.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No constitutional merits claim is resolved after the gateway fails; the evidence is not found false in every respect, and death is not held mandatory. The accepted procedural classifications and cause determination are not reopened. The prior Caldwell ruling, a new challenge to the wording of an aggravator, and the distinct lower-court competency claim outside this petition remain unadjudicated here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Fifth Circuit's refusal of further habeas review. No new factual hearing, sentencing proceeding, trial or release follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83, supplies the underlying suppressed-evidence claim but no merits disposition here. Strickland, 466 U.S. 668, does not allow ordinary sentencing prejudice to replace the gateway. Sawyer v. Smith, 497 U.S. 227, retains its distinct Caldwell retroactivity disposition.

#### Schlup v. Delo

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7901.
**Decided:** January 23, 1995.

##### Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway

**Holding:** A prisoner seeking to overcome otherwise applicable successive or abusive habeas barriers to independent constitutional claims through innocence of the offense must present reliable new evidence and show, considering the probative record as a whole, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. This procedural gateway does not itself establish innocence, prove any constitutional violation or shift the ultimate burden on the underlying claims; Sawyer's clear-and-convincing rule remains confined to its distinct death-eligibility setting.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's gateway and all-evidence portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: applies and specifies its probability-based crime-innocence exception without changing cause and prejudice.
- Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333: preserves the clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway and resolves the crime-innocence question that decision left open; it is not overruled or given a categorical impeachment exclusion.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467: preserves successive/abusive-claim barriers and the exceptional innocence route, without treating potential constitutional merit as sufficient to pass it.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436: uses the relevant plurality discussion of the whole probative record as persuasive guidance, preserving its actual authority limit.
- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307: distinguishes trial-record legal sufficiency from the likely effect of an augmented record on reasonable jurors; its sufficiency rule remains intact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court considers all relevant probative evidence, including evidence excluded at trial or becoming available afterward; trial-admissibility rules do not categorically delimit the gateway record, and even improperly admitted trial evidence may have probative force subject to reliability. The court must assess reliability and likely credibility, not accept every affidavit as true or treat the original verdict as a conclusive answer. Timing, delay and inconsistency may matter; no categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discredits a witness solely because the witness is a prisoner. A mere possibility of acquittal, a juror who might disagree, ordinary constitutional-merits prejudice or the reviewing judge's personal reasonable doubt is insufficient. The reasonable juror fairly considers the evidence and follows the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt instruction.

##### This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission

**Holding:** The courts' use of the rejected clear-and-convincing crime gateway and reliance on the original jury's credibility function require vacatur and renewed assessment on this record; §2244(b)'s permission to refuse a qualifying subsequent petition does not itself supply an adequate independent ground to sustain that legally mistaken refusal. The Court remands through the Eighth Circuit to the District Court for application of the probability rule and a reasoned determination of necessary factual development, without deciding whether every successful innocence showing compels review, choosing a general appellate standard, or ordering a hearing, writ, retrial, release or stay automatically.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's statutory-refusal and remand portion, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth judgment vote without joining the separate §2244(b) rationale.

**Current-force modification:** AEDPA §106 replaces former §2244(b)'s refusal permission for successive §2254 district-court applications. A claim already presented must be dismissed. A new claim must be dismissed unless it rests on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court, or both a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence and facts that, if proved and considered with the whole evidence, establish by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt. Advance authorization by a three-judge court-of-appeals panel requires a prima facie showing; authorization does not remove the district court's duty to dismiss a claim unless the applicant proves the statutory requirements. This is an independent statutory condition, not a replacement of Schlup's probability gateway or a ruling that the gateway itself satisfies the new text. The original former-law remand stands. Pending-case application is not generally resolved; Felker v. Turpin, June 28, 1996, No. 95-8836, applies the post-enactment authorization-review bar while reserving whether §§2244(b)(1)–(2) directly bind original habeas consideration in this Court.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McCleskey, 499 U.S. 467: applies the distinction between conditional consideration of new, nonabusive grounds and legally constrained refusal of successive or abusive grounds; neither permission nor exception becomes an unconditional command.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, 504 U.S. 1, and Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293: preserve necessary factual development within their properly presented, available-claim setting and the deliberate-bypass qualification; ordinary counsel negligence alone does not forfeit a required hearing there, but no successive/abusive gateway is eliminated.
- Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: requires use of the selected crime-innocence rule before the present refusal can be sustained on its innocence assessment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2244(b) provides:

> (b) When after an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue, or after a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, a person in custody pursuant to the judgment of a State court has been denied by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States release from custody or other remedy on an application for a writ of habeas corpus, a subsequent application for a writ of habeas corpus in behalf of such person need not be entertained by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States unless the application alleges and is predicated on a factual or other ground not adjudicated on the hearing of the earlier application for the writ, and unless the court, justice, or judge is satisfied that the applicant has not on the earlier application deliberately withheld the newly asserted ground or otherwise abused the writ.

The prior-hearing alternatives are a factual evidentiary merits hearing or a legal merits hearing, each followed by a federal denial of release or other habeas remedy to a person held under a state judgment. Both unless conditions must be preserved: the later application must allege and rest on an unadjudicated factual or other ground, and the court must be satisfied that the newly asserted ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise an abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground; absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. McCleskey requires consideration when both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent. The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner's burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate; cause and prejudice is not pressed in this review. Subsection (a)'s separate wording is not inserted into subsection (b), and no new subsection (c) predicate is established.

The videotape was trial evidence. Green's asserted prompt call, Faherty's movement account and other later identification accounts must be assessed with the existing record; the Court finds no witness truthful or false and does not find the gateway satisfied. Proper claim classification, exhaustion, default, abuse, retroactivity and a genuinely sufficient independent merits ground retain their separate operation. Remand is not mandatory constitutional-merits review. Any necessary live testimony is for a reasoned determination under the applicable rules, not an automatic consequence of affidavits or vacatur. The merits of the suppression and ineffective-assistance claims, a freestanding innocence entitlement and any ultimate guilt determination remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit judgment is vacated and remanded through that court to the District Court for the probability gateway and a reasoned decision on necessary factual development. No gateway satisfaction, automatic hearing, constitutional-merits review, writ, retrial, release or stay is ordered.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996, Pub. L. 104-132, Title I, enacted April 24, 1996: §106, replacement §2244(b)(1)–(4) district-court successive-application mechanism; see also Felker v. Turpin, June 28, 1996, No. 95-8836, distinct original jurisdiction and reserved direct statutory reach. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

#### Lynce v. Mathis

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-7452.
**Decided:** February 19, 1997.

##### Exhaustion and the qualified futility concession before favorable habeas relief

**Holding:** Before a favorable habeas merits adjudication on Lynce's sole Ex Post Facto claim, the lower courts must determine the actual exhaustion predicate and the scope and legal effect of the State's qualified futility concession. The election to pursue only the cancellation claim neither creates a mixed petition from hypothetical additional theories nor by itself establishes exhaustion, express waiver, or the absence or ineffectiveness of available state corrective process.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins the judgment only.

**Material application:** Florida raised exhaustion and refused waiver while conceding futility for the common cancellation question concerning prisoners still in custody. It distinguished prior release and possible reincarceration claims; Lynce later confirmed only the Ex Post Facto claim. That election supports a claim-specific exception argument but does not conclusively resolve the qualification. Morales provides intervening law relevant to whether older adverse Florida precedents now make state process ineffective.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No Ex Post Facto merits rule, credit-system continuity, protected amount, lawful release date, procedural default or cause-and-prejudice holding is made. The 1996 amendment's general temporal application is undecided; the ruling rests on no relevant version difference and creates no implied waiver. Permission to deny despite nonexhaustion is not permission to grant on an assumed exhaustion predicate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Unanimous vacatur of the certificate-denial disposition and remand require the actual exhaustion and concession determination under ordinary certificate procedures. A legally sufficient exception remains available; no unconditional state-court detour, new filings, credit restoration or release is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Granberry distinguishes appellate power from exhaustion but concerned an omitted defense, unlike Florida's maintained objection. Duckworth rejects merits clarity or economy as independent excuses. Rose concerns actual claims, not hypothetical additions. Morales and Cavanaugh retain their meaningful-opportunity rules without deciding Florida credits or dispensing with habeas prerequisites. Engle cautions against equating anticipated failure with cause; Schall's report of a lower-court futility conclusion creates no general exception.

### Freestanding innocence and execution stays

#### Herrera v. Collins, 506 U.S. 390 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7328.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### This record does not establish entitlement to federal innocence relief

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** Herrera's delayed and materially inconsistent affidavits, considered with the identification, physical and documentary evidence supporting his convictions, do not establish entitlement to the requested federal habeas relief or justify the district court's innocence-based stay, even assuming an exceptionally persuasive post-trial showing could constitutionally bar execution. This record-specific rejection establishes no uniform freestanding-innocence burden and does not decide whether such a constitutional claim exists.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's plurality opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, and White's express agreement with its reasons for finding this submission insufficient in his concurrence in the judgment: five Justices adopt this limited record conclusion. White does not join the plurality's broader discussion or an unspecified extraordinarily high threshold. No whole opinion commands five votes; no Marks synthesis establishes a common general test.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307 — trial-record sufficiency remains distinct; White uses its rational-factfinder formulation only as a minimum for an assumed new-evidence claim, without a five-Justice adoption of that general burden.
- Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880 — applied to require a substantial legal basis for an execution stay.
- Delo v. Stokes, 495 U.S. 320 — applied to the substantial-ground requirement on a second or successive federal petition.
- Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293, and Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, May 4, 1992 — preserved; necessary factual development of a properly available claim remains distinct from legal sufficiency, cognizability, exhaustion and abuse.
- Sawyer v. Whitley, June 22, 1992 — distinguished; its capital-eligibility gateway does not decide freestanding guilt innocence or supply its merits burden.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No controlling opinion recognizes or rejects a freestanding constitutional innocence claim or fixes its general burden. The undefined extraordinary threshold and White’s rational-factfinder minimum have no established logical-subset relationship under Marks. The hearing, exhaustion, abuse, Teague and distinct Sawyer capital-eligibility gateway rules retain independent operation. The federal habeas disposition does not determine the authority of a State to establish independent postconviction procedures for newly discovered evidence, actual innocence, or scientific developments undermining evidence relied upon at trial. Neither the existence nor the scope of such a state statutory remedy is before the Court. No general federal entitlement to a hearing upon any subsequent scientific disagreement is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm denial of the requested relief, 5–4; the vacation of the innocence-based federal stay remains. No credibility finding, innocence hearing, new writ, stay or execution date is ordered.

### Habeas records and law of the case

#### Dobbs v. Zant, 506 U.S. 357 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-5579.

**Decided:** January 19, 1993.

##### Accurate transcript before law-of-the-case review

**Holding:** When a recovered capital-sentencing transcript materially contradicts the factual account supporting an earlier habeas decision, an appellate court may not exclude it without justification and then invoke that exclusion to defeat its own manifest-injustice exception to law of the case. On this record, delay substantially caused by the State's erroneous representations that no transcript existed does not justify exclusion; the transcript must receive consideration through proper further proceedings.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; six of nine participating Justices adopt this rule and the remand. Scalia and Thomas concur only in the judgment; White would use plenary review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gardner v. Florida, 430 U.S. 349 — its plurality's complete-record concern supports this confined correction; no broader plurality proposition becomes a holding by citation.
- Gregg v. Georgia, 428 U.S. 153 — its joint opinion's approval of complete capital-review records supplies the reliability rationale; Georgia's entire sentencing scheme is not reexamined.
- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 — performance and prejudice remain unchanged and undecided here.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, May 4, 1992 — preserved; its Townsend deliberate-bypass rule for inadequate state factual development does not displace the distinct record-correction question or independent claim bars.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The confined record-correction ruling does not establish ineffective assistance or prejudice, abolish independent procedural bars, or create a general capital exception to exhaustion, default or record rules. Proper procedural implementation and any harmlessness assessment remain below.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for consideration of the recovered sentencing transcript. Strickland performance and prejudice remain undecided.

### Miranda habeas review and unpleaded coercion

#### Withrow v. Williams, 507 U.S. 680 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1030.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### A fair state opportunity does not itself bar Miranda habeas review

**Holding:** Powell’s prudential restriction on Fourth Amendment exclusionary claims does not extend to a state prisoner’s claim that the conviction rests on statements obtained in violation of Miranda. A full and fair state opportunity therefore does not itself foreclose that claim; ordinary habeas requirements, merits analysis and the governing harmless-error inquiry remain necessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, I–II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Powell, 428 U.S. 465 — distinguished and not extended; its actual prudential scope remains.
- Miranda, 384 U.S. 436 — preserved on habeas for a conviction resting on improperly obtained statements.
- Kimmelman, 477 U.S. 365; Jackson, 443 U.S. 307; Rose v. Mitchell, 443 U.S. 545 — applied as limits on extending Powell to distinct trial protections.
- Miller v. Fenton, 474 U.S. 104, and Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 — preserved: independent voluntariness review remains available on its own predicates.

##### An unpleaded, unconsented coercion theory requires fair litigation

**Holding:** A habeas court may not grant relief on the independent postwarning involuntariness theory presented here when it was neither pleaded nor tried by express or implied consent and the State lacked an opportunity to present relevant evidence. Litigation of Miranda custody and warning issues does not by itself supply consent; any later presentation remains subject to ordinary pleading, exhaustion, retroactivity and other applicable requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 — preserved: actual police coercion is necessary to due-process involuntariness; absent warnings alone do not establish it.
- Miller v. Fenton, 474 U.S. 104 — preserved: voluntariness requires its own legal determination on the proper record.
- Teague, 489 U.S. 288 — reserved here; no new exception or merits resolution of the retroactivity objection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Miranda and due-process involuntariness are independent inquiries. Rule 15(b) consent is absent on this record; custody/warning litigation alone does not provide it. Coercion, exhaustion and Teague questions remain for any lawfully presented claim. No automatic writ, universal prophylactic-safeguard review rule or reopening of Powell-barred search claims follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm availability of Miranda review; reverse the independent postwarning voluntariness ruling for lack of pleading, consent and fair opportunity to litigate; remand for proper merits and remedial analysis.

### Capital habeas preparation and stays

#### McFarland v. Scott, 512 U.S. 849

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-6497.

**Decided:** 1994-06-30.

##### Qualified capital counsel may be appointed before the merits application

**Holding:** A defendant who is or becomes financially unable to obtain adequate representation or reasonably necessary investigative, expert or other services may initiate the postconviction proceeding contemplated by 21 U.S.C. §848(q)(4)(B) through a request for counsel to prepare an identified federal §2254 or §2255 challenge seeking to vacate or set aside a death sentence. The statute entitles the qualifying defendant to counsel before a formal merits application, subject to the qualifications and service conditions stated below; a request unrelated to that federal capital challenge does not qualify.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Seven Justices directly adopt the statutory rule and its application to the October 22 motion.

**Current-force modification:** The preapplication counsel entitlement under §848(q)(4)(B) is unchanged. AEDPA §108 replaces paragraph (9)'s service authorization: upon a finding that investigative, expert or other services are reasonably necessary for representation concerning guilt or sentence, the court may authorize counsel to obtain them and, if authorized, must order payment under paragraph (10). An ex parte proceeding, communication or request requires a proper showing of need for confidentiality and must be transcribed into the appellate-review record. The original mandatory service-authorization language and express retroactive-authorization provision describe former text, not a continuing command under the replacement. The April 24 source admission does not decide this amendment's temporal application to a particular pending proceeding; the original adjudication is unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 848(q)(6), governing appointment after judgment, requires at least one appointed attorney admitted to the relevant court of appeals for five years with three years of experience handling felony appeals there. Paragraph (5)'s separate pre-judgment rule requires five years' admission in the trial court and three years' actual felony-trial experience there; it does not replace paragraph (6). Under paragraph (7), good cause permits another attorney whose background, knowledge or experience enables proper representation, with due consideration of the possible penalty's seriousness and the litigation's unique and complex nature. Under paragraph (8), representation continues through subsequent available judicial proceedings, all available postconviction process, stay applications and other appropriate motions and procedures, and available competency and clemency proceedings unless similarly qualified counsel replaces the attorney on the attorney's or defendant's motion. The full reach of that provision into later state proceedings is not decided.

Under the then-operative paragraph (9), an ex parte finding that investigative, expert or other services are reasonably necessary for representation concerning guilt or sentence requires authorization and payment under paragraph (10). If timely procurement could not practicably await prior authorization, the court may authorize the services and payment retroactively; it is not required to do so. Paragraph (10) requires compensation and service fees and expenses at rates or amounts the court determines reasonably necessary, notwithstanding generally applicable criminal-case rates and maxima. These conditions do not authorize unconditional payment for unidentified services.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Murray v. Giarratano, 492 U.S. 1 (1989): distinguished; its constitutional collateral-counsel question does not restrict the express federal statutory appointment mechanism.
- Herrera v. Collins, January 25, 1993: its record-specific rejection of the asserted innocence showing remains intact; no general freestanding-innocence standard is imported into this appointment question.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996, Pub. L. 104-132, Title I, enacted April 24, 1996: §108, replacement §848(q)(9) service authorization and confidentiality. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

##### The statutory preparation proceeding satisfies §2251 pendency

**Holding:** A qualifying request invoking §848(q)(4)(B) counsel to prepare the identified federal capital challenge commences a habeas proceeding for §2251's limited preparatory purpose. The federal justice or judge before whom it is pending therefore has authority to consider a stay of covered state proceedings although the formal merits application has not yet been filed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt this construction; O'Connor does not join it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The judge may act before final judgment, after final judgment of discharge, or pending appeal. The covered state proceeding must be against the detained person in a state court or under state authority and concern a matter involved in the habeas proceeding. After a stay is granted, covered subsequent state proceedings are void; without a stay, they remain valid as though no federal habeas proceeding or appeal were pending.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880 (1983): its capital merits and successive-review principles are preserved in their settings; they do not eliminate the statutory preparatory stage.
- Herrera v. Collins, January 25, 1993: distinguished as adjudication of an actual substantive submission, not the initiation of this statutory preparation proceeding.

##### Section 2251 supplies the express statutory exception to §2283

**Holding:** For a covered state-court proceeding within the pending habeas matter described above, §2251 expressly authorizes a stay and thus satisfies §2283's exception for an Act of Congress expressly authorizing an injunction. This ruling does not independently find §2283's distinct exceptions for aid of jurisdiction or protection or effectuation of judgments satisfied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Justice Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt this statutory conclusion; it is distinct from the appointment entitlement and from the exercise of stay discretion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Financial eligibility and a covered counsel request establish the appointment entitlement. Section 2251 separately supplies stay power after the Court's pendency construction. Section 2283's express-authorization exception answers the prohibition on staying state-court proceedings. None of those conclusions alone requires a stay.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880 (1983): preserved; this statutory authorization ruling neither displaces its applicable review principles nor creates an automatic capital stay.

##### Reasonable preparation and individualized stay discretion govern the remand

**Holding:** The district court must appoint qualified counsel and afford a reasonable opportunity for meaningful investigation and presentation of available habeas claims, considering a stay when necessary to protect that work. A stay is neither automatic nor indefinite: the court must assess the actual circumstances and may deny relief where the prisoner inexcusably ignores an available opportunity and obstructs the process; no such finding is made here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Justice Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six adopt the stay-discretion rule and common remand; O'Connor separately joins the appointment direction under Part I only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No conviction or death sentence is vacated, no release or merits writ is ordered, no new execution date is found, and no decision concerns the separate October 26 action's dismissal or exhaustion. The grant of leave to proceed without prepayment of fees remains undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880 (1983): preserved for merits and successive-review questions; distinguished from reasonable preparation under the enacted counsel right.
- Murray v. Giarratano, 492 U.S. 1 (1989): does not sustain denial of the statutory federal assistance recognized here.
- Reed v. Farley, June 20, 1994: collateral-relief and exhaustion rules remain separate; exhaustion retains exceptions where state corrective process is absent or circumstances make it ineffective to protect the prisoner's rights.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Appointment denial is reversed 7–2, and categorical denial of preparatory stay authority is reversed 6–3. Remand requires qualified appointment and individualized consideration of a necessary stay, not an automatic or indefinite stay. The certificate denial is removed only insofar as it blocks that statutory consideration; no certificate for an unidentified constitutional claim or general §2253 rule follows. Fee leave remains undisturbed; neither the conviction, sentence nor separate October 26 action is adjudicated.

### Statutory habeas claims and collateral thresholds

#### Reed v. Farley, 512 U.S. 339

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5418.

**Decided:** 1994-06-20.

##### The compact supplies federal law and a federal habeas question

**Holding:** The congressionally sanctioned Interstate Agreement on Detainers is federal law, and a state prisoner's claim that custody violates its trial-period provisions presents a federal statutory question within 28 U.S.C. §2254(a) as in force at decision. Jurisdiction to consider that question does not itself establish entitlement to collateral relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. These five Justices directly adopt the jurisdictional conclusion; the dissent also accepts the compact's federal character but does not join the opinion.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cuyler v. Adams, 449 U.S. 433 (1981): applied to the congressionally sanctioned compact's federal-law character; voluntary state participation does not remove that character.
- Fex v. Michigan, February 23, 1993: its application of the compact as federal law remains intact; its Article III actual-receipt trigger does not replace Article IV's arrival trigger or decide collateral entitlement.
- Stone v. Powell, 428 U.S. 465 (1976): its restriction concerning the Fourth Amendment exclusionary remedy is not extended to decide this statutory claim.
- Withrow v. Williams, April 21, 1993: its refusal to extend Powell merely from a fair state opportunity to a different safeguard is preserved; its Miranda holding does not itself decide the distinct statutory-relief threshold here.

##### The assumed timing violation does not establish the required collateral defect on this record

**Holding:** An ordinary nonconstitutional statutory error asserted under §2254 must satisfy the collateral standard of a fundamental defect inherently resulting in a complete miscarriage of justice, or an omission inconsistent with the rudimentary demands of fair procedure; the §2255 standard applies to this claim despite the different source of the conviction. Assuming Article IV(c) was violated, Reed's presented record—including his predeadline written invocations, the absence of a specific contemporaneous objection to the scheduled dates, and no established defense impairment from the initial overrun—does not establish that defect, so the federal denial of habeas relief is affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and III of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly join the standard and this record's denial. The stronger-demand discussion in Part IV has three votes and supplies no additional Court holding; no combination of separate opinions is required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes rather than decides the Article IV(c) breach and does not adopt automatic motion-pendency tolling, find a valid continuance, or resolve the competing statutory-merits positions. It does not impose a required oral formula or article-number citation, find complete silence or knowing waiver, or decide that an adequately presented demand necessarily produces habeas relief. No independent Sixth Amendment claim is presented. Barker and Doggett retain their complete constitutional inquiry, including Doggett's qualified treatment of presumed defense impairment; neither a 120-day overrun alone nor absence of separately proved prejudice alone answers that inquiry. The transfer-hearing and habitual-offender issues are outside the question decided. No categorical bar to every IAD claim, affirmative entitlement in an aggravated case, or general rule governing every statutory habeas claim is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hill v. United States, 368 U.S. 424 (1962): its ordinary nonconstitutional-error threshold is extended from §2255 to the presented §2254 claim; its distinction between formal omission and fundamental defect remains operative.
- United States v. Timmreck, 441 U.S. 780 (1979): its collateral distinction is applied to this timing claim without treating every statutory entitlement as a technicality or deciding every aggravated case.
- Davis v. United States, 417 U.S. 333 (1974): its statutory no-offense holding remains available; this procedural timing claim does not establish that Reed's conduct lay outside the criminal prohibition.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993: its retention of Chapman for the preserved constitutional Doyle claim is distinguished, not displaced; this case decides statutory collateral entitlement rather than the harmlessness of an established constitutional trial error.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit's denial of habeas relief is affirmed, 5–4, without adopting the lower courts' distinct statutory-merits grounds. No writ, dismissal, remand, new trial or release is ordered; the conviction and sentence remain undisturbed by this claim.

### Habeas custody under consecutive sentences

#### Garlotte v. Fordice

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-6790.
**Decided:** May 30, 1995.

##### Custody under a continuous consecutive series

**Holding:** A prisoner serving a continuous unexpired series of consecutive sentences remains in custody under the earlier conviction when invalidating it would advance release from the aggregate term, although its first segment has been served. This establishes the custody threshold, leaving the conviction’s validity and remaining procedural and merits conditions for review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; eight Justices directly support this rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An independently expired sentence's collateral consequences alone remain insufficient under Maleng. The rule concerns a continuous unexpired aggregate whose release date would advance; ordinary habeas procedural and merits requirements remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Peyton v. Rowe, 391 U.S. 54: extends its aggregate treatment from a future segment to a completed first segment of a still-unexpired consecutive series when invalidation advances release.
- Maleng v. Cook, 490 U.S. 488: distinguishes a wholly expired independent sentence and preserves the rule that collateral consequences alone do not supply custody under that conviction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit's jurisdictional dismissal and remand for remaining procedural and merits review, including review of the district court's merits denial. No conviction is invalidated, writ awarded or immediate release ordered.

### Habeas procedure and execution stays

#### Lonchar v. Thomas, 517 U.S. 314

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-5015.
**Decided:** April 1, 1996.

##### General equitable disapproval of delay cannot replace the governing dismissal predicates

**Holding:** A first personal federal habeas petition satisfying jurisdictional and pleading requirements cannot be terminated solely for the reported delay and litigation purpose by an additional equitable sanction dispensing with habeas statutes and Rules. Delay may support dismissal under Rule 9 or another lawful source only when that source's predicates are satisfied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2243's law-and-justice direction operates within the statutory scheme. Its initial writ-or-show-cause command excepts an application showing nonentitlement; Rule 4 requires prompt examination, summary dismissal with notice if nonentitlement plainly appears from the petition and exhibits, otherwise an answer or other appropriate action and service on respondent and the state attorney general. Neither requires a merits writ or evidentiary hearing.

Pre-AEDPA §2254 retains federal custody requirements and exhaustion, including absent or ineffective state-process exceptions. Rule 9(a) permits dismissal for filing delay prejudicing the State's ability to respond unless reasonable diligence could not have disclosed the grounds before prejudice arose; losing an execution date or generalized finality costs alone do not establish that prejudice, and no five-year presumption was enacted. Rule 9(b) separately requires repeated grounds after an actual prior merits determination or new grounds omitted through abuse. Failed next-friend captions alone establish neither successive status nor prior personal merits review.

The then-effective §2244(b) permission follows federal denial after a material factual evidentiary hearing or legal merits hearing. Its exception requires both an unadjudicated ground and no deliberate withholding or other abuse; new evidence is not necessarily a new ground, and absence of withholding does not eliminate every abuse. When both conditions hold and other barriers are absent, McCleskey requires consideration; clear, particular State abuse pleading precedes the prisoner's burden. No such subsequent-petition predicate is established here. Exhaustion, default, source-authorized sanctions and unpresented fraud or inherent-power issues are not erased or decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** McCleskey's structured abuse conditions remain. Schlup and Sawyer retain their distinct innocence gateways without making innocence an entry condition for every first petition. Delo v. Stokes's successive-petition rule supplies no automatic first-petition merits or abuse determination.

##### Independent equitable stay discretion survives the rejection of an additional dismissal sanction

**Holding:** First-petition status does not exclude lawful independent consideration of State enforcement interests, unjustified delay and proved obstruction in deciding an execution stay. That discretion cannot sustain this appellate order on its erroneous premise that the petition could be dismissed through the additional sanction rejected above.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2251 permits the judge before whom habeas is pending to stay covered state proceedings before final judgment, after final judgment of discharge, or pending appeal, involving the detained person and a matter in the habeas proceeding. Covered proceedings after a stay are void; without a stay they remain valid as if habeas were not pending. Filing alone suspends nothing. Serious claims and irreversibility alone do not compel late relief, and the reported conduct does not establish that required process is complete or a claim-specific disposition available.

**Operative remedy or transition:** All nine set aside the appellate displacement of the district stay and require prompt district examination of the actual petition and proper State objections, with reasoned redetermination of necessary protection. Eight formally vacate and remand; Stone-Zsela uses reverse and remand with the same operative effect. The displaced district stay is restored subject to lawful modification or dissolution, without a separate Supreme Court stay, numerical term, indefinite protection, merits writ or compelled hearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gomez remains an independent equitable rule even on an assumed open procedural route; its four prior petitions are not a minimum trigger. McFarland preserves denial for proved inexcusable disregard of a reasonable opportunity and obstruction while protecting actual statutory preparation. Neither creates an unsourced permanent dismissal.

##### Necessary protection must preserve genuinely required habeas process when lawful expedition cannot

**Holding:** If execution would prevent completion of process actually required by habeas law and lawful prompt disposition cannot finish in time, the court must provide interim protection necessary to make that identified opportunity meaningful. Mere filing, first-petition status and a capital sentence establish neither predicate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, joined by Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Kennedy joins only necessary opportunity, without deciding its extent here. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas reserve the general proposition; Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court must identify process still due. Rule 4 screening, lawful procedural dismissal and expedition remain; if they permit timely lawful disposition, no further protection follows. The rule guarantees neither every requested merits step nor an evidentiary hearing, and establishes no particular undisclosed claim or necessary period here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Barefoot's meaningful-review principle is extended by analogy to genuinely required district process, without importing its initial-certified-appeal trigger. On an initial appeal with a certificate of probable cause, meaningful opportunity and an appellate merits decision remain required; if execution would moot review before considered disposition, a stay must issue, with adequate notice permitting expedited or simultaneous merits/stay disposition. Certification requires a substantial federal showing beyond nonfrivolity, not ultimate success or sentence severity alone. Successive-petition stays still require substantial grounds. Certiorari-stage protection separately requires reasonable probability of four votes to review, significant possibility of reversal and likely irreparable harm, adequate materials and prior resort to the court of appeals, whose action generally receives considerable weight. McFarland's actual-opportunity and obstruction limits and Gomez's independent equity remain intact.

### Successive habeas petitions and execution stays

#### Bowersox v. Williams, 517 U.S. 345

**Docket or dockets:** Application No. A-828.
**Decided:** April 9, 1996, final application disposition.

##### The identified grounds do not sustain this successive-petition execution stay

**Holding:** A stay pending a second or successive federal habeas petition requires substantial grounds on which relief might be granted. On the identified claims and expressly bounded assumptions below, no sufficient ground sustains this stay, which is vacated under abuse-of-discretion review.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Parts I–V, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: five Justices adopt all following propositions at the limited application-assessment level. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Petition count, certification, scheduled argument and denied rehearing do not establish substantial grounds automatically; an unexplained stay does not establish their absence. The first petition's nonexhaustion dismissal was not a merits denial; the second's actual merits adjudication bears on the repeated instruction ground. No universal appellate-explanation duty, habeas merits judgment or categorical bar to further review is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Grant the application and vacate the March 8 Eighth Circuit stay, 5–4. No substitute Supreme Court stay, fixed grace period, writ, appeal dismissal, execution direction or finding of execution is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Barefoot and Delo v. Stokes supply the successive-petition substantial-grounds inquiry and review of its actual basis. Barefoot's initial-certified-appeal and certiorari tests retain their distinct triggers; Delo v. Blair's equal division supplies no controlling rationale. Gomez, McFarland and Lonchar preserve independent equity and actual necessary-process protection but establish no unidentified further obligation or necessary interval here.

##### The reported death preference supplies no substantial cause ground by itself

**Holding:** A death preference leading a prisoner to withhold permission for a sentence challenge does not by itself establish an external objective factor preventing presentation. On the reported account, that issue supplies no substantial available ground for maintaining this stay.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, the same five Justices.

**Material application:** Contrary evidence that prison conditions caused the preference remains relevant, but preference alone does not establish preventive impairment. The reported no-causation finding, conditions litigation and representation that legitimate sentence challenges were raised support the limited assessment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No expert-credibility, incapacity, independent ineffective-assistance, undisclosed-mitigation or constitutional-prejudice finding is made. The 1990 opportunity bears on diligence without a categorical duty to request remand. Clear, particular State abuse pleading must precede the prisoner's burden; missing filings establish no defect or concession.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** McCleskey distinguishes an actual external impediment from inexcusable neglect; intentional abandonment is not indispensable to abuse.

##### Genuine supported noncapital options defeat the asserted all-or-nothing instruction ground

**Holding:** Where genuine, evidence-supported noncapital verdict choices are supplied, Beck does not require every supported lesser-offense instruction. Williams's ordinary second-degree murder and manslaughter options defeat the all-or-nothing premise, and old trial evidence and prior judicial disagreement do not independently require redetermination or sustain this stay.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, the same five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Nominal unsupported alternatives would not suffice. Both trial parties requested the omitted first-degree instruction, and disputed intent, kidnapping and witness evidence remain real disputes, not conclusive prosecution proof. No new evidence, changed Missouri law or separately current first-degree equal-protection claim is supplied.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Schad Part III supplies the genuine-supported-alternative limit; Beck and Hopper preserve the rational lesser-conviction-and-greater-acquittal requirement. Sanders retains constrained reconsideration discretion and the applicant's ends-of-justice burden, but discretion creates neither a federal merit nor an obligation to reopen the previously adjudicated ground.

##### The bounded second-degree instruction assumptions do not establish substantial due-process or equal-protection grounds

**Holding:** Assuming the omitted second-degree felony-murder instruction was available under then-applicable state rules, differently withheld from materially similar cases and procedurally available for review, that limited inconsistency does not present a substantial Hicks arbitrary-deprivation claim when genuine supported noncapital options remain without a separate posited defect. Separately, the assumed differing treatment alone supplies no substantial unconstitutional unequal-administration ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV, the same five Justices, independently adopting both constitutional assessments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** These are assessment assumptions, not merits findings. State-law availability alone does not establish a mandatory instructional duty. The Court requires no protected class, finds no absence of discriminatory purpose, and announces no general immunity for arbitrary state adjudication. The second-degree theories remain distinct from the prior first-degree ground; ultimate habeas relief is undecided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Schad resolves the Beck demand for an additional option, not by itself the separate constitutional theories. Hicks protects state-created opportunities from arbitrary deprivation; its invalid mandatory rule removed statutory jury discretion, unlike the bounded omission leaving genuine lawful noncapital choices here.

##### Missing comparison files and the proposed late new subject do not sustain this stay

**Holding:** An allegation of approximately two hundred missing comparison files does not by itself establish arbitrary deprivation of Missouri's mandated proportionality-review opportunity. Introducing that distinct statewide comparative-review dispute after completed discovery and preparation of the magistrate report, when the district court was ready to rule, supplies an apparent discretionary justification for declining expansion; elapsed years alone would not.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part V, the same five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The state opinion reports mandated review and actual comparison of crime and defendant. Neither every missing file's irrelevance nor the sufficiency of every stated review is found. No bad faith, specific prejudice, early discoverability or actual district rationale is attributed. A meritorious newly discovered deprivation could warrant amendment despite the stage, but that predicate is not established; no categorical deadline is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pulley rejects an invariable federal comparative-proportionality right without displacing Hicks's protection of state-created opportunities. Foman preserves liberal amendment while allowing a justification apparent in the record or declared by the court; Habeas Rule 11 permits civil rules only when appropriate and consistent with habeas rules.

Pre-AEDPA Rule 9(b) distinguishes repeated grounds after merits determination from new grounds omitted through abuse. Section 2244(b)'s refusal permission follows federal denial after a material factual evidentiary hearing or legal merits hearing; the exception requires an unadjudicated ground and absence of deliberate withholding or other abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground; permission is not mandatory dismissal. No delay-prejudice, exhaustion or undisclosed-default ground is added. Schlup's requirement of reliable new evidence, assessed with the whole probative record, showing that more likely than not no reasonable properly instructed juror would convict, and Sawyer's requirement of clear and convincing proof that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find death eligibility, are not satisfied. Discretionary mitigation alone is insufficient under Sawyer. Neither gateway establishes an underlying violation or automatic relief, and the available second-degree claim requires neither gateway. No new §2251 construction is adopted.

### Successive habeas petitions and original jurisdiction

#### Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-8836; associated application A-890.
**Decided:** June 28, 1996.

##### Whether §2244(b)(3)(E) bars review of circuit authorization while preserving original habeas

**Holding:** Section 2244(b)(3)(E) bars ordinary certiorari review of a court of appeals' grant or denial of authorization to file a second or successive district-court habeas application; the present certiorari request must be dismissed for lack of jurisdiction. The provision does not repeal the distinct original habeas authority retained by §2241, which permits independent consideration of this petition without affirming or reviewing the circuit's authorization judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part I-A, unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 2241 still names the Court and its Justices; §2244(b)(3)(A) concerns advance authorization for district filing, and the amended transfer rule does not name this Court. Direct filing is procedurally original but constitutionally appellate; §1651 aids existing jurisdiction rather than creates it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Unpresented certification, interlocutory and supervisory channels are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Ordinary certiorari dismissed for want of jurisdiction, 9–0, without affirming the authorization judgment.

##### When the new authorization-review bar governs

**Holding:** The authorization and review proceedings begun after AEDPA's April 24, 1996 enactment are governed by its specified authorization-review bar. That conclusion does not decide the temporal reach of every Title I provision or apply a new substantive restriction backward merely because a petition is pending.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine support the channel result; O’Connor’s Part I-B has Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, six. Thomas, Scalia and Kennedy use a distinct relevant-activity approach.

**Material application:** Landgraf’s provision-specific temporal sequence governs the majority reasoning; a procedural/jurisdictional label alone does not answer retroactivity. The May authorization and subsequent review proceedings began after April 24.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No holding determines every Title I provision’s temporal reach or Georgia’s chapter 154 qualification. Chapter 154’s express pending-case direction does not automatically govern other amendments.

##### What successive restrictions and discretionary conditions govern an original petition

**Holding:** The district-court authorization mechanism does not govern filing this original application. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform the Court's consideration of repeated applications, but whether those substantive provisions bind this Court directly is reserved. Retained jurisdiction does not itself entitle an applicant to an original writ.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** For statutory second/successive §2254 applications, (b)(1) dismisses previously presented claims. New claims under (b)(2) require either a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court, or both previously undiscoverable facts despite due diligence and facts which, if proved with the whole evidence, clearly and convincingly establish that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would find guilt. A request to announce a new rule is not the first exception. Before district filing, (b)(3) requires circuit permission by a three-judge panel upon prima facie showing within thirty days; the determination permits no appeal, rehearing or certiorari. Under (b)(4), district dismissal is required unless the applicant actually establishes the conditions, despite authorization.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether (b)(1)–(2) bind this Court directly remains reserved; they inform the Court’s consideration. Rule 20 separately requires reasons for bypassing district court, exhaustion or satisfaction of §2254(b), exceptional circumstances and unavailability of adequate relief in another form or court. Section 2254(b)(1) permits exhaustion, absence of available state corrective process or circumstances rendering it ineffective; (b)(2) permits, but does not require, denial on merits despite nonexhaustion; counsel’s express waiver is necessary under (b)(3). No exhaustion, process adequacy, waiver or affirmative diligence-failure fact is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Original relief denied, 9–0, on the exceptional-original-showing ground; no return, transfer or hearing is ordered.

##### Whether the channel and successive scheme suspend meaningful review or dictate an unlawful outcome here

**Holding:** The specified review restriction and consideration of the operative successive scheme do not suspend constitutionally sufficient habeas review or prescribe a forbidden judicial outcome as applied to these claims. The original petition receives independent examination and fails on an adequate traditional original-writ ground, without deciding that every future statutory exclusion or every nominal surviving remedy is constitutional.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** The retained original route supplies actual independent claim examination and a sufficient traditional denial ground. Klein distinguishes changed law from a command to falsify facts or decide an unchanged rule in a prescribed manner.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No approval of every nominal forum, future exclusion of otherwise sufficient innocence claims, all AEDPA, §§2254(d)–(e), or unpresented supervisory restriction.

##### Whether the submitted medical evidence establishes the Schlup gateway

**Holding:** This submission does not establish Schlup's gateway: reliable new evidence, assessed with all probative evidence, making it more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict. That conclusion does not adjudicate the separately pleaded constitutional innocence theory or make the gateway and AEDPA's successive conditions interchangeable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part IV-A, all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela withholds this additional application.

### Immigration review channels and statutory habeas

#### Kolster v. INS

**Docket or dockets:** First Circuit No. 96-1194; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1997-06-23.

##### I. The saved proceeding remains subject to AEDPA's independent restriction

**Holding:** Corrected IIRIRA §§306(c)(1) and 309(c)(1) preserve the former review scheme for this proceeding begun before April 1, 1997, subject to their particular exceptions; §242(g) reaches old proceedings but does not itself withdraw review of this legal objection to a completed adjudication. **Holding:** AEDPA §440(a) withdraws ordinary appellate review of Kolster's final deportation order based on the admitted drug and aggravated-felony grounds, independently supported by the conviction documents, notwithstanding his February 28, 1996 petition. **Holding:** Section 440(a)'s replacement of a particular INA review channel does not clearly repeal the independent jurisdiction enacted in 28 U.S.C. §2241. **Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join this construction.

**Current-force modification:** Calcano-Martinez v. INS, June 25, 2001, extends the retained section 2241 channel to the permanent criminal-removal review scheme. Kolster's earlier-scheme and temporal holdings are unchanged.

The Court does not determine Kolster's habeas predicates, make every statutory error constitutional, reopen discretionary weighing or factual review generally, or define all constitutional minima. **Treatment of earlier authority:** Felker's distinction between a withdrawn review channel and retained statutory habeas is extended to §2241 in this immigration setting. McCardle and Yerger support channel-specific repeal analysis; Heikkila distinguishes ordinary immigration review from habeas. The legal-eligibility objection resolves the reservation left in Hincapie-Nieto and Salazar-Haro at this conditional scope; Flores's custody safeguards do not themselves answer review of deportation authority.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Calcano-Martinez v. INS, June 25, 2001 — survival and effective scope of independent habeas under the permanent review provisions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Souter's opinion is joined by all nine Justices.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The ordinary petition remains dismissed, unanimously. The sentencing recommendation does not bind the immigration decisionmaker. Any collateral challenge must establish its own jurisdictional and procedural predicates before legal eligibility or requested relief is decided.

**Holding:** To pass the innocence gateway for an otherwise barred constitutional claim, reliable new evidence, assessed with the entire probative record, must show that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. The lower court must conduct that inquiry and separately apply former §2254(d)(8); its legally inconsistent analysis cannot sustain the present new-trial instruction, but the Court does not conclusively reject the gateway on this submission.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by five votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The whole record includes probative impeachment, recantations and otherwise inadmissible evidence, with reliability assessed rather than categorically excluded. A reasonable alternative inference alone does not defeat a qualifying state credibility finding; former §2254(d)(8) requires the pertinent produced state record as a whole. No gateway success or failure, unsupported state finding, freestanding innocence entitlement or automatic hearing is found.

The State must plead abuse clearly and particularly, identifying prior history and new claims, before the prisoner's responsive burden arises. Former §2244(b)'s refusal permission concerns a prisoner held under a state judgment after a federal court, justice or judge denied release or another habeas remedy following either an evidentiary merits hearing on a material factual issue or a merits hearing on law. Its exception requires both an unadjudicated factual or other ground and satisfaction that the ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise abusive. When both conditions are met and no other barrier applies, McCleskey requires consideration. New evidence does not necessarily create a new ground. Cause and prejudice, the exceptional innocence route, and independent claim conditions remain distinct. No discretionary refusal may rest on a mistaken gateway standard; gateway passage establishes neither a violation nor automatic relief.

Former §2254(d) protects a qualifying factual determination made by a competent state court after a merits hearing involving the applicant and the State or its officer or agent, evidenced by adequate written indicia. Paragraph (8) requires consideration of whether the pertinent produced state record as a whole fairly supports the finding.

##### The mandate preserves the bounded acquisition question without deciding it

**Holding:** After the required gateway analysis, the lower courts must decide the Brady claim if the gateway or an independent lawful route permits merits review. Actual case-team attribution and the proposed duty to obtain the State's identified corrections file on its own central witness remain distinct available theories; the mandate neither makes proof of actual team possession the exclusive route nor adopts the proposed acquisition duty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls the remand by five votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not adopt the proposed acquisition duty or universal government imputation. If lawful review and a complete conviction-level Brady violation are established, conditional new-trial relief remains available. Any properly available Teague question retains its finality-era inquiry and substantive-punishment and watershed-procedure exceptions; no new-rule classification, unasserted defense or presupposed waiver is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** If actual attribution is absent or insufficient and the acquisition theory remains necessary and properly presented through a lawful merits route, its constitutional merits and any actually applicable collateral conditions must be considered. The remand does not require extra fact development solely to avoid an otherwise properly decidable legal question.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The present new-trial writ instruction is set aside unanimously. The complete renewed gateway/deference and bounded Brady remand is adopted 5–4 by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Independently available unaddressed claims are preserved unanimously. No acquittal, release, fixed retrial period, hearing, new sentence or execution protection is ordered. The conditional Brady materiality holding appears under Criminal Procedure.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Lindh v. Murphy (June 23, 1997):** Its ordinary chapter 153 temporal construction is extended from the pending noncapital application there to this pending capital application outside established chapter 154 qualification. Separately directed provisions remain separate.
- **Schlup v. Delo (January 23, 1995):** Its reliable-new-evidence, whole-record probability standard is applied without changing the burden, categorical evidence rules or independent habeas conditions. The present gateway is remanded, not held satisfied.
- **Marshall v. Lonberger, 459 U.S. 422 (1983):** Its protection of state historical and credibility findings is applied with former §2254(d)(8)'s whole-record exception, not converted into conclusive deference or unrestricted appellate factfinding.
- **McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467 (1991):** Its abuse pleading and responsive-burden structure survives; the innocence gateway does not eliminate independent claim conditions.
- **Herrera v. Collins, 506 U.S. 390 (1993):** Its limited insufficiency holding is preserved; no general freestanding innocence entitlement or threshold is settled.
- **Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996):** Its authorization-specific temporal ruling and submission-specific gateway assessment are distinguished from universal AEDPA retroactivity or a categorical innocence rule.

### Recall of completed habeas merits mandates

#### Calderon v. Thompson

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-215.

**Decided:** 1998-04-29.

##### The recall judgment is reviewable through the ordinary channel

**Holding:** Felker’s §2244(b)(3)(E) restriction on review of permission to file a successive district-court application does not bar ordinary review of this separate mandate-recall judgment confined to the first application’s claims and evidence. Under Lindh and Carriger, the identified amended §2254 merits and replacement factual-presumption provisions do not govern this capital application filed in January 1990, outside established chapter 154 qualification; other statutory provisions retain their own temporal and procedural predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of O'Connor’s opinion is joined by all eight other Justices and controls unanimously.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court-initiated label cannot disguise a successive application. Here the recall is confined to the first application’s evidence and excludes Leitch’s later parole account. No blanket AEDPA exemption or hearing-provision holding follows.

##### Completed habeas merits cannot be reopened on ordinary reconsideration alone

**Holding:** When recall reopens the completed merits of a state prisoner’s habeas proceeding, rather than correcting a genuinely different defect in judicial process, exceptional recall requires the applicable miscarriage-of-justice showing: Schlup’s crime-innocence gateway for an attack on a conviction, or Sawyer’s distinct gateway for an eligibility-only attack. A clerical correction, fraud, a mandate that does not express the court’s judgment or another genuinely different process defect remains distinct; capital punishment or disagreement with the prior merits result alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II–III are joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, including the author. These five directly adopt this rule, its application and the remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule extends the distinct innocence gateways to this completed-merits recall category. It does not abolish exceptional recall authority or treat every court-initiated recall as a successive district-court application.

##### The augmented first-application record meets neither distinct gateway

**Holding:** A conviction attack under Schlup requires reliable new evidence assessed with the whole probative record showing that, more likely than not, no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict beyond a reasonable doubt. An eligibility-only attack under Sawyer requires clear and convincing evidence that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find the necessary rape special circumstance and other state-law eligibility predicates; Thompson’s first-application record satisfies neither showing.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same five-Justice Parts II–III coalition directly adopts both component-specific applications. No fractured-decision synthesis is involved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Added impeachment is assessed with the record, not categorically excluded. No separate ineffective-assistance merits ruling, state finding on every added item, universal judicial awareness or dishonest purpose is found. Evidence may be new relative to trial without postdating the mandate; no AEDPA diligence rule is imported into Schlup.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed 5–4, Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas supporting, with instructions to reinstate the June 11, 1997 denial mandate. Only recall-dependent relief and directions are removed. No new execution date, revival of an expired date, adjudication of another application or displacement of independent protection follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Felker v. Turpin:** Applied channel by channel. The successive-application authorization-review bar does not decide this distinct recall judgment.
- **Lindh v. Murphy; Carriger v. Stewart:** Applied to the identified pre-AEDPA capital-application provisions; no blanket exemption or automatic gateway remand follows.
- **Schlup v. Delo:** Extended to conviction-innocence review for this category of completed-merits recall, retaining reliable new evidence, the whole record and its probability standard.
- **Sawyer v. Whitley:** Extended separately to an eligibility-only recall claim, retaining clear and convincing proof concerning state-law death eligibility rather than mitigation or sentence selection.
- **Hazel-Atlas Glass Co. v. Hartford-Empire Co.:** Its exceptional process-protection power remains, with the actual fraud predicate distinguished.
- **United States v. Ohio Power Co.:** Its exceptional procedural reopening remains; it supplies no general entitlement to revisit refused rehearing.
- **Carlisle v. United States:** Applied to rule-consistent inherent authority, without inventing a statute abolishing every recall.
- **Lonchar v. Thomas:** Required first-application process remains protected; discretionary en banc reconsideration is not made mandatory.
- **Marshall v. Lonberger:** State historical and credibility findings retain former §2254(d)’s protection only with its prerequisites and exceptions.
- **Strickland v. Washington:** Performance and reasonable-probability prejudice remain distinct from gateway innocence; no independent ineffective-assistance merits holding issues.

### Collateral review of a misdescribed guilty plea

#### Bousley v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-8516.
**Decided:** 1998-05-18.

##### 1. Correct knowledge of the offense is necessary to an intelligent plea

**Holding:** A guilty plea requires real notice of the charge's essential nature. A materially mistaken description of a statutory element can render the plea unintelligent; Bailey's explanation of what the firearm statute prohibits is substantive law, not a new constitutional procedure barred by Teague.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Ginsburg’s opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct supporters.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule concerns the plea’s own informed validity, not every antecedent claim surrendered by a valid plea or a later disappointed expectation. Competence alone does not establish informed assent.

##### 2. Direct-review default requires cause and prejudice or factual innocence

**Holding:** Failure to challenge plea intelligence on direct review defaults this claim. Neither anticipated defeat nor adverse precedent establishes cause where the legal argument was available; absent cause, the claim may proceed only through a sufficient factual-innocence showing, not a showing of legal error alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II–III are joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct supporters.

**Material application:** Schlup nevertheless permits review where, considering the whole probative record under the correct offense, it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The district court has not determined factual innocence, and the Government does not assert a distinct waiver from failure to raise innocence below. The gateway precedes adjudication of constitutional invalidity and does not itself establish entitlement to relief.

##### 3. The evidence and actual plea bargain define the innocence inquiry

**Holding:** The Government may offer admissible evidence outside the plea record to rebut factual innocence. Where it actually forgave an identified more-serious charge in the plea bargain, the innocence showing must also cover that charge; hypothetical charges, unproved bargains and alternative statutory verbs create no added burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** The complete rule has the five direct supporters of Parts III and IV.A: O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela additionally joins Part III’s beyond-the-plea-record inquiry and IV.B’s use-only application and conditional remedy, but not IV.A’s affirmative burden concerning an actually forgone more-serious charge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This indictment charged use alone; no actually relinquished carrying or other identified more-serious charge is established. Carrying cannot be presumed to preserve this conviction. A successful gateway requires adjudication of plea validity, not automatic acquittal or vacatur.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Seven Justices reverse the Eighth Circuit; the six joining Parts II–III and IV.B require an opportunity to establish factual innocence followed, if successful, by adjudication of the plea-intelligence claim. Stevens would vacate the firearm conviction immediately. The controlling mandate does not vacate that conviction now, affect the drug conviction, or require an oral hearing, release or dismissal.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Bailey:** Active employment remains necessary for the charged use offense; possession alone is insufficient.
- **Henderson and Smith:** Applied to real notice of the charge's essential nature.
- **Rivers:** Supplies the substantive effect of an authoritative statutory construction.
- **Teague:** Its new-procedure limitation does not bar this substantive statutory claim.
- **Broce and Brady:** Distinguished from an attack on the plea's informed character; a disappointed expectation and misinformation about an element remain different.
- **Godinez:** Competence does not itself establish informed assent.
- **Reed and Engle:** An available argument's expected failure does not establish cause.
- **Schlup:** Extended to this defaulted plea claim with its demanding factual test intact.
- **Carriger:** Passing the gateway remains distinct from proving constitutional error and entitlement to relief.
- **Hyde:** Its presentence withdrawal setting does not decide this collateral claim.

### Previously premature competency claims and successive habeas review

#### Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-300.
**Decided:** 1998-05-18.

##### I. The circuit's threshold classification is reviewable

**Holding:** Section 2244(b)(3)(E) bars ordinary review of a circuit decision granting or denying authorization to file a second or successive application, but it does not bar review of this distinct determination that authorization is unnecessary. The Court may decide whether the circuit correctly placed this proceeding outside that mechanism; the circuit's label does not itself establish the answer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is adopted by all nine Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The circuit’s label is not conclusive; an actual authorization determination remains subject to Felker’s review bar.

**Holding:** A Ford claim actually presented in the initial merits application, dismissed as premature and expressly preserved for later consideration, is not a second or successive application when renewed after the impediment of prematurity has ended. Competency and Arizona’s procedures remain undecided; no live hearing, writ or stay is guaranteed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmed, 7–2, for the particular claim’s return to the District Court under otherwise applicable habeas procedures. All nine permit review of the threshold classification. No competency finding, stay, writ, hearing requirement or execution direction follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Felker v. Turpin: applied to actual authorization decisions; its review bar and distinct original-habeas route remain unchanged.
- Triestman v. United States and In re Dorsainvil: distinguished because they concerned actual authorization refusals; this case creates no exception permitting ordinary review of those refusals.
- Calderon v. Thompson: its action-specific channel distinction is applied; its completed-merits recall rule is distinguished from a claim dismissed only as premature.
- Lonchar v. Thomas: its requirement of legally supported dismissal predicates informs the classification; no equitable power to disregard enacted successive limits is recognized.
- Ford v. Wainwright: supplies the incompetency-to-be-executed claim without deciding competency or procedural sufficiency here.

### Interstate extradition and asylum-state habeas

#### New Mexico ex rel. Ortiz v. Reed

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1217.

**Decided:** 1998-06-08.

##### Anticipated mistreatment does not negate fugitive status

**Holding:** After a governor grants extradition, asylum-state habeas review is confined to whether the documents are facially adequate, the person is charged in the demanding State, the person is the one requested, and the person is a fugitive. **Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling does not approve threatened abuse, decide the truth or merits of Reed's federal claims, guarantee a particular remedy on facts not before the Court, or eliminate review of a genuine document, charge, identity or fugitive-status defect. It decides no broader question about statutory surrender of persons who are not fugitives.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Summary reversal and remand require compliance with the federal extradition obligation under its actual prerequisites. No individual transfer deadline or decision on the underlying federal claims is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Michigan v. Doran, 439 U.S. 282:** Applied to confine asylum-state habeas review to documents, charge, identity and fugitive status.
- **Sweeney v. Woodall, 344 U.S. 86:** Applied to reject asylum-state adjudication of threatened mistreatment in the demanding State; appropriate separate challenges remain distinct.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-8986.

**Decided:** June 15, 1998.

##### A judicial certificate-denial proceeding is reviewable under §1254(1)

- **Felker v. Turpin:** Its actual authorization-review bar and separately retained original-habeas jurisdiction remain intact, including their independent conditions.

**Holding:** The certificate denial is vacated and the matter returned for reconsideration in light of the Government’s changed constitutional characterization and governing law. ### Commuted capital punishment, former habeas law and bounded relief

#### Lucas v. Johnson

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 96-10389.

**Decided:** 1998-09-28.

##### Life custody preserves conviction claims, but the death-only requests are moot

**Holding:** The continuing conviction and imprisonment keep the existing conviction-level claims live, while commutation removes the requested execution and death-selection relief in claims 1, 2, the death-only branch of 4 and claim 16 without a demonstrated surviving consequence. ##### The death-only federal merits rulings receive limited equitable vacatur

**Holding:** Under §2106, the corresponding death-only federal merits adjudications are vacated because executive commutation frustrated their review while review was being pursued, without established voluntary settlement or abandonment. The equitable disposition does not vacate the conviction, revive death eligibility or erase the live claim rulings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor). O'Connor's Part II is joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela disagrees with vacatur.

The Court does not decide those rulings' substantive correctness or give clemency the effect of a constitutional adjudication. This is direct disposition of the judgment before the Court, not a collateral reopening power.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bancorp applies an actual equitable assessment under §2106; neither mootness nor executive clemency creates an automatic vacatur rule.

##### Former factual presumptions do not displace independent constitutional review

**Holding:** The identified amended §2254(d) and replacement §2254(e)(1) rules do not govern this pending first federal application outside established chapter 154 qualification: former §2254(d) presumes qualifying state factual determinations correct, placing on the applicant the burden to establish their error by convincing evidence unless a statutory exception is established or otherwise appears. Constitutional sufficiency, waiver, fairness and Brady conclusions remain independently reviewed, and claims 3–7 may be denied on their sufficient independent grounds with gateway availability assumed rather than decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor). O'Connor's Part I, joined by all nine; the claim 15 application also has the five votes in Parts VII–VIII.

**Material application:** Lindh v. Murphy and Carriger v. Stewart supply the applicable chapter 153 construction without deciding every AEDPA amendment. Paragraph (8) removes that presumption when the pertinent produced state record as a whole does not fairly support the finding. The applicant's otherwise-applicable burden is convincing evidence of factual error under former §2254(d), not the replacement §2254(e)(1) formulation. West preserves independent legal assessment rather than general deference to a reasonable state constitutional conclusion.

Thompson retains its adequate-independent-ground and exception requirements if default becomes decisive. Schlup v. Delo and Carriger govern any then-necessary whole-record probability inquiry; the verdict, corroborative character or trial inadmissibility cannot categorically dispose of reliable evidence. No advisory gateway finding is necessary now. The claim 15 remand has no established default bar in this record.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carriger applies its pending-capital and distinct state-finding rules; it orders no automatic hearing here.
- Wright preserves independent constitutional assessment and Jackson sufficiency.
- Trest preserves nonjurisdictional default without requiring an invented defense.

- Bousley v. United States preserves factual innocence as distinct from legal insufficiency; its plea-bargain conditions are not imported.

##### Claim 10 requests a rule not dictated at finality

**Holding:** At the reported 1990 finality, rules governing continuing courtroom restraints did not dictate categorical exclusion of a brief recorded custodial appearance showing handcuffs. Teague therefore bars that requested retroactive rule, which falls within neither then-existing exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor). O'Connor's Part VI, joined by all nine.

**Material application:** Teague v. Lane requires the actual finality-era dictated-rule comparison, not a label of novelty or the bare existence of disagreement. A brief recorded image accompanying a confession differs from keeping the accused visibly restrained throughout trial. The requested categorical recording rule neither places conduct or a class of persons beyond the State's substantive punishment authority nor supplies a watershed procedure essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. Lambrix preserves those requirements and rejects treating every application of an older principle as new. The Court does not approve every restraint image or reject a demonstrated whole-trial unfairness claim on a materially different record.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague applies the dictated-rule test and both contemporary exceptions to this precise requested rule.
- Lambrix preserves genuine finality-era comparison without an automatic novelty rule for extensions.

##### Claim 17 remains within Powell's limited exclusion bar

**Holding:** The presented request to exclude confessions as fruits of an arrest allegedly lacking probable cause is barred under Powell because the supplied proceedings afforded a full and fair state opportunity and identify no concrete denial of that opportunity. The Court neither decides the arrest lawful nor extends this Fourth Amendment exclusion restriction to independent Fifth, Sixth Amendment or Brady claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor). O'Connor's Part VI, joined by all nine.

**Material application:** Stone v. Powell limits this exclusion-based habeas remedy after a full and fair opportunity to litigate the Fourth Amendment theory. The present claim identifies no defect in that opportunity; the Court does not infer adequate process merely from unavailable transcripts. A demonstrated opportunity defect would require a different Powell inquiry. No new coercion claim is created, and no approval of the arrest's merits follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Powell applies only its full-and-fair-opportunity exclusion rule.

##### The Brady remand orders correct adjudication, not another hearing or a writ

**Holding:** Claim 15 returns for application of its actual constitutional elements and former factual-presumption rule on the existing claim; no new hearing, discovery, production order, conditional writ, retrial or release is ordered. Sain's deliberate-bypass qualification for inadequately developed material facts on a properly presented claim; ordinary counsel negligence alone does not forfeit a necessary hearing under that rule. The lower court must identify the actual material gap, existing state and federal development and applicable independent barriers before ordering more proceedings.

### Capital habeas corpus and effective mitigation

#### Weeks v. Angelone, 528 U.S. 225 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-5746.
**Decided:** January 19, 2000.

##### Whether the jury-response claim was fairly presented and procedurally defaulted

**Holding:** A federal claim is fairly presented when the state submission identifies the challenged conduct and the same federal protection later invoked; a merits rejection without an actual independent state procedural ground supplies no such bar.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Assignment 44 identified the later inquiry, clarification request and refusal and incorporated assignment 41's federal mitigation ground. The state court rejected that claim on its merits, without an independent procedural ground; the later untimely collateral filing does not undo completed direct presentation. A renewed request and objection preserved the response issue despite initial use of the charge. Section 2254(b)(2) permits, but does not require, merits denial despite nonexhaustion and supplies no authority for a grant. Section 2254(b)(1) separately preserves relief if no state corrective process is available or circumstances make it ineffective; section 2254(b)(3) requires counsel's express waiver rather than deemed waiver or estoppel. No exception or waiver is needed here.

##### Whether constitutional error alone permits relief under amended section 2254(d)(1)

**Holding:** Because the federal application was filed after April 24, 1996 and the state court adjudicated this claim on the merits, a writ requires satisfaction of §2254(d)(1): a state decision contrary to, or involving an unreasonable application of, clearly established federal law determined by this Court.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part I, unanimous.

**Holding:** The requirement that the capital sentencing mechanism permit effective use of relevant mitigation applies to a response reasonably likely to leave that function unavailable; it creates no new entitlement to particular words or mandatory rephrasing of an adequate instruction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The defined claim applies the preexisting effective-mitigation function, not an entitlement to specified words or compulsory rephrasing of an adequate instruction. **Holding:** When a capital jury asks whether an aggravator makes death mandatory, the response, read with the complete charge and trial, must resolve a reasonable likelihood that the jury cannot give supported mitigation effect in choosing life. No particular wording is required; a reference to an existing instruction can suffice when it unambiguously resolves the actual difficulty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The complete charge and trial include the accurate life alternative and broad mitigation direction, but returning the jury to the same conditional paragraph failed to resolve its expressed uncertainty about life after an aggravator. Advocacy, further deliberation, the mitigation recital and polling do not conclusively remove that likelihood. The holding preserves Buchanan's original-charge ruling, leaves mitigation weight to the jury, requires no particular wording, and establishes neither structural error nor present causal effect.

**Authority of the judgment:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer vacate the denial of the sentencing-response claim and remand, 5–4. Constitutional error and rejection of the actual presentation, exhaustion, default and Teague defenses bind the return. Breyer's Part IV has only Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg as additional joins; Stone-Zsela agrees with the judgment while finding statutory eligibility and injurious effect already established. **Limits and questions not reached:** No majority chooses the causal-effect standard, burden or treatment of uncertainty for this jury-response category. Brecht's preserved-Doyle category, O'Neal and Roy's defined personal-intent category, and Calderon v. Coleman's commutation-misinformation category retain their own force; none conclusively classifies this error. Their distinct rules cannot be combined into a general standard.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The lower court must independently decide section 2254(d)(1) eligibility and may deny relief if a necessary condition fails. If statutory relief is available, it must resolve the open effect-standard question, including burden and uncertainty, on a legal basis consistent with existing holdings and assess the actual sentence on the complete record. This reservation neither rejects Chapman nor allows arbitrary selection. Only an available harmful violation warrants conditional penalty relief allowing a reasonable prospective opportunity for adequate sentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence. No present writ, sentence vacatur, deadline, hearing, stay or release is ordered; guilt and unrelated claims remain undisturbed.

### Habeas factual development and claim-specific diligence

#### Williams v. Taylor (Michael Wayne Williams), 529 U.S. 420 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-6615.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. The hearing restriction requires applicant-attributed failure

**Holding:** Section 2254(e)(2) is triggered by deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel in developing the claim's state-court factual basis; an incomplete record alone is insufficient. When triggered, the applicant must show either a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court on collateral review or a factual predicate previously undiscoverable through due diligence, and must also show by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion.

**Material application:** Reasonable effort is measured against known leads and available procedures. A generic development request is neither categorically sufficient nor categorically deficient; the public existence of a document is not an informative lead by itself. This post-enactment application falls within section 2254(e)(2).

##### II. The known psychiatric report triggers the statutory bar

**Holding:** Counsel's failure to pursue an identified psychiatric report through reasonably available means, after receiving a transcript repeatedly referring to it, constitutes deficient diligence here. Williams's conceded inability to satisfy section 2254(e)(2)(B) therefore forbids the requested hearing on that report claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined by all seven other Associate Justices and controls with eight votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Failure to pursue the identified report is not a new Brady merits determination or a finding of bad faith, and it is not attributed to the two relationship claims. The conceded failure to satisfy the cumulative innocence condition makes other exception questions unnecessary.

##### III. Unknown relationships do not establish a neglected available lead

**Holding:** The applicant did not fail to develop the juror-relationship and prosecutor-nondisclosure claims where counsel lacked a lead to the concealed relationships, reasonably sought investigative assistance, and the State opposed and the court denied that assistance. The public existence of a divorce record does not by itself establish deficient diligence, so section 2254(e)(2) does not bar factual development of these claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Part III of Justice Ginsburg's opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory hearing eligibility does not establish bias, dishonesty, constitutional misconduct or entitlement to a writ. Independent lawful grounds may resolve either claim without immaterial factfinding; remaining hearing and merits predicates still apply.

##### IV. Cause is established only for the specified concealed-fact nonpresentation

**Holding:** The concealed relationships, lack of an informative lead, reasonable unsuccessful investigative efforts and later unavailability of state relief establish cause insofar as default consists of failing to present these two relationship-based claims in the Virginia proceeding. That conclusion does not excuse a different default or establish prejudice; the appropriate prejudice standard and its application remain for the lower courts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Justice Ginsburg's opinion has the support of all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees with rejection of the lower no-cause ground without joining this reasoning.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Exhaustion, actual independent and adequate state default, section 2254(d)'s state-merits predicate, section 2254(e)(1)'s factual presumption and clear-and-convincing rebuttal burden, and ordinary hearing requirements remain distinct. Section 2254(b)(2) permits denial of an unexhausted claim on the merits but does not require it; unavailable or ineffective state process and express State-counsel waiver have their own conditions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The report-hearing denial is affirmed, 8–1. The eight-Associate Court sets aside the relationship claims' diligence barriers; all nine support removing those barriers and the specified no-cause ground, although Stone-Zsela seeks further diligence inquiry and joins no cause rationale. Remaining default, prejudice and hearing issues return for adjudication. The separate plea-agreement disposition and identified unreviewed rulings stand; no writ or universal hearing command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Keeney and Townsend retain their deliberate-bypass former-law hearing rule. The later statute changes the consequence of deficient diligence in applications it governs; the Court does not rewrite Keeney's prior holding.
- Lindh is applied for provision-specific temporal reach; this post-enactment application is governed by section 2254(e)(2).
- Coleman remains controlling for an actual adequate and independent state claim default; hearing eligibility does not eliminate it.
- Vasquez retains the distinction between supplementing an exhausted claim and fundamentally altering it into a new claim.
- Weeks preserves the actual state-merits predicate and independent statutory conditions for relief; no general construction of section 2254(d)(1) beyond that holding is supplied here.
- Brady supplies the alleged disclosure duty, but neither that allegation nor the hearing ruling establishes suppression or materiality on the report claim.

### Habeas legal review and sentencing assistance

#### Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-8384.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits

**Holding:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state court, the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses perform independent work: conflict with this Court's governing legal rule can satisfy the first without factual identity, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a count of judges; clearly established federal law consists of this Court's holdings at the relevant state merits decision, and a governing rule such as Strickland is not excluded merely because it is general.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for these common propositions. It does not resolve the competing exclusive general allocations between the clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither four-Justice exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses controls, and the Court adopts no Marks synthesis or universal extension/refusal-to-extend rule. The relevant state merits date is June 6, 1997. The legal inquiry acquires no clear-and-convincing burden from factual-review provisions; sections 2254(d)(2), (e)(1) and (e)(2) keep their independent conditions. No facial Article III or Suspension Clause question is decided.

##### II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia's unanimity rule

**Holding:** In an ordinary sentencing ineffective-assistance claim, Strickland requires a reasonable probability of a different lawful result, sufficient to undermine confidence, assessed on the full supported aggravating and mitigating record; Fretwell adds no separate unfairness requirement. Because Virginia requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death suffices to change the legally available outcome; a bare possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, with the same eight-Associate coalition, directly adopts these rules. The seven-Justice Part II applies them in finding the actual constitutional violation.

##### III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law

**Holding:** A state court unreasonably applies Strickland when its prejudice inquiry materially truncates the supported mitigation or weighs it in an objectively indefensible way; mere disagreement among reasonable jurists does not satisfy section 2254(d)(1). The actual treatment of Williams's documentary deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence, considered with the complete sentencing record, is objectively unreasonable and independently satisfies the statutory condition for review of his constitutional entitlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes. The four Justices favoring broader governing-rule review independently join this actual objective-unreasonableness conclusion; their broader construction is unnecessary to it.

**Material application:** The actual prejudice analysis materially understated the documentary childhood deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence. The full assessment includes severe aggravation, adverse juvenile history, later family improvement, custodial misconduct and possible parole. The holding relies on neither a new diagnosis nor presumed credit or admissibility for every proffer.

##### IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation

**Holding:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make a reasonable, informed decision that further investigation is unnecessary; a later assertion of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to investigate identified records. Williams's counsel performed deficiently by failing to obtain the available youth records and develop the supported mitigation, independently of tactical decisions about particular cumulative witnesses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's same seven-Justice coalition adopts the performance rule and actual deficiency finding. Scalia and Thomas assume deficiency without deciding it; their votes do not support a performance holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable investigation enables an informed presentation choice; it does not compel use of every record or cumulative witness. The Court resolves the preserved adequate-performance defense on the available investigation record, not on assumptions below, illness allegations or a freestanding expert-access claim.

##### V. The complete sentencing picture establishes actual prejudice and sentence-only relief

**Holding:** When the supported evidence competent mitigation investigation would have produced is considered with the actual aggravation and defense evidence, there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious Virginia juror would have declined death. Williams therefore establishes both Strickland requirements and is entitled to a conditional writ requiring constitutionally adequate resentencing within a reasonable period or a lawful noncapital sentence, while his convictions and independent ungranted claims remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's seven-Justice coalition adopts actual prejudice and the stated remedy, independently of any broader general construction of section 2254(d)(1).

**Operative remedy or transition:** The lower federal court must implement a conditional sentence writ, allowing Virginia a reasonable period that court fixes for constitutionally adequate resentencing or replacement of death with a lawful noncapital sentence. Convictions and independent ungranted claims remain. No life-without-parole sentence, new evidentiary hearing, acquittal, immediate release or execution/stay action is ordered.

##### VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground

**Independent alternative holding:** A state merits rejection that requires additional fundamental unfairness beyond ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice for this lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with Strickland and Fretwell's express limitation and is contrary to clearly established Supreme Court law. On this record the state court's nominal ordinary-probability discussion does not furnish an adequate independent ground, so this contrary-rule conclusion supplies a separate statutory basis for the same constitutional review and sentence relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for this alternative gateway ground. It does not establish either group's exclusive general allocation of the statutory clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This six-Justice alternative supplies statutory eligibility in addition to the seven-Justice unreasonable-application ground. It does not replace the separate seven-Justice findings of actual deficient performance and prejudice needed for the writ, or establish either competing exclusive general clause allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland is applied to professionally reasonable investigation and ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice on the entire supported sentencing record; neither deficiency alone nor additional mitigation automatically establishes prejudice.
- Fretwell is applied according to its already controlling limitation: the lost benefit of a rejected substantive rule is distinct from ordinary lawful-mitigation prejudice. No additional unfairness requirement is permitted in this usual claim.
- Lockett is applied to the legal relevance of individual mitigation; it does not require indiscriminate presentation of every item.
- Eddings is applied to lawful consideration of mitigating background; it does not assign that evidence automatic dispositive weight.
- Skipper is applied to the relevance of supported adjustment to confinement; it supplies no finding of lifelong confinement or future nonviolence.
- Lindh and Carriger retain the application-date limits of their respective pre-enactment holdings; this later federal filing falls outside those premises.
- Weeks is applied to the independence of statutory eligibility, actual constitutional error and remedy; its reserved comprehensive statutory construction is resolved only to the scope of the present majority propositions.
- Smith v. Robbins is distinguished at its missing appellate-assistance function; it does not presume prejudice from defective sentencing investigation.
- Flores-Ortega is distinguished at its causal loss of an appeal; contextual performance remains required, and the absence of a probable-appellate-success prerequisite there does not remove actual sentencing-prejudice review here.

### Defaulted assistance claims offered as cause

#### Edwards v. Carpenter, 529 U.S. 446 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-2060.
**Decided:** April 25, 2000.

##### A constitutional assistance claim retains its own procedural requirements when offered as cause

**Holding:** An ineffective-assistance claim offered as cause for another default is not exempt from an otherwise applicable independent, adequate and properly applied state procedural bar merely because state remedies have been exhausted. If that cause claim is itself defaulted, the prisoner must establish cause and actual prejudice excusing that default, or satisfy an applicable recognized miscarriage-of-justice gateway, before the claim may do that work.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Souter's opinion has seven votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stevens and Breyer support the remand without joining this rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State must establish the independent, adequate and properly applied bar. Federal cause requires an external impediment or qualifying constitutional ineffectiveness, not ordinary negligence or a presumed collateral-counsel right; actual prejudice means actual and substantial constitutional disadvantage. The offense-innocence gateway requires reliable new evidence considered with the whole probative record showing that more likely than not no reasonable properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. That gateway establishes neither the constitutional claim nor entitlement to a writ.

**Material application:** The District Court found Ohio Rule 26(B)'s good-cause practice inadequate, and the court of appeals did not decide that preserved ground. Exhaustion conceded by the State establishes neither adequacy nor merits. A successful inadequacy challenge removes this bar without federal cause for it; state good cause is a different inquiry.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The exhaustion-only rationale and dependent conditional culpability-hearing relief are reversed for the remaining adequacy, applicable default and excuse, merits and statutory inquiries. No final denial or automatic reinstatement of the District Court's different appellate remedy is ordered. Default remains nonjurisdictional, with no mandatory universal default-first sequence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carrier is applied to constitutionally ineffective assistance as an independent cause claim; its state-presentation requirement is not read as immunity from default after exhaustion.

- Coleman retains independent and adequate state grounds and their recognized exceptions; the decision applies them to a constitutional claim offered as cause.

- Gray preserves distinct claims and their actual procedural predicates rather than merging related allegations.

- O'Sullivan retains the distinction between completing state review and exhausting an unavailable route; its former-law source application does not classify this federal petition as pre-AEDPA.

- Trest and Lambrix retain the nonjurisdictional character of default and reject a mandatory default-first sequence for every habeas issue.

- Schlup supplies the demanding offense-innocence gateway; innocence, ordinary prejudice and legal insufficiency remain different inquiries.

- Strickland, Evitts and Jones retain constitutional performance and prejudice requirements for appellate assistance, without a duty to raise every possible issue.

- Smith v. Robbins and Flores-Ortega retain their distinct appellate-function and causally lost-appeal rules; neither automatically establishes this omitted-issue claim.

- The two Williams decisions preserve separate statutory eligibility, factual development, actual constitutional error and remedy; neither supplies a finding about Ohio adequacy or Carpenter's excuse.

### Habeas appellate certification and exhaustion returns

#### Slack v. McDaniel, 529 U.S. 473 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-6322.
**Decided:** April 26, 2000.

##### I. The judicial certificate denial is reviewable

**Holding:** A court of appeals' judicial denial of appellate certification is a case reviewable by certiorari under section 1254(1), whether the certificate was correctly or incorrectly required. That review does not itself issue a certificate or authorize the underlying merits appeal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls unanimously.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Judicial certificate review is distinct from review of a circuit authorization determination concerning a successive district application. The latter restriction is unchanged.

##### II. The post-enactment appeal requires the amended certificate

**Holding:** Amended section 2253(c) governs an applicant's appeal initiated after April 24, 1996, even when the identified pre-AEDPA law governs district adjudication of the earlier habeas application. Slack's April 29, 1998 notice therefore invokes the certificate-of-appealability requirement; the prior CPC label and the State's earlier contrary legal position do not dispense with that required authorization.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas with its author, Ginsburg: five direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with applying that regime but joins no Part II rationale. Stevens, Souter and Breyer would retain former CPC treatment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The appellate-date rule does not apply amended section 2254(d) to the pre-enactment district application or establish a universal claim-by-claim transition rule. A mislabeled CPC request or notice may be treated as a request under Rule 22; neither is an issued certificate. The State's legal concession cannot supply the required authorization.

##### III. A procedural-dismissal certificate requires both constitutional and procedural showings

**Holding:** When a district court dismisses on procedure without reaching the constitutional claim, a certificate of appealability requires reasonable debate both about whether the petition states a valid claim of denial of a constitutional right and about whether the procedural ruling was correct. Both are threshold showings; the court may address the more apparent component first, and any certificate must identify the qualifying issue or issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has nine direct joins: Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Stevens, Souter and Breyer adopt this interpretation conditionally while maintaining that former CPC governs this appeal; their Part III agreement does not enlarge the Part II coalition.

**Material application:** Reasonable debate requires more than a nonfrivolous assertion but not final proof of success. Where the district court has actually rejected constitutional merits, reasonable jurists must be able to debate that assessment or find it wrong. A plain correctly applied procedural bar defeats certification without an unnecessary constitutional inquiry; the constitutional component may instead be considered first when more apparent.

##### IV. This exhaustion return remains a first application, including its additional grounds

**Holding:** When an initial federal application is dismissed without prejudice solely to permit completion of state exhaustion before any federal merits adjudication, the renewed application is not second or successive merely because it follows that filing or adds grounds. Rule 9(b)'s new-ground abuse branch does not supply the missing second-or-successive predicate in this exhaustion-return setting; the contrary classification here is rejected, while actual independent default, exhaustion, timing and lawful case-management requirements remain.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Ginsburg's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, making seven direct votes for this rule, its application and reversal with remand. Scalia and Thomas dissent from this classification and disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 9(a)'s distinct delay provision permits dismissal where filing delay prejudiced the State's ability to respond, unless reasonable diligence could not have disclosed the grounds before that prejudice arose. No such prejudice or diligence finding is made here. **Holding:** Virginia's definition of a qualifying conviction governs whether the unentered Domino's verdict already counted under its parole statute. **Limits and questions not reached:** Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams) requires independent work by the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses. These common holdings do not adopt either competing exclusive allocation of the clauses or a universal refusal-to-extend rule.

O'Dell makes the confined effective-response protection available against Teague; it does not classify every proposed entitlement to predicted events as old. A claim within that protection receives its existing treatment. A materially different proposed rule requires its own finality and exception analysis if those questions become necessary. The Court adds no Teague exception and substitutes no retroactivity ruling for statutory eligibility. Allridge and O'Dell are present-law authorities, not constitutional holdings retroactively placed in the November 1994 benchmark. Williams supplies the present statutory construction without moving that benchmark.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-9136.
**Decided:** April 25, 2001.

**Holding:** Section 2255 does not reopen an expired counseled state conviction for ineffective-assistance or involuntary-plea claims solely because it enhances a current federal sentence when meaningful original review was available and no external obstacle is identified. **Holding:** Section 2255 remains available to challenge a predicate obtained through complete unconstitutional denial of required counsel without valid waiver, subject to applicable procedure and proof that it adversely affected the current sentence. ##### What Supreme Court action satisfies the successive-application retroactivity condition

**Holding:** Section 2244(b)(2)(A) is satisfied by an express Supreme Court retroactivity holding or by Supreme Court holdings whose necessary legal propositions jointly compel retroactivity; a lower court's persuasive extension, prediction or independent assessment does not suffice. The bar on review of an authorization order does not bar review of this later final judgment, and preliminary appellate permission does not excuse the district court's obligation to verify the statutory conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is review of a final judgment after filing, not review of advance authorization. The new-rule and previous-unavailability conditions remain distinct generally but are conceded here. No factual-innocence, original-habeas or preserved-premature-claim exception is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Felker preserves separate authorization and district verification and the authorization-order review bar. Stewart’s previously presented and expressly preserved premature claim is distinguished; Schiro and Caspari retain their distinct ordinary nonretroactivity rules.


##### Whether existing holdings establish retroactivity for the verdict-wide reasonable-doubt defect

**Holding:** Teague's preserved exception for watershed procedures and Sullivan's necessary holding that a verdict-wide unconstitutional reasonable-doubt charge leaves no valid jury verdict jointly establish retroactivity for that confined Cage rule. This claim therefore satisfies section 2244(b)(2)(A)'s Supreme-Court-retroactivity requirement without a separate prior opinion reciting an express declaration about Cage.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is confined to destruction of the reasonable-doubt standard throughout the jury’s verdict. Structural classification, importance, or inability to apply harmless-error review alone does not make every rule retroactive. The decision does not find Tyler’s charge unconstitutional or change the constitutional standard governing the earlier state adjudication.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Teague supplies the contemporary fundamental-accuracy and bedrock-procedure exception. Sullivan’s necessary absence-of-a-valid-verdict holding, together with Teague, resolves the separately reserved collateral-gate question for this Cage rule; Sullivan alone had not decided it. Cage identifies the confined underlying rule.


##### What follows after satisfaction of the Supreme-Court-retroactivity gate

**Holding:** The Fifth Circuit's sole ground for refusing this application is erroneous; the case returns for review of the complete instruction and the district court's separate section 2254(d) merits disposition. A new retroactivity declaration is unnecessary because existing holdings already supply the required status, and passing this gate neither establishes a constitutional violation nor itself satisfies section 2254(d).

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for review of the district court’s distinct merits disposition using the complete charge, actual state adjudication and Supreme Court law established at the legally relevant time. Newness and previous unavailability are not reopened. No immediate release, acquittal, retrial or evidentiary hearing is ordered. If the confined defect and all applicable merits requirements are ultimately established, conditional relief permits lawful retrial or termination of custody resting on the invalid conviction; terms remain for the lawful merits proceeding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No independent prerequisite is preserved merely through a generic formula; an additional matter requires an actually raised and legally available basis. Other rejected claims and unrelated instructional theories are not reopened. No new retroactivity declaration, advisory ruling on a later application, freestanding innocence claim or Suspension Clause determination is needed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Felker keeps actual statutory satisfaction distinct from authorization. Sullivan bars a harmless-error replacement for an established absent valid verdict; neither case makes passage through this gate equivalent to merits relief.
