## Federal Indian Law

### State taxation of reservation fee land

#### County of Yakima v. Confederated Tribes and Bands of the Yakima Indian Nation, 502 U.S. 251 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-408 and 90-577.
**Decided:** January 14, 1992.

##### Whether state ad valorem taxation may reach reservation land patented in fee under the General Allotment Act

**Holding:** The General Allotment Act's established fee-land rule and the Burke Act proviso authorize state ad valorem taxation of reservation land validly patented in fee under that Act, including qualifying land later held by the Nation. The tax follows the land, but patent source and independently applicable federal protection must be determined parcel by parcel.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; these eight Justices support the ad valorem holding. Blackmun dissents on this component.

**Material application:** The stipulated record did not establish every parcel's patent source. The Indian Reorganization Act did not automatically restore every existing fee parcel to tax-protected status, and the specific land-tax authority did not create general personal taxing jurisdiction over reservation Indians.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns qualifying General Allotment Act fee land and preserves independently applicable federal protections. It does not rest on alienability alone or authorize a governmental-impact inquiry under Brendale.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is affirmed and the case remanded with modified instructions for parcel-specific patent-source analysis and relief consistent with the two distinct tax holdings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Goudy is reaffirmed for taxation of qualifying General Allotment Act fee land. Blackfeet supplies the requirement of unmistakably clear congressional authorization. Mancari requires compatible enactments to operate together. Moe's rejection of general personal taxing jurisdiction is preserved. Brendale's fractured analysis of tribal regulation of nonmember land supplies no governmental-impact test for this statutory tax question.

##### Whether permission to tax the land also authorizes a sales excise imposed on an Indian owner

**Holding:** Congressional permission to tax qualifying fee land ad valorem does not unmistakably authorize a tax imposed on an Indian owner's sale of that land. Securing the transactional tax with a lien does not change its legal incidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion states this proposition for all nine Justices; all nine Justices support the sales-excise holding.

**Material application:** The annual property levy is triggered by ownership and burdens the realty; the sales excise is triggered by a transaction. A collection lien cannot enlarge Congress's permission by transforming an unauthorized transactional charge into a land tax.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sales excise remains prohibited, and remand relief must respect that prohibition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Blackfeet requires authorization clear at the level of the tax actually imposed. Capoeman's allotment-context discussion of taxation after fee patent does not supply blanket permission for every tax related to land.

### Kansas criminal jurisdiction

#### Negonsott v. Samuels, 507 U.S. 99 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-5397.

**Decided:** February 24, 1993.

##### Kansas-specific concurrent authority over state offenses

**Holding:** Under 18 U.S.C. §3243, Kansas may prosecute offenses committed by or against Indians on Indian reservations, including trust or restricted allotments, within Kansas to the same extent its courts may prosecute offenses elsewhere in the State under state law, even when the conduct also violates the Indian Major Crimes Act. Federal courts retain their jurisdiction over federally defined offenses; this concurrent authority over conduct does not authorize Kansas courts to adjudicate federal criminal charges.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's unanimous opinion, joined by the other eight Justices, controls the grant, the saving clause and the disposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- County of Yakima v. Confederated Tribes and Bands of the Yakima Indian Nation (January 14, 1992): its requirement of specific congressional authorization is applied at that level; its land-tax holding is neither converted into criminal jurisdiction nor enlarged.
- No earlier decision is overruled. The ordinary federal allocation under the Indian Major Crimes Act remains subject to this express Kansas-specific enactment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Authority rests on this express Kansas enactment, not general state police power. Other States, tribal adjudicatory authority, double jeopardy and successive prosecutions are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm denial of relief from Kansas’s aggravated-battery conviction; the state prosecution falls within §3243.

### State taxation of resident tribal members

#### Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Sac and Fox Nation, 508 U.S. 114 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-259.
**Decided:** May 17, 1993.

##### Member income protection depends on Indian-country residence and source

**Holding:** Absent express congressional authorization, a State may not tax a tribal member’s income earned in Indian country when the member resides there; tribal employment alone does not establish that protection. The residence inquiry includes §1151 reservations, dependent Indian communities and allotments whose Indian titles have not been extinguished, including rights-of-way, rather than only an undiminished formal reservation.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McClanahan v. Arizona State Tax Commission, 411 U.S. 164: applied with both residence and income-source predicates.
- Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe, 498 U.S. 505: applied against a reservation-label-only geography.
- County of Yakima v. Confederated Tribes, January 14, 1992: distinguished; limited land-tax authorization supplies no income-tax authority.

##### The challenged vehicle levies cannot be sustained merely as off-country use charges

**Holding:** For tribal members residing in Indian country, the challenged vehicle excise and registration levies fall within the prohibition on state property taxation absent congressional authorization; collecting the same forbidden charges from later purchasers does not save them. Their application to the established residents must be reconsidered on remand; the Court does not decide a genuinely apportioned off-country road-use charge or every tax connected to a vehicle sale.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Tribal employment or registration alone does not establish protection. Outside-Indian-country self-government claims, genuinely apportioned off-country road-use charges and nonmember taxation are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate in the reviewed respects and remand for actual member residence, income source and the vehicle levies. Qualifying allotments or other Indian country may resolve residence without deciding every reservation boundary; no blanket member exemption or refund amount.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Moe v. Confederated Salish and Kootenai Tribes, 425 U.S. 463: applied to member vehicle property taxation.
- Washington v. Confederated Tribes of the Colville Indian Reservation, 447 U.S. 134: applied to the substance of the vehicle levies; genuinely tailored off-country use charges remain distinct.

#### Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Chickasaw Nation

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-771.
**Decided:** June 14, 1995.

For the other questions in Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Chickasaw Nation, decided June 14, 1995, No. 94-771. see Federal Indian Law — State fuel-tax incidence in Indian country (Legal incidence of the reviewed motor-fuels tax).

##### Resident taxation and the surviving treaty promise

**Holding:** Oklahoma may apply its generally applicable income tax to members residing outside Indian country who earn wages from tribal work in Indian country; the Treaty of Dancing Rabbit Creek does not exempt that employee income. Members residing and earning tribal income in Indian country retain the ordinary protection under McClanahan and Sac and Fox.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg’s income portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices adopt the off-country-resident treaty construction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The treaty construction rests on the surviving territorial promise, not implied repeal at statehood. Article IV's retained council authority is conditioned on consistency with the federal Constitution, treaties and laws, and expressly permits congressional laws within constitutional Indian-affairs authority. Nonmember wages, nontribal-source income and the independent unpressed Williams infringement theory remain outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McClanahan v. Arizona State Tax Commission; Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Sac and Fox Nation: retain the residence-and-source protection within Indian country, including statutory Indian country beyond formal reservation labels.
- Mescalero Apache Tribe v. Jones: applies ordinary off-reservation state authority absent contrary federal law.
- Graves v. New York ex rel. O’Keefe: distinguishes employee income taxation from taxation of government as employer.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the bar on the reviewed tribal-retailer-incidence fuel tax; reverse the exemption for the specified off-country-resident tribal wages and remand for ordinary tax law and residence/source predicates. No tax amount, refund, individual residence finding or replacement-tax approval is entered.

### Treaty exclusion and acquired recreation lands

#### South Dakota v. Bourland, 508 U.S. 679 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2051.
**Decided:** June 14, 1993.

##### Acquisition displaces exclusion-derived regulation

**Holding:** The Flood Control Act and Cheyenne River Act displaced the treaty right of exclusive occupation on the taken former trust lands and the non-Indian hunting-and-fishing regulation derived from that exclusion right. Retained tribal access and personal hunting-and-fishing privileges do not themselves preserve authority to license or regulate non-Indian public users.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, Acquisition displaces exclusion-derived regulation portion; White, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Dion, 476 U.S. 734 — clear congressional choice required, without an invariable express-words requirement.
- Montana, 450 U.S. 544 — exclusion-derived authority and effects of alienation applied.
- Brendale, 492 U.S. 408 — land-specific setting considered without inventing a universal rule from its fracture.
- Menominee, 391 U.S. 404 — protection against casual implied abrogation preserved; particular enactments distinguished.

##### Residual authority remains a distinct inquiry

**Holding:** Loss of treaty-based exclusion does not decide whether a Montana exception or properly established federal authorization permits particular regulation. Remand preserves the exceptions for consensual commercial dealings, contracts, leases or other arrangements with the Tribe or its members, and for conduct threatening or directly affecting tribal political integrity, economic security, health or welfare, within Montana’s limits. General inherent sovereignty alone does not restore the abrogated regulatory power.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, Residual authority remains a distinct inquiry portion; White, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Taken former trust lands and formerly non-Indian fee lands retain distinct histories. Unreviewed Montana findings and properly established federal authorization remain for consideration; the undeveloped Corps litigating position receives no deference. No reservation-boundary, criminal-jurisdiction or universal exclusive-state-power ruling.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for residual authority and actual superior-federal-rule conflicts without reviving the displaced treaty exclusion. No result on the undeveloped questions is directed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Montana, 450 U.S. 544 — both consensual-relationship and serious tribal-interest exceptions expressly retained.
- United States v. Wheeler, 435 U.S. 313 — retained internal tribal powers do not themselves establish nonmember regulation after the abrogation found here.

### Reservation diminishment

#### Hagen v. Utah, 510 U.S. 399

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-6281.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### Congressional diminishment and the jurisdiction asserted at Myton

**Holding:** Congress diminished the Uintah Reservation as to the unallotted lands covered by the opening program: the 1902 operative direction restoring those lands to the public domain remained effective through the 1903–1905 amendments and implementation. Myton's location in that opened area is therefore outside the reservation on this record, and Utah may exercise the territorial criminal jurisdiction asserted over Hagen's offense; opening or fee ownership alone does not establish that result elsewhere.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six Justices adopt the statutory construction, its clear-intent basis and its application to this prosecution. There is a direct majority rationale; no fractured-opinion inference is necessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The collateral-estoppel issue arising from Ute Indian Tribe v. Utah was omitted from the petition and expressly disavowed by Hagen; the Court does not decide its merits. This case does not adjudicate private titles, all remaining trust or allotted lands, every tract of the larger Uintah and Ouray territory, unrelated tribal powers, or every sovereign's jurisdiction over every offense. The $70,064.48 payment is not relied upon as a purchase of the surplus land. Later population and state administration do not independently change boundaries.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Solem v. Bartlett, 465 U.S. 463: applies its clear-intent inquiry and unequal weighting of text, contemporaneous context and later treatment; distinguishes its nonoperative public-domain reference from the surviving operative command here.
- Rosebud Sioux Tribe v. Kneip, 430 U.S. 584: applies statutory continuity across a sequence of enactments and preserves the rule that no particular formula or fixed payment is indispensable.
- Seymour v. Superintendent, 368 U.S. 351: preserves the distinction between opening and boundary change and the rule that fee ownership within retained boundaries does not itself remove Indian-country status.
- Mattz v. Arnett, 412 U.S. 481: preserves the need for congressional diminishment beyond mere sale and settlement; this sequence supplies the additional direction.
- DeCoteau v. District County Court, 420 U.S. 425: preserves clear congressional cession as sufficient evidence without making its precise words or payment structure mandatory.
- South Dakota v. Bourland, June 14, 1993: preserves its particular displacement of exclusion-derived regulation and its separate residual-authority inquiry; it does not itself establish reservation boundaries.
- Negonsott v. Samuels, February 24, 1993: preserves its express Kansas-specific criminal authorization; it supplies no independent Utah authorization within Indian country.
- Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Sac and Fox Nation, May 17, 1993: preserves the distinct §1151 categories and the need to determine actual Indian-country status.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Utah Supreme Court is affirmed, 6–3, leaving its reinstatement of Hagen's conviction intact. No adjudication of private titles or general reallocation of regional jurisdiction is ordered.

#### South Dakota v. Yankton Sioux Tribe

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1581.

**Decided:** 1998-01-26.

For the concrete continuing declaration-and-permit controversy, see South Dakota v. Yankton Sioux Tribe, No. 96-1581, decided 1998-01-26, Federal Courts — Mootness and continuing territorial declarations.

##### The cession and purchase diminished the reservation as to the ceded lands

**Holding:** The Act of August 15, 1894 diminished the Yankton Sioux Reservation by removing the ordinary ceded, unallotted lands disposed of under its settler-opening program. The comprehensive surrender and unconditional federal purchase, read with the entire agreement, establish clear congressional intent. Article XVIII's treaty-saving language protects its annuity and associated entitlement assurances without preserving the 1858 boundary over those ceded lands.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The cession and unconditional $600,000 federal purchase control under the whole Act; no mandatory words, demographic threshold or quantified burden is adopted. Genuine unresolved ambiguity favors retained reservation status. Later administration, settlement and demographics remain subordinate evidence and cannot themselves change boundaries.

##### The supported parcel category determines the mandate's limits

**Holding:** The identified waste-site tract falls outside the reservation because it is established as ceded, unallotted land disposed of under this program, not merely because a non-Indian now owns it in fee. The declaration must be corrected on that ground. Total disestablishment and the status or authority associated with other land categories remain undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Allotments, trust parcels, Article VIII agency/school lands, later acquisitions, other §1151 categories and surviving federal or tribal powers retain their own predicates. Fee ownership alone does not end reservation status.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded unanimously to recognize diminishment of the ceded lands, including the established waste-site tract, and address remaining parcel-specific or statutory questions. State primary territorial jurisdiction is not universal sovereign authority. The unappealed tribal regulatory/injunction ruling and EPA waiver stay undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hagen is applied for an operative congressional-act inquiry, unequal evidentiary weights and geographic limits. Its surviving public-domain restoration command is distinguished from this Act's cession-and-purchase ground; neither mere opening nor fee ownership suffices.
- Solem is applied to the complete Act and its strongly weighted cession/guaranteed-compensation inference. Its sales-agency setting is distinguished; its tribal ambiguity canon and subordinate treatment of later evidence remain intact.
- DeCoteau's controlling clear-cession principle supports the reading without making its precise words, purchase structure or negotiations mandatory for every case.
- Dion retains the clear-and-plain requirement concerning the particular treaty right affected; no general cancellation of treaty obligations is adopted.
- Rosebud preserves the absence of a mandatory verbal formula. Seymour and Mattz continue to distinguish sale, settlement and fee ownership from congressional diminishment.
- Bourland's separate inquiry into exclusion-derived regulation and residual authority is not converted into a boundary rule. Sac and Fox's distinct Indian-country categories remain intact; Negonsott's Kansas-specific authorization supplies no general territorial rule here.
- Lee is applied to the actual continuing permit/declaration controversy, without treating corrective jurisdiction alone as power to decide merits.

### Reservation cigarette-tax collection

#### Department of Taxation and Finance of New York v. Milhelm Attea & Bros., Inc., 512 U.S. 61

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-377.

**Decided:** 1994-06-13.

##### Federal trader regulation does not displace reasonable collection of valid nonmember taxes

**Holding:** The Indian Trader Statutes do not categorically preempt state requirements reasonably directed to collecting cigarette taxes validly imposed on nonexempt consumers. New York's state-agent, stamping, advance-collection, certification, invoice, recordkeeping and reporting requirements survive this facial challenge insofar as they serve that collection function without taxing protected consumption or displacing the federal trader appointment.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's unanimous Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg adopt the limited federal-statute and collection holding.

**Material application:** Ultimate incidence is on the consumer. Protected purchases are by an enrolled member of an exempt New York nation or tribe within a qualified reservation for personal use or consumption there, not resale. Untaxed wholesale deliveries require a valid exemption certificate, intended distribution to exempt consumers and reservation delivery; the buyer shows its certificate initially and signs later invoices. Consumers separately certify exemption initially and show identity on later purchases. Wholesalers keep buyer-specific untaxed-sale records and report monthly. These duties do not authorize taxing protected consumption or overcoming tribal immunity.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general state power to regulate reservation commerce, revoke a federal trader appointment, tax exempt consumption or sue an immune tribe is recognized. The Seneca treaty contention was not addressed below and is not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Warren Trading Post Co. v. Arizona Tax Commission, 380 U.S. 685: preserves its protection against the particular direct tax on federally regulated reservation trade; its broader language does not create universal collection immunity.
- Moe v. Confederated Salish and Kootenai Tribes, 425 U.S. 463: applies valid nonmember taxation and reasonable collection assistance while preserving exempt member purchases.
- Washington v. Confederated Tribes of the Colville Indian Reservation, 447 U.S. 134: applies the distinction between taxable purchases and protected tribal trade, including reasonable documentation burdens.
- Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe, 498 U.S. 505: preserves valid nonmember tax obligations and reasonable collection tools without overcoming the tribe's immunity from suit.
- White Mountain Apache Tribe v. Bracker, 448 U.S. 136: applies attention to the actual federal, tribal and state regulatory setting rather than a universal territorial or licensing rule.

##### Probable-demand limits and prior approval survive only at facial scope

**Holding:** New York may limit unstamped deliveries to probable demand by qualifying exempt purchasers and require advance approval as reasonably necessary collection devices, provided exempt purchases remain adequately available and the requirements remain connected to the valid nonmember tax. The challenged scheme survives the present facial objections because no inadequate implemented quota or denied exempt supply is established; stamped sales have no corresponding numerical ceiling, and unreasonable applications remain subject to challenge.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's unanimous Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg adopt the qualified quota-and-approval holding and reversal of the facial injunction.

**Material application:** A tribe may enter a regulatory agreement for tribal approval. Without an agreement, the Department calculates probable demand using submitted evidence where the tribe regulates, licenses or controls distribution, and otherwise enrollment and statewide consumption. Trade territory uses consultation with a regulating tribe and otherwise available Department information. Valid orders and quantities reasonably related to exempt demand govern approval through monthly coupons. Withholding approval from past or current violators and canceling noncompliant certificates remain tied to lawful collection, not destruction of the exempt channel. No particular quota is approved and stamped supply has no numerical ceiling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Moe v. Confederated Salish and Kootenai Tribes, 425 U.S. 463: extends reasonable collection assistance to a qualified supply-control mechanism directed to valid taxes.
- Washington v. Confederated Tribes of the Colville Indian Reservation, 447 U.S. 134: applies the distinction between collection of nonexempt taxes and preservation of protected consumption.
- Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe, 498 U.S. 505: leaves alternative lawful collection methods available while preserving sovereign immunity.
- Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Sac and Fox Nation, May 17, 1993: preserves its residence, source and incidence distinctions; it supplies no exemption for every wholesale sale to a reservation retailer.
- County of Yakima v. Confederated Tribes, January 14, 1992: preserves the exact authorization and legal-incidence analysis for its distinct land taxes; collection labels do not authorize prohibited taxes.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The New York Court of Appeals is reversed on both grounds supporting its facial judgment and the case remanded. The facial injunction cannot rest on categorical trader preemption or the rejected facial excessive-burden ground. Concrete challenges to inadequate exempt supply, arbitrary approval or allocation, and excessive compliance burdens remain available; no particular quota, recovery from a tribe or treaty claim is adjudicated.

### State fuel-tax incidence in Indian country

#### Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Chickasaw Nation

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-771.
**Decided:** June 14, 1995.

For the other questions in Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Chickasaw Nation, decided June 14, 1995, No. 94-771. see Federal Indian Law — State taxation of resident tribal members (Resident taxation and the surviving treaty promise).

##### Legal incidence of the reviewed motor-fuels tax

**Holding:** Without clear congressional authorization, Oklahoma cannot enforce a tax whose legal incidence falls on the Tribe or tribal members for Indian-country sales. Under the reviewed statute’s payment, remittance and credit arrangement, incidence falls on the tribal retailer, so the fuel tax is barred in that application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices support the fuel-incidence rule and application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Retailer incidence is the supported construction of payment, remittance and credits, not an express statutory declaration. Hayden-Cartwright authorization was not raised below or in the petition and is not decided. A lawfully shifted non-Indian-incidence tax still requires separate preemption and collection analysis; proposed bills do not establish an enacted replacement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Moe v. Confederated Salish and Kootenai Tribes; Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe: distinguish immune tribal incidence from valid nonmember taxation and collection.
- County of Yakima v. Confederated Tribes: applies tax-specific congressional authorization, without transferring one tax’s permission to another.
- Department of Taxation v. Milhelm Attea & Bros.: preserves reasonable controls for valid nonmember taxes; supplies no tribal-incidence tax authority.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the bar on the reviewed tribal-retailer-incidence fuel tax; reverse the exemption for the specified off-country-resident tribal wages and remand for ordinary tax law and residence/source predicates. No tax amount, refund, individual residence finding or replacement-tax approval is entered.

### Nonmember public-highway claims

#### Strate v. A-1 Contractors

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1872.
**Decided:** 1997-04-28.

##### This authorized public highway falls within Montana's nonmember framework

**Holding:** For ordinary public motorists using this lawfully open, state-maintained federal right-of-way, the Tribe lacks the exclusion power that would otherwise govern access; Montana's nonmember framework therefore applies to this road-use tort. That consequence lasts within the authorized public use and neither conveys fee title nor removes every compatible tribal power.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, unanimous in Parts I, III and IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The May 8, 1970 grant applies to trust lands only, excludes non-trust property, and acknowledges $5,546.50 plus other good consideration. Tenure is unlimited only while used for the specified highway purpose and remains subject to valid existing rights or adverse claims. The United States grantor may terminate all or part after thirty days' written notice and failure to correct a breach of grant terms or applicable regulations, two consecutive years of nonuse for the purpose, abandonment, or failure to file the completion affidavit when construction ends. Indian owners, lessees, successors and assigns retain reasonably necessary crossings, constructed and maintained at their risk with responsibility for resulting right-of-way damage. The stated conditions bind and benefit the grantee's successors. No termination trigger is established on this record. The header's zero tribal acres and 87.48 individually owned acres do not identify the collision parcel; missing incorporated maps and a separate consent instrument establish no grant defect.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Montana supplies the nonmember framework; Bourland supplies a particular public-use/exclusion consequence with residual authority preserved. Sac and Fox's Indian-country classification does not settle governing power over this claim.

##### A tribal forum requires an adequate source of governing power

**Holding:** Subject to controlling treaties or statutes and Montana's two exceptions, tribal adjudicatory authority over nonmembers does not exceed the corresponding substantive governing authority. A tribal court cannot compel these defendants to litigate this highway claim solely by proposing to apply another sovereign's law; completed exhaustion supplies no independent jurisdictional grant.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by all seven other Associates; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** National Farmers Union and Iowa Mutual supply review and initial tribal examination, not unlimited ultimate authority. Williams's resident-member trading relationship differs. Merrion preserves sovereign taxation distinct from private exclusion and rejects surrender by silence.

##### The subcontract does not bring this ordinary highway claim within the consensual exception

**Holding:** Montana's first exception requires a meaningful relation between the asserted authority and consensual commercial dealings, contracts, leases or similar arrangements with the Tribe or its members. Even assuming Stockert's trip served A-1's tribal community-center subcontract and Fredericks resided on the reservation, this non-Indian motorist's ordinary highway-negligence claim lacks that relation; contracting with a tribal enterprise does not submit a contractor to every reservation claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, unanimous in Parts I, III and IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Express forum consent and contractual privity are not universally necessary for the exception. Injury to the Tribe, a member, the project or a participant in the consensual activity is reserved; the asserted extra-record forum clause supplies no ground.

##### The supported interests do not satisfy the self-government exception

**Holding:** Montana's second exception preserves authority over non-Indian conduct threatening or directly affecting tribal political integrity, economic security, health or welfare. The serious individual injury, family relationships, assumed residence and ordinary road-safety interests shown here do not establish the threat or direct tribal effect necessary to bring this nonmember highway claim within that exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, unanimous in Parts I, III and IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not deny the injury's real familial or community consequences, but they do not establish the required direct tribal effect. A possible state forum is not the independent reason tribal power is absent; no negligence or limitations result is decided.

##### The jurisdictional judgment does not itself establish every predicate for an injunction

**Holding:** Affirmance requires federal relief consistent with the absence of tribal jurisdiction over the reviewed claim, but does not itself issue an injunction or establish entitlement to every coercive remedy. On remand the federal court must respect the actual claims and lawful parties, immunity limits and ordinary equitable requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by all seven other Associates; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The jurisdiction-only declaratory consent and resolution do not waive immunity for every claim or remedy. The sons' consortium claims, dismissed insurer, personal jurisdiction, tribal/internal roads, member conduct, express consent, superior federal authorization and distinct police/criminal powers remain outside decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of jurisdiction over Fredericks's reviewed claim unanimously, without further exhaustion or factfinding on the assumed work-trip and residence facts. Eight Associates require relief fitted to lawful parties, immunity and equity; no injunction automatically issues.

### Dependent Indian communities

#### Alaska v. Native Village of Venetie Tribal Government

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1577.

**Decided:** 1998-02-25.

##### Dependent-community status requires a federal land relationship

**Holding:** A dependent Indian community under 18 U.S.C. §1151(b) requires land set aside by the Federal Government for Indian use and federal superintendence over that community’s land relationship. Tribal recognition, Indian ownership and federal assistance to residents do not independently supply those territorial predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither federal fee title, a reservation label nor a prescribed administrative form is essential. The federal set-aside and supervision predicates cannot be replaced by balancing cohesion, services and political dependence.

##### ANCSA removed both predicates from these lands

**Holding:** These former-reservation lands, conveyed in unrestricted fee after ANCSA expressly revoked the reservation and without continuing federal supervision of their use or disposition, are not a dependent Indian community. The Tribe’s asserted territorial authority to tax this nonmember construction consequently fails.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** ANCSA expressly revoked this reservation and authorized unrestricted fee conveyances; the corporations’ election and later transfer to the Tribe did not restore a federal set-aside. Section 1636(d) protects unsold, unleased and undeveloped lands from adverse possession, real-property taxes and certain judgments, but does not supply federal control of ordinary transfer or land use.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Other Native parcels, allotments, trust land, tribal identity, benefits, member-based authority and independently established consensual authority remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit’s dependent-community classification is reversed; the asserted territorial basis for this nonmember construction tax fails. No broader title or regional-jurisdiction decree follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sandoval and McGowan supply the federal set-aside and supervision predicates; their allowance for nonfederal fee ownership remains intact.
- Hagen and Yankton Sioux retain the operative-congressional-act inquiry and the distinction between title and territorial status; ANCSA’s express revocation and unrestricted settlement disposition supply the relevant change here.
- Cherokee Nation and Worcester do not convert recognized political dependence into territorial jurisdiction over every tribal-owned parcel.

### Tribal restitution of invalid state taxes

#### Montana v. Crow Tribe of Indians

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1829.
**Decided:** 1998-05-18.

##### 1. Invalidity of these taxes does not establish entitlement to every receipt

**Holding:** An invalid state tax does not automatically entitle a tribe to all sums a nontribal taxpayer paid. The claimant must establish entitlement to the particular fund or remedy; lawful tribal taxing authority and a protected interest in the taxed resource do not alone transfer ownership of the whole state collection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined in full by all eight Associate Justices. That coalition controls both holdings; the additional partial-restitution framework in Souter's concurrence is not adopted by the Court.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The invalidity judgment does not negate all possible concurrent state taxation. Taxpayer refunds, economic burden, tribal entitlement, actual agreements and prior distributions remain distinct; no compensatory loss measure is adjudged.

##### 2. Equitable analysis must use the actual legal and procedural interests

**Holding:** The remand must distinguish lawful tribal tax coverage, actual payment arrangements, independent claims against the State and funds already distributed. None of the approval dispute, taxpayer refund history or Tribe-taxpayer agreement categorically extinguishes a properly supported independent tribal claim on this record; further relief must follow its lawful basis and proven scope.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The 1988 ruling established approval coverage and distributed registry funds under the 1982 agreement; it did not determine an earlier taxpayer debt. Practical approval and pass-through difficulties remain facts. Properly supported independent partial restitution remains open without a prescribed amount, compulsory amendment or reopening of proof. Souter and O’Connor’s additional affirmative restitution framework is not a Court holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The compulsory whole-collection award is reversed unanimously and remanded for lawful claimant-specific remedial analysis. Tax invalidity and completed registry distributions remain intact. No amount, revived taxpayer refund claim or compensatory award is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Prior Crow Tribe tax-invalidity judgment:** Preserved; no relitigation of these taxes' invalidity.
- **Cotton Petroleum:** Preserves possible concurrent state and tribal taxation and distinguishes all receipts from an excessive share.
- **United States v. California:** Separates taxpayer refunds, economic burdens and another claimant's independent entitlement.
- **Fulton and Reich:** Preserve adequate, procedurally lawful correction for the appropriate claimant without predetermining this award.
- **Chickasaw Nation:** Its incidence analysis does not create a general right to another taxpayer's entire payments.
- **1988 registry-distribution ruling:** Retains its approval-coverage determination and distribution effect, without becoming a decision of pre-agreement taxpayer liability.

### Direct tribal contract immunity outside reservation land

#### Kiowa Tribe of Oklahoma v. Manufacturing Technologies, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1037.
**Decided:** 1998-05-26.

##### Whether off-reservation commercial character permits a direct contract suit against the Tribe

**Holding:** A federally recognized Indian tribe retains sovereign immunity from a direct suit on its contract, whether the activity is governmental or commercial and whether the contract concerns activity on or off reservation land, unless the tribe clearly waives immunity or Congress unequivocally authorizes the suit. No such waiver or authorization supports this action, so the direct contract claim against the Kiowa Tribe must be dismissed without deciding the debt's merits or resolving the disputed place of execution.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter: six direct supporters including Breyer for the complete rule and dismissal.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Signing or defaulting on a note that preserves sovereign rights supplies no clear consent. Sovereign immunity does not extinguish substantive contract obligations or decide individual-agent liability, tort immunity, collateral security, asset execution or an alternative remedy’s actual availability. The disputed execution location is immaterial to this contract-immunity rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed, 6–3, and remanded to dismiss the direct action against the Tribe on immunity grounds, without adjudicating debt, breach, damages or any separate authorized route.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe, 498 U.S. 505:** Applied for immunity distinct from substantive obligation and its rejection of a commercial-business limitation in the trust-land setting; the present holding extends the direct contract-immunity rule to the off-reservation commercial setting.
- **Santa Clara Pueblo v. Martinez, 436 U.S. 49:** Applied to the requirement of an unequivocal waiver and Congress's authority over the immunity rule.
- **Puyallup Tribe, Inc. v. Department of Game of Washington, 433 U.S. 165:** Applied to the distinction between the tribal sovereign and individual members; jurisdiction over others supplies no automatic authority to bind the Tribe.
- **Mescalero Apache Tribe v. Jones, 411 U.S. 145:** Distinguished as a rule concerning substantive state-law application outside reservation borders, not a direct contract-suit immunity exception.
- **Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Chickasaw Nation, June 14, 1995:** Its territorial and tax-incidence holdings remain intact and create no automatic contract remedy against the Tribe.
- **Alaska v. Native Village of Venetie Tribal Government, February 25, 1998:** Its separation of tribal recognition from Indian-country land status is preserved; loss of a territorial classification does not itself eliminate sovereign status or decide suit immunity.
- **Montana v. Crow Tribe of Indians, May 18, 1998:** Its requirement of an independently grounded restitutionary remedy is preserved; it establishes neither automatic tribal recovery nor an automatic private contract action.

### Nelson Act disposal and tribal reacquisition

#### Cass County v. Leech Lake Band of Chippewa Indians

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-174.

**Decided:** 1998-06-08.

##### Completed Nelson Act disposal and subsequent tribal repurchase

**Holding:** A parcel actually conveyed into the ordinary non-Indian fee estate through the Nelson Act's completed public-sale or homestead-disposal program is subject to county ad valorem land taxation; the Band's later ordinary fee purchase does not itself restore federal tax protection. The county must identify the qualifying statutory chain parcel by parcel, and an independently applicable federal exemption, retained restriction or legally effective trust protection remains controlling.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by all eight other Justices, supplies nine direct votes for this rule and explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule does not reach unsold, reserved, restricted or incompletely patented land by analogy. A retained reservation boundary is consistent with a particular congressional land-tax authorization. The Court decides neither boundary diminishment nor general personal or regulatory jurisdiction. The thirteen allotted parcels, transaction excises, income taxes, collection or execution immunity, and particular refund or penalty amounts are not decided. Applicability of the Nonintercourse Act to reacquired land is outside the presented question and remains unresolved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The exemption ruling for the eight Nelson Act sale parcels is reversed and remanded for proof of each qualifying statutory chain and application of independent federal protection. The thirteen allotted parcels are outside review; no refund, interest or penalty amount is fixed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- County of Yakima is applied for its statutory-source, land-tax-incidence and parcel-specific protection rules; its GAA/Burke Act holding is not converted into a general alienability rule or permission for transactional taxes.
- Montana v. Blackfeet Tribe supplies the clear-authorization requirement, satisfied here by the particular completed congressional disposal.
- Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Sac and Fox Nation preserves distinct predicates for member income and vehicle taxation; it supplies no automatic exemption or authorization for this land tax.
- Hagen v. Utah preserves the distinction between fee ownership and congressional reservation diminishment. No boundary changes follow from today's tax holding.
