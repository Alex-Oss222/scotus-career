### Adequate and independent state grounds

#### Sochor v. Florida, 504 U.S. 527 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-5843.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

For the judicial aggravator, general advisory recommendation and sentence-correction holdings in *Sochor v. Florida*, June 8, 1992, No. 91-5843, see Criminal Procedure — Capital sentencing.

##### Whether Florida's express failure-to-object ground forecloses review of the jury-heinousness instruction despite alternative merits discussion

**Holding:** The Court cannot review a federal challenge to a penalty instruction when the state judgment clearly rests on an adequate independent failure-to-object ground, even if the state court also rejects the claim on its merits. Florida's express preservation ruling forecloses this jury-heinousness challenge; no sufficient basis to reject its adequacy or independence is established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; seven Justices. Blackmun and Stevens would reach the instructional claim.

**Material application:** Florida separately stated that the penalty-instruction claims were unpreserved and rejected their merits; its note includes heinousness. Availability of fundamental-error review does not establish that this preservation ruling depends on the federal merits, and later citations to the merits discussion do not withdraw the independent ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide the instruction's constitutional merits or foreclose a supported challenge to a different procedural ground. This direct-review holding creates no habeas exception.

**Operative remedy or transition:** No relief is granted on the jury-heinousness instruction claim; the separate judicial weighing error and inadequate cure require the sentence vacatur described under Criminal Procedure — Capital sentencing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Michigan v. Long*, 463 U.S. 1032, preserves an expressly independent state disposition despite alternative federal merits discussion. *Herb v. Pitcairn*, 324 U.S. 117, supplies the jurisdictional limit. *Coleman v. Thompson*, 501 U.S. 722, retains its distinction between independent state procedure and federal merits.

### Administrative exhaustion

#### McCarthy v. Madigan, 503 U.S. 140 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6861.
**Decided:** March 4, 1992.

##### Whether Congress requires exhaustion of the general BOP grievance procedure for this federal medical-care damages action

**Holding:** An express congressional exhaustion requirement controls according to its terms. Where Congress has not required exhaustion, courts must exercise sound discretion consistent with the statutory scheme, weighing prompt access to court against the actual interests in agency authority and judicial efficiency; neither the general BOP delegation nor the then-current §1997e imposes a mandatory gate for this federal prisoner’s *Bivens* medical-care claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices control the governing inquiry and statutory application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated under the statutory scheme adjudicated.

**Material application:** General prison-administration authority under 18 U.S.C. §4042 contains no exhaustion command. The then-current 42 U.S.C. §1997e addresses specified state-prisoner §1983 actions through limited discretionary stays, not this federal action. The Bureau’s general grievance procedure is not a comprehensive congressional replacement for the existing medical-care damages channel.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No exception to an express congressional requirement or new *Bivens* action is created. Liability, immunity, causation and damages are not determined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Patsy v. Board of Regents of Florida*, 457 U.S. 496, makes congressional design central. *McKart v. United States*, 395 U.S. 185, supplies the agency-authority, expertise, error-correction and efficiency purposes. *McGee v. United States*, 402 U.S. 479, supports sound judicial discretion absent congressional mandate. *Coit Independence Joint Venture v. FSLIC*, 489 U.S. 561, distinguishes statutory exhaustion and the adequacy of the administrative route. *Carlson v. Green*, 446 U.S. 14, remains the established medical-care damages channel without extension. *Bush v. Lucas*, 462 U.S. 367, and *Schweiker v. Chilicky*, 487 U.S. 412, are distinguished because there is no comparable comprehensive congressional remedial replacement here.

##### Whether the actual BOP procedure justifies requiring exhaustion of this damages-only claim absent a statutory mandate

**Holding:** Absent a statutory mandate, a federal prisoner seeking only damages for the completed medical-care wrong alleged here need not exhaust the described BOP process: it cannot award compensation, its short filing periods risk forfeiture, and its limited adjudicative benefit does not outweigh access to court. The result does not decide a request for prospective relief, a procedure able to grant the sought compensation, or a materially different administrative adjudication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices join this particular balancing and application. Scalia and Thomas concur in the judgment solely because monetary relief is unavailable; their votes do not support the deadline or broader balancing rationale.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for this claim and grievance procedure.

**Material application:** The process provides investigation and written responses but no hearing, formal evidentiary findings or monetary award. It requires an initial complaint within 15 days and appeals within 20 and 30 days, with extensions for valid reasons. The rejected regional appeal illustrates forfeiture risk without establishing official misconduct. Speculative FTCA conversion or settlement does not establish an effective damages remedy under these regulations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The deadline analysis does not invalidate short filing periods categorically. No finding of bias, deliberate indifference, damages entitlement or loss of a defense is made. The holding does not convert negligence into a constitutional violation or expand the established damages action.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the exhaustion dismissal and remand for ordinary proceedings on the damages complaint without completion of this grievance process as a prerequisite. Liability, defenses and damages remain for adjudication.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *McKart*, 395 U.S. 185, supports comparing actual individual and institutional interests. *Coit*, 489 U.S. 561, informs practical adequacy without categorically invalidating deadlines. *Turner v. Safley*, 482 U.S. 78, preserves judicial cognizance of valid prisoner claims without deciding this claim’s validity. *Hillsborough Township v. Cromwell*, 326 U.S. 620, and *Union Pacific Railroad Co. v. Board of County Commissioners*, 247 U.S. 282, address uncertainty in an administrative route to the requested relief.

### Appellate jurisdiction and notices of appeal

#### Smith v. Barry, 502 U.S. 244 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-7477.
**Decided:** January 14, 1992.

##### Whether a timely appellate brief may function as a notice of appeal

**Holding:** A timely document styled as an appellate brief may serve as a notice of appeal if it objectively identifies the appealing party, the judgment or order appealed, and the appellate court as Rule 3(c) requires. Rule 4 treats a notice mistakenly filed in the court of appeals as filed in the district court upon receipt; informal form or title alone cannot require dismissal.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; these eight Justices supply the controlling rationale. Scalia concurs in the judgment and agrees with the express form-or-title ground.

**Material application:** Smith returned the clerk's informal-brief form within the valid appeal period. Use of that form did not negate the notice the document objectively might convey, and a requirement of separate notice and brief documents could not add a jurisdictional condition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Each item required by Rule 3 remains necessary. The first notice remains ineffective under the then-current posttrial-motion rule. The Court does not decide whether the May 4 document actually supplies each required item or which judgment or orders it fairly identifies.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is reversed and the case remanded for the Fourth Circuit to examine the informal brief's actual contents.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Torres makes Rule 3 compliance mandatory while allowing functional compliance with its required information. Foman supports contextual reading where intent to seek review is manifest. Griggs is applied. Ibarra remains confined to timely criminal reconsideration motions.

### Appellate stays and mandatory agency relief

#### Benten v. Kessler, 505 U.S. 1084 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Application No. A-40.
**Decided:** July 17, 1992.

##### A substantial procedural challenge does not alone establish entitlement to mandatory return

**Holding:** An applicant seeking to vacate an appellate stay of an order granting effectively final individual relief must show a substantial likelihood of entitlement to that ordered relief and a demonstrable error in the appellate stay. Where the asserted procedural defect would restore agency discretion rather than compel the requested substantive outcome, a strong procedural claim and serious irreparable injury do not alone establish entitlement to immediate judicially ordered return of the drug.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: seven Justices. The Court assumes the procedural challenge's strength for this remedial inquiry; it does not adopt Stone-Zsela's separate assessment that the then-operative FDA procedural regulation was likely violated.

**Material application:** The personal-use policy permits admission rather than compels it. Even assuming a strong procedural challenge, removing automatic detention restores agency discretion. The Court accepts serious imminent injury without inventing individual danger or an alternative treatment placement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not finally uphold the Import Alert, reject the asserted 1989 procedural undertaking, establish an across-the-board exhaustion command, decide §10.70 or arbitrary-or-capricious merits, or deny the possibility of prompt lawful agency-action relief. The constitutional claim discussed by Stevens was not addressed below or presented in the applicants' filings here and is not properly before the Court. Neither that claim nor the right to a particular abortion method is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The application to vacate the Second Circuit's July 15 stay is denied, 7–2. The appellate stay retains its terms; the District Court's mandatory return order is not reinstated. No drug approval, general import authorization or constitutional access judgment is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Western Airlines, Inc. v. Teamsters, 480 U.S. 1301 (1987) (in chambers): applied for the demanding standard governing intervention in an appellate stay; urgent injury does not eliminate the required merits-and-remedy showing.
- Ford Motor Co. v. NLRB, 305 U.S. 364 (1939): its flexibility to make lawful administrative action effective is preserved; it does not clearly establish entitlement to judicial selection of the discretionary admission decision on this application.
- United States v. Rutherford, 442 U.S. 544 (1979): the statutory approval framework remains in force; this application creates no judicial drug-approval exemption and makes no general safety finding.

### Bankruptcy appellate jurisdiction

#### Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, 503 U.S. 249 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1791.
**Decided:** March 9, 1992.

##### Whether §158(d) precludes otherwise available §1292 review of district-court bankruptcy appellate orders

**Holding:** Section 158(d) does not preclude court-of-appeals review under §1292 of a district court’s order entered in its bankruptcy appellate capacity. Review remains subject to the particular §1292 route’s requirements, including certification and circuit permission where §1292(b) applies; the provision does not grant an automatic appeal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion, joined by Stone, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; five Justices adopt the text/coexistence rationale. White, Blackmun, Stevens and O’Connor agree in judgment on their separate stated reasoning.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The applicable §1292 requirements remain, including certification of a controlling legal question, substantial ground for difference of opinion, material advancement of termination, timely application and circuit permission under §1292(b). No jury entitlement or extension of district-court language to bankruptcy appellate panels is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The jurisdictional dismissal is reversed and remanded to the Second Circuit to address the remaining statutory requirements and whether to permit the appeal.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wood v. United States, 16 Pet. 342 — applied: overlapping statutes remain effective absent positive conflict.
- United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, 489 U.S. 235 — applied: the enacted bankruptcy-related text governs.
- Rubin v. United States, 449 U.S. 424 — applied: unambiguous statutory words control the inquiry.
- United States v. Goldenberg, 168 U.S. 95 — applied: courts give effect to legislative text rather than assumed omissions.

### Civil jury right

#### Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71, 502 U.S. 93 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-967.  
**Decided:** December 4, 1991.

##### Whether lost hiring-hall referrals support legal damages carrying a Seventh Amendment jury right

**Holding:** A union member's claim for wages and benefits lost because hiring-hall referrals allegedly were withheld is a claim for compensatory legal relief carrying a Seventh Amendment jury right. Accompanying equitable requests do not eliminate the jury right on the legal issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; all eight participants.

**Material application:** Wooddell seeks replacement of lost earnings, not return of identified funds held by the union or backpay incidental to reinstatement in a former job.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Jury entitlement decides the factfinder, not retaliation, liability, or recoverable amount. Equitable relief remains separately adjudicated, with legal and equitable issues sequenced consistently with the Seventh Amendment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The jury-denial component is reversed and remanded to permit a jury on the LMRDA legal damages claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chauffeurs, Teamsters & Helpers Local 391 v. Terry supplies the historical-analogue inquiry and makes the legal or equitable character of relief the more important consideration.

For the member-beneficiary enforcement holding in Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71, decided December 4, 1991, No. 90-967, see Labor and Employment — Union-constitution enforcement.

### Collateral procedural authority

#### Willy v. Coastal Corp., 503 U.S. 131 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1150.
**Decided:** March 3, 1992.

##### Whether the Rules and Article III permit this Rule 11 sanction after the court is held to lack jurisdiction over the action

**Holding:** A federal district court may impose and preserve a valid Rule 11 sanction for litigation misconduct before it even when its assertion of subject-matter jurisdiction over the action is later held erroneous. The sanction must address an independently authorized procedural violation collateral to the merits; it does not validate the jurisdictional error or authorize adjudication of the underlying claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous holding on both authorization under the Rules and the Article III objection.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the then-effective Rule 11 and the independent procedural violation adjudicated.

**Material application:** Rules 1 and 81(c) govern civil proceedings after removal, and Rule 11 supplies the particular sanctioning authority. The completed misconduct was misuse of the court’s procedure, not Willy’s successful jurisdictional objection. The propriety of the violation, fee response and joint liability was accepted on this limited review.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No hypothetical merits jurisdiction, general inherent sanctioning power or universal collateral-order exception is recognized. A different order requires its own lawful source and genuine independence. The accepted misconduct and amount are not reconsidered.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the $19,307 sanction, jointly and severally against Willy and his attorney, without enlargement. The prior jurisdictional direction returning the employment claims to state court remains operative; the invalid federal merits judgment is not revived.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Sibbach v. Wilson & Co.*, 312 U.S. 1, preserves the prohibition on procedural rules enlarging substantive rights or jurisdiction. *Cooter & Gell v. Hartmarx Corp.*, 496 U.S. 384, is extended from voluntary dismissal to this erroneous assertion of merits jurisdiction because Rule 11 adjudication is collateral. *United States v. Mine Workers*, 330 U.S. 258, supports orderly determination of disputed authority without assumed merits power. *United States Catholic Conference v. Abortion Rights Mobilization, Inc.*, 487 U.S. 72, is distinguished: coercive discovery serving unauthorized merits adjudication lacks the independence of this completed sanction.

### Diversity jurisdiction and domestic-relations abstention

#### Ankenbrandt v. Richards, 504 U.S. 689 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-367.
**Decided:** June 15, 1992.

##### Whether Article III or the domestic-relations exception to §1332 excludes this diverse tort damages action

**Holding:** Article III does not exclude domestic-relations disputes from the federal judicial power. Section 1332 carries a settled domestic-relations exception limited to issuance of divorce, alimony, and child-custody decrees; that exception does not exclude a diverse parties’ tort action seeking damages for alleged child abuse without such a decree.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; five Justices adopt this proposition. The judgment reversing dismissal is unanimous, but the separate judgment concurrences do not add joins to the Court's foundational statutory holding.

**Material application:** The complaint seeks compensation from the children's father and his companion for alleged abuse, without requesting a divorce, alimony, custody decree or review of a state decree. The settled statutory understanding survives the 1948 recodification; Article III itself contains no domestic-relations exclusion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The exception concerns issuance of divorce, alimony and child-custody decrees, not every dispute among relatives. No federal tort is created; liability, parental immunity, damages, the alleged abuse and any custody or support decree are not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is reversed and the complaint remanded for ordinary proceedings and defenses. The Court issues or modifies no domestic decree and awards no damages.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Barber v. Barber, 62 U.S. (21 How.) 582 (1859): retained as the basis for the limited decree exception and distinguished from a prohibition of ordinary damages actions.
- De la Rama v. De la Rama, 201 U.S. 303 (1906): its distinction between territorial authority and ordinary decree jurisdiction remains; it does not exclude this tort action.
- In re Burrus, 136 U.S. 586 (1890): broad domestic-relations language does not enlarge the exception to this diverse damages suit.
- Patterson v. McLean Credit Union, 491 U.S. 164 (1989): statutory stare decisis supports preservation of the settled limited construction; no separate civil-rights issue is decided.
- Fourco Glass Co. v. Transmirra Products Corp., 353 U.S. 222 (1957): a recodification is not presumed to alter substantive law absent a clear indication.
- Lorillard v. Pons, 434 U.S. 575 (1978): congressional retention of language against an established judicial construction supports the retained limited exception.

##### Whether Younger abstention supports dismissal without an ongoing state proceeding

**Holding:** Younger abstention cannot sustain this dismissal because there is no ongoing state proceeding with which the federal damages action would interfere.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; five Justices adopt this proposition. The judgment reversing dismissal is unanimous, but the separate judgment concurrences do not add joins to the Court's foundational statutory holding.

**Material application:** The action seeks compensation for past misconduct and does not halt, supervise or displace an ongoing state adjudication. No state proceeding is alleged to be pending.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court leaves Younger's outer range in other domestic-relations controversies undecided. A family setting or the possibility of a future state application supplies no ongoing proceeding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Younger cannot sustain the dismissal; ordinary proceedings on the damages complaint must resume, subject to the separately stated limits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Younger v. Harris, 401 U.S. 37 (1971): retained; its ongoing-proceeding premise is absent here.
- New Orleans Public Service, Inc. v. Council of New Orleans, 491 U.S. 350 (1989): applied to require a concrete abstention basis rather than general reluctance to exercise federal jurisdiction.

##### Whether Burford abstention supports dismissal without unsettled domestic status or relevant state-policy interference

**Holding:** Burford abstention does not authorize dismissal of this ordinary diversity tort action where adjudication neither requires resolution of an unsettled domestic status nor threatens the state-policy interference that supports that form of abstention.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; five Justices adopt this proposition. The judgment reversing dismissal is unanimous, but the separate judgment concurrences do not add joins to the Court's foundational statutory holding.

**Material application:** Family status has already been determined and its present legal character does not decide the alleged torts or compensation. State expertise and an important family-policy interest alone cannot replace the absent interference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Materially different future circumstances remain open. An unresolved status issue necessary to the federal action together with threatened interference in the relevant state process would present a different question; that hypothetical is not adjudicated here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Burford cannot sustain this dismissal; the reinstated tort complaint returns for ordinary proceedings and defenses.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Burford v. Sun Oil Co., 319 U.S. 315 (1943): retained within its state-policy and administrative-coherence rationale; no basis for abstention is shown here.
- New Orleans Public Service, Inc. v. Council of New Orleans, 491 U.S. 350 (1989): applied to confine Burford abstention to concrete interference, not the mere importance of state interests.

### Execution stays

#### Coleman v. Thompson, 504 U.S. 188 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. A-877; associated petition No. 91-8336.
**Decided:** May 20, 1992.

##### Whether the renewed habeas presentation satisfies the requirements for a stay pending certiorari review

**Holding:** A stay of execution pending certiorari review requires a reasonable probability that four Justices would find the issue sufficiently meritorious to grant review, a significant possibility of reversal, and likely irreparable harm without a stay. Coleman's combined presentation does not establish the necessary possibility of overcoming the surviving independent claim-default and successive-petition barriers; denial decides neither ultimate innocence nor a general freestanding-innocence or hearing rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: five of nine participating Justices adopt this record-specific application of Barefoot. Stevens concurs in the denial without joining that opinion. Stone, Blackmun and Souter dissent.

**Material application:** The disputed recantation, competing confession accounts, physical-evidence allegations and scientific interpretations are considered together but do not show the needed prospect of overcoming independent claim-default and successive-petition barriers. Irreparable harm alone does not establish the missing likelihood.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no ultimate innocence, freestanding-innocence merits rule, conclusive-exoneration requirement, automatic hearing entitlement, categorical credibility or expert-selection rule, or reversal of the preterm Coleman holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The stay application is denied, 6–3. The denial is an interim disposition and does not finally decide associated petition No. 91-8336.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880 (1983): applied; the ordinary capital-stay prerequisites remain distinct and no automatic stay is created.
- Coleman v. Thompson, 501 U.S. 722 (1991): applied; independent and adequate state claim-default rules and their established exceptions remain in force.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467 (1991): applied to the independent successive-petition and abuse barriers; its cause-and-prejudice rule and innocence exception are not replaced.
- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478 (1986): applied for the extraordinary miscarriage-of-justice exception; the Court does not require conclusive exoneration.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436 (1986): its plurality formulation identifies the lower court's stated factual-innocence inquiry; no broader controlling rule is attributed to that plurality.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, May 4, 1992, No. 90-1859: distinguished and preserved; the simulated holding concerns inadequate factual development of a properly available claim, not automatic removal of an independent claim-default bar.
- Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293 (1963): preserved through Keeney; its material-fact hearing framework is not displaced.

#### Gomez v. United States District Court for the Northern District of California, 503 U.S. 653 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. A-767.
**Decided:** April 21, 1992.

##### Whether unjustified delay independently defeats equitable support for the April 20 execution stays

**Holding:** A court considering an execution stay may weigh the State's interest in enforcing its judgment and an applicant's unjustified delay in presenting a long-available claim independently of any habeas procedural bar. On this record, the unexplained postponement of the cyanide-gas challenge through four prior federal habeas proceedings leaves the April 20 stays without sufficient equitable support; those stays must be vacated even assuming the claim is cognizable under §1983 and not barred by abuse-of-writ doctrine.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Six of nine participating Justices adopt this independent equitable ground. Stone, Blackmun and Stevens dissent. No other ground is necessary to the judgment, and no fractured-opinion rule is invoked.

**Material application:** The recent Harding account adds support but does not explain failure to raise the established cyanide-gas challenge through four prior federal habeas proceedings. Medical submissions and execution’s irreversibility do not outweigh the unexplained delay on this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The §1983 channel, the final Eighth Amendment method claim, the application of every habeas-default element, Younger abstention and any universal alternative-method requirement remain undecided. The decision creates no permanent injunction or direction barring another federal stay.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The application is granted and the April 20, 1992 execution stays in Ninth Circuit No. 92-70237 are vacated, 6–3. The order adds no injunction or general restriction on later federal stays.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Delo v. Stokes, 495 U.S. 320 — applied for full-Court review and vacatur of an unsupported execution stay; its successive-habeas holding remains intact. The equitable assessment here does not treat Kennedy's separate writing as prior Court law.
- Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880 — applied: successive litigation does not itself require a stay, and substantial grounds for relief matter to interim protection.
- In re Blodgett, January 13, 1992 — distinguished: its requirement to seek focused relief below before mandamus neither resolves this application nor supplies a broader rule about capital-litigation delay.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467 — application to this §1983 action is reserved; its cause-and-prejudice and miscarriage-of-justice rules remain governing within their proper habeas domain.
- Preiser v. Rodriguez, 411 U.S. 475; Younger v. Harris, 401 U.S. 37; Huffman v. Pursue, Ltd., 420 U.S. 592; Pennzoil Co. v. Texaco Inc., 481 U.S. 1 — their respective channel and abstention rules are left intact; the Court decides no extension to this execution-method claim.

### Extraordinary writs

#### In re Blodgett, 502 U.S. 236 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-716.
**Decided:** January 13, 1992.

##### Whether mandamus is available before focused relief is sought from the court that entered the operative abeyance order

**Holding:** Mandamus is unavailable where the petitioner has not first sought focused relief from the court that entered the operative abeyance order and has not shown that avenue inadequate or futile. Denial may be without prejudice to a renewed petition after the ordinary avenue is pursued.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion for Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas, together with Stevens's concurrence joined by Blackmun, expressly states this dispositive prerequisite for all nine Justices. This is an actual majority-supported threshold proposition across writings.

**Material application:** Washington did not ask the Ninth Circuit panel to modify the August 1991 order awaiting related proceedings. Earlier general inquiries and a resubmission request did not establish that focused relief was unavailable or inevitably futile.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not approve every period of delay, find abuse of the writ, resolve Campbell's sentence, dissolve the stay, or impose an automatic special burden on future capital litigants. It creates no habeas-merits rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Mandamus is denied without prejudice to renewed application after the ordinary avenue is pursued; no habeas result or execution schedule is directed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Kerr, Will, and Fahey are applied with Rule 20.1: mandamus requires an extraordinary failure to act, no adequate alternative avenue, and a clear and indisputable entitlement. Barefoot, Rose, and McCleskey are not extended.

### Federal corporate charters and removal

#### American National Red Cross v. S. G., 505 U.S. 247 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-594.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

##### Whether the Red Cross charter's State-or-Federal court clause confers original federal jurisdiction

**Holding:** The Red Cross charter’s authorization to sue and be sued in courts of law and equity, State or Federal, confers original federal jurisdiction over actions to which the Red Cross is a party. In construing a congressional charter’s sue-and-be-sued clause, a specific mention of federal courts permits a jurisdictional reading that general corporate-capacity language does not; the charter grant here operates independently of federal incorporation alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens and Thomas; five of nine participating Justices adopt the charter construction at this level of generality. Stone-Zsela agrees on his separate charter-specific ground but supplies no formal join.

**Material application:** Congress added the explicit federal-forum reference in 1947 against the charter precedents. The clause confers both capacity and jurisdiction and preserves concurrent state-court access. Section 1349's restriction on incorporation alone does not erase this specific additional grant.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Materially different charter wording and §1349's application to nonstock corporations remain unresolved. Jurisdiction creates no substantive federal tort rule, liability or immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The First Circuit's contrary charter ruling is reversed; the special grant supports the removal holding below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bank of the United States v. Deveaux, 9 U.S.61: distinguished; its general-capacity clause remains insufficient to grant federal jurisdiction.
- Osborn v. Bank of the United States, 22 U.S.738: applied for the jurisdictional effect of express federal-forum language.
- Bankers Trust Co. v. Texas & Pacific Railway, 241 U.S.295: distinguished; its generalized court language remains a capacity provision.
- D’Oench, Duhme & Co. v. FDIC, 315 U.S.447: applied for its stated jurisdictional use of materially comparable State-or-Federal language; no FDIC substantive rule is imported.
- Cannon v. University of Chicago, 441 U.S.677: applied for Congress’s use of language with an established judicial meaning.
- United States v. Merriam, 263 U.S.179: applied for interpreting statutory words against their established judicial meaning.

##### Whether the special charter grant permits §1441 removal of a complaint asserting state-law torts

**Holding:** Because the charter gives original federal jurisdiction over this Red Cross action, §1441 permits its removal even though the complaint alleges state-law torts. The well-pleaded-complaint limitation on statutory arising-under jurisdiction under §1331 does not eliminate this separate charter grant.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens and Thomas; the same five Justices adopt this necessary answer to the alternative removal objection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Diversity, joinder and independently available abstention retain their own requirements and remain unresolved. No tort element, defense, immunity or substantive-law issue is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Removal is sustained on the charter ground and the case is remanded through the First Circuit for federal proceedings consistent with that grant. The Court neither requires nor forbids joinder and does not establish diversity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Verlinden B. V. v. Central Bank of Nigeria, 461 U.S.480: applied to distinguish §1331’s statutory arising-under limits from a separate original-jurisdiction grant.
- Osborn v. Bank of the United States, 22 U.S.738: the special charter basis remains distinct from the substantive law governing the controversy.
- D’Oench, Duhme & Co. v. FDIC, 315 U.S.447: its charter jurisdiction is not merged into the substantive law governing a particular controversy.

##### Whether Article III permits the Red Cross corporate-party jurisdiction granted by Congress

**Holding:** Article III’s arising-under judicial power permits Congress to confer original federal jurisdiction over actions involving a federally chartered corporation such as the Red Cross, although the immediate claim arises under state law. The charter’s specific jurisdictional grant therefore lies within Congress’s constitutional authority.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens and Thomas; five Justices decide the constitutional objection. The dissent does not reach it, and Stone-Zsela supplies only his separate ground.

**Material application:** Federal law supplies the corporation's legal existence and powers; state-law issues do not remove that federal foundation. Article III's capacity is distinct from Congress's narrower choices in general jurisdiction statutes.

### Fee-waiver screening and appellate review

#### Denton v. Hernandez, 504 U.S. 25 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1846.
**Decided:** May 4, 1992.

##### When a complaint filed without prepayment of fees may be dismissed as factually frivolous

**Holding:** A court may dismiss a claim brought in forma pauperis (without paying filing fees first) as factually frivolous under 28 U.S.C. §1915(d) when its allegations are clearly baseless—irrational or wholly incredible—even without judicially noticeable facts contradicting them. Stevens’s dissent, joined by Blackmun, agrees with this factual-screening standard but disagrees with the additional appellate remand; their agreement is not needed to establish controlling authority.

**Material application:** The magistrate relied on the complaints’ combined pattern while acknowledging that each was not necessarily frivolous. Particular supporting affidavits must be considered without deciding truth from the pattern alone.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No particular district dismissal is affirmed, no allegation is found true or false, and no liability or damages are determined. The circuit’s categorical notice-and-amendment rule, an absolute right to amend and a universal preclusion rule for later fee applications remain undecided. Availability of an affidavit is not a condition of an arguable claim.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The appellate judgment is vacated and remanded, 7–2, for review of Nos. CIV S-83-0645, CIV S-83-1348 and CIV S-85-0084 under the proper standards. **Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Neitzke v. Williams, 490 U.S. 319 (1989): applied and clarified; its clearly-baseless category reaches irrational or wholly incredible allegations without requiring a judicially noticeable contradiction, while preserving the distinction from Rule 12(b)(6).
- Haines v. Kerner, 404 U.S. 519 (1972): applied; liberal treatment of pleadings filed without counsel remains part of the screening inquiry and does not adjudicate their truth.

**Holding:** A dismissal under section 1915(d) is reviewed for abuse of discretion; an erroneous legal premise remains independently correctable as law. Seven participating Justices adopt this review rule and its remand instructions. - Neitzke v. Williams, 490 U.S. 319 (1989): applied; section 1915(d)’s screening function remains distinct from disposition of the merits under ordinary pleading rules.

### Foreign sovereign immunity

#### Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, Inc., 504 U.S. 607 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-763.
**Decided:** June 12, 1992.

##### Whether negotiable sovereign debt issued and rescheduled for a monetary program is commercial activity under the FSIA

**Holding:** Under 28 U.S.C. §§1603(d) and 1605(a)(2), an activity is commercial when its nature is the kind of conduct a private participant in a market can undertake; the sovereign's public purpose does not control that characterization. Argentina and Banco Central acted commercially in issuing and rescheduling these negotiable, dollar-denominated debt instruments, even though the instruments served a foreign-exchange program and refinanced existing obligations.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this proposition. Stone concurs separately in the judgment without joining the opinion.

**Material application:** Negotiable dollar repayment promises bearing market-linked interest are means private borrowers can use. The foreign-exchange objective and refinancing of existing debt concern purpose and do not change the instruments' commercial nature.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling concerns these instruments and this rescheduling. It does not make all public borrowing, currency regulation, expropriation or sovereign activity affecting money commercial. The second-clause domestic-act theory is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The denial of FSIA dismissal is affirmed; ordinary contract proceedings continue. No contract liability, damages, attachment or execution is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Verlinden B. V. v. Central Bank of Nigeria, 461 U.S. 480 (1983): applied for the restrictive-immunity framework and statutory exception channel; no additional citizenship or commercial-purpose restriction is derived from it.

##### Whether failure to make the contractually selected New York payment creates a direct effect in the United States

**Holding:** For the third clause of §1605(a)(2), an effect is direct when it follows as an immediate consequence of the defendant's activity; the statute adds no separate substantiality or foreseeability requirement, although wholly trivial effects remain outside the ordinary reach of the exception. Failure to make payment in New York, after the holders validly exercised the instruments' contractual payment option, produces the required direct effect in the United States even though the holders are foreign and their ultimate economic loss is abroad.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this proposition. Stone concurs separately in the judgment without joining the opinion.

**Material application:** The holders validly selected New York under the instruments, and previous payments credited their accounts there. The promised dollars failed to arrive at the agreed place of performance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Wholly trivial effects remain outside the exception's ordinary reach. New York's financial-center reputation is too remote to establish the required effect. Other payment arrangements and merely incidental domestic financial connections remain open; foreign plaintiffs' ultimate losses abroad do not negate this agreed domestic performance obligation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** This domestic nonpayment satisfies the third clause of §1605(a)(2), sustaining the denial of immunity-based dismissal without deciding contract merits or asset immunity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Verlinden B. V. v. Central Bank of Nigeria, 461 U.S. 480 (1983): applied to permit jurisdiction over qualifying foreign-plaintiff actions; its general jurisdictional discussion is not a substantiality or foreseeability test for this clause.
- The Second Circuit's immediate-consequence formulation in the judgment below is sustained; its additional substantiality/foreseeability formulation is rejected, and its financial-center-reputation rationale supplies no controlling ground.

##### Whether the assumed constitutional-contacts objection requires a narrower reading of the direct-effect exception

**Holding:** Assuming solely for Argentina's interpretive objection that a foreign state receives the relevant constitutional protection, Argentina's purposeful dollar debt and offered, selected and previously used New York payment arrangements provide sufficient transaction-related contacts to defeat the asserted constitutional doubt. Constitutional avoidance therefore does not justify narrowing the statutory direct-effect clause in this case; the Court decides neither foreign-sovereign constitutional personhood nor an independent constitutional personal-jurisdiction claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this proposition. Stone concurs separately in the judgment without joining the opinion.

**Material application:** Argentina purposefully offered New York payment in dollar instruments, honored that selection before the dispute and then failed to perform there; the connection is more than foreseeable routing through an American bank.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Foreign-state constitutional personhood is assumed solely to answer the interpretive contention and remains undecided. No independent constitutional personal-jurisdiction claim, jurisdiction over unrelated sovereign conduct, or alternative subject-matter jurisdiction ground is adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The constitutional-avoidance objection supplies no ground to narrow the statutory exception or dismiss this action; undecided contract defenses and asset-immunity questions remain available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- International Shoe Co. v. Washington, 326 U.S. 310 (1945): applied on an express assumption of constitutional protection, for purposeful contacts related to this dispute; no holding extends constitutional personhood to foreign states.

### Habeas procedural gateways

#### Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-6382.
**Decided:** June 22, 1992.

##### What capital-innocence showing permits review of an otherwise barred habeas claim without cause and prejudice

**Holding:** A prisoner seeking review of an otherwise successive, abusive or procedurally defaulted capital habeas claim without the ordinarily required cause and prejudice must establish by clear and convincing evidence that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find the prisoner eligible for death under applicable state law. The inquiry includes the offense, required aggravating facts and every other eligibility condition; additional mitigation affecting only discretionary selection of the punishment does not satisfy this gateway.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt both the eligibility-only scope and clear-and-convincing quantum. Stone, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor concur only in the judgment on broader grounds; their votes do not enlarge the controlling rule. No Marks synthesis is necessary.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the capital-eligibility gateway to otherwise barred claims.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The crime-innocence standard is unchanged. Ordinary cause and prejudice, exhaustion, retroactivity, merits requirements and factual development of a properly available claim remain distinct. Passing the gateway does not itself grant relief. No freestanding innocence claim or general bar to consideration of impeachment evidence is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The precise capital formulation previously left open is established effective June 22, 1992. Sawyer's refusal of further review is affirmed on the application stated below; no new hearing, sentencing proceeding or release is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478, preserves cause and prejudice and its miscarriage exception; only the capital-eligibility branch is specified here.
- Smith v. Murray, 477 U.S. 527, has its open sentencing application specified through eligibility rather than discretionary mitigation reweighing.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467, is applied: abuse remains an independent obstacle unless cause and prejudice or the applicable exceptional gateway is established.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436, supplies relevant plurality discussion without becoming a retrospective majority holding.
- Dugger v. Adams, 489 U.S. 401, is applied: possible sentencing inaccuracy does not itself establish exceptional innocence.
- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862, and Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231, supply the distinction between death eligibility and selection of punishment.

### Prospective equitable relief

#### Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail; Rapone v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, 502 U.S. 367 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-954 and 90-1004.
**Decided:** January 15, 1992.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Rufo and Rapone are consolidated petitions presenting the same modification request; both judgments are vacated.

##### What change justifies modification of an institutional consent decree under Rule 60(b)(5)

**Holding:** Under Rule 60(b)(5), the party seeking to modify an institutional consent decree must prove a significant change in fact or law that makes continued enforcement inequitable; an unforeseeable catastrophe is not required. Actual anticipation and acceptance of the relevant risk impose a heavy burden to justify relief, and dissatisfaction, a new administration, or the availability of a cheaper constitutional alternative alone does not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; five of eight participating Justices support the exact proposition. O'Connor concurs in the judgment without joining this rationale; Thomas did not participate.

**Material application:** Officials reaffirmed the single-occupancy obligation in 1985 after the original 1979 decree. The centrality of that term and acceptance of known risks matter, but the lower court must assess inequity without treating unforeseeability as an absolute condition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Population growth, expense, and the need for the proposed design are asserted grounds, not Supreme Court findings satisfying the burden.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both First Circuit judgments are vacated and the cases remanded for the district court to determine whether proven changed circumstances make continued enforcement inequitable and whether the proposed modification responds suitably to those changes. The existing decree remains operative until lawfully modified.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Swift & Co.*, 286 U.S. 106 — its disposition is preserved; its demanding factual formulation is confined to its setting rather than imposed universally.
- *System Federation No. 91 v. Wright*, 364 U.S. 642 — applied to preserve equitable power to adjust continuing obligations to changed conditions.
- *United States v. United Shoe Machinery Corp.*, 391 U.S. 244 — applied to connect modification to the continuing decree's purpose and operation.
- *Board of Education v. Dowell*, 498 U.S. 237 — reinforces contextual treatment of institutional equitable relief; no school-desegregation termination rule is imported.

##### What fit and protections a justified consent-decree modification must preserve

**Holding:** A justified modification must be suitably tailored to the proven change and preserve lawful protection of the decree's remaining purposes; the court may not simply rewrite a consent decree to the constitutional minimum. Bell's clarification of constitutional limits does not by itself eliminate a voluntarily accepted single-cell obligation, and financial pressure cannot authorize unconstitutional confinement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; five of eight participating Justices support the exact proposition. O'Connor concurs in the judgment without joining this rationale; Thomas did not participate.

**Material application:** The district court may consider practical administration and cost in selecting lawful relief, while exercising its own equitable judgment; these considerations cannot replace proof of a qualifying change. Bell does not dissolve the officials' independently accepted single-cell promise.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court approves no double-cell design or number of cells and makes no determination that the proposed arrangement is constitutional or that remaining safeguards are adequate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Local No. 93, International Association of Firefighters v. Cleveland*, 478 U.S. 501 — consent obligations may exceed what constitutional liability alone would require.
- *Bell v. Wolfish*, 441 U.S. 520 — its constitutional treatment of double-celling does not erase a consensual single-cell requirement.
- *System Federation No. 91 v. Wright*, 364 U.S. 642 — preserves adjustment for changed circumstances while maintaining judicial control of lawful relief.

**Material application:** The purchasing record, the extraction tax, and evidence of available production capacity support an inference of lost severance receipts from compelled substitution away from Wyoming coal. The fiscal inference is supported on the summary-judgment record; it is not itself a stipulated fact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary injury, causation and redressability requirements apply. No sovereign exemption or standing based on unrelated future fiscal effects is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Maryland v. Louisiana*, 451 U.S. 725 — applied to a State's demonstrated injury from another State's commercial restriction.
- *Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission*, 432 U.S. 333 — geographic market exclusion is a concrete commercial injury; Wyoming still proves its own fiscal stake.
- *Warth v. Seldin*, 422 U.S. 490, and *Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization*, 426 U.S. 26 — ordinary personal-injury and causation requirements are preserved, not relaxed for States.

##### Whether this interstate fiscal controversy warrants exercise of original jurisdiction

**Holding:** The Court exercises original jurisdiction over this substantial interstate controversy because Wyoming's own fiscal injury and the challenged statewide mandate warrant direct resolution and no equivalent pending private action would fully resolve the State's claim. Exclusive jurisdiction under §1251(a) does not eliminate the Court's established discretion in administering its original docket.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices support this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The possibility of a producer's or utility's suit does not require surrender of this distinct state injury, but the holding does not promise an original forum for every indirect effect on tax receipts. Acceptance of the bill does not bar jurisdictional reconsideration on the developed record.

**Material judgment:** Original jurisdiction is exercised by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter. Scalia and Thomas would dismiss and do not reach the Commerce Clause merits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Massachusetts v. Missouri*, 308 U.S. 1 — applied for seriousness of the sovereign interest and adequacy of other available relief.
- *Texas v. New Mexico*, 462 U.S. 554 — preserved for administration of substantial interstate original controversies.
- *Illinois v. City of Milwaukee*, 406 U.S. 91 — alternative forums remain relevant; no equivalent proceeding resolves this State's injury here.
- *Arizona v. California*, 460 U.S. 605 — an earlier jurisdictional ruling is not an irrevocable bar to reconsidering jurisdiction on the developed record.

### Standing and personal injury

#### Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1424.
**Decided:** June 12, 1992.

##### What evidence of Article III injury, causation and redressability is required at summary judgment

**Holding:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury, a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant rather than independent third parties, and a likelihood that the requested relief will redress the injury. At summary judgment, the plaintiff must supply specific facts by affidavit or other evidence on the challenged elements; favorable reasonable inferences remain available, but allegations cannot substitute for the missing evidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the elements and litigation-stage evidentiary requirements. Stone concurs in the judgment without joining this section.

**Material application:** Earlier survival of the pleading stage does not establish the specific facts needed at summary judgment. The Court resolves this action on insufficient threatened personal injury.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The general redressability element controls. Its application to absent funding agencies and uncertain foreign-project responses appears only in the three-Justice Part III-B and is not controlling. Favorable reasonable inferences remain available; the decision does not replace Rule 56 or the separate APA programmatic-review holding of National Wildlife Federation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The merits declaration, mandatory rulemaking relief and dependent §1540(g)(4) fee award are vacated, and the action is remanded for jurisdictional dismissal. Six Justices support dismissal for lack of standing; seven support reversal, with Stevens relying on a noncontrolling statutory ground. No independent fee-entitlement rule or substitute award is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975): applied for the plaintiff's personal stake and burden to establish standing; its statutory-rights discussion remains subject to concrete injury.
- Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied for the constitutional elements and the limits on generalized judicial supervision.
- Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization, 426 U.S. 26 (1976): applied for fair traceability and likely redress; no new majority application concerning these absent agencies follows.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied for the evidentiary distinction between pleading and summary judgment; its separate APA programmatic-review holding is not enlarged.
- Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317 (1986), and Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242 (1986): applied to require evidence of challenged essential elements while preserving genuine material disputes and reasonable inferences.

##### Whether past visits, indefinite return intentions and general wildlife interests establish imminent personal injury

**Holding:** Aesthetic, recreational and professional interests in observing wildlife can support standing, but these plaintiffs must show threatened interference with their own concrete use. Prior visits and an indefinite intention to return, without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure, do not establish standing for these prospective claims; an ecosystem, animal or professional connection that supplies only general interest in distant species does not cure that defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, controls with five votes. Stone's separate concurrence supplies a sixth vote for the record-specific personal-injury conclusion without a formal opinion join.

**Material application:** The affidavits describe visits to Egyptian and Sri Lankan project areas but no sufficiently definite return undertaking or continuing activity showing imminent exposure. General ecosystem, animal and professional connections supply no missing evidence of these members' own threatened use.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document prerequisite is imposed. Actual use of an affected ecosystem and demonstrated injury transmitted across geographic distance remain undecided. Aesthetic, recreational and professional harm remains cognizable when it affects the claimant's own concrete activity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Jurisdictional dismissal leaves the statute's geographic reach unresolved and does not preclude a later action supported by sufficient standing facts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): applied; aesthetic injury is cognizable, but an organization's special interest cannot replace injury to a member.
- Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission, 432 U.S. 333 (1977): applied to require an injured member for this associational claim; no other associational requirement is changed.
- Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95 (1983): applied for the distinction between past exposure and a present or imminent injury supporting prospective relief.
- Whitmore v. Arkansas, 495 U.S. 149 (1990): applied for actual or imminent rather than conjectural injury.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied to specific affected use; no categorical bar to geographically transmitted injury is established.
- Japan Whaling Association v. American Cetacean Society, 478 U.S. 221 (1986): distinguished because its whale-watching and studying members alleged an adverse effect on their own activities; concrete professional or ecological injury remains possible.

##### Whether ESA citizen-suit permission and an asserted procedural right replace threatened concrete injury

**Holding:** ESA §11(g)'s authorization for any person to sue does not give Article III standing to a plaintiff asserting only the public's common interest in lawful administration. A procedural right may protect a plaintiff's threatened concrete interest without requiring proof that the procedure will certainly change the ultimate agency decision, but the procedural label cannot replace that personal interest; these respondents have not shown the required connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the citizen-suit and concrete-interest rule. Stone does not join this section.

**Material application:** Section 11(g), 16 U.S.C. §1540(g), permits enforcement but identifies no concrete injury to these members independent of the unsupported future use. The public's common interest in lawful administration cannot supply the missing personal connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may recognize concrete factual injuries previously inadequate in law; the outer reach of that power is not settled. Procedural protections tied to a threatened concrete interest remain cognizable without certainty of a changed ultimate decision. Neither §7(a)(2)'s geographic reach nor the binding effect of a revised rule on absent agencies is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The action is dismissed for want of Article III standing; no new consultation rule or cancellation of any foreign project is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fairchild v. Hughes, 258 U.S. 126 (1922): applied to reject a general right to compel lawful administration as a sufficient personal injury.
- Massachusetts v. Mellon, 262 U.S. 447 (1923), and Ex parte Levitt, 302 U.S. 633 (1937): applied for the distinction between direct injury and an undifferentiated public grievance.
- United States v. Richardson, 418 U.S. 166 (1974), and Schlesinger v. Reservists Committee to Stop the War, 418 U.S. 208 (1974): applied to generally shared governmental grievances without distinctive concrete harm.
- Valley Forge Christian College v. Americans United for Separation of Church and State, Inc., 454 U.S. 464 (1982), and Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied; a claimed right to lawful government conduct alone does not satisfy Article III.
- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975), and Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): preserved for Congress's power to recognize injuries and broaden protected interests while retaining the plaintiff's own concrete injury.
- Trafficante v. Metropolitan Life Insurance Co., 409 U.S. 205 (1972), and Hardin v. Kentucky Utilities Co., 390 U.S. 1 (1968): distinguished as statutory recognition of concrete personal interests, not authorization of a wholly abstract grievance.

##### Federal merits failure does not retroactively destroy jurisdiction

### Foreign sovereign commercial activity

#### Saudi Arabia v. Nelson, 507 U.S. 349 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-522.

**Decided:** March 23, 1993.

##### The first commercial-activity clause requires the action to rest on that activity

**Holding:** Under the first clause of 28 U.S.C. §1605(a)(2), commercial activity carried on in the United States—activity having substantial United States contact under §1603(e)—must supply the basis of the action, rather than merely precede the injury or establish a factual connection with it. The inquiry identifies the conduct constituting the elements that would entitle the plaintiff to relief; recruitment, a United States employment contract and a commercial retaliatory motive do not by themselves make the intentional detention-and-torture claims an action based on domestic commercial activity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion for the Court, excluding its warning paragraph, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: six. White and Blackmun agree in the intentional-claim judgment on a separate domestic-nexus ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, June 12, 1992, decision: applies the nature-of-conduct rule; distinguishes its third-clause direct-effect holding from the first-clause basis requirement.

##### Alleged abuse of sovereign police power is not commercial conduct

**Holding:** Arrest, imprisonment and torture alleged to have been inflicted through a foreign state's police authority are sovereign in nature for §1605(a)(2), even if undertaken to retaliate against a commercial employee's safety complaints. The asserted commercial-activity exception therefore does not support jurisdiction over these intentional-tort claims; this statutory immunity ruling neither legalizes the abuse nor decides an unasserted exception or liability of absent individual actors.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: six. Intentional-claim reversal receives eight judgment votes; White and Blackmun reach it without joining the sovereign-nature characterization.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, June 12, 1992, decision: applies nature rather than purpose; its market-participant distinction remains controlling.

##### Warning claims and absence of a controlling rationale

**Authority of the judgment:** The warning claims are dismissed under the asserted commercial-activity exception, 5–4: Souter, O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas and White support reversal; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy oppose that component. Souter’s four-Justice warning paragraph rejects relabeling the sovereign wrong. White instead treats retaliatory employment conduct as commercial but finds actionable conduct abroad and an insufficient domestic nexus. Neither rationale is a logical subset of the other, so Marks supplies no controlling general warning rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The judgment binds these parties but does not establish that every independently pleaded domestic recruitment-warning omission is barred. The opposing writings establish no jurisdictional predicates or merits findings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The first-clause basis inquiry is distinct from Weltover’s third-clause direct effects. No general rule requires every act in an action to be commercial, and no separate ruling on recruitment contacts is necessary. Other exceptions and absent individuals’ liability remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and require jurisdictional dismissal under the asserted exception: 8–1 for intentional claims and 5–4 for warning claims. No tort merits, duty, attribution, knowledge, causation, entity identity or preclusion determination follows.

### Forfeiture jurisdiction and effective monetary relief

#### Republic National Bank of Miami v. United States, 506 U.S. 80 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-767.

**Decided:** December 14, 1992.

##### Removal does not itself terminate perfected forfeiture jurisdiction

**Holding:** Once valid attachment has established in rem jurisdiction, removal of the property or substituted proceeds does not by itself defeat appellate jurisdiction. The bank’s failure to obtain a stay or bond therefore does not foreclose this appeal where a favorable judgment could still provide lawful effectual relief; an entirely useless judgment presents a distinct limit.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I, II and IV of Blackmun’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; eight Justices adopt this rule. Thomas supports the judgment on a different statutory ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- The Rio Grande, 23 Wall. 458 — applied; removal alone does not terminate jurisdiction properly acquired.
- The Little Charles, 26 F. Cas. 979 — applied; the ability to effectuate relief is distinct from uninterrupted custody.
- The Brig Ann, 9 Cranch 289 — distinguished; abandonment before institution differs from removal after valid attachment.
- Taylor v. Carryl, 20 How. 583 — distinguished; initial jurisdiction does not establish perpetual custody as a condition.
- Church of Scientology of California v. United States, November 16, 1992 — applied by analogy on effectual relief; its IRS-copy rule supplies no payment authority.

##### The return statute and judgment appropriation permit effective relief upon success

**Holding:** If the bank prevails in the underlying forfeiture and obtains the requisite judgment, 28 U.S.C. §2465 supplies a substantive right to return of the substituted proceeds; 31 U.S.C. §1304 and 28 U.S.C. §2414 supply the judgment-payment route when payment is not otherwise provided, the Comptroller General certifies payment, and the obligation satisfies the statutory final-judgment and payable-judgment requirements. That conditional lawful route defeats the Government’s claim that this appeal can produce no effective relief, without ordering payment now or recognizing judicial spending power apart from an applicable appropriation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s statutory-payment opinion, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies six express joins and independently controls. Stevens expressly accepts the same sufficient statutory proposition in his own writing, making seven supporters without an invented formal join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Office of Personnel Management v. Richmond, 496 U.S. 414 — applied; a substantive statutory entitlement and applicable appropriation, not equity alone, are required for this payment route.
- Knote v. United States, 95 U.S. 149 — preserved; the statutory resolution leaves the broader constitutional question about contested forfeiture proceeds undecided.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The bank must establish its entitlement; no payment source or appropriation-free constitutional return theory is decided. The Court leaves §1355(c)'s application to pre-enactment transfers or appeals unresolved. Pub. L. 102-550 §1521 independently added §1355(b)–(d) effective October 28, 1992: subsection (c) preserves jurisdiction upon removal by the prevailing party in a case in which a final civil-forfeiture order is appealed and, upon the appellant's motion, requires an order necessary to protect full value, which may include a stay or bond. This decision does not determine that amendment's temporal application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Eleventh Circuit jurisdictional dismissal and remand the forfeiture appeal; no immediate payment, ownership or lien decree.

##### The FTCA does not authorize this Antarctic tort action

**Holding:** The foreign-country exception in § 2680(k), read with the FTCA's enacted liability and place-of-law provisions, excludes this claim based entirely on acts or omissions in Antarctica even though no other sovereign is recognized there. - United States v. Kubrick, 444 U.S. 111 (1979): applied to respect the intended scope of the FTCA waiver without judicial expansion or contraction.

- United States v. Nordic Village, February 25, 1992: preserved at its actual scope concerning clear consent to affirmative bankruptcy monetary relief; it does not independently decide this different express waiver.
- Molzof v. United States, January 14, 1992: preserved; its construction of punitive damages and independent local-law entitlement do not establish Antarctic territorial coverage.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The claim concerns acts wholly in Antarctica. No blanket rule forecloses every offshore FTCA, constitutional or independently authorized remedy. ### Miranda habeas review and unpleaded coercion

#### Withrow v. Williams, 507 U.S. 680 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1030.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### A fair state opportunity does not itself bar Miranda habeas review

**Holding:** Powell’s prudential restriction on Fourth Amendment exclusionary claims does not extend to a state prisoner’s claim that the conviction rests on statements obtained in violation of Miranda. A full and fair state opportunity therefore does not itself foreclose that claim; ordinary habeas requirements, merits analysis and the governing harmless-error inquiry remain necessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, I–II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Powell, 428 U.S. 465 — distinguished and not extended; its actual prudential scope remains.
- Miranda, 384 U.S. 436 — preserved on habeas for a conviction resting on improperly obtained statements.
- Kimmelman, 477 U.S. 365; Jackson, 443 U.S. 307; Rose v. Mitchell, 443 U.S. 545 — applied as limits on extending Powell to distinct trial protections.
- Miller v. Fenton, 474 U.S. 104, and Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 — preserved: independent voluntariness review remains available on its own predicates.

##### An unpleaded, unconsented coercion theory requires fair litigation

**Holding:** A habeas court may not grant relief on the independent postwarning involuntariness theory presented here when it was neither pleaded nor tried by express or implied consent and the State lacked an opportunity to present relevant evidence. Litigation of Miranda custody and warning issues does not by itself supply consent; any later presentation remains subject to ordinary pleading, exhaustion, retroactivity and other applicable requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 — preserved: actual police coercion is necessary to due-process involuntariness; absent warnings alone do not establish it.
- Miller v. Fenton, 474 U.S. 104 — preserved: voluntariness requires its own legal determination on the proper record.
- Teague, 489 U.S. 288 — reserved here; no new exception or merits resolution of the retroactivity objection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Miranda and due-process involuntariness are independent inquiries. Rule 15(b) consent is absent on this record; custody/warning litigation alone does not provide it. Coercion, exhaustion and Teague questions remain for any lawfully presented claim. No automatic writ, universal prophylactic-safeguard review rule or reopening of Powell-barred search claims follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm availability of Miranda review; reverse the independent postwarning voluntariness ruling for lack of pleading, consent and fair opportunity to litigate; remand for proper merits and remedial analysis.

### Organizational eligibility for fee relief

#### Rowland v. California Men’s Colony, Unit II Men’s Advisory Council, 506 U.S. 194 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1188.

**Decided:** January 12, 1993.

##### Associations may apply

**Holding:** An association is not categorically excluded from then-effective §1915: the Dictionary Act’s inclusion of associations within person applies because §1915’s affidavit, financial-inability and counsel provisions can operate through ordinary agency and representation rules. The context exception remains operative, but these features do not establish an incompatible statutory setting or authorize a natural-person-only restriction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy: five votes for this construction and its operative limits. Kennedy’s separate account of permissible workability analysis adds no condition to the Court’s holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wilson v. Omaha Indian Tribe, 442 U.S. 653 (1979): applied for the Dictionary Act’s inclusive default subject to actual context; no requirement of an express contrary definition is created.
- Neitzke v. Williams, 490 U.S. 319 (1989): applied to preserve statutory construction against a policy substitution based on administrative convenience.
- Mallard v. United States District Court, 490 U.S. 296 (1989): preserved; §1915(d) permits a request for counsel and does not compel service.
- Osborn v. Bank of the United States, 22 U.S. (9 Wheat.) 738 (1824): preserved; an entity’s lawful counsel requirement is separate from financial eligibility.

##### Eligibility does not establish the showing

**Holding:** An eligible association must submit a properly authorized affidavit establishing its actual inability to pay costs or give security and its asserted entitlement to redress; the court may examine legally available resources and sham concealment without automatically attributing every member’s assets to it. Inability to hold an entity bank account or a balance-sheet deficit alone does not compel fee relief, so the Council’s application returns for the actual financial and representation determinations; §1915 neither licenses lay entity advocacy nor guarantees appointed counsel.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy: five votes for this construction and its operative limits. Kennedy’s separate account of permissible workability analysis adds no condition to the Court’s holding. The dissenters would require denial because of entity status and do not join the Court’s application process.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Adkins v. E. I. DuPont de Nemours & Co., 335 U.S. 331 (1948): applied to actual financial inability; its individual-applicant facts do not categorically exclude associations or prescribe a corporate insolvency test.
- Osborn and Mallard: preserved as distinct counsel and representation limits rather than grounds for automatic financial disqualification.
- Neitzke and Denton v. Hernandez (May 4, 1992): preserved for their respective legal and factual screening rules, with Denton’s abuse-of-discretion review and independent correction of legal error unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Relief remains discretionary after the statutory showing. The Council’s restriction on holding funds is relevant but does not preclude examination of resources lawfully available through direct payment. No automatic aggregation of members’ assets, universal organizational insolvency formula, lay entity advocacy, compelled counsel, subsidized-litigation constitutional right or underlying tobacco-policy merits ruling is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of categorical association ineligibility, vacate the automatic fee grant and remand for an authorized affidavit, actual resources, lawful representation and statutory screening.

### Pleading municipal civil-rights claims

#### Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit, 507 U.S. 163 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1657.

**Decided:** March 3, 1993.

##### The Federal Rules supply the pleading standard for municipal § 1983 claims

**Holding:** A federal court may not impose a judge-created heightened factual-particularity requirement on a § 1983 complaint against a municipality beyond the then-operative Federal Rules. Rule 8 governs the complaint, Rule 9(b)'s fraud-or-mistake categories do not create a municipal-liability category, and Monell and Canton retain their separate substantive requirements.

- Owen v. City of Independence, 445 U.S. 622 (1980): applied to reject municipal qualified immunity and the pleading argument built on it.

### Independent patent counterclaims and appellate vacatur

#### Cardinal Chemical Co. v. Morton International, Inc., 508 U.S. 83 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-114.
**Decided:** May 17, 1993.

##### An intermediate noninfringement ruling does not terminate counterclaim jurisdiction

**Holding:** The Federal Circuit’s affirmance of noninfringement does not deprive it of jurisdiction over a live, independent invalidity counterclaim merely because its own ruling would resolve the infringement claim. That intermediate ruling remains subject to this Court’s review and does not justify treating validity review as automatically advisory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Altvater v. Freeman, 319 U.S.359: applies independent-counterclaim distinction.
- Electrical Fittings Corp. v. Thomas & Betts Co., 307 U.S.241: distinguished; unnecessary affirmative-defense adjudication is different.

##### An established invalidity controversy does not disappear without a change

**Independent alternative holding:** Once an independent invalidity counterclaim presents an actual controversy, the party asserting later mootness must demonstrate a change that eliminates it. Affirmance of noninfringement alone does not establish that change or authorize automatic vacatur of the invalidity judgment.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Altvater v. Freeman, 319 U.S.359: applies independent-counterclaim distinction.
- Aetna Life Insurance Co. v. Haworth, 300 U.S.227, and United States v. W.T.Grant Co., 345 U.S.629: distinguishes initial controversy burden from later mootness burden.
- United States v. Munsingwear, Inc., 340 U.S.36: actual intervening mootness rule preserved; automatic application rejected.
- INS v. Chadha, 462 U.S.919: agreement on one legal issue does not eliminate underlying adversity.

##### The automatic practice cannot be saved as discretion on this record

**Holding:** The Federal Circuit may not routinely vacate a live invalidity judgment merely because it affirms noninfringement, nor save that same automatic practice by calling it discretionary. On this record the validity appeal should be considered; unusual case-specific grounds for declining review or actual mootness remain available, but no universal duty to decide validity in every patent appeal is imposed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual later mootness and lawful unusual-case discretion remain available; no universal command to decide validity, technical validity ruling or fee ruling follows. The first two jurisdictional grounds independently defeat automatic mootness; the seven-Justice discretionary holding is distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate only the vacatur of the invalidity judgment and remand for consideration of the live validity appeal on this record; the noninfringement affirmance remains intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Blonder-Tongue Laboratories, Inc. v. University of Illinois Foundation, 402 U.S.313: applies distinct public and preclusion significance of invalidity.
- Altvater, 319 U.S.359: independent judgment cannot be discarded solely because infringement fails.

### Concurrent claims against the United States

#### Keene Corp. v. United States, 508 U.S. 200 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-166.
**Decided:** May 24, 1993.

##### Section1500 jurisdiction is measured when the action is filed

**Holding:** Section1500 bars the reviewed Claims Court action when, at filing, a suit for or in respect to the same claim is pending elsewhere against the United States or a covered federal actor; later dismissal of that other suit does not cure the original action’s jurisdictional defect. Claims Court decisions inconsistent with that filing-time requirement cannot support jurisdiction here; the Court does not decide the reversed order of filing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Corona Coal Co. v. United States, 263 U.S.537: statutory pendency bar applied.
- Brown v. United States, 175 Ct.Cl.343, 358 F.2d1002, and Hossein v. United States, 218 Ct.Cl.727: disapproved insofar as inconsistent with filing-time jurisdiction.
- Tecon Engineers, Inc. v. United States, 170 Ct.Cl.389, 343 F.2d943: reversed filing sequence reserved, not overruled here.

##### Overlapping facts and relief cannot be separated merely by changing legal theories

**Holding:** For the claims presented, §1500 applies when the suits arise from substantially the same operative facts and seek overlapping relief, even if they plead different legal theories or those theories cannot be brought in the same forum. **Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

- Casman v. United States, 135 Ct.Cl.647, and Boston Five Cents Savings Bank v. United States, 864 F.2d137: wholly different relief question reserved, not overruled here.

##### The dismissals do not depend on a new rule requiring prospective-only treatment

**Holding:** Keene’s reviewed suits fail under established filing-time and factual-identity principles; the Federal Circuit’s broader repudiation of precedents unnecessary to this result does not make the controlling rule new as applied here. **Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Claims Court-first filing, wholly different relief, future-action tolling and general pure prospectivity remain unresolved. The holding does not adopt the Federal Circuit’s broader repudiation of those distinct precedents.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm jurisdictional dismissal of Keene I and Keene II; later termination elsewhere does not revive these actions. No asbestos merits, indemnity, compensation, transfer or new filing opportunity is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron Oil Co. v. Huson, 404 U.S.97: prospective-only request rejected at its new-rule premise; general availability not decided.
- Corona Coal Co. v. United States, 263 U.S.537, and British American Tobacco Co. v. United States, 89 Ct.Cl.438: existing governing principles applied.

- McKesson, 496 U.S.18 — pay-first systems require meaningful backward-looking relief; alternatives preserved.
- Davis, 489 U.S.803 — correction of unequal taxation distinguished from a particular refund formula.
- Beam, 501 U.S.529 — procedural and remedial questions kept distinct from temporal governing law.

### Social Security remands and EAJA fees

#### Shalala v. Schaefer, 509 U.S. 292 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-311.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### Sentence-four judgment ends the action and confers prevailing status

**Holding:** A sentence-four judgment reversing the Secretary’s denial of benefits with a remand ends the §405(g) civil action and makes the successful claimant a prevailing party; the court must enter judgment rather than retain the merits action pending an administrative award. Sentence-six remands instead retain jurisdiction for the statutorily specified proceedings and later return to court.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sullivan v. Finkelstein, 496 U.S.617, and Melkonyan v. Sullivan, 501 U.S.89: the two statutory remand routes applied.
- Texas State Teachers Assn. v. Garland Independent School District, 489 U.S.782, and Farrar, December 14, 1992: enforceable judicial relief establishes prevailing status, not automatic fee amount.

##### The missing separate judgment prevents the fee deadline from expiring

**Holding:** Under the then-effective rules, EAJA’s thirty-day application period follows a properly entered judgment and expiration of the applicable sixty-day appeal period; the absence of the required Rule 58 separate judgment prevents that deadline from expiring. Schaefer’s application is timely although the sentence-four remand was itself a final merits disposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Indrelunas, 411 U.S.216: separate-document requirement applied as then in force.
- Bankers Trust Co. v. Mallis, 435 U.S.381: ability to take an appeal without the document distinguished from expiration of appellate time.
- Melkonyan, 501 U.S.89: final court judgment, not agency decision, governs the EAJA clock.

##### Hudson does not preserve administrative-fee coverage after an ordinary terminated sentence-four action

**Holding:** Hudson’s integrated administrative-proceeding fee rationale does not apply to a sentence-four remand properly entered without retained jurisdiction, or to retention of jurisdiction that is challenged. It remains applicable to qualifying sentence-six proceedings in which the court retains the action, subject to EAJA’s other requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Sentence six permits a pre-answer remand on the Secretary’s good-cause motion, or new material evidence with good cause for earlier omission, retaining jurisdiction for return and later judgment. Prevailing status does not establish every EAJA condition or amount. No later deemed-entry deadline is imported.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the timeliness determination and reviewed award; enter the required separate judgment where still absent. No new administrative-services award or reopening of unreviewed fee issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sullivan v. Hudson, 490 U.S.877: retained-jurisdiction rationale limited in light of the statutory distinction; sentence-six coverage preserved.
- Finkelstein, 496 U.S.617, and Melkonyan, 501 U.S.89: sentence-four termination controls; statutory retention is reserved for sentence six.

### Review of federal preclearance determinations

#### Shaw v. Reno, 509 U.S. 630 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-357.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993, No. 92-357, see Election Law — Territorial racial design and constitutional deprivation.

##### Federal preclearance dismissal remains separate

**Holding:** The district court outside the District of Columbia cannot use this action to review the Attorney General’s preclearance determination under the Voting Rights Act; § 14(b)’s designated channel and Morris’s restriction on review leave dismissal of the federal defendants undisturbed. That disposition does not immunize the State’s plan from an otherwise cognizable constitutional or statutory challenge.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Preclearance is neither a command to maximize majority-minority districts nor a certificate of compliance with every law. The federal disposition does not immunize the State’s plan against an independently cognizable challenge.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Leave dismissal of the federal officials undisturbed, 9–0, under §14(b)’s designated channel and Morris’s nonreviewability rule; no alternative merits defense or order to approve another map is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morris v. Gressette, 432 U.S.491: restriction on reviewing the Attorney General’s preclearance determination applied; independent challenges to the state enactment remain available under their own rules.
- Beer v. United States, 425 U.S.130: nonretrogression distinguished from maximization or certification of compliance with every law.

### Scientific expert evidence

#### Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, 509 U.S. 579 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-102.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

##### The enacted Rules displace mandatory general acceptance

**Holding:** Frye’s mandatory general-acceptance prerequisite does not govern admission of scientific expert evidence in federal trials under the Federal Rules of Evidence. Acceptance remains potentially relevant under Rule 702, but its absence does not alone require exclusion and its presence does not dispense with the Rules’ other requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Frye v. United States, 293 F.1013: displaced as a mandatory prerequisite in federal trials; not a Supreme Court Rule 702 holding being overruled.
- Beech Aircraft v. Rainey, 488 U.S.153: statutory interpretation of enacted Rules applied.
- United States v. Abel, 469 U.S.45: compatible common-law guidance retained, incompatible prerequisite rejected.
- Bourjaily v. United States, 483 U.S.171: Rules supersede incompatible common law.

##### Reliable scientific foundation and fit require flexible preliminary screening

**Holding:** Under original Rules 104(a) and 702, the proponent must establish by a preponderance the preliminary foundation for scientifically reliable knowledge that fits the disputed factual question and will assist the factfinder. The judge examines principles, methodology and valid application using relevant nonexclusive considerations—testability and testing, peer review/publication, meaningful error information and operational standards, and acceptance—without making any one consideration an invariant prerequisite; admissibility does not determine ultimate causation or sufficiency.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The original Rules govern. No later Rule702 text, universal technical-specialty rule, appellate-review standard or substantive causation finding is adopted. Cross-examination supplements screening for admissible evidence; Rules403, 703, 706 and ordinary sufficiency rules remain available.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Frye-based summary judgment and remand for evaluation of material proffers and reconsideration of summary judgment. Further fair foundation submissions may be received; no direction to admit every expert.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bourjaily v. United States, 483 U.S.171, 175–176: preponderance for preliminary admissibility applied, not a substantive causation burden.
- Rock v. Arkansas, 483 U.S.44: adversarial testing remains appropriate for admissible evidence; no excuse for unsupported foundation.
- United States v. Downing, 753 F.2d 1224: helpful fit and flexible reliability discussion informs interpretation, not a binding Supreme Court multifactor checklist.
