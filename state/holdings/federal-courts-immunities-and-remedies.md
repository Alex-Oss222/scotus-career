## Federal Courts: Immunities and Remedies

### Federal sovereign immunity

#### United States v. Nordic Village, Inc., 503 U.S. 30 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1629.
**Decided:** February 25, 1992.

##### Whether original Bankruptcy Code §106(c) waives immunity from this affirmative personal money judgment

**Holding:** Original Bankruptcy Code §106(c) does not unequivocally waive the United States’ immunity from an affirmative personal money judgment recovering an unauthorized transfer under §550. A plausible construction permitting binding determinations of governmental rights and estate liabilities without an affirmative payment order defeats the required clear consent to this monetary remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices control the statutory threshold.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for original §106(c) and the personal monetary remedy adjudicated.

**Material application:** The trustee avoided an unauthorized transfer under §549 and sought $20,000 from the United States under §550 after corporate money paid an officer’s individual taxes. Ordinary recovery authority under §550 does not answer the separate sovereign-consent question; no claim-linked waiver under §106(a) or (b) supplies this judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not validate the diversion, strip §106(c) of effect, decide state constitutional immunity, or construe a different or later waiver. It does not alter §106(a) or (b); neither subsection supplies consent to the actual judgment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit’s judgment is reversed and the $20,000 personal money judgment set aside for want of sovereign consent. No substitute award is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs*, 498 U.S. 89; *United States v. Mitchell*, 445 U.S. 535; and *United States v. King*, 395 U.S. 1, supply the requirement of unequivocal consent to the particular monetary remedy. *Hoffman v. Connecticut Department of Income Maintenance*, 492 U.S. 96, supplies plurality statutory reasoning adopted for this federal question; its fractured state judgment is not a controlling federal-waiver holding. *Ruckelshaus v. Sierra Club*, 463 U.S. 680, and *McMahon v. United States*, 342 U.S. 25, prevent enlarging consent beyond Congress’s language. *United States v. Menasche*, 348 U.S. 528, supports giving both paragraphs of §106(c) operative work. *Dellmuth v. Muth*, 491 U.S. 223, supplies textual clear-statement reasoning without a new ruling about States.

##### Whether bankruptcy jurisdiction, property and trust characterizations, or equitable recoupment sustain this money judgment

**Holding:** A general grant of jurisdiction over bankruptcy-estate property in §1334(d), an asserted trust interest, or the origin of transferred money in estate assets does not supply federal consent to this personal money judgment. Because the trustee seeks a sum rather than identified property under the court’s control, this order cannot be sustained as a genuine property adjudication or as equitable recoupment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; the same seven Justices reject each alternative offered to sustain the monetary judgment.

**Material application:** The order imposes a general obligation to pay $20,000. No identified property or fund supports it, and the relief is an affirmative recovery rather than defensive recoupment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A genuine specific-property proceeding requires its own facts and authority and is not adjudicated here. No traceable fund is assumed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The alternative grounds do not preserve the personal money judgment. Genuinely distinct, properly supported remedies remain for ordinary proceedings; none is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Blatchford v. Native Village of Noatak*, 501 U.S. 775, distinguishes subject-matter jurisdiction from immunity. *United States v. Whiting Pools, Inc.*, 462 U.S. 198, is distinguished because tangible debtor-property turnover does not authorize this Treasury payment. *Begier v. IRS*, 496 U.S. 53, does not supply an identified fund here. *United States v. Shaw*, 309 U.S. 495, is preserved against inferring monetary consent from a property characterization. *Bull v. United States*, 295 U.S. 247, and *United States v. Dalm*, 494 U.S. 596, preserve recoupment’s limited defensive operation, which does not sustain this affirmative award.

#### United States Department of Energy v. Ohio, 503 U.S. 607 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-1341 and 90-1517.
**Decided:** April 21, 1992.

##### Whether the Clean Water Act and Resource Conservation and Recovery Act citizen-suit provisions waive immunity from the requested punitive fines

**Holding:** The CWA and RCRA citizen-suit provisions authorize proceedings against the United States but do not unequivocally expose it to the punitive civil penalties incorporated from their separate enforcement provisions. Those incorporations include the limits of the penalty provisions, whose generally defined liable persons exclude the United States.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter, Part II-A, joined by Stone, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; six votes control. White, Blackmun and Stevens dissent on this issue.

**Material application:** The express inclusion of the United States among defendants does not alter the separate incorporated penalty provisions, whose general definitions of liable persons omit the United States.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling concerns the provisions effective April 21, 1992 and the requested punitive fines. It decides no later amendment, other express waiver, particular coercive contempt sanction, settled pollution allegation, independent cause of action or fine amount. The stayed technical-study claim is unaffected.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The consolidated judgment is reversed and remanded. In No. 90-1341, the CWA facilities and RCRA citizen-suit punitive-waiver rulings are reversed, 6–3. In No. 90-1517, rejection of RCRA facilities waiver is sustained, 9–0, and the CWA citizen-suit alternative is rejected, 6–3. The contingent amount stipulation supplies no independent liability and operates only if a lawful remaining basis warrants it. Clear compliance duties and coercive relief remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Mitchell, 445 U.S. 535 — applied: federal consent to the asserted liability must be unequivocal.
- United States v. Nordic Village, Inc., decision of February 25, 1992 — applied: a plausible narrower construction defeats asserted consent to the particular affirmative monetary remedy.
- Engel v. Davenport, 271 U.S. 33 — applied: statutory incorporation includes the referenced provision’s limitations.
- Ruckelshaus v. Sierra Club, 463 U.S. 680, and McMahon v. United States, 342 U.S. 25 — applied: waiver is not enlarged beyond what the enacted language requires.

##### Whether the Clean Water Act federal-facilities process-and-sanctions language waives immunity from past-violation punishment

**Holding:** The CWA federal-facilities provision’s submission to requirements, process and sanctions does not unequivocally waive immunity from the punitive fines sought for completed violations. In its statutory context, sanctions directed to enforcing process support coercive compliance relief without clearly adding retrospective punishment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter, Part II-B.1, joined by Stone, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; six votes control. White, Blackmun and Stevens dissent.

**Material application:** The statute twice links sanctions to process. That context permits the narrower reading of coercive measures securing compliance rather than punishment for completed substantive violations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling concerns the provisions effective April 21, 1992 and the requested punitive fines. It decides no later amendment, other express waiver, particular coercive contempt sanction, settled pollution allegation, independent cause of action or fine amount. The stayed technical-study claim is unaffected.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The consolidated judgment is reversed and remanded. In No. 90-1341, the CWA facilities and RCRA citizen-suit punitive-waiver rulings are reversed, 6–3. In No. 90-1517, rejection of RCRA facilities waiver is sustained, 9–0, and the CWA citizen-suit alternative is rejected, 6–3. The contingent amount stipulation supplies no independent liability and operates only if a lawful remaining basis warrants it. Clear compliance duties and coercive relief remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Penfield Co. of California v. SEC, 330 U.S. 585 — applied to distinguish coercive compliance sanctions from punishment.
- Hicks v. Feiock, 485 U.S. 624 — applied to the conditional character of coercive sanctions, without deciding a contempt case here.
- Shell Oil Co. v. Iowa Department of Revenue, 488 U.S. 19 — applied: statutory context determines the relevant meaning.
- United States v. Mitchell, 445 U.S. 535, and United States v. Nordic Village, Inc., February 25, 1992 — applied: ambiguity about the particular punitive remedy defeats the claimed waiver.

##### Whether EPA approval makes state penalties arise under federal law for the Clean Water Act waiver

**Holding:** State-law punitive penalties do not arise under federal law for the CWA federal-facilities waiver merely because EPA approved the state pollution program. The provision’s qualified reference to civil penalties, read with its coercive-enforcement language and the separate penalty definitions, does not unequivocally authorize the requested punitive fines under either asserted source.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter, Part II-B.2, joined by Stone, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; six votes control. White, Blackmun and Stevens dissent.

**Material application:** Federal approval does not transform Ohio’s penalty statute into a federal source of liability. The reference to civil penalties does not independently enlarge the incorporated federal penalty definition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling concerns the provisions effective April 21, 1992 and the requested punitive fines. It decides no later amendment, other express waiver, particular coercive contempt sanction, settled pollution allegation, independent cause of action or fine amount. The stayed technical-study claim is unaffected.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The consolidated judgment is reversed and remanded. In No. 90-1341, the CWA facilities and RCRA citizen-suit punitive-waiver rulings are reversed, 6–3. In No. 90-1517, rejection of RCRA facilities waiver is sustained, 9–0, and the CWA citizen-suit alternative is rejected, 6–3. The contingent amount stipulation supplies no independent liability and operates only if a lawful remaining basis warrants it. Clear compliance duties and coercive relief remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gully v. First National Bank in Meridian, 299 U.S. 109 — applied by statutory analogy: federal permission does not make a state-law claim arise under federal law.
- International Bridge Co. v. New York, 254 U.S. 126 — applied: congressional approval does not itself create the state-law right.
- ICC v. Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers, 482 U.S. 270 — applied: familiar statutory language may carry its established construction into another statute.
- Arkansas v. Oklahoma, decision of February 26, 1992 — distinguished: federal character of permit administration does not determine punitive-waiver scope or convert every state penalty into federal law.
- United States v. Mitchell, 445 U.S. 535, and United States v. Nordic Village, Inc., February 25, 1992 — applied: uncertain implications do not expand a clearer, narrower consent.

##### Whether the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act federal-facilities provision waives immunity from punitive fines

**Holding:** RCRA’s federal-facilities provision does not unequivocally waive federal immunity from punitive fines for past violations. Its submission to substantive and procedural requirements, including permits, reporting, injunctive relief and sanctions enforcing that relief, reaches compliance mechanisms without clearly authorizing retrospective penalties.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter, Part II-C, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine Justices join this holding.

**Material application:** Permitting, reporting, injunctions and sanctions enforcing injunctions address compliance; the text identifies no unequivocal mechanism for punishing completed violations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling concerns the provisions effective April 21, 1992 and the requested punitive fines. It decides no later amendment, other express waiver, particular coercive contempt sanction, settled pollution allegation, independent cause of action or fine amount. The stayed technical-study claim is unaffected.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The consolidated judgment is reversed and remanded. In No. 90-1341, the CWA facilities and RCRA citizen-suit punitive-waiver rulings are reversed, 6–3. In No. 90-1517, rejection of RCRA facilities waiver is sustained, 9–0, and the CWA citizen-suit alternative is rejected, 6–3. The contingent amount stipulation supplies no independent liability and operates only if a lawful remaining basis warrants it. Clear compliance duties and coercive relief remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mitzelfelt v. Department of Air Force, 903 F.2d 1293 — adopted on this point: requirements can encompass standards and their implementation without punitive measures.
- Hancock v. Train, 426 U.S. 167 — distinguished: subjection to permitting procedures does not determine the scope of punitive remedies; amended statutory language receives its enacted effect.
- EPA v. California, 426 U.S. 200 — retained as pre-amendment statutory context, not authority to disregard the later express compliance duties.
- United States v. Mitchell, 445 U.S. 535 — applied: no unequivocal consent to punitive liability appears in the specified injunctive-enforcement language.

#### Lane v. Peña, 518 U.S. 187 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-365.
**Decided:** June 20, 1996.

##### Whether the Rehabilitation Act waives compensatory-damages immunity for the federally conducted academy program

**Holding:** Sections 504(a) and 505 of the Rehabilitation Act, read with §1003 of the Rehabilitation Act Amendments of 1986, do not unequivocally waive the United States’ immunity from Lane’s compensatory-damages claim arising from this federally conducted academy program. The substantive nondiscrimination duty does not itself supply consent to the particular Treasury remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices.

**Material application:** Section 505(a)(2)’s Title VI remedies cover recipients or federal providers of financial assistance; that formulation does not unequivocally reach every federal program operator. Section 505(a)(1)’s Title VII channel concerns §501 federal employment. Section 505(b) permits reasonable attorney’s fees as costs, in the court’s discretion, to a prevailing party other than the United States. Section 1003 removes specified State immunity; its public-entity comparison can reach nonfederal recipients and supplies no independent general federal damages consent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No differently situated federal assistance-provider claim, fee application, officer action or implied damages cause is decided. Franklin’s intentional-discrimination, recipient-responsibility, causation and compensable-loss conditions are not decided because waiver fails.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Damages rejection affirmed, 7–2; the discrimination determination and unchallenged reinstatement remain undisturbed. No damages calculation or new trial.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **United States v. Nordic Village, Inc., 503 U.S. 30 (1992):** Applies the requirement of unequivocal enacted consent to the particular affirmative monetary remedy; a plausible narrower statutory operation defeats the asserted waiver. The original §106(c) holding and its limits remain unchanged.
- **United States Department of Energy v. Ohio, 503 U.S. 607 (1992):** Applies the distinction between federal substantive or adjudicatory coverage and the limits of incorporated monetary-remedy provisions; no pollution-remedy question is reopened.
- **Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992):** Preserves appropriate relief in the established Title IX action and distinguishes that remedial inquiry from federal sovereign consent. Compensation still requires proved intentional discrimination, recipient responsibility, causation and compensable loss; their satisfaction is not decided here.
- **FDIC v. Meyer, 510 U.S. 471 (1994):** Preserves the separate inquiries into waiver, substantive cause and recovery, including the effect of a genuinely broad sue-and-be-sued clause. No such clause supplies this academy claim's waiver; no agency Bivens question is decided.

### Foreign sovereign immunity

#### Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, Inc., 504 U.S. 607 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-763.
**Decided:** June 12, 1992.

##### Whether negotiable sovereign debt issued and rescheduled for a monetary program is commercial activity under the FSIA

**Holding:** Under 28 U.S.C. §§1603(d) and 1605(a)(2), an activity is commercial when its nature is the kind of conduct a private participant in a market can undertake; the sovereign's public purpose does not control that characterization. Argentina and Banco Central acted commercially in issuing and rescheduling these negotiable, dollar-denominated debt instruments, even though the instruments served a foreign-exchange program and refinanced existing obligations.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this proposition. Stone concurs separately in the judgment without joining the opinion.

**Material application:** Negotiable dollar repayment promises bearing market-linked interest are means private borrowers can use. The foreign-exchange objective and refinancing of existing debt concern purpose and do not change the instruments' commercial nature.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling concerns these instruments and this rescheduling. It does not make all public borrowing, currency regulation, expropriation or sovereign activity affecting money commercial. The second-clause domestic-act theory is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The denial of FSIA dismissal is affirmed; ordinary contract proceedings continue. No contract liability, damages, attachment or execution is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Verlinden B. V. v. Central Bank of Nigeria, 461 U.S. 480 (1983): applied for the restrictive-immunity framework and statutory exception channel; no additional citizenship or commercial-purpose restriction is derived from it.

##### Whether failure to make the contractually selected New York payment creates a direct effect in the United States

**Holding:** For the third clause of §1605(a)(2), an effect is direct when it follows as an immediate consequence of the defendant's activity; the statute adds no separate substantiality or foreseeability requirement, although wholly trivial effects remain outside the ordinary reach of the exception. Failure to make payment in New York, after the holders validly exercised the instruments' contractual payment option, produces the required direct effect in the United States even though the holders are foreign and their ultimate economic loss is abroad.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this proposition. Stone concurs separately in the judgment without joining the opinion.

**Material application:** The holders validly selected New York under the instruments, and previous payments credited their accounts there. The promised dollars failed to arrive at the agreed place of performance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Wholly trivial effects remain outside the exception's ordinary reach. New York's financial-center reputation is too remote to establish the required effect. Other payment arrangements and merely incidental domestic financial connections remain open; foreign plaintiffs' ultimate losses abroad do not negate this agreed domestic performance obligation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** This domestic nonpayment satisfies the third clause of §1605(a)(2), sustaining the denial of immunity-based dismissal without deciding contract merits or asset immunity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Verlinden B. V. v. Central Bank of Nigeria, 461 U.S. 480 (1983): applied to permit jurisdiction over qualifying foreign-plaintiff actions; its general jurisdictional discussion is not a substantiality or foreseeability test for this clause.
- The Second Circuit's immediate-consequence formulation in the judgment below is sustained; its additional substantiality/foreseeability formulation is rejected, and its financial-center-reputation rationale supplies no controlling ground.

##### Whether the assumed constitutional-contacts objection requires a narrower reading of the direct-effect exception

**Holding:** Assuming solely for Argentina's interpretive objection that a foreign state receives the relevant constitutional protection, Argentina's purposeful dollar debt and offered, selected and previously used New York payment arrangements provide sufficient transaction-related contacts to defeat the asserted constitutional doubt. Constitutional avoidance therefore does not justify narrowing the statutory direct-effect clause in this case; the Court decides neither foreign-sovereign constitutional personhood nor an independent constitutional personal-jurisdiction claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this proposition. Stone concurs separately in the judgment without joining the opinion.

**Material application:** Argentina purposefully offered New York payment in dollar instruments, honored that selection before the dispute and then failed to perform there; the connection is more than foreseeable routing through an American bank.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Foreign-state constitutional personhood is assumed solely to answer the interpretive contention and remains undecided. No independent constitutional personal-jurisdiction claim, jurisdiction over unrelated sovereign conduct, or alternative subject-matter jurisdiction ground is adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The constitutional-avoidance objection supplies no ground to narrow the statutory exception or dismiss this action; undecided contract defenses and asset-immunity questions remain available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- International Shoe Co. v. Washington, 326 U.S. 310 (1945): applied on an express assumption of constitutional protection, for purposeful contacts related to this dispute; no holding extends constitutional personhood to foreign states.

### Prospective equitable relief

#### Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail; Rapone v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, 502 U.S. 367 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-954 and 90-1004.
**Decided:** January 15, 1992.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Rufo and Rapone are consolidated petitions presenting the same modification request; both judgments are vacated.

##### What change justifies modification of an institutional consent decree under Rule 60(b)(5)

**Holding:** Under Rule 60(b)(5), the party seeking to modify an institutional consent decree must prove a significant change in fact or law that makes continued enforcement inequitable; an unforeseeable catastrophe is not required. Actual anticipation and acceptance of the relevant risk impose a heavy burden to justify relief, and dissatisfaction, a new administration, or the availability of a cheaper constitutional alternative alone does not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; five of eight participating Justices support the exact proposition. O'Connor concurs in the judgment without joining this rationale; Thomas did not participate.

**Current-force modification:** The Rule 60(b)(5) holding survives, but PLRA §802 supplies independent routes for federal-law civil prison-conditions relief, excluding habeas challenges to the fact or duration of confinement and expressly including prospective relief granted before enactment. Under §3626(b)(1), relief is terminable on a party's or intervener's motion two years after grant or approval, one year after denial of termination under that paragraph, or, for an order issued on or before enactment, two years after enactment. Section 3626(b)(2) entitles a defendant or intervener to immediate termination of prospective relief granted without findings that it is narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary to correct the federal violation and the least intrusive necessary means. The original §3626(b)(3) prevents termination when record-based written findings establish that relief remains necessary to correct a current or ongoing federal violation, is no broader than necessary, and is narrowly drawn and least intrusive. This current-or-ongoing formulation is preserved as enacted. Under original §3626(e)(2), relief subject to a pending motion is automatically stayed from day 30 for a (b)(1) or (b)(2) motion, or day 180 for a motion under other law, until final ruling. Admission establishes no motion, missing finding, termination or elapsed stay period; relief entered by a state court solely on state-law claims is outside §3626's limitations. The former equitable rule cannot alone foreclose these independent statutory avenues.

**Material application:** Officials reaffirmed the single-occupancy obligation in 1985 after the original 1979 decree. The centrality of that term and acceptance of known risks matter, but the lower court must assess inequity without treating unforeseeability as an absolute condition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Population growth, expense, and the need for the proposed design are asserted grounds, not Supreme Court findings satisfying the burden. Modification under Rule 60(b)(5) concerns the continuing prospective operation of an existing institutional decree following a demonstrated significant change in fact or law. It does not establish a legislative power to compel reinstatement of a completed private damages or limitations judgment, nor does it decide whether a separate valid statutory remedy may provide collateral relief from such a judgment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both First Circuit judgments are vacated and the cases remanded for the district court to determine whether proven changed circumstances make continued enforcement inequitable and whether the proposed modification responds suitably to those changes. The existing decree remains operative until lawfully modified.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Swift & Co.*, 286 U.S. 106 — its disposition is preserved; its demanding factual formulation is confined to its setting rather than imposed universally.
- *System Federation No. 91 v. Wright*, 364 U.S. 642 — applied to preserve equitable power to adjust continuing obligations to changed conditions.
- *United States v. United Shoe Machinery Corp.*, 391 U.S. 244 — applied to connect modification to the continuing decree's purpose and operation.
- *Board of Education v. Dowell*, 498 U.S. 237 — reinforces contextual treatment of institutional equitable relief; no school-desegregation termination rule is imported.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Prison Litigation Reform Act of 1995, Pub. L. 104-134, Title VIII, enacted April 26, 1996: §802, independent §3626(b)(1)–(2) termination, (b)(3) limitation, (d) state-law exception, original (e)(2) stay and §802(b)(1) enactment transition. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

##### What fit and protections a justified consent-decree modification must preserve

**Holding:** A justified modification must be suitably tailored to the proven change and preserve lawful protection of the decree's remaining purposes; the court may not simply rewrite a consent decree to the constitutional minimum. Bell's clarification of constitutional limits does not by itself eliminate a voluntarily accepted single-cell obligation, and financial pressure cannot authorize unconstitutional confinement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; five of eight participating Justices support the exact proposition. O'Connor concurs in the judgment without joining this rationale; Thomas did not participate.

**Current-force modification:** Rufo's equitable tailoring rule remains controlling where its premise is otherwise lawful. For covered prison-conditions relief, PLRA §802 additionally requires findings before entry or approval of a consent decree that the prospective relief is narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary to correct the particular plaintiff's federal violation and the least intrusive necessary means, with substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice effects. Relief requiring or permitting officials to exceed state or local authority, or otherwise violating state or local law, additionally requires that federal law permit that contrary relief, that it be necessary to correct the federal violation and that no other remedy correct the violation. The original current-or-ongoing-violation limitation on statutory termination appears in the preceding question. A consensual obligation exceeding the constitutional minimum does not by itself defeat these statutory conditions. Private settlements not judicially enforceable beyond reinstatement of the settled proceeding remain distinct, and their breach may be pursued through available state-law remedies; relief entered by a state court solely under state law retains §3626(d)'s exception. No decree is dissolved by this source admission.

**Material application:** The district court may consider practical administration and cost in selecting lawful relief, while exercising its own equitable judgment; these considerations cannot replace proof of a qualifying change. Bell does not dissolve the officials' independently accepted single-cell promise.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court approves no double-cell design or number of cells and makes no determination that the proposed arrangement is constitutional or that remaining safeguards are adequate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Local No. 93, International Association of Firefighters v. Cleveland*, 478 U.S. 501 — consent obligations may exceed what constitutional liability alone would require.
- *Bell v. Wolfish*, 441 U.S. 520 — its constitutional treatment of double-celling does not erase a consensual single-cell requirement.
- *System Federation No. 91 v. Wright*, 364 U.S. 642 — preserves adjustment for changed circumstances while maintaining judicial control of lawful relief.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Prison Litigation Reform Act of 1995, Pub. L. 104-134, Title VIII, enacted April 26, 1996: §802, consent-decree limits, private-settlement distinction and surviving equitable rule. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

#### Morales v. Trans World Airlines, Inc., 504 U.S. 374 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1604.
**Decided:** June 1, 1992.

For the direct-reference and independently sufficient fare-marketing-effects grounds of preemption in *Morales v. Trans World Airlines, Inc.*, June 1, 1992, No. 90-1604, see Constitutional Structure — Federal preemption.

##### Whether threatened fare-guideline enforcement permits federal prospective relief before the airlines expose themselves to repeated sanctions

**Holding:** Federal prospective relief may protect a party against imminent state enforcement forbidden by federal law when repeated serious sanctions make awaiting enforcement an inadequate practical remedy. Texas's announced enforcement of these fare guidelines presents that condition; preemption alone does not dispense with the required threatened injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five of eight participants. Stone states a compatible equitable ground separately without joining the opinion; Souter does not participate.

**Material application:** Texas formally announced intended suits. Continuing the advertisements would accumulate substantial liability, while suspending them during a test case and review would surrender the challenged conduct. Neither option provides an adequate practical substitute on this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Texas did not argue *Younger* abstention, which is not decided. A materially different threat still requires its own adequate-remedy and threatened-injury showing; no general exception for every preemption claim is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Retain declaratory and injunctive protection against the specified fare-guideline enforcement, subject to the separate scope limitation below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Ex parte Young*, 209 U.S. 123, applies to imminent unlawful enforcement with repeated sanctions and no adequate practical test-case alternative. *O'Shea v. Littleton*, 414 U.S. 488, preserves adequate-remedy and threatened-injury requirements. *Younger v. Harris*, 401 U.S. 37, is not modified because abstention is not presented.

##### Whether the injunction may extend beyond the unlawful enforcement actually threatened

**Holding:** A preventive injunction against state enforcement must be confined to the unlawful enforcement actually threatened; repeating a federal preemption clause does not justify protection against hypothetical future applications. The decree's coverage of unthreatened airline advertising and operations exceeds the established controversy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices. All eight participants vote to remove the excess, but Stone, Blackmun and Stevens supply no additional joins to this rationale.

**Material application:** The enforcement notices concern fare advertising, not the entire range of airline operations covered by the decree. The decree would otherwise adjudicate state applications before the State asserted them.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Frequent-flyer programs, boarding compensation and other unthreatened practices are not adjudicated merely because the NAAG materials discuss them. Extension of relief to 33 other Attorneys General and the separately petitioned personal-jurisdiction dispute in No. 90-1606 are outside this review. No damages, immunity for other airline conduct or new territorial extension is ordered.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the excess injunction and remand for narrowing to the specified fare-guideline controversy, retaining the declaratory and injunctive protection supported by the actual threat.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Public Service Commission of Utah v. Wycoff Co.*, 344 U.S. 237, excludes hypothetical regulatory disputes from preventive relief. *Ex parte Young*, 209 U.S. 123, supplies the imminent-enforcement boundary, and *O'Shea v. Littleton*, 414 U.S. 488, excludes conjectural injury.

#### Lee v. Weisman, 505 U.S. 577 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1014.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

For the Establishment Clause grounds in *Lee v. Weisman*, June 24, 1992, No. 90-1014, see First Amendment — Official school prayer.

##### Whether continued exposure at a forthcoming graduation preserves a live claim for prospective relief

**Holding:** A student who remains in the same public-school system and faces its challenged official prayer policy at her forthcoming high-school graduation has a live claim for prospective protection although her earlier graduation is over. Deborah Weisman's continued exposure permits review and preservation of the injunction without deciding her father's taxpayer standing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices adopt the threshold proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the student's continuing prospective exposure.

**Material application:** Deborah remains enrolled at Classical High School, and the stipulated record makes clergy-led prayer at her later graduation likely. Her relationship to that forthcoming policy application is concrete; formal optional attendance does not remove it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no taxpayer-standing rule and creates no general advisory-jurisdiction exception for completed school controversies that might recur.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Preserve review and the injunction against the challenged official practice. The injunction's substantive scope follows the First Amendment holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Engel v. Vitale, 370 U.S. 421, and School District of Abington Township v. Schempp, 374 U.S. 203, supply the substantive school-prayer interest without supplying a new taxpayer-standing rule.

#### Chamber of Commerce v. Reich

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 95-5242; no Supreme Court docket assigned.

### Foreign sovereign commercial activity

#### Saudi Arabia v. Nelson, 507 U.S. 349 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-522.

**Decided:** March 23, 1993.

##### The first commercial-activity clause requires the action to rest on that activity

**Holding:** Under the first clause of 28 U.S.C. §1605(a)(2), commercial activity carried on in the United States—activity having substantial United States contact under §1603(e)—must supply the basis of the action, rather than merely precede the injury or establish a factual connection with it. The inquiry identifies the conduct constituting the elements that would entitle the plaintiff to relief; recruitment, a United States employment contract and a commercial retaliatory motive do not by themselves make the intentional detention-and-torture claims an action based on domestic commercial activity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion for the Court, excluding its warning paragraph, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: six. White and Blackmun agree in the intentional-claim judgment on a separate domestic-nexus ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, June 12, 1992, decision: applies the nature-of-conduct rule; distinguishes its third-clause direct-effect holding from the first-clause basis requirement.

##### Alleged abuse of sovereign police power is not commercial conduct

**Holding:** Arrest, imprisonment and torture alleged to have been inflicted through a foreign state's police authority are sovereign in nature for §1605(a)(2), even if undertaken to retaliate against a commercial employee's safety complaints. The asserted commercial-activity exception therefore does not support jurisdiction over these intentional-tort claims; this statutory immunity ruling neither legalizes the abuse nor decides an unasserted exception or liability of absent individual actors.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: six. Intentional-claim reversal receives eight judgment votes; White and Blackmun reach it without joining the sovereign-nature characterization.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, June 12, 1992, decision: applies nature rather than purpose; its market-participant distinction remains controlling.

##### Warning claims and absence of a controlling rationale

**Authority of the judgment:** The warning claims are dismissed under the asserted commercial-activity exception, 5–4: Souter, O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas and White support reversal; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy oppose that component. Souter’s four-Justice warning paragraph rejects relabeling the sovereign wrong. White instead treats retaliatory employment conduct as commercial but finds actionable conduct abroad and an insufficient domestic nexus. Neither rationale is a logical subset of the other, so Marks supplies no controlling general warning rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The judgment binds these parties but does not establish that every independently pleaded domestic recruitment-warning omission is barred. The opposing writings establish no jurisdictional predicates or merits findings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The first-clause basis inquiry is distinct from Weltover’s third-clause direct effects. No general rule requires every act in an action to be commercial, and no separate ruling on recruitment contacts is necessary. Other exceptions and absent individuals’ liability remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and require jurisdictional dismissal under the asserted exception: 8–1 for intentional claims and 5–4 for warning claims. No tort merits, duty, attribution, knowledge, causation, entity identity or preclusion determination follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the original premature action; no tort liability, transfer or new-action timeliness is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hallstrom v. Tillamook County, 493 U.S. 20: applied to enforce a congressionally fixed prefiling sequence.
- McCarthy v. Madigan, March 4, 1992: statutory primacy applied; its damages-only BOP exception is distinguished.
- Haines v. Kerner, 404 U.S. 519: liberal pleading construction preserved; it does not excuse the prefiling requirement.

### Concurrent claims against the United States

#### Keene Corp. v. United States, 508 U.S. 200 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-166.
**Decided:** May 24, 1993.

##### Section 1500 jurisdiction is measured when the action is filed

**Holding:** Section 1500 bars the reviewed Claims Court action when, at filing, a suit for or in respect to the same claim is pending elsewhere against the United States or a covered federal actor; later dismissal of that other suit does not cure the original action’s jurisdictional defect. Claims Court decisions inconsistent with that filing-time requirement cannot support jurisdiction here; the Court does not decide the reversed order of filing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mollan v. Torrance, 9 Wheat.537: filing-time jurisdiction applied.
- Corona Coal Co. v. United States, 263 U.S. 537: statutory pendency bar applied.
- Brown v. United States, 175 Ct.Cl. 343, 358 F.2d 1002, and Hossein v. United States, 218 Ct.Cl. 727: disapproved insofar as inconsistent with filing-time jurisdiction.
- Tecon Engineers, Inc. v. United States, 170 Ct.Cl. 389, 343 F.2d 943: reversed filing sequence reserved, not overruled here.

##### Overlapping facts and relief cannot be separated merely by changing legal theories

**Holding:** For the claims presented, §1500 applies when the suits arise from substantially the same operative facts and seek overlapping relief, even if they plead different legal theories or those theories cannot be brought in the same forum. The Court leaves open suits seeking wholly different relief and does not adopt the Federal Circuit’s broader disposition of those precedents.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- British American Tobacco Co. v. United States, 89 Ct.Cl.438: operative-facts construction adopted for overlapping relief.
- Matson Navigation Co. v. United States, 284 U.S. 352: historical statutory setting applied; different causes do not negate the bar.
- Casman v. United States, 135 Ct.Cl. 647, and Boston Five Cents Savings Bank v. United States, 864 F.2d 137: wholly different relief question reserved, not overruled here.

##### The dismissals do not depend on a new rule requiring prospective-only treatment

**Holding:** Keene’s reviewed suits fail under established filing-time and factual-identity principles; the Federal Circuit’s broader repudiation of precedents unnecessary to this result does not make the controlling rule new as applied here. The Court therefore rejects the requested prospective-only treatment without deciding the general availability of pure prospectivity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Claims Court-first filing, wholly different relief, future-action tolling and general pure prospectivity remain unresolved. The holding does not adopt the Federal Circuit’s broader repudiation of those distinct precedents.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm jurisdictional dismissal of Keene I and Keene II; later termination elsewhere does not revive these actions. No asbestos merits, indemnity, compensation, transfer or new filing opportunity is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron Oil Co. v. Huson, 404 U.S. 97: prospective-only request rejected at its new-rule premise; general availability not decided.
- Corona Coal Co. v. United States, 263 U.S. 537, and British American Tobacco Co. v. United States, 89 Ct.Cl. 438: existing governing principles applied.

### Social Security remands and EAJA fees

#### Shalala v. Schaefer, 509 U.S. 292 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-311.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### Sentence-four judgment ends the action and confers prevailing status

**Holding:** A sentence-four judgment reversing the Secretary’s denial of benefits with a remand ends the §405(g) civil action and makes the successful claimant a prevailing party; the court must enter judgment rather than retain the merits action pending an administrative award. **Holding:** Under the then-effective rules, EAJA’s thirty-day application period follows a properly entered judgment and expiration of the applicable sixty-day appeal period; the absence of the required Rule 58 separate judgment prevents that deadline from expiring. Schaefer’s application is timely although the sentence-four remand was itself a final merits disposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Holding:** Hudson’s integrated administrative-proceeding fee rationale does not apply to a sentence-four remand properly entered without retained jurisdiction, or to retention of jurisdiction that is challenged. ### Federal agency immunity and constitutional damages

#### FDIC v. Meyer, 510 U.S. 471

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-741.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### FTCA exclusivity does not encompass this federal constitutional claim

**Holding:** Section 2679(a) makes FTCA relief exclusive as against an agency's sue-and-be-sued authority only for claims cognizable under the whole of §1346(b), including its private-person and state-law requirements. Meyer's federal constitutional tort is outside that category, so §2679(a) does not bar this suit in the agency's own name; this statutory conclusion neither supplies a cause of action nor guarantees recovery.

**Proposition-level authority:** The statutory-exclusivity portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this separate threshold holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The §1346(b) category requires a claim against the United States for money damages for property injury or loss, personal injury or death caused by an employee's negligent or wrongful act or omission within employment scope, under circumstances in which a private person would be liable under the law of the place. That last requirement means state substantive law; §2679(b)(1)'s differently worded employee rule is not imported.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Richards v. United States, 369 U.S. 1: applies the FTCA's state-law source and private-person limitation.
- Miree v. DeKalb County, 433 U.S. 25: applies state law as the substantive source under the law-of-the-place provision.
- Loeffler v. Frank, 486 U.S. 549: preserves FTCA coordination while specifying that §2679(a) refers to cognizability under §1346(b), not everything described as tortious.
- United States v. Smith, 499 U.S. 160: distinguishes employee exclusivity under §2679(b)(1) and its treatment of the foreign-country exception from the differently worded agency provision.
- Hubsch v. United States, 338 U.S. 440: preserves cognizability despite failure of proof on an element; distinguishes a claim that never falls within §1346(b)'s category.

##### Former FSLIC's sue-and-be-sued clause waives immunity

**Holding:** Former 12 U.S.C. §1725(c)(4) waives FSLIC's sovereign immunity for this constitutional tort claim, even though a private entity ordinarily would not bear that constitutional duty. An implied restriction on its broad sue-and-be-sued authority requires a clear showing that the suit is inconsistent with the statutory or constitutional scheme, that restriction is necessary to avoid grave interference with governmental functions, or that Congress plainly intended the clause in a narrow sense; FDIC establishes none here.

**Proposition-level authority:** The waiver portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Nine Justices adopt the specific waiver and its stated limits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- FHA v. Burr, 309 U.S. 242: applies liberal construction and all three grounds for a clearly established implied restriction; mere absence of private constitutional liability does not establish one.
- Loeffler v. Frank, 486 U.S. 549: applies the broad waiver rule while keeping separate statutory limits distinct.
- Franchise Tax Board v. United States Postal Service, 467 U.S. 512: preserves private-business liability as support for the incidents of suit recognized there, without turning it into an outer boundary for every waiver.
- United States v. Nordic Village, February 25, 1992: preserves the requirement to identify the particular sovereign consent; the ambiguous bankruptcy provision there does not narrow this express agency clause.

##### Waiver does not create a direct agency Bivens action

**Holding:** A waiver of sovereign immunity removes a defense to suit but does not itself authorize constitutional damages; Bivens does not supply Meyer a direct damages cause of action against FSLIC. Extending an individual-officer action to the federal agency would create a distinct defendant and public obligation, rather than merely provide relief within an existing agency action.

**Proposition-level authority:** The cause-of-action portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this independently sufficient ground for rejecting the agency award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Mitchell, 463 U.S. 206: applies the analytical separation of sovereign consent and a substantive source authorizing relief.
- Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 403 U.S. 388: retains its individual-officer action and declines extension directly to a federal agency.
- Davis v. Passman, 442 U.S. 228: preserves the individual employment-discrimination action; it does not supply an agency defendant here.
- Carlson v. Green, 446 U.S. 14: preserves individual-officer liability and its deterrent rationale without making every useful compensatory arrangement an authorized agency action.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February 26, 1992: preserves appropriate relief within an established private action; distinguishes creation of a new action and defendant.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming that a direct agency remedy could advance the compensatory and deterrent purposes of Bivens, the resulting expansion of direct federal financial liability and redesign of the remedial structure counsel against judicial creation of that remedy. Those institutional considerations independently require rejection of this implied agency damages action; the Court does not find that Meyer received an adequate substitute remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** The independent fiscal-and-remedial portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Nine Justices adopt this independently sufficient alternative ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The $130,000 agency award cannot stand for want of an agency cause. Pattullo's separate immunity judgment is outside review and remains undisturbed. The Court does not decide Meyer's property entitlement, the underlying due-process violation, entitlement under another source, a universal federal waiver, or elimination of existing individual Bivens remedies. Former §1725(c)(4), rather than a silently substituted successor clause, governs this 1982 agency claim. Section 2679(b)(1)'s separate employee rule is not rewritten.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 403 U.S. 388: applies its special-factors limitation to the proposed direct public liability, independently of the individual-defendant distinction.
- United States v. Standard Oil Co. of California, 332 U.S. 301: applies the concern against judicial determination of a new federal fiscal obligation.
- Bush v. Lucas, 462 U.S. 367: applies institutional restraint concerning a new damages remedy; does not find that Bush's civil-service scheme governed or compensated Meyer.
- Schweiker v. Chilicky, 487 U.S. 412: applies caution about remedy creation despite incomplete compensation, without importing its Social Security system as Meyer's actual remedy.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The agency damages judgment is reversed and the $130,000 award cannot stand for want of a direct agency cause. Pattullo's separate, unreviewed immunity judgment remains undisturbed. No constitutional merits finding or new damages trial is ordered.

### Speech injunctions and remedial scope

#### Madsen v. Women’s Health Center, Inc., 512 U.S. 753

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-880.

**Decided:** 1994-06-30.

For the other questions in Madsen v. Women’s Health Center, Inc., 1994-06-30, No. 93-880, see First Amendment — The decree as confined is not categorically content based; First Amendment — A neutral speech injunction must burden no more speech than necessary; First Amendment — The qualified public access arrangement survives; First Amendment — The private-property extension lacks the necessary remedial connection; First Amendment — The qualified medical-noise restriction survives; First Amendment — The prohibition on observable images is too broad; First Amendment — Peaceful initial approach cannot depend on advance invitation throughout 300 feet; First Amendment — Residential privacy does not justify this territorial exclusion.

##### The limited scope of geographic and paragraph (4) review

**Holding:** Geographic tailoring of the 36-foot zone is fairly included in the presented challenge to exclusion from peaceful expression in designated zones. **Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp.*, November 30, 1993: applies its strong prudential presumption and exercises its retained exceptional route on the stated combination of circumstances.
- *Yee v. City of Escondido*, question-presented portion: preserves the distinction between a subsidiary argument and a related independent question; usefulness alone does not establish fair inclusion.

**Holding:** An injunction may bind named responsible defendants and persons legally shown to act in concert or participation with them who have notice; notice, presence, sympathy, organizational affinity or a shared viewpoint alone does not establish that relation. **Holding:** The Court declines the independent facial and separately framed as-applied challenges to the disputed substantive terms in paragraphs (7), (8) and (9), despite actual adversarial merits briefing, because those additional contextual constructions are neither fairly included in the designated-zone and party-coverage questions nor necessary to their resolution, and no further prudential exception is warranted. **Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-A, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight on the Rule 14 ground. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the nonreach disposition for these independently disputed terms and applications and adds no ground.

**Material application:** Paragraph (7) addresses physical abuse, grabbing, intimidation, harassment, touching, pushing, shoving, crowding and assault involving clinic users and persons seeking specified homes, and contains its own invited-contact qualification. Paragraph (8) concerns harassment, intimidation, physical abuse, assault and threats against present or former clinicians, staff, employees or volunteers, without that invited-contact text. Paragraph (9) concerns encouraging, inciting or securing another prohibited act. Their separate terms are not merged. Clinic-property entry and access-obstruction provisions (1) and (2) are unchallenged; no contempt, absent person's liability or separate Cheffer claim is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Izumi*: applies the strong prudential presumption and declines a further exceptional extension; briefing is not petition presentation.
- *R. A. V. v. City of St. Paul*, June 22, 1992, and *Claiborne Hardware*: their protections for expression and facial challenge remain available, without deciding these omitted independent claims.

**Holding:** Paragraph (9) cannot support enforcement for encouraging, inciting or securing conduct prohibited only by a restriction set aside here, and the invalidated exclusion cannot be reinstated simply by relabeling that protected activity under another clause. The private-extension, images, initial-consent and residential-territory predicates must be removed without deciding paragraph (9)'s independent substantive validity.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-B, joined by all eight other Justices: nine at the invalidated-predicate and no-evasion level. This agreement does not settle the permissible content of a new decree.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Claiborne Hardware*: applies the connection among unlawful conduct, responsibility and effective relief; no general incitement holding follows.

##### Remand permits lawful relief without requiring a personal zone

**Holding:** The lower court must conform the decree to this judgment and may consider independently lawful, precisely limited conduct relief supported by the record and the governing constitutional rules. This remand commands neither a replacement post-refusal injunction nor any personal-separation distance; the existing independently lawful restrictions are not automatically dissolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-C, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight. Stone-Zsela dissents from the refusal to require his bounded post-refusal replacement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Claiborne Hardware*: preserves the relation between conduct relief, responsibility and protected advocacy.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Florida judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded for a conforming decree. The qualified public-access arrangement and medical-noise restriction remain; the private extension, images prohibition, initial-invitation requirement and residential territorial ban must be removed, including dependent use under paragraph (9). The predecessor and independently lawful unreviewed terms are not automatically dissolved. The court may consider precisely fitted lawful conduct relief, but no replacement injunction, personal distance, new sound threshold, damages or contempt judgment is commanded.

#### United States v. National Treasury Employees Union

### State immunity and bistate compact entities

#### Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp., 513 U.S. 30

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1197.
**Decided:** November 14, 1994.

##### PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity

**Holding:** A bistate compact entity does not acquire the States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity merely by exercising governmental power: its actual legal, financial and institutional relationship must supply good reason to conclude that the States structured it as their arm and Congress concurred, with state-treasury exposure highly probative but not categorically indispensable. PATH is a distinct, financially self-supporting enterprise responsible for its obligations; these employee suits impose no judgment obligation on either State and do not treat either State itself as a defendant, so PATH lacks that immunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Lake Country Estates, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency*, 440 U.S. 391 (1979), applies its entity-specific compact inquiry: exercising state power does not itself make a bistate corporation the States, and the compact’s actual structure matters.
- *Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle*, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), preserves the distinction between a separate public entity and an arm of a State; a governmental function alone supplies no immunity.
- *Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp. v. Feeney*, 495 U.S. 299 (1990), is distinguished as deciding federal-forum consent while assuming, rather than deciding, PATH’s arm status. Its consent ruling is not an earlier holding that PATH is immune.
- *Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission*, 502 U.S. 197 (1991), preserves state-court FELA coverage without constructive waiver of federal-forum immunity; no abrogation or waiver rule is enlarged here.
- *Puerto Rico Aqueduct & Sewer Authority v. Metcalf & Eddy, Inc.*, 506 U.S. 139 (1993), preserves immediate review of a qualifying immunity denial without establishing that a particular entity is an immune arm. Its appealability holding does not decide PATH’s status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every compact entity nonimmune, make a debt disclaimer alone dispositive, decide a materially different entity’s practical dependence, abolish state sovereign immunity or hold that FELA abrogates federal-forum immunity. Negligence, causation, damages, the Boiler Inspection Act merits, every independently applicable nonimmunity limitations defense and hypothetical consent exceptions remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both the Hess and Walsh immunity dismissals are reversed and remanded. The Eleventh Amendment bar is removed; ordinary nonimmunity defenses and the separate actions' merits remain undecided.

### Prison access and prospective relief

#### Lewis v. Casey, 518 U.S. 343 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1511.
**Decided:** June 24, 1996.

##### Whether books alone satisfy the right of meaningful access

**Holding:** Bounds requires a meaningful practical opportunity for court access through effective libraries, trained assistance or other effective arrangements considered as a whole; adequate books do not discharge that duty for prisoners unable to use them without needed assistance. It guarantees neither a preferred library program, physical browsing in every case, professional counsel for every prisoner nor the decree's numerical requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** Bartholic’s dismissal and Harris’s inability to file establish actual obstruction at their supported scope. An inventory-based paging system with prompt delivery may work for prisoners capable of independent use; that conditional possibility proves neither Arizona’s actual adequacy nor adequacy for differing literacy or language needs.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No fixed number of all injured prisoners, right to ultimate success, constitutional library specification or automatic appointed counsel is established.

##### What connection to a legal claim is required for access liability

**Holding:** A Bounds access-liability claim requires actual or evidence-supported imminent hindrance of a qualifying, nonfrivolous legal claim, subject to the validity of restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests. The prisoner need not win the obstructed case or complete an impossible filing, but a deficient inventory, preferred staffing arrangement or abstract wish for better research does not alone establish the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices.

**Material application:** Technical dismissal, inability to file a contemplated qualifying claim or sufficiently concrete threatened obstruction can supply the connection. A procedural defect does not itself make the underlying claim substantively frivolous. Evidence and reasonable inferences may prove the real claim and obstacle before avoidable filing failure.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The additional Article III preliminary-merits screen, subgroup-standing requirement and categorical sentence/conditions and initial-filing restrictions have only four votes. Ordinary Lujan injury, causation and stage-appropriate proof remain. Genuine state-created-liberty claims remain possible under Sandin; no categorical exclusion of every other retained right, universal end of assistance upon filing or general private-litigation subsidy is adopted. Independent communications and petition claims retain their own predicates.

##### What proof and governmental role support classwide prison-access relief

**Holding:** Common relief must correspond to a demonstrated common barrier and an identified affected group; individual obstruction or a resource deficiency alone does not establish an unrestricted statewide violation. Relief must respect restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests and give the State a genuine opportunity to devise an effective cure, while permitting measures actually necessary to repair the proved wrong.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** Common evidence may prove a common barrier without proof that every class member has already lost a suit, but geographical and functional reach require support. The actual findings do not establish a denial at every facility or independent broad entitlements for all non-English-speaking or lockdown prisoners. Opportunity to rebut a presumptive Gluth statewide template did not supply Arizona’s genuine initial remedial design role.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Turner requires examination of the actual reasonable relationship to legitimate penological interests with appropriate deference; injury alone does not invalidate a valid restriction, and merely asserting security does not establish validity. Jenkins permits necessary repair measures without making every remedial detail a freestanding constitutional right.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Unsupported classwide approval set aside and remanded, 8–1; Stevens accepts a narrower remedial remand. Established obstruction, supported findings and common evidence remain. No unrestricted statewide violation, automatic dismissal or fixed library, telephone, staffing or resource minimum.

##### How original PLRA §802 governs this existing prospective decree and any replacement

**Holding:** Section 802 applies amended section 3626 to prospective relief granted before, on or after April 26, 1996; relief here requires findings that it is narrowly drawn, extends no further than necessary to correct the federal-right violation of the particular plaintiffs, and is the least intrusive means necessary, with substantial weight given to adverse effects on public safety or criminal-justice-system operation. Relief exceeding official state-law authority or otherwise violating state or local law additionally requires federal permission, necessity to correct the federal violation, and absence of any other corrective relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part IV, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees only with the statutory remand disposition.

**Material application:** Amended §3626 applies to relief granted before, on or after April 26, 1996. Prospective relief means relief other than compensatory monetary damages. Existing findings may satisfy actual predicates without ritual wording; reasonable cost or absence of hardship does not substitute for need, narrowness and least intrusiveness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3626(a)(1)(B)’s federal-permission, necessity and no-other-relief conditions govern state/local-law conflicts separately from (A). Subsection (C) supplies no authority to build prisons or raise taxes and preserves existing limits. New preliminary relief must be otherwise authorized and satisfy narrowness, need, least intrusiveness, safety and the separate state-law terms; it expires ninety days after entry unless the required prospective findings are made and the order made final before expiration. ##### When termination and an automatic stay arise under the original §3626

**Holding:** The original section 3626 termination provisions do not dissolve relief merely upon enactment: the immediate-termination entitlement for relief entered without the required findings remains subject to written, record-based findings that relief is necessary to correct a current OR ongoing federal-right violation and remains narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary, and least intrusive. **Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part IV, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees only with the statutory remand disposition.

##### I. The original PLRA supplies an independent, conditional termination rule

**Holding:** For covered federal prison-conditions prospective relief, including relief entered before April 26, 1996, original §3626(b)(2) entitles a defendant or intervener to immediate termination when the relief was approved without findings that it was narrowly drawn, extended no further than necessary to correct the federal-right violation and was the least intrusive necessary means. Under original §3626(b)(3), relief must nevertheless continue upon written, record-based findings that it remains necessary to correct a current **or** ongoing federal violation, is no broader than necessary, and is narrowly drawn and the least intrusive means; existing substantive findings may satisfy the statute without ritual wording.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt the following rules and qualifications.

**Material application:** Section 802 expressly reaches existing prospective relief. The State must establish the termination basis and the court must independently apply (b)(3); missing ritual phrases, age of the decree or relief exceeding constitutional minima alone does not establish dispensability.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is (b)(2) termination, not the waiting-period route under (b)(1) or automatic stay under (e). The legal appeal establishes neither a preserved failure to consider qualifying findings nor a supported violation requiring a new hearing; no statewide compliance finding or accrued monetary liability is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lewis supplies the complete original findings and preservation conditions. Rufo remains the equitable modification rule, but its significant-change threshold is no additional prerequisite to this independent statutory route.

**Current-force modification:** From November 26, 1997, including pending cases, Pub. The independent (b)(2) route and the substantive written-findings, need, narrowness and least-intrusiveness requirements remain. The announced Holding continues to state the original-text rule; the amendment does not rewrite that adjudication.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Pub. L. 105-119, §123(a)(2), (b), effective 1997-11-26, changes the preservation condition for pending cases. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail v. ##### III. Negotiated decree protections are not automatically independent federal rights

**Holding:** Incorporating agreed protections into a federal consent decree does not make every protection exceeding underlying federal law the independent federal right whose violation prevents termination under §3626(b)(2). Continuing prospective enforcement remains subject to the statute, while relief necessary to correct an actual federal violation must be preserved under the full original (b)(3) conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt the following rules and qualifications.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Relief exceeding a constitutional minimum may still be necessary to correct an actual federal violation. Separate contractual or state-law claims and independently accrued rights are not adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rufo permits negotiated obligations beyond constitutional minima without insulating their future enforcement from valid legislation. Lewis permits necessary corrective measures; Joseph A.'s performance-condition ruling, made without a PLRA prison-conditions predicate, does not displace this statutory route.

**Current-force modification:** The distinction between negotiated decree terms and independent federal rights remains controlling. For the preservation condition incorporated in this proposition, Pub. L. 105-119, §123(a)(2), (b), effective November 26, 1997 and applicable to pending cases, substitutes current and ongoing for current or ongoing; the other preservation requirements remain. **Holding:** Beneficiaries of this mutable prospective decree have no vested entitlement to perpetual federal enforcement of its unchanged terms despite valid later law. On the presented legal challenge, the judicial motion process, opportunity to oppose termination and appellate review satisfy due process; no additional evidentiary remand follows from an unestablished possibility that a statutory preservation condition was overlooked.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt the following rules and qualifications.

- **Howlett v. Rose:** Applied to require state-court fidelity to federal substantive law and to forbid discrimination or nullification, without imposing an identical federal appellate system.

### Modification of continuing injunctions

#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Material judgment:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5–4, for tailored prospective Rule 60(b)(5) relief; Lemon modified as the governing test; Aguilar's categorical rule overruled and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale displaced at the stated scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### III. The Court's present change supplies the legal change required for prospective relief

**Holding:** When this Court expressly removes the constitutional premise supporting a continuing adjudicated injunction, that present change is a significant change in law under Rule 60(b)(5), and continued categorical enforcement on the displaced premise is inequitable. The Court may apply its ruling in the pending review of denied prospective relief without requiring a second motion merely to invoke the rule it has just announced; lower courts remain bound by directly controlling precedent until this Court changes it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join the present-change rule and its distinction between this continuing decree and a completed judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Denial of Rule 60(b)(5) relief is reviewed for abuse of discretion, with its governing legal premises reviewed as questions of law. Rufo requires a significant change making continued enforcement inequitable and a modification fitted to that change. The legal change here is the Court's ruling today. It is not the expectation that the Court might reconsider Aguilar, an increase in the expense of compliance, or a supposed earlier overruling. Kiryas Joel expressly preserved Aguilar and Ball. Zobrest decided antecedent statutory and regulatory questions without deciding establishment. Rosenberger's actual printing benefit and Pinette and Chabad's private-expression holdings did not authorize this public teaching program. Rodriguez de Quijas required the lower courts to apply the directly governing rule.

The objection that Rule 60 review cannot become an untimely rehearing of the original judgment does not bar this relief. The motion addresses the decree's continuing prospective operation, and the preserved question presents the constitutional rule supporting that operation. The Court changes that rule expressly; it does not hold that the district court should have anticipated the change. Requiring a new motion solely to invoke today's decision would preserve a now-rejected prohibition without a remaining legal reason.

Rufo's limits remain. Dissatisfaction, a cheaper lawful alternative and anticipated doctrinal change are insufficient. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a risk can impose a heavy burden, but mere foreseeability is not an absolute bar and an unforeseeable catastrophe is unnecessary. A clarification of constitutional permission does not automatically erase a voluntarily accepted stricter consent obligation. This injunction enforces an adjudicated constitutional prohibition; the record establishes no negotiated promise independently requiring that prohibition. The Court neither reopens completed claims nor finds an independently sufficient factual change in costs.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail is applied to the present legal change and retained in its significant-change, accepted-risk and tailored-relief requirements. Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc. is applied to preserve lower-court obedience to direct precedent. Browder v. Director, Department of Corrections does not foreclose adjustment of continuing relief on the basis of the legal change actually adopted here; its limit on using a Rule 60 appeal to reopen the underlying completed judgment remains.

##### IV. Relief must remove the rejected prohibition while preserving independently lawful obligations

**Holding:** On remand the district court must remove the injunction's prohibition of the described secular Title I service insofar as that prohibition rests on religious location, presumed inculcation from location or ordinary supervision of public personnel; if no independently supported legal basis sustains the onsite prohibition, it must be dissolved. Any remaining obligation must have an identified lawful basis, and any further proceedings must concern a specified material issue under the surviving law rather than require proof that future misconduct is impossible.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join this tailored relief and its public-control limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Rufo connects modification to the proven change while preserving the lawful purposes of continuing relief. The change today eliminates the categorical site-and-monitoring ground. The district court therefore cannot simply reinstate that same ground, presume that public service and religious instruction are inseparable, or treat ordinary secular supervision as itself forbidden. It must remove the prohibition to that extent. Supported questions about actual religious instruction, diversion, religious control of public employees or another independent legal prohibition remain subject to ordinary procedure and the governing law; hypothetical misconduct does not justify preserving Aguilar's rejected inference.

No additional negotiated obligation is presumed. If an independent statutory, contractual or decree obligation is established, constitutional permission does not by itself extinguish it. Existing lawful services may continue while the mandate is implemented promptly. Children currently receiving those services should continue to receive them during implementation, with existing statutory duties preserved and no new service entitlement created. Any implementation period must address identified tasks and circumstances; the Court sets no number of days, and administrative delay cannot indefinitely preserve the rejected categorical prohibition. The district court modifies the decree under the mandate. Until it acts, the City must obey the operative injunction.

The holding permits the described program; it does not command onsite delivery, create an unconditional funding entitlement, approve a replacement program, award damages or repayment, cancel contracts, or excuse past disobedience. It makes no finding that every present implementation satisfies the required secular controls.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rufo's suitable-tailoring requirement controls implementation and preserves any independently lawful obligation. Its consent-decree rule is not converted into a presumption that this adjudicated injunction contains a voluntary stricter undertaking.

For the Lemon test and the onsite secular-service rule in Holdings I–II, see First Amendment, “Public instruction on religious-school premises,” Agostini, June 23, 1997, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.

**Holding:** Sections 1122(a)–(b) and 1125(a)(1)(B), (a)(2) unmistakably subject States and their instrumentalities to federal suit and otherwise available legal and equitable remedies for covered Lanham Act violations occurring on or after October 27, 1992. **Holding:** A State's continued operation of an otherwise lawful commercial undertaking does not unequivocally submit it to federal jurisdiction merely because Congress clearly and prospectively subjects that activity to private federal enforcement, even when the undertaking is realistically optional. ### Congress unmistakably authorized this federal patent action against States

#### Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-531.
**Decided:** 1999-06-23.

##### Congress unmistakably authorized this federal patent action against States

**Holding:** Sections 271(h) and 296 expressly subject States and their instrumentalities to private patent actions in federal court for covered violations on or after October 28, 1992. Section 296(b) makes the otherwise available patent remedies available on their own statutory terms; that authorization does not itself establish constitutional power, State consent, infringement, or entitlement to a particular award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by all nine Justices, directly adopts this statutory proposition and its limits.

##### Appropriate respondent alternatives are legally available within their limits

**Holding:** A respondent may defend an unchanged favorable judgment on an appropriately supported alternative that neither enlarges its rights nor reduces the adversary's rights under that judgment. CSB's posture permits consideration of its preserved defenses of the immunity denial, but preservation and briefing alone do not expand the §5 question or establish an alternative's merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I has all nine Justices' joins at this precise level. Their actual decisions on the alternative grounds remain distinct.

##### Section 5 independently sustains the authorized patent action

**Holding:** Section 5 permits Congress to prevent unconstitutional property deprivations through congruent and proportional enforcement legislation reaching some conduct that is not independently unconstitutional. Sections 271(h) and 296 validly authorize this patent action on that basis; the plaintiff need not establish a separate constitutional violation to proceed under the Act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, states an independently sufficient holding of the Court. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas disagree.

##### Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action

**Holding:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State's exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports this federal patent remedy as an independent ground for sustaining the immunity denial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, states an independently sufficient holding of the Court. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas oppose the extension.

##### Continued commercial participation does not supply consent to this suit

**Holding:** A State's continued commercial participation after Congress authorizes suit does not constitute consent: Florida Prepaid did not consent to federal patent suit by continuing its prepaid-tuition program after §§271(h) and 296 took effect. Actual consent through a distinct State choice, such as an express waiver or voluntary invocation of federal jurisdiction, remains available. Whether Florida Prepaid's litigation conduct waived immunity need not be decided, and no waiver finding is made.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined by all nine Justices, applies College Savings Part III and states these limits. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, who did not join College Savings Part III, accept it as controlling. Neither constitutional holding depends on consent.

#### Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents / United States v. Florida Board of Regents, 528 U.S. 62 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 98-791 and 98-796, consolidated.
**Decided:** January 11, 2000.

##### Whether the ADEA unmistakably authorizes these private federal actions against state employers

**Holding:** Sections 630(b), 626(b)–(c), and the incorporated FLSA provisions, read together, authorize private ADEA actions against state employers in federal court. That authorization establishes neither constitutional power, State consent, discrimination nor entitlement to relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Seminole Tribe and Florida Prepaid separate unmistakable authorization from constitutional power; combined enactments satisfy the former question here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five Justices.

Coverage, defenses, proof and award-specific statutory and constitutional limits remain for ordinary adjudication. College Savings Bank, Part III, is applied to the ADEA's authorized employment-enforcement measure; its Article I rule remains controlling beyond its patent application. Pennsylvania v. ### Automatic stays of prospective prison relief

#### Miller v. French / United States v. French, 530 U.S. 327 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 99-224 and 99-582.
**Decided:** June 19, 2000.

##### I. The automatic stay displaces ordinary equitable continuation

**Holding:** For the presented motion under 18 U.S.C. §3626(b)(2), subsection (e) automatically stays covered prospective relief beginning on the thirtieth day after filing and ending when the court enters a final order resolving the motion; the court may postpone the starting date for good cause by no more than sixty days, but general calendar congestion is not good cause. Beyond that express postponement, ordinary equitable authority does not permit the court to suspend the statutory stay.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's opinion of the Court has six direct rationale votes: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the judgment without joining this statutory-construction part; Stevens and Breyer dissent from the construction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3626(e)(4) makes orders staying, suspending, delaying or barring the automatic stay appealable as refusals to dissolve or modify an injunction under 28 U.S.C. §1292(a)(1), regardless of their label or preliminary or final character; it supplies review, not additional substantive authority to block the stay. The express subsection (e)(3) postponement is excluded from that special appeal treatment. Constitutional avoidance cannot replace this detailed statutory direction with a competing scheme. Subsection (e)(1)'s prompt-disposition duty and mandamus remedy remain. The retained 180-day timing language for motions under other law does not govern the immediate-termination motion presented here; its broader interaction with the amended under-subsection-(b) opening is not decided. Section 3626(a)(2)'s separate ninety-day expiration for newly entered preliminary relief does not turn this existing permanent decree into a preliminary order.

##### III. The remand preserves a genuine adjudication of supported application objections

**Holding:** In each docket, the Seventh Circuit's categorical invalidation is reversed and the case remanded, permitting a properly supported claim that the statutory timing, including available postponement, actually denies a meaningful opportunity to adjudicate to be presented and decided under Article III as well as any applicable due-process protection. No such violation is presumed, no feasibility hearing is required merely because time has elapsed, and no ordinary equitable exception or automatic continuation of the existing blocking injunction follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices directly join this operative remand and its limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Benjamin requires a real opportunity to offer material current proof when it is necessary for statutory adjudication; Gavin and Hadix require the unperformed termination inquiry without making a new hearing universal. The reported postponements of the termination hearing establish neither constitutional impossibility nor prison compliance, present violations or deliberate delay. They therefore neither compel an evidentiary hearing about timing nor foreclose a supported application objection. On remand, such an objection must receive genuine consideration under ordinary procedure; this judgment does not restrict it to due process or decide the appropriate remedy in advance. The court must also decide the actual termination conditions: the movant must establish that covered relief was entered without substantive necessity, narrowness and least-intrusion findings, and sufficient findings require no ritual wording. Under amended §3626(b)(3), relief shall not terminate if written, record-based findings establish that it remains necessary to correct a current and ongoing federal-right violation, extends no further than necessary, is narrowly drawn and is the least intrusive necessary means. Each retained duty requires those findings; obsolete relief may not survive through disregard of the new law. This appeal neither decides those merits nor terminates the decree. Hadix's distinct pending automatic-stay appeal is not adjudicated here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The categorical Article III invalidation cannot sustain the blocking injunction. The remand permits a supported inability-to-adjudicate objection under Article III as well as due process to be presented and decided; it neither automatically continues the blocking order nor denies consideration of a properly supported constitutional claim. No prison-compliance finding or termination of the underlying decree is entered.

### Sovereign immunity and accepted IDEA funding

#### Bradley v. Arkansas Department of Education

**Docket or dockets:** Eighth Circuit No. 98-1010; no Supreme Court docket number supplied.
**Decided:** June 12, 2000.

##### Actual acceptance of the disclosed IDEA funding condition supplies consent

**Holding:** A State that voluntarily accepts IDEA assistance after the effective express condition in §§1403 and 1415 consents to federal judicial enforcement of the obligations of that funded program, within the recipient, claim and remedy covered by the condition. Arkansas's acceptance under that notice removes sovereign immunity from the Bradley IDEA action, including the same sovereign immunity asserted for the named officer in his official capacity; it supplies neither a general waiver for Arkansas nor consent inferred from ordinary regulated activity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, unanimous.

**Material application:** The condition identifies the State, the federal forum and violations of IDEA. Section 1415 places federal judicial review among the procedures attached to the funded education program. Arkansas conceded both the spending-power validity of IDEA and its receipt of IDEA funds; its acceptance followed the express immunity condition. The present record supplies no retroactive surprise or coercive demand defeating that acceptance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The consent ground resolves the immunity appeal. The Court need not decide independent Article I or Section 5 abrogation. A difference between IDEA's protection and the constitutional minimum does not itself defeat preventive enforcement, as Boerne and Florida Prepaid establish, but neither does it establish IDEA's constitutional validity. No individual constitutional violation is an added prerequisite to proceeding on the consent ground.

Section 1403(c) applies to violations occurring in whole or in part after October 30, 1990. Section 1403(b) makes legal and equitable remedies available only to the same extent as against another public entity; it creates no additional category of relief. The Bradleys seek prospective compliance, not compensatory or punitive damages. Lane's distinction between substantive coverage and consent to a particular remedy remains applicable; no unrequested damages question is decided.

Section 1415(i)(2) permits an aggrieved party's civil action concerning the presented complaint after the specified administrative decision and appeal route: a party aggrieved under subsection (f) or (k) without a subsection (g) appeal, or a party aggrieved by the administrative appeal decision, may proceed in a competent state court or federal district court without an amount-in-controversy requirement. The court receives the administrative record, hears additional evidence at a party's request, and grants appropriate relief on the preponderance of the evidence. Section 1415(l) preserves rights, procedures and remedies under the Constitution, the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990, title V of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973, and other federal laws protecting children with disabilities. Before a civil action under those laws seeking relief also available under IDEA is filed, it requires exhaustion of subsections (f) and (g) to the same extent as for an IDEA action. Immunity denial neither finds these predicates satisfied nor excuses them.

The Court decides no educational violation, hearing violation, exhaustion issue, individual liability or particular decree. An independent prospective-officer claim still requires a connected officer, an ongoing federal violation and genuinely prospective relief; a treasury claim for past injury cannot become prospective by naming an employee. The Court creates no new defendant or officer claim. Jim C. and its Section 504 claim are outside the grant; IDEA acceptance establishes no Section 504 waiver. That statute's own federal-suit notice, accepted assistance and remedial scope require separate consideration in a case properly presenting them.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment denying sovereign immunity in the Bradley IDEA action is affirmed on the funding-consent ground. The case returns through the Eighth Circuit for ordinary adjudication in the District Court. The Court establishes no violation of a free appropriate public education obligation or hearing requirement and awards no damages, injunction, fees or other relief. The same sovereign-immunity defense asserted for Crowley in official capacity fails within the accepted condition; personal-capacity liability is not decided. No judgment is entered concerning Jim C., Section 504 or other ungranted claims.

Florida Prepaid's Article I holding and Kimel's application remain controlling; this consent decision neither relies on nor rejects an independent application of that power to IDEA.

### Private ADA Title I enforcement against state employers

#### Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett, 531 U.S. 356

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1240.
**Decided:** February 21, 2001.

##### Whether Congress validly authorizes private Title I employment actions against state employers

**Holding:** Congress may subject a State to the expressly authorized private Title I federal action when the covered employment obligation and its enforcement measure fall within the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. Those conditions are satisfied here, so the State defendants have no categorical immunity from these actions; authorization and amenability establish neither liability nor entitlement to a particular award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; the same five Justices adopt both independent grounds.

**Material application:** Section 12202 unmistakably authorizes the actions and section 12117 supplies the enforcement channels. Covered employment is an economic relationship in actual labor markets; the ordinary private action enforces that valid duty rather than compelling state administration of a federal program. Consent and proof of a separate constitutional tort are unnecessary to maintain these actions.

**Independent alternative holding:** Title I's protection of qualified workers against disability discrimination, including reasonable accommodation subject to its enacted qualifications and defenses, is congruent and proportional preventive and remedial enforcement of equal protection against state employers. Section 5 therefore independently sustains the expressly authorized actions without requiring each employee to prove a separate constitutional violation.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** The same five Justices adopt Section 5 as an independently sufficient ground.

**Material application:** Congress may use bounded prevention against irrational disability prejudice without turning every statutory violation into a constitutional violation. Differentiated evidence of public-employment exclusion supports that inference; no quota of adjudicated violations, formal-findings requirement, geographic restriction or sunset is imposed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Covered-employer status requires an industry affecting commerce and ordinarily at least 15 employees for each working day in at least 20 calendar weeks of the current or preceding year; the original two-year transition used 25 employees. Agents are included; the United States, wholly owned federal corporations, Indian tribes and bona fide private membership clubs other than labor organizations that are exempt from taxation under 26 U.S.C. section 501(c) are excluded from this employer definition. Actual coverage remains to be proved.

Disability retains impairment substantially limiting a major life activity, record-of-impairment and regarded-as branches. A qualified worker must perform essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation. Employer judgment receives consideration; a job description prepared before advertising or interviews is evidence, not a conclusive veto. Accommodation examples are permissive and include reassignment to a vacant position. Section 12112(b)(5)(A) concerns known limitations and allows the employer to demonstrate undue hardship: significant difficulty or expense assessed by cost, facility and entity resources, workforce, operational and organizational factors. Section 12112(b)(6) separately addresses screening criteria shown job-related for the position and consistent with business necessity; section 12113(a) separately requires that performance cannot be accomplished by reasonable accommodation. Direct threat concerns a significant risk to the health or safety of others that reasonable accommodation cannot eliminate. No general reasonableness exception or Title II fundamental-alteration defense replaces those provisions.

No medical-inquiry, foreign-workplace, religious-entity, food-handling or drug/alcohol defense is established or adjudicated here. The Court creates no exception or holding about those distinct provisions. Title II, Rehabilitation Act waiver, individual liability and award-specific limitations lie outside this Title I abrogation question; no Title II dismissal or abandonment is ordered. Qualification, discrimination, causation and relief are ordinary remaining merits issues, not unresolved aspects of the constitutional enforcement holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eleventh Circuit immunity judgment is affirmed. Coverage, qualification, discrimination, causation, defenses and lawful relief remain for ordinary adjudication; no payment, accommodation or employment change is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999: applies both its express Article I enforcement rule and its independent congruent-and-proportional prevention principle; neither requires consent or an individual constitutional tort.
- Kimel, January 11, 2000: applies its Article I employment-enforcement holding; its reservation of the ADEA's Section 5 validity remains untouched.
- Lopez: applies the identifiable commerce connection before aggregation; no general social-cost theory or automatic jurisdictional-word test is adopted.
- Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming: apply their support for valid federal regulation of state employment.
- Boerne: applies congruence and proportionality, preserving preventive authority and the prohibition on constitutional redefinition.
- Cleburne, Heller and Romer: apply actual rationality review without heightened disability scrutiny or a constitutional guarantee of accommodation.
- Olmstead: distinguishes its statutory integration holding and express nondecision of immunity and Section 5; its Title II defense is not imported.
- Union Gas and Seminole Tribe: preserve the constitutional conclusion extended in Florida Prepaid and the distinction between authorization and power; no categorical Article I prohibition displaces current law.
- College Savings Bank: preserves the rejection of consent inferred from ordinary State activity; these actions rest on legislative authority.

### Copyright service protections and feasible injunctions

#### A&M Records, Inc. v. Napster, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** Supreme Court docket not supplied; judgment below, 239 F.3d 1004 (9th Cir. 2001).
**Decided:** April 23, 2002.

For Napster's underlying preliminary infringement, secondary-liability and defense holdings, April 23, 2002, judgment below 239 F.3d 1004, see Business and Commercial Law — Music-file exchanges and preliminary secondary copyright liability.

##### Section 512 independently limits liability and relief

**Holding:** Secondary-liability status does not itself defeat a §512 protection. The lower court must determine each invoked service category and condition independently and apply the corresponding injunction limits before imposing relief beyond them; failure under one category does not decide another.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, unanimous; all nine join Parts I–VII and every qualification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Secondary-liability status alone does not defeat a safe harbor. The functions in (a)–(d) must be tested separately, and failure under one does not establish failure under another. Subsection (a) concerns automatic user-initiated transmitting, routing or connecting through the provider's system without provider selection of material or recipients except an automatic response, with no content modification. Any intermediate copy must not be maintained in a manner ordinarily accessible to anyone other than anticipated recipients, and must not remain ordinarily accessible to those recipients longer than reasonably necessary for transmission, routing or connection. Subsection (d)'s location-tool protection requires absence of actual knowledge or apparent-infringement awareness, or expeditious removal/disablement upon obtaining it; no directly attributable financial benefit where the provider has the right and ability to control; and expeditious response to a qualifying notice identifying the reference or link sufficiently to locate it. Subsection (i) additionally requires adoption and reasonable implementation of a policy terminating repeat infringers in appropriate circumstances, with subscribers and account holders informed of that policy and accommodation without interference of standard technical measures. Such measures must arise from broad open, fair, voluntary multi-industry consensus, be reasonably and nondiscriminatorily available, and impose no substantial provider cost or system burden.

A qualifying notice must substantially identify the copyrighted work or representative site list, the material or location reference and sufficient location information, provide contact information, bear an authorized signature, state a good-faith belief that the use lacks authorization from owner, agent or law, and state that the information is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that the complainant is authorized to act for the owner of an allegedly infringed exclusive right. Under §512(c)(3)(B), incorporated by (d)(3), a notice that fails substantial compliance may not be considered in determining statutory actual knowledge or awareness of facts making infringement apparent. When a notice delivered to the designated agent substantially supplies the work, material or link location, and contact information but fails other elements, that exclusion applies only if the provider promptly attempts to contact the sender or takes other reasonable steps to assist receipt of a notice substantially satisfying all required elements. The preliminary notices support the underlying knowledge issue, but final satisfaction of each formal notice condition, service category and policy requirement remains for determination on remand.

Section 512(m) does not permit conditioning safe harbors on affirmative monitoring or searching for facts of infringement, apart from qualifying standard technical measures, or on access/removal forbidden by law. Failure to qualify is not affirmative evidence against an independent noninfringement defense under (l). Under (j), a provider protected from monetary relief still may receive only the permitted forms of injunction: for non-(a) conduct, specified-site access restraint, termination of specified infringing accounts, or other necessary relief addressing specified copyrighted material at a particular online location that is least burdensome among comparably effective measures; for (a) conduct, only termination of specified infringing accounts and reasonable steps blocking a specific identified foreign online location. The court must consider cumulative system burden, likely owner harm, technical feasibility and effectiveness, effects on access to lawful material elsewhere, and comparably effective less burdensome means. Notice and an opportunity to appear are required except evidence-preservation or other orders with no material adverse network effect. These limitations must govern if their predicates are satisfied; an unresolved classification is not permission to evade them through a generalized policing command.

##### Preliminary discovery, hearing, security and compulsory-royalty objections

**Holding:** On this preliminary record, no demonstrated abuse of discretion requires more discovery or an evidentiary hearing, the $5 million security is supported against the competing evidence, and no statutory basis or special circumstance requires a compulsory-royalty substitute.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, unanimous; all nine join Parts I–VII and every qualification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Security need not equal the claimed enterprise value merely because that value is asserted. Technological novelty alone permits no judicial compulsory license. Later materially supported security evidence remains available; the ruling concerns this preliminary record, not final liability.

##### The replacement injunction must connect usable notice to feasible service action

**Holding:** Owners must identify protected works and sufficiently locate infringing files for practical response, and Napster must act within its actual index and account capabilities, including reasonable action against identified repeat infringers. The decree must preserve lawful files and authorized exchange, satisfy the remaining equitable predicates and any applicable §512 limits, and cannot make Napster guarantee prevention of every unidentified infringement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, unanimous; all nine join Parts I–VII and every qualification.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The mixed preliminary disposition is affirmed and remanded for a conforming injunction. The original July 26, 2000 restraint remains stayed until modification; no later replacement order or final liability is assumed. The $5 million security stands. A particular statutory classification must be resolved before relief exceeding its limits is imposed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **New York Times Co. v. Tasini:** Its separation of actual statutory rights, use permissions and individualized remedy remains; it supplies neither a general digital-use exemption nor a compulsory license.
- **Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc.:** Its demand for a remedy fitted to the proved conduct informs preservation of independent lawful expression; infringement does not justify restraints on unrelated lawful files.

##### Whether authorized removal waives immunity from state-permitted state-law claims

**Holding:** A State that authorizes voluntary removal of an action to federal court relinquishes its federal-forum immunity from state-law claims for which it has already waived immunity in its own courts, even though its law expressly reserves federal-forum immunity and does not separately authorize counsel to declare a waiver. That actual choice of federal adjudication has a uniform federal consequence; it does not enlarge substantive liability, surrender unrelated defenses or decide removal of claims for which the State retains immunity in its own courts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Georgia Tort Claims Act's scope and manner conditions survive. The holding does not decide removal of claims immune in the State's own courts, an otherwise valid federal claim, or other defensive participation. Actual removal, not the state-court waiver alone or counsel's motives, supplies federal consent.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the immunity ruling without adjudicating tort liability or compelling federal retention of the state claims.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Clark, Gunter and Gardner are applied to authorized voluntary removal; College Savings Bank and Florida Prepaid retain their distinction between actual submission and constructive commercial consent; Schacht's reserved issue is resolved at this scope; Raygor is distinguished; Ford is overruled only insofar as its contrary authorization rationale applies to this invocation.

##### Whether removal waiver creates section 1983 liability against the State

**Holding:** A State's waiver of federal-forum immunity does not make it a person subject to the pleaded §1983 damages claim or create a valid cause of action. The independent statutory defendant rule remains applicable despite removal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Will's statutory exclusion is applied independently of immunity. Schacht and Steel Co. preserve the distinction among jurisdiction, claim defenses and statutory merits.

#### In re Prudential Insurance Co. of America Sales Practice Litigation

##### Whether released-transaction facts may be barred from an expressly retained claim

**Holding:** A settlement court may enjoin actual prosecution of a released claim or adjudication inconsistent with a matter conclusively resolved, within the release's lawful scope and §2283's judgment-protection exception. ### State ports and privately initiated federal administrative adjudication

#### Federal Maritime Commission v. South Carolina State Ports Authority, 535 U.S. 743

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-46.
**Decided:** May 28, 2002.

##### Whether state immunity bars this Shipping Act complaint before the Commission

**Holding:** State immunity from private judicial suit does not prevent the Federal Maritime Commission from administering the Shipping Act through this statutory complaint adjudication against a state-operated port. Private initiation, a request for personal reparations and court-like safeguards do not transform the Commission's determination of the regulatory controversy into an independent private judicial suit.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would require a distinct statutory-authorization inquiry and do not join this tribunal rule.

The rule does not govern every tribunal, and separate Shipping Act abrogation is unnecessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** California v. Florida, March 27, 1996, retains its conclusive-judicial-function holding. EEOC v. **Holding:** The port's ordinary commercial operations and its defensive challenge establish no consent to suit; the Court's administrative-forum holding rests on the Commission's statutory function, not constructive waiver. The existing rule permitting unmistakably authorized State exposure within valid Article I power remains controlling, but this decision finds no affirmative or negative Shipping Act abrogation rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's five-Justice opinion controls; the four dissenters also reject supplied consent and categorical Article I incapacity but do not join the tribunal rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding permits both administrative reparations and prospective relief; it creates no prospective-only immunity exception or connected officer. Substantive coverage, authorization for a proceeding, and waiver remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999, Alden v. Maine, June 23, 1999, and Board of Trustees v. Garrett, February 21, 2001, retain their actual legislative-power and enforcement holdings. College Savings Bank, June 23, 1999, rejects constructive commercial consent. Lapides v. Board of Regents, May 13, 2002, remains confined to authorized removal; Verizon Maryland, May 20, 2002, preserves the distinct connected-officer route.

**Holding:** The Commission may adjudicate the complaint's requests under the Shipping Act's applicable coverage, violation and remedy provisions, without an immunity-based severance of reparations from prospective compliance. Any later judicial enforcement, collection or interim injunction must independently satisfy its own statutory, jurisdictional and immunity requirements; this decision establishes no substantive violation or award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes. The four dissenters share the later-proceeding limits but would require authorization before this compulsory adjudication.

An agency finding’s evidentiary effect does not create court jurisdiction or defeat immunity. ### Judicial review of claimant-funded Social Security representation fees

#### Gisbrecht v. Barnhart, 535 U.S. 789

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-131.
**Decided:** May 28, 2002.

##### Whether section 406(b) requires an exclusive lodestar starting point

**Holding:** When §406(b)'s favorable-judgment and attorney-representation conditions are satisfied, the court begins with the lawful contingent-fee agreement and independently determines a reasonable fee for the actual court representation, no more than 25 percent of the statutorily defined past-due benefits. The agreement carries no presumption of reasonableness, the attorney bears the burden of substantiating the requested award, and an exclusive lodestar cannot replace the agreement at the outset merely because it is customary in adversary fee-shifting cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct votes. Scalia rejects the agreement-centered method.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** City of Burlington v. Dague, 505 U.S. 557 (1992), and Blanchard v. Bergeron, 489 U.S. 87 (1989), are distinguished by the statutory obligation and payor; their fee-shifting holdings remain in force.

##### How the court reviews a contingent fee for reasonableness

**Holding:** The court must assess the actual services and results and explain the fee allowed. It may reduce the contractual request for substandard representation, attorney-caused delay inflating accumulated benefits, or compensation disproportionate to the representation; time records and rate information may assist that inquiry, but a high effective hourly rate alone does not establish unreasonableness and no universal multiplier or hourly limit governs.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Part II has the same eight-Justice coalition. Scalia separately agrees with mandatory review, substantiation and the ceiling, without joining the agreement-centered implementation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Hours and rates are evidence, not mandatory replacement arithmetic. Contingency gives no separate entitlement to a risk premium, unrelated-loss reimbursement or a fee justified merely by generic Social Security litigation risk. Each representation requires its own determination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the affirmance of Gisbrecht’s, Miller’s and Sandine’s awards and remand for the three determinations to be set aside and redetermined. No maximum or other amount is directed; Anderson is outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Buckhannon Board & Care Home, Inc. v. West Virginia Department of Health and Human Resources, May 29, 2001, supplies only the distinction between eligibility and reasonable amount; its statute-specific eligibility holding is not transferred to §406(b).

##### Which statutory payment conditions and EAJA overlap rules remain distinct

**Holding:** Section 406(b) permits a judicially allowed fee for successful court representation from, and not in addition to, the prescribed past-due benefits, subject to its 25 percent ceiling and applicable statutory payment conditions. EAJA separately permits government-paid fees and other expenses under its own conditions; when counsel receives fees under both provisions for the same work, counsel must refund the smaller fee to the claimant under Public Law 99-80's saving provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III is adopted by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela additionally joins favorable judgment, court representation, the cap, independent allowance, separate EAJA operation and the overlapping-fee refund, without a position on individual certification, assessment, benefit-base or criminal-liability applications.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 406(a) administrative representation is separate, and the aggregate administrative-and-court ceiling is not decided. EAJA rates do not determine the §406(b) award; the smaller-fee comparison concerns fees for the same work, not every expense. A contract alone is not an award. Apart from the EAJA saving provision, no additional fee may be payable or certified for the same court representation outside §406(b).

**Operative remedy or transition:** The applicable benefit-base provision excludes continued benefits under §423(g) or (h) and calculates benefits before an applicable §1320a-6(a) reduction. Its 1994 addition applies to favorable judgments made after 180 days after August 15, 1994. The assessment amendment applies when certification is required after January 31, 2000. Subsection (d) assesses the attorney, permits offset collection, prohibits direct or indirect claimant reimbursement, and permits collection and availability for obligation only to the extent and in the amount provided in advance by appropriations Acts. The pre-2001 rate is 6.3 percent; after 2000 the Commissioner sets the cost-recovery rate, capped at 6.3 percent. Actual dates and inputs govern those applications on remand. Section 406(b)(2) forbids charging, demanding, receiving or collecting more than the court allows, with a maximum $500 fine, one year of imprisonment, or both, subject to the specific EAJA saving rule. The methodology dispute establishes no violation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Shalala v. Schaefer, 509 U.S. 292 (1993), and Forney v. Apfel, 524 U.S. 266 (1998), retain their distinct remand, fee and review functions. Martin v. Hadix, June 21, 1999, does not replace these express judgment and certification transitions with its service-date rule.

#### Hope v. Pelzer, 536 U.S. 730

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-309.
**Decided:** June 27, 2002.

##### The accepted constitutional premise and individual-responsibility limits of this immunity review

**Holding:** The granted immunity review takes the supported, plaintiff-favorable premise that each occasion involved knowingly imposed painful restraint continued after a security need and any work refusal ended, as gratuitous punishment. That conduct violates the existing Eighth Amendment rule, but personal liability requires each defendant's own supported participation, actual culpable awareness and causal contribution; a reasonable response to a known risk remains protected under Farmer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Eighth Amendment characterization is accepted for the granted immunity inquiry, not newly decided after trial. May involved two hours with quarter-hour necessities; June involved roughly seven hours and greater aggravations. May-only officers cannot be assigned June conduct, and Gates’s alleged June attachment alone does not establish supervision, removal of clothing, deprivation or taunting.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Farmer requires actual awareness, personal causal responsibility and unreasonable disregard; obviousness permits but does not compel an awareness inference, and reasonable responses remain protected. Hudson retains its confined nontrivial malicious-beating holding. Rhodes and Whitley distinguish justified control from gratuitous punishment. Delaney’s assumed-fact method supplies no missing allegations or retrospective notice.

##### Fair warning can be clear without a previously litigated replica

**Holding:** An officer has qualified immunity unless the law when he acted made the unlawfulness of his actual conduct apparent to a reasonable official. An established rule can give that warning through an obvious application without a materially identical prior case; context, contrary authority and judicial hierarchy matter, and an earlier express reservation of the particular application may require greater factual specificity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General moral disapproval is insufficient. An official need not have consulted the controlling law; state policy and administrative warnings contextualize rather than create or erase the constitutional right. Later decisions cannot supply 1995 notice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow’s objective defense and Anderson’s particularized clarity remain. Lanier permits obvious-application notice without importing criminal willfulness. Wilson, Denius and Saucier preserve conduct-date context; Saucier imposes no substitute defense or compulsory merits-first sequence. Elder requires consideration of all relevant preconduct law.

##### Pre-1995 law warned against the assumed gratuitous restraint on both occasions

**Holding:** By May 11 and June 7, 1995, Gates v. Collier and Ort v. White gave fair warning that knowingly prolonging painful restraint as punishment after the prisoner was controlled and no work refusal continued violated the Eighth Amendment. The assumed conduct on each occasion therefore cannot receive qualified immunity merely because no earlier case involved this precise device, duration or combination of conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Material application:** The notice conclusion separately covers May 11 and June 7, 1995, on the assumption of knowingly prolonged painful restraint after control and work refusal ended. May’s offered necessities do not justify that assumed gratuitous punishment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The state restraint regulation’s return-to-work, quarter-hour necessities and log safeguards reinforce the distinction but create no federal right by themselves; compliance with some safeguards permits no needless pain. The preconduct federal report does not prove any officer received it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gates and Ort provide the specific punishment/coercion boundary; Bonner supplies circuit hierarchy. Contrary district decisions involved continued refusal, opportunities to return, necessities or shorter duration and cannot displace the superior circuit warning at the accepted premise.

##### Reversal resolves notice while preserving the ungranted responsibility issues

**Holding:** The reviewed similarity-based immunity judgment is reversed because the legal warning was sufficient on the accepted gratuitous-punishment account of both occasions. Ordinary further proceedings determine each respondent's supported acts, knowledge, causation and independent defenses; the June aggravations may not be attributed to May-only defendants or to Gates without a sufficient personal basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The immunity judgment is reversed, 7–2. Ordinary individual-responsibility proceedings remain; Stone-Zsela separately makes remand express. Notice is settled on the accepted account, while materially different actual facts and lawful independent defenses retain their force. Withdrawn claims remain withdrawn.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Farmer governs actual awareness and reasonable response in the remaining individual inquiry. Delaney preserves the distinction between assumed-fact legal review and supported personal liability without inventing a factual dispute.

#### People Who Care v. Rockford Board of Education, School District No. 205

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 00-3200 (246 F.3d 1073).
**Decided:** June 27, 2002.

##### The complete release showing governs every obligation proposed for termination

**Holding:** A school district seeking release from an adjudicated desegregation obligation must establish full and satisfactory compliance, elimination of the relevant vestiges to the extent practicable, and demonstrated good faith toward the whole decree. A court may release a completed domain while retaining supervision elsewhere only after separately assessing whether continued control in that domain is necessary or practicable to achieve compliance elsewhere; release may not obstruct the remaining remedy, and satisfaction of the prerequisites permits, rather than compels, partial withdrawal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Age, expense and program installation do not substitute for completed repair. National achievement parity, maximum educational potential and every desirable program goal are not release conditions. Criticism alone neither establishes bad faith nor excuses noncompliance; good faith concerns whole-decree conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Freeman’s complete release rule permits rather than compels partial withdrawal and independently requires attention to retained-control necessity or practicability and nonobstruction. Jenkins distinguishes repair from program funding and installation. Dowell requires an end to supervision when the required work is complete. Green and Swann preserve effective, violation-bounded remedies. Rufo’s modification inquiry adds no changed-circumstances requirement to release from completed duties.

##### The district bears the causal showing, with ordinary protection for settled findings

**Holding:** The district must show that remaining imbalance or educational conditions for which it disclaims remedial responsibility are not traceable to its adjudicated violation; the students need not establish that violation anew or supply a particular comparative study to preserve the existing burden. Existing findings receive clear-error review, but findings about one domain do not decide unexamined causal and remedial connections in another.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent causes impose no perpetual balancing obligation. Existing testimony, records and sound inference may suffice; no comparative statistical study is invariably required or conclusive. Assignment integration and open advanced-course enrollment receive their supported weight without resolving different retained duties. Settled liability is not retried.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Freeman and Jenkins place the release-related causal showing on the district and preserve settled findings absent clear error while requiring resolution of unexamined causal and remedial relationships.

##### The June 30 termination direction must be vacated

**Holding:** The categorical direction to terminate the decree effective June 30, 2002 is vacated because the appellate reasoning does not establish the complete release and causal showing. On remand the lower courts must apply Parts I and II, identify any supported full or partial release and define the obligations and supervision that remain justified.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the unanimous opinion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the June 30 termination direction and remand, 9–0. The displaced direction effects no dissolution; the courts must make a timely supported decision on full or partial release and define retained obligations and supervision. No automatic further supervision period or presumption that all retained measures are necessary follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The Freeman/Jenkins completion inquiry remains distinct from Ruiz v. United States (Ruiz v. Johnson)’s PLRA termination rule.
