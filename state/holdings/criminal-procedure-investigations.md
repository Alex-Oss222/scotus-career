## Criminal Procedure: Search, Seizure and Interrogation

### Fourth Amendment protection of possession

#### Soldal v. Cook County, 506 U.S. 56 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-6516.

**Decided:** December 8, 1992.

##### A seizure of a home protects possession independently of privacy

**Holding:** Meaningful government-attributable interference with possession of a home is a seizure under the Fourth Amendment even without a search, invasion of privacy or liberty, or criminal investigation. The family’s alleged forcible dispossession meets that threshold, but coverage alone does not establish unreasonableness or every element of §1983 liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; nine Justices adopt the same rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Jacobsen, 466 U.S. 109 — applied; possession and privacy remain independently protected interests.
- United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696 — applied; seizure scrutiny does not require an accompanying search.
- Arizona v. Hicks, 480 U.S. 321 — applied; plain-view seizure remains subject to the Fourth Amendment.
- Adickes v. S. H. Kress & Co., 398 U.S. 144 — applied to the summary-judgment joint-action inference, without a final conspiracy finding.
- Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 — preserved; seizure coverage does not establish municipal liability.
- Wyatt v. Cole, May 18, 1992 — preserved; attribution does not settle immunity or a private merits defense.

##### A due-process characterization does not displace the specific seizure claim

**Holding:** A court may not reject an otherwise applicable Fourth Amendment claim merely because the same conduct also concerns a deprivation of property under the Due Process Clause. Each applicable constitutional protection must be examined on its own terms; this disposition does not adjudicate the family’s separate substantive-due-process claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Graham v. Connor, 490 U.S. 386 — applied; the specific Fourth Amendment source governs its covered conduct and is not displaced by a dominant-character theory.
- Parratt v. Taylor, 451 U.S. 527 — distinguished; postdeprivation procedural remedies do not answer the distinct seizure claim.
- Hudson v. Palmer, 468 U.S. 517 — confined to its prison setting for the relevant coverage proposition; no rule excluding possession of a home follows.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Private action alone is insufficient. Ultimate reasonableness, municipal policy, causation, immunity, damages and the separate substantive-due-process claim remain undecided. The holding addresses a home, without deciding coverage of every property category.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse adverse summary judgment and remand the seizure claim; no final finding of unreasonableness or award of damages.

### Personal Fourth Amendment rights

#### United States v. Padilla, 508 U.S. 77 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-207.
**Decided:** May 3, 1993.

##### Suppression depends on the defendant’s own protected interest

**Holding:** A defendant’s participation in a conspiracy neither creates nor defeats a personal Fourth Amendment claim: the defendant must show interference with his or her own protected property interest in a seizure or reasonable expectation of privacy in a search. The conspiracy exception is rejected, and the lower court must evaluate each reviewed defendant’s actual interests without reopening the unchallenged illegality of the stop.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Conspiracy neither supplies nor destroys an actual protected interest. The Court does not decide that every respondent lacks an interest or that all evidence must be admitted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for individual assessment of Donald and Maria Sylvia Simpson, Xavier Padilla, and Jorge and Maria Padilla. Strubbe’s unpetitioned loss and the unchallenged illegality of the stop remain untouched.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alderman v. United States, 394 U.S. 165: applies express rejection of vicarious coconspirator rights.
- Rakas v. Illinois, 439 U.S. 128; United States v. Salvucci, 448 U.S. 83; Rawlings v. Kentucky, 448 U.S. 98: applies individualized protected-interest inquiry.
- United States v. Jacobsen, 466 U.S. 109: preserves distinct search and seizure interests.
- Soldal v. Cook County, December 8, 1992: consistent possessory/privacy distinction; home holding not enlarged into categorical vehicle standing.

#### United States v. Wellons

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-496.
**Decided:** May 30, 1995.

##### Vehicle privacy and the rental agreement

**Holding:** Absence from a rental agreement does not by itself defeat a driver’s Fourth Amendment privacy claim when he possesses and controls the vehicle with the authorized renter’s permission and without theft, fraud or comparable wrongful possession. Courts must evaluate the actual legitimate relationship, including the right to exclude, the owner’s retained rights and any effective termination of possession; renter permission alone does not establish privacy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, joined in this proposition by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine directly adopt it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rental company's impoundment request may bear on whether possession remained legitimate at search time; its effect is not found here. Permission alone, title alone or the contract's name list alone does not settle the individual's privacy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Rakas v. Illinois, 439 U.S. 128: applies its requirement of the claimant’s own legitimate interest, rejecting proxy rights while refusing a contract-name shortcut.
- Rawlings v. Kentucky, 448 U.S. 98: applies the circumstance-specific privacy inquiry; possession or property alone is not conclusive.
- Minnesota v. Olson, 495 U.S. 91: extends its recognition that an invited user can have personal privacy without title to this bounded inquiry; it supplies no automatic rental-driver privacy.
- United States v. Padilla, May 3, 1993: applies the individualized search/seizure inquiry; no automatic derivative rights are created.

##### Independent privacy in closed personal luggage

**Holding:** A person’s lack of vehicle privacy does not by itself extinguish a legitimate expectation of privacy in that person’s closed luggage inside the vehicle. Ownership or lawful control and measures preserving privacy must be established independently, and a valid warrant exception reaching the container may still authorize its search.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, joined on this separate proposition by all eight Associate Justices; nine directly adopt it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Each claimed container requires its own ownership or lawful-control and privacy inquiry, including any disclaimer. The Court makes no ownership or container-specific probable-cause finding and does not restore Sanders's displaced automobile warrant rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Chadwick, 433 U.S. 1: preserves personal closed-luggage privacy as distinct from vehicle mobility, subject to the later applicable automobile rule.
- Rawlings v. Kentucky, 448 U.S. 98: applies the requirement of a demonstrated personal privacy interest; ownership or lawful control and privacy measures require assessment.
- California v. Acevedo, 500 U.S. 565: preserves automobile probable-cause authority to search containers within its scope; personal container privacy does not impose a categorical warrant requirement.
- Arkansas v. Sanders, 442 U.S. 753: retains container privacy only consistently with Acevedo’s displacement of its contrary automobile warrant rule; that displaced rule is not revived.

##### Remedy after rejection of the categorical grounds

**Holding:** When the reviewed categorical privacy grounds are erroneous but personal interests and preserved search justifications remain unresolved, the judgment must be vacated and remanded for those determinations before a suppression consequence is imposed. A recognized interest does not foreclose lawful probable-cause search, consent, standardized noninvestigatory inventory after lawful custody, or another preserved warrant exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight Associate Justices; nine directly adopt this case’s remedial holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The company's request is not itself lawful impoundment or consent to search every personal bag. A dog alert does not establish legality of the preceding detention or sniff or unlimited probable-cause scope.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- California v. Acevedo, 500 U.S. 565: applies the distinction between an interest and a lawful probable-cause search reaching a container; no automatic suppression follows recognition of privacy.
- United States v. Padilla, May 3, 1993: applies its individual-interest remand discipline without deciding unproved exception predicates.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the conviction affirmance and remand for the claimant's legitimate vehicle possession and personal closed-luggage interests, including permission, theft, fraud, comparable wrongful possession or effective repossession. Determine lawful impoundment; detention and dog-sniff legality and consequences; probable cause and container scope; lawful consent and scope; standardized noninvestigatory inventory; or another preserved warrant exception. Suppression depends on supported findings and law, with ordinary harmlessness if applicable. No acquittal, automatic exclusion, new exclusionary exception or resentencing is ordered.

### Tactile recognition during a protective frisk

#### Minnesota v. Dickerson, 508 U.S. 366 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2019.
**Decided:** June 7, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in Minnesota v. Dickerson, June 7, 1993, No. 91-2019, see Federal Courts — Collateral consequences of diversion.

##### Authorized touch can supply probable cause without an additional search

**Holding:** An officer lawfully conducting a protective patdown may seize contraband when the authorized touching itself supplies probable cause of its incriminating character and the officer has lawful access to it. The officer may not manipulate or otherwise explore a nonthreatening object beyond the frisk to obtain that probable cause; immediate recognition requires probable cause, not certainty.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Part II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Terry v. Ohio, 392 U.S. 1: the protective purpose limits the scope of the frisk.
- Michigan v. Long, 463 U.S. 1032: seizure of contraband found within an authorized protective search applied.
- Horton v. California, 496 U.S. 128: lawful access and immediately apparent incriminating character extended to lawful touch.
- Arizona v. Hicks, 480 U.S. 321: probable cause may not be manufactured through a separate unauthorized search.
- Soldal v. Cook County, December 8, 1992: independent possessory protection preserved; no enlargement of a frisk follows.

##### Manipulation after excluding a weapon requires suppression here

**Holding:** When the accepted findings establish that the officer identified contraband only by squeezing and manipulating an object after determining that it was not a weapon, the examination exceeds Terry and cannot justify the seizure. That independent ground sustains suppression here despite the state court’s erroneous categorical rejection of tactile recognition.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Parts III and IV; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Probable cause must arise from authorized contact, with lawful access; exploratory touching is not a new exception. The diversion record’s identified legal uses, rather than an invented conviction, support review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm suppression on the accepted finding of manipulation after the weapon concern ended; no further evidentiary opportunity for the State is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Terry, 392 U.S. 1: protective authority ended when the object was known not to be a weapon.
- Hicks, 480 U.S. 321: information obtained by the additional exploration cannot validate it retrospectively.

### Custodial interrogation

#### Stansbury v. California, 511 U.S. 318

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5770.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

##### Custody turns on the circumstances communicated to the person questioned

**Holding:** Miranda custody exists upon formal arrest or restraint on freedom of movement of the degree associated with formal arrest, assessed from all objective circumstances as a reasonable person in the interviewee’s position would understand them; an officer’s uncommunicated view that the person is or is not a suspect, and the interviewee’s undisclosed subjective belief, have no independent role. An officer’s words or conduct conveying suspicion matter only to the extent they affect that objective understanding: communicated suspicion is not automatically custody, and undisclosed beliefs bearing on a witness’s credibility do not thereby become independent custody factors.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt the rule and reversal with remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966): applied to the custodial-interrogation trigger; no new exception to warnings is created.
- Oregon v. Mathiason, 429 U.S. 492 (1977), and California v. Beheler, 463 U.S. 1121 (1983): applied for the arrest-equivalent restraint standard; station questioning and suspect status alone remain insufficient.
- Berkemer v. McCarty, 468 U.S. 420 (1984): applied to exclude uncommunicated intentions from the objective custody inquiry.
- Beckwith v. United States, 425 U.S. 341 (1976): applied to distinguish investigative focus from custody.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual custody, independent coercion, admissibility, harmlessness and capital punishment are not decided. An undisclosed belief bearing on witness credibility does not become an independent custody factor. Withrow and Powell's different review and remedy rules remain unchanged.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The California Supreme Court judgment is reversed and remanded to reconsider custody without the private investigative-focus criterion. No suppression, new trial, acquittal, release or resentencing is ordered; ultimate custody and any properly reached consequences remain for the state court.

#### Davis v. United States, 512 U.S. 452

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1949.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### A reasonable possible present request requires neutral clarification

**Holding:** After a valid waiver, words that a reasonable officer would understand in context as possibly requesting counsel for the present interrogation require a pause in substantive questioning and limited, neutral clarification; a passing unrelated reference to a lawyer or speculation about an unexpressed wish does not. Officers may resume after a clarified voluntary choice to proceed, but must maintain the pause while a reasonable possible present request remains unresolved, and may not demand justification for counsel, promise benefits for continuing, discourage counsel, predict counsel's advice, or repeatedly pressure the suspect under the guise of clarification.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt the mandatory clarification rule, its trigger and its limits; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas decline this part.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966): develops its protection of an effective custodial counsel choice through the stated postwaiver clarification rule.
- Edwards v. Arizona, 451 U.S. 477 (1981): preserves cessation upon actual invocation; clarification addresses the antecedent genuinely uncertain choice.
- Smith v. Illinois, 469 U.S. 91 (1984): preserves its prohibition on using later answers to defeat an initially clear invocation and resolves the distinct ambiguity question it left open.
- Connecticut v. Barrett, 479 U.S. 523 (1987): applies its attention to the scope of the suspect's communicated choice, without requiring legal terminology.
- McNeil v. Wisconsin, 501 U.S. 171 (1991): preserves the need for an actual expression concerning custodial counsel and does not treat a different judicial-proceeding request as such an expression.

##### The actual clarification and ensuing statements were lawful

**Holding:** Following Davis's valid initial waiver and genuinely uncertain first reference to counsel, the agents lawfully paused offense questioning, neutrally clarified that he was not requesting counsel, and resumed questioning; their later brief reminder did not supply a new full warning or new written waiver. Admission of the challenged November 4 statements therefore supplies no ground to reverse the military judgment on this invocation theory, without using later answers to defeat an initially clear request.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this actual-clarification application and affirmance; the four Justices who reject a duty accept the permissibility of the clarification actually used.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Connecticut v. Barrett: applies respect for the scope of the actual communicated choice to the credited clarification and disclaimer.
- Smith v. Illinois: distinguishes genuine clarification of uncertainty from impermissible later-answer erosion of a clear invocation.

##### A clear request requires cessation and cannot be undone by later answers

**Holding:** A clear request for custodial counsel stops police-initiated substantive interrogation unless counsel is present or the suspect initiates further communication and validly waives the protection under the governing rules; no prescribed legal formula is required. An initially clear request cannot be rendered ambiguous by later answers elicited in violation of that protection, and Davis's later clear request was honored by ending the interview.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this preservation and application of the clear-invocation rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Edwards v. Arizona: applies and preserves cessation, counsel-presence and suspect-initiation/valid-waiver requirements.
- Smith v. Illinois: applies and preserves the ban on undoing a clear initial invocation through later answers elicited in violation of counsel protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides postwaiver invocation in the Miranda/Edwards framework accepted by the parties and applied through the military rules. It does not independently resolve direct Fifth Amendment operation in military proceedings, Sixth Amendment attachment, prewaiver conduct, initial-warning adequacy, invalid initial waiver or the separate October 20 interview and evidence dispute. Section 3501 was not the Government's merits ground and supplies no alternative holding on construction, applicability or constitutionality. Withrow's collateral and coercion rules and Stansbury's objective custody inquiry remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court of Military Appeals is affirmed unanimously on the postwaiver invocation issue. No exclusion, new trial or remand follows; the separate October 20 dispute and any §3501 ground remain undecided.

### Federal presentment and confessions

#### United States v. Alvarez-Sanchez, 511 U.S. 350

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1812.

**Decided:** 1994-05-02.

##### The relevant presentment duty precedes the measurement of delay

**Holding:** In a federal or District of Columbia prosecution, detention solely on state charges does not itself start §3501(c)’s federal-presentment period; the period concerns delay in performing the duty to bring the person before an officer empowered to commit defendants for federal or District of Columbia offenses. Any law-enforcement officer can hold a person on a basis or arrangement engaging that duty, but suspicion of a federal offense, ordinary interagency notification or a state label alone does not decide it; the Court does not make formal federal charges or arrest indispensable in every possible arrangement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion for the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; all nine adopt the same bounded rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McNabb v. United States, 318 U.S. 332 (1943), and Mallory v. United States, 354 U.S. 449 (1957): used to identify the federal-presentment setting; their complete relationship with §3501 is not decided.
- Anderson v. United States, 318 U.S. 350 (1943): preserved as the relevant improper-working-arrangement authority; the effect of §3501 on its rule and the full range of qualifying arrangements remain open.
- Stansbury v. California, decided April 26, 1994: distinguished; objective Miranda custody does not itself establish the separate federal-presentment duty.

##### The limited statutory protection does not decide all confession admissibility

**Holding:** Section 3501(c) prevents exclusion solely for delay in presentment when the trial judge finds the confession voluntary, its weight is left to the jury, and it was made within six hours of the relevant arrest or detention; the six-hour limit does not apply when the judge finds the longer delay reasonable considering the means of transportation and distance to the nearest available qualifying magistrate or other committing officer. The Court does not decide the treatment of confessions obtained more than six hours after relevant federal arrest outside that proviso, the general relationship among subsections (a), (b) and (c), or §3501’s entire relationship to Miranda.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this limited statutory explanation. Ginsburg’s concurrence, joined by Blackmun, emphasizes the reservation without changing either Justice’s full join or supplying a separate controlling answer.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Withrow v. Williams, 507 U.S. 680 (1993): preserved in its distinction between Miranda and independent coercion; no new collateral-review rule is announced.
- Gerstein v. Pugh, 420 U.S. 103 (1975), and County of Riverside v. McLaughlin, 500 U.S. 44 (1991): their constitutional rules remain unchanged; the unpreserved alternative is not adjudicated here.
- Powell v. Nevada, decided March 30, 1994: preserved with its separate timing and remedy questions; it does not excuse the missing presentation of this alternative below.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reported district-court no-collusion finding is procedural posture, not an adopted correctness finding. Section 3501's effect on Anderson, the full range of improper arrangements, post-six-hour confessions outside the proviso, and the statute's general relationship to Miranda remain unresolved. A waiver does not settle independent coercion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and remanded on the state-detention aggregation error. No admission or suppression of the confession, reinstatement of conviction, retrial, acquittal or release is directed. The Gerstein/McLaughlin alternative, unpresented below, is not adjudicated or affirmatively reopened.

### Prompt judicial probable-cause review

#### Powell v. Nevada, 511 U.S. 79

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8841.

**Decided:** 1994-03-30.

##### McLaughlin applies to a case still pending on direct review

**Holding:** A criminal defendant whose judgment was not final when McLaughlin issued receives that decision's prompt-probable-cause-review rule on direct review, even if the arrest or prosecution began earlier and even assuming the decision announced a new rule. The prosecution's commencement date does not permit withholding the governing rule; applying it does not itself determine the remedy for a violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's direct-review portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; all nine adopt this proposition. Thomas separately opposes vacatur on a remedial ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Griffith v. Kentucky, 479 U.S. 314 (1987): applied directly to the nonfinal criminal judgment; the commencement-date cutoff is rejected.
- County of Riverside v. McLaughlin, 500 U.S. 44 (1991): applied to Powell without deciding whether it announced a new rule.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993): its equal-application principle and separation of genuine remedy questions are preserved.

##### The four-day interval requires justification, with remedy separately determined on remand

**Holding:** A judicial probable-cause determination within forty-eight hours of a warrantless arrest generally complies with the prompt-review requirement, but the arrestee may prove that the actual delay was unreasonable—for example, because it served to gather evidence justifying arrest, arose from ill will or occurred for delay's own sake, without limiting the challenge to those examples; after forty-eight hours the government must prove a bona fide emergency or other extraordinary circumstance, and intervening weekends or the administrative convenience of combining procedures do not alone excuse the delay. Powell's four-day interval therefore requires that justification inquiry; the Court vacates for consideration under this rule and properly available remaining grounds without deciding suppression, causal product, attenuation, preservation, good-faith reliance or harmlessness.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's timing-and-remand portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; eight Justices adopt this bounded disposition and reservation of remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gerstein v. Pugh, 420 U.S. 103 (1975): applied to neutral judicial review, while preserving that unlawful detention alone does not void a conviction.
- County of Riverside v. McLaughlin: applied with all timing qualifications and the government's post-forty-eight-hour burden unchanged.
- New York v. Harris, 495 U.S. 14 (1990): retained as a distinct protected-interest and remedial analogy; its home-entry facts do not settle this statement's causal source.
- Segura v. United States, 468 U.S. 796 (1984), and United States v. Crews, 445 U.S. 463 (1980): causal-product principles remain available without a new application finding here.
- Illinois v. Krull, 480 U.S. 340 (1987): its separate statutory-reliance defense remains available for lawful consideration, without a merits ruling on its predicates.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A police declaration does not perform neutral judicial review or restart the clock. Personal appearance is separate. A state-law arraignment waiver does not itself waive federal prompt-review rights; a prearrest statement alone does not establish federal harmlessness. Suppression, causal product, attenuation, properly available preservation, good-faith reliance and harmlessness remain separate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Nevada Supreme Court judgment is vacated and the case remanded, 8–1. Timing and lawful justification must be addressed before any necessary remaining remedial issues. No suppression, new trial, acquittal, release, damages or capital resentencing is ordered by this disposition.

### Court-employee warrant-record errors

#### Arizona v. Evans

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1660.
**Decided:** March 1, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Federal Courts — Adequate and independent state grounds (Federal review of the suppression judgment).

##### A qualifying court-employee error and objectively reasonable reliance

**Holding:** The federal exclusionary rule does not require suppression for a clerical warrant-record error attributable to a non-law-enforcement court employee when the arresting officer's reliance on the apparently current warrant information is objectively reasonable. The rule does not validate the arrest, rest on subjective innocence alone, resolve police-generated or mixed errors, or excuse reliance made unreasonable by known or sufficiently evident defects in the information system.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Leon is extended to the bounded court-employee setting while retaining its objective limits; Sheppard supports reasonable reliance despite judicial clerical error. Calandra and Krull preserve the separate remedial inquiry. Whiteley's rule concerning the invalidity of originating information remains; its treatment of violation and suppression as synonymous does not determine this post-Leon remedy. Hensley found no constitutional violation and did not settle the distinct suppression question here.

##### The rule requires a limited remand, not an admission order

**Holding:** Because the trial court expressly left the source of the warrant-record error unresolved, reversal of Arizona's categorical federal exclusion rule requires remand for proper application of the court-employee/objective-reliance rule. The rejected intermediate attribution inference, the street officer's ordinary response to the reported warrant, and the clerk's frequency estimate do not establish sole court fault, a reliably maintained system or entitlement to final admission.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Leon's distinction between rule and application governs. Dickerson preserves the importance of supported findings under the governing search rule; it does not supply a computer-error exception or authorize an appellate finding on a question the trial judge left unresolved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Leon retains its exclusions for an affidavit knowingly or recklessly misleading the magistrate, abandonment of the neutral judicial role, an affidavit so lacking in probable-cause indicia that reliance is entirely unreasonable, and a facially deficient warrant on which an officer cannot reasonably rely. Its rule assumes proper execution within the warrant's scope and considers relevant officers who obtain or supply information as well as the executing officer. The present court-employee rule adds no immunity for police recordkeeping, mixed responsibility, deliberate disregard of defects or objectively unreasonable system reliance. The Court decides neither who caused this entry to remain nor any universal error frequency, statewide maintenance standard, guilt, civil damages, renewed state-law ground or general database-safeguards test. Full Arizona statutory text is not construed beyond the categories quoted and treated in the lower opinion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Six Justices reverse and remand Arizona's suppression judgment; one would affirm and two would dismiss. The source of the error remains unresolved and must be considered under the bounded court-employee/objective-reliance rule. No final admission, conviction, sole-court-fault finding or renewed independent state-law ground is ordered.

### Thermal examination of a closed home

#### United States v. Pinson

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-164.
**Decided:** May 8, 1995.

##### The targeted examination of this closed home's heat patterns is a search

**Holding:** A targeted technological examination of a closed home constitutes a Fourth Amendment search when, as here, its reported heat-pattern information reveals otherwise unobservable information concerning concealed domestic operation. External reception and the absence of an image identifying a person or object do not defeat protection; investigative purpose or use of equipment alone is insufficient, and the Court adopts no general rule governing every sensor or exterior emission.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Katz v. United States, 389 U.S. 347: applied; privacy protection does not require physical penetration of the home.
- United States v. Karo, 468 U.S. 705, Part III: applied to the reported concealed domestic information; its protection does not depend on the information arriving as an ordinary visual image.
- Arizona v. Hicks, 480 U.S. 321: applied by analogy to the additional examination of concealed information and its rejection of an unimportant-information exemption; no physical manipulation is found here.
- Florida v. Riley, 488 U.S. 445: distinguished as naked-eye observation through actual openings; its opinions supply none of this record's missing altitude or public-flight facts.
- California v. Greenwood, 486 U.S. 35: distinguished; purposeful placement of garbage for collection does not establish surrender of domestic information conveyed by heat.
- United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696: distinguished; the narrow contraband-information character of the sniff is absent from this heat examination.
- Dow Chemical Co. v. United States, 476 U.S. 227: distinguished; the outdoor industrial photographs do not authorize this examination of a closed dwelling.
- Minnesota v. Dickerson, decided June 7, 1993: consistent with the distinction between information obtained through authorized observation and additional unauthorized probing; no tactile search is presented.

##### No warrant exception justifies this scan on the established record

**Holding:** This search of protected home information required a warrant unless a recognized exception applied. The July 25 scan had no warrant, and no consent, exigency, or other recognized exception is established; the subsequent warrant for physical entry does not retroactively authorize the scan.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: six Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Katz v. United States, 389 U.S. 347: applied; protected surveillance is subject to the warrant requirement and its established exceptions.
- United States v. Karo, 468 U.S. 705, Part III: applied; the warrant presumption for concealed home information retains recognized exceptions, including true exigency, none established here.

##### Correct the preserved search ruling without reopening unpresented warrant questions

**Holding:** The Eighth Circuit's judgment is vacated insofar as its rejection of the preserved suppression claim and affirmance of the conviction rest on the categorical no-search ground, and the case is remanded for lawful disposition of that claim under the corrected search rule. Pinson's failure to challenge affidavit sufficiency on appeal remains operative: the mandate directs no new excision-and-probable-cause hearing, revives no waived or forfeited argument automatically, and permits further warrant, suppression, or reliance questions only to the extent properly available under the existing record and ordinary preservation and remedial law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Karo, 468 U.S. 705, Part IV: retained as the distinct possibility of sufficient untainted warrant information; no determination of Pinson's affidavit or entitlement to a new hearing follows.
- United States v. Olano, decided April 26, 1993: retained; intentional waiver differs from forfeiture. If Rule 52(b) is actually invoked, error, plainness, ordinarily outcome-affecting prejudice, and the separate discretionary inquiry concerning fairness, integrity, or public reputation of judicial proceedings must be satisfied as applicable. This mandate does not establish any predicate or revive an argument automatically.
- United States v. Leon, 468 U.S. 897: left available only if procedurally open and factually supported. Objective reliance affords no protection for knowing or reckless deception of the magistrate, abandonment of judicial neutrality, an affidavit so lacking probable-cause indicia that reliance is wholly unreasonable, or facial warrant deficiency precluding reasonable reliance. The inquiry includes information-obtaining and supplying officers and assumes execution within the warrant's scope; no final reliance finding is made.
- Arizona v. Evans, decided March 1, 1995: confined to qualifying non-law-enforcement court-employee clerical error plus objectively reasonable reliance. Reserved police or mixed institutional errors supply no technology exception for this deliberate scan, and no final exclusion ruling follows here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the challenged suppression ruling and conviction affirmance only insofar as they rest on the no-search ground, and remand for lawful disposition of the preserved claim. The appellate failure to challenge affidavit sufficiency remains operative. No new excision hearing, automatic suppression, acquittal, release, trial or replacement sentence is ordered; the separately affirmed lesser-harms sentencing ruling receives no vacatur.

#### United States v. Myers

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit 94-1912; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied
**Decided:** June 11, 1996

##### Is targeted thermal acquisition of concealed domestic operation a search?

**Holding:** A targeted technological examination of a closed home's heat patterns is a search when it reveals otherwise unobservable information about concealed domestic operation. Passive external reception and absence of a recognizable interior image do not defeat protection; equipment use or investigative purpose alone does not establish a search.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven.

**Material application:** The Agema 210 pattern revealed operation of indoor cultivation lights not revealed by ordinary outside observation. Roof vents, heat emissions and lack of curbside garbage did not establish surrender of the underlying domestic information.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No rule governing every instrument, emission, visible smoke observation, market availability or future technological capability is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pinson, May 8, 1995, is applied without change. Katz protects concealed domestic information, and Karo confirms technological acquisition can occur without physical entry. Greenwood's discarded garbage and Place's contraband-confined detection are distinguished.

##### Can a later entry warrant authorize the earlier thermal search?

**Holding:** The scan required a warrant unless a recognized exception applied; none was established, and the later entry warrant could not retroactively authorize it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; six. Stone-Zsela does not join this additional ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pinson's warrant requirement controls. Whren requires authority for the particular intrusion; its ordinary traffic-stop rule supplies none for this home scan.

##### What follows for the preserved derivative-warrant suppression claim?

**Holding:** The judgment rejecting a preserved suppression claim on the erroneous no-search ground must be vacated so the actual claim can be decided under the proper search rule and independently supported, procedurally available remedial grounds. Remand neither presumes exclusion nor creates a new defense or revives a waived claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part III, the same seven as Part I.

**Material application:** Myers preserved the contention that the later warrant depended on the scan; Pinson's different affidavit nonchallenge does not defeat that reservation. The record establishes no final untainted sufficiency, objective reliance, independent source or inevitable discovery finding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Leon remains available only on supported facts and proper presentation; it does not protect knowing or reckless deception, an abandoned neutral judicial role, an affidavit wholly lacking reasonable probable-cause indicia, or a facially deficient warrant precluding reasonable reliance. Its inquiry includes obtaining and information-supplying officers and assumes execution within scope. Evans's qualifying non-law-enforcement court-clerical error does not excuse a deliberate police scan. New genuinely forfeited issues remain subject to Olano's error, plainness, substantial-rights and separate discretionary fairness/integrity/public-reputation requirements; intentional waiver extinguishes the error. Those requirements do not attach to the preserved claim. Rule 11(a)(2) permits plea withdrawal upon prevailing on appeal; this limited correction does not itself order it.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Partial vacatur and remand, 7–2, of the suppression ruling and dependent conviction affirmance. No final admission or exclusion, acquittal, release, retrial, new sentence, mandatory hearing, discovery or automatic affidavit-excision proceeding is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pinson's separate remedy inquiry and Karo's possible untainted-warrant inquiry remain. Leon, Evans and Olano retain their actual predicates; Ornelas's legal/factual allocation neither insulates categorical legal error nor changes review of an issuing magistrate's warrant determination.

### Announcement before home entry

#### Wilson v. Arkansas

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-5707.
**Decided:** May 22, 1995.

##### Announcement and reasonable home entry

**Holding:** Common-law announcement of identity and authority forms part of Fourth Amendment reasonableness when officers enter a dwelling. Supported countervailing circumstances, including danger, futility, escape or threatened destruction of evidence, may justify unannounced entry; actual justification and remedy remain for the state court.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The exact quantum of suspicion required for each exception and whether every violation requires suppression remain undecided. The federal entry statutes' every detail is not constitutionalized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miller v. United States, 357 U.S. 301: supplies common-law announcement background; federal statutory particulars are not all constitutionalized.
- Sabbath v. United States, 391 U.S. 585: supplies federal statutory entry analysis without deciding this constitutional application.
- Ker v. California, 374 U.S. 23: applies state-entry reasonableness with circumstance-dependent excuses.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for the state court to evaluate the actual entry, record-supported excuses and remedy. No violation, automatic suppression or final vacation of the conviction is established.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Richards v. Wisconsin, April 28, 1997, resolves the reserved quantum for the identified warrant-execution exceptions as particularized reasonable suspicion and rejects the categorical felony-drug exception; constitutional exclusion after a violation remains open.

### Student searches and athletic drug testing

#### Vernonia School District 47J v. Acton

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-590.
**Decided:** June 26, 1995.

For the other questions in Vernonia School District 47J v. Acton, decided June 26, 1995, No. 94-590. see Federal Courts — Scope of appellate disposition (Federal correction requires reconsideration of the Oregon ground).

##### Urine testing is a search; athletic participation is no waiver

**Holding:** Collection and chemical analysis of a student's urine are Fourth Amendment searches. Enrollment or voluntary athletic participation does not waive that protection; the school and athletic setting instead informs the reasonableness inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine Justices adopt the search and no-waiver rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Voluntary participation informs expectations but is not consent dispensing with constitutional reasonableness. Unrelated medical investigation and ordinary police use are not authorized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Skinner v. Railway Labor Executives' Association, 489 U.S. 602: applied; urine collection and chemical analysis each constitute searches.
- New Jersey v. T.L.O., 469 U.S. 325: applied; school warrant and probable-cause adjustments preserve constitutional reasonableness rather than extinguishing protection.

##### This interscholastic-athlete testing program is reasonable

**Holding:** In this documented school-athletic setting, objective seasonal and random urinalysis is reasonable without individualized suspicion because the protective safety need, athletic privacy expectations, limited collection and drug information, confidentiality and restricted counseling or athletic consequences together justify the intrusion. The holding concerns this interscholastic program and record; it creates no authority to test every student, conduct unrelated medical searches or use results for ordinary law enforcement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt the rule and application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual policy qualifications below bound this interscholastic-athlete holding. All-student programs, other extracurricular populations, materially more intrusive practices and other uses remain undecided. No universal exhaustion of less intrusive alternatives, confirmed drug catastrophe or quantified efficacy showing is required. Stone-Zsela's necessity/proportionality proposal and the suspicion-centered dissent are not law.

**Material application:** The challenged policy concerns interscholastic athletes, not all extracurricular participants or the separate voluntary all-student program. Testing occurs initially each season and by weekly random selection of about 10 percent of competing athletes. A student draw with adult witnesses conceals selected identities and leaves no individualized official selection discretion. Boys remain clothed with backs toward a monitor, whose presence permits hearing and sometimes observing production but not viewing genitals. Girls produce in enclosed stalls with a monitor outside who can hear. These are actual privacy intrusions. Initial refusal excludes the athlete for the current season.

The basic laboratory targets are amphetamines, marijuana and cocaine; occasional District-requested LSD testing also occurs. Alcohol and steroids are not tested. Anonymous laboratory numbers, restricted school-result access, one-year retention and no police transmission or suspension, expulsion or general discipline confine the use. Prescription verification permits a prescription copy OR doctor's authorization. Failure to verify combined with a positive result triggers positive-test procedures, not automatic exclusion merely for missing verification. The written policy permits a second test and directs no action if it is negative; testified operation supplies confirmation of positive results. The Court does not rewrite written permission into a mandatory statutory retest rule. Parents are notified and a hearing follows confirmed positives. First offense permits counseling with six weekly tests OR current-and-next-season athletic suspension. Written second-offense consequences are current-and-next-season suspension, while testimony describes a counseling/weekly-testing alternative in practice. Third offense excludes the current and next two seasons. Retesting occurs before the next eligible season.

The trial findings establish increased drug use and discipline difficulties, athletes leading drug groups, observed impaired safety performance and previously attempted educational and deterrent responses. A suspected drug-related wrestling injury is not a toxicologically established causal finding. No finding establishes that Acton uses drugs, that every athlete caused the problem, or that the program has a quantified efficacy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- New Jersey v. T.L.O., 469 U.S. 325: applied for school responsibilities and contextual reasonableness; its reservation of suspicionless searches is resolved only for this program.
- Skinner v. Railway Labor Executives' Association, 489 U.S. 602: applied; a substantiated protective need can justify suspicionless testing after attention to actual privacy burdens and safeguards.
- National Treasury Employees Union v. Von Raab, 489 U.S. 656: applied; special-needs balancing is contextual, not confined to a universally required catastrophic-risk category.

##### The facial prescription-verification objection does not invalidate the policy

**Holding:** The prescription-verification provision permits a prescription copy or a doctor's authorization and does not require personal delivery to teachers or coaches. On this facial policy challenge the Court may consider a confidential means of compliance and does not presume the most intrusive implementation; the rule does not approve every specimen form, nonprescription disclosure, physician-name demand or actual personal-disclosure practice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt this limited facial rule. Stone-Zsela does not join it. It independently answers this objection, not the entire testing challenge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A confidential method, including sealed laboratory delivery, is textually possible, not found to have been used. Confidentiality does not erase the information-privacy intrusion. The complete specimen form is not established and actual more intrusive implementation remains undecided; no separate surviving disclosure claim is created.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Skinner v. Railway Labor Executives' Association, 489 U.S. 602: applied at the narrow medication-information context; no general approval of unrelated medical disclosure follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Ninth Circuit judgment and remand for independent reconsideration of the Oregon constitutional question after correction of its federal premise. No unconditional reinstatement of final dismissal, athletic-eligibility remedy, damages, refund, drug finding, program redesign or reopening of the rejected school-authority issue follows.

### Federal habeas review of Miranda custody

#### Thompson v. Keohane, 516 U.S. 99

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-6615.
**Decided:** November 29, 1995.

##### Independent determination of ultimate custody and statutory protection of historical facts

**Holding:** Whether established interrogation circumstances would cause a reasonable person to understand freedom of movement as restrained to the degree of formal arrest is a mixed constitutional question for independent federal habeas review. The governing §2254(d) factual presumption does not attach to that ultimate custody conclusion; historical events and supported credibility findings remain subject to the statute's conditions and exceptions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct votes. Thomas dissents.

**Current-force modification:** The original statutory factual-presumption and independent-legal-review analysis remains controlling for proceedings governed by former §2254. AEDPA §104 replaces that scheme: new §2254(e)(1) presumes a state factual determination correct and places on the applicant the burden of rebuttal by clear and convincing evidence. New §2254(d) separately forbids granting relief on a claim adjudicated on the merits in state court unless the decision was contrary to or unreasonably applied clearly established federal law determined by this Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the state-court evidence. These new conditions are not the former eight exceptions; enactment supplies no judicial construction of their terms and does not reclassify the underlying constitutional question as a historical fact. The April 24 source admission does not decide this amendment's temporal application to a particular pending proceeding; the original adjudication is unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The historical-fact presumption requires a competent state court's factual determination after a merits hearing involving the applicant and State or its officer or agent, with a written finding, opinion or other reliable adequate written indicia. It yields when the applicant establishes, it otherwise appears or the respondent admits: unresolved factual-dispute merits; an inadequate full-and-fair factfinding procedure; inadequate development of material facts; lack of subject-matter or applicant jurisdiction; unconstitutional denial of appointed counsel to an indigent; absence of a full, fair and adequate hearing; or other denial of due process. The eighth exception requires production of the pertinent state record as the statute provides and lack of fair support for the finding in the record as a whole. At a federal evidentiary hearing, after due proof of the state determination, the applicant must prove error by convincing evidence unless one of the first seven circumstances is shown, appears or is admitted, or the federal court finds the eighth. No exception is established here; a paper ruling alone establishes none.

**Material application:** The federal courts incorrectly presumed the ultimate noncustody conclusion correct. Voluntary arrival, accusations, duration, setting, communicated freedom to leave and actual departure require consideration together; undisclosed intentions or unsupported later-surveillance allegations do not become communicated custody circumstances.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand, 8–1, for independent ultimate custody review. No finding of custody, suppression, harmlessness, coercion, retroactivity, entitlement to a writ or need for a new evidentiary hearing follows; no abandoned default revives.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Miller v. Fenton's functional law/fact inquiry applies; Berkemer and Stansbury preserve the objective arrest-equivalence standard. Patton and Witt retain their distinct factual classifications and do not make every legal-standard inference factual. Wright supplies no general reasonable-state-decision deference rule. Withrow permits Miranda habeas review subject to ordinary limits and separate presentation of coercion claims.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996, Pub. L. 104-132, Title I, enacted April 24, 1996: §104, the former §2254(d) custody-review setting and new §2254(d), (e)(1). This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

### Appellate review of suspicion and probable cause

#### Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690

**Docket or dockets:** 95-5257; Seventh Circuit Nos. 94-3349 and 94-3350
**Decided:** May 28, 1996

##### Which suppression questions remain preserved for appellate review through these conditional pleas?

**Holding:** Review encompasses both ultimate reasonable suspicion for the initial stop and ultimate probable cause for the warrantless search, as preserved in these conditional pleas. Agreement between the parties about a proposed review standard neither determines that legal standard nor expands review to unpreserved discovery or suppression-merits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part I, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 11(a)(2) permits a written reservation of specified adverse pretrial determinations with the court's approval and the Government's consent. The selected orders establish preservation of both thresholds here; incompleteness of other documents does not establish litigant default. The Court reviews the allocation of appellate responsibility, not a newly presented database-discovery question or the ultimate suppression outcome.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Success in obtaining the proper review standard does not itself establish a right to withdraw the pleas. The ordinary requirement of prevailing on the reserved issue remains.

##### How must appellate courts review reasonable suspicion and probable cause for a warrantless search?

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently determine the ultimate constitutional sufficiency of reasonable suspicion for a stop and probable cause for a warrantless search under the objective totality of the circumstances. Historical facts, credibility determinations and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences receive clear-error protection, while grounded contextual inferences drawn by resident judges and experienced officers receive genuine due weight without deference to the ultimate constitutional conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts II-III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight-Justice majority. Scalia dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** Independent review develops workable constitutional standards and permits comparison of recurring circumstances. The reviewing court must nevertheless respect what occurred, the trial court's credibility role, local context and grounded police experience. Miller v. Fenton and Thompson v. Keohane support allocating mixed questions by function rather than a single label; First Options preserves separate legal and factual review. Brinegar, Gates, Sokolow and White supply the substantive totality inquiry but do not decide who finally applies it on appeal. Gates's substantial-basis review of a magistrate's warrant decision concerns a different institutional task. Mere acknowledgment of an officer's inference is insufficient due weight, but experience cannot manufacture an unproved fact or entitle the officer's ultimate conclusion to a presumption of correctness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** There is no numerical weighting formula, intermediate standard, new evidentiary fact or police-correctness presumption. Pierce and Cooter & Gell retain deference for their task-specific discretionary determinations; Salve Regina's treatment of legal review remains. The Court imports neither habeas burdens from Thompson nor a general solution to every mixed question; Markman's reserved appellate issue remains open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Miller v. Fenton and Thompson v. Keohane supply functional allocation of legal and factual review; Thompson's habeas setting does not transfer its burdens here.
- First Options preserves independent legal review and protection of historical findings.
- Brinegar, Gates, Sokolow and White retain objective totality-of-circumstances substantive standards.
- Gates's warrant-issuance substantial-basis rule is distinguished from independent review of a warrantless search.
- Pierce and Cooter & Gell retain their task-specific deference; Salve Regina supports independent legal review.
- Markman does not decide the reserved appellate-review question by analogy alone.

##### What follows for these two appeals and the district court's suppression findings?

**Holding:** Both appellate judgments must be vacated and reconsidered under this allocation, without automatically ordering suppression or reopening the evidentiary record. Under section 636(b)(1)(B), the district judge independently determines specifically objected-to portions of the magistrate's recommendation and may receive evidence or recommit; that duty does not invariably require a new hearing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part III, joined by the same eight-Justice majority.

**Controlling explanation:** The district court accepted the credited loose-panel finding; rust was rejected, scratches were inconclusive, and actual NADDIS identification was unproved. Independent legal review cannot improve those facts by invoking experience. Raddatz permits the district judge's specified independent determination without an invariably repeated hearing; its concern about rejecting credibility findings is not triggered by the acceptance here. Section 636(b)(1)(A) does not set the ultimate suppression standard or the appellate allocation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes the circuit's limited-consent premise: consent did not authorize dismantling the panel. It adopts no general consent rule and does not revive waived inevitable discovery. The allocation also governs authorized Government suppression appeals, but section 3731's actual conditions remain: the statutory restriction after jeopardy attaches and before verdict, required certification of no delay and substantial material proof, the thirty-day and diligence requirements, and double-jeopardy limits. No independent Government appeal is authorized here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Seventh Circuit judgments, reported at 16 F.3d 714 and 52 F.3d 328, are vacated and remanded, 8-1. The Court orders reconsideration of both constitutional thresholds, not suppression, plea withdrawal, release, a new evidentiary hearing or a particular ultimate result.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Raddatz preserves the distinction between a required independent judicial determination and an invariably new hearing; its reserved credibility-rejection question remains untriggered.

### Automobile searches

#### Pennsylvania v. Labron; Pennsylvania v. Kilgore, 518 U.S. 938 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-1691 and 95-1738.
**Decided:** July 1, 1996.

##### Whether probable cause and ready mobility require an additional federal exigency

**Holding:** Police may search a readily mobile automobile without a warrant when probable cause supports belief that it contains evidence or contraband; the Fourth Amendment requires no additional showing of unforeseen circumstances, imminent departure or insufficient time to obtain a warrant. Police custody, the ability to guard the vehicle or an earlier opportunity to seek a warrant does not itself impose that extra condition. The object sought limits the search to places and containers capable of containing it.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven Justices. Stevens and Ginsburg oppose review on jurisdictional/discretionary grounds, not an additional-exigency merits rule.

**Material application:** Carroll/Chambers supply automobile mobility; Carney adds reduced regulated-vehicle privacy; Johns preserves search authority during custody, with its separate unreasonable-delay limitation. Ross ties the permissible search to its object.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No independent authority to enter a home or protected curtilage, search an immobile structure as an automobile, omit probable cause or exceed object-based scope. Different residential uses and protected-premises access retain their own rules.

##### How that rule applies to the two reviewed searches

**Holding:** The accepted probable cause and adjudicated automobile circumstances bring these searches within the exception for purposes of the federal additional-exigency ground on which the judgments rest. The Court reverses that ground in each case and remands; its application is neither an unconditional approval of every entry and search act nor an instruction merely to reconsider whether the rejected urgency requirement should remain.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven Justices. Stevens and Ginsburg oppose review on jurisdictional/discretionary grounds, not an additional-exigency merits rule.

**Material application:** Labron’s accepted probable cause arose from drug transactions and retrievals from an ordinary street-parked Lincoln. Its failed start on attempted impoundment and highway-legal exterior establish neither a running engine nor known pre-search incapacity. Kilgore’s accepted probable cause arose from the controlled purchase and truck connection; private parking, police custody and available guards do not add a federal urgency requirement. Lack of consent or visible contraband does not defeat an otherwise applicable automobile exception.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general known-mechanical-incapacity rule, lawful boundary-crossing finding, curtilage classification, conversion of house consent into truck consent, new consent/access hearing or revived Labron privacy-interest objection.

##### What the reversal and remand permit

**Holding:** Each judgment is reversed, and each case is remanded for proceedings consistent with the Court's automobile and jurisdictional holdings. Pennsylvania may apply an actually adequate and independent state ground if properly available, and may address genuinely remaining scope or other distinct issues under its procedures; this mandate establishes neither their availability nor their merits and directs no automatic admission or prosecution outcome.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven Justices. Stevens and Ginsburg oppose review on jurisdictional/discretionary grounds, not an additional-exigency merits rule.

**Material application:** The exception is applied to the actual reviewed additional-exigency issue, not left wholly undecided. Distinct genuinely remaining scope, access and independent state-law issues are subject to ordinary procedural predicates.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new preservation, waiver, admission of evidence, conviction, retrial or automatic hearing is ordered.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Certiorari granted in each case; both suppression judgments summarily reversed and remanded, 7–2, in a reasoned decision without plenary argument. The undisclosed petition poll is not a merits tally. For jurisdictional holdings, see Federal Courts — Finality and adequate independent state grounds, these cases, July 1, 1996.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Cox Broadcasting v. Cohn:** The Court applies the third practical-finality category to these binding suppression determinations and the State's inability to obtain later effective review. No general interlocutory-review rule is added.
- **Michigan v. Long:** Its federal/interwoven-ground inquiry is applied separately to Labron and Kilgore. A State's power to afford greater protection remains, but possible state authority is not proof of an actual independent judgment.
- **Arizona v. Evans:** Its Long holding remains controlling. Its separate exclusionary-rule exception and unresolved clerical-error attribution do not authorize these searches or require the same factual remand in a different case.
- **Sochor and Middlebrooks:** Their actually sufficient independent preservation and state constitutional grounds remain respected; the Court distinguishes them from the grounds under review here.
- **Carroll v. United States and Chambers v. Maroney:** The established automobile exception is applied without a new case-specific warrant-impracticability requirement. Police control does not by itself require a different federal rule.
- **California v. Carney:** Mobility and reduced automobile privacy support the rule; its actual setting and reservation concerning differently situated residential uses remain. It creates no general authority to enter protected property.
- **United States v. Johns:** Custody alone does not eliminate vehicle-search authority. The Court does not remove Johns's separate protection against unreasonable delay or decide a delay claim here.
- **United States v. Ross:** Probable cause's object determines the permitted places and containers to search; it is not a license for a search beyond that object.
- **Ornelas v. United States:** Independent review of ultimate constitutional probability questions, clear-error protection for historical facts and due weight to grounded inferences remain. The case supplies no new fact, waiver or probable-cause presumption.
- **Whren v. United States:** Objective authority for the initial stop remains separate from search, manner, scope and duration. Its unanimity does not supply an additional vote or factual premise here.
- **Wilson v. Arkansas and Pinson:** The distinct protections governing dwelling entry and the particular technological examination of domestic information remain. Neither is a general automobile rule or establishes Kilgore's property boundary or lawful access.

No prior decision is overruled. The Court applies the inherited automobile exception to reject the added federal condition; it does not adopt a new general mechanical-defect, curtilage or premises-consent rule.

### Convicted-offender bodily identification

#### Rise v. Oregon

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit 93-35521; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied
**Decided:** June 14, 1996

##### Do conviction and ordinary future-crime identification justify this suspicionless blood program?

**Holding:** A qualifying conviction and ordinary future-crime identification objective do not, on this record, justify compulsory suspicionless blood extraction and protected examination under Oregon's program. Reduced privacy, useful identification and medically modest means with statutory safeguards do not establish an exception authorizing the bodily acquisition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg; six. Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer dissent on this component.

**Material application:** The actual program has objective conviction coverage, medical collection safeguards, sample and health conditions, regulated identification and anonymous population-frequency uses, restricted disclosure, inspection and accuracy procedures, and qualified destruction. Those constraints matter but do not eliminate separate bodily protection or justify the ordinary-investigation extension. The ruling assumes neither misuse nor inevitable medical harm.

**Limits and questions not reached:** ORS 137.076 covers listed sexual offenses, associated burglary, promoting or compelling prostitution, attempts or conspiracies to the listed felonies, and murder or aggravated murder; its attempt provision does not include murder. An adequate-prior-sample notice excuses another draw, an inadequacy notice requires another, and a court finding of substantial and unreasonable health risk excuses collection. There is neither an absolute one-draw limit nor authority for arbitrary repeats. ORS 181.085 permits identification and nonidentifying population-frequency analysis, not unrestricted use; court or grand-jury process concerns otherwise unprivileged evidence. A written request with certified reversal generally requires destruction, except for an independent qualifying conviction, juvenile adjudication or guilty-except-for-insanity obligation, or destruction that would also destroy evidence relating to another covered person. Set-aside under §137.225 and release alone do not create destruction rights. No universal perpetual-retention or express freestanding predisposition-analysis prohibition is established. Different security or health programs, consent, supported warrants or exceptions, materially different noninvasive identification and hypothetical misuse are undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Fourth Amendment summary judgment reversed and remanded, 6–3. Relief remains tied to actual claims, defendants, capacities, immunities and ordinary remedies; collection, analysis, disclosure and retention stay distinct. No automatic damages, universal material destruction or liability of every official is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Schmerber and Winston separate lawful bodily-entry grounds from safe means. Skinner and Von Raab retain their particular safety/supervisory needs; Vernonia remains confined to its documented confidential nonpolice athletic-safety program. Bell's security rationale and Sitz's brief highway intervention do not authorize this biological extension. Whren and Myers provide no missing bodily-search exception.

##### Is the presented identification obligation retrospective punishment?

**Holding:** Application of the regulated obligation to persons convicted before enactment does not violate the Ex Post Facto Clause on this record because its design and demonstrated operation do not establish additional punishment. Its Fourth Amendment invalidity is a distinct question.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, unanimous.

**Material application:** Controlled identification, limited medical procedure and reimbursement excusal for inability to pay establish no enlarged sentence, lost release opportunity, new crime or eliminated defense. A civil label alone is insufficient, but blood collection, retention, probation conditions and potential deterrence do not establish added penal severity here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No unspecified sanction, imprisonment or extension beyond lawful release is approved. Milligan's unauthorized demands and discipline are not lawful statutory applications demonstrating general punitive operation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Collins and Morales distinguish punishment from other later burdens. Morales's meaningful-release-opportunity protection and particular substantiation rule do not transfer to every new regulation. The related process and personal-causation holdings appear under Civil Rights, Convicted-offender collection procedures and personal liability.

### Traffic stops and objective justification

#### Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-5841.
**Decided:** June 10, 1996.

##### Investigative motive and customary enforcement in an ordinary traffic stop

**Holding:** An ordinary initial traffic stop supported by objective probable cause of a traffic violation does not become unreasonable under the Fourth Amendment because officers also investigate an unrelated offense. Validity does not additionally depend on whether reasonable officers ordinarily would enforce the violation absent that other aim. Independent constitutional limits on manner, scope, and duration remain; detention or search materially exceeding what the traffic violation reasonably justifies requires independent grounds.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous, including the application and qualifications.

**Material application:** The accepted observations of waiting unusually long at a stop sign, failing to signal, and unreasonable speed supply traffic probable cause. Petitioners' proposed objective “would-have” test receives the same rejection as a subjective-motive prerequisite: variable local priorities, uniforms, assignments, markings, and enforcement customs do not change constitutional authority for the known violation. A separate balance of ordinary traffic-safety gains against plainclothes-intervention anxiety supplies no additional requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Inventory and administrative-inspection authority retain their genuine-purpose and standardized or other source-specific constraints; motive is not irrelevant to every constitutional question. General Order 303.1 compliance, its immediate-safety-threat exception for otherwise unauthorized plainclothes or unmarked enforcement, and its extreme-circumstances oral-warning exception are not adjudicated. Neither speed nor missing execution details establish an exception or constitutional execution finding. The later search is not independently presented.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Scott* applies objective assessment of justified action; *Robinson*, *Gustafson*, and *Villamonte-Marquez* retain their distinct lawful-arrest, search, and customs-inspection predicates without creating new power. *Prouse* distinguishes suspicionless discretionary stops; *Martinez-Fuerte* and *Brignoni-Ponce* do not require a further balance for this ordinary probable-cause stop. *Bertine* and *Wells* preserve inventory constraints and forbid investigatory ruses; *Burger* preserves purpose-limited administrative inspection. *Abel* and *Bannister*'s regular-procedure and no-pretext observations do not establish the proposed customary-enforcement condition. *Wilson* preserves reasonable-execution review with its supported danger, futility, escape, and evidence-destruction qualifications, leaving suspicion quantum and automatic suppression open. *Garner*, *Welsh*, and *Winston* retain their distinct deadly-force, home-entry, and bodily-intrusion limits.

##### Review of the accepted predicate and separation of discriminatory selection

**Holding:** The accepted traffic observations permit resolution under *Ornelas*: historical facts, credibility, and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review, with genuine due weight to grounded contextual inferences; the legal standard and ultimate constitutional sufficiency properly at issue receive independent judgment. No new factual inquiry is required to decide this legal-rule challenge.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Material application:** Due weight means substantive consideration of supported trial and local-officer context, not a numerical weight, officer-credibility presumption, or intermediate standard insulating constitutional conclusions. Direct review imports no habeas presumption or clear-and-convincing burden and supplies no supposed antecedent drug probable cause.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purposeful racial selection remains independently forbidden by equal protection; traffic probable cause does not establish nondiscrimination. No discrimination finding, police-stop discovery entitlement, immunity rule, suppression consequence, or damages remedy is decided, and assumed adequacy of another remedy is not a ground for judgment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of suppression on the initial-stop ground, 9–0. The completed vacatur of lesser-included §841 convictions and §860 resentencing, including nonreimposition of Whren's fine, remain undisturbed. Later sentencing appeals are not resolved. No new trial, hearing, release, sentencing proceeding, or determination of every admissibility ground follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Ornelas*'s review allocation is applied unchanged. *Kimberlin* permits circumstantial proof when motive is an underlying element without adding one here. *Armstrong* remains confined to federal charging and discovery; its requirements and unresolved discrimination remedy do not become police-stop rules.

#### Ohio v. Robinette

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-891.

**Decided:** November 18, 1996.

For the other questions in Ohio v. Robinette, No. 95-891, decided November 18, 1996, see Federal Courts - Adequate and independent state grounds (Review of the interwoven federal and state grounds).

##### Objective legality during and after a traffic stop

**Holding:** An officer's uncommunicated investigative motive or private decision to issue only a warning does not invalidate an objectively lawful traffic stop or an exit order while lawful traffic business is being completed. Once that justification ends, further detention requires independent legal grounds; the court must examine the objective circumstances to determine whether the person could terminate the encounter, and return of a license is neither an automatic end of seizure nor an automatic license to continue it.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II.A concerning motive and the exit order has six joins: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Part II.B concerning continued detention has those six plus Stone-Zsela.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual encounter remains for application to supported historical facts. Ornelas is not extended into a general appellate-review rule for every consent issue.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Whren's objective initial-stop rule and independent duration limits remain intact; Mimms is confined to lawful traffic business. Bostick's actual-circumstances inquiry is applied. Ornelas is not extended into a general rule for all consent issues.

##### No indispensable federal release warning; voluntary consent and attenuation remain distinct

**Holding:** The Fourth Amendment does not invariably require an officer to give a free-to-go warning before seeking consent to search. Consent must be voluntary under all the circumstances, and when an unlawful detention preceded or accompanied it, voluntariness alone does not establish that the consent was sufficiently independent of that illegality to permit use of the evidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Absence of a release warning remains relevant. Neither voluntary consent automatically cures illegal detention nor unlawful detention makes every later consent incurably tainted; independent Ohio warnings remain permissible.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Schneckloth is applied without a new warning element. Royer and Brown preserve the separate inquiry into the effect of prior illegality; no automatic cure or automatic suppression rule is created.

##### The remedy corrects the federal rules without deciding admissibility

**Holding:** Rejection of the warning rule and the motive-based exit-order premise requires further proceedings on the actual objective detention, voluntary consent and any necessary attenuation question. It does not itself establish the search's validity, require admission of the evidence or reinstate the conviction.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer join Part IV; Stone-Zsela also joins its remand inquiries and limits but votes for vacatur. The formal reversal has seven votes, including Ginsburg's separate judgment vote and excluding Stone-Zsela.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The mandate orders neither admission of evidence, reinstatement of conviction nor a new hearing as such.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The rule/application distinction retained in Evans and the separate consent and causation inquiries in Schneckloth, Royer and Brown govern the scope of remand without enlargement.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand, seven votes: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela would vacate; Stevens would affirm. The state courts must decide actual detention, voluntariness and necessary attenuation under corrected federal standards and any genuinely independent state law.

### Cordless interception and testimonial just cause

#### In re Askin

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 94-5428.

**Decided:** December 9, 1996.

For the other questions in In re Askin, Fourth Circuit No. 94-5428, decided December 9, 1996, see Federal Courts - Mootness and effectual relief (The contempt adjudication remains reviewable).

##### The former wire and electronic definitions exclude the acquired radio portion

**Holding:** Former 18 U.S.C. §2510(1) and (12)(A) exclude the radio portion transmitted between a cordless handset and its base from the statutory wire and electronic categories, including both speakers' words acquired at that link. The other participant's use of a landline or cellular telephone does not overcome those particular exclusions; the ruling does not classify every segment of the complete call as unprotected.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II-A, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Other segments of the complete call are not classified by the acquired segment's exclusion; oral coverage and constitutional privacy remain separate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The express former statutory exclusions govern the intercepted link; no constitutional permission follows from those exclusions.

##### The former oral definition requires its own inquiry

**Holding:** The former exclusions from the wire and electronic categories do not categorically foreclose oral-communication coverage. Former §2510(2) requires an exhibited expectation against interception under circumstances justifying that expectation and excludes statutory electronic communication; because former §2510(12)(A) excludes this radio portion from that electronic category, the lower court must determine whether the distinct expectation requirements are met.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II-B, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The required exhibited and justified expectations must be found rather than presumed from technological transmission, chosen endpoint, accessibility, knowledge or ignorance. The decision adopts no mandatory multifactor test and reaches no ultimate expectation finding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The statutory oral category retains its own requirements; the separate wire and electronic exclusions neither establish oral protection nor categorically defeat it.

##### The 1994 deletion does not reclassify completed acquisition

**Holding:** Public Law 103-414 §202 does not make completed preenactment interception unlawful by retroactively deleting the former category exclusions. A genuinely distinct later disclosure, use, evidentiary reception or refusal must be assessed on its own actual date and statutory predicate; pending appellate review alone establishes no such later-act violation, and preexisting oral-communication protection remains available on its own terms.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part III has nine joins on completed acquisition and eight Associate joins on distinct later acts and the preserved oral route.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The amendment took effect upon enactment; Title I's separate four-year delay does not govern Title II section 202. A later use must satisfy its own prohibition and remedial predicates, including the interception-violation condition; no later-act violation is established by the pending appeal.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf and Rivers are applied to the actual completed acquisition and genuinely distinct later conduct without merging their temporal methodologies.

##### Statutory noncoverage and another caller's endpoint do not decide personal constitutional privacy

**Holding:** A statute's failure to prohibit an interception is not affirmative constitutional permission, and the other speaker's vulnerable endpoint alone does not establish that Askin surrendered every legitimate privacy interest in his own conversation. If the constitutional question remains necessary after the statutory inquiry, the lower courts must assess his personal interest, actual exposure and any legally available justification on the record, without presuming his knowledge or ignorance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part IV has nine joins rejecting the statutory-permission and other-endpoint shortcuts; the eight Associates join its conditional sequence and reservation of the ultimate privacy determination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No search, absence of lawful justification or exclusion entitlement is finally found. Participant cooperation and knowing exposure cannot be presumed; selecting a protected endpoint is not by itself sufficient.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Katz protects personal conversational privacy; Wellons, Alderman and Rakas require the claimant's own legitimate interest and separate the right from remedy. Pinson and Myers reject a receiver-location shortcut at their home-information scope. White's willing-participant principle supplies no finding of cooperation or knowing exposure here.

##### Statutory just cause is conditional; the constitutional remedial extension is reserved

**Holding:** Under *Gelbard*, a witness may establish just cause under §1826(a) when his own covered wire or oral communication was intercepted in violation of Title III, after consideration of applicable authorization or exceptions, and §2515 prohibits reception of the compelled testimony as intercepted contents or derived evidence. The surviving potential category here is oral, and each refused question's derivation and the witness's personal remedial entitlement must be determined; the Court leaves unresolved whether independently unconstitutional acquisition alone supplies just cause for this immunized witness's criminal-trial refusal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part V and implementing remand, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The criminal-trial setting is not Calandra's grand jury. Immunity does not remove section 2515's protection; electronic classification alone does not obtain that remedy. The Government's admitted monitoring answers occurrence under section 3504, not illegality or derivation. No automatic Leon or Krull good-faith rule applies; actual authorization, independent source, attenuation and other exceptions require their own predicates.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gelbard supplies the conditional statutory just-cause defense. Calandra's grand-jury exclusion ruling neither compels nor forecloses the reserved criminal-trial constitutional defense. Leon and Krull require their actual reliance predicates; no automatic good-faith result follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the civil-contempt affirmance and remand, unanimously in judgment. The eight-Justice implementing rationale requires separate statutory coverage, personal-interest, prohibition, remedy and question-specific derivation inquiries, and constitutional inquiry only if necessary. Civil contempt may be lawfully reinstated or set aside; no automatic suppression, blanket privilege, criminal-contempt disposition, new confinement or new hearing is ordered.

### Traffic stops and passenger-exit directions

#### Maryland v. Wilson

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1268.
**Decided:** February 19, 1997.

##### Passenger-exit directions during a lawful traffic stop

**Holding:** During a lawful traffic stop, an officer may direct a passenger to leave the vehicle without additional passenger-specific suspicion as a limited safety measure incident to the stop. This authority concerns the exit direction itself; it does not establish categorical forcible detention for the entire stop, permission to frisk or search, or power to prolong detention after the lawful traffic business ends.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether every passenger is seized at the initial stop or may be forcibly held throughout it. The observed movements and nervousness were not preserved as an independent justification; review does not cure that omission.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mimms's safety-and-incremental-intrusion analysis extends to the limited passenger-exit command. Terry, Brignoni-Ponce, Whren and Robinette retain their distinct intrusion, duration and search limits. ### Announcement and warrant execution

#### Richards v. Wisconsin

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-5955.
**Decided:** 1997-04-28.

##### The felony-drug category cannot replace the required execution inquiry

**Holding:** The Fourth Amendment does not permit officers to dispense with announcement solely because a warrant seeks evidence of felonious drug delivery or possession with intent to deliver. A valid search warrant authorizes the search but does not itself establish that an unannounced forcible entry is reasonable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts I–II and IV, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Wilson constitutionalizes announcement subject to supported exceptions. Buie's adjoining-space sweep and Maryland v. Wilson's passenger-exit rule retain their specific justifications; the decision does not invalidate every categorical Fourth Amendment rule.

##### Reasonable suspicion governs a supported execution exception

**Holding:** Officers must identify particular circumstances and rational inferences supporting reasonable suspicion that knocking and announcing would be dangerous or futile, or would inhibit effective investigation, including by allowing destruction of evidence. The threshold is below probable cause but requires more than the offense label or an unsupported hunch, and the relevant facts must be known when the challenged force begins.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A destruction-risk exception must connect announcement delay with threatened evidence loss; later discoveries cannot supply earlier justification. No warrantless-search authority, universal waiting time or general police-created-exigency rule follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Wilson's reserved suspicion quantum is resolved for these execution exceptions through a practical Buie analogy, without transplanting every protective-sweep rule.

##### Historical findings and constitutional application have different review roles

**Holding:** In reviewing this execution under the reasonable-suspicion standard, the historical facts, credibility determinations and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences receive clear-error protection, with grounded experience given real weight; the ultimate constitutional sufficiency of those facts is independently assessed. That allocation permits review of the supported record without manufacturing new facts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven direct joins. Stone-Zsela and Scalia do not join this review-method extension.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Ornelas's functional fact/legal allocation is extended to this warrant-execution question; Robinette separates correcting a general rule from application and remedy. Long permits review of the interwoven federal/state ruling without displacing genuinely independent Wisconsin law.

##### The credited contemporaneous facts justified this entry

**Holding:** The credited look toward a nearby uniformed officer and surprised reaction, combined with the specifically sought disposable cocaine, supplied particularized reasonable suspicion of destruction risk before the first attempt to enter; the subsequent rapid closure and locking strengthened that justification for the later force. The entry was therefore reasonable despite the invalid categorical rule and the judge's advance refusal of no-knock authorization.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruse was not consent to forced entry; late announcement cannot cure earlier unlawful force. The Court decides no independent futility ground, exclusionary remedy after a violation, cross-petition objection or automatic independent-source/inevitable-discovery effect of a valid warrant.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the conviction and denial of suppression on particularized destruction risk. The original hearing suffices; no suppression, new hearing, plea vacatur or sentence change is directed.

#### United States v. Ramirez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1469.

**Decided:** 1998-03-04.

##### Property damage does not raise the announcement threshold

**Holding:** Damage during warrant execution does not categorically require a greater exigency than particularized reasonable suspicion that ordinary announcement would be dangerous or futile, or would inhibit effective investigation, including through destruction of evidence. The facts supporting that exception must be known when the challenged force begins, and the means and extent of any destruction remain independently subject to Fourth Amendment reasonableness.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Particular facts and rational inferences must support the exception; an offense category, hunch or warrant label is insufficient. There is no universal waiting period or changed review standard.

##### The contemporaneous danger and limited damage justified this execution

**Holding:** The reliable report of a dangerous escapee, corroborating observation and location-specific information about garage weapons supplied reasonable suspicion that ordinary announcement would create danger. Breaking one garage window to control access to those weapons, with a firearm directed through the opening for that purpose, was reasonable on this record; the justification permits neither gratuitous destruction nor any amount of damage merely because announcement may be excused.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ramirez’s later shot cannot justify the entry retrospectively, and the later failure to locate the escapee does not erase the information reasonably known beforehand. Gratuitous, punitive, disproportionate or objective-unrelated damage remains unapproved.

##### Section 3109 preserves a supported common-law danger exception

**Holding:** Section 3109 ordinarily permits breaking to execute a search warrant after notice of authority and purpose and refused admission, and separately permits breaking when necessary to liberate the officer or someone assisting execution. Its codification of the common-law announcement rule also preserves a danger exception satisfied by particularized reasonable suspicion that following the ordinary process would expose officers or occupants to danger; the pre-entry escapee and garage-weapons information satisfies that exception here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), unanimous.

**Material application:** Simultaneous announcement and breaking do not prove prior refusal, and no liberation predicate is established. The supported common-law danger qualification independently sustains the entry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The statutory danger exception does not automatically incorporate every constitutional exception; futility, escape and evidence-destruction applications remain open. The Court decides no attenuation, independent-source, inevitable-discovery or exclusionary consequence of an actual violation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The suppression judgment is reversed and remanded because the challenged entry and single-window damage violated neither the Fourth Amendment nor §3109. No particular prosecution outcome follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wilson v. Arkansas is applied for the common-law announcement protection and supported exceptions; it does not constitutionalize every federal statutory detail.
- Richards v. Wisconsin is applied for particularized reasonable suspicion and contemporaneous justification; its announcement quantum is not increased because damage occurs, and its separate review-method holding is unchanged.
- Miller v. United States, 357 U.S. 301, supplies the statutory common-law background. Its reservation of a general exigency exception is respected rather than described as an earlier holding.
- Sabbath v. United States, 391 U.S. 585, retains its rule that opening a closed unlocked door is breaking and its rejection of unsupported danger. Its common-law discussion supports the danger qualification adopted here; physical damage creates no new announcement tier.
- Aponte Matos v. Toledo Dávila retains its separate conduct-date qualified-immunity scope and supplies no direct suppression immunity here.

### Search incident to arrest

#### United States v. Ortiz; United States v. Hurtado

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit Nos. 95-1386 and 95-1869; Supreme Court dockets unassigned.
**Decided:** 1997-05-12.

For Hurtado's distinct sentence and guideline holding, see Criminal Procedure — Contempt sentencing, these consolidated cases and dockets, May 12, 1997.

##### Immediate retrieval from this on-person numerical pager falls within the arrest exception

**Holding:** Activating the seized pager to disclose stored numerical messages is a Fourth Amendment search distinct from physical seizure. A lawful custodial arrest nevertheless permits this contemporaneous examination of a numerical pager taken from the arrestee's person under the established personal-effects search-incident authority, without a separate showing that this particular pager faced imminent overwrite; the holding does not authorize delayed retrieval, remotely stored information or searches of electronic devices generally.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I: unanimous that retrieval is a search separate from seizure; the eight Associates join the incident-search rule and affirmance. Stone-Zsela rejects that categorical justification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No capacity, overwrite behavior, imminent page or inability to preserve unread data is proved; there is no independent particularized-exigency holding. Vehicle, firearm and next-day wristwatch issues remain outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm Ortiz's pager-admission ruling and reviewed conviction judgment, 8–1, without pager-based remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Robinson's contemporaneous on-person effects rule reaches this numerical pager. Chimel and Chadwick preserve temporal and possession/search limits; Chadwick's remote search of property not immediately associated with the person differs. Pinson and Wellons preserve concealed-information and closed-content interests while leaving exceptions available; Richards and Chandler do not prohibit every Fourth Amendment category.

### Suspicionless candidate drug testing

#### Chandler v. Miller

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-126.
**Decided:** April 15, 1997.

For the continuing prospective controversy after the election, see *Chandler*, April 15, 1997, No. 96-126 (Federal Courts — Mootness and election disputes).

##### Whether seeking office waives protection against compelled urinalysis

**Holding:** Government-required collection and chemical analysis of urine are Fourth Amendment searches. Seeking public office does not waive bodily privacy; candidacy's public character informs reasonableness but does not itself authorize the search.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Material application:** The personal-physician option and initial disclosure to the candidate reduce the intrusion without eliminating it. No public disclosure, observed collection, police use, or unrelated testing is attributed to this program.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Skinner* supplies the search rule; *Vernonia* preserves it despite voluntary participation in the underlying activity.

##### Whether Georgia established an adequate special need for its broad scheduled candidate program

**Holding:** A suspicionless search claimed to serve a need beyond ordinary law enforcement must be reasonable in light of the actual need, operation, and privacy interests. General desires for drug-free officials and public confidence do not justify Georgia's broad, advance-scheduled candidate testing, even with its reduced intrusion, on the combined deficiencies of this program.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Material application:** The covered offices share no comparable protective risk, and the record identifies neither a concrete drug problem nor another substantial operational need connecting this class to bodily testing. A predictable negative test within thirty days before qualification does not adequately substantiate the asserted assurance of future service.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding creates no mandatory checklist: catastrophe, quantified efficacy, exhaustion of alternatives, and safety-sensitive duties are not universal conditions. Scheduled tests are not invariably useless or unlawful. Different supported programs, medical-fitness examinations, financial disclosures, particularized-suspicion testing, genuine safety screening, private testing, and independent First Amendment or state-law qualification claims are undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for declaratory and appropriate prospective relief barring enforcement of this candidate-testing condition. No past election, damages, individual test result, drug use, or disqualification is adjudicated, and no replacement testing or qualification system is prescribed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Skinner* and *Von Raab* preserve contextual protective and integrity interests. *Vernonia*'s school-athletic program is distinguished by custody, privacy, and protective circumstances; its separate coalitions and holdings remain unchanged. *T.L.O.*'s school-responsibility rationale does not transfer wholesale to adults seeking office.

### Warrant execution and private observers

#### Ayeni v. Mottola

**Docket or dockets:** Supreme Court No. 94-1202 (Mottola v. Ayeni); Second Circuit Nos. 94-6041(L), 94-6047.
**Decided:** May 27, 1997.

For the separate immunity and conduct-date holdings in Ayeni v. Mottola, May 27, 1997, No. 94-1202, see Civil Rights — Qualified-immunity review.

##### A warrant does not authorize purposeless media entry and recording

**Holding:** Officers violate the Fourth Amendment when they procure the entry and recording of private media observers inside a home during execution of a search warrant and that presence serves no legitimate execution or investigative purpose. A valid warrant authorizes its evidentiary intrusion, not the additional exposure of home life for an unrelated purpose; genuine assistance, valid occupant consent and necessary investigative documentation present different circumstances.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), Parts I–II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3105 permits requested aid while the authorized officer is present and executing the warrant; a technician, interpreter or property identifier can provide genuine assistance. A statutory breach alone does not establish the constitutional wrong. Compelled submission to officers is not consent to a separate audience, and press status supplies no entry authority. No general copying-as-seizure rule or publication restraint follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bivens and Andresen supply warrant bounds and restraint of unnecessary documentary exposure. Wilson v. Arkansas and Richards preserve reasonableness review of execution independently of warrant validity.

### Residential privacy, warrantless entry and suppression

#### United States v. Fields / Crawley

**Docket or dockets:** Second Circuit Nos. 96-1168 and 96-1257; no separate Supreme Court docket stated.

**Decided:** 1998-02-09.

##### Each defendant has his own protected relationship to the room

**Holding:** Fields's sustained paid access, key, independent entry and permission to bring guests establish his own legitimate privacy interest in the apartment and private room. Crawley's repeated, expressly permitted sharing of the closed room's privacy also establishes his own interest on these facts, although he had no established overnight plan that evening; neither mere presence nor another person's rights would suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I-A has all nine Justices. Part I-B has O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. The other four assume Crawley's interest to decide the observation question and do not join that extension.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Fields’s control and Crawley’s recurring permitted closed-room sharing are separate personal grounds; no protection for mere presence or every commercial visitor is established. Predominant cocaine activity does not itself destroy an otherwise legitimate privacy interest.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Minnesota v. Carter and Minnesota v. Johns, No. 97-1147, December 1, 1998: extends the shared-privacy holding to the actual host-authorized private occupancy without making recurring use indispensable; Fields's independent-control ground and exclusion of mere presence remain intact.

##### Limited residential sharing is not itself general publication

**Holding:** Limited visibility of bedroom activity to cotenants using shared residential space is not, by that fact alone, general publication. An incompletely closed blind cannot establish absence of subjective privacy independently of the actual vantage from which the challenged police observation was obtained.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A has O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. The Court does not adopt Part II-B's four-Justice application as a majority search determination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no majority finding that the shared yard was curtilage or observation was a search. The street and sidewalk gave no view; the gap, shared access and ordinary viewing measures matter, but neither fencing nor imperfect screening alone determines the result.

##### Ultimate home-entry exigency receives independent appellate review

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently determine whether the established circumstances supplied a constitutionally sufficient exigency for warrantless home entry. Historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences retain clear-error protection, and grounded contextual experience receives genuine due weight without controlling the ultimate constitutional conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A has O'Connor, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia withholds this extension; Stone-Zsela does not join it.

##### Entry authority and announcement require separate contemporaneous justification

**Holding:** Probable cause alone does not authorize warrantless home entry; an applicable consent or exigency justification is required. Separately, an announcement exception requires particular facts and rational inferences known when force begins supporting reasonable suspicion of danger, futility or threatened evidence loss from announcement or delay; later discoveries cannot supply earlier justification.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion: Part III-B has all eight Associate Justices. Four reserve the ultimate applications and four would sustain them; neither application has a controlling majority.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Announcement requires more than a hunch but less than probable cause. The porch warning, known history and disposability are contemporaneous facts; later weapon discovery and flight are not. Forced common-door entry counts despite the open apartment door. No majority adopts actual entry legality or a general police-created-exigency test.

##### A later warrant or consent does not automatically decide suppression

**Holding:** If an antecedent intrusion was unlawful, a later warrant is an independent source only when both the decision to seek it and its issuance are genuinely independent of the illegality. Consent must satisfy its own authority, scope and voluntariness requirements and any applicable taint analysis; neither a warrant's arrival nor a rights explanation and signature automatically validates an earlier intrusion or decides exclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion: Part IV has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela additionally agrees in returning suppression, consent, independence, attenuation and later-warrant consequences to the lower courts without final suppression, but joins no additional remedial rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Leon excludes knowing/reckless deception, abandoned neutrality, affidavits wholly lacking probable-cause indicia and facially deficient warrants; obtaining/supplying officers and scope remain relevant. Evans remains confined to qualifying court-clerical error. Inevitable discovery and any Rule 52(b) claim require their actual predicates; no waiver or forfeiture is established here.

##### The judgment does not establish a unified search or entry application

**Authority of the judgment:** Five Justices vacate the reviewed suppression determination and dependent appellate affirmances and remand. O'Connor, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would require a renewed actual-vantage application while reserving search and entry legality. Stone-Zsela finds the observation a search and the planned exigency insufficient, leaving remedies for remand. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer would affirm because they find the actual shared view and specific pre-force exceptions sufficient.

The directly supported propositions above control their subjects. The two judgment-supporting application rationales are not treated as a single Marks holding: the plurality leaves open a lawful observation and entry that the concurrence rejects on this record, while the concurrence adds a police-created-exigency ground the plurality does not adopt. Neither full rationale is a logical subset supplying a common answer. The binding judgment therefore requires lawful further proceedings under the actual majority rules, without imposing either noncontrolling application as the Court's final answer. No automatic exclusion, plea withdrawal, verdict vacatur or unrelated appellate reopening follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** By Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, the reviewed suppression determinations and dependent appellate conviction affirmances are vacated to that extent and remanded. No evidence is ordered excluded or admitted; Fields’s jury verdict is not vacated and Crawley’s conditional plea is not automatically withdrawn. Unreviewed appellate branches remain undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Rakas and Jones supply the personal-interest inquiry; Olson's protection of shared residential privacy is extended to Crawley's specific recurring, permitted closed-room use without an all-visitors rule.
- Katz, Pinson and Myers supply protection of private domestic information and the distinction between a protected relationship, exposure, search justification and remedy. Their thermal-search holdings are not converted into a rule about every naked-eye view or every device.
- Ciraolo and Riley retain their actual public-vantage settings. Dunn supplies contextual proximity, enclosure, use and screening considerations; no first-fence rule or classification of every shared yard follows.
- Ornelas is extended to ultimate home-entry exigency, preserving factual protection and genuine due weight. Its limits on hearings, remedies and other mixed questions remain intact; Gates's warrant-issuance review is unchanged.
- Payton and Hayden retain the need for an actual home-entry exception. Wilson and Richards retain separate, particularized announcement rules and Richards's already governing independent ultimate review. No general police-created-exigency doctrine or announcement-exclusion rule is adopted.
- Murray and Karo retain genuine independence and supported untainted-warrant possibilities. Leon, Evans and Olano retain their stated institutional, factual and procedural limits; no later warrant or consent is treated as an automatic cure.

### Additional custody for civil licensing violations

#### Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-501.

**Decided:** 1998-05-04.

##### Probable cause does not automatically justify additional station-house custody

**Holding:** Probable cause and state or local authority to enforce a fine-only civil licensing ordinance do not, by themselves, establish that transporting an identified operator to a station and confining him is reasonable. The added restraint must reasonably serve the actual enforcement function; nonexclusive possible justifications include securing identification or appearance, ending an actual continuing violation, protecting persons or evidence, or necessary processing in the circumstances. The rule imposes neither least restrictive means nor a duty to issue a citation whenever one is conceivable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg’s Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer. Those five, including the author, directly adopt this rule and the targeted reversal; Stone-Zsela does not join Part II’s affirmative reservation of actual reasonableness.

**Current-force modification:** Atwater v. City of Lago Vista, April 24, 2001, extends the actual-enforcement requirement to added station transport and confinement for an observed fine-only criminal misdemeanor. The extension preserves the nonexclusive, unpresumed needs inquiry and rejects both a universal citation duty and least-restrictive-means requirement. Ricci's original civil-licensing rule and its unresolved application fracture remain unchanged; Atwater itself supplies no majority conclusion about the ultimate reasonableness of its custody.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Fine-only status creates no categorical arrest immunity, and the listed justifications are possibilities rather than findings. The continuing operation, possible daily fines, processing papers, one-hour custody and recognizance release remain relevant supported circumstances. A municipal policy cannot supply its own constitutional justification; the separate warrant theory and settled or abandoned claims remain outside review.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Atwater v. City of Lago Vista, No. 99-1408, April 24, 2001: extension of the additional-custody rule to fine-only criminal enforcement.

##### No controlling conclusion resolves the actual custody’s reasonableness

**Authority of the judgment:** Five Justices support targeted reversal and remand, but only Stone-Zsela concludes the actual custody was unreasonable. Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer leave that application open; Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas find the supported short enforcement processing reasonable and would affirm.

Part I’s rule controls by direct majority agreement. Part II’s application reservation has four joins, Stone-Zsela’s contrary application has one, and the dissent’s favorable application has four. These positions cannot be combined into a majority holding that this custody was unlawful or lawful, and no Marks synthesis is adopted. The lower courts must proceed under the controlling rule without a Supreme Court finding of ultimate violation, liability or immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The adverse summary judgment on the preserved custody-reasonableness claim is set aside by targeted reversal and remand, 5–4, with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer supporting. The settled search claim and unappealed probable-cause dismissal remain undisturbed. Policy causation, individual responsibility, conduct-time immunity and relief retain their own predicates.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Whren v. United States:** Preserved for ordinary initial probable-cause stops and applied at its independent execution limit; it does not establish unlimited custody for every civil violation.
- **Ohio v. Robinette:** Its separate later-restraint inquiry informs the new application without a mandatory citation or warning script.
- **Ayeni v. Mottola:** Its purpose-bound execution principle informs the added-restraint inquiry; the home setting and separate immunity decision retain their limits.
- **Wilson v. Arkansas:** History remains relevant to reasonableness, and reversal for a deficient framework need not establish a completed violation. No state arrest rule is automatically constitutionalized.
- **Soldal v. Cook County:** Civil enforcement remains within Fourth Amendment coverage, while reasonableness and liability stay distinct; its summary-judgment remedy does not establish those predicates automatically.
- **United States v. Robinson; Gustafson v. Florida:** Their lawful-arrest premise is preserved; search consequences cannot establish antecedent arrest lawfulness by assumption.
- **County of Riverside v. McLaughlin:** Its timing rule for post-arrest probable-cause review does not itself authorize this custody.
- **United States v. Lanier:** Conduct-time legal clarity remains separate from a present constitutional ruling; no newly stated rule retroactively defeats personal immunity.

#### Mitchell v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-7541.
**Decided:** 1999-04-05.

##### The plea did not waive privilege concerning reserved quantity

**Holding:** A guilty plea does not itself waive the privilege against compelled self-incrimination concerning disputed sentencing facts while testimony creates a genuine risk of additional criminal punishment. This accepted plea, its limited admissions and its trial-silence warning did not waive protection concerning expressly reserved quantity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Part I, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Estelle's sentencing protection and Brown's scope-based testimonial-waiver rule are applied without making every plea statement inadmissible or establishing a permanent privilege.

##### Protected silence cannot prove the crime's circumstances at sentencing

**Holding:** A sentencing court may not draw an adverse factual inference from a defendant's protected silence in determining the circumstances and details of the crime. The Government must establish disputed quantity through lawful affirmative evidence; permission for judicial sentencing findings does not authorize this source of proof.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg (six Justices).

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Griffin's no-inference protection is applied to proof of crime facts at criminal sentencing; Estelle supplies continuing protection. Baxter and Woodard are distinguished by their civil settings. Witte, Watts and Dunnigan retain their stated lawful operations.

##### Other potentially sufficient evidence does not establish harmlessness here

**Holding:** The Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that this constitutional error did not contribute to the affected sentencing determination. Because the judge expressly relied on silence in crediting disputed quantity testimony, the finding cannot be preserved merely because the remaining evidence could support it; the sentence must be vacated and quantity reconsidered without that inference.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg (six Justices).

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chapman supplies the contribution-based constitutional harmlessness standard. Wright's existing lawful-basis requirement is preserved and applied at its actual sentencing scope.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The privilege exists while testimony presents a genuine risk of additional criminal punishment; no permanent privilege or ban on lawful voluntary-statement use follows. No quantity-element classification, universal jury rule, mandatory-minimum rule or entitlement to acceptance credit is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the sentence and remand for quantity determination from lawful affirmative evidence without the adverse silence inference. No fixed term, automatic minimum, plea withdrawal, acquittal or jury hearing is ordered; consequential components follow their own governing law.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Estelle and Brown govern continuing privilege and actual waiver. Griffin now forbids adverse use of protected silence to determine crime facts at sentencing; its established trial operation remains. Baxter and Woodard do not authorize the present criminal inference. Witte, Watts and Dunnigan preserve lawful sentencing findings and consequences without removing independent constitutional safeguards. Chapman governs harmlessness; Wright retains its separate sentencing-basis requirement. Jones and Monge supply no quantity classification or universal mandatory-minimum rule here. No precedent is overruled.

### Custodial statements and constitutional safeguards

#### Dickerson v. United States, 530 U.S. 428 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-5525.
**Decided:** June 26, 2000.

##### I. Miranda's constitutional safeguard cannot be replaced by voluntariness alone

**Holding:** Miranda's warnings and waiver requirements implement the constitutional privilege against compelled self-incrimination in custodial interrogation, and Congress cannot authorize case-in-chief use of a statement that those requirements exclude merely because the statement is voluntary under 18 U.S.C. §3501(a)–(b). Genuinely equally effective safeguards remain permissible; no exact verbal formula is indispensable, and an unwarned statement is not necessarily actually coerced for every purpose.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Seven direct rationale votes control the constitutional rule, statutory application, qualifications and suppression remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Subsection (a) concerns federal or District of Columbia criminal prosecutions. It requires the judge to resolve voluntariness outside the jury's presence and, upon finding the confession voluntary, to allow the jury to hear relevant voluntariness evidence and instruct it to give the confession the weight it deserves in light of all the circumstances. Its admission command remains subject to the constitutional safeguard stated above. Under subsection (b), all circumstances must be considered, including the arrest-to-arraignment interval when applicable, knowledge of the suspected offense, advice or knowledge concerning silence and use, prior advice of counsel rights, and absence of counsel during questioning and confession. The presence or absence of a listed factor need not be conclusive; consideration is mandatory, conclusiveness is not. Independent voluntariness review survives.

The Court does not invalidate the whole section. Subsection (c) protects a voluntary confession against exclusion solely for presentment delay when its weight is left to the jury and it is given within six hours of the relevant arrest or detention; the time limit does not apply when the judge finds a longer delay reasonable considering transportation and distance to the nearest available qualifying magistrate or other committing officer. Alvarez-Sanchez's relevant-duty inquiry and unresolved post-six-hour and McNabb–Mallory questions remain. Subsection (d) does not bar voluntary statements given without interrogation or while the speaker is not under arrest or detention; those alternatives remain distinct. Subsection (e) includes an admission of guilt and any oral or written self-incriminating statement. Each provision remains subject to constitutional requirements.

No automatic civil liability, general fruit-of-the-statement rule, mandatory warning wording or elimination of recognized exceptions follows. The separate apartment-evidence ruling, car-evidence dispute, refusal to reopen the suppression record and circuit authority to raise section 3501 are outside this review. The Court orders no new suppression hearing, dismissal, acquittal, release or vacatur of a conviction; none exists at this interlocutory stage.

##### II. The Court retains Miranda's core against the separate request for judicial abandonment

**Holding:** The Court declines to replace Miranda's core case-in-chief safeguard with a voluntariness-only rule: congressional preference for that rule and the existing distinctions between unwarned and actually compelled statements do not supply sufficient justification for abandonment here. Retention of the core leaves genuinely effective alternatives and the recognized, separately bounded exceptions available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six directly adopt the additional stare-decisis ground. Stone-Zsela agrees that the alternative request for judicial abandonment is rejected but adds no ground and does not join Part II. No Marks combination is needed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's judgment is reversed insofar as section 3501 supplied its basis for reversing statement suppression. The District Court's order suppressing the disputed statement from the prosecution's case in chief is restored, subject to a properly preserved exception, and the prosecution returns for further proceedings consistent with Miranda. The independent physical-evidence disposition is undisturbed. The Court neither reopens the suppression record nor directs a new hearing, dismisses the indictment, acquits Dickerson, orders release or vacates a nonexistent conviction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966):** Its constitutional custodial-interrogation safeguard, case-in-chief exclusion and allowance for equally effective alternatives are retained. Voluntariness alone cannot replace its applicable requirements.
- **Withrow v. Williams, 507 U.S. 680 (1993):** Its constitutional habeas operation supports Miranda's status; its distinction between a warning claim and separately presented coercion remains. No new collateral-review rule is adopted.
- **Michigan v. Tucker, 417 U.S. 433 (1974):** Its derivative-evidence setting and distinction between safeguards and actual compulsion remain; it does not authorize general case-in-chief use of the original unwarned statement.
- **Oregon v. Elstad, 470 U.S. 298 (1985):** Its treatment of a later voluntary warned statement remains; the original unwarned statement's exclusion is not erased.
- **Harris v. New York, 401 U.S. 222 (1971), and Oregon v. Hass, 420 U.S. 714 (1975):** Their impeachment uses remain subject to their own requirements and do not become ordinary prosecution case-in-chief permission.
- **New York v. Quarles, 467 U.S. 649 (1984):** Its public-safety exception remains limited to its governing conditions; the decision does not replace the general warning rule.
- **Colorado v. Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 (1986):** Police coercion remains necessary to due-process involuntariness; absence of warnings alone does not establish that distinct defect.
- **Smith v. Robbins, January 19, 2000:** Its distinction between equivalent form and preservation of the required function informs the substitute inquiry; its appellate-counsel setting does not itself decide confession admissibility.
- **United States v. Alvarez-Sanchez, 511 U.S. 350 (1994):** Its subsection-(c) relevant-presentment-duty rule, six-hour protection, transportation-and-distance proviso and reservations remain. Today's decision resolves the distinct voluntariness-only displacement question that it reserved.
- **Stansbury v. California, 511 U.S. 318 (1994), and Thompson v. Keohane, 516 U.S. 99 (1995):** Their objective custody rule and distinction between ultimate constitutional classification and historical facts remain; no new custody or credibility finding is made.
- **Davis v. United States, 512 U.S. 452 (1994):** Its actual postwaiver clarification, clear-invocation and waiver rules remain; its reservation of the overall section-3501 question is not treated as an earlier answer.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-166.
**Decided:** June 5, 2000.

##### Compelled testimonial selection cannot become an immunized witness's investigative roadmap

**Holding:** When a broad subpoena compels an immunized individual to use personal knowledge to identify, locate and assemble previously unidentified incriminating documents, and that production communicates information beyond a foregone conclusion, sections 6002–6003 bar direct criminal use of the compelled testimony and evidence derived from it. The Government bears the affirmative burden of proving legitimate sources wholly independent of that testimony; declining to introduce either the production act or the papers at trial does not satisfy that burden.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The mandate requires implementation of dismissal of this indictment under the immunity ruling and the conditional agreement. No new source inquiry is ordered. Other defendants' charges and the excluded Independent Counsel authority ruling remain outside the judgment; independent evidence retains its ordinary legal availability without any finding that such evidence exists here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Fisher v. United States:** Applies the act/contents distinction and foregone-conclusion boundary; voluntary preparation does not excuse derivative use of compelled testimony.
- **United States v. Doe (1984):** Applies protection for an incriminating individual act of production despite unprivileged contents.
- **Kastigar v. United States:** Applies direct and derivative immunity and the Government's affirmative wholly independent-source burden.
- **Doe v. United States (1988):** Distinguishes a noninformative hypothetical consent act from disclosure of the individual's knowledge.
- **Braswell v. United States:** Preserves collective-entity doctrine and its treatment of the individual production act; it does not resolve this immunized personal subpoena by collapsing act into contents.
- **United States v. Balsys:** Preserves domestic use-and-derivative-use immunity; its independent-foreign-risk rule does not narrow the protection here.

### Suspicionless checkpoints

#### City of Indianapolis v. Edmond, 531 U.S. 32

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1030.
**Decided:** November 28, 2000.

##### Primary ordinary-crime-control purpose and authority for a suspicionless seizure

**Holding:** A checkpoint stop seizes a vehicle before any exterior canine examination. A program primarily designed to obtain evidence of ordinary narcotics offenses cannot dispense with individualized suspicion because it operates briefly and systematically or includes license and sobriety checks; it needs an independently lawful justification for the seizure.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** Indianapolis's admitted principal narcotics objective makes a purpose-finding remand unnecessary. The need for antecedent authority cannot be supplied by reduced intrusion, the gravity of the drug problem, or an ancillary lawful task. Program instructions, official statements, and actual practice may establish purpose without a required statistical study, universal numerical efficacy threshold, catastrophe, or exhaustion of every less intrusive option.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule reaches comparable ordinary-crime-detection roadblocks. It does not condemn every program connected to criminal enforcement or action on evidence lawfully encountered during a justified stop. Whether a primarily lawful license or sobriety program may be expanded to detect narcotics remains open. Emergency roadblocks, urgent threats, airport and government-building security, other border settings, individualized-suspicion searches, and canine examination itself are outside this decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is affirmed, with remand for preliminary prospective restraint against this program. No replacement program, suppression, damages, or invalidation of an independently justified traffic stop is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Whren v. United States preserves action on actual probable cause despite an officer's further motive; it does not provide authority for a suspicionless program. Chandler v. Miller requires an adequate actual justification without a universal checklist. Michigan Department of State Police v. Sitz and United States v. Martinez-Fuerte retain their highway-safety and border-control rules in their settings. Delaware v. Prouse does not make systematic operation independently sufficient. Bond v. United States distinguishes lawful presence from authority for a further intrusion and supplies no new checkpoint-purpose rule.

### Temporary preservation of a home pending a warrant

#### Illinois v. McArthur, 531 U.S. 326

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1132
**Decided:** February 20, 2001.

##### Whether the combined evidence-preservation need and execution justified this restriction on home access

**Holding:** Officers may temporarily restrict unaccompanied entry into a home while obtaining a warrant when particularized probable cause, a reasonable actual risk of evidence destruction, a restraint reasonably confined to preservation and diligent pursuit of judicial authorization together justify that intrusion. The approximately two-hour restriction here satisfies those conditions in combination; probable cause, refusal of consent, awareness of an investigation, disposable drugs, jailability or duration alone creates no automatic exception or safe harbor.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s opinion of the Court, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** Uncontested probable cause, the wife’s observation of hidden drugs, awareness of investigation and ready access to the identified evidence supported the destruction concern. Officers diligently sought a warrant, permitted limited accompanied entries, and conducted no evidentiary search before authorization. Offense gravity weighs in the balance even though the modest offenses allowed imprisonment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An observed destruction attempt is not invariably necessary; consent refusal is neither guilt nor an independent exigency. No fixed two-hour allowance, general home occupation or search power, universal exception checklist, police-created unlawful exigency rule, or duty to exhaust every imaginable alternative is decided. Later discoveries do not justify the earlier restraint.

##### The consequence for the presented suppression ground

**Holding:** The Illinois appellate judgment is reversed insofar as suppression rests on this temporary restriction of residential access, and the case is remanded for ordinary proceedings. Any actually preserved independent warrant, admissibility or exclusionary-rule issue remains governed by its own law; none is found established or newly created here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s opinion of the Court, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the temporary-restraint suppression ground and remand. No finding of guilt, warrant validation on every ground, new admissibility issue, charge dismissal, release, new trial or evidentiary hearing is supplied.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: preserves the home's warrant protection; no general residential-entry authority is created.
- Whren v. United States: preserves objective ordinary-enforcement analysis and independent limits on greater intrusion, scope and duration; traffic probable cause is not residential authority.
- Bond v. United States: applies the requirement of independent justification for an added intrusion; lawful presence and limited observation do not imply consent to search.
- City of Indianapolis v. Edmond: distinguishes suspicionless ordinary-crime checkpoints from this individualized preservation measure; its program-purpose holding remains intact.
- United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696: applies attention to actual duration and diligence in temporary preservation; no fixed permissible interval is transferred from luggage to a home.
- Welsh v. Wisconsin, 466 U.S. 740: preserves the importance of offense gravity and home protection; distinguishes its civil, nonjailable nighttime arrest without making every jailable offense sufficient for restraint.
- Segura v. United States, 468 U.S. 796: preserves its independent-source holding while declining to treat it as approval of every initial seizure; the broader securing-premises discussion in Part IV remains a two-Justice position, not a majority holding.

### Testimonial exposure and professed innocence

#### Ohio v. Reiner, 532 U.S. 17

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-1028.
**Decided:** March 19, 2001.

##### Whether professed innocence defeats a supported privilege against self-incrimination

**Holding:** A witness who denies wrongdoing may invoke the Fifth Amendment when the circumstances create reasonable cause to fear that truthful answers would incriminate the witness, including by supplying a link in a chain of evidence. The court must determine that the risk is real; the assertion of fear alone and imaginary or remote possibilities do not suffice, but a denial of guilt cannot categorically eliminate an otherwise supported privilege.

**Proposition-level authority:** Reasoned per curiam opinion of the Court. The published action discloses no numerical tally or individual join lineup.

**Material application:** The defense identified the babysitter as a possible perpetrator, and disputed injury timing supplied a real possibility of testimonial exposure. That supports privilege without finding that she inflicted the injuries.

##### What correction of the federal privilege premise decides about Ohio immunity and the conviction

**Holding:** A state judgment necessarily resting on the mistaken federal rule that a professedly innocent witness lacks the privilege cannot stand on that premise; the judgment is reversed and remanded for further proceedings under the reasonable-exposure rule. Recognizing that privilege neither requires nor establishes the validity of transactional immunity under Ohio law and does not decide preserved independent grounds for relief arising from the immunity's use or presentation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Reasoned per curiam opinion of the Court. The published action discloses no numerical tally or individual join lineup.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Under Ohio Rev. Code section 2945.44 (1999), if a criminal-proceeding witness refuses information on self-incrimination grounds, the common pleas court must compel a response when the prosecutor requests it and the court advises that responding confers transactional immunity, unless the court finds the order would not advance the administration of justice. Those state conditions and any preserved independent defense-prejudice claim remain for Ohio courts. A possible state ground is not treated as an already established adequate independent basis; no privilege holding itself requires or validates immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reasoned summary judgment reverses and remands the state judgment on its mistaken categorical federal premise. It does not automatically reinstate a final conviction or determine either person's guilt.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hoffman v. United States is applied to require a real, circumstances-based danger of incrimination, including evidentiary links.
- Grunewald v. United States is applied to reject an innocence-based forfeiture of that privilege.
- Mitchell v. United States preserves the distinction between privilege and procedural or evidentiary consequences; its sentencing-inference question is not decided here.
- United States v. Hubbell retains its compelled-production and derivative-use scope. Its immunity rule is not transformed into a rule requiring Ohio transactional immunity.

### Hospital testing coordinated with criminal prosecution

#### Ferguson v. City of Charleston, 532 U.S. 67

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-936.
**Decided:** March 21, 2001.

##### Special-needs justification for a public hospital's coordinated drug-testing and prosecution program

**Holding:** Assuming consent was legally insufficient, this planned urine-collection, testing, evidence-preservation, police-disclosure and arrest program cannot be justified by special needs. Its genuine clinical work and health objectives do not independently justify the designed prosecution operation; the decision adopts no categorical rule against all mixed purposes or distinction based solely on immediate versus ultimate purpose.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Material application:** The health purpose remains genuine, but chain-of-custody and arrest arrangements, including initial arrests without a treatment alternative, required a justification adequate to their actual scope. Ordinary patients lacked Griffin's lawful probationary supervisory relationship.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Medical attendance is not waiver. Consent to care and consent to the evidence operation require fact-sensitive analysis, without a universal three-permission formula or a holding that disclosure alone constitutes a separate search. Private hospitals, independent reporting duties, genuine emergencies, materially different clinical programs and suppression in another prosecution are outside the decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the special-needs affirmance and remand for the preserved challenge to the consent verdict, followed by any live responsibility, municipal attribution, immunity, causation, injury and remedy issues. No patient-specific lack of consent, final liability, damages, universal injunction, automatic new trial or new hearing is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chandler requires an actual operational need; Edmond prevents additional legitimate functions from automatically validating ordinary narcotics-evidence gathering. Vernonia supplies the urine-search and no-waiver rules but not police-testing power over patients. Griffin retains its mixed supervision/enforcement rule, so police cooperation alone is not disqualifying. Skinner and Von Raab remain confined to their actual protective arrangements.

### Attached counsel protection for an inseparable criminal episode

#### Texas v. Cobb, 532 U.S. 162

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1702.
**Decided:** April 2, 2001.

##### Attached counsel protection for interrogation reconstructing the same represented criminal episode

**Holding:** After prosecution and representation begin, Sixth Amendment protection reaches questioning about an additional offense inseparable from the charged occurrence, such that the interrogation necessarily reconstructs that same criminal episode. Distinct statutory elements do not exclude it; shared actors, similar methods or general criminal association without that integration do not bring other crimes within protection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices, also adopting the waiver holding below.

**Material application:** The murders occurred during the represented burglary and the confession reconstructed that occurrence, despite the actual elements of the multiple-murder charge. Earlier counsel permissions did not authorize the challenged interview.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary attachment requirements survive. The rule covers neither every factual association nor unrelated crimes, does not depend on hidden police motive and does not bar questioning represented persons as witnesses.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** McNeil's offense specificity is preserved with the inseparable-occurrence boundary; Moulton's anti-circumvention protection is extended to this additional prosecution. Brewer retains protection against deliberate elicitation. Blockburger and Brown retain their double-jeopardy roles but do not exclusively define the right to counsel.

##### Whether a custodial waiver suffices after assertion of the attached counsel right

**Holding:** A Miranda waiver alone does not authorize police-initiated deliberate elicitation concerning the protected episode after assertion of the attached right under Michigan v. Jackson. Given the state court's established assertion and prejudice determinations, the confession must be excluded and its new-trial judgment stands.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same five-Justice opinion of the Court (Breyer).

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not make appointment universally invoke Fifth Amendment protection, decide defendant-initiated questioning or a genuinely valid Sixth Amendment waiver, or adjudicate coercion. It does not reopen Jackson.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the new-trial judgment and exclude the challenged confession. The State may retry with independent admissible evidence and investigate lawfully; there is no acquittal or prosecution bar.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Jackson governs the established assertion and police-initiated encounter; Miranda and Dickerson preserve a distinct custodial safeguard rather than a substitute Sixth Amendment waiver. Davis's clarification requirement for a reasonable possible present request remains effective, without a new request or violation found here.

### Additional station custody for fine-only criminal offenses

#### Atwater v. City of Lago Vista, 532 U.S. 318

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1408.
**Decided:** April 24, 2001.

##### Whether probable cause alone justifies station transport and confinement for an observed fine-only misdemeanor

**Holding:** Probable cause for an observed fine-only criminal misdemeanor does not alone justify added station transport and confinement. The additional restraint must reasonably serve a supported actual enforcement function, which may include identification, appearance, safety, stopping a continuing violation, protecting evidence or necessary processing; those possibilities are neither presumed nor exhaustive, and the rule requires neither the least intrusive imaginable alternative nor citation whenever possible.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer, controls the functional rule and removal of the categorical summary judgment.

**Material application:** Missing physical identification papers must be considered together with the supplied license number and address and known local residence. Neither the earlier warning, which followed recognition that the child was belted, nor routine booking preferences establishes an otherwise unsupported custody need.

**Authority of the judgment:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer support vacatur and remand. Ginsburg, Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer would additionally find the supported account constitutionally insufficient; Stone-Zsela requires actual verification and custody-need determinations. Neither ultimate application has a majority, and Marks does not combine these different application positions into one controlling conclusion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision establishes no constitutional or unconstitutional final application to this custody and no damages liability. Personal immunity uses March 1997 law; later decisions supply no retrospective notice. Municipal liability still requires attributable policy or custom, applicable fault, causation and injury. Other punishment classes are outside the question.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the categorical summary judgment and remand under the functional rule, preserving genuine supported disputes and defenses. The return permits no speculative reopening of established facts, requires no new hearing, and leaves the seatbelt convictions intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Ricci's actual-enforcement requirement extends from fine-only civil licensing to this criminal custody while retaining its rejection of categorical arrest immunity and universal citation or least-restrictive-means duties. Whren governs the initial objective stop and preserves limits on greater restraint; Knowles, Robinson and Gustafson do not derive antecedent custody authority from incident-search consequences. Wilson v. Arkansas preserves relevant historical inquiry without making state authorization dispositive. McArthur's residential-preservation application, and Wilson v. Layne and Wallis's separate immunity and municipal predicates, retain their distinct scopes.

### Gun-possession information, particularized suspicion and brief submission

#### United States v. Valentine

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit No. 00-1425; judgment below, 232 F.3d 350; no Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** May 14, 2001.

##### Whether reliable knowledge of gun possession establishes suspicion of wrongdoing

**Holding:** A recent personal eyewitness report can strengthen the reliability of gun-possession information, but reliable possession and reasonable suspicion of crime remain distinct. On this record the report, late hour, generalized neighborhood shootings and ordinary departure supply no individualized suspicion without a supported further connection, assessed with all actual circumstances together.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins to all three rules and the remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical ban on unidentified eyewitness reports or demand that each fact independently indicate crime follows. Grounded experience, actual licensing circumstances and innocent conduct considered in context may matter; a more particular report or supported conduct may justify restraint. The Court does not decide all possible admission grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Wardlow requires individualized totality rather than location or departure shortcuts. J. L. distinguishes identification from wrongdoing without an ordinary-gun exception. Ornelas requires independent constitutional evaluation while protecting supported facts and grounded inferences.

##### Whether a bare presumption of nonlicensure substitutes for particularized suspicion

**Holding:** A state evidentiary presumption that a handgun possessor lacks a permit cannot alone replace the Fourth Amendment's particularized factual basis. Concrete known circumstances, including supported licensing conditions, may contribute under ordinary totality review; the Government's full preserved permitting theory must receive that review.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same five-Justice opinion directly adopts this limit; the dissent's reservation supplies no additional join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Officers need not invariably check a database, observe a completed crime or obtain a prescribed extra fact. Permit status, permit prevalence, the evidentiary presumption's trial validity and the licensing scheme's constitutionality are not decided. Missing published detail does not establish that officers lacked all licensing knowledge.

##### Whether genuine brief compliance constitutes a seizure

**Holding:** Objectively manifested yielding to a command can complete a seizure despite its brevity, and later flight does not undo it. There is no minimum duration, but hesitation, inquiry, equivocal movement or an unheeded command without actual yielding remains insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same five-Justice Court opinion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand, 5-4, for application to the supported first-seizure chronology and preserved independent grounds. The reported continuous charge and attempted pushing may precede the first grab if no earlier submission occurred; actual earlier yielding changes which facts could justify inception. The gun's later discovery supplies no antecedent justification. The Court finds no pause or necessary factual conflict and orders no new hearing, proof reopening, suppression, acquittal or dismissal; distinct attenuation or exclusionary grounds require their own predicates.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hodari D.'s force-or-submission boundary includes genuine brief yielding. Ornelas creates no missing facts or mandatory hearing. McArthur's residential-preservation setting and Atwater's added station custody after probable cause do not supply the initial suspicion or submission predicates here.

### Traffic stops, added custody and inventories

#### Arkansas v. Sullivan, 532 U.S. 769

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-262.
**Decided:** May 29, 2001.

##### Whether investigative motive invalidates a supported stop or establishes later custody and search authority

**Holding:** An ordinary traffic stop supported by objective probable cause is not unconstitutional solely because the officer also investigates another offense. The stop does not itself authorize later custody, impoundment or search, each of which must satisfy its own legal requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** The unanimous reasoned per curiam merits opinion controls all three criminal-procedure propositions in this entry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Whren v. United States supplies the ordinary stop rule while preserving independent restraint and search limits. Knowles v. Iowa keeps search authority distinct from a lawful encounter.

##### Whether added custody for a known fine-only offense requires an actual enforcement function

**Holding:** Added station transport and confinement for a known fine-only criminal offense must reasonably serve a supported actual enforcement function; probable cause alone is insufficient. Identification, appearance, safety, a continuing violation, evidence protection and necessary processing are possible, nonexclusive functions, not facts presumed from the arrest.

**Proposition-level authority:** Unanimous per curiam opinion applying Atwater.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither least restrictive means nor a citation whenever conceivable is required. The Court does not find that every asserted offense was fine-only, that the hatchet supplied a valid weapons ground, that identification failed, or that processing required custody. Actual offense classifications and supported arrest grounds govern where material; investigative interest supplies no missing authority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Atwater v. City of Lago Vista applies Ricci v. Village of Arlington Heights's actual-enforcement inquiry to known fine-only criminal custody without adopting a universal citation duty or deciding Sullivan's custody.

##### Whether impoundment and an asserted inventory satisfy independent constitutional conditions

**Holding:** Impoundment and inventory require their own lawful authority, genuine caretaking purpose and compliance with standardized practices. Investigative interest does not defeat every otherwise lawful act, but an investigatory ruse fails the inventory exception and a policy label alone does not establish compliance; subsequently discovered drugs cannot supply antecedent probable cause.

**Proposition-level authority:** Unanimous per curiam opinion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The suppression judgment is vacated for consideration of actual preserved custody, impoundment, inventory and independent state-law grounds under the correct standards. The Court directs no new hearing, reopening of settled facts, particular admissibility result or outcome of the prosecution, and establishes no unsupported search-incident theory.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Colorado v. Bertine and Florida v. Wells supply independent inventory authority, purpose and standardized-practice requirements preserved by Whren. Knowles requires actual lawful custody for the search-incident rationale rather than deriving search power from the stop alone.

For reviewability and the state court's federal-law duty, see Federal Courts - Review of settled federal issues in pending state prosecutions, Arkansas v. Sullivan, No. 00-262, decided May 29, 2001.

### Thermal acquisition of concealed domestic information

#### Kyllo v. United States, 533 U.S. 27

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-8508.
**Decided:** June 11, 2001.

##### The targeted thermal examination acquired protected domestic information

**Holding:** Targeted technological examination of a closed home’s heat patterns is a Fourth Amendment search when it reveals otherwise unobservable information about concealed domestic operation. Passive external reception, lack of a recognizable interior image and the equipment’s market availability do not remove that protection; equipment use or investigative purpose alone does not establish a search.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes for this holding and its qualifications. Kennedy and Breyer dissent.

**Material application:** The Agema 210 pattern of unusually warm roof and wall areas revealed hidden domestic operation, including an inference of cultivation lights. Findings that the device was crude, passive and produced no identifiable interior image do not eliminate the information it acquired.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general-public-use condition limits the rule. Ordinary exterior observation does not become a search merely because it aids investigation. The Court decides no all-instrument rule, future technology or open-commercial-premises question.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pinson and Myers are applied without change. Katz and Karo retain protection of concealed domestic information without physical entry. Greenwood’s discarded-property rule and Place’s contraband-confined detection are distinguished.

##### The later entry warrant did not authorize the earlier scan

**Holding:** This search required an advance warrant or a recognized warrant exception. No exception justified the scan on the established record, and the later warrant for physical entry could not retroactively authorize it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes for this holding and its qualifications. Kennedy and Breyer dissent.

**Material application:** The agents held no scan warrant, and no consent, contemporaneous exigency or other recognized exception is established. Later discovery of contraband creates no antecedent emergency. Corroborative use of the scan does not turn the later entry warrant into prior authorization.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pinson and Myers retain their separate warrant requirement. Katz and Karo preserve recognized exceptions when their actual predicates exist; none is established for this scan.

##### The preserved derivative-evidence claim requires a lawful determination under the corrected search rule

**Holding:** A conviction affirmance and rejection of a preserved suppression claim that depend on this erroneous no-search premise must be vacated to the extent necessary to decide the claim under the proper search rule. On remand, the court must determine whether the later warrant and evidence remain lawfully supportable without the scan or through a properly available, fact-supported alternative; suppression follows if its predicates are established and no lawful ground defeats exclusion, with conditional-plea consequences governed by Rule 11(a)(2).

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes for this holding and its qualifications. Kennedy and Breyer dissent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No final untainted-probable-cause, causal-dependence, independent-source, inevitable-discovery or objective-reliance finding is made. Leon requires an available, fact-supported claim of objectively reasonable reliance and excludes knowing or reckless deception, abandonment of judicial neutrality, an affidavit wholly lacking probable-cause indicia and facial deficiency precluding reasonable reliance; its inquiry includes information-supplying officers and assumes execution within scope. The 1992 conduct date alone proves no reliance. Evans concerns qualifying non-law-enforcement court-clerical error. Pinson's affidavit nonchallenge does not govern this preserved claim. For a genuinely forfeited issue, Olano separately requires error, plainness, ordinarily outcome-affecting prejudice and the discretionary fairness, integrity or public-reputation inquiry; intentional waiver remains distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The suppression disposition and dependent conviction affirmance are vacated only to the extent necessary for the corrected inquiry. Exclusion follows if its predicates are met and no supported lawful ground defeats it. Rule 11(a)(2) permits conditional-plea withdrawal when the defendant prevails on appeal; the remand itself orders no exclusion, withdrawal, acquittal, release, discovery, new hearing, retrial or sentence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Myers supplies the preserved-claim remand; Pinson’s different preservation posture is distinguished. Karo retains the untainted-warrant inquiry. Leon, Evans and Olano retain their actual predicates and limits; Rule 11(a)(2) retains its conditional-plea consequence.
