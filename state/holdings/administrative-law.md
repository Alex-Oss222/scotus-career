## Administrative Law

### Administrative fee awards

#### Ardestani v. INS, 502 U.S. 129 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1141.  
**Decided:** December 10, 1991.

##### Whether an INA deportation hearing is an adjudication under section 554 for administrative EAJA fees

**Holding:** An administrative deportation hearing governed by the INA's exclusive procedure rather than by 5 U.S.C. §554 is not an adjudication under section 554 and therefore falls outside EAJA §504's administrative fee authorization, even if the hearing resembles formal APA adjudication.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion joined by Stone, White, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; six of eight participants.

**Material application:** The exclusive INA procedure supplies the hearing's governing authority. Functional similarity, agency regulations, and remedial purpose cannot enlarge the enacted fee cross-reference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Substantial justification is not reached because coverage fails. Covered judicial EAJA claims and separately presented constitutional claims are not decided; the asylum grant and its factual basis are undisturbed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eleventh Circuit is affirmed; the administrative fee award is unavailable under §504.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Marcello supplies the INA procedure's displacement of APA hearing provisions. Sullivan v. Hudson is distinguished as involving administrative work integral to a qualifying court action. Shaw's monetary-authorization rule and Pierce's substantial-justification rule remain unchanged; neither supplies missing §504 coverage.

### Bank regulatory review

#### Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System v. MCorp Financial, Inc., 502 U.S. 32 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-913 and 90-914.  
**Decided:** December 3, 1991.

##### Whether bankruptcy jurisdiction or Leedom v. Kyne permits an injunction against ongoing Board proceedings despite section 1818(i)(1)

**Holding:** Section 1818(i)(1) bars a district court from enjoining an ongoing Board notice-or-order proceeding except through channels that §1818 itself authorizes. Neither general bankruptcy jurisdiction nor Leedom v. Kyne supplies an alternative intervention channel where the proceeding falls within the governmental regulatory exception and meaningful review of a final order remains available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; all eight participants.

**Material application:** The adversary action fits none of §1818's authorized channels for temporary-order challenges, final-order review, or Board enforcement. Section 362(b)(4) permits governmental regulatory proceedings to continue; §§362(a)(3) and (a)(6) do not halt these nonfinal proceedings before seizure of estate property or recovery of a prepetition claim. Section 1334 supplies no competing jurisdiction because the Board is not another court and continuation does not displace control of estate property.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The regulation's validity and any actual conflict at enforcement are reserved. The decision changes no agency-deference rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** No. 90-913 is reversed, removing the source-of-strength injunction; No. 90-914 is affirmed, leaving the refusal to enjoin the §23A proceeding intact. Both proceedings may continue through the statutory channels.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Leedom v. Kyne is confined to circumstances without meaningful later review; §1818(h)(2) supplies that review here and §1818(i)(1) expressly precludes intervention. Boire's narrow understanding of Kyne is applied.

### Census determinations and judicial review

#### Franklin v. Massachusetts, 505 U.S. 788 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1502.
**Decided:** June 26, 1992.

For the separate standing and constitutional-counting holdings in Franklin v. Massachusetts, June 26, 1992, No. 91-1502, see Federal Courts — Standing and representation injury and Election Law — Interstate congressional apportionment.

##### The Secretary's completed census determination is reviewable

**Holding:** The Secretary's completed census determination supplies final agency action under APA §704 even though the President later transmits the resulting apportionment statement. The census statutes neither preclude review nor commit the counting decision entirely to unreviewable discretion: the duty to count the proper population supplies law to apply, while leaving substantial methodological choice to the Secretary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter, controls with five votes. White, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas disagree with this rule while agreeing with reversal on their own reviewability ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Presidential transmission under §2a does not confer an independent presidential choice of the population counted under §141. The President's own APA status and susceptibility to coercive relief remain undecided; Waterman's independent foreign-affairs decision structure is different.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The District Court's judgment is reversed and its corrective relief dissolved. No replacement allocation, further factual proceedings or injunction against the President is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136 (1967): applied for reviewability and practical completion of an agency determination with concrete consequences; the statutory transmittal here does not postpone finality.
- Chicago & Southern Air Lines, Inc. v. Waterman Steamship Corp., 333 U.S. 103 (1948): distinguished because Congress there required an independent presidential foreign-affairs judgment; its rule for that decision structure is preserved.
- Japan Whaling Assn. v. American Cetacean Society, 478 U.S. 221 (1986): applied for review against statutory limits despite broad administrative judgment; methodological latitude is not absence of law to apply.

##### The statutory and administrative objections fail on this record

**Holding:** Section 141 permits the supported inclusion of temporarily overseas personnel with enduring state ties, and the Secretary's comparative choice of supplemented home-of-record information is not arbitrary or capricious on this record. The agency must supply a contemporaneous, reasonably discernible explanation connecting its available information and residence objective to its choice; imperfection alone does not require rejection of every available dataset.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter, controls with five votes. The other four Justices do not join the statutory or APA merits determination.

**Material application:** The contemporaneous record supplies supplementary personnel information and continuing residence reasons despite cancellation of the proposed survey. Home of record may identify enduring ties; tax residence and last duty station have different defects. The holding relies on actual agency reasons, not stricken later declarations or counsel's substitute explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No replacement allocation, judicially selected dataset, injunction against the President, or general APA status for the President is decided. No claim that another residence method necessarily restores a seat is found proved. The statutory and administrative holdings are record-specific applications; no earlier Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The District Court's judgment is reversed and its corrective relief dissolved. No replacement allocation, further factual proceedings or injunction against the President is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Assn. v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co., 463 U.S. 29 (1983): applied for consideration of relevant factors, reasoned explanation and review on the agency's actual rationale; no exemption from review accompanies census discretion.
- Bowman Transportation, Inc. v. Arkansas-Best Freight System, Inc., 419 U.S. 281 (1974): applied for a reasonably discernible decisional path despite imperfect exposition; a court may not substitute a new rationale.

### Coastal permits and Administrative Procedure Act review

#### United States v. Alaska, 503 U.S. 569 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 118, Original.
**Decided:** April 21, 1992.

For the other questions in *United States v. Alaska*, April 21, 1992, No. 118, Original, see Federal Courts — Standing and original jurisdiction (live title controversy), Constitutional Structure — Federal and state submerged-land boundaries (§10 condition), and Administrative Law — Coastal permits and Administrative Procedure Act review (regulatory and APA objections).

##### Whether the governing regulation permits shelf-rights review and the Corps unlawfully delegated its decision

**Holding:** The public-interest and coastline provisions of 33 C.F.R. §320.4(a) and (f) permit consideration of the Nome project's prospective effect on federal shelf rights; subsection (g)(6)'s exclusion of disputes about an applicant's construction-site property interest does not forbid that inquiry. The Corps' documented adoption of the boundary concern and its own permit decision reject Alaska's claim of unlawful delegation on this record, without authorizing Interior or the Attorney General to exercise an independent permitting veto.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, Part III, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; eight Justices support both propositions. Stone agrees with the disposition of this component only.

**Material application:** Section 320.4(a) and (f) authorize shelf-effects review; subsection (g)(6) concerns the applicant’s construction-site property interest. The Corps adopted Interior’s stated concern and made its own permit determination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No independent Interior or Attorney General veto is authorized. Compliance with unpresented internal-routing requirements and automatic disclaimer rules for other permits are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Federal summary judgment is granted and Alaska’s competing motion and general injunction request are denied. The declaration enforces the disclaimer only as to boundary change and corresponding entitlement attributable to the permitted Nome extension. Existing state property, claims and disputes over the precise preproject line remain intact; the permit and construction are not vacated. No remand, monetary award, nationwide permitting injunction or surrender of unrelated lands is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Arkansas v. Oklahoma, February 26, 1992 — applied at its source-specific limit: a valid regulation guides the agency's own permit determination; it does not transfer responsibility to a different agency.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992 — applied: the agency's own discernible ground supports review; agreement with an adviser is not a substitute for finding that ground in the agency record.

##### Whether publication or actual and timely notice defeats the §552 challenge to the Nome condition

**Holding:** Section 552(a)(1) does not invalidate this condition as adverse application of a secret review criterion: the governing public-interest and coastline regulations published consideration of the project's effects on federal outer continental rights. The absence of an express catalogue of every possible curative disclaimer does not make that published criterion an undisclosed rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, Part IV-A, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground for rejecting the §552 challenge. Stone agrees with its disposition only.

**Material application:** The published coastline regulation expressly identified baseline shifts and effects on federal outer continental rights. A catalogue of every possible curative disclaimer was unnecessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This applied published criterion does not validate an undisclosed binding rule for every coastal project or dispense with separate §553 duties.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Federal summary judgment is granted and Alaska’s competing motion and general injunction request are denied. The declaration enforces the disclaimer only as to boundary change and corresponding entitlement attributable to the permitted Nome extension. Existing state property, claims and disputes over the precise preproject line remain intact; the permit and construction are not vacated. No remand, monetary award, nationwide permitting injunction or surrender of unrelated lands is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morton v. Ruiz, 415 U.S. 199 (1974) — distinguished on the applied criterion and preserved: undisclosed eligibility restrictions and disregard of required agency procedures remain impermissible; this case applies a published shelf-effects criterion.

**Independent alternative holding:** Independently, §552(a)(1)'s bar on adverse application of unpublished matter does not protect a person who had actual and timely notice of its terms. Alaska had that notice of the Nome condition and its stated ground before executing the disclaimer, so any failure separately to publish those particular terms supplies no basis for invalidating the disclaimer as applied to Alaska.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** White's opinion, Part IV-B, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this independently sufficient alternative holding. Stone agrees with its disposition only.

**Material application of alternative holding:** The July 1, 1983 communication supplied the condition and ground, followed by approximately ten months for consideration and negotiation before Alaska executed the disclaimer on May 9, 1984.

**Limits of alternative holding:** Notice of this condition is not notice of every general policy and does not itself cure §553 or another mandatory-procedure violation.

**Treatment of earlier authority for alternative holding:**

- Morton v. Ruiz, 415 U.S. 199 (1974) — applied with the statutory actual-notice qualification intact; its protection against undisclosed restrictions is not converted into invalidation regardless of timely knowledge.

##### Whether §553 required an additional legislative rule for the individual Nome permit condition

**Holding:** The underlying public-interest and coastline provisions were adopted through notice and comment, and §553 did not require the Corps to promulgate an additional legislative rule before specifying this curative condition in Nome's individual licensing proceeding. This holding depends on application of those existing criteria and the absence of an established, separately binding Corps rule requiring every coastal permit to contain a disclaimer.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, Part V, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; eight Justices support the rule and application. Stone agrees with the disposition of the §553 component only.

**Material application:** The 1973 proposal and 1974 final rule underwent notice and comment. The individual condition applied those criteria in licensing, which §551 includes within adjudication.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No public-property exception or exemption for legislative rules is adopted. The record establishes no separately binding Corps policy requiring all coastal permits to carry disclaimers.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Federal summary judgment is granted and Alaska’s competing motion and general injunction request are denied. The declaration enforces the disclaimer only as to boundary change and corresponding entitlement attributable to the permitted Nome extension. Existing state property, claims and disputes over the precise preproject line remain intact; the permit and construction are not vacated. No remand, monetary award, nationwide permitting injunction or surrender of unrelated lands is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- SEC v. Chenery Corp., 332 U.S. 194 (1947) — applied: permissible development through adjudication does not require a separate rule merely because a particular decision articulates policy; statutory and procedural limits remain.
- NLRB v. Bell Aerospace Co., 416 U.S. 267 (1974) — applied: an agency may ordinarily choose adjudication for an individual determination, subject to review of an improper exercise of that choice.
- NLRB v. Wyman-Gordon Co., 394 U.S. 759 (1969) — its divided rationales are kept distinct: the case supplies no majority rule requiring notice and comment before every individual licensing condition. No new rule is assembled from its separate opinions.

##### Whether the Corps supplied a reasoned basis for the Nome condition under §706

**Holding:** The Corps' Nome condition is not arbitrary or capricious under §706(2)(A): its contemporaneous record identifies the prospective loss of federal shelf rights, adopts that concern, and evaluates the project with a condition that directly removes the concern. The independent statutory, regulatory, and procedural analyses above also reject the asserted excess-authority and unlawful-procedure grounds under §706(2)(C) and (D); lawful authority alone would not excuse an unexplained exercise of discretion.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, Part VI, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; eight Justices support this review holding. Stone agrees with the §706 disposition only.

**Material application:** The Corps adopted the identified shelf-boundary concern in July 1983 and evaluated the conditioned project in July 1984. The condition removed that concern while permitting the documented public benefits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Lawful authority alone does not establish reasoned discretion; later mineral information cannot repair a missing contemporaneous reason. No separate quantitative mineral valuation or universal harmless-error rule is imposed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Federal summary judgment is granted and Alaska’s competing motion and general injunction request are denied. The declaration enforces the disclaimer only as to boundary change and corresponding entitlement attributable to the permitted Nome extension. Existing state property, claims and disputes over the precise preproject line remain intact; the permit and construction are not vacated. No remand, monetary award, nationwide permitting injunction or surrender of unrelated lands is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Association v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co., 463 U.S. 29 (1983) — applied: the agency must connect relevant facts to its choice; a discernible path suffices, but post hoc grounds do not.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992 — applied: this contemporaneously expressed and adopted ground satisfies the own-ground requirement; no new agency rationale is supplied by the Court.
- Marsh v. Oregon Natural Resources Council, 490 U.S. 360 (1989) — applied for careful review of the agency record within the governing statutory setting, without adding a quantitative mineral-valuation duty.
- Arkansas v. Oklahoma, February 26, 1992 — preserved: judicial review tests the agency decision against its actual governing law and record, without replacing it with a judicial policy.

##### Whether §706(2)(E) governs this permit and the record supplies a factual foundation under §706(2)(A)

**Holding:** Section 706(2)(E)'s substantial-evidence requirement applies to the hearing or statutory-record proceedings identified in that provision; a public notice and opportunity for comments alone do not establish that predicate. Alaska has established no such predicate for this permit, and its separate assertion that the condition lacks a factual foundation fails under §706(2)(A) because the stipulated project and contemporaneously identified boundary effect support the actual ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, Part VII, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; eight Justices support the statutory-review holding and the distinct record application. Stone agrees with the disposition of this §706 component only.

**Material application:** Section 706(2)(E) concerns proceedings subject to §§556–557 or otherwise reviewed on a statutorily required agency-hearing record. No such predicate is established; the stipulated plans and contemporaneous correspondence support the separate arbitrary-action review.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Public notice and an opportunity to comment do not alone establish the hearing predicate. No evidentiary hearing is ordered, and proceedings that actually satisfy the statutory predicate remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Federal summary judgment is granted and Alaska’s competing motion and general injunction request are denied. The declaration enforces the disclaimer only as to boundary change and corresponding entitlement attributable to the permitted Nome extension. Existing state property, claims and disputes over the precise preproject line remain intact; the permit and construction are not vacated. No remand, monetary award, nationwide permitting injunction or surrender of unrelated lands is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Arkansas v. Oklahoma, February 26, 1992 — distinguished as to the review channel: its whole-record substantial-evidence rule does not establish §706(2)(E)'s hearing predicate for every informal permit decision.

### Commission findings and appellate reconsideration

#### FTC v. Ticor Title Insurance Co., 504 U.S. 621 (1992)

##### How the Third Circuit must reconsider Connecticut and Arizona supervision and Commission findings

**Holding:** The Third Circuit must reconsider Connecticut and Arizona active-supervision determinations by requiring actual independent state judgment and control over the private rates, rather than available regulatory capacity or nominal approval, and by treating supported Commission fact findings as conclusive under §5(c), 15 U.S.C. §45(c). ### Federal permit interpretation and review

#### Arkansas v. Oklahoma, 503 U.S. 91 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-1262 and 90-1266.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.
**Companion or consolidated matters:** *EPA v. Oklahoma*, No. 90-1266, consolidated with *Arkansas*; both concern the same federal permit.

##### How EPA’s incorporated downstream standards govern this federal permit

**Holding:** For this interstate federal permit, EPA’s valid regulation incorporates the applicable, federally approved downstream standards into federal permitting law, and its reasonable, consistently held construction receives substantial deference. EPA permissibly construed Oklahoma’s no-degradation requirement to prohibit an actual detectable or measurable violation, rather than every infinitesimal theoretical effect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous construction of the standards as used in this federal permit.

**Material application:** The standards were developed with federal guidance, approved by EPA and incorporated through 40 C.F.R. §122.4(d). In this federal permitting setting, the detectable-or-measurable formulation implements the standards without treating every theoretical additional molecule as a downstream veto.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not decide state law for unrelated purposes, excuse unreliable scientific findings, authorize disregard of a demonstrated detectable violation, or create a general exemption for pollution called trivial. General agency-deference doctrine is not revised.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reasonable construction supports sustaining the permit in both dockets under its existing compliance, study and modification conditions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837, applies to this authorized construction. *Bowles v. Seminole Rock & Sand Co.*, 325 U.S. 410, supplies the received regulatory-interpretation principle within valid delegated authority. *INS v. National Center for Immigrants’ Rights, Inc.*, decided December 16, 1991, supports consistent source-specific regulatory construction without importing immigration rules.

##### Whether supported EPA findings sustain this permit despite competing findings and the court of appeals’ policy objection

**Holding:** A reviewing court must accept agency adjudicative findings supported by substantial evidence on the record as a whole and may not replace them merely because competing findings also could be supported. Here EPA’s supported finding that Fayetteville carried the administrative preponderance burden of no actual detectable violation sustains the permit; the court’s contrary policy does not establish arbitrary agency action.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous appellate-review holding and application.

**Material application:** The administrative law judge addressed nutrient enrichment, appearance, dissolved oxygen and metals under the corrected no-detectable-violation standard. The phosphorus-stream error was harmless because the findings addressed the more vulnerable lake; the standards-edition error was harmless because the relevant editions did not materially differ. The lower court’s categorical degradation rule did not make EPA’s action arbitrary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The city’s administrative preponderance burden and the reviewing court’s whole-record substantial-evidence inquiry remain distinct. No new discharge limit, scientific threshold or finding concerning later water quality is supplied.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse in both dockets and sustain the supported permit determination, preserving the existing study and modification conditions as safeguards for further information.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Universal Camera Corp. v. NLRB*, 340 U.S. 474, requires whole-record support for agency findings rather than substitution of merely permissible alternatives. *Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Association v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co.*, 463 U.S. 29, applies to the actual governing problem, not a court-created categorical policy.

For *Arkansas v. Oklahoma*, Nos. 90-1262 and 90-1266, decided February 26, 1992, on EPA’s statutory downstream-permit authority and the absence of a categorical bar for already-degraded waters, see Environmental Law, “Interstate water-pollution permits.”

### Freedom of Information Act

#### United States Department of State v. Ray, 502 U.S. 164 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-747.
**Decided:** December 16, 1991.

##### Whether FOIA Exemption 6 permits withholding returnee identifiers after segregable interview content has been released

**Holding:** Under FOIA Exemption 6, identifying information may be withheld when linking names to sensitive personal interviews would cause a substantial privacy invasion and the identifiers add little demonstrated value to public understanding of agency conduct after reasonably segregable substantive material has been released. A confidentiality assurance informs the privacy interest but does not itself create an exemption.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion; Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter support this operative rule. Scalia and Kennedy do not join Part III's broader discussion of possible derivative public benefits. The operative rule has the support of all eight participants.

**Material application:** Released reports disclosed the interview questions and answers, dates, sampling, and reported treatment. Names would connect sensitive family, employment, and living information to identifiable returnees who cooperated under an assurance of confidentiality. The requesters supplied a general hope of finding inaccuracies through renewed interviews, without concrete contradictory information or a demonstrated incremental public contribution sufficient to outweigh that privacy invasion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not require proof of government wrongdoing before invoking FOIA, decide that derivative investigation can never matter, or adjudicate Haiti's safety or any asylum claim. Waived Exemptions 1 and 7(C) are not reached.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The disclosure judgment is reversed as to identifiers; identifiers remain withheld while the segregated substantive reports remain disclosed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rose supplies the privacy/public-interest balance and disclosure after removal of identifiers. Washington Post supplies the application of “similar files” to information about particular persons. Reporters Committee is applied and preserved.

### Passenger-rail acquisition and agency review

#### National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., 503 U.S. 407 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-1419 and 90-1769.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp.*, March 25, 1992, Nos. 90-1419 and 90-1769: whether the passenger-service condemnation satisfies public use despite private reconveyance (Property and Economic Rights — Public-use condemnation).

##### Whether §562(d) permits passenger-service acquisition followed by private reconveyance

**Holding:** Section 562(d) permits the ICC’s construction that property required for passenger service includes property usefully and appropriately acquired for Amtrak’s rail operations; it does not require indispensable permanent Amtrak ownership. The statutory need presumption and express service-promoting reconveyance authority therefore permit this transaction, subject to the statute’s independent conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Souter; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The independent inability-to-agree condition, passenger-service connection, compensation obligation and conjunctive rebuttal remain. The railroad must establish both significant impairment of common-carrier duties and adequate available alternative property to overcome presumed need. The applicable 1990 amendment expressly permits service-promoting reconveyance in pending cases.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The D.C. Circuit’s judgments in both consolidated dockets are reversed and remanded for resolution of compensation challenges and other review issues not previously decided. The Court sustains the ICC order only on the reached acquisition, agency-ground, prerequisite and public-use issues; it fixes no new valuation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. NRDC, 467 U.S. 837 — applied: permissible agency construction governs the unresolved statutory ambiguity.
- K mart Corp. v. Cartier, Inc., 486 U.S. 281 — applied: statutory text and structure constrain deference.
- Sullivan v. Everhart, 494 U.S. 83 — applied: the meaning of a disputed term is assessed within the whole statute.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, January 27, 1992 — preserved: no prior binding construction is displaced here.

##### Whether an agency interpretation necessarily embodied in its decision supplies its own reviewable ground

**Holding:** A reviewing court may recognize an interpretation necessarily embodied in the agency’s own decision even when the agency did not state that interpretation in the exact words later used by counsel. That rule sustains this ICC order because its application of the need presumption and its disposition of the competing remedies disclose the statutory construction; it does not permit a court to supply a new agency ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Souter; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court may identify the agency’s discernible construction, but may not invent omitted findings or substitute a new rationale supplied by counsel.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The D.C. Circuit’s judgments in both consolidated dockets are reversed and remanded for resolution of compensation challenges and other review issues not previously decided. The Court sustains the ICC order only on the reached acquisition, agency-ground, prerequisite and public-use issues; it fixes no new valuation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- SEC v. Chenery Corp., 318 U.S. 80 — applied: the agency’s own ground must sustain its action; a necessarily discernible premise suffices here.
- Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Assn. v. State Farm, 463 U.S. 29 — distinguished on the record, not weakened: new appellate rationalizations remain insufficient.

##### Whether the record satisfies §562(d)’s inability-to-agree prerequisite

**Holding:** Section 562(d)’s inability-to-agree prerequisite permits the ICC to find from the parties’ positions that further sale negotiations will not produce agreement; it does not impose an unstated mandatory bargaining ritual. The supported finding here satisfies that condition, without making every unilateral offer sufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Souter; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An unaccepted unilateral offer is not invariably sufficient; the agency must find inability to reach agreement from the parties’ actual positions. Presumed need does not dispense with that antecedent finding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The D.C. Circuit’s judgments in both consolidated dockets are reversed and remanded for resolution of compensation challenges and other review issues not previously decided. The Court sustains the ICC order only on the reached acquisition, agency-ground, prerequisite and public-use issues; it fixes no new valuation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. NRDC, 467 U.S. 837 — applied to the ICC’s interpretation of the independent sale-negotiation prerequisite.

##### Whether the railroad rebuts §562(d)’s presumed passenger-service need

**Holding:** To defeat §562(d)’s presumption of need, the railroad must establish both statutory rebuttal conditions. In assessing significant impairment of its common-carrier obligations, the ICC may consider the transaction as a whole, including preserved trackage rights and compensation; the supported finding of no significant impairment independently prevents rebuttal here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Souter; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both significant common-carrier impairment and an adequate available alternative are required to rebut need. Preserved freight rights and compensation are part of the transaction; the correctness of the compensation amount remains open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The D.C. Circuit’s judgments in both consolidated dockets are reversed and remanded for resolution of compensation challenges and other review issues not previously decided. The Court sustains the ICC order only on the reached acquisition, agency-ground, prerequisite and public-use issues; it fixes no new valuation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. NRDC, 467 U.S. 837 — applied: the statute permits consideration of mitigation within the transaction.
- Arkansas v. Oklahoma, February 26, 1992 — consistent with, and unchanged by, review of an agency conclusion against the actual record.

**Independent alternative holding:** The ICC permissibly reads the alternative-property condition of §562(d) to ask whether an adequate alternative route is available on the statutory terms, rather than whether a lesser interest in the same segment would suffice. The absence of such an alternative independently prevents rebuttal of the need presumption here.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Souter; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The alternative inquiry concerns an adequate available route on the statutory terms, not a lesser interest in the same segment; it does not authorize acquisition unrelated to passenger service. Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. NRDC, 467 U.S. 837 — applied: the ICC’s route-based construction is permissible within the statutory scheme.

### Filed rates, counterclaims and primary jurisdiction

#### Reiter v. Cooper, 507 U.S. 258 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1496.

**Decided:** March 8, 1993.

##### The statutory claim may be asserted before payment

**Holding:** A shipper may assert its claim for unreasonable-rate damages under 49 U.S.C.§ 11705(b)(3) as a counterclaim in the carrier's action for undercharges on the same shipments; Rule 8(c) allows correction of its designation as a defense when justice requires. Under§ 11706(g), the claim accrues upon delivery or tender of delivery, so payment of the disputed tariff charge is not a prerequisite; a negotiated rate alone supplies no common-law defense to the filed tariff.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this statutory rule and application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Maislin Industries, U.S., Inc. v. Primary Steel, Inc., 497 U.S. 116: applied; its rejection of the negotiated-rate unreasonable-practice policy remains, alongside the statutory unreasonable-rate remedy.
- United States ex rel. Louisville Cement Co. v. ICC, 246 U.S. 638: its payment-based accrual construction was superseded by Congress's delivery-accrual provision; it cannot establish the proposed prerequisite.
- Southern Construction Co. v. Pickard, 371 U.S. 57: applied for assertion of related counterclaims under ordinary federal procedure.
- Texas & Pacific Railway v. Mugg, 202 U.S. 242; Louisville & Nashville Railroad v. Maxwell, 237 U.S. 94; Pittsburgh, Cincinnati, Chicago & St.Louis Railway v. Fink, 250 U.S. 577: their bars on common-law avoidance of filed tariffs remain; statutory reparations are distinct.
- T.I.M.E. Inc. v. United States, 359 U.S. 464: its rejection of a free-standing unreasonable-rate defense does not eliminate the later express statutory cause of action.

##### Same-transaction recoupment survives the affirmative limitation

**Holding:** The two-year period in§ 11706(c)(2) for an affirmative unreasonable-rate damages action does not bar a shipper from reducing liability in a timely carrier action through recoupment arising from the same shipments. This defensive recovery extends only to the asserted liability; the decision creates no unlimited extension for unrelated claims or affirmative recovery beyond it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices support this independent limitation ruling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bull v. United States, 295 U.S. 247: applied; same-transaction recoupment survives while the principal action is timely.
- United States v. Western Pacific Railroad, 352 U.S. 59: applied for the analogous statutory limitation rationale; the Government's distinct setoff power is not made a condition of private recoupment.

##### Primary jurisdiction does not withdraw judicial jurisdiction

**Holding:** A court hearing the statutory unreasonable-rate claim must allow a reasonable opportunity for the parties to obtain the ICC's determination of rate reasonableness, but that primary-jurisdiction procedure does not deprive the court of jurisdiction. The court may retain the action, ordinarily staying proceedings for that purpose, or dismiss without prejudice only if the parties would not be unfairly disadvantaged; neither prior payment nor a completed ICC ruling is required to file the civil claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this allocation and its qualifications.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Western Pacific Railroad, 352 U.S. 59: applied to allocate the expert rate issue while preserving the judicial claim.
- Mitchell Coal & Coke Co. v. Pennsylvania Railroad, 230 U.S. 247: applied for a stay affording a reasonable opportunity to request an agency ruling.
- Carnation Co. v. Pacific Westbound Conference, 383 U.S. 213: applied to retention or nondisadvantaging dismissal without prejudice.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, 467 U.S. 837: applied to the ICC's longstanding reasonable construction of its reparations authority, without new deference doctrine.
- Myers v. Bethlehem Shipbuilding Corp., 303 U.S. 41, and Heckler v. Ringer, 466 U.S. 602: distinguished; their exhaustion setting does not supply the missing statutory prerequisite here.

##### Separate judgment requires the ordinary discretionary determination

**Holding:** A separate final judgment on the carrier's claim requires Rule 54(b)'s express determination that there is no just reason for delay, considering judicial administration and the actual equities. Prompt suit by a solvent carrier ordinarily favors separate judgment, while actual or threatened insolvency of the party seeking judgment weighs against it without creating an absolute bar; appropriate security and an enforcement stay under Rule 62(h) remain available where other equities justify them.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices support the rule and remand rather than deciding discretion initially.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sears, Roebuck & Co. v. Mackey, 351 U.S. 427, and Cold Metal Process Co. v. United Engineering & Foundry Co., 351 U.S. 445: applied for Rule 54(b)'s separate-judgment authority and safeguards.
- Curtiss-Wright Corp. v. General Electric Co., 446 U.S. 1: applied; actual or threatened insolvency is relevant, not an absolute bar, and protective conditions may address competing risks.
- Crancer v. Lowden, 315 U.S. 631: distinguished; it sustained discretionary collection without an available judicial reparations counterclaim after the shippers chose the separate rail-agency route, rather than requiring collection in every case.
- Keogh v. Chicago & Northwestern Railway, 260 U.S. 156; Arizona Grocery Co. v. Atchison,Topeka & Santa Fe Railway, 284 U.S. 370; Lowden v. Simonds-Shields-Lonsdale Grain Co., 306 U.S. 516: applied for the continuing force of filed rates not disapproved by the ICC.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Referral means allowing a party time to seek an ICC determination, not commanding the agency to answer. Negotiated rates do not supplant filed tariffs. Same-transaction recoupment fixes the estate’s proper claim rather than paying an unrelated preference. No rate, affirmative excess damages or separate-judgment outcome is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for the statutory counterclaims, an appropriate opportunity to obtain the ICC rate ruling and an express Rule 54(b) determination. No automatic carrier priority or insolvency bar applies.

### Freedom of Information Act source confidentiality

#### United States Department of Justice v. Landano, 508 U.S. 165 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2054.
**Decided:** May 24, 1993.

##### Confidentiality requires an assurance or a supported inference, not an FBI label

**Holding:** Under Exemption7(D), a source is confidential when the information was furnished under an express assurance or circumstances supporting a reasonable inference of confidentiality; an assurance of absolute secrecy is unnecessary. The Government bears the burden and cannot presume every FBI criminal-investigation source confidential, but may rely on a supported, rebuttable inference tied to particular circumstances or a properly defined category, including the crime and the source’s relationship to it.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press, 489 U.S.749: distinguished; privacy categories do not establish every source’s confidentiality.
- John Doe Agency v. John Doe Corp., 493 U.S.146: compilation analysis preserved; it does not eliminate the confidentiality requirement.
- Department of State v. Ray, December16,1991: distinct Exemption6 privacy holding preserved.

##### Source identity and criminal-source information receive distinct statutory protection

**Holding:** Exemption7(D) protects identity when law-enforcement disclosure could reasonably be expected to reveal a confidential source, including an agency, authority or private institution furnishing information confidentially; it also protects information furnished by a confidential source in a criminal investigation conducted by a criminal-law-enforcement authority. The statute separately covers a lawful national-security intelligence investigation; reasonably segregable nonexempt material remains subject to release, but redacting identity alone does not remove protection for covered source information.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Public-testimony waiver and other exemptions remain unresolved. In-camera affidavits and protective procedures may be used when justified, but are not universally required. The separate national-security clause is not construed beyond its stated statutory coverage.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand for confidentiality determinations with the Government retaining its burden and the requester able to rebut supported inferences using nonprotected evidence. Release reasonably segregable nonexempt material, not protected source information merely because names are redacted; no blanket disclosure or criminal relief.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- John Doe Agency v. John Doe Corp., 493 U.S.146: statutory compilation requirement applied.
- Department of Air Force v. Rose, 425 U.S.352: nonexempt segregable disclosure preserved, without overriding protected source information.

### Lump-sum appropriations and agency organization

#### Lincoln v. Vigil, 508 U.S. 182 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1833.
**Decided:** May 24, 1993.

##### Allocation of this unearmarked lump sum is committed to agency discretion

**Holding:** When Congress supplies a lump-sum appropriation without a binding program earmark or other meaningful allocation standard, an agency’s distribution among authorized statutory purposes is committed to its discretion under §701(a)(2). Legislative-history references and the general federal trust relationship do not require continued funding of this particular program; enforceable statutory boundaries and the separately presented constitutional claim remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Heckler v. Chaney, 470 U.S.821: discretionary resource-allocation rationale applied beyond the enforcement setting within the statutory limits stated.
- ICC v. Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers, 482 U.S.270: recognized discretionary administrative categories applied.
- Citizens to Preserve Overton Park v. Volpe, 401 U.S.402: distinguished; an actual statutory standard remains enforceable.
- Webster v. Doe, 486 U.S.592: separate constitutional review preserved.

##### This organizational and policy change does not require notice and comment

**Holding:** Even assuming the program’s termination is a rule, this reallocation and organizational policy change falls within §553(b)(A)’s exceptions for agency organization and general policy statements and does not require notice and comment. The ruling concerns a change in service availability, not a new binding eligibility rule, and leaves the separate §552 publication question unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Enacted program limits remain enforceable. The independent constitutional claim, §552 publication question and unreviewed remedial issue are reserved. The decision relies on §553(b)(A), not the benefits exception in §553(a)(2), which the Department had undertaken not to invoke.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand; continued funding cannot be compelled on the rejected APA grounds. No entitlement to a particular hearing or authority to spend outside the statutory mission.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morton v. Ruiz, 415 U.S.199: distinguished; compliance with applicable agency procedures does not create a universal notice-and-comment duty.

### Medicare reasonable-cost adjustments

#### Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, 508 U.S. 402 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2079.
**Decided:** June 7, 1993.

##### Corrective adjustments operate within the authorized cost-method system

**Holding:** Section 1395x(v)(1)(A)(ii) requires suitable retroactive corrective adjustments when, for a provider and fiscal period, aggregate reimbursement produced by the methods proves inadequate or excessive; the Secretary permissibly construes that command as reconciliation of interim reimbursement to the amount payable under lawful reasonable-cost methods and limits. The clause does not require an individualized exception whenever actual costs exceed a ceiling, and authorized exceptions and independent challenges to unlawful methods remain intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S.837: traditional tools followed by permissible construction of remaining ambiguity applied.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S.204: unauthorized retroactive rulemaking distinguished; its holding does not decide this clause’s meaning.
- Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, March 9, 1992: text/coexistence principle preserved; the dispute concerns what these provisions require, not implied repeal.

##### A changed agency interpretation is not automatically disqualified

**Holding:** An agency’s change of statutory interpretation may reduce the weight accorded its view, but change alone does not make a considered, otherwise permissible construction unlawful. The Court must examine the text, delegated setting and reasons; an unsupported litigation position cannot substitute for the legally required agency explanation or obtain automatic controlling weight.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The hospitals concede no applicable regulatory exemption or exception. Independent challenges to unlawful reimbursement methods remain available outside this review; no automatic actual-cost escape or replacement of Chevron is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm; no unconditional individualized reimbursement above otherwise valid ceilings or particular additional payment is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- FEC v. Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee, 454 U.S.27: reasonable agency interpretation within the statutory delegation applied.
- INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca, 480 U.S.421: judicial interpretation and the significance of inconsistent agency positions preserved.
- Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S.204: unsupported litigation rationalizations receive no automatic controlling weight; change itself is a different issue.

### Exhaustion of superior-agency review

#### Darby v. Cisneros, 509 U.S. 137 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2045.
**Decided:** June 21, 1993.

##### Section 704 controls exhaustion of superior-agency review

**Holding:** In an APA action, an otherwise final agency decision is reviewable without an optional superior-agency appeal unless a statute expressly requires exhaustion or an agency rule both requires the appeal and makes the challenged action inoperative pending it. Courts cannot add their own exhaustion requirement beyond those alternatives; remaining APA finality, reviewability and adequate-remedy conditions still apply.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, Section 704 controls exhaustion of superior-agency review portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Otherwise final action remains subject to reviewability and adequate-remedy requirements; express statutory exhaustion and lawful mandatory-and-inoperative agency appeals survive. No universal common-law exhaustion rule is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand without requiring HUD’s optional appeal; resolve remaining review questions, without automatic restoration of a district-court merits result or exoneration from debarment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- 5 U.S.C.§704 — statutory requirement OR mandatory-and-inoperative agency rule; alternatives and conjunction preserved.
- McKart, 395 U.S.185 — exhaustion policy subordinate to enacted conditions.
- McCarthy, March 4, 1992 — express statutory primacy retained; different damages-only setting.
- McNeil, May 17, 1993 — express FTCA exhaustion distinguished.
- Lincoln, May 24, 1993 — distinct reviewability limits preserved.

### Filed tariffs and cancellation

#### [Security Services, Inc. v. Kmart Corp., 511 U.S. 431](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Security_Services_v_Kmart_merits_1994-05-16.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-284.

**Decided:** 1994-05-16.

##### Published cancellation of essential mileage participation defeats these undercharges

**Holding:** A carrier whose filed monetary tariff incorporates another filed tariff for essential mileage information cannot recover undercharges for shipments after its participation has been effectively canceled through the required public tariff filing, absent an effective replacement filing: the asserted rate then lacks a legally operative essential component under 49 CFR §§1312.4(d), 1312.27(e), and 1312.30. Private revocation or nonpayment alone is insufficient—§1312.10(a) leaves existing rates applicable until lawfully revised—and this rule neither erases charges for earlier shipments nor creates a negotiated-rate or bankruptcy exception to an otherwise effective filed rate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, and Kennedy; seven Justices adopt the rule and affirmance. Thomas and Ginsburg separately dissent from the statutory-authority and remedy conclusions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Maislin Industries, U.S., Inc. v. Primary Steel, Inc., 497 U.S. 116 (1990): preserved for applicable filed rates; distinguished because the asserted rate lacks an effective incorporated component.
- ICC v. American Trucking Assns., Inc., 467 U.S. 354 (1984): distinguished; its limits on rejection of effective tariffs do not displace this prospective operation of preexisting filing and participation rules.
- Berwind-White Coal Mining Co. v. Chicago & Erie R.R., 235 U.S. 371 (1914): distinguished; technical defects in an otherwise effective tariff differ from the legally missing mileage component.
- Davis v. Portland Seed Co., 264 U.S. 403 (1924): distinguished; its remedy for an effective tariff's violation does not create a complete rate here.
- Kansas City Southern Ry. v. Carl, 227 U.S. 639 (1913): applied; constructive notice includes the relevant tariff filings and cancellation, not only selected rate pages.
- Reiter v. Cooper, 507 U.S. 258 (1993): preserved for statutory counterclaims, recoupment, and appropriate agency referral; it supplies no holding that this asserted tariff was effective.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Physical ascertainability of the monetary and mileage numbers does not replace legally effective incorporation. No general tariff-nullification power, negotiated-rate exception or special bankruptcy exception is adopted; contract carriage, rate reasonableness and unpresented statutory relief remain separate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Third Circuit and Kmart's summary judgment are affirmed, 7–2. No tariff replacement, undercharge award, calculation, agency referral or bankruptcy distribution is ordered. Unpresented defenses and Negotiated Rates Act relief are not adjudicated; waived or unpreserved theories are not revived.

### Freedom of information and employee privacy

#### [Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority, 510 U.S. 487](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Department_of_Defense_v_FLRA_merits_1994-02-23.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1223.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### The labor duty incorporates the Privacy Act's FOIA exception without alteration

**Holding:** Section 7114(b)(4)'s duty to supply qualifying bargaining data operates only to the extent disclosure is not prohibited by law; when the asserted exception to the Privacy Act is §552a(b)(2), the question is whether FOIA itself requires disclosure. Retained Reporters Committee precedent governs the ordinary third-party public-interest inquiry, so collective-bargaining utility alone does not enlarge the incorporated FOIA balance; the Court does not decide that this precedent's restriction was inevitably compelled by FOIA's original text.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt this statutory coordination and precedent-based explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The labor duty concerns requested data normally maintained by the agency in its regular course of business, reasonably available and necessary for full and proper discussion, understanding and negotiation of subjects within collective bargaining, and not guidance, advice, counsel or training provided for management officials or supervisors relating to collective bargaining. These conditions are not independently disputed or newly adjudicated here. For a record contained in a system of records, §552a(b) bars agency disclosure absent a written request by, or prior written consent of, the individual to whom it pertains, or an applicable enumerated exception. A union request is not the subject individual's request or consent. Routine use under §552a(b)(3) and other exceptions are distinct and undecided; their applicability is not guaranteed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press: retained and applied as statutory precedent for ordinary third-party public-interest analysis and requester neutrality; its original-text justification is not newly endorsed as inevitable, and requester identity can still matter when disclosure is opposed on a privilege ground and the requester is the person protected by that privilege, a situation absent here.
- Department of the Treasury, IRS v. FLRA: applied for respecting enacted limits on labor-agency authority; the uncontested bargaining-necessity determination is not reconsidered.
- Department of State v. Ray: preserved; the incorporated Exemption 6 inquiry remains a concrete balance, not a rule that another statute's useful purpose automatically establishes FOIA-required disclosure.

##### The added home addresses do not justify disclosure on this record

**Holding:** On these requests, official disclosure of employee home addresses would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy under Exemption 6 because the real interest in controlling dissemination of home locations outweighs the absent or negligible demonstrated addition to public understanding after names and work stations were supplied. Exemption 6 permits withholding and FOIA therefore does not require disclosure; the Privacy Act supplies the resulting prohibition against compelling release through the invoked §552a(b)(2) route, without deciding a different demonstrated transparency use, routine use, subject consent or another exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt the same record-specific balance and reversal of both enforcement components.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Exemption 6 applies to personnel, medical and similar files whose disclosure would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy; the requested employee-address records fall within that category. Exemption 7(C) instead concerns information compiled for law-enforcement purposes and disclosures that could reasonably be expected to constitute an unwarranted invasion; that different standard is not used here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Department of State v. Ray: applied for concrete privacy injury and demonstrated incremental public understanding; its confidentiality assurance is not a prerequisite or automatic exemption, and no general bar to derivative benefits or prior-wrongdoing requirement is added.
- Department of the Air Force v. Rose: applied; disclosure remains the general rule, with the enacted Exemption 6 privacy balance preserved.
- Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press: applied to the relevant public interest and continued control over officially disseminated personal information despite scattered public availability; its rap-sheet sensitivity and Exemption 7(C) threshold do not replace the different record or Exemption 6 standard here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Enforcement of both the Gulfport and Lowry disclosure orders is reversed on the asserted §552a(b)(2) ground. No new necessity determination, constitutional privacy remedy or approval of a routine use is ordered; other disclosure routes must meet their own requirements.

### Resource-recovery ash and statutory exclusions

#### [City of Chicago v. Environmental Defense Fund, 511 U.S. 328](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/City_of_Chicago_v_Environmental_Defense_Fund_merits_1994-05-02.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1639.

**Decided:** 1994-05-02.

For the other questions in City of Chicago v. Environmental Defense Fund, 1994-05-02, No. 92-1639, see Environmental Law — The facility exclusion does not exempt generated hazardous ash.

##### Agency reconsideration does not authorize an interpretation beyond the statute

**Holding:** Under Chevron, an agency may choose a permissible construction within a genuine statutory ambiguity, including through a lawful change of view, but cannot extend an exclusion beyond Congress’s resolved statutory limit. **Proposition-level authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion: Scalia, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stevens and O’Connor disagree about the statutory range, not the existence of Congress’s limiting authority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984): applied; Congress’s resolved limit precedes the permissible-agency-construction inquiry.
- Pauley v. BethEnergy Mines, Inc., 501 U.S. 680 (1991): preserved for lawful agency choices in technical statutory context, not read to eliminate statutory boundaries.
- John Hancock: applied consistently with its reservation of general deference questions and rejection of a categorical exemption beyond the enacted terms.

The pre-effective-date validity of the household-only regulation and household waste outside resource-recovery facilities remain open. **Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit judgment is affirmed, 7–2. Exemption-based summary judgment remains unavailable; coverage and any relief require their own predicates. No finding that all ash is hazardous, universal permit duty, disposal technology, injunction, penalty or cleanup allocation is imposed.

### Statutory review channels

#### [Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich, 510 U.S. 200](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Thunder_Basin_Coal_Co_v_Reich_merits_1994-01-19.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-896.

**Decided:** 1994-01-19.

##### This Mine Act dispute must follow the statutory review channel

**Holding:** The Mine Act precludes original district-court review of this anticipatory challenge to miners' representation and posting requirements because Congress's intent to channel the dispute through the independent Commission and then a court of appeals is fairly discernible from the Act's language, structure, and purpose, and these claims can receive meaningful review there. The claim's relation to the statutory enforcement process, relevant agency expertise, and actual availability of judicial review inform that inquiry; they are not three newly mandatory elements governing every administrative statute.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, statutory-channel portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg; all nine participants adopt this proposition at the stated level of generality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Block v. Community Nutrition Institute, 467 U.S. 340 (1984): applied to discern congressional review allocation from the statutory scheme.
- Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136 (1967): distinguished because that scheme preserved supplemental judicial review; the presumption favoring review does not require every forum.
- McNary v. Haitian Refugee Center, Inc., 498 U.S. 479 (1991): distinguished because its collateral systemic challenge lacked meaningful review through individual proceedings; that protection remains.
- Board of Governors v. MCorp Financial, Inc., December 3, 1991: its scheme-specific channel rule supports the analysis, without extending its express bar to the Mine Act by assumption.
- Darby v. Cisneros, June 21, 1993: preserved; limits on judicially added APA exhaustion do not displace Congress's allocation of original review.

##### The available route does not impose an intolerable choice on this record

**Holding:** The operator has not shown that compliance with these representation and posting requirements, or their contest through the Mine Act's review process, imposes a constitutionally intolerable obstacle to meaningful judicial review. This conclusion depends on the actual absence of demonstrated irreversible injury, review before contested penalties become payable, expedited proceedings, and the statute's bounded opportunities for temporary relief; it creates neither immunity from lawful penalties nor a rule that delayed payment alone always makes review adequate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, meaningful-review and due-process portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; seven participants adopt this ground. Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment on a distinct constitutional formulation and are not counted as joining this explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Temporary relief is discretionary, not automatic. Under §815(b)(2), Commission relief from covered orders requires a hearing with an opportunity for every party to be heard, a substantial likelihood of favorable Commission findings, and no adverse effect on miners' health and safety; relief is forbidden for citations under §814(a) or (f). Section 816(a)(2) permits appellate temporary relief only after notice and an opportunity to be heard, a substantial likelihood of success, and protection of miners' health and safety; it excludes orders concerning §817(a) imminent-danger withdrawals and §814(a) or (f) citations. Section 816(c) makes clear that commencing review does not itself stay an order. Thus this decision promises no stay of a posting citation and no suspension of every accruing penalty. The separate availability of contest, expedition, review before collection, and the unproved irreparable-compliance injury supports the present result.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123 (1908): distinguished on practical access; its protection against a penalty structure effectively foreclosing judicial review remains intact.
- McNary v. Haitian Refugee Center, Inc., 498 U.S. 479 (1991): its requirement of meaningful practical review is preserved and satisfied on this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Final agency action, cause of action, ripeness and exhaustion are unnecessary to the preclusion disposition. Commission inability finally to decide a constitutional question does not eliminate appellate review. The representation, posting and labor-law merits remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit's jurisdictional judgment is affirmed; the district-court injunction remains unavailable. The representation/posting challenge must use the authorized Commission and appellate process. No labor-law merits determination, penalty assessment, guaranteed stay or new administrative outcome is ordered.

#### [Dalton v. Specter, 511 U.S. 462](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Dalton_v_Specter_merits_1994-05-23.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-289.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### The approved closure selection cannot be reopened through this implementation challenge

**Holding:** The 1990 Base Closure Act precludes the present APA challenge seeking to stop implementation of an approved indivisible closure selection because of alleged antecedent selection-process defects. Its combined package-approval structure, strict deadlines, temporary Commission, mandatory implementation, congressional review and expressly confined implementation review overcome the presumption of review for this relief; they do not erase every earlier procedural duty or decide distinct constitutional or unauthorized-action claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court is joined by all nine Justices. The statutory-preclusion ground alone commands the Court; a four-Justice concurrence on recommendation finality is not controlling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bowen v. Michigan Academy of Family Physicians, 476 U.S. 667 (1986): applies the strong review presumption, overcome only for this remedy by the combined statutory design.
- Block v. Community Nutrition Institute, 467 U.S. 340 (1984): applies structural evidence of preclusion.
- Franklin v. Massachusetts, June 26, 1992: preserves review of the completed census determination and its reservation of presidential APA status; prescribed transmission does not automatically insulate subordinate action.
- Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich, January 19, 1994: uses scheme-specific channel analysis, distinguishing its meaningful later-review route.
- Lincoln v. Vigil, May 24, 1993: preserves the limits of its particular discretionary allocation; it creates no general military or executive immunity.
- Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer, 343 U.S. 579 (1952): preserves actual constitutional limits without converting every alleged statutory mistake into a constitutional cause of action.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Presidential APA status, the four-Justice recommendation-finality theory, genuine constitutional claims, distinct timely hearing or publication claims and unauthorized additions remain undecided. Section 2905(c) excludes NEPA from presidential and Commission actions and Defense Department actions except property disposal and relocation after selection of the receiving installation but before functions move. In covered processes, the Secretaries may omit the need for selected closure or realignment, the need to transfer to the selected installation, and alternative military installations; omission is permitted, not compelled. Review of a covered NEPA act or failure must be sought within 60 days of that act or failure, not presidential approval or congressional receipt. No such claim is adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Third Circuit and remand for dismissal of the claim seeking to stop implementation because of alleged selection defects. No finding that all procedures were followed, advisory injunction or remedy for reserved claims is entered.

### Administrative burdens of persuasion

#### [Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries, 512 U.S. 267](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Director_Office_of_Workers_Compensation_Programs_v_Greenwich_Collieries_merits_1994-06-20.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-744, including Maher Terminals under Rule 12.2.

**Decided:** 1994-06-20.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Maher Terminals is included under Rule 12.2, covering lifetime-disability and death-benefit claims; Greenwich concerns black-lung benefits.

##### The APA assigns persuasion to the proponent of an order

**Holding:** When APA §556(d) governs and no applicable statutory exception provides otherwise, the proponent of a rule or order bears the burden of persuasion, not merely the obligation to produce evidence. With the preponderance standard governing these benefits proceedings, a claimant cannot prevail on a necessary element left in genuine evidentiary equipoise after complete weighing and application of operative statutory presumptions and lawful defense burdens.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six participating Justices directly adopt this allocation, its application to both proceedings, and the limited treatment of prior precedent; no aggregation of different grounds is necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981): preserves its preponderance standard and uses its explanation of the APA framework; its proof-quantum holding is not recast as an earlier decision squarely settling allocation.
- NLRB v. Transportation Management Corp., 462 U.S. 393, 404 n.7 (1983): disapproves the production-only construction of APA §7(c), now §556(d), to the extent inconsistent with the persuasion allocation; preserves the Wright Line holding requiring the General Counsel to prove unlawful motivation and permitting an employer same-decision affirmative defense.

##### The Longshore Act's evidentiary flexibility does not displace the APA allocation

**Holding:** Longshore Act §19(d), 33 U.S.C. §919(d), requires APA adjudication notwithstanding other provisions of the chapter, and §23(a), 33 U.S.C. §923(a), does not exempt these claims from §556(d)'s persuasion allocation by relaxing evidentiary and technical procedural rules. Section 923(a) separately requires reception of a deceased employee's declarations concerning the injury under investigation and makes them sufficient to establish the injury when corroborated by other evidence; that preserved rule does not establish Santoro's disputed aggravation and hastened death on the claim presented here, and the vacatur and remand for a lawful preponderance determination are affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six adopt the Longshore incorporation holding and apply it to both the lifetime-disability and death-benefit claims in Maher Terminals.

**Material application:** Section 923(a) frees the deputy commissioner or Board from common-law or statutory evidence rules and technical or formal procedure except as provided by the chapter; it permits, rather than commands, a chosen investigative or hearing method best suited to ascertain the parties' rights. Its deceased-declaration reception and corroborated-sufficiency commands are separate. The §20(a) presumption that a claim comes within the Act operates in the absence of substantial evidence to the contrary; the ALJ found it rebutted here, and it is no longer evidence supporting ultimate persuasion. The causal questions concern aggravation and hastened death, not an established finding that the workplace incident originally caused the cancer. Incomplete weighing remains a question for administrative reconsideration and ordinary review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981): applies the preponderance quantum in APA adjudication while retaining the distinction between agency factfinding and appellate review.
- NLRB v. Transportation Management Corp., 462 U.S. 393 (1983): retains genuine affirmative-defense burdens; it does not authorize transfer of claimant persuasion merely because a benefits scheme is remedial.

##### The invoked black-lung regulation does not establish a departure from the APA

**Holding:** The Black Lung Benefits Act's §932(a) incorporates the relevant Longshore adjudicatory provisions subject to its statutory and regulatory qualifications; even assuming it authorizes an appropriate regulatory departure from APA §556(d), §718.3(c)'s statement of a congressional reasonable-doubt policy does not establish that departure. The APA persuasion rule therefore governs the disputed pneumoconiosis and total-disability determinations here, and the Greenwich vacatur and remand are affirmed without deciding the full scope of §932(a)'s regulatory power or adopting an independent ground under §718.403 or Mullins.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the actual-regulation ground, the express reservation of broader delegated power, and the bounded administrative remedy.

**Material application:** Section 932(a) applies after December 31, 1973 during periods when the State's compensation law is not on the Secretary's §931(b) list, to coal-mine operators respecting death or total disability due to pneumoconiosis arising from mine employment, or entitlements under §921(c)(5). It excludes Longshore Act §§1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 37, 38, 41, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 48, 49, 50 and 51. Incorporation is except as otherwise provided in §932(a) or by the Secretary's regulations, and references to the employer refer to fund trustees as the Secretary considers appropriate consistently with 26 U.S.C. §9501(d). Additional provisions the Secretary prescribes in the Federal Register to provide payment to entitled persons may not be inconsistent with the provisions specifically excluded. The cross-reference to §28(h)(1) of the 1984 Longshore amendments excludes that Act's §7 medical-services amendments from black-lung claims. None of those excluded-provision, fund-substitution or medical-services matters is adjudicated here.

Section 718.3(c) expresses the policy of giving claimants the benefit of all reasonable doubt about total or partial disability or death due to pneumoconiosis; it does not expressly prescribe an ultimate equipoise award. Section 718.403 places proof of an alleged fact on the party alleging it except as provided in the subchapter, but its independent effect is not decided. Ondecko's more than ten years of coal-mine employment bears on whether established pneumoconiosis arose from that employment; it does not supply the separate disease and total-disability findings disputed here. An ALJ must not stop weighing at an apparent tie or rename a tie a slight preponderance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court determines no final entitlement, medical fact, benefit amount or award of relief to either employer. It preserves statutory exceptions and presumptions according to their own triggers and effects, does not settle the full regulatory-exception power under §932(a), and does not decide the independent §718.403/Mullins ground. There is no separate alternative holding and no fractured rationale requiring a Marks inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mullins Coal Co. v. Director, OWCP, 484 U.S. 135 (1987): preserves its requirement of actual weighing under its interim-presumption regulation and its reservation of the APA question; the Court does not adopt the Third Circuit's separate use of Mullins and §718.403 as an independent bar.
- Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981): preserves the preponderance standard while the incorporated APA allocation determines the consequence of genuine equipoise.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Third Circuit judgments are affirmed: the Greenwich award and Maher lifetime-disability and death-benefit orders remain vacated for administrative reconsideration. The ALJs must complete weighing under the persuasion rule, operative presumptions and lawful defense burdens. No final medical finding, unconditional denial or restoration of benefits, employer entitlement or dollar amount is ordered.

### Medicare educational-cost redistribution

#### [Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala, 512 U.S. 504](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/Thomas_Jefferson_University_v_Shalala_merits_1994-06-24.md)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-120.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### The redistribution provision reasonably limits newly shifted educational costs

**Holding:** Under the governing rule giving an administering agency's interpretation of its own regulation controlling weight unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with that regulation, the Secretary reasonably construes 42 C.F.R. §413.85(c) to bar increased reimbursement resulting from transferring previously medical-college-borne educational costs to the hospital's patient-care account, even when the underlying clinical education is generally eligible. That construction sustains the challenged denial for the reporting year ending June 30, 1985; it does not establish a universal historic-dollar ceiling, make every newly measured expense ineligible, or decide the separate community-support ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Part I, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Scalia and Souter. Five participating Justices directly adopt this regulatory construction, its application and its limits.

**Material application:** Section 413.85(a)(1) provides that allowable cost may include the net cost of approved educational activities under paragraph (g), apportioned for Medicare utilization under §413.50. Permission to include those costs is not an unconditional payment command. Paragraph (g) deducts tuition revenue and includes trainee stipends, teacher compensation and other direct and indirect educational-activity costs under Medicare cost-finding principles. The recodified paragraph (a)(2) directs payment for approved residency programs in medicine, osteopathy, dentistry and podiatry to §413.86 for cost reporting periods beginning on or after July 1, 1985. That later-period exception does not itself disallow the reporting year ending June 30, 1985, and the Court fixes no later-period payment or base-year amount.

Section 413.17(a), subject to the separate exception in paragraph (d), includes the cost of services, facilities and supplies furnished by organizations related through common ownership or control at the related organization's cost, capped at the price of comparable items that could be purchased elsewhere. It does not independently make an otherwise excluded educational cost allowable. No party's entitlement under paragraph (d) is decided, and its separate exception is not a premise of this holding. Section 1395x(v)(1)(A)'s reasonable-cost system takes direct and indirect costs into account within authorized methods and included-item rules, excludes unnecessary costs in efficient delivery, and bars shifting Medicare costs to non-Medicare patients or the reverse. It does not guarantee payment of every asserted actual expense.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bowles v. Seminole Rock & Sand Co., 325 U.S. 410 (1945): applies controlling weight to a permissible agency interpretation of its own regulation; the plainly-erroneous-or-inconsistent limit remains operative.
- Udall v. Tallman, 380 U.S. 1 (1965): applies the same own-regulation standard; an alternative reasonable reading does not by itself displace the Secretary's permissible construction.
- Gardebring v. Jenkins, 485 U.S. 415 (1988): preserves the limit where an alternative reading is compelled by the regulation's plain language or other indications of the Secretary's intent at promulgation; no such compulsion is established here.
- Martin v. Occupational Safety and Health Review Commission, 499 U.S. 144 (1991): applies its account of reasonable own-regulation interpretation and administrative expertise; its allocation of interpretive authority under the Occupational Safety and Health Act remains specific to that statutory structure.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: preserves reimbursement within lawful cost methods and limitations, including authorized exceptions and independent challenges to unlawful methods; actual expenditure alone does not establish entitlement here.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., June 17, 1994: preserves the requirement to respect operative statutory limits; its mandatory-tariff holding does not invalidate this textually supported regulatory restriction or replace the own-regulation standard.
- Director, Office of Workers' Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries, June 20, 1994: preserves the distinction between delegated authority and its actual exercise; its treatment of a general reasonable-doubt policy does not render every statement of regulatory intent nonoperative or create a Medicare proof burden.
- Ohio State University v. Secretary, Department of Health and Human Services, 996 F.2d 122 (6th Cir. 1993): rejects its contrary construction insofar as it excludes this transfer of otherwise eligible clinical-education costs from the redistribution provision; its separate community-support analysis is not resolved.

##### The actual agency ground survives the asserted inconsistency

**Holding:** The Administrator's expressed redistribution ground and the established history of college-borne costs support this denial; a memorandum's silence about redistribution and an intermediary's prior allowance do not, on this record, establish an authoritative contrary interpretation that defeats it. A considered, otherwise permissible interpretive change is not automatically invalid, but contrary text, genuine inconsistency and the required agency explanation must be examined, and a reviewing court may not substitute a new ground or missing finding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Scalia and Souter. The same five Justices adopt this answer to the consistency and actual-ground objections; it supports application of the redistribution holding rather than furnishing an alternative reimbursement bar.

**Material application:** Letter 78-7 requires that faculty-service costs would be allowable if incurred directly, be allocated consistently among recipient components, comply with related-organization and comparable-price limits, and satisfy the applicable patient-care relation, documentation, reasonable-allocation and reasonable-amount requirements. Its identified additional nonfaculty costs are limited to direct and space costs. It does not permit duplicative additions where the hospital maintains its own corresponding library, administrative structure, office or clerical support. Those conditions survive; the Court does not read silence about redistribution as an unconditional promise of all overhead. The record does not explain the failure to claim reimbursement before 1974, and the Court does not turn that silence into a finding of community support or an estoppel rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court affirms on redistribution alone. It neither endorses nor rejects the separate construction treating all non-Medicare funding as community support; decides no merits of the Administrator's additional university general-and-administrative-cost ruling under §§413.9, 413.85 and Letter 78-7; and orders no reimbursement, penalty, recoupment, reopening of the settled faculty-compensation amounts, or relief on the unadjudicated alternative enforcement claim. The original $2,861,247 disputed difference is not adjudicated as an amount remaining after settlement. Materially different retrospective applications, later §413.86 calculations and entitlement under §413.17(d) remain unresolved. No independently sufficient alternative holding is adopted, and no fractured-rationale synthesis is needed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: applies its rule that considered interpretive change is not automatically disqualifying while preserving scrutiny of text, delegation and reasons; unsupported litigation positions receive no automatic controlling weight.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: applies the requirement of a discernible actual agency rationale; the express redistribution ground satisfies it without a new judicial explanation.
- SEC v. Chenery Corp., 318 U.S. 80 (1943): preserves review on the agency's own ground rather than a substitute invented on review.
- Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Assn. v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co., 463 U.S. 29 (1983): preserves required reasoned agency decisionmaking and the prohibition on appellate rationalizations supplying missing findings.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204 (1988): preserves the rejection of unsupported litigation rationalizations; this decision rests on the Administrator's stated rationale and decides no new retroactive-rulemaking question.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The challenged reimbursement denial is affirmed, 5–4, on redistribution alone. No additional reimbursement or remand is ordered. The separate community-support construction and general-and-administrative-cost ruling are neither independently sustained nor rejected; settled faculty amounts and the unadjudicated alternative enforcement claim remain untouched.

### Tariff-filing modification authority

#### [MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218](https://github.com/Alex-Oss222/scotus-career/blob/main/terms/OT1993/records/MCI_Telecommunications_Corp_v_AT_and_T_merits_1994-06-17.md)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-356 and 93-521.

**Decided:** 1994-06-17.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** United States et al. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co. et al., No. 93-521, is consolidated with No. 93-356; the same rule invalidation applies to both.

##### The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing

**Holding:** Under 47 U.S.C. §203(b)(2), the Commission may, in its discretion and for good cause shown, modify tariff requirements in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions, but may not replace §203(a)'s mandatory filing obligation throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system or lengthen the statutory notice period beyond 120 days. The challenged permissive-filing rule exceeds that delegation and is invalid; Chevron does not require deference to a construction outside this statutory boundary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five of eight participating Justices directly adopt the structural modification ground, its application and its stated limits; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** The November 1992 rule makes filing optional, not forbidden, for nondominant long-distance carriers; AT&T alone remains classified as dominant in this record. A modification can have consequential effects. Courts must examine the obligation changed, the scope of the modification clause and whether the agency adjusts the obligation's operation or removes its essential operation for the regulated class. This inquiry does not turn on a numerical market-share threshold or automatically invalidate a class-wide adjustment. Changes to filing form, contents, timing or place remain possible under the statutory conditions; timing changes cannot extend the 120-day notice ceiling. The Court does not categorically foreclose deferral or waiver in limited circumstances, and decides no particular proposed waiver. Good cause alone cannot authorize replacement of mandatory filing throughout this sector.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether the challenged order independently fails the special-circumstances-or-conditions requirement; no separate alternative ground commands a majority. It prescribes no tariff rates or replacement program, decides no damages or retrospective nonfiling consequences, resolves no general rule for individual nonenforcement decisions, and adopts no independently sufficient ratification or prohibition theory from §§226 or 332.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984): applies the inquiry into statutory meaning before deference; no new exception based on economic importance and no replacement of Chevron is adopted.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: preserves contextual interpretation within an actual delegation and the requirement of a discernible agency ground; the agency's competitive-policy rationale is examined, not replaced by a judicial rationale.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: preserves permissible interpretive change within a statutory system; the defect here is the rule's statutory reach, not the fact that the Commission changed its policy.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, January 27, 1992: preserves the principle that agency expertise operates within binding statutory limits; its distinct nonemployee-access rule is not imported into tariff regulation.
- NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America, May 23, 1994: distinguishes a permissible functional construction preserving the statute's operative distinctions from removal of the filing obligation here; its supervisory-status holding remains intact.
- PUD No. 1 of Jefferson County v. Washington Department of Ecology, May 31, 1994: distinguishes broad conditioning authority grounded in that Act's operative compliance language; no general rule against consequential agency action follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment setting aside the permissive-detariffing rule is affirmed in Nos. 93-356 and 93-521. No tariff rate, replacement program, damages or retrospective monetary consequence of earlier nonfiling is decided, and no new direction is given in the distinct complaint proceeding.
