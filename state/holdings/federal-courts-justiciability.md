## Federal Courts: Justiciability

### Mootness and completed sentences

#### United States v. R. L. C., 503 U.S. 291 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1577.
**Decided:** March 24, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *United States v. R. L. C.*, March 24, 1992, No. 90-1577: whether §5037(c)(1)(b)’s juvenile ceiling includes the applicable adult guidelines maximum (Criminal Procedure — Juvenile sentencing).

##### Whether completion of the reduced juvenile term moots a Government challenge seeking longer detention

**Holding:** Completion of the reduced juvenile detention term does not moot the Government’s challenge when success could restore the longer detention it seeks. The possibility of renewed detention keeps this controversy live.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Souter’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; seven Justices adopt the live-controversy determination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Government seeks restoration of the three-year statutory-maximum approach after the reduced eighteen-month term was served. The threshold ruling neither authorizes renewed detention nor decides the lawful sentencing ceiling.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court reaches the sentencing-ceiling question. Its affirmance on that question leaves the eighteen-month disposition in place and orders no additional detention.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

*United States v. Villamonte-Marquez*, 462 U.S. 579 — applied to preserve a live controversy where a longer detention may be restored.

#### Spencer v. Kemna

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-7171.

**Decided:** 1998-03-03.

##### Custody at filing and a continuing controversy are separate requirements

**Holding:** Section 2254’s custody requirement is satisfied when the petitioner is held under the challenged revocation at filing; later expiration does not retroactively defeat that threshold. Article III nonetheless requires a concrete continuing injury after the challenged sentence expires.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part I: O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela agrees with the custody disposition without this additional ground; Stevens also accepts custody at filing.

**Material application:** Spencer was held under the challenged revocation when he filed. His unrelated later incarceration is not an unexpired consecutive-sentence aggregate; it does not preserve this controversy.

##### The asserted collateral consequences and exceptions do not keep this petition live

**Holding:** After this parole-revocation sentence expires, collateral injury is not presumed merely from the revocation; the petitioner must show an actual continuing consequence redressable in this action. Speculative future parole, sentencing or evidentiary uses, the possibility of recurrence and litigation delay do not supply that showing on this record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part II: Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the continuing-injury rule and mootness result. The seven Associates also adopt the exception and litigation-delay applications; Stone-Zsela agrees in those dispositions without their additional rationales.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision distinguishes conviction challenges and adopts no categorical exclusion of reputational injury. The capable-of-repetition exception requires both duration too short for review and a reasonable expectation that this claimant will again face the action. Delay, possible injury to others and fault or estoppel do not create jurisdiction; expiration by the sentence’s terms establishes no strategic cessation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Dismissal is affirmed as moot alone, without constitutional-merits preclusion. No writ, new hearing, expungement, damages or exoneration is ordered.

##### An unresolved future civil remedy does not create this habeas controversy

**Holding:** Heck and Edwards do not establish an unavoidable favorable-invalidation bar for every former prisoner: their controlling rules expressly reserve that category and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity. The unresolved scope of a possible later civil action does not create a damages claim in this habeas case or dispense with continuing Article III injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part III: O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela agrees in the ancillary disposition without this rationale.

**Current-force modification:** Randell v. Johnson, April 25, 2001, supplies the affirmative civil exception Spencer left undecided: qualifying former prisoners need no invalidation if realistic lawful custody review never existed or was lost beyond their control despite timely pursuit; unexcused loss of a usable opportunity retains the prerequisite. Release or current habeas unavailability alone supplies neither exception nor invalidation. Spencer's custody-at-filing rule, continuing-injury requirement and mootness judgment remain unchanged, and the later civil rule creates no damages claim within its closed habeas action.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reservations for former prisoners and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity remain open. No affirmative §1983 exception or universal favorable-invalidation bar is adopted, and no damages claim is pleaded in this habeas case.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lane is applied for the continuing-interest inquiry while its unchallenged-revocation posture is distinguished; no automatic consequence is inferred for this revocation.
- Lujan and Arizonans preserve an actual personal controversy throughout review. Arizonans’ vacatur remedy does not require clearing a nonexistent intervening merits judgment here.
- Garlotte’s continuous consecutive-sentence aggregate is distinguished from Spencer’s unrelated later custody; custody at filing remains sufficient for the statutory threshold.
- MacArthur preserves supported actual personal information interests without creating a universal reputation-based habeas rule.
- Heck and Edwards retain their bounded invalidation prerequisite and express former-prisoner/realistic-opportunity reservations; no affirmative section 1983 exception or universal bar is added.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Randell v. Johnson, judgment below 227 F.3d 300, April 25, 2001: partial resolution of the expressly reserved civil invalidation question.

#### Suter v. Artist M., 503 U.S. 347 (1992)

**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *Suter v. Artist M.*, March 25, 1992, No. 90-1488: whether §671(a)(15) creates the asserted individual reasonable-efforts right enforceable through §1983 (Civil Rights — Child-welfare statutory enforcement); whether §671(a)(15) creates a separate implied private action (Civil Rights — Child-welfare statutory enforcement); whether the asserted §671(a)(9) reporting claim is enforceable through §1983 (Civil Rights — Child-welfare statutory enforcement); whether §671(a)(9) creates the asserted separate implied action (Civil Rights — Child-welfare statutory enforcement).

##### Whether the separate B.H. consent decree moots or precludes this statutory-enforcement controversy

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, January 15, 1992, decision — its preservation of consent obligations beyond constitutional minima provides analogous support; its Rule 60(b)(5) standard is not applied to modify B.H., and its holding remains unchanged.

### Mootness and election disputes

#### Norman v. Reed, 502 U.S. 279 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-1126 and 90-1435.
**Decided:** January 14, 1992.

##### Whether completion of the election moots the ballot-access dispute

**Holding:** Completion of an election does not moot a ballot-access dispute where election results carry continuing party-qualification consequences and the short election cycle presents a reasonably recurring controversy that otherwise evades review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, and Kennedy; these seven Justices supply a majority of the eight participants.

**Material application:** Several candidates exceeded the five-percent threshold that could alter later party qualification. The challenged restrictions operated on a timetable too short for complete review before voting, and the dispute retained concrete consequences.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not revive the unappealed water-district candidates or order a new election.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Moore v. Ogilvie is applied to election restrictions with continuing legal consequences after ballots are counted.

For the party-name and signature holdings in Norman v. Reed, decided January 14, 1992, Nos. 90-1126 and 90-1435, see Election Law, “Ballot access and political parties.”

### Political questions and constitutional review

#### United States Department of Commerce v. Montana, 503 U.S. 442 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-860.
**Decided:** March 31, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *United States Department of Commerce v. Montana*, March 31, 1992, No. 91-860: whether equal proportions satisfies the presented interstate population-equality challenge (Election Law — Interstate congressional apportionment); whether count i reversal disposes of the distinct automatic-apportionment claim in count ii (Federal Courts — Scope of appellate disposition); whether count i presents a continuing representation injury redressable by review of the allocation formula (Federal Courts — Standing and representation injury).

##### Whether Congress’s equal-proportions apportionment choice is judicially reviewable

**Holding:** Courts may decide whether the equal-proportions method violates the constitutional requirement to apportion Representatives among States according to their respective numbers. Congress's responsibility to choose a method, and the possibility that several methods satisfy the Constitution, do not make this particular constitutional challenge a nonjusticiable political question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine participating Justices adopt this proposition at its stated scope; it controls by an express majority, with no judgment-only or narrower-ground vote.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The possibility that several constitutional methods exist does not foreclose this constitutional challenge. Baker’s political-question limits in other settings remain; Count II is separate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse Count I’s judgment, remove its declaration of invalidity and dissolve its permanent injunction, and direct judgment for the Government on Count I only. Remand Count II without deciding it. The allocation remains operative subject to further lawful order; no seat transfer, new enactment or presidential command is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Baker v. Carr, 369 U.S. 186 (1962) — applies its issue-specific political-question inquiry; political subject matter alone does not foreclose constitutional adjudication.
- United States v. Muñoz-Flores, 495 U.S. 385 (1990) — applies ordinary judicial review of constitutional constraints on Congress; its Origination Clause holding is not extended to decide Count II.

#### Aktepe v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** Eleventh Circuit No. 96-2167; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1998-03-10.

##### The pleaded duties cannot be adjudicated without choosing military training policy

**Holding:** A military setting alone does not make a damages action nonjusticiable. These claims nevertheless cannot proceed because determining the alleged duties concerning drill notification, command terminology, live arming and questioning orders would require choosing among military training, obedience and safety policies without an independently manageable legal standard on the supplied record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer; five of nine participating Justices directly adopt the rule, application and limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No independently fixed operational command is established that separates the pleaded duties from policy selection; this is not a finding that none exists. NATO participation, potential embarrassment and military status alone do not bar review. A materially different claim enforcing an independent manageable duty remains open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Political-question summary judgment is affirmed. The Court decides no negligence, damages, maritime statutory coverage, sovereign immunity or Feres question, and orders no further merits proceedings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Baker v. Carr supplies the claim-specific political-question inquiry; military subject matter alone remains insufficient.
- Nixon v. United States preserves review of independently fixed legal limits while recognizing the confined nonjusticiability of an actually committed procedural choice; its impeachment holding is not military immunity.
- Gilligan v. Morgan, 413 U.S. 1, is applied to the policy-selection concern, while its reservation of specific-injury controversies remains intact and supplies no categorical damages exception.
- Weiss v. United States and Loving v. United States preserve constitutional and statutory review in military matters; their appointment and delegation holdings do not establish the maritime duty proposed here.
- Dalton v. Specter retains scheme-specific statutory preclusion and is not extended to these maritime statutes.
- Richenberg v. Cohen preserves substantial military weight within actual legal constraints, without creating a military-tort immunity. No precedent is overruled.

### Ripeness of future statutory obligations

#### New York v. United States, 505 U.S. 144 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-543, 91-558 and 91-563.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

The consolidated matters are New York, County of Allegany and County of Cortland. For the responsibility-clause, incentive, compulsory-title, Guarantee Clause and severability holdings in *New York v. United States*, June 19, 1992, these three dockets, see Constitutional Structure — Federal incentives and compulsory state administration.

##### Whether the 1996 take-title obligation is ripe for review while it requires present siting decisions

**Holding:** A challenge to a future statutory obligation is ripe where the legal issue is fit for decision and avoiding that obligation requires substantial present action over many years. New York may challenge the 1996 take-title command now because its siting choices must be made now and waste production will continue.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Material application:** Developing a disposal site takes years; avoiding the future consequence requires present preparations, and no party suggests the State's generators will stop producing waste. Waiting would not sharpen the legal question and would require the very preparations challenged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling does not relax concrete-injury requirements, permit review of every distant possibility or revive the abandoned due-process and Eleventh Amendment claims.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The constitutional challenge to the future obligation is heard now; component-specific relief appears at its primary Constitutional Structure location.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136 (1967): applied as the received fitness-and-hardship framework; no standing relaxation follows.
- Pacific Gas & Electric Co. v. State Energy Resources Conservation and Development Commission, 461 U.S. 190 (1983): applied in its presently consequential future-obligation setting, as reproduced in the reviewed report.
- Regional Rail Reorganization Act Cases, 419 U.S. 102 (1974): applied; a future statutory obligation can warrant present review when it already requires consequential action, within the passage reproduced in the reviewed report.

### Standing and original jurisdiction

#### Wyoming v. Oklahoma, 502 U.S. 437 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 112, Original.
**Decided:** January 22, 1992.

For the separate questions whether the coal quota violates the Commerce Clause and whether it can survive solely as a public-utility purchasing directive, see *Wyoming v. Oklahoma*, January 22, 1992, No. 112, Original, under Constitutional Structure — Interstate commerce.

##### Whether Wyoming's severance-tax loss establishes its own Article III injury

**Holding:** A documented loss of severance-tax revenue caused by a mandatory substitution away from the State's taxed coal establishes Wyoming's own Article III injury where the record supports causation and an injunction would remove the challenged purchasing mandate. Redressability does not require a guarantee that every displaced purchase will return, but generalized hopes of future tax growth do not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices support this proposition.

**Material application:** The purchasing record, the extraction tax, and evidence of available production capacity support an inference of lost severance receipts from compelled substitution away from Wyoming coal. The fiscal inference is supported on the summary-judgment record; it is not itself a stipulated fact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary injury, causation and redressability requirements apply. No sovereign exemption or standing based on unrelated future fiscal effects is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Maryland v. Louisiana*, 451 U.S. 725 — applied to a State's demonstrated injury from another State's commercial restriction.
- *Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission*, 432 U.S. 333 — geographic market exclusion is a concrete commercial injury; Wyoming still proves its own fiscal stake.
- *Warth v. Seldin*, 422 U.S. 490, and *Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization*, 426 U.S. 26 — ordinary personal-injury and causation requirements are preserved, not relaxed for States.

##### Whether this interstate fiscal controversy warrants exercise of original jurisdiction

**Holding:** The Court exercises original jurisdiction over this substantial interstate controversy because Wyoming's own fiscal injury and the challenged statewide mandate warrant direct resolution and no equivalent pending private action would fully resolve the State's claim. Exclusive jurisdiction under §1251(a) does not eliminate the Court's established discretion in administering its original docket.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices support this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The possibility of a producer's or utility's suit does not require surrender of this distinct state injury, but the holding does not promise an original forum for every indirect effect on tax receipts. Acceptance of the bill does not bar jurisdictional reconsideration on the developed record.

**Material judgment:** Original jurisdiction is exercised by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter. Scalia and Thomas would dismiss and do not reach the Commerce Clause merits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Massachusetts v. Missouri*, 308 U.S. 1 — applied for seriousness of the sovereign interest and adequacy of other available relief.
- *Texas v. New Mexico*, 462 U.S. 554 — preserved for administration of substantial interstate original controversies.
- *Illinois v. City of Milwaukee*, 406 U.S. 91 — alternative forums remain relevant; no equivalent proceeding resolves this State's injury here.
- *Arizona v. California*, 460 U.S. 605 — an earlier jurisdictional ruling is not an irrevocable bar to reconsidering jurisdiction on the developed record.

### Standing and personal injury

#### Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1424.
**Decided:** June 12, 1992.

##### What evidence of Article III injury, causation and redressability is required at summary judgment

**Holding:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury, a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant rather than independent third parties, and a likelihood that the requested relief will redress the injury. At summary judgment, the plaintiff must supply specific facts by affidavit or other evidence on the challenged elements; favorable reasonable inferences remain available, but allegations cannot substitute for the missing evidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the elements and litigation-stage evidentiary requirements. Stone concurs in the judgment without joining this section.

**Material application:** Earlier survival of the pleading stage does not establish the specific facts needed at summary judgment. The Court resolves this action on insufficient threatened personal injury.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The general redressability element controls. Its application to absent funding agencies and uncertain foreign-project responses appears only in the three-Justice Part III-B and is not controlling. Favorable reasonable inferences remain available; the decision does not replace Rule 56 or the separate APA programmatic-review holding of National Wildlife Federation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The merits declaration, mandatory rulemaking relief and dependent §1540(g)(4) fee award are vacated, and the action is remanded for jurisdictional dismissal. Six Justices support dismissal for lack of standing; seven support reversal, with Stevens relying on a noncontrolling statutory ground. No independent fee-entitlement rule or substitute award is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975): applied for the plaintiff's personal stake and burden to establish standing; its statutory-rights discussion remains subject to concrete injury.
- Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied for the constitutional elements and the limits on generalized judicial supervision.
- Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization, 426 U.S. 26 (1976): applied for fair traceability and likely redress; no new majority application concerning these absent agencies follows.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied for the evidentiary distinction between pleading and summary judgment; its separate APA programmatic-review holding is not enlarged.
- Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317 (1986), and Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242 (1986): applied to require evidence of challenged essential elements while preserving genuine material disputes and reasonable inferences.

##### Whether past visits, indefinite return intentions and general wildlife interests establish imminent personal injury

**Holding:** Aesthetic, recreational and professional interests in observing wildlife can support standing, but these plaintiffs must show threatened interference with their own concrete use. Prior visits and an indefinite intention to return, without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure, do not establish standing for these prospective claims; an ecosystem, animal or professional connection that supplies only general interest in distant species does not cure that defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, controls with five votes. Stone's separate concurrence supplies a sixth vote for the record-specific personal-injury conclusion without a formal opinion join.

**Material application:** The affidavits describe visits to Egyptian and Sri Lankan project areas but no sufficiently definite return undertaking or continuing activity showing imminent exposure. General ecosystem, animal and professional connections supply no missing evidence of these members' own threatened use.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document prerequisite is imposed. Actual use of an affected ecosystem and demonstrated injury transmitted across geographic distance remain undecided. Aesthetic, recreational and professional harm remains cognizable when it affects the claimant's own concrete activity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Jurisdictional dismissal leaves the statute's geographic reach unresolved and does not preclude a later action supported by sufficient standing facts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): applied; aesthetic injury is cognizable, but an organization's special interest cannot replace injury to a member.
- Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission, 432 U.S. 333 (1977): applied to require an injured member for this associational claim; no other associational requirement is changed.
- Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95 (1983): applied for the distinction between past exposure and a present or imminent injury supporting prospective relief.
- Whitmore v. Arkansas, 495 U.S. 149 (1990): applied for actual or imminent rather than conjectural injury.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied to specific affected use; no categorical bar to geographically transmitted injury is established.
- Japan Whaling Association v. American Cetacean Society, 478 U.S. 221 (1986): distinguished because its whale-watching and studying members alleged an adverse effect on their own activities; concrete professional or ecological injury remains possible.

##### Whether ESA citizen-suit permission and an asserted procedural right replace threatened concrete injury

**Holding:** ESA §11(g)'s authorization for any person to sue does not give Article III standing to a plaintiff asserting only the public's common interest in lawful administration. A procedural right may protect a plaintiff's threatened concrete interest without requiring proof that the procedure will certainly change the ultimate agency decision, but the procedural label cannot replace that personal interest; these respondents have not shown the required connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the citizen-suit and concrete-interest rule. Stone does not join this section.

**Material application:** Section 11(g), 16 U.S.C. §1540(g), permits enforcement but identifies no concrete injury to these members independent of the unsupported future use. The public's common interest in lawful administration cannot supply the missing personal connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may recognize concrete factual injuries previously inadequate in law; the outer reach of that power is not settled. Procedural protections tied to a threatened concrete interest remain cognizable without certainty of a changed ultimate decision. Neither §7(a)(2)'s geographic reach nor the binding effect of a revised rule on absent agencies is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The action is dismissed for want of Article III standing; no new consultation rule or cancellation of any foreign project is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fairchild v. Hughes, 258 U.S. 126 (1922): applied to reject a general right to compel lawful administration as a sufficient personal injury.
- Massachusetts v. Mellon, 262 U.S. 447 (1923), and Ex parte Levitt, 302 U.S. 633 (1937): applied for the distinction between direct injury and an undifferentiated public grievance.
- United States v. Richardson, 418 U.S. 166 (1974), and Schlesinger v. Reservists Committee to Stop the War, 418 U.S. 208 (1974): applied to generally shared governmental grievances without distinctive concrete harm.
- Valley Forge Christian College v. Americans United for Separation of Church and State, Inc., 454 U.S. 464 (1982), and Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied; a claimed right to lawful government conduct alone does not satisfy Article III.
- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975), and Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): preserved for Congress's power to recognize injuries and broaden protected interests while retaining the plaintiff's own concrete injury.
- Trafficante v. Metropolitan Life Insurance Co., 409 U.S. 205 (1972), and Hardin v. Kentucky Utilities Co., 390 U.S. 1 (1968): distinguished as statutory recognition of concrete personal interests, not authorization of a wholly abstract grievance.

#### General Motors Corp. v. Tracy

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1232.
**Decided:** February 18, 1997.

For the separate questions in General Motors Corp. v. Tracy, No. 95-1232 decided February 18, 1997, see Constitutional Structure — Discriminatory state taxation (This record does not establish the asserted discrimination); Civil Rights — Rational basis for economic classifications (The economic classification has a rational basis).

##### The assessed purchaser may bring this challenge

**Holding:** A purchaser directly liable for a tax has standing to challenge an exemption that allegedly burdens its interstate purchases. ### Mootness and settlement vacatur

#### U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership, 513 U.S. 18

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-714.
**Decided:** November 8, 1994.

##### Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment

**Holding:** When a judgment has been lawfully brought before the Court for review, subsequent mootness bars adjudication of the underlying merits but does not eliminate the Court’s authority under 28 U.S.C. §2106 to make a just disposition of that judgment and the pending proceeding. Section 2106 permits appropriate relief; it does not require vacatur merely because review has become moot.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Walling v. James V. Reuter, Inc.*, 321 U.S. 671 (1944), applies the distinction between inability to complete merits review and authority to make an appropriate disposition of the judgments before the Court.
- *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), confirms §2106 dispositional authority when appellate merits review becomes unavailable; it does not make every available disposition mandatory.
- *Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America*, 511 U.S. 375 (1994), is distinguished: jurisdiction over a new settlement-enforcement controversy is different from disposition of a judgment already lawfully under review.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither enforces the settlement nor supplies a ruling on bankruptcy priority, plan confirmation or the former automatic-stay dispute. The authority under §2106 to make an equitable disposition of a judgment lawfully before an appellate court differs from authority to reopen a completed judgment after the ordinary review process has ended. This decision does not create a new collateral reopening procedure, displace independently applicable Rule 60 requirements, or make a settlement agreement itself sufficient grounds for vacatur.

##### Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur

**Holding:** A party that voluntarily settles while pursuing review does not ordinarily establish equitable entitlement to vacatur of the judgment it sought to overturn; the movant must show a further sufficient equitable reason. Exceptional settlement circumstances remain possible, but a settlement term calling for vacatur is not itself such a circumstance; Bancorp’s showing establishes none, so vacatur is denied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), preserves equitable relief when review is frustrated by happenstance; its actual refusal to erase an unchallenged judgment does not create automatic vacatur after settlement.
- *Karcher v. May*, 484 U.S. 72 (1987), extends its reason for denying vacatur after voluntary abandonment of review to the voluntary settlement here, while preserving its distinct party-capacity setting.
- *Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp.*, 510 U.S. 27 (1993), remains a threshold decision that left settlement vacatur undecided. Its separate vacatur discussion supplies no earlier controlling rule.
- *Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc.*, 511 U.S. 863 (1994), remains a collateral-order decision; it does not establish a private right to erase a judgment following settlement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mootness caused by circumstances beyond the movant’s choice, including unilateral action of the prevailing party, remains distinct. No categorical ban on exceptional settlement relief or universal rule of collateral estoppel is adopted. No Rule 60(b) relief or remand to seek it is ordered.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacatur is denied and the Supreme Court proceeding dismissed as moot. The Ninth Circuit judgment remains undisturbed without endorsement of its bankruptcy merits; no other order or underlying Chapter 11 proceeding is vacated or dismissed, and no settlement-enforcement or Rule 60(b) remand is ordered.

### Associational standing and statutory enforcement

#### United Food & Commercial Workers Union Local 751 v. Brown Group, Inc., 517 U.S. 544

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-340.
**Decided:** May 13, 1996.

##### Express authority for representative enforcement of WARN monetary liability

**Holding:** Section 2104(a)(5), read with WARN's exclusive-representative definition and employee-liability provisions, authorizes the representative to enforce represented employees' back-pay and benefit entitlements. The provision supplies enforcement authority as well as venue; the union does not become the beneficiary of employee recovery or acquire the local government's distinct penalty claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** Reading “aggrieved” to require every named plaintiff to be personally entitled to payment would deprive the enacted representative language of operative force. Deletion of an earlier union-specific remedy did not delete the authority actually enacted. This is express authority for the pleaded action, rather than a general implied cause of action.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Authorization enlarges no statutory remedy and establishes no violation, employee eligibility, amount, or entitlement to fees. WARN's exclusive remedies do not authorize an injunction against a closing or mass layoff. Statutory coverage, notice requirements and exceptions, employee-specific limits and reductions, defenses, and discretionary fee and good-faith reduction provisions retain their own predicates; none is adjudicated satisfied here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Warth* and *Brock* did not decide Congress's express authorization of this representative damages action. *Varity* and *Meghrig* preserve the distinction between authorized enforcement and additional remedies without transplanting their different statutory relief.

##### Congress's displacement of Hunt's individual-participation condition

**Holding:** *Hunt*'s third condition—that neither the claim nor requested relief require individual-member participation—is prudential. Congress may displace it and has done so through WARN's express representative authorization. Represented employees must nevertheless establish concrete injury, traceability, and redressability with support appropriate to the litigation stage. Individual proof may remain necessary, but its potential necessity no longer categorically bars this authorized action.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the same unanimous Court opinion.

**Material application:** Accepted allegations of employment loss and deficient notice, undisputed representative status, and relief payable to the employees establish the controversy at dismissal. Germaneness is satisfied. Different employment dates, wages, and medical expenses may require individual proof under applicable procedures; pleading sufficiency is not trial proof. Germaneness does not automatically establish adequate representation for binding absent persons.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Without clear congressional authorization, the third condition remains operative. Whether simplified damages could satisfy it without displacement and whether germaneness is itself prudential remain open. The Court prescribes no class certification, joinder, adequacy proceeding, jury arrangement, assignment, payroll-only proof rule, or universal employee-testimony requirement. Representation, duplicate recovery, lawful binding effect, and other procedural protections retain their separate law.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical standing dismissal without prejudice and remand, 9–0. No back pay, benefits, or fees are awarded. Direct organizational injury is unnecessary and undecided; the unpetitioned amendment ruling is untouched, and no new declaratory claim is added. Notice to the International, alleged early layoffs, and notice exceptions remain merits issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Hunt*'s first condition and *Lujan*'s constitutional minimum remain unchanged; the third condition is displaced only within the statutory authorization. *Northeastern Florida* did not classify every *Hunt* condition as constitutional. *Rowland*'s association-capacity ruling is statutory rather than Article III law. *Matsushita* preserves actual adequacy and binding-effect protections without making possible individual issues a categorical bar to filing this action.

##### Compelled participation: injury and ripeness

**Holding:** Absence of an express criminal or statutory penalty does not by itself defeat Article III injury: an actually applicable legal obligation or supported threatened civil enforcement may burden present conduct. **Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part V-B, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition only.

### Expired preliminary relief and necessary review of an entered dismissal

#### Newton County Wildlife Association v. Rogers

**Docket or dockets:** 97-774; Eighth Circuit Nos. 96-1994 and 96-3463.

**Decided:** 1998-09-28.

##### Completion of the requested plans ends this prospective planning request

**Holding:** A request to stop timber sales only until identified river plans exist is moot once those plans have been completed, when no surviving remedy or concrete recurrence is established for that request. On the equities here, the stale appellate merits treatment must be vacated and the request dismissed; the January 14, 1997 district interpretation remains vacated on mootness grounds.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg). Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part I, joined by all nine Justices, supplies direct controlling authority.

**Material application:** The Government reports that the plans were completed and incorporated into adjacent forest plans in October 1996. An order to await that completed work can no longer afford the particular relief requested. The September 30, 1995 deadline was missed, but that historical failure supplies no continuing controversy over whether to stop sales until the plans exist. No surviving damages demand or independent remedy is established for this request.

U.S. Bancorp permits an equitable disposition under §2106 after merits review becomes unavailable; it does not make vacatur automatic. Petitioners neither settled nor abandoned review. The prevailing agency's reported performance exhausted the request before the appellate merits adjudication. Arizonans for Official English permits correction of that stale adjudication without a new merits ruling. These circumstances justify vacatur and dismissal. They also require leaving the January 1997 district interpretation vacated, without adopting the proposition that any interlocutory appeal divests a district court of all related merits authority.

The Court does not excuse the missed deadline, approve the completed plans, decide whether plan preparation was a legal precondition to these sales, or resolve the territorial extent of planning duties. Distinct coordination, adjacency-protection and site-effects obligations are neither extinguished nor newly adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** U.S. Bancorp is applied as an equitable, rather than automatic, judgment-clearing rule; Arizonans for Official English is applied to correction of merits treatment after the particular controversy ended.

##### The final adjudication displaces the former preliminary MBTA interval

**Holding:** When final adjudication has ended the period for which preliminary relief was requested, an appeal seeking protection only during that former interval is moot even though an underlying claim may remain reviewable. The obsolete appellate affirmance of preliminary MBTA relief is vacated and that preliminary appeal dismissed; no old preliminary motion is revived.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg). Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by all nine Justices.

**Material application:** University of Texas v. Camenisch distinguishes temporary preservation of the parties' positions from final adjudication of their rights. The final district-court judgment and April 1, 1998 affirmance ended the pending-trial interval addressed by the July 29, 1996 preliminary order. The Court therefore clears the superseded appellate treatment of that interval rather than deciding whether an injunction should once have issued.

This disposition does not rest on an unsupported finding that every timber operation has finished. Nor does it vacate the later final judgment on other statutory or administrative-record grounds. The actual earlier dismissal of the MBTA claim is a separate component and receives its own review. Any request for future relief on that returned claim must establish a current legal and equitable basis; success in obtaining this limited vacatur supplies none by itself.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Camenisch is applied to the distinct duration and function of preliminary relief. U.S. Bancorp supplies authority for the confined equitable disposition without making vacatur of the independent final adjudications automatic.

##### The entered dismissal was lawfully reviewable with the injunction denial

**Holding:** An injunction appeal permits review of an actually entered and separately appealed dismissal of the same equitable claim when the identical dispositive legal obstacle makes examination of that dismissal necessary to meaningful review and resolving the obstacle requires no additional unresolved factual adjudication. Those conditions are met here; §1254(1) permits review of the MBTA component actually affirmed in the court-of-appeals case without a newly invented appeal or petition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg). Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by all nine Justices. Breyer joins this actual Deckert-based formulation; his earlier judgment-only position in Clinton is not treated as a join to Clinton's rationale.

**Material application:** The injunction was refused because the asserted MBTA claim was legally unavailable. One week later the District Court entered a categorical dismissal on that same obstacle, and plaintiffs appealed both orders. Accepting the obstacle would sustain both; rejecting it without correcting the entered dismissal would leave the same claim legally foreclosed. This identity of the legal issue and the equitable claim supplies the necessary connection.

Deckert permits examination of a legal objection defeating the very equitable claim while excluding unrelated interlocutory matters. Swint rejects review of a separate party's independently answerable liability question and reserves genuinely necessary review; this case involves the same entered legal foreclosure. Clinton v. Jones permits review of an integrated temporal-protection order, supplying a confined analogy rather than a general efficiency exception. Camenisch forbids converting a preliminary estimate into final merits adjudication. Here there is an actual dismissal, not an estimate relabeled as a judgment.

Common facts, efficiency and a merits issue's relevance to likelihood of success are insufficient by themselves. Additional independent legal or factual issues remain outside the proper appeal. Later final-judgment merger does not cure an earlier jurisdictional excess, and the Court does not rely on it to do so. Section 1254(1) also does not impose §1257's state-court finality requirement. The later final judgment is posture evidence, not an expanded grant encompassing all its other statutory rulings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Deckert is applied to the same equitable claim's actual legal dismissal; Swint's separate-liability exclusion is preserved; Clinton's integrated-order principle is applied by bounded analogy; Camenisch's preliminary/final distinction is preserved.

##### The separately dismissed MBTA claim has not been shown moot

**Holding:** Completion of river plans and final adjudication of other statutory claims do not, without loss of all effective relief on the MBTA authorization challenge, moot review of that claim's separate legal dismissal. Review may correct the dismissal on this record, but any affirmative remedy on remand requires a present, independently supported basis and must cease if the claim is actually moot.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg). Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by all nine Justices.

**Material application:** The controversy concerns identified sale authorizations and alleged injury to protected birds; the Forest Service conceded that some nesting birds would be killed. Final rejection of the claim does not itself extinguish the controversy over that rejection. Neither the reported plan completion nor the later final appeal decided the MBTA claim anew or established that no meaningful relief remains.

The December 1997 report that approximately three-fourths of road work and harvesting was complete proves neither completion by September 1998 nor an exact present amount of unfinished work. The Court infers neither continued cutting nor completed harvesting from the absence of a later report. Arizonans requires a controversy throughout review. Bennett preserves the distinct, stage-appropriate requirements of injury, causation and redressability. Ohio Forestry's premature programmatic challenge differs from these concrete sale authorizations; it supplies no blanket immunity from review and no exemption from the remaining conditions.

The Court decides a legal dismissal and orders no current operational relief. A supported showing on remand that no effective remedy remains must receive its own mootness determination. Conversely, uncertainty about precise harvest status cannot itself establish either mootness or entitlement to an injunction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Arizonans and Bennett retain the continuing-case and stage-of-proof requirements. Ohio Forestry is distinguished at its absence of a concrete project authorization; its independent jurisdictional and review conditions remain intact.

Related questions in Newton County Wildlife Association v. Rogers, 1998-09-28, dockets 97-774; Eighth Circuit Nos. 96-1994 and 96-3463, appear under Administrative Law — APA review of Forest Service sale authorizations under the MBTA.

### Moot amended election claims and equitable judgment treatment

#### Kentucky Right to Life, Inc. v. Stengel

**Docket or dockets:** 97-60; Sixth Circuit No. 95-6581.

**Decided:** 1998-09-14.

##### The removed applications no longer present a live constitutional dispute

**Holding:** The obsolete §121.180(6)(a) definition-based reporting and §121.035(2) corporate-employee claims, §121.025 corporate independent/issue-spending claim, §121.190(1) issue-only identification claim, and KRL/Zoeller and independent-spending branches of the §121A.030(4) gubernatorial claim are moot because the amendments removed the challenged applications and no surviving injury or supported recurrence remains. The theoretical power to reenact does not preserve those disputes; a credible threat, continuing enforcement or legally available retrospective relief would require separate analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor). Part I of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices.

**Material application:** The Court holds that the claims must be assessed individually against the law that now operates. The revised permanent-committee definition requires a primary purpose of expressly advocating election or defeat of identified candidates, slates or political parties and regular operation throughout the year; it excludes campaign committees, political issues committees, inaugural committees and party executive committees. Independent expenditures no longer fall within contributions. Section 121.035(3)'s permission for contributions concerning constitutional amendments, ballot public questions and issues of public importance is distinct from its nonprofit independent-expenditure permission, which requires that the nonprofit not derive a substantial portion of its revenue from for-profit corporations. Nothing here decides every nonprofit's eligibility under that condition. The changed text, removed applications and reported enforcement history support mootness, without a surviving damages claim or adverse current application. Arizonans requires a live claim throughout review. The mere possibility of reenactment is insufficient, but an express announcement is not the exclusive way a recurrence concern could be established. No merits conclusion about an obsolete restriction follows from dismissing its challenge.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Arizonans applies the requirement of a continuing controversy and the separation of live relief from dead constitutional questions. City of Mesquite's voluntary-cessation principle remains available on supported recurrence facts; it does not make bare legislative power sufficient or announced reenactment indispensable.

**Holding:** Existing dismissals for want of a controversy remain nonmerits dismissals; underlying merits adjudications of the now-moot applications, including the former issue-advertisement application, are vacated and those claims are to be dismissed as moot. The relief is confined to those applications and does not erase still-live judgments or confer constitutional merits jurisdiction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor). Part II of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins only this limited remedial judgment.

**Material application:** The Court holds that jurisdiction and equitable treatment of a judgment are different questions. Bancorp permits a just disposition under §2106 when ordinary merits review becomes unavailable, while denying any automatic entitlement to vacatur. Here the legislature changed the law as the losing challengers pursued review; they did not settle or voluntarily abandon that review. Leaving obsolete constitutional merits determinations in place would retain decisions that cannot now receive the review sought. Arizonans supports clearing such merits rulings while preserving the nonmerits character of jurisdictional dismissals. Those equities justify the limited cleanup, but supply no reason to disturb the independently reviewed live claims. The Court neither approves the repealed scheme nor treats the original merits rulings as void when entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bancorp supplies equitable §2106 authority and retains its settlement-specific limits. Arizonans applies corrective disposition without merits jurisdiction. Munsingwear supports judgment clearing when review becomes unavailable, subject to the equitable inquiry rather than an automatic rule.

### The preliminary-period authority controversy remains live

#### Grupo Mexicano de Desarrollo, S.A. v. Alliance Bond Fund, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-231.
**Decided:** 1999-06-17.

##### The preliminary-period authority controversy remains live

**Holding:** When a preliminary restraint is challenged as beyond the court's legal power during the period before judgment, later success on the underlying contract and conversion to a permanent injunction do not necessarily extinguish that distinct controversy. Here the asserted preliminary-period injury and existing bond preserve a consequential authority question, while actual bond liability and damages remain undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I is joined by all nine Justices and controls at this limited level of generality.

##### General equity does not supply this unsecured-creditor asset freeze

**Holding:** A federal court may not use general equitable authority, at the request of a general unsecured creditor suing solely for money damages, to freeze a defendant's unencumbered assets before judgment when the creditor has no lien, equitable interest, statutory authorization or other recognized source of prejudgment control. Likely success and threatened inability to collect do not supply the missing power.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices adopt the complete rule, explanation and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not restrict injunctions protecting specific property in dispute, statutory freezes, independently authorized fraudulent-transfer remedies, bankruptcy orders, Rule 64 attachment or postjudgment enforcement. It does not decide bond recovery, create a lien, establish fraud or adjudicate the rights of absent Mexican creditors.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- University of Texas v. Camenisch: applies the distinction between a surviving case and the precise preliminary issue; the distinct legal-power consequence here survives without a present bond-liability adjudication.
- De Beers Consolidated Mines v. United States: applies its limit connecting preliminary relief to recognized authority and the permissible final remedy; it supports rejection of this unsecured-creditor freeze.
- Deckert v. Independence Shares Corp.: distinguishes its equitable rescission setting; its preservation authority remains intact within that setting.
- United States v. First National City Bank: distinguishes its federal-tax and statutory predicates; personal jurisdiction and preservation language do not alone establish private unsecured-creditor power.
- Mertens v. Hewitt Associates: preserves its statutory equitable-category rule; it does not itself govern every private injunction or prohibit all equitable monetary remedies.
- Varity and Peacock: preserve their ERISA rules concerning appropriate individual equity and an independently violated obligation; neither supplies this private-creditor power.
- Department of the Army v. Blue Fox: preserves the distinction between an equitable label and an actual legal entitlement within its sovereign/APA setting.
- West v. Gibson: leaves intact the EEOC's specifically enacted compensation authority; it creates no general private-creditor injunction power.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General federal equity supplies no prejudgment security freeze on the stated unsecured, purely monetary claim without an independent recognized source of control. **Material application:** Specific intended or continuing scholarly activity supports the condition challenge even though no plaintiff sought statutory approval and no denial is established. Website blocking is not recast as a rejected statutory request, and a submission labeled undisputed does not establish all assertions were conceded.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The amended definition governs actual continuing injury and relief. Review of the mandatory condition neither decides a future refusal nor permits merits adjudication on hypothetical jurisdiction. For the substantive rules and remedy, see First Amendment — University scholarship and approval of public equipment use, Urofsky v. Gilmore, February 20, 2001, Fourth Circuit No. 98-1481.

### Standing in districting litigation

#### Sinkfield v. Kelley, 531 U.S. 28

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 00-132 and 00-133.
**Decided:** November 27, 2000.
**Companion or consolidated matters:** The private-intervenor and Alabama-official direct appeals share the same disposition.

##### Personal exposure to the challenged electoral assignment

**Holding:** A districting plaintiff must show personal exposure to the challenged treatment, causation, and redressability. Residence in a majority-white district next to majority-minority districts and evidence that those neighboring districts influenced the map do not, without a plaintiff-specific connection, establish personal racial-assignment injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** The Court's per curiam opinion. No numerical tally or individual joins are publicly disclosed.

**Material application:** The allegations and evidence establish neighboring-design effects but no further connection to these plaintiffs' own racial assignment. They establish no independent dilution, exclusion, unequal voting weight, or inferior membership injury. Direct or circumstantial proof may establish personal exposure; no expert study, numerical racial threshold, or electoral defeat is required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Personal unequal treatment can establish standing even if the constitutional theory later fails. Neither unusual shape nor residence is universally sufficient or universally disqualifying. The design-only merits theory and independently supported claims not presented here receive no decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both judgments are vacated; the seven-district injunction is dissolved and the asserted complaint must be dismissed for lack of standing. This is jurisdictional dismissal, without map certification, a replacement map, election cancellation, or an invented alternative claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Johnson v. Miller, June 29, 1995, supplies the separate personal-exposure rule and permits shape evidence when connected to the claimant. Bush v. Vera, June 13, 1996, preserves the personal-stake requirement apart from substantive entitlement. Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co., June 2, 1997, requires jurisdiction before contested merits adjudication. Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co., May 17, 1999, permits bounded nonmerits sequencing, not assumed Article III injury. Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993, and the substantive districting holdings remain unchanged.

### Recurring election controversies after the challenged election

#### Wood v. Meadows

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 99-1069; district court No. CA-94-47-D; judgment below, 207 F.3d 708. No Supreme Court docket is established.
**Decided:** June 11, 2001.

For the deadline's constitutional validity in Wood v. Meadows, June 11, 2001, Fourth Circuit No. 99-1069, see Election Law — Independent Senate nomination deadlines.

##### The continuing deadline dispute remains fit for decision

**Holding:** Completion of the 1994 election does not end this challenge to the unchanged nomination deadline, where Wood's continuing voter and candidate interests and the documented repeated litigation over prospective enforcement establish a reasonably recurring controversy on an election timetable too short for complete review. The later signature amendment does not moot the surviving deadline question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, directly adopts the threshold rule. Stevens and Ginsburg also reach the merits before disagreeing with the disposition.

**Material application:** The deadline itself remained unchanged, and documented repeated litigation plus Wood's continuing voter and candidate interests established recurrence. The holding does not assume a promised future candidacy from a petition allegation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The claim concerns prospective enforcement, not reopening the completed 1994 election. The separate signature-distribution amendment and contempt proceedings remain outside the accepted question.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Norman v. Reed applies Moore v. Ogilvie to continuing, recurring election disputes that evade full review on the election timetable; the actual continuing interests and repeated litigation satisfy that rule here.

#### Utah v. Evans, 536 U.S. 452

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-714.
**Decided:** 2002-06-20.

For the June 20, 2002 holdings in No. 01-714 on statutory and constitutional permission for residual hot-deck imputation, see Election Law.

##### Completed transmission does not defeat redress of the representation injury

**Holding:** A State has standing to contest a census method that causes an identified loss of a House seat when authoritative relief concerning the responsible census determination would likely correct the allocation through the statutory sequence. Completed transmission and certification do not categorically bar correction of court-determined legal error in the count.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion of the Court is adopted by all nine Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Likely redress rests on correction through the enacted allocation sequence, not a coercive presidential injunction or a general presumption that absent persons obey judicial advice. The President's APA status and susceptibility to coercive relief remain reserved; Utah need not prevail on illegality to establish standing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Franklin v. Massachusetts: applied to likely redress through the allocation sequence. Wisconsin v. City of New York: applied to continuing representational injury. Department of Commerce v. United States House of Representatives: applied to plaintiff-specific injury rather than statutory authorization alone.
