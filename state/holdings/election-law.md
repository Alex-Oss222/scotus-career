## Election Law

### Ballot access and political parties

#### Norman v. Reed, 502 U.S. 279 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-1126 and 90-1435.
**Decided:** January 14, 1992.

##### Whether Illinois may prohibit authorized party-name expansion or invalidate qualified slate components for another district's signature deficiency

**Holding:** Illinois may require meaningful support within each separate electorate, but it may not categorically prevent authorized candidates from expanding an existing party under its name or disqualify otherwise qualified countywide and city candidates solely because candidates in a different district lack sufficient signatures.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, and Kennedy; these seven Justices supply a majority of the eight participants.

**Material application:** Protection against unauthorized appropriation of a party name did not justify a geographic bar that also prohibited authorized expansion. The suburban candidates' insufficient support justified their exclusion, but did not justify disqualifying countywide and city candidates who met their own constituencies' requirements.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Other district structures, cumulative burdens, and actual unauthorized name use remain open. The unaddressed state-law judicial-candidate objection is left for authoritative state-court construction; the unappealed water-district candidates are not revived, and no new election is ordered.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded. The suburban exclusion is retained; name-based and whole-slate exclusions are reversed. The unresolved judicial-candidate objection is returned to the Illinois Supreme Court.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Anderson supplies examination of the burden, the State's precise interests, and the restriction's necessity. Socialist Workers Party rejects an inadequately justified local signature burden more onerous than the statewide route. Jenness is applied and preserved. Bacchus Imports supports returning the consequential unresolved state-law issue for authoritative construction.

For the election-mootness holding in Norman v. Reed, decided January 14, 1992, Nos. 90-1126 and 90-1435, see Federal Courts, “Mootness and election disputes.”

#### Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-535.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

##### What level of constitutional scrutiny applies to a restriction on voting or candidate access

**Holding:** A court must weigh the character and magnitude of the injury to First and Fourteenth Amendment rights against the State's precise asserted interests and the extent to which they require the burden. Severe restrictions must be narrowly drawn to advance a compelling interest; reasonable, nondiscriminatory restrictions ordinarily are justified by important regulatory interests, so strict scrutiny does not govern every election rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices control the complete rule. Kennedy's dissent, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, and Stone's dissent also accept the calibrated framework without making their contrary application controlling.

**Material application:** A write-in prohibition must be examined with the available routes to ballot access and its role in the selection system. Neither the prohibition alone nor the general need for election administration decides the burden.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary administration remains subject to meaningful scrutiny. The holding neither imposes strict scrutiny on all ballot regulations nor extends *Burson*'s public-sidewalk campaign-content holding to the official ballot.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, supplies the burden, precise-interest and necessity inquiry. *Norman v. Reed*, January 14, 1992, preserves actual access review and permissible electorate-specific support requirements, including its protection against cross-component exclusion. *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, recognizes substantial regulation of candidate selection while preserving review of severe barriers. *Burson v. Freeman*, May 26, 1992, concerns ordinary public sidewalks; neither its strict-scrutiny proposition nor its fractured judgment changes this ballot analysis.

##### Whether Hawaii's complete write-in prohibition is valid within the ballot-access system reviewed

**Holding:** Where the complete ballot-access system imposes only reasonable burdens, a write-in prohibition is presumptively valid and may be sustained by proportionate state interests. Hawaii's party and nonpartisan routes provide adequate access, and its prohibition validly supports primary finality, prevention of sore-loser candidacies and party raiding, and its treatment of uncontested primaries.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support both the rationale and affirmance. Stone, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy disagree with this application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the complete access system reviewed and its reasonable burdens.

**Material application:** New parties need one percent of registered-voter signatures 150 days before the primary; qualifying established parties retain access. Candidates need 15 signatures for legislative or county offices, or 25 for statewide or federal offices, 60 days before the primary. Nonpartisan candidates advance with ten percent of the primary vote or the votes sufficient to nominate a partisan candidate, whichever is lower; voters choose one party or nonpartisan primary ballot for all offices. These combined routes and restrictions support the limited-burden finding. Lawful selection deadlines need not accommodate every later preference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both primary and general-election applications within this system are reached. The Court does not independently validate every surrounding access rule or decide a prohibition within materially more restrictive access rules. Candidate qualifications and reasonable write-in procedures elsewhere remain open; no absolute write-in right or guarantee of competitive races is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit; its reversal of the district court's injunction remains effective. No prospective injunction or replacement election-administration scheme issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Jenness v. Fortson*, 403 U.S. 431, permits reasonable demonstrations of support. *Munro v. Socialist Workers Party*, 479 U.S. 189, permits primary winnowing and preventive regulation without proof of a particular past disruption. *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, supports primary finality and restrictions against circumvention. *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, governs this limited-burden application; the decision creates no exception to its inquiry. *Norman v. Reed*, January 14, 1992, retains protection against unjustified party-name and cross-component disqualification.

##### Whether the Constitution requires the State to count and publish fictional candidates or arbitrary protest messages

**Holding:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments do not require a State to count and publicize write-in votes for fictional figures or arbitrary protest messages as an independent form of expression. Elections select public officials, and a desire to use the ballot for such messages does not create an entitlement to a write-in option.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas, controls. Kennedy's dissent, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, and Stone's dissent expressly agree on rejection of this broader claim; all nine support the component without issuing a joint opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A genuine interest in supporting an actual alternative candidate requires the contextual access analysis separately stated above. Political expression outside the ballot and every restriction on eligible candidates in a different system are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reject the arbitrary-message tally claim; it supplies no independent ground for relief from the prohibition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, supplies the office-selecting function of elections; the ballot is not a general expressive forum. *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, protects electoral participation without requiring an arbitrary-message tally.

#### Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1608.
**Decided:** 1997-04-28.

For the separate questions in Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party, 1997-04-28, see Federal Courts — Mootness and conditional reenactment.

##### This limitation is justified within the complete nomination system

**Holding:** Minnesota may prevent this otherwise qualified minor party from placing its consenting nominee on the ballot under its name when he also seeks or holds the other major party's nomination, where the complete system leaves the candidate available and the party able to nominate other candidates, endorse and campaign. In this application the burden is less than severe and proportionate interests in ballot integrity, defined nomination choices and a stable electoral system justify the restriction under Anderson and Burdick.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The challenged 1994 §204B.06 subdivision 1(b) barred an affidavit when another affidavit was on file for any office at the same primary or next general election. Section 204B.04 subdivision 2 barred petition nomination for the same office by a primary candidate, subject to two distinct exceptions. Under §204D.10 subdivision 2, if any major-party primary candidate received ten percent of the average vote for the party's state officers at the last state general election in the relevant district, its highest-vote candidates became nominees; if none met that threshold, none were nominated and all could seek petition nomination under §§204B.07–.09. The relevant state officers were governor, lieutenant governor, secretary of state, state auditor, state treasurer and attorney general. The §204B.13 subdivision 4 nonpartisan-vacancy exception allowed a petition within one week of the vacancy and no later than four calendar days before election; an eligible voter could sign despite voting in the primary or signing another petition. Neither exception applied to Dawkins. The distinct ban on nomination by two major parties is not decided.

The conditional replacement permitted a major-party and one-or-more-minor-party nomination for the same partisan office with written consent of the parties' state chairs; petition nomination also required the candidate's written consent. Losing the major-party primary forfeited the simultaneous minor-party nominations, and a party candidate could not also file as an independent. It retained the nonpartisan-vacancy exception, barred a defeated major-party primary candidate except to fill a §204B.13 vacancy, and repealed the old primary-turnout provision. Its affidavit rule referred to any other office, required petition acceptance and the consent forms, and contained a distinct dual-office exception for a soil-and-water district supervisor outside Anoka, Hennepin, Ramsey and Washington counties and a mayor/council or town-supervisor office in a city or town of no more than 2,500 people wholly or partly within the district. That different-office exception is not fusion. A multiply nominated candidate's name appeared once with party names; major/minor nomination used the major-party position and multiple-minor nomination the first filing party's position. Votes for multiply nominated candidates were excluded from the specified party-status qualification or retention counts. These features neither establish compliance in this case nor receive independent constitutional approval.

The Court recognizes no right to duplicate votes, separate party vote totals or a particular ballot design and adjudicates no independent challenge to consent, counting, format, support or deadline rules.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Anderson and Burdick retain contextual burden, precise interest and fit review. Norman's authorized expansion and independent-candidate protections and Colorado Republican's independent campaign speech remain distinct. Tashjian and Eu protect association without compelling every ballot mechanism; Storer, Jenness and Munro permit reasonable preventive selection rules without proof of past disruption. No voter-incapacity premise, incumbent-protection rationale, pure rational-basis rule or universal anti-fusion approval is adopted.

##### Reversal has the enacted transition consequence, without a retrospective election ruling

**Holding:** Chapter 419 §10 makes its amendments effective for the 1996 state primary and thereafter, suspends them while the McKenna judgment is stayed or its mandate recalled, and provides that reversal causes the amendments to expire and prior law to revive. This reversal engages that expiration-and-revival condition; the remand must respect it without assuming an unproved stay, recall or continuous intervening statutory regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Parts I and III, joined by all seven other Associates; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees to reach the merits without an additional statutory rationale.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand, 5–4, subject to expiration of the amendments and revival of prior law. The completed 1994 election is not reopened, and no intervening filing is adjudicated.

### Interstate congressional apportionment

#### United States Department of Commerce v. Montana, 503 U.S. 442 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-860.
**Decided:** March 31, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *United States Department of Commerce v. Montana*, March 31, 1992, No. 91-860: whether congress’s equal-proportions apportionment choice is judicially reviewable (Federal Courts — Political questions and constitutional review); whether count i reversal disposes of the distinct automatic-apportionment claim in count ii (Federal Courts — Scope of appellate disposition); whether count i presents a continuing representation injury redressable by review of the allocation formula (Federal Courts — Standing and representation injury).

##### Whether equal proportions satisfies the presented interstate population-equality challenge

**Holding:** In allocating the statutorily fixed House among States using whole Representatives and respecting each State's constitutional minimum, Article I, §2 and the Fourteenth Amendment permit the equal-proportions method's population-based choice of relative representational equality. An alternative's improvement in selected absolute population disparities does not establish that equal proportions violates the constitutional population command when the change worsens other legitimate measures of equality; the Court neither requires equal proportions uniquely nor approves every population-related formula.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine participating Justices adopt this proposition at its stated scope; it controls by an express majority, with no judgment-only or narrower-ground vote.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The House’s size of 435 is statutory, not constitutional. Wesberry and Karcher’s intrastate equality and justification rules remain; no percentage tolerance, new burden sequence, uniquely required method, or approval of every population-based method is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse Count I’s judgment, remove its declaration of invalidity and dissolve its permanent injunction, and direct judgment for the Government on Count I only. Remand Count II without deciding it. The allocation remains operative subject to further lawful order; no seat transfer, new enactment or presidential command is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wesberry, 376 U.S. 1 — preserves its intrastate equal-vote holding while distinguishing allocation of indivisible seats among States; its historical explanation does not select Montana's interstate metric.
- Karcher v. Daggett, 462 U.S. 725 (1983) — distinguishes its intrastate line-drawing problem; its good-faith and specific-justification requirements remain unchanged, with no numerical safe harbor imported here.

#### Franklin v. Massachusetts, 505 U.S. 788 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1502.
**Decided:** June 26, 1992.

For standing and APA review in Franklin v. Massachusetts, June 26, 1992, No. 91-1502, see Federal Courts — Standing and representation injury and Administrative Law — Census determinations and judicial review.

##### Temporarily absent residents may be included in the constitutional count

**Holding:** The constitutional command to count persons in each State permits inclusion of federal personnel temporarily overseas whose enduring ties support residence in a particular State. It does not require a literal census-day physical presence rule, and it does not authorize attribution to a State without a supportable residence connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine adopt the constitutional inclusion rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule requires a supportable residence connection; it does not mandate inclusion of every person abroad or relax intrastate district equality. Different connections and methodological records remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The District Court's judgment is reversed and its corrective relief dissolved. No replacement allocation, further factual proceedings or injunction against the President is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States Department of Commerce v. Montana, March 31, 1992, this Court: applied for the constitutional structure of interstate apportionment and the distinction from intrastate districting; no new tolerance for unequal districts is adopted.
- Wesberry v. Sanders, 376 U.S. 1 (1964): distinguished as an intrastate congressional district-equality rule; its equal-population command remains effective in its own setting.
- Karcher v. Daggett, 462 U.S. 725 (1983): distinguished on the same interstate/intrastate ground; the present residence decision does not excuse avoidable inequality among a State's districts.

### Minority opportunity and population equality

#### Voinovich v. Quilter, 507 U.S. 146 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1618.

**Decided:** March 2, 1993.

##### Section 2 does not make a State prove remedial necessity before choosing majority-minority districts

**Holding:** Section 2 does not prohibit a State from choosing majority-minority districts merely because no federal violation compels their creation; challengers bear at least the initial burden of showing, under the totality of circumstances, unequal electoral opportunity caused by the challenged arrangement. A federal court may order such districts only when their creation is necessary to remedy an established federal-law violation, but that limit does not become a categorical restriction on state redistricting authority; independently applicable federal constitutional and statutory requirements remain enforceable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part II.B, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine adopt the complete proposition; it directly controls the rejection of the lower court's statutory prohibition and burden allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30 (1986): followed for the challengers' burden and the totality-of-circumstances inquiry; no district form is automatically unlawful under Section 2.
- Growe v. Emison, decision of February 23, 1993: applied to distinguish the State's primary districting authority from federal judicial remedial authority; its actual majority holdings remain controlling.

##### The assumed influence-dilution claim fails for lack of the required majority bloc voting

**Holding:** Assuming that Section 2 reaches the asserted loss of minority influence districts, the claim fails because challengers did not establish sufficiently cohesive white majority voting usually to defeat the minority's preferred candidates. The Gingles prerequisites apply with attention to the claim's nature: an influence claim would require modification or elimination of the first prerequisite that the minority could constitute a district majority, but the Court need not select that adaptation or decide whether influence claims are cognizable because the independently necessary bloc-voting showing is absent.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Parts II.A and II.C, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine adopt this independently dispositive rejection of the presented Section 2 claim and each stated reservation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30 (1986): applied to the necessity of majority bloc voting and the causal mechanism of dilution; its reservations about influence claims remain unresolved.
- Growe v. Emison, decision of February 23, 1993: followed for application of the threshold framework to single-member district challenges, with its influence-claim reservation preserved rather than silently decided.

##### The particular finding of intentional discrimination is clearly erroneous

**Holding:** Assuming that the Fifteenth Amendment reaches the asserted vote-dilution claim, the finding that Ohio intentionally injured black voters cannot stand on this record: attention to perceived federal-law requirements and possession of an opponent's speculative documents, without evidence of reliance, do not establish the prohibited purpose the District Court inferred. The Court reverses that finding as clearly erroneous without deciding the Amendment's coverage of dilution or its relationship to race-conscious districting generally.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine adopt this record-specific constitutional disposition; none adopts a general immunity for political objectives or racial awareness.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Beer v. United States, 425 U.S. 130 (1976): followed in leaving unresolved whether the Fifteenth Amendment reaches vote-dilution claims; the assumption made here creates no new coverage holding.
- Mobile v. Bolden, 446 U.S. 55 (1980): its plurality and separate writings are background to the alleged intentional-discrimination claim, not treated as a single majority rationale or a new categorical rule governing every race-conscious map.

##### Population deviations above ten percent require a genuine justification inquiry

**Holding:** A state legislative plan whose maximum total population deviation exceeds ten percent establishes a prima facie case requiring the State to justify the disparity; crossing that threshold does not make preservation of county boundaries legally unavailable as a justification. The District Court must determine whether the actual plan reasonably advances that rational state policy, including its substantiated connection to the deviations and consistency of implementation, and whether the resulting disparities nevertheless exceed constitutional limits; the Court does not decide that Ohio has met its burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine adopt this standard and the population-only remand; neither the State's success nor a particular remedial map receives a controlling vote.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brown v. Thomson, 462 U.S. 835 (1983): followed for the prima facie threshold, the required connection to a rational state policy, and the separate constitutional limit; departures below ten percent remain generally minor, not categorically immune.
- Mahan v. Howell, 410 U.S. 315 (1973): followed in allowing a substantiated political-subdivision policy to justify departures above ten percent; it does not automatically validate Ohio's particular departures.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State remains subject to independent federal constitutional and statutory limits. Influence-claim cognizability and adaptation of the first Gingles prerequisite, Fifteenth Amendment dilution coverage and the distinct unpresented racial-classification claim remain unresolved. Neither political aims nor racial awareness receives categorical immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand only the population-equality claim for substantiated county-preservation justification and independent constitutional limits. The statutory and intent grounds do not return for retrial; no map or election date is ordered.

### State redistricting priority and single-member dilution

#### Growe v. Emison, 507 U.S. 25 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1420.

**Decided:** February 23, 1993.

##### Timely state redistricting includes the State's courts

**Holding:** When a State's legislature or judiciary is timely undertaking redistricting, a federal court must defer its own redistricting action unless the record shows that the State will not produce a lawful plan in time for the election. The federal court may set a reasonable deadline and retain jurisdiction as a safeguard, but may neither obstruct timely state work nor displace it merely because it prefers another plan; completion of every state appeal before the election is not required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion on state priority, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine adopt this rule. The separate eight-Justice dismissal direction concerns this completed record, not the general availability of retained jurisdiction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Scott v. Germano, 381 U.S. 407 (1965): applied to both legislative and congressional redistricting through state courts; its timely-action condition and reserve federal jurisdiction remain.
- Reynolds v. Sims, 377 U.S. 533 (1964): applied for primary state responsibility and the obligation to remedy unconstitutional apportionment; population-equality requirements are unchanged.

##### A superseding state judgment must receive legal effect

**Holding:** Once a state court's final judgment replaces the challenged legislative plan, the federal court must give the replacement legal effect rather than treat it as one optional proposal. A challenge to the old plan becomes moot unless it also reaches the superseding plan; allegations against the old plan alone do not establish a violation in the replacement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion on the live-plan issue, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine adopt the stated rule. No party-preclusion rule beyond that necessary legal effect is announced.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Atlantic Coast Line Railroad Co. v. Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers, 398 U.S. 281 (1970): applied for respect for state judgments and the federal-state judicial relationship; no enlarged preclusion rule adopted.
- Wise v. Lipscomb, 437 U.S. 535 (1978): applied for the need to evaluate the legally operative replacement before federal remedial displacement.

##### Fragmentation claims require proof of the causal voting conditions

**Holding:** A §2 claim that single-member districts fragment a minority group's potential electoral majority requires proof that the group is sufficiently large and geographically compact to form a majority in a single-member district, is politically cohesive, and faces majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat its preferred candidate, absent special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate. Without the required cohesion and majority-bloc evidence, the court may not order a minority district by adding distinct groups' populations; the Court assumes without deciding that a supported coalition claim is permissible and leaves influence-only claims and the population denominator unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's statutory opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine adopt the stated single-member extension and record application. No fractured-opinion or Marks inference is required.

**Current-force modification:** Valdespino v. Alamo Heights Independent School District, June 17, 1999, modifies the exclusivity of the district-majority condition for one protected group. The group may instead prove a lawful, geographically compact district in which cohesive voting and dependable crossover support give it actual ability to elect its candidates of choice. The numerical-majority route survives; speculation, isolated electoral success, near-majority arithmetic and influence alone do not establish the alternative. Cohesion, the relevant majority-bloc showing with its special-circumstances qualification, causation, independent totality and constitutional limits remain necessary. The distinct-group coalition and influence-only reservations are not resolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30 (1986): extended from multimember dilution to the single-member fragmentation claim at issue; its causal prerequisites and totality inquiry remain distinct, and its influence-only reservation remains open.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Gingles prerequisites are necessary, not sufficient; the totality inquiry remains. No proportional representation, mandatory statistical method, filing-date priority or enlarged party-preclusion rule is adopted. Coalition permissibility is assumed; influence claims and population denominator remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the federal redistricting judgment. Eight Justices—White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas—direct dismissal of this action; Stone-Zsela supports reversal but would allow conditional retention. That case-specific mandate does not abolish the unanimous general rule permitting reserve jurisdiction.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Valdespino v. Alamo Heights Independent School District, lower docket 98-50227, June 17, 1999: first-condition functional ability-to-elect alternative for a single protected group.

### Territorial racial design and constitutional deprivation

#### Shaw v. Reno, 509 U.S. 630 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-357.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993, No. 92-357, see Federal Courts — Review of federal preclearance determinations.

##### Territorial racial design alone does not state the asserted deprivation

**Holding:** Alleged deliberate or predominant consideration of race in drawing territorial constituencies, even with departures from ordinary districting criteria, does not by itself state this equal-protection claim where no racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership or other independently cognizable constitutional deprivation is alleged. This is a substantive disposition of the design-only theory, not heightened pleading, a universal requirement of additional downstream loss, or an added constitutional-injury prerequisite for an independent statutory claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary notice pleading applies; no expert proof or replacement map is required at pleading. Independent exclusion, dilution, unequal participation, civic-status and statutory claims retain their own elements. No statewide racial-strength defense, general replacement of Croson, predominance test or added constitutional-injury gate for §2 is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the design-only state claim (5–4); no injunction, new map or trial on that theory. Other properly presented claims and any properly sought amendment remain subject to ordinary procedure and preclusion. Leave federal-official dismissal undisturbed (9–0) on its separate channel and nonreviewability grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United Jewish Organizations v. Carey, 430 U.S. 144: its fractured reasoning supports a limited distinction, not a comprehensive controlling test or a statewide-white-representation defense.
- Wright v. Rockefeller, 376 U.S. 52: distinguished; racial purpose was not established there, so it did not decide the present assumed-purpose question.
- City of Richmond v. Croson, 488 U.S. 469, and Shelley v. Kraemer, 334 U.S. 1: personal equality retained; the Court declines to equate territorial design alone with Croson’s racial allocation of opportunities.
- Anderson v. Martin, 375 U.S. 399: official racial ballot labeling remains prohibited without proof that an election winner changed.
- Gomillion v. Lightfoot, 364 U.S. 339: racial municipal disenfranchisement remains prohibited; Whittaker’s broader concurrence is not adopted as a rule covering every racial district adjustment.
- White v. Regester, 412 U.S. 755, and McLaurin v. Oklahoma State Regents, 339 U.S. 637: impaired participation may be unconstitutional despite formal access; no universal downstream-loss requirement.
- Conley v. Gibson, 355 U.S. 41; Leatherman, March 3, 1993: ordinary notice pleading retained, no demand for expert proof or a replacement map.
- Growe and Voinovich: statutory prerequisites, state primacy and unresolved coalition/influence questions remain distinct; no additional constitutional-injury gate imposed on § 2.

#### Johnson v. Miller (Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-631; 94-797; 94-929.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** The appellate alignment is Johnson v. Miller; the three docket aliases identify one constitutional challenge and one reviewed judgment for Georgia.

For the other questions in Johnson v. Miller (Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson), decided June 29, 1995, Nos. 94-631; 94-797; 94-929. see Federal Courts — Standing and representation injury (Personal exposure and standing are distinct from success on the merits); Federal Courts — Direct review of three-judge merits judgments (The merits denial of an injunction is directly reviewable).

##### Racial predominance alone does not establish the asserted constitutional violation

**Holding:** Predominant racial consideration in drawing a common territorial constituency, even when ordinary districting criteria are subordinated, does not by itself establish the asserted equal-protection deprivation or trigger strict scrutiny. An independently cognizable deprivation remains governed by its own elements, burdens, justification requirements and remedy; it need not arise from a second governmental act or produce a later loss.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five votes, applying Shaw.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual exclusion, intentional dilution, unequal participation, inferior civic status and other recognized wrongs retain their own elements. The Court neither applies rational-basis review to a conceded racial burden nor immunizes policies labeled inclusive.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Shaw v. Reno: applies its territorial-design rule and independent-injury qualification; the rule is retained despite the dissent’s express proposal to replace it.
- Gomillion v. Lightfoot: preserves review of boundaries that themselves accomplish racial exclusion.
- Richmond v. J.A. Croson Co. and Adarand Constructors v. Peña: distinguished by the operative allocation of opportunities; their own governing standards remain intact.

##### The asserted sorting, status and representation inferences do not establish a distinct deprivation here

**Holding:** A status or participation injury can be complete when government itself imposes unequal racial treatment; no additional monetary loss, electoral defeat or later denial of service is necessary. But predominant composition of this shared electorate does not alone establish imposed political beliefs, racial ineligibility, inferior civic status or a representative legally responsible only to one race, and these asserted inferences therefore do not establish a distinct violation on this record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part III, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A discriminatory participation or constituent-service practice requires its own examination; no such practice is established and no general entitlement to favorable political attention is created. Gingles's statutory proof is not subject to a new constitutional-injury gate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Anderson v. Martin: preserves protection against official racial electoral treatment without requiring a changed winner.
- McLaurin v. Oklahoma State Regents and White v. Regester: preserve meaningful equality and participation beyond formal eligibility; no comparable operative restriction is established here.
- Thornburg v. Gingles: preserves claim-specific use of voting evidence, not racial stereotyping or an added constitutional-injury gate for statutory claims.

##### Absence of federal compulsion does not itself prohibit voluntary inclusion

**Holding:** A State’s otherwise lawful consideration of racial conditions to improve electoral opportunity is not unconstitutional merely because no prior adjudication or federal statute requires the precise district chosen. This negative proposition leaves every independent constitutional and statutory restriction intact and creates neither a duty to maximize majority-minority districts nor a right to proportional officeholding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part IV, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine votes for the limited permission-versus-compulsion proposition. Only Stone, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer conclude that this plan survives the asserted constitutional challenge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** All nine adopt only the absence-of-compulsion negative proposition; the plan's survival has five votes. No finding that this plan achieved inclusion or a newly proved remedial purpose follows. Sections 2 and 5 keep their distinct elements and channels, and no federal administrative action is set aside.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Voinovich v. Quilter: applies the distinction between lawful state choice and federal obligation.
- Johnson v. De Grandy: retains the no-maximization rule and claim-specific totality inquiry, without proportionality as a safe harbor.
- Beer v. United States and City of Pleasant Grove v. United States: preserve distinct nonretrogression and discriminatory-purpose requirements.
- Morris v. Gressette: preserves restrictions on reviewing federal preclearance determinations, distinct from section 14(b)’s designated channel; this judgment sets aside no federal administrative action.

##### District shape is evidence, not a threshold requirement

**Holding:** Bizarre shape is not a necessary gateway to an otherwise cognizable constitutional districting claim, and unusual shape does not itself prove the violation. Direct and circumstantial evidence may establish the facts material to the particular claim, subject to its own elements and proof requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part V-A, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine votes at this evidentiary level; they disagree about the substantive effect of proved predominance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purpose evidence can include shape, demographics, statements, instructions, departures from ordinary criteria and actual operation, with supported alternatives considered. No universal compactness cutoff, expert report, commissioned study or replacement map is required; any comparator or proof required by the actual claim remains necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gomillion v. Lightfoot: treats geography as evidence of an operative constitutional wrong, not a universal shape test.
- Wright v. Rockefeller: distinguished because this case accepts predominant racial purpose rather than finding it unproved.
- Shaw v. Reno: preserves ordinary pleading and claim-specific proof without an aesthetic threshold.

##### The supported predominance finding is accepted without dictating its legal consequence

**Holding:** Trial findings concerning districting purpose receive ordinary clear-error review, while the legal consequence of those findings is decided separately. The supported finding that race predominated and traditional criteria were subordinated is accepted here; no supported basis for setting it aside is supplied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part V-B, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine votes on acceptance and review of the finding; the constitutional consequence divides 5–4 as stated above.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Acceptance of racial predominance does not establish every constitutional element. The conditional statutory-compliance defense discussion, although joined on its hypothesis by all nine, is nonessential to the judgment and supplies no independent alternative holding, scrutiny trigger or invalidation of DOJ action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Anderson v. Bessemer City: applies ordinary clear-error review to the supported factual finding.
- Shaw v. Reno: controls the distinct legal consequence; changing an assumed predicate into an accepted finding does not itself displace the rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the three-judge district court's judgment for Georgia and denial of injunctive relief in Nos. 94-631, 94-797 and 94-929, without remand. No injunction or redrawing order is unwound and no new injury inquiry, map order, election cancellation or retained supervision is imposed. The judgment does not certify the entire plan or adjudicate separate claims against federal officials.

**Decided:** June 13, 1996

##### Does racial predominance in a shared constituency establish the sole design-only equal-protection claim?

**Holding:** Race-conscious design of a shared territorial constituency, including predominant racial consideration subordinating ordinary districting criteria, does not alone establish this equal-protection deprivation or trigger strict scrutiny. ### Voting Rights Act comparisons and unequal opportunity

#### Holder v. Hall

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2012.

**Decided:** 1994-06-30.

##### The county's governing-body size is within Section 2's coverage

**Holding:** The number of members of this elected county governing authority is a standard, practice or procedure within Section 2, 42 U.S.C. §1973; its combination of legislative and executive functions and its present single-member form do not categorically exempt it from the statute. Coverage alone establishes neither a usable measure of dilution nor unequal electoral opportunity, and it does not prescribe a remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices directly adopt this threshold rule. O'Connor joins this part while opposing statutory liability; Kennedy reserves the affirmative coverage question, and Scalia and Thomas reject coverage of dilution.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chisom v. Roemer, 501 U.S. 380 (1991): applies its rejection of artificial exclusions from Section 2's electoral coverage; the decision does not itself settle the body-size comparison.
- Houston Lawyers' Association v. Attorney General of Texas, 501 U.S. 419 (1991): follows its separation of coverage, legitimate state interests, liability and remedy; extends no automatic right to additional offices.
- Presley v. Etowah County Commission, January 27, 1992: preserves its Section 5 voting/governing boundary and its reservation of independent Section 2 claims; it supplies no Section 2 size benchmark.
- City of Rome v. United States, 446 U.S. 156 (1980): distinguishes Section 5 review of changes against an existing practice from the proof required for this Section 2 results claim.

##### A reasonable comparison must have legal and practical roots independent of a desired racial tally

**Holding:** A Section 2 challenge to this sole-commissioner structure may use a reasonable, workable alternative grounded in the actual legal and institutional setting, suitable to the governmental task, and justified independently of the desired number of minority-preferred winners; a uniquely ideal size is not required. The county-specific authorization of five district-elected commissioners plus an at-large chair, the existing school-board districts and the compact potential majority satisfy that comparison requirement here, without making population share, a possible seat, or any proposed enlargement sufficient by itself.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices directly adopt the complete comparison rule and its application. O'Connor and Kennedy reject that comparison; Thomas and Scalia reject dilution coverage on a different, broader ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30 (1986): extends its comparison inquiry to this locally grounded sole-commissioner challenge while retaining its causal predicates and totality inquiry.
- Houston Lawyers' Association, 501 U.S. 419: applies meaningful consideration of the State's institutional interests; those interests are neither ignored nor converted into categorical structural immunity.
- Growe v. Emison, February 23, 1993: preserves the State's districting responsibility and causal proof requirements; it neither decided nor barred this size comparison.

##### The accepted comparison does not replace the required proof of unequal opportunity

**Holding:** This size-dilution claim requires a sufficiently large and geographically compact minority to form a district majority, political cohesion, and majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat its preferred candidate, absent special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate, together with unequal opportunity established under the totality of circumstances; the comparison alone is insufficient and discriminatory purpose is not required. The extent of protected-class electoral success may be considered but creates no right to election in proportion to population, and the preserved appellate findings establish statutory liability here without reopening cohesion or bloc voting outside the limited review question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices directly adopt the stated application and preservation of the existing proof requirements. There is no separate holding that the Gingles predicates alone establish liability or shift the ultimate statutory burden.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gingles, 478 U.S. 30: follows the causal prerequisites and independently necessary totality inquiry; no universal statistical method or proportional representation rule is adopted.
- Growe, February 23, 1993: preserves the need for local cohesion and bloc-voting proof, including the stated special-circumstances qualification; coalition cognizability, influence claims and the population denominator remain unresolved.
- Voinovich v. Quilter, March 2, 1993: follows the distinction between unequal-opportunity proof and preference for a district form; no change to its initial burden rule or its influence-claim reservations.
- Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993: preserves the independent elements of Section 2; its rejection of the design-only constitutional theory adds no constitutional-injury prerequisite to this statutory claim.

##### The existing remand must provide a lawful remedy for the established violation

**Holding:** Because Section 2 liability is affirmed, the existing remand for formulation of a workable remedy continues, with genuine county-government interests and the responsible state institutions receiving consideration and relief confined to curing the established violation. The local authorization and district pattern inform that inquiry but compel neither their exact adoption, a particular number of officers, a racial quota nor any candidate's victory; no new liability trial is ordered and the constitutional alternative need not be decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; the five-Justice judgment coalition adopts these remedial limits and the affirmance of the Eleventh Circuit's existing remedy remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Houston Lawyers' Association, 501 U.S. 419: follows its instruction to consider genuine state structural interests in fitting relief to a proved violation.
- Growe, February 23, 1993: preserves state responsibility and the lawful federal remedial role; its case-specific dismissal direction is not a universal bar to retaining remedial proceedings.
- Voinovich, March 2, 1993: applies the distinction between state districting choice and a federal remedy justified by an established violation; no mandate to maximize districts follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eleventh Circuit's Section 2 liability judgment and existing remand for formulation of a lawful remedy are affirmed, 5–4. Relief must cure the established violation with attention to genuine county-government interests and responsible state institutions. No new liability trial, mandatory board size, map, racial quota or electoral winner is ordered; the constitutional alternative remains unnecessary.

#### Johnson v. De Grandy, 512 U.S. 997

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 92-519, 92-593 and 92-767.

**Decided:** 1994-06-30.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Johnson v. De Grandy, No. 92-519; De Grandy v. Johnson, No. 92-593; United States v. Florida, No. 92-767. Holder v. Hall, decided June 30, 1994, precedes this decision in the expressly coordinated same-day sequence.

For the other questions in Johnson v. De Grandy, 1994-06-30, Nos. 92-519, 92-593 and 92-767, see Federal Courts — The state validation does not preclude the reserved factual claims; Federal Courts — The United States brings an original enforcement suit.

##### Necessary voting conditions do not require maximization

**Holding:** This Section 2 fragmentation claim requires a minority sufficiently large and geographically compact to form a district majority, political cohesion, and majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat its preferred candidate, absent special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate; the claimant must also establish unequal opportunity to participate and elect chosen representatives under the totality of circumstances, without proving discriminatory purpose. Even when those necessary conditions are satisfied, Section 2 does not require every feasible additional majority-minority district; the disputed first condition is assumed here, leaving citizenship, population-denominator and coalition or influence-claim questions unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg; seven Justices directly adopt the complete necessary-versus-sufficient distinction and rejection of maximization. Thomas and Scalia's statutory noncoverage position is a different rationale and supplies no join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30: follows its causal conditions, special-circumstances qualification and independent totality inquiry; the conditions do not establish liability by themselves.
- Growe v. Emison, February 23, 1993: preserves local cohesion and majority-bloc proof for single-member fragmentation and the unresolved coalition, influence and population questions.
- Voinovich v. Quilter, March 2, 1993: follows the challengers' burden and the distinction between a State's authority to choose districts and a court's power to remedy a proved violation.
- Holder v. Hall, June 30, 1994: applies its separation of coverage, comparison, proof and remedy; a permissible comparison creates no maximization command.
- Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993: preserves the independent statutory elements; its constitutional injury rule adds no further element to Section 2.

##### Proportional opportunity is relevant evidence, not a safe harbor

**Holding:** The relationship between a group's share of the relevant population and the share of districts in which it has an effective opportunity to elect its preferred candidates is relevant evidence within Section 2's totality inquiry, whose weight depends on the other evidence; it is neither a conclusive safe harbor nor a burden-shifting affirmative defense, and disproportionality alone likewise does not establish liability. The inquiry concerns demonstrated electoral opportunity, not nominal district labels or the race of officeholders; it guarantees no proportional election, racial floor or ceiling, statewide offset, or universal geographic or population denominator, and cannot excuse proved local packing, fragmentation or another barrier merely because opportunities exist elsewhere.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg; six Justices directly adopt this contextual rule and its explanation. Kennedy separately accepts the exact limited propositions that proportionality is relevant, that the necessary conditions alone do not establish liability, and that proportionality supplies no safe harbor; his concurrence does not join Part III's full rationale or Parts IV and V's affirmative applications. No combined rationale under Marks is needed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gingles, 478 U.S. 30: follows its contextual inquiry and treatment of electoral success; adopts no conclusive proportional-success presumption.
- Voinovich, March 2, 1993: preserves the need for proof of actual unequal opportunity rather than automatic condemnation or approval of a district form.
- Holder, June 30, 1994: preserves independent totality proof after an adequate comparison; its local institutional comparison supplies neither a quota nor an evidentiary safe harbor.

##### The House liability and dependent remedy cannot stand on this record

**Holding:** Assuming the disputed first Gingles condition in the challengers' favor and retaining the supported findings of discrimination and polarized voting, the tried House record does not establish unequal opportunity under the totality beyond the erroneous inference that every feasible additional Hispanic district must be created. The liability judgment and dependent substitution of Modified De Grandy Plan 268 are reversed; no new liability trial, Supreme Court replacement map or numerical district minimum is ordered, and implementation must follow lawful election administration.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt this full contextual application. Kennedy supports reversal and the mandate under Parts II and VI on the narrower rejection of maximization, without joining Part IV; the House disposition therefore has seven votes, against Thomas and Scalia's vacatur-and-dismissal alternative.

**Material application:** The Court considers the distinct eighteen- and twenty-district House frames, actual effective opportunities, accepted discrimination and polarization evidence, and testimony about neighborhood splitting and concentration. No finding of inconsistent treatment of comparable minority and other neighborhoods is made or imposed as a new liability element. Supported findings retain Rule 52(a) protection; the legal error lies in making feasible additional districts obligatory.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gingles, 478 U.S. 30: follows the totality standard and Rule 52(a)'s protection of supported findings; corrects a legally infected liability determination without creating a ratio presumption or new intent requirement.
- Voinovich, March 2, 1993: applies the need for an established federal violation before judicial imposition of remedial districts.
- Holder, June 30, 1994: distinguishes a sufficient comparison from the separate proof of unequal opportunity; its established liability and remedy remand do not supply missing liability here.

##### The Senate judgment stands because no violation was proved

**Holding:** On the tried Senate record, the asserted unequal opportunity is not established under the totality merely because an additional Hispanic district and a different additional Black district are each feasible; the judgment leaving the State's Senate plan operative is affirmed for lack of a proved violation, not because a proved violation may remain when proposed remedies conflict. The existing Black electoral opportunity with crossover support is relevant evidence, not a second Black-majority district, proof of general cohesion between Hispanic and Black voters, or a legal entitlement to a crossover district.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part V, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt this full contextual application. Kennedy supports affirmance under Parts II and VI on the narrower no-maximization ground without joining Part V; the Senate disposition has seven votes, against Thomas and Scalia's vacatur-and-dismissal alternative.

**Material application:** The operative July 2 Senate judgment found no violation; the July 17 explanation's competing-remedies account cannot excuse a proved wrong. The seven-district, five-wholly-Dade and broader South Florida frames remain distinct, and cohesion within each group does not establish a combined Hispanic–Black coalition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gingles, 478 U.S. 30: applies contextual unequal-opportunity proof without inferring liability from separately feasible district increases.
- Growe, February 23, 1993: preserves the need for proved common cohesion before distinct groups can be treated as one electoral coalition.
- Voinovich, March 2, 1993: preserves the unresolved influence-claim question and ties compelled relief to an established violation.
- Holder, June 30, 1994: preserves the obligation to formulate lawful relief for an established wrong; no competing-remedies exception to liability or relief is recognized.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The House liability judgment and dependent Modified De Grandy Plan 268 remedy are reversed in No. 92-519; the Senate judgment is affirmed in Nos. 92-593 and 92-767, each 7–2. Implementation must follow lawful election administration. No new liability trial, extra Senate district, Supreme Court replacement map or special election timetable is ordered. Congressional proceedings, the Escambia settlement, dismissed claims and preclearance determinations remain undisturbed.

#### Southern Christian Leadership Conference of Alabama v. Sessions

**Docket or dockets:** No separate Supreme Court docket assigned; source petition No. 95-647; judgment below, 56 F.3d 1281.
**Decided:** December 18, 1995.

##### Actual electoral opportunity under the Section 2 results inquiry

**Holding:** Section 2 covers these at-large judicial elections and requires unequal opportunity for minority voters to elect their actual preferred candidates under the applicable Gingles conditions and totality of circumstances. It requires neither a Black preferred candidate nor racial motivation by voters or legislators; candidate preference, incumbency, appointments, partisanship, qualifications and success require contextual treatment in the actual challenged jurisdictions, without conclusive reliance on success elsewhere or nondiscriminatory enactment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Gingles requires a minority sufficiently large and geographically compact for a district majority, political cohesion, and majority bloc voting ordinarily defeating preferred candidates, with special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate considered; those conditions alone do not prove ultimate liability. Supported subsidiary findings survive, but factual deference cannot resolve use of incorrect legal criteria. White-only and statewide contests are not categorically excluded; no expert is chosen, statistical formula prescribed, or dilution found.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chisom and Houston Lawyers preserve judicial-election coverage. Gingles retains its causal conditions and qualifications without a motive element. **Holding:** Section 2 comparisons and remedies in judicial elections must have legal and practical grounding and fit the governmental task, giving genuine weight to electorate/jurisdiction linkage, the qualified-candidate pool and judicial administration. **Proposition-level authority:** The same five-Justice Souter opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A feasible subdistrict alone proves neither liability nor a duty to restructure. No uniquely ideal benchmark is required, no plan selected and no particular map's constitutionality or practicality decided. No added constitutional-injury gate is imposed on Section 2.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Houston Lawyers makes linkage legitimate but nonconclusive. Holder requires a legally and practically grounded comparison rather than structure selected solely for desired racial winners, and preserves the distinction between comparison, liability and relief. Shaw and Miller reject design or racial predominance alone as constitutional injury; actual exclusion, intentional dilution, unequal participation and inferior civic status remain reviewable without presuming racially partial judges.

**Holding:** Because legal errors affect both the independent no-dilution inquiry and categorical remedial premises, the lower judgment must be reconsidered under the correct opportunity, comparison and remedy standards while preserving supported historical findings and plaintiffs' burden on every condition and ultimate unequal opportunity. **Proposition-level authority:** The same five-Justice Souter opinion.

### Federal voter-registration duties

#### Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson, judgment below 60 F.3d 1411

**Docket or dockets:** Petition identifier No. 95-673; Ninth Circuit No. 95-15449; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** February 26, 1996.

##### Whether Congress may impose the challenged congressional-election registration duties

**Holding:** Article I, §4 authorizes the NVRA's challenged distribution, assistance, acceptance and transmittal commands to motor-vehicle, public-assistance and disability-service personnel, including registration at home where the designated disability office already serves the person there. Assigning electoral work to previously multipurpose agencies is within that allocation, and California's demand for federal reimbursement does not excuse compliance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The duties are not sustained as costless, trivial or voluntary; no general authority to command state administration follows. The sustained duties retain the following enacted qualifications, rather than new judicial conditions:

- The Act governs federal elections. Section 4(b) exempts a State with no federal-election registration requirement under law continuously effective on and after March 11, 1993; it also exempts a State permitting every voter to register at the polling place during a general federal election under law continuously effective on and after that date, or enacted by that date to take effect on enactment of the NVRA and continuously maintained thereafter. Those conditions are not a present opt-out for California.
- Under §5(a), a driver's-license or renewal application serves as a federal voter-registration application unless the applicant fails to sign the registration application; a submitted registration application updates prior registration. Under the distinct §5(d) exception, a driver's-license change-of-address form serves as a voter-registration address change unless the registrant states that it is not for that purpose. The license-registration portion may not demand duplicate license information except a second signature or information necessary for the required eligibility statement and attestation, and may require only the minimum information needed to prevent duplicates and permit eligibility assessment and administration.
- Section 7 requires designation of all public-assistance offices and offices providing state-funded programs primarily serving persons with disabilities, as well as other registration offices. Federal and nongovernmental offices may be designated with their agreement. Designated offices must distribute, assist with and accept forms for transmittal; a designated disability office providing services at home must provide those registration services there. An applicant may refuse assistance. Distribution with the public-assistance or disability-service application, recertification, renewal or address-change form may be omitted when the applicant declines registration in writing; applicants who do not decline receive the same degree of assistance as with the office's own forms unless they refuse it. Failure to mark either registration-choice box counts as declination for the assistance provision. Staff may not seek to influence or display political preference, discourage registration, or suggest that registration choice affects services or benefits; declination and source information retain their statutory confidentiality protections.
- Sections 5(e) and 7(d) ordinarily require transmittal to the proper state election official within ten days after acceptance. When an application is accepted within five days before the last registration day for an election, transmittal is due within five days after acceptance. These are statutory duties, distinct from the district court's implementation-plan direction. State officials retain registration and roll decisions within the statute; the decision does not require identical federal and state rolls or determine a particular implementation practice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Smiley includes registration in the manner power; Siebold permits partial federal alteration through shared state administration. New York's ordinary-commerce structural prohibition remains, distinguished by Article I, §4's express allocation.

##### The distinct constitutional basis for presidential registration

**Holding:** The challenged safeguards for California's chosen popular presidential election are valid under the national protective authority recognized by Burroughs, implemented through necessary-and-proper legislation. Article I, §4 alone does not supply presidential-election authority.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Act neither chooses the appointment method nor selects or allocates electors. This holding concerns registration opportunities, eligibility attestations and reliable rolls for the chosen popular election, not unlimited redesign or identical scope of congressional and presidential authority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Burroughs's national protective principle extends from its political-committee corrupt-practices setting to these registration safeguards at the stated scope.

##### Whether the methods themselves redefine substantive voter qualifications

**Holding:** The challenged prescribed registration methods do not themselves redefine who is qualified to vote. Article I, §2 and the Seventeenth Amendment retain their qualification rules; eligibility statements, citizenship information and signed perjury attestations remain, with state officials assessing eligibility within the Act's procedural limits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The federal mail form may not require notarization or other formal authentication. Permissible information and roll maintenance remain federally regulated; concrete eligibility, information and maintenance controversies are unresolved. Identical state and federal rolls are not required.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Term Limits preserves the genuine-administration/additional-qualification distinction without expanding its candidate holding into every voter-qualification dispute.

##### The public-enforcement basis and limits of the compliance injunction

**Holding:** NVRA §11(a) independently authorizes the Attorney General's declaratory and injunctive action against California's established categorical noncompliance; private §11(b) notice conditions do not govern that action. The State's immunity objection cannot defeat the United States' authorized prospective enforcement by treating the action as a private damages suit.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No damages, general private-enforcement immunity holding or finding of private notice compliance is made. The Fourteenth/Fifteenth Amendment alternative remains unneeded and undecided; implementation complexity alone supplies no basis for vacating a permanent injunction without an identified unauthorized command.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the permanent injunction, existing implementation remand and retained appellate jurisdiction. Implementation must impose no burden unauthorized by the Act impairing retained state-election administration and must respect federalism. No original ten-day plan deadline is restarted; no new plan, process, severance or compliance finding is ordered. Reserved coalition claims and dismissals without prejudice of the FEC and chair remain as entered; appellate costs remain denied to every party.

No unprecleared primary-to-convention switch is established. Coverage requires no antecedent finding of racial purpose, effect or fee illegality and establishes none. **Treatment of earlier authority:** Allen's functional voting protection and Smith and Terry's nomination cases reject private-form exemptions. Presley distinguishes direct voting prerequisites from internal allocations of governing authority. Sheffield informs the statutory inquiry without making every constitutional state actor a covered government. Hurley preserves actual expressive choice while supplying no categorical immunity for this fee.

##### Section 10 supports the particular private voting-protection action

**Holding:** Affected voters may pursue the asserted private §10 action against an alleged unlawful voting poll tax or qualifying substitute. The express Attorney General route does not alone require dismissal; this provision-specific action does not establish the fee's illegality, repayment or a general implied-action rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by the same six Justices as Part II.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The §10(a) findings and constitutional declaration, subsection (b)'s official-enforcement direction and subsection (c)'s judicial channel retain distinct functions. The substitute-tax language concerns a substitute enacted after November 1, 1964; former subsection (d)'s late-payment exception was repealed in 1975. Remaining poll-tax defenses, the proper repayment defendant, monetary authority and effects of lawful §5 proceedings require separate adjudication. No §1983 theory or unpresented §2 claim is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Allen's private-§5 reasoning supports but did not already decide this §10 action. Suter requires provision-specific congressional intent, supplied by this Act's design and received enforcement context rather than remedial usefulness. Central Bank offers a limited received-action analogy; Varity's express equity authorization creates no VRA action. Neither fees nor jurisdiction provisions independently create the action.

##### The baseline, designated preclearance process and statutory merits constrain relief

**Holding:** The local court must enforce §5 submission for a legally relevant unprecleared fee change from the operative baseline while leaving purpose and effect to the designated D.D.C./Attorney General process. The private §10 action proceeds under its own requirements and defenses; reversal establishes neither substantive invalidity nor a monetary award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV, joined by the same six Justices. Both statutory dismissals are reversed 6–3.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Virginia's baseline is November 1, 1964. Section 5 permits the prescribed D.D.C. declaration of absence of prohibited purpose and effect or Attorney General submission followed by sixty-day nonobjection. Good cause permits expedited affirmative nonobjection, subject to reexamination on additional information during the remaining sixty days. Neither nonobjection nor the declaration bars a later enforcement challenge. An earlier fee does not preclear a later change, and coverage alone is not retrogression.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse Counts III and IV for the stated proceedings. No expired preliminary convention request is revived; no new convention, election, nominee, classwide repayment, general damages or fee amount is ordered. Separate constitutional Counts I–II and Count V's reported voluntary dismissal remain undisturbed; further prospective relief needs its own live basis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Shaw preserves the designated preclearance channel, and Morris bars replacing its purpose-and-effect determination through local coverage review. Holder's distinct §2 coverage, comparison, liability and remedy remain unchanged.

### Voting Rights Act preclearance

#### Lopez v. Monterey County

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1201.

**Decided:** November 6, 1996.

##### State compulsion does not exempt a covered County's administration

**Holding:** A covered political subdivision administers a voting change when its officials give the change operative effect within the subdivision. Section 5 therefore requires Monterey County to obtain preclearance before administering a postbaseline judicial-election change even if noncovered California compels the change and leaves the County no discretion; the duty is the County's and does not require California to preclear its laws in the abstract.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, Parts I-IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The duty belongs to the covered County. No general state-delegation test, statewide submission obligation, state-law supersession ruling, discriminatory-purpose finding or retrogression finding is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Presley is applied to the direct electoral operation; its internal-governance exclusion remains. Morse's bounded functional and statutory-sequencing principles are applied without enlarging its party-convention holding.

##### Electorate changes and the limits of prior submission

**Holding:** Replacing nine district electorates with one countywide electorate for municipal judges is a change affecting voting under §§5 and 14(c)(1). A later related submission does not preclear earlier changes that it failed to identify; the district court must determine the precise reach of the 1983 submission as to the final ordinance while treating the earlier unidentified consolidations as unprecleared.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, Parts I-IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The later submission did not identify the earlier ordinances. Whether it precleared the final 1983 ordinance depends on its actual terms; no blanket approval of the entire consolidation follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Presley, Allen and Perkins retain coverage for direct changes in electoral choice. McCain v. Lybrand and Clark v. Roemer retain their requirements of specific submission and narrow treatment of claimed administrative approval.

##### Effective designation and prospective enforcement

**Holding:** Section 4(b)'s administrative coverage determination may not be collaterally retried in this §5 enforcement action; statutory bailout under §4(a) is a distinct route. Delay cannot authorize prospective administration of an unprecleared change, although reliance, completed acts and practical timing may bear on lawfully available retrospective relief or the form of a lawful transition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, Parts I-IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide bailout eligibility or retrospective relief involving completed elections, judicial tenure, damages or judicial acts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Clark and NAACP v. Hampton County Election Commission are applied to prospective enforcement; their remedial attention to actual circumstances is preserved. No new general retrospective equitable rule is announced.

##### Enforcement and a genuinely judicial temporary remedy

**Holding:** A §5 enforcement court may determine coverage and submission status and enforce nonuse, but may neither grant substantive preclearance nor authorize an unprecleared County or State electoral plan by calling it court ordered. A necessary, temporary plan wholly formulated by the court may operate without separate §5 preclearance only when independent lawful remedial authority supports it; it must comply with the Constitution and Voting Rights Act, displace state law no further than necessary, and yield to a lawful precleared arrangement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, Parts I-IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ordinary six-year at-large elections do not satisfy Clark's reserved extreme-circumstance possibility. The prior precleared four-division arrangement is an option only: neither preclearance nor Johnson's rule against a design-only racial injury resolves state-law linkage, population, municipality or residence objections, or establishes remedial authority. No replacement plan is selected.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Connor and McDaniel preserve the genuinely judicial/governmental-plan distinction; Clark supplies the nonuse rule and bounded emergency reservation; Morse and Shaw preserve the designated channels; Johnson governs the design-only constitutional objection without approving this particular plan.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand unanimously. Vacate authorization of the at-large election, enjoin prospective use until all operative post-November 1, 1968 changes are precleared, determine the actual 1983 submission and require expeditious submission of remaining changes through the Attorney General or D.D.C. No completed election, judicial office or judicial act is undone.

#### Young v. Fordice

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-2031.
**Decided:** March 31, 1997.

##### Whether discretionary implementation of the NVRA escapes preclearance

**Holding:** A covered jurisdiction must preclear voter-registration changes that embody state policy choices even when undertaken to comply with federal legislation. The NVRA preserves Voting Rights Act duties; wholly federally compelled conduct leaving no state discretion is not decided.

**Holding:** This Provisional Plan never became the practice in force or effect for §5 comparison because its forty-one days of registration use, implementation by about one-third of officials, absence of an election held or imminent, and prompt abandonment when anticipated legislation failed establish its tentative character together. **Holding:** The New System's altered instructions, agencies, manuals, reporting, assistance, and communication of federal-only and combined status are covered changes from the Old System despite retention of the old state-election qualifications. Minor and major changes alike require submission without proof of minority harm in the coverage suit.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The former submission contemplated NVRA registration serving federal and state elections; it did not submit the later dual arrangement's altered communications and administration. Federal enactment and known legislative uncertainty do not supply blanket approval.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual submission questions require examination of their terms. The Court neither rescinds the earlier approval nor authorizes review of its merits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *McCain* and *Lopez* supply the specific-submission rule.

**Holding:** The enforcement court decides coverage and submission status and prevents use of covered unprecleared practices; substantive preclearance belongs to the Attorney General or District Court for the District of Columbia. Relief must preserve lawful NVRA obligations and stay within independent remedial authority.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse summary judgment and remand to identify the components and submission status and enforce nonuse through fitted prospective relief. A State-selected plan does not become exempt merely by being called court ordered. A necessary temporary plan wholly formulated by a court may operate without separate approval only under independent lawful authority, consistently with the Constitution and Voting Rights Act, displacing state law no further than necessary and yielding to a lawful precleared arrangement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No particular interim plan, emergency exception, merged rolls, registration cancellation, damages, discrimination or retrogression finding, or completed-election consequence is ordered. No new private-cause ruling is necessary given the United States' parallel suit.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Clark*, *Morse*, *Shaw*, and *Lopez* preserve nonuse and designated preclearance channels. *Connor* and *McDaniel* distinguish wholly judicial temporary relief from judicial adoption of governmental policy.

#### Reno v. Bossier Parish School Board; Price v. Bossier Parish School Board

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-1455 and 95-1508.
**Decided:** 1997-05-12.

##### Distinct statutory inquiries

**Holding:** Section 5's effect inquiry compares the proposed voting change with the operative benchmark for retrogression; a violation of §2 does not itself establish prohibited §5 effect. A proved §2 violation is also not an independent ground for withholding §5 preclearance, but clearance leaves a separate §2 challenge available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part I: all nine join the effect-benchmark and independent-action rules; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer supply six direct votes that a proved §2 violation is not an independent clearance bar. Stone-Zsela does not join that last proposition; Stevens and Souter oppose it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Stipulated nonretrogression and the feasible two-majority-Black-district alternative establish neither §2 liability nor an entitlement to proportional representation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Beer and Lockhart retain the operative retrogression benchmark. De Grandy requires distinct §2 predicates and a totality inquiry and rejects maximization; clearance does not immunize a plan from independent §2 enforcement.

##### Relevant evidence remains relevant to purpose

**Holding:** Evidence bearing on racial dilution may also bear on the purpose of a voting change and may not be categorically excluded from §5 purpose review merely because it supports a §2 theory. When such an exclusion may have affected the purpose determination, the ultimate preclearance judgment must be vacated for consideration of the relevant evidence without treating it as conclusive proof or altering the jurisdiction's existing burden to establish absence of prohibited purpose.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The evidence is not conclusive or presumptive proof of unlawful purpose. No same-decision defense, new causation rule or burden shift is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Arlington Heights supplies contextual intent evidence, including impact, history, sequence, departures, contemporary explanations and pretext. Pleasant Grove and Richmond preserve purpose as distinct from nonretrogressive effects.

##### Unconstitutional purposeful racial dilution is not confined to intended retrogression

**Holding:** Section 5 prohibits preclearance of a covered voting change adopted with an unconstitutional purpose to dilute voting opportunity on account of race, even when that purpose does not include worsening the existing benchmark. Nonretrogressive effect therefore does not resolve that purpose inquiry; the court must determine actual purpose, preserving the existing preclearance burden and without treating every consideration of race or rejection of a feasible alternative as unlawful.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five direct votes control this instruction. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reserve the broader purpose question.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the ultimate preclearance judgment in both appeals unanimously. Determine actual purpose on the existing record and lawful supplementation under the five-Justice rule, with the jurisdiction's existing burden intact. No actual purpose, §2 violation, replacement plan, additional district or election injunction is found or ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Beer's effects rule remains intact; Pleasant Grove and Richmond require independent attention to unlawful purpose. Shaw distinguishes purposeful dilution from racial territorial design alone, and De Grandy bars automatic liability from a feasible alternative. Morse and Young preserve separate coverage, benchmark, purpose, effects and review-channel tasks.

### Political-committee coverage and membership communications

#### Akins v. FEC

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 94-5088; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

For informational standing and §437g(a)(8) review, see Akins v. FEC, D.C. Circuit No. 94-5088, decided 1997-06-25, Federal Courts — Electoral informational injury and statutory review.

##### III. Major purpose is not a categorical exemption for otherwise covered candidate financing

**Holding:** Buckley and MCFL do not establish an organization-wide major-purpose exemption from political-committee treatment for genuinely covered candidate financing under §431(4)(A). That provision reaches a committee, club, association, or other group of persons receiving contributions aggregating in excess of $1,000 in a calendar year or making expenditures aggregating in excess of $1,000 in a calendar year, subject to the actual definitions, exclusions and constitutional limits; it does not literally make every group that makes a contribution a political committee.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III, including III-A for actual candidate cash transfers and genuine conduits and III-B for other genuinely covered candidate-connected financing, is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct votes with Souter. Kennedy supplies a sixth vote only for III-A. Stone-Zsela reserves both subparts. No combination of separate rationales is necessary to make the five-Justice rule controlling.

**Material application:** Covered purpose, amounts and exclusions must be found for actual transactions. Where contribution treatment depends on candidate connection, cooperation, consultation, concert, request or suggestion must actually exist; ideological alignment, policy cooperation, lobbying or partisan identity alone is insufficient.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Genuine independent expression remains protected. The Court does not conclusively classify AIPAC or uphold every organizational or donor-disclosure duty in every application; supported particular-duty challenges remain possible.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Buckley and MCFL's independent-expression narrowing does not create the claimed categorical exemption for covered candidate financing. Colorado Republican preserves independent expression and requires real candidate connection rather than organizational identity.

##### IV. Membership exclusions and their distinct reporting proviso govern the remand

**Holding:** The Commission must determine membership, covered transactions, candidate connection, calendar-year thresholds and applicable exclusions before imposing political-committee duties. Section 431(9)(B)(iii)'s membership-communication exclusion retains its separate reporting proviso; §441b(b)(2)'s exclusions and the §431 cross-references retain their different terms.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Souter's Court opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct votes. Part III's five-Justice construction governs the remand notwithstanding Stone-Zsela's and Kennedy's narrower positions.

**Material application:** Section 431(9)(B)(iii) excludes communications by a membership organization or corporation to its members, stockholders, or executive or administrative personnel if it is not organized primarily to influence nomination or election to federal office. The proviso requires a membership organization, including a labor organization, or a corporation to report costs directly attributable to communications expressly advocating election or defeat of a clearly identified candidate when those costs exceed $2,000 per election, except a communication primarily devoted to other subjects. Reporting follows §434(a)(4)(A)(i) and, for a general election, §434(a)(4)(A)(ii). This is neither the $1,000 calendar-year committee threshold nor an exception transplanted into §441b. Section 441b(b)(2) separately excludes corporate communications on any subject to stockholders and executive or administrative personnel and their families, and labor-organization communications to members and their families. Its nonpartisan registration and get-out-the-vote exclusion covers the same respective recipients. Its separate-fund exclusion covers establishment, administration and solicitation of contributions to a separate segregated fund used for political purposes by a corporation, labor organization, membership organization, cooperative or corporation without capital stock. The loan exception concerns a national or State bank's ordinary-course loan made under applicable banking law and regulations. Sections 431(8)(B)(vi) and (9)(B)(v) cross-reference activity allowed by §441b without importing the $2,000 proviso. The agency must apply each provision to the actual record. No final membership finding, coordination finding, threshold calculation, later membership rule, donor list, liability or penalty is supplied by this decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The five-Justice coverage construction governs remand notwithstanding the narrower separate preferences. Membership, actual covered transactions and each distinct exclusion require lawful Commission determination; no registration, donor list, disclosure award, enforcement order, liability or penalty is supplied.

### Congressional nomination routes and relevant-electorate support

#### Libertarian Party of Illinois v. Rednour

**Docket or dockets:** 97-25.

**Decided:** 1998-09-21.

##### The actual congressional access system survives this limited challenge

**Holding:** Under Anderson and Burdick, Illinois may require meaningful support in the relevant congressional electorate and use different primary and general-election nomination routes where the complete actual system leaves a reasonable available route to the general ballot. On this record, trustee-based statewide status does not compel district-established status, and the combined requirements do not establish the severe or unjustified burden asserted; the holding creates no categorical safe harbor for percentages, slates or cumulative deadlines.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter). Souter's Part II is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes.

**Material application:** Anderson v. Celebrezze requires assessment of the injury's character and magnitude, the State's precise interests and their need for the burden. Burdick v. Takushi requires narrow fit to a compelling interest for severe burdens and ordinarily permits reasonable, nondiscriminatory burdens justified by important regulatory interests. The inquiry considers the whole system, not a nominal alternative alone.

Norman v. Reed permits distinct-electorate support requirements while forbidding party-name barriers and loss of independently qualified components because another electorate failed. Statewide trustee support does not itself show congressional-district support. The different denominators and stages make the petition comparison more than a simple tenfold disparity. Jenness v. Fortson permits meaningful support requirements in an actual workable system; Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party likewise examines the available nomination and advocacy alternatives. Here the capped general-ballot petition route supplies an available alternative without an automatic constitutional right to a public party primary.

The substantial numerical difference and combined conditions deserve examination. The 1994 successes are relevant but not conclusive; disputed 1996 acceptances cannot prove full compliance, and the exceptional 1992 route cannot establish ordinary-year accessibility. No supported genuine dispute of the asserted impossible cumulative burden requires trial. Nor does the record establish Norman's disqualification of an independently qualified component through another electorate's failure. The strongest contrary argument requires a demonstrated heavier cumulative burden or automatic transfer of statewide status that this record does not support. Literal impossibility is not a universal prerequisite to a constitutional claim. Incumbent preservation is no independent justification, and a properly supported future burden challenge remains available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Anderson applies the actual-burden, precise-interest and necessity inquiry.
- Burdick preserves complete-system review and the severe-burden boundary; its write-in presumption is not generalized.
- Norman preserves electorate-specific support and independently qualified components; it creates no automatic statewide-to-district status.
- Jenness permits a meaningful demonstration of support, not universal approval of five-percent petitions.
- Timmons applies proportionate regulation within actual alternatives, not an incumbent-protection interest or categorical approval of every nomination rule.

##### The judgment supplies no replacement election system

**Holding:** The access judgment is affirmed and the distinct Qualifications Clause claim rejected without reopening the primary, declaring automatic district-established status, substituting a signature number or granting a new statewide qualification. Independently qualified components retain Norman's protection, and supported challenges to different actual cumulative burdens remain open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter). Souter's Part IV has all eight Associates on the affirmance and its grounds; Stone-Zsela agrees only with rejection of the distinct Qualifications Clause claim.

**Material application:** The supported record permits disposition of the actual claims without inventing a genuine factual dispute or designing an alternative nomination scheme. The Court's conclusion does not validate every complete-slate condition, deadline combination or future application. Nor does it decide the separate equal-protection claim that was not pleaded. A later claim must establish its actual injury, applicable interests and remedy rather than treating this bounded affirmance or isolated successful qualification as conclusive.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Norman retains its independently qualified-component rule and bounded election remedy; neither is displaced by this affirmance.

### Contextual express advocacy and statutory limits

#### Federal Election Commission v. Maine Right to Life Committee, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** 96-1818.

**Decided:** 1998-09-14.

##### The rule's cumulative restrictions preserve the statutory express-advocacy boundary

**Holding:** Under 2 U.S.C. §441b, 11 C.F.R. §100.22(b) does not exceed the statute merely because it permits limited context rather than an exclusive list of electoral slogans. Its coverage requires a communication considered as a whole, only limited reference to external events, an unmistakable and unambiguous electoral portion suggestive of only one meaning, and no reasonable difference over whether it encourages election or defeat of a clearly identified candidate rather than another kind of action; a reasonable nonelectoral interpretation excludes coverage.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens). Parts II–III of Stevens's Opinion of the Court are joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct votes. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent from the statutory disposition and do not join this construction.

**Material application:** The Court holds that Buckley v. Valeo and FEC v. Massachusetts Citizens for Life require express electoral advocacy without prescribing a single verbal formula. The lower court itself recognized that MCFL included a marginally less direct explicit appeal; the disagreement is whether paragraph (b)'s contextual route necessarily crosses the narrowed statute's boundary. Its cumulative conditions need not do so. Election proximity, expected electoral effect, policy agreement, hostility toward a candidate, or an agency's preferred reading cannot replace the required unambiguous electoral direction. Limited context may inform the communication's meaning, but cannot displace a reasonable call to issue debate or some other action. The contrary concern that unchanged issue speech will be reclassified whenever an election approaches is answered at this statutory level by enforcing every restriction together, not by deferring to the Commission. A court must independently test the communication against them. This interpretation rejects categorical statutory invalidity; it neither decides the unresolved constitutional vagueness claim nor holds the rule valid in every application. It finds no MRLC publication unlawful and does not order enforcement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Buckley and MCFL retain their narrowing construction and protection of genuine issue discussion; the rule is permissible only within that boundary. Akins v. FEC's insistence on actual statutory conditions and independent judicial resolution is applied, without enlarging its distinct coverage holdings.

##### The unresolved constitutional challenge returns for adjudication

**Holding:** The categorical declaration that §100.22(b) exceeds statutory authority is vacated, and the case is remanded for the unresolved vagueness challenge and any properly presented application issues under the restrictive construction stated above. Subsection (a), separate contribution rules, distinct membership exclusions, and nonprofit protection based on actual characteristics are not invalidated or generally approved by this disposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes.

**Material application:** The Court removes the statutory premise of the judgment below, not the separate questions that premise made unnecessary to decide. A statutory construction alone cannot establish constitutional clarity in every application. No injunction against an otherwise unsupported enforcement threat, replacement rule, or judicial supervision of rulemaking follows. Day v. Holahan and MCFL require attention to an organization's actual political resources and financing; the acceptance of some corporate contributions does not establish MRLC's ultimate category, and no such determination is needed here. Lawful contribution enforcement and the Attorney General's dismissal remain separate. The remand permits the unresolved claims to be adjudicated without treating today's decision as a directive to prosecute.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Day and MCFL retain their fact-dependent nonprofit protection. Austin v. Michigan Chamber of Commerce remains within its surviving corporate-treasury scope; nothing here approves every corporate restriction.

### Corporate gifts, paid identification and distinct contribution limits

#### Kentucky Right to Life, Inc. v. Stengel

**Docket or dockets:** 97-60; Sixth Circuit No. 95-6581.

**Decided:** 1998-09-14.

##### Nonprofit status does not require a categorical exemption for corporate candidate gifts

**Holding:** Kentucky may apply §121.025's direct candidate-contribution restriction to KRL's general corporate treasury without creating a categorical exemption merely because KRL is an ideological nonprofit. The rejection of that facial exemption does not authorize restrictions on protected independent advocacy or determine whether KRL satisfies an independent-expenditure funding condition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor). Part III of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices.

**Material application:** The Court holds that a payment to a candidate remains different from an association's own expression. NRWC supports prophylactic treatment of the corporate contribution channel, and MCFL expressly distinguishes direct contributions from the independent spending it protects. The destination of the payment and the risk of candidate obligation supply the relevant ground; the State need not first prove that this corporation completed a corrupt transaction. KRLPAC supplies a separate voluntary path for candidate-directed support, while lawful independent advocacy remains distinct. Day protects an association according to its actual resources and structure; its nonprofit holding expressly left direct corporate gifts undecided. The contrary categorical exemption would erase that distinction. The Court does not find KRL to be a commercial treasury or conduit, decide its funding eligibility, or endorse every restriction that can be attached to incorporation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** NRWC applies to the contribution channel; MCFL's independent-expression protection remains intact. Day's nonprofit holding is distinguished according to the transaction it decided. Austin is neither overruled nor enlarged beyond its surviving corporate-treasury scope.

##### The paid candidate-advertisement identification rule survives this facial challenge

**Holding:** The surviving facial challenge to §121.190(1)'s payer-identification requirement fails in the paid candidate-finance and organizational setting presented. The rule requires the payer's name and address, introduced by the paid-for-by designation, on newspaper and magazine advertising, posters, circulars, billboards, handbills, sample ballots and paid television or radio announcements expressly advocating election or defeat of a clearly identified candidate, slate or group of candidates; ordinary issue advocacy is not covered merely because it mentions a candidate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor). Part IV of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices. Thomas dissents.

**Material application:** The Court holds that identifying the financer of organized paid candidate advocacy serves source-tracing and enforcement interests different from general curiosity about an ordinary issue leafleteer's identity. The address helps identify the legal payer and connect an advertisement to financial transactions; it is a burden requiring justification, not dispensable statutory language. Buckley's disclosure interests and McIntyre's express reservation of materially different campaign-finance and organizational settings permit this bounded facial judgment. The on-message command is more intrusive than filing a report, but the actual financer-identification function supplies the necessary connection here without limiting the amount or content of the independent expenditure. The State cannot presume corruption or coordination merely from an advertisement's independence or political identity. McIntyre continues to protect ordinary anonymous issue leaflets, and materially different individual applications remain open. So does a claim supported by a reasonable probability of threats or reprisals. No actual retaliation, coordination, general membership-disclosure authority or requirement of permission before publication is found.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** McIntyre is distinguished on its reserved candidate-finance and organizational context without diminishing its individual-leaflet rule. Buckley supplies the distinct finance-disclosure interests. Colorado Republican preserves independence and forbids identity-based substitution for an actual candidate connection. Brown v. Socialist Workers preserves protection against demonstrated associational harms without creating a new blanket exemption.

##### The preserved challenges to the two direct $1,000 limits fail

**Holding:** The preserved facial challenge to §121.150(6)'s $1,000 giving and acceptance limit in any one election is rejected under the direct-contribution distinction. The separate challenge to §121A.050(1)'s $1,000 slate limit in any one election is likewise rejected, preserving that section's knowing-acceptance and knowing-giving terms and the giving prohibition's express exception for independent expenditures as statutorily defined.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor). Part V of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; the coalition covers both distinct provisions.

**Material application:** The Court holds that the surviving direct-transfer challenges can be resolved under Buckley's contribution rule without treating contribution limits as constitutionally unlimited. A direct payment to the candidate presents a corruption and circumvention concern different from financing one's own advocacy. The lower court reported that counsel had proposed the $1,000 figure and essentially conceded its constitutionality. That reported position confines the preserved controversy; it is not a general unseen waiver or a reason to accept an incorrect legal rule. The direct-transfer character and bounded claim supply the judgment. The Court does not move the slate provision's knowledge requirement or independent-expenditure exception into a different section. Nor does it turn a per-election limit into an annual one, establish $1,000 as a constitutional minimum or safe harbor for every office, or authorize limits meant to equalize political influence. Future demonstrated cumulative or prohibitory burdens remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Buckley's direct-contribution distinction and fitted anticorruption rationale apply; its historical figure is not a constitutional floor. Day preserves scrutiny of meaningful association and cumulative burdens without equating contributions with independent expenditures.

##### The annual all-intermediary aggregate requires the inquiry the lower court bypassed

**Holding:** A $1,500 ceiling on one person's annual contributions to all permanent committees and contributing organizations together must be assessed for meaningful collective political association and fit to an identified corruption or circumvention mechanism. Because the lower judgment sustained §121.150(10) without giving that inquiry its required force, the judgment on this component is vacated and remanded for application of Day to the existing record and ordinary remaining remedial questions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor). Part VI of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer: six Justices. Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Ginsburg dissent from this remand.

**Material application:** The Court holds that the ability to regulate a real candidate-funding conduit does not answer the fit of an aggregate that exhausts one allowance across unrelated recipients. Day requires meaningful opportunity to pool resources for political expression. California Medical permits prevention of circumvention through candidate-transfer channels, but its four-Justice low-protection reasoning is not a controlling categorical answer to every intermediary contribution. KRLPAC's candidate transfers identify a legitimate concern; they do not establish the fit of the law's complete combined-recipient reach. The lower court must consider actual recipient functions, the aggregate's breadth, the supported burden and the separate opportunity for direct candidate gifts under the proper standard. A higher figure than Day's $100 does not end the inquiry, and a general reference to smaller state campaigns does not supply it. Conversely, the Court finds neither every application invalid nor the State's evidentiary showing absent. This is a legal-standard remand, not a compulsory new trial, discovery order, new burden of proof, constitutional dollar minimum or direction to exempt all independent recipients automatically.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Day applies its meaningful-association and fit rule beyond the particular $100 ceiling while retaining its limits. California Medical preserves genuine conduit regulation without converting plurality reasoning into law. Citizens Against Rent Control protects collective expression; NCPAC preserves independent advocacy without immunizing candidate contributions.

##### Both gubernatorial receipt branches survive KRLPAC's facial challenge

**Holding:** KRLPAC's facial challenge to both §121A.030(4) receipt limits is rejected: the Fund participant's permanent-committee receipts are limited to 25 percent of qualifying contributions received, up to $150,000 per election, while the nonparticipant is subject to the separate $150,000 ceiling. Direct candidate receipts present a source-specific dependency concern, and this facial record does not demonstrate obstruction of effective advocacy or association; concrete later burdens under either branch remain open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor). Part VII of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate and remand both branches.

**Material application:** The Court holds that candidate receipts from one organized financing channel differ from the annual pool across independent recipients addressed in Part VI. Buckley's contribution distinction permits a fitted response to dependence on that channel without requiring proof of a completed corrupt bargain. The percentage branch limits the share measured against the participant's own qualifying contributions; it remains operative at receipts below the maximum. The nonparticipant branch uses its separate fixed ceiling. The district court reported no indication that the provision had actually limited PAC contributions or was likely to do so on this record. That reported conclusion supports this facial judgment; it is not a statutory exception or an irrebuttable fact about every race. The contrary concern that earlier donors may exhaust a later association's opportunity is substantial but does not establish facial invalidity here. Neither the maximum-fundraising example nor the number of committees needed to reach a maximum is a premise of this judgment. The Court does not rely on consent to unknown funding conditions, disfavor of PAC views, or equalization. Later proof of ordinary fundraising disabled by the percentage at low receipts, selective foreclosure or prohibitory cumulative effects requires separate consideration.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Buckley's direct-contribution rationale applies at this source-specific and facial level. Day preserves meaningful association and rejects a permanent dollar safe harbor; its different annual-intermediary and responsive-financing components do not resolve these candidate-receipt rules. Colorado Republican preserves independent advocacy without converting direct candidate receipts into independent spending.

### Demonstrated-support allocation of party expenses

#### Libertarian Party of Florida v. Smith

**Docket or dockets:** 96-1867.

**Decided:** 1998-09-21.

##### This objective support criterion is constitutional at the demonstrated burden

**Holding:** A State may allocate this limited party-expense benefit according to an objective five-percent registered-support criterion where lawfully collected fees, continued candidate access, unrestricted private political spending and the demonstrated record establish no severe associational burden, and the measure reasonably serves stable public participation. The holding permits demonstrated-support financing; it does not permit preserving named incumbents or suppressing political viewpoints as independent objectives.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens). Stevens's Part II is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes, including the stated limitations.

**Material application:** Anderson v. Celebrezze requires comparing the character and magnitude of actual injury with the State's precise interests and their need for the burden. Burdick v. Takushi applies that inquiry to the complete electoral setting: severe burdens demand narrowly fitted compelling justification; reasonable, nondiscriminatory burdens ordinarily may rest on important regulatory interests. A funding label does not excuse examination of the classification, but a comparative benefit alone does not establish severe restriction.

Buckley v. Valeo, Part III, permits demonstrated-support conditions on election funding and distinguishes withholding a subsidy from excluding a candidacy. Here the objective, potentially changing registration threshold reasonably measures stable party support. Supporting established public participation without subsidizing every splinter organization is a legitimate allocation interest. Wilson's individual ballot qualification does not establish an automatic entitlement to party-wide financial assistance. Norman v. Reed protects independently qualified candidacies; this provision does not remove one from the ballot.

The strongest contrary argument is that selective aid magnifies existing parties' advantages after rivals qualify. Such an allocation remains reviewable for substantial suppression, viewpoint favoritism or discriminatory administration. This record establishes no such burden beyond the limited benefit's existence. Florida's registration measure differs from Buckley's vote criteria, expenditure ceilings and post-election payments; those features are not imported here. The Court does not adopt unqualified rhetoric about discouraging minor parties, a general rational-basis exemption for funding, or validity for every five-percent threshold.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Anderson applies the actual-burden and precise-interest inquiry to this financial classification.
- Burdick preserves burden-sensitive scrutiny; its write-in presumption is not transferred to all subsidies.
- Buckley supplies demonstrated-support funding authority while retaining constitutional limits and political fluidity; the schemes are not identical.
- Norman preserves qualified-component ballot protection without creating automatic party-expense funding.
- Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes preserves review of genuine support criteria and viewpoint discrimination; its forum classification is not an election-finance rule.
- National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley supplies no universal subsidy immunity; its arts-program holding and divided spending rationales retain their actual scope.

##### Affirmance supplies no new payment or allocation entitlement

**Holding:** Because the presented federal challenge fails, the judgment sustaining §99.103(1)'s limited allocation is affirmed without a rebate, refund or judicially prescribed formula. The lawful collection of candidate fees, access to the ballot and a materially different source-of-funds exaction remain outside the adjudicated challenge.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens). Stevens's Part III is joined by all seven other Associates; eight direct rationale votes.

**Material application:** The challengers sought relief against discriminatory operation of the allocation, while conceding lawful fee collection. The Court's bounded constitutional conclusion supplies no basis for enjoining this scheme or directing new payments. No state-law refund claim, expenditure ceiling or future financing system is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Buckley remains a source of lawful financing distinctions, not a command to adopt its formula or award money in this case.

### Independent voter guides and candidate inquiries

#### Clifton v. Federal Election Commission

**Docket or dockets:** 97-786.

**Decided:** 1998-09-14.

##### Asking a candidate about public issues does not itself finance the candidate

**Holding:** Under 2 U.S.C. §441b, 11 C.F.R. §114.4(c)(4) cannot prohibit a publisher's mere inquiry to a candidate about positions or recorded votes as a contribution or candidate-directed expenditure. The required connection concerns campaign spending: actual cooperation, consultation, concert, request or suggestion concerning the expenditure remains governed by its own statutory predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous, at the stated narrow statutory and review-specific scope.

**Material application:** The Court holds that gathering information is not itself spending on a candidate's behalf. Buckley v. Valeo distinguishes independent expression from candidate financing; Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. Federal Election Commission requires a real candidate connection rather than a label substituting for that connection; Akins v. FEC likewise requires actual covered financing. MRLC's effort to learn the meaning of public votes supplies no finding that a candidate directed the guide's content, collaborated in distribution, or received an agreed campaign service. The Commission may investigate such arrangements when supported. Its concern that a survey could conceal collaboration cannot make every inquiry collaboration. This holding protects neither staged questions supplying campaign work nor a paid political advertisement disguised as an independent guide.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Buckley's contribution/expenditure distinction, Colorado Republican's actual-connection requirement and Akins's transaction-specific coverage remain in force and apply to this information-gathering setting.

##### The existing written-only and equal-space declaration stands

**Holding:** In this private-petitioner review, the declaration invalidating §114.4(c)(5)'s compulsory written questions and replies and equal space and prominence remains in place. General rulemaking and enforcement authority does not authorize treating every independently edited guide involving candidate inquiry as a prohibited contribution or imposing these indiscriminate contact and editorial conditions on that premise.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous, at the stated narrow statutory and review-specific scope. Stevens and Ginsburg join only within this private-petitioner review and its limited statutory ground; they do not adopt a general constitutional ban on contact safeguards.

**Material application:** The Court's limited statutory ground respects the difference between a real contribution and dialogue used to compose independent speech. A written record can aid enforcement, but administrative convenience does not establish that an oral discussion transfers value to a candidate. Miami Herald Publishing Co. v. Tornillo protects editorial allocation; Hurley v. Irish-American Gay, Lesbian and Bisexual Group of Boston protects the speaker's selection of an expressive composition. Those principles reinforce the need for actual statutory authority before requiring a private publisher to give every candidate equal space and prominence. The petition seeks broader protection for the private plaintiffs; no established cross-petition seeks enlargement of the Commission's rights by restoring restrictions already invalidated. That review limit independently preserves the existing relief. The Court does not decide that Congress could never authorize a specifically fitted contact safeguard, that accurate disclosure could never be required under lawful authority, or that proved coordinated services are exempt. A government-authored guide or a condition on a properly defined public resource presents different questions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Tornillo and Hurley reinforce editorial protection without becoming a universal prohibition on regulation of campaign contributions. Austin v. Michigan Chamber of Commerce is neither overruled nor treated as a delegation of every restriction on corporate speech.

##### The unfinished provisions and organizational status remain for the existing remand

**Holding:** The Court does not expand the declaration to invalidate the whole regulation or uphold the unresolved electioneering-message clauses. Their operative meaning, ripeness and severability remain for the District Court; MRLC's entitlement to nonprofit constitutional protection remains undecided on its actual funding characteristics.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous, at the stated narrow statutory and review-specific scope.

**Material application:** The Court cannot supply an operative definition the appellate record leaves unsettled or decide severability from the label alone. The existing remand permits those questions and any properly supported interim-relief request to be decided under their own standards. Day v. Holahan prevents automatic denial of MCFL protection solely because an ideological nonprofit accepts some corporate money; it does not establish that MRLC's funding is insignificant, noncommercial or free of conduit features. The Court therefore neither grants an organizational exemption nor assumes commercial-treasury status. Genuine-coordination restrictions, express-advocacy provisions not successfully challenged, and distinct statutory membership exclusions retain their lawful scope.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Day and MCFL require actual organizational and funding characteristics; Austin's surviving corporate-treasury rule remains available in its materially different setting. No prior decision is overruled.


### A primary barrier must be tested against actual nomination opportunity

#### Brooks v. Barnes

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1521; judgment below, Brooks v. Miller, 158 F.3d 1230.
**Decided:** 1999-04-22.

##### A primary barrier must be tested against actual nomination opportunity

**Holding:** A Section 2 challenge to a primary-runoff barrier turns on protected voters' equal opportunity in the affected nomination or election process, supported political cohesion and majority bloc voting in the affected electoral settings, and the totality of circumstances. It does not require proof that every additional minority-preferred nominee would win the general election, and neither candidate race, the rarity of runoffs among all primaries nor a conclusive statewide offset of winners can replace the inquiry into the affected voters' actual opportunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Parts I–II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer (six Justices).

##### The supported purpose findings sustain constitutional rejection

**Holding:** A constitutional challenge requires proof of discriminatory purpose attributable to the challenged enactment, assessed from the whole relevant record rather than electoral outcomes alone. The supported findings distinguishing the failed 1963 proposal from the enacted 1964 measure are not clearly erroneous; the historical racial climate and earlier motives do not compel a contrary purpose finding for the later enactment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, unanimous.

##### The independent same-enactment finding also defeats causation

**Independent alternative holding:** If discriminatory purpose was a substantial or motivating factor, the defenders bear the burden of showing that the same measure would have been enacted without that factor. The supported independent finding that the 1964 measure would have passed for the credited anti-manipulation and election-administration reasons satisfies that burden here and independently sustains constitutional affirmance.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Souter's Part III, unanimous; the same-enactment determination is independently sufficient.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2 results and constitutional purpose remain distinct. No violation, plurality system, alternate threshold, racial vote guarantee or new statistical study is ordered; a remedy requires a proved violation and lawful comparison.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand the statutory claim under corrected nomination-opportunity standards; affirm the constitutional judgment on supported purpose findings and the independent same-enactment ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gingles supplies causal predicates, totality and deference to supported findings under the correct legal standard.
- Voinovich preserves the claimant's burden and separates lawful state choices from a compelled federal remedy.
- Holder requires a grounded comparison; it supplies no ideal electoral system or quota.
- De Grandy rejects maximization and conclusive numerical offsets while preserving actual local opportunity.
- Southern Christian Leadership Conference of Alabama v. Sessions requires correct opportunity and remedy standards while retaining supported subsidiary findings.
- Arlington Heights makes the full historical and decisionmaking context relevant to purpose.
- Hunter v. Underwood and Mt. Healthy supply the distinct motivating-factor and same-enactment sequence, including the defenders' burden.

### The reimbursement condition does not establish the claimed exclusion or transfer of electoral authority

#### Brower v. Washington

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1510; judgment below Brower v. State, 137 Wash. 2d 44, 969 P.2d 42.
**Decided:** 1999-03-25.

##### The reimbursement condition does not establish the claimed exclusion or transfer of electoral authority

**Holding:** A legislature may refer this particular public-private proposal subject to the beneficiary's undertaking to reimburse public election costs when the arrangement preserves equal franchise, viewpoint neutrality and meaningful legal participation. On this record the condition neither charges citizens for voting or ordinary petition access nor transfers electoral administration or the public's approval power to the beneficiary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's unanimous opinion of the Court.

##### The supported timing and resource-disparity claim establishes no right to delay or equalize the campaign

**Holding:** The short campaign and opposition's inability to match the beneficiary's resources do not, on this record, establish the asserted abridgment of political expression or a right to postpone the election or equalize advocacy. A supported practical inability to organize or communicate, or a deliberately disabling calendar, would require analysis of that actual burden and the State's precise scheduling justification.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Holding:** The affiliate's agreement to reimburse costs is an antecedent financial condition of this legislative proposal, not an allocated vote in the public electorate's decision. Because it gives the affiliate no ballot and changes no citizen's voting weight or the public tally, it does not violate the asserted federal equal-vote rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general private-reimbursement or campaign-duration safe harbor, right to resource parity or transfer of electoral voting power follows. **Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the presented federal components without a new election, changed tally, injunction, damages or unwinding of reimbursement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Anderson and Burdick supply the actual-burden and justification inquiry; Burdick's write-in application creates no categorical presumption for private reimbursement.
- Buckley v. American Constitutional Law Foundation, decided January 12, 1999, supplies protection against direct restraints on political conversation. Its invalid registration, badge and disclosure restrictions are not facts of this case.

### I. Registered-voter condition

### I. State compulsion does not exempt covered administration

#### Lopez v. Monterey County

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1396; Northern District of California No. C-91-20559-RMW.
**Decided:** 1999-01-20.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court. The eight Associate Justices join Parts I–V. Stone-Zsela joins Parts I, II and IV, and Part III only insofar as it rejects delay as authorization for prospective use and rejects the internal-administration characterization of a direct electorate change. He agrees in rejection of the collateral-designation defense and in the dependent tenure-order disposition without adding grounds to those parts. Each controlling part thus has at least eight direct joins. No separate constitutional justification for Section 5 is adopted.

##### I. State compulsion does not exempt covered administration

**Holding:** A covered political subdivision must obtain Section 5 preclearance before administering a postbaseline voting change within that subdivision, even when a noncovered State mandates the change and leaves no local discretion. The duty attaches to the County's administration; it does not require California to submit its laws in the abstract.

##### II. The actual submission controls the reach of approval

**Holding:** Replacing district electorates with a countywide electorate for judges is a covered change in voting, and a submission does not preclear earlier operative changes it failed to identify. The District Court must determine the actual reach of the 1983 submission as to the final ordinance; its attachment and the Attorney General's nonobjection do not establish blanket approval of the preceding consolidations.

##### III. Delay and a collateral designation challenge do not authorize prospective use

**Holding:** Delay does not authorize prospective administration of an operative covered change that lacks preclearance, and the effective Section 4(b) coverage determination cannot be collaterally retried in this Section 5 enforcement action. Statutory bailout remains a distinct route; reliance, completed acts and practical timing may bear on legally available retrospective relief or a lawful transition, without supplying clearance.

##### IV. Nonuse and the confined judicial-plan alternative

**Holding:** The District Court must prevent prospective use of established operative changes lacking preclearance until approval is obtained through the Attorney General or the District Court for the District of Columbia. ##### V. The dependent tenure order requires lawful reconsideration

**Holding:** The disposition of the judicial-term-extension order must be set aside insofar as it depends on the rejected exemption and reconsidered under the corrected legal premise and actual circumstances. Reversal does not automatically revive an expired arrangement, extend any judge's tenure, install or remove an officeholder, cancel a completed election or invalidate judicial acts.

- **Connor v. Johnson:** Preserved for a necessary plan wholly formulated by the court within independent lawful authority.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The County's obligation to preclear administered changes is unchanged. State compulsion, delay and attachment of a related ordinance do not dispense with it.
