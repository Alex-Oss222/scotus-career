## Criminal Procedure: Sentencing and Capital Punishment

### Capital sentencing

#### Sochor v. Florida, 504 U.S. 527 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-5843.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

For the preservation bar on the jury-heinousness instruction claim in *Sochor v. Florida*, June 8, 1992, No. 91-5843, see Federal Courts — Adequate and independent state grounds.

##### Whether the judge's conscious-victim strangulation application supplies a sufficiently narrowed heinousness aggravator

**Holding:** A sentencing judge may apply an otherwise vague heinousness aggravator through an authoritative and sufficiently specific state narrowing construction. Florida's consistently recognized conscious-victim strangulation construction supplies adequate guidance here; inconsistent applications in other factual settings do not establish that this judge lacked guidance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-B of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; seven Justices. Blackmun and Stevens do not join this disposition because they would require correction of the separate jury defect.

**Current force:** Remains controlling at the stated judicial application and record.

**Material application:** Florida's identified narrowing decisions address the fear and conscious suffering of strangulation. The victim's resistance, fear and strangulation support that concrete construction; the judge did not merely repeat the statutory adjectives.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court approves neither every Florida heinousness decision nor the bare instruction given to this jury, and it does not decide a different victim's condition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reject the preserved constitutional challenge to the judge's narrowed heinousness application; that factor supplies no basis for relief.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Godfrey v. Georgia*, 446 U.S. 420, and *Maynard v. Cartwright*, 486 U.S. 356, require meaningful narrowing. *Walton v. Arizona*, 497 U.S. 639, permits reliance on authoritative state constructions and presumes the judge's familiarity with them. *Proffitt v. Florida*, 428 U.S. 242, preserves a narrowed heinousness category without approving every later application.

##### Whether submission of a lawful but unsupported coldness aggravator establishes that a general advisory jury recommendation relied on it

**Holding:** Submitting a legally available aggravating circumstance without evidentiary support does not itself establish that a Florida advisory jury relied on it in a general death recommendation that identifies no aggravator findings. The asserted inference of jury-coldness error is rejected without a finding that the jury actually rejected the factor or resolution of a legally invalid instruction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Souter's opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices. Stone reserves this independent question, Blackmun and Stevens would include the submission in correction, and Scalia does not join this holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling at this narrow factual-insufficiency inference and general advisory recommendation.

**Material application:** Cold, calculated and premeditated murder was a lawful aggravator, but proof of its heightened premeditation requirement failed. The jury identified no aggravator findings, so submission alone cannot establish its reliance. The judge's express coldness finding establishes a separate error.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding makes no factual jury finding, does not assimilate every capital recommendation to a guilt verdict, does not decide that all instructional errors are harmless, and leaves broader capital-jury doctrine open. Legally invalid alternatives remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Griffin v. United States*, December 3, 1991, is extended narrowly through its factual-versus-legal distinction to this asserted inference from a general advisory recommendation. *Stromberg v. California*, 283 U.S. 359, and *Yates v. United States*, 354 U.S. 298, retain their rules for legally invalid alternatives. *Estelle v. McGuire*, December 4, 1991, retains whole-charge reasonable-likelihood review, which neither removes the preservation bar nor supplies an automatic inference of factual reliance here.

##### Whether the judge's express weighing of the evidentially unsupported coldness aggravator creates constitutional error

**Holding:** When a Florida capital sentencing judge expressly weighs an aggravator that the state supreme court holds unsupported by the evidence, that invalid factor creates Eighth Amendment error in the weighing decision. Lawful reweighing, constitutional harmless-error review or resentencing is required; other valid aggravators do not themselves cure the error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-B-1 of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; eight Justices. Scalia rejects the constitutional weighing-error premise.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for documented reliance on an unsupported aggravator.

**Material application:** The judge expressly found and weighed heightened coldness, which Florida held unproved. Proof of ordinary murder premeditation does not establish this additional aggravating requirement. Surviving death eligibility therefore does not eliminate the actual weighing error.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The convictions and mitigation findings are not disturbed. Removal of an aggravator does not itself require a life sentence or a new penalty jury. The no-mitigation finding does not mean no mitigating evidence was presented.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence requires a supported determination under *Chapman*, lawful reweighing if Florida law authorizes it, or resentencing. The separate holding below identifies why the existing appellate treatment fails to provide that correction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Clemons v. Mississippi*, 494 U.S. 738, requires adequate correction of capital weighing error. *Parker v. Dugger*, 498 U.S. 308, applies that principle to unsupported Florida aggravators and the actual sentencing record. *Stringer v. Black*, March 9, 1992, supplies the correction principle for this documented reliance; its habeas retroactivity and default reservations do not bar direct review. *Zant v. Stephens*, 462 U.S. 862, is distinguished because its nonweighing setting does not make surviving eligibility an automatic cure. *Chapman v. California*, 386 U.S. 18, supplies constitutional harmlessness with the State bearing the burden.

##### Whether Florida's proportionality discussion and surviving-factor count constitutionally cure the weighing error

**Holding:** A state appellate court affirming this sentence must substantively establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the invalid aggravator did not contribute to the sentence, or provide another authorized constitutional cure. Florida's existing proportionality discussion, count of surviving factors and mixed citations do not establish that inquiry; no verbal formula is either required or sufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts III-B-2 and IV of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Kennedy; six Justices. White and Thomas accept the error framework but find the existing review sufficient; Scalia would affirm on a separate ground.

**Material application:** Proportionality compares sentences across cases; it does not itself determine whether this invalid factor influenced this sentencing balance. Three valid factors and no found mitigation may bear on harmlessness but do not establish the missing inquiry. The State bears the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither undertakes reweighing nor determines the outcome of lawful correction. It requires no prescribed phrase and rejects a bare harmless-error label as sufficient. No automatic life sentence, mandatory new penalty jury, new mitigation finding or new habeas standard is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the judgment sustaining the death sentence and remand to Florida for supported *Chapman* review, lawful reweighing if Florida law permits, or resentencing. The convictions remain intact; the state courts must choose and perform an adequate lawful correction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Chapman v. California*, 386 U.S. 18, retains the State's burden to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute. *Clemons v. Mississippi*, 494 U.S. 738, and *Parker v. Dugger*, 498 U.S. 308, require corrective analysis of the sentence and actual record rather than proportionality alone. *Stringer v. Black*, March 9, 1992, preserves the inadequacy of factor counting and proportionality as standalone cures.

#### Morgan v. Illinois, 504 U.S. 719 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-5118.
**Decided:** June 15, 1992.

##### Whether an automatic-death juror is disqualified from a capital sentencing jury

**Holding:** When a State provides a jury to decide a capital sentence, due process requires an impartial jury. A juror who would automatically impose death upon conviction regardless of the facts and required consideration of mitigation is subject to challenge for cause; if such a juror serves and death is imposed, that sentence cannot be executed.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices adopt this proposition. Stone concurs in the judgment without a formal opinion join; Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Material application:** Illinois requires consideration of aggravation and mitigation. A juror who fixes death in advance regardless of the evidence refuses that task; the conclusion a qualified juror reaches after genuine consideration is different.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State need not provide jury sentencing, but a jury it supplies must be impartial. General support for capital punishment is not disqualifying, and the Court prescribes no weight for mitigation or required punishment. Witt's substantial-impairment standard remains intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The death-sentence affirmance is reversed and remanded, 7–2. The conviction is undisturbed; no automatic life sentence or constitutional right to jury sentencing is imposed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wainwright v. Witt, 469 U.S. 412 (1985), and Adams v. Texas, 448 U.S. 38 (1980): applied to views disabling lawful performance; their governing substantial-impairment rule is not replaced.
- Witherspoon v. Illinois, 391 U.S. 510 (1968): its impartiality principle applies to the opposite categorical disability; ordinary death-penalty views remain permissible.
- Ross v. Oklahoma, 487 U.S. 81 (1988): its automatic-death-juror principle is expressly adopted; its result rested on removal of the identified juror.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 (1978), and the received mitigation line: retained as requiring a real opportunity for individualized consideration, not a prescribed result.

##### Whether requested inquiry must expose automatic-death views despite general assurances of fairness and obedience

**Holding:** On a capital defendant’s request, voir dire must adequately inquire into whether prospective sentencing jurors would automatically impose death regardless of lawful consideration of the facts and mitigation. General promises of fairness and obedience to instructions did not cover that subject here; the denied inquiry requires reversal of the death-sentence affirmance and remand, without disturbing the conviction.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices adopt this proposition. Stone concurs in the judgment without a formal opinion join; Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Material application:** Generic assurances can conceal a prospective juror's belief that automatic death is fair. One juror's volunteered disqualifying answer and removal do not show adequate subject coverage for the others. The defendant need not prove answers to an inquiry he was forbidden to make.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No actual bias is found in a seated juror. Trial judges retain control over wording and follow-up, and no fixed questionnaire or new proof quantum is required. Unrelated voir dire omissions, collateral review and retroactivity remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The death sentence is set aside for lawful further sentencing proceedings without this defect. If Illinois uses a future capital jury, selection must comply with the inquiry rule; the Court does not require jury sentencing, order a guilt retrial or impose life imprisonment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wainwright v. Witt, 469 U.S. 412 (1985): its requirement of demonstrating bias through questioning supports the necessary opportunity to inquire.
- Mu’Min v. Virginia, 500 U.S. 415 (1991): distinguished; discretion over the detail of an inquiry does not authorize complete omission of this necessary subject.
- Lockhart v. McCree, 476 U.S. 162 (1986): its recognition of the opportunity to identify disqualified jurors is applied to the defendant’s corresponding challenge.
- Turner v. Murray, 476 U.S. 28 (1986): its protection against unacceptable capital-sentencing risk supports required subject coverage and sentence-only relief.

#### Espinosa v. Florida, 505 U.S. 1079 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7390.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

##### A legally influential recommendation cannot rest on an inadequately narrowed aggravator

**Holding:** When a weighing State requires its sentencing judge to give great weight to a jury's recommendation, an inadequately narrowed aggravating instruction to that jury can infect the final sentence even if the judge directly finds only valid aggravators. The especially wicked, evil, atrocious or cruel instruction given here lacks the guidance the Eighth Amendment requires; the judge's separate findings do not by themselves cure the recommendation's legal influence.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt the defect and corrective requirements. White favors argument and casts no present merits vote; Scalia dissents.

**Material application:** The jury returned an 11–1 general death recommendation under the unelaborated instruction. The judge gave operative weight to four aggravators and found two mitigators. No factor-specific jury finding is inferred; the required legal weight of the recommendation supplies the indirect channel of error.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 (1980), Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 (1988), and Shell v. Mississippi, 498 U.S. 1 (1990): apply their requirement of meaningful guidance for a capital aggravating category; the instruction here is insufficiently narrowed.
- Stringer v. Black, simulated March 9, 1992: applies the distinction between valid eligibility and constitutionally defective weighing; surviving valid aggravators alone do not remove the error.
- Sochor v. Florida, simulated June 8, 1992: applies its weighing-error rule while distinguishing its independent state preservation bar to a different jury claim and its separate factual-invalidity issue.
- Mills v. Maryland, 486 U.S. 367 (1988): applies the refusal to presume that jurors disregard an instruction's constitutionally defective route; no factor-specific verdict is invented.
- Walton v. Arizona, 497 U.S. 639 (1990): preserves genuine judicial sentencing and the presumption that judges apply legal narrowing; distinguishes a system in which a separate unguided recommendation carries required weight.
- Baldwin v. Alabama, 472 U.S. 372 (1985), and Spaziano v. Florida, 468 U.S. 447 (1984): preserve States' latitude in allocating sentencing responsibility; that latitude does not validate influential unguided weighing.
- Tedder v. State, 322 So.2d 908 (Fla. 1975), Smith v. State, 515 So.2d 182 (Fla. 1987), and Grossman v. State, 525 So.2d 833 (Fla. 1988): supply the state-law requirement to give great weight to jury recommendations of life or death.
- Smalley v. State, 546 So.2d 720 (Fla. 1989): rejects its premise that the jury's advisory designation alone makes this vague instruction constitutionally immaterial.

##### Correction must address the recommendation's influence on the death sentence

**Holding:** Reversal of the rejection of this sentencing claim requires further proceedings that remove the influence of the invalid jury recommendation through lawful reweighing or harmless-error review satisfying Chapman beyond a reasonable doubt. If no lawful cure is available, a new sentencing proceeding is required; valid remaining aggravators alone do not establish a cure, and neither an automatic life sentence nor disturbance of the guilt judgment follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt the defect and corrective requirements. White favors argument and casts no present merits vote; Scalia dissents.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No automatic reduction to life, retrial of guilt, adjudication of unrelated aggravators, factor-by-factor jury finding, or collateral-review gateway rule follows. The petition's severance and mitigation/time/funding/proportionality questions are not granted or adjudicated in this limited summary action; no separate denial of them is invented. No prior Supreme Court decision is overruled.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court grants limited review and reverses the rejection of the sentencing claim. Remand requires lawful reweighing or Chapman review addressing the recommendation's influence, and new sentencing if no lawful cure is available. Guilt and the ungranted severance and mitigation/time/funding/proportionality questions remain outside the disposition; no separate denial or automatic life sentence follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 (1990): applies lawful appellate reweighing or harmless-error correction; merely counting valid aggravators does not cure the error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applies harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt; no lesser standard or burden on the defendant is created.
- Stringer v. Black, simulated March 9, 1992, and Sochor v. Florida, simulated June 8, 1992: apply actual cure requirements to the recommendation's transmitted influence; no unreasoned harmlessness label suffices.
- Parker v. Dugger, 498 U.S. 308 (1991): applies review grounded in the real sentencing record and mitigation, not an assumed alternative record.

### Capital sentencing and habeas retroactivity

#### Stringer v. Black, 503 U.S. 222 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6616.
**Decided:** March 9, 1992.

##### Whether the Mississippi vague-aggravator claim seeks a new rule after February 19, 1985 finality

**Holding:** For a sentence final on February 19, 1985, Godfrey already prohibited the unguided heinousness aggravation later addressed in Maynard, and applying that protection to Mississippi’s weighing system does not create a new rule. Teague therefore does not bar consideration of this claim on the ground that Maynard or the corrective principles discussed in Clemons postdated finality.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding is confined to the old-rule application established by Godfrey for this finality date. No Teague exception or unresolved procedural-default defense is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit’s Teague disposition is reversed and remanded for consideration of the preserved procedural-default defense and adequate state correction. If relief otherwise becomes warranted, a conditional writ may permit lawful resentencing or authorized appellate correction. No unconditional release, automatic life sentence, or finding that a particular state cure failed is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288 — applied, not replaced: the claim seeks an old rule at the relevant finality date.
- Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied: both novelty of the later rule and novelty of its application must be considered.
- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 — applied: meaningful guidance was already required for the heinousness aggravator.
- Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 — treated as applying Godfrey’s old rule to equivalent language.
- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 — applied as identifying permissible correction, not a newly created protection in this setting.

##### What correction a constitutionally invalid aggravator requires in a capital weighing system

**Holding:** When a capital sentencer weighs a constitutionally invalid aggravating factor against mitigation, remaining valid aggravators do not by themselves cure the error. The state judicial system must supply genuine reweighing without the invalid factor, where authorized, or constitutional harmless-error review establishing that the invalid factor did not affect the sentence; the adequacy of any cure here remains for further proceedings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Genuine authorized reweighing and constitutional harmlessness are distinct corrective routes. The latter places on the State the burden of proving harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt. Remaining valid aggravators or bare proportionality review alone do not cure the error; death eligibility is not automatically negated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit’s Teague disposition is reversed and remanded for consideration of the preserved procedural-default defense and adequate state correction. If relief otherwise becomes warranted, a conditional writ may permit lawful resentencing or authorized appellate correction. No unconditional release, automatic life sentence, or finding that a particular state cure failed is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 — distinguished: its nonweighing holding does not automatically cure a weighing-system error.
- Barclay v. Florida, 463 U.S. 939 — distinguished: its actual review and state-law invalidity do not justify uncorrected constitutional aggravation.
- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 — applied: genuine appellate reweighing or constitutional harmlessness can correct a weighing error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 — applied to the harmless-error route: the State must establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.
- Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231 — distinguished: duplication in narrowing does not answer vague-factor weighting and cannot supply a prefinality reliance premise.

#### Gray v. Netherland, 518 U.S. 152

**Docket or dockets:** 95-6510
**Decided:** June 20, 1996

##### Did reopening the due-process claim also reopen the independently dismissed Brady ground?

**Holding:** The partial amendment reopening the identified Sorrell due-process claim while expressly preserving other dismissals did not reopen the independent Brady ground. That dismissal stays outside general availability or merits reconsideration, while related evidence remains usable to prove the distinct live preparation injury and its effect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part I, all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment. Dismissal left undisturbed, 9–0.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Shared evidence does not merge claims or satisfy separate procedural predicates. Reported uncontested state-habeas factual availability does not resolve trial counsel's disputed knowledge. No new suppression, materiality, appellate-abandonment or comprehensive state-default finding is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Kyles's collective lawful-use rule applies to available Brady claims and does not reopen dismissals. Picard, Anderson and Vasquez require claim-specific comparison; Coleman preserves established independent and adequate state claim defaults.

##### Is meaningful opportunity to answer this capital evidence a rule dictated at finality?

**Holding:** The established opportunity to deny or explain material capital-sentencing information is denied when supported State assurances and a materially different last-minute presentation deprive the defendant of a real opportunity to investigate and answer. This supported loss of protected function, rather than a fixed disclosure interval, invokes law dictated before October 5, 1987 finality and is not a new rule barred by Teague.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer; six. O'Connor's join does not extend to the final violation, effect or remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal notice period, automatic exclusion or relief for mere surprise is created. No Teague exception is invoked or abolished. Separate affirmative-deception formulations still require their own proper presentation and legal predicates.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gardner supplies the prefinality opportunity to respond; eventual public disclosure is not necessarily effective response. Weatherford's no-general-discovery rule remains. Graham supplies functional comparison, not new law inserted at finality; Teague and Caspari retain the dictated-at-finality inquiry and both contemporary exceptions for substantive restrictions and watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness.

##### Are presentation, denial of opportunity and prejudicial effect established here?

**Holding:** The reopened claim fairly encompasses the preparation injury, and accepted existing findings establish denial of the protected opportunity and prejudice in selecting death. Neither absence of a formal continuance request nor surviving vileness eligibility defeats relief on this record; no newly recast deception or dismissed Brady claim must first succeed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five.

**Material application:** State Claim IX, the state appeal and federal Ground A presented delayed disclosure, impaired preparation and unfairness. Accepted evidence supplements that same injury. The prosecutor's statements-only assurance, reasonable reliance and last-minute photographs, police and medical-examiner evidence impaired investigation, impeachment, objections and response. Actual preparation objections and exclusion requests were made; the absent formal continuance motion establishes no feasible cure or adequate bar. The findings establish substantial probability of a different sentence, substantial injurious influence, and inability to find beyond reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal collateral error-effect standard is selected because relief follows under both competing approaches. No structural-error category, automatic exclusion of all uncharged-crime evidence, other-perpetrator finding or universal exhaustion of related theories is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Picard and Anderson require claim substance, not identical language; Vasquez permits supplementation without fundamental alteration while retaining its deliberate-withholding reservation. Keeney retains Townsend's fifth hearing circumstance for inadequately developed material facts of a properly presented claim, subject to Fay's knowing, considered choice by the prisoner to bypass state process. Ordinary counsel negligence or counsel's choice without prisoner participation is not automatically bypass. Established deliberate bypass permits refusal of the otherwise required hearing rather than requiring denial; Coleman remains distinct. Tuggle separates lawful eligibility from actual sentence selection. Brecht's preserved-Doyle and O'Neal's personal-intent holdings retain their limits; Chapman and Kotteakos supply the two satisfied competing effect inquiries without a general extension.

##### How must conditional sentencing relief be implemented?

**Holding:** The established violation and prejudicial effect require reversal of the adverse due-process disposition and restoration and implementation of conditional death-sentence relief. Before an operative writ, any genuinely applicable, unadjudicated statutory condition material to relief must be resolved; once satisfied or inapplicable, Virginia must provide a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding within a reasonable prospective District Court period or a lawful noncapital disposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five. Reversed in part and remanded, 5–4, on this claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The established constitutional violation and effect are not returned for unrestricted reconsideration. Actually applicable amended §2254(d), state-fact-presumption or chapter 154 conditions require temporal and predicate analysis; §107(c)'s pending-case clause neither establishes Virginia qualification nor resolves chapter 153 transition. No express exhaustion waiver, statutory exception or satisfaction is presumed. The completed 1994 hearing differs from a new hearing; none is authorized or exempted categorically from §2254(e)(2). The reservation concerns actual legal conditions, not hypothetical impediments.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The old 180-day clock is neither reinstated nor declared expired. No guilt retrial, acquittal, fixed replacement sentence, release from independent lawful custody, new Supreme Court stay or execution authorization follows. Independent Brady, Smallwood, investigator-assistance, guilt-sufficiency and ungranted vileness-instruction rulings are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Graham and Simmons preserve sentence-only relief and unaffected guilt. Landgraf requires provision-specific temporal analysis; Keeney does not erase applicable later statutory restrictions. No general AEDPA temporal holding is made.

#### Lambrix v. Singletary

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-5658.
**Decided:** 1997-05-12.

##### The Court may decide the accepted nonretroactivity issue before the unresolved default defense

**Holding:** Procedural default and Teague nonretroactivity are distinct, nonjurisdictional limits, with no inflexible rule requiring one always to precede the other. In this case the Court decides the accepted Teague issue while reserving the disputed state-default predicates; that choice neither excuses default nor decides the constitutional merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State did not wholesale waive default. No jurisdictional defect is bypassed and neither universal default-first nor Teague-first sequencing is imposed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Stringer permits old-rule adjudication before unresolved default without prescribing sequence; Schiro preserves nonjurisdictional nonretroactivity and Caspari distinguishes presentation from subsidiary-defense preservation.

##### This claim invokes an old guidance rule at November 24, 1986 finality

**Holding:** For Lambrix's judgment final on November 24, 1986, the claim that inadequate aggravator guidance in Florida's legally consequential capital jury recommendation can infect the final death sentence invokes an already dictated rule. Teague therefore does not bar this claim as new merely because the judge separately must exercise independent judgment; the ruling establishes no actual instructional defect, harmful influence or failed cure on this record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins. O'Connor joins once Teague is reached despite disagreeing with sequence; Stone-Zsela takes no position on this question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Judicial independence remains an additional protection, not proof that the influential jury recommendation disappears. No actual defect, harmful transmission or failed cure is found, no new Teague exception is adopted, and current comparison cases are not treated as available at 1986 finality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Godfrey's prefinality meaningful-guidance duty, applied through Teague/Caspari's precise comparison and Stringer's functional analysis, governs. Espinosa's influential-recommendation rule remains distinct from this finality question. Spaziano and Baldwin preserve judicial sentencing without automatic cure; Zant's nonweighing and Barclay's state-law invalidity settings do not authorize uncured unconstitutional influence.

##### Vacatur returns the preserved default defense for resolution

**Holding:** The Eleventh Circuit's disposition of the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded for resolution of the preserved, unresolved state procedural-default defense and any applicable exception. If the state ground is adequate, independent, preserved and unexcused, relief must be denied on that ground; this common remand direction does not itself decide Teague, a constitutional violation or sentencing relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct joins at this common-remand scope.

**Material application:** Lambrix requested a limiting instruction at trial; the asserted state bar concerns failure to pursue the issue on direct appeal. State successive treatment of appellate-ineffectiveness claims and alternative merits rejection establish no federal cause finding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Coleman and Ford require the actual independent, adequate, preserved and unexcused state ground; Sawyer's clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway remains distinct from ordinary cause and prejudice and mere selection error.

##### The old-rule determination binds the subsequent proceedings

**Holding:** The Eleventh Circuit's disposition of the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded with the new-rule ground conclusively rejected. The lower court must first resolve the preserved, unresolved procedural-default defense; if the claim is barred and unexcused it must deny relief on that ground, while an available claim proceeds to actual merits, effect and lawful correction subject to genuinely applicable unresolved statutory conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III-B, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes for the complete binding mandate. Stone-Zsela joins common vacatur but not this old-rule instruction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Teague cannot again bar this claim as new. If default permits review, decide actual defect, transmitted effect and lawful cure. An established violation requires genuine authorized reweighing or Chapman harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt addressing the recommendation's influence; valid aggravators, proportionality review or an independent-judge label alone do not suffice. If no lawful cure is available and relief is otherwise warranted, a constitutionally adequate new sentencing proceeding is required, but none is now ordered.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Chapter 153 temporal reach, amended §2254(d), chapter 154 qualification and other genuinely applicable statutory conditions remain unresolved. No present writ, hearing, sentence vacatur, guilt retrial, release, stay or execution authorization issues; unrelated claims are untouched.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Espinosa and Stringer supply actual-cure requirements; Chapman supplies the State's harmlessness burden. Gray preserves genuinely applicable unresolved statutory gates before an operative writ.

#### O'Dell v. Netherland

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6867.
**Decided:** June 19, 1997.

##### The confined Simmons opportunity-to-answer claim is available against Teague on two independent grounds

**Holding:** When the State relies on future dangerousness to seek death and the only lawful noncapital sentence actually excludes ordinary parole, the defendant must have an effective opportunity to give the jury accurate information about that legal ineligibility. Gardner and Skipper dictated that limited protection before O'Dell's October 3, 1988 finality; Teague therefore does not bar the claim, although eligibility, violation and relief still require their own predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join the old-rule holding.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even if the parole-information protection were classified as new, Teague's watershed exception permits its application where future dangerousness is an operative basis for choosing death, the only lawful noncapital alternative excludes ordinary parole, and the defendant is denied an effective opportunity to correct the materially misleading release premise through accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction. The rule establishes retroactive availability in that confined category, not that O'Dell has proved those predicates or is presently entitled to sentencing relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct votes. O'Connor does not join; no Marks synthesis supplies either ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction may suffice; no fixed script or guarantee against clemency is required, and the State may prove danger within prison. Refusal of an instruction alone does not establish the watershed conjunction. The alternative establishes neither that Simmons is new nor that every capital procedure is watershed. It does not make an error structural or dispense with its consequence analysis.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Part III, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, vacates and remands. The Teague bar is foreclosed, but an operative sentencing writ requires actual legal parole ineligibility, lack of effective corrective opportunity, the applicable error consequence and genuinely applicable unresolved habeas conditions. The prior conditional order is not automatically reinstated. The conviction and unrelated claims remain undisturbed; no hearing, clock, release, stay or execution order follows.

**Material judgment:** Six Justices reject the Teague bar and set aside the adverse disposition; five support the exact vacatur mandate. Stone-Zsela instead favors reversal and restoration of the conditional-relief framework. No Justice grants an unconditional present writ.

**Material application:** O’Dell’s statements about sixteen years for a prior parole violation and never leaving prison remain part of the presentation but do not themselves establish accurate fixed-law information. The vileness aggravator and earlier prison murder do not automatically cure sentence-selection error.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gardner and Skipper supply the pre-1988 effective-response protection; Ramos and Caldwell do not authorize withholding the fixed ordinary-parole answer. Simmons retains its triggers and alternative means of informing the jury. Teague and Sawyer preserve distinct old-rule and watershed inquiries; Gideon and Crane supply a confined fair-adversarial-process analogy. Graham, Gray and Lambrix support functional comparison without adding later decisions to the 1988 law. Gilmore and Caspari preserve genuinely new procedural rules and both contemporary exceptions. Allridge’s ordinary-parole release predictions differ. Brecht and O’Neal retain category-specific Chapman holdings; Gray chose no universal collateral standard. This claim’s necessary standard must be resolved under those holdings or an outcome supported under both competing approaches; no harm result or universal prisoner-prejudice rule is assumed.

### Detention credit

#### United States v. Wilson, 503 U.S. 329 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1745.
**Decided:** March 24, 1992.

##### Who initially computes §3585(b) detention credit and when

**Holding:** Section 3585(b) does not authorize a district court to compute detention credit at sentencing; the Attorney General through BOP must make the initial computation after the federal sentence commences. Administrative review and proper subsequent judicial review remain available, so the calculation is not unreviewable discretion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A process capable of granting detention credit is distinct from McCarthy’s damages-only claim and compensation-incapable grievance procedure. The decision leaves proper administrative and judicial review available.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit’s direction to the sentencing court to award credit is reversed. Initial computation remains with the Attorney General through BOP after sentence commencement, subject to proper review; no days of credit or new detention order are awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Otte v. United States, 419 U.S. 43 — applied: Congress’s choice of verb tense informs statutory timing.
- Gwaltney of Smithfield v. Chesapeake Bay Foundation, 484 U.S. 49 — applied for the same temporal-text principle.
- Russello v. United States, 464 U.S. 16 — applied as a rebuttable inference, overcome here by the rewritten text and administration scheme.
- Robertson v. Railroad Labor Board, 268 U.S. 619 — applied: a departure from long-established procedure is not lightly inferred.
- McCarthy v. Madigan, March 4, 1992 — distinguished: its damages-only process could not provide the requested relief, unlike a credit computation process.

##### Whether detention already credited to another sentence may also receive federal credit

**Holding:** Section 3585(b) forbids federal detention credit for time already credited against another sentence when the federal sentence begins. A defendant cannot avoid that condition merely because the federal sentencing hearing occurred before the other court awarded its credit.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Tennessee credited 429 days before the federal term began, although after the federal sentencing hearing. The Court does not fix a numerical federal credit, define every form of official detention, or decide a challenge to a completed BOP calculation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit’s direction to the sentencing court to award credit is reversed. Initial computation remains with the Attorney General through BOP after sentence commencement, subject to proper review; no days of credit or new detention order are awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Turkette, 452 U.S. 576 — applied: avoid an arbitrary statutory result unsupported by the text’s purpose.

#### Reno v. Koray

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-790.
**Decided:** June 5, 1995.

##### Official detention and federal conditional release

**Holding:** Time under a federal Bail Reform Act conditional-release order is not official detention under § 3585(b), even when the restrictions are severe, so Koray’s release period requires no conditions-based credit inquiry. This rule concerns the presented federal release/detention distinction, preserves qualifying uncredited detention and judicial review, and does not decide state custody or require physical BOP control for every creditable day.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven directly adopt the status rule and its application without an independently sufficient administrative-deference holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mandatory credit remains for qualifying detention resulting from the offense sentenced or another charge for which arrest occurred after that offense, if not credited against another sentence. BOP initial computation and exhausted judicial review remain. State custody, a universal physical-BOP-control requirement and the unpresented bail-choice notice question are not decided. Administrative practice does not supply an independent deference holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Wilson, March 24, 1992: applies BOP initial computation, no duplicate credit and subsequent lawful review; Wilson did not itself define official detention.
- Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala, June 24, 1994: distinguishes qualified controlling weight for an agency’s own-regulation interpretation from a program statement construing a statute; its own-regulation rule remains intact.

### Guidelines departure review

#### Williams v. United States, 503 U.S. 193 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6297.
**Decided:** March 9, 1992.

##### Whether reliance on a forbidden departure factor incorrectly applies the Guidelines

**Holding:** A departure based on a factor that the Commission has forbidden, or has already fully considered in the applicable range, rests on an incorrect application of the Guidelines under §3742(f)(1). A policy statement expressly prohibiting a sentencing action is authoritative guidance in applying the relevant Guideline, and an arrest record itself is not a permissible departure ground here.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices support the proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The policy-statement holding concerns an express prohibition guiding the relevant Guideline, not every policy statement for every purpose. Whether nonsimilar old convictions justify departure is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence-affirmance judgment is vacated and remanded to the Seventh Circuit. It must decide causal harmlessness and independently reasonable extent on the whole record; if either required condition fails, it must remand for resentencing. The convictions remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- No earlier Supreme Court decision is overruled; this is a construction of §§3553(b), 3742(f)(1) and the applicable §4A1.3 prohibition.
- Griffin v. United States, December 3, 1991 — kept distinct: unsupported facts under a valid legal theory do not establish the rule for a prohibited sentencing factor.

##### What showing permits affirmance despite reliance on an invalid departure ground

**Holding:** Once the challenger establishes reliance on an invalid departure factor, remand is required unless the reviewing court determines from the whole record that the district court would have imposed the same sentence without that factor. The challenger need not additionally prove that the error determined the sentence; the party defending the sentence must persuade the reviewing court that the error was harmless.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices support the proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The inquiry asks whether this judge would have imposed the same sentence, not whether that sentence could lawfully have been imposed. Chapman’s constitutional proof standard is not adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence-affirmance judgment is vacated and remanded to the Seventh Circuit. It must decide causal harmlessness and independently reasonable extent on the whole record; if either required condition fails, it must remand for resentencing. The convictions remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 52(a) — applied as the governing harmless-error rule for this nonconstitutional sentencing error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 — not imported: the Court adopts no beyond-reasonable-doubt burden for this statutory error.

##### Whether departure reasonableness is separate from causal harmlessness

**Holding:** Even when an invalid departure ground was harmless under §3742(f)(1), a reviewing court must separately determine under §3742(f)(2) that the departure’s extent is reasonable in light of the valid grounds actually identified by the sentencing court. A reasonable sentence that the district court could have imposed does not establish that the erroneous factor was harmless.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices support the proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Review concerns valid grounds actually identified by the sentencing court; comments used to select a sentence after departure do not become new departure grounds. The lawful use of nonsimilar old convictions and the ultimate reasonable magnitude remain unresolved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence-affirmance judgment is vacated and remanded to the Seventh Circuit. It must decide causal harmlessness and independently reasonable extent on the whole record; if either required condition fails, it must remand for resentencing. The convictions remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Solem v. Helm, 463 U.S. 277 — applied for the primary sentencing role of the trial court, not to add a proportionality issue.

#### Koon v. United States; Powell v. United States, 518 U.S. 81

**Docket or dockets:** 94-1664 and 94-8842
**Decided:** June 13, 1996

##### How are departures under the 1992 Guidelines reviewed and classified?

**Holding:** Departure decisions receive abuse-of-discretion review, independently correcting legal error and unsupported grounds. Legal interpretation and permission are independently reviewed, historical findings stand unless clearly erroneous, and supported judgments of case-specific unusualness and degree receive substantial deference; under §3553(b), a circumstance inadequately considered in kind or degree must warrant the different sentence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), Part I. All nine join abuse-of-discretion review and correction of legal error and unsupported grounds. The eight Associates join the additional factor-classification, degree, unmentioned-factor and commentary methodology; Stone-Zsela does not join those additional propositions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Commission treatment is determined from Guidelines, policy statements and official commentary. Forbidden factors cannot support departure; encouraged factors may when not already accounted for; discouraged factors or encouraged factors already accounted for require exceptional degree or a supported distinguishing feature. When a guideline and adjustments account for a factor, §5K2.0 requires degree substantially exceeding the ordinary. Unmentioned factors require attention to the relevant guideline's structure and theory and the Guidelines as a whole, with unguided departures highly infrequent. Rarity alone does not justify departure. Just punishment, deterrence, public protection, training, medical care and correctional treatment remain relevant without additional categorical bans beyond the Commission's scheme. Legal or calculation errors receive no deference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Stinson preserves authoritative interpretive commentary unless unconstitutional, contrary to statute, inconsistent with the Guideline or plainly erroneous; commentary cannot amend contrary text. Cooter & Gell and Pierce support task-sensitive deference with legal correction; Ornelas does not prescribe independent review of every sentencing application. Rivera informs the categories without imposing a mandatory sequence or displacing Williams.

##### Did victim misconduct support the separate five-level departure?

**Holding:** Under §5K2.10, wrongful victim conduct significantly provoking offense behavior may support a reduction, with extent governed by the provision's qualifications. The complete findings support both this ground and five levels despite the end of resistance and danger before the force became criminal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II, all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela agrees in this component's judgment only. Application and extent are sustained, 9–0.

**Material application:** Significant earlier provocation and transition from lawful to unlawful force within seconds distinguish the conduct from typical aggravated assaults under color of law, including unprovoked assaults; the six-level official-conduct addition covers both. This is more than chronological causation and does not excuse the crime or find continuing danger.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Extent considers comparative size, strength and relevant physical characteristics; persistence and efforts to avoid confrontation; reasonably perceived danger, including violent reputation; actual danger; and other victim conduct substantially contributing to danger. Ended danger remains material but does not make prior provocation instantly irrelevant. Victim misconduct ordinarily does not justify the provision for criminal sexual abuse and usually does not make it relevant to nonviolent offenses; unusual substantial misconduct, such as extended provocation and harassment leading to property retaliation, may support mitigation in a nonviolent case. No later-added factor applies.

##### Which employment and criminal-history grounds are unavailable here?

**Holding:** Loss of public employment and law-enforcement prospects following these official civil-rights offenses is adequately contemplated by the relevant guideline and does not justify departure here. A first offender already in category I cannot depart below its lower range because recidivism risk is exceptionally low: §4A1.3 expressly includes the first offender with the lowest risk.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III-A rejects employment consequences with eight joins, all except Stevens. Part III-B's recidivism holding is unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The employment holding is not a categorical ban on every unusual job consequence or an equation with socioeconomic status. Section 5H1.5 makes employment record not ordinarily relevant outside the range; separate use in probation or supervised-release conditions does not authorize a prison-range reduction. Section 5H1.11 likewise discourages military, civic, charitable or public service, employment contributions and prior good works; §5H1.10 separately excludes race, sex, national origin, creed, religion and socioeconomic status. Category I's express lower bound cannot be bypassed by unusually favorable predictions, but independently valid circumstances in another departure inquiry remain available.

##### May the supported prison vulnerability and successive-prosecution burdens be considered?

**Holding:** The sentencing court may consider the extraordinary vulnerability supported by the combination of these defendants' police status and exceptional notoriety, and its finding of a significant burden from lengthy state proceedings followed by federal prosecution for the same conduct. Police status or lawful succession alone creates no entitlement, and neither permitted ground independently establishes three levels or any particular sentence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Parts III-C and III-D, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five on each component. Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer oppose both.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Extraordinary publicity distinguishes these defendants without establishing inevitable continuous solitary confinement, occupational preference or mitigation for every notorious crime. Lawful separate-sovereign authority does not erase a genuinely unusual, inadequately considered burden. No double-jeopardy violation, prosecutorial misconduct or automatic benefit for two sovereign prosecutions is found. Permission remains subject to valid grounds and independently reasonable extent.

##### What follows when a combined departure relies on invalid factors?

**Holding:** After invalid-factor reliance is established, the sentence defender must show from the whole record that the judge would have imposed the same sentence without it; the extent must independently be reasonable on valid reasons the judge actually identified. That showing is absent, requiring district-court resentencing while retaining the separately sustained five levels.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part IV, unanimous.

**Material application:** The judge combined four considerations, said none alone justified three levels, and did not determine that the two surviving grounds would yield the same sentences. Petitioners defending those sentences failed their burden; the challenger need not establish changed outcome. Valid factors individually insufficient may combine, but cannot preserve invalid factors or replace explanation of reasonable extent.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both dockets are affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded. Sentence vacatur remains, 9–0; neither 30-month sentence is reinstated. No replacement term is set, and convictions and the bodily-injury adjustment remain outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Williams, March 9, 1992, is applied unchanged: actual whole-record harmlessness of invalid reliance and a separate reasonable-extent inquiry are necessary. An appellate court cannot invent replacement reasons or rely on mere possibility of reimposing the same length.

**Docket or dockets:** 95-5661
**Decided:** June 17, 1996

##### What Government request authorizes a sentence below a statutory minimum?

**Holding:** Section 3553(e) requires Government request or consent to below-minimum sentencing to reflect substantial assistance in investigating or prosecuting another offender. ### Capital sentencing and correction of weighing error

#### Richmond v. Lewis, 506 U.S. 40 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7094.

**Decided:** December 1, 1992.

##### An actual cure is necessary

**Holding:** When a death sentence in a State that weighs aggravation against mitigation rests on an unconstitutionally vague aggravating factor, remaining valid factors do not alone cure the error. **Holding:** On the parties’ premise that a majority of the Arizona Supreme Court had to cast valid curative votes, this sentence cannot stand: even assuming the two Justices retaining the narrowed aggravator lawfully reweighed, the two other affirming Justices relied conclusively on the remaining criminal history without a new balance of mitigation. **Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: eight votes. Thomas also writes separately; Scalia dissents. No rule demanding identical reasoning from every member of a state appellate majority is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Walton v. Arizona, 497 U.S. 639 (1990), and Lewis v. Jeffers, 497 U.S. 764 (1990), preserved: narrowing constructions and appropriately reviewed state applications may be valid, but do not supply a curative vote absent here. State v. Gretzler, 135 Ariz.42, 659 P.2d1 (1983), remains the state narrowing source; its proper application by Holohan and Hays is not decided. Clemons and the operative weighing-error decisions require the stated remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Arizona is a weighing State. A general duty of independent review is not actual reweighing; no prescribed verbal formula is required, and a federal habeas court cannot perform the missing state sentencing balance. The validity of the Holohan–Hays narrowing and reweighing is assumed, not decided. The accepted state-majority premise establishes no requirement that every curative vote use identical reasoning.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the denial of sentence relief and remand for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutional correction or vacation of death and imposition of a lawful lesser sentence. The conviction is untouched; no automatic life sentence is imposed.

### Effective capital mitigation under Texas special issues

#### Graham v. Collins, 506 U.S. 461 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7580.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality

**Holding:** Teague does not bar Graham's claim that Texas's submitted sentencing questions prevented effective use of his youth and unstable background as material mitigation independent of deliberateness or future danger. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated that functional requirement before his conviction became final on September 10, 1984; no new procedural-rule exception is created.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this prefinality-law holding, its application to youth and background, and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288, and Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied; the relevant result must be dictated at finality, not merely supported by a later plausible extension.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — preserved as qualified approval; it does not immunize every application of the special issues.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied as the pre-1984 effective-mitigation rule.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied as recognition of that existing rule, not as law inserted retroactively into 1984.
- Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992 — applied at its existing Teague scope; disagreement alone does not establish novelty.
- Franklin v. Lynaugh, 487 U.S. 164, and Saffle v. Parks, 494 U.S. 484 — distinguished; evidence whose mitigating use fits the questions, and unrestricted sympathy, differ from material culpability mitigation excluded by them.

##### Youth requires an effective route for its independent mitigating force

**Holding:** When the record supports a material mitigating judgment about culpability that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the submitted mechanism prevents that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Graham's youth at seventeen has that independent significance notwithstanding deliberate conduct or predicted future danger, and the three submitted special issues provide no adequate route for it here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground for sentencing relief and its conditional remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied to youth's independent culpability significance; jurors may assign weight but cannot be legally prevented from giving it effect.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — its conditional framework remains; this defective application does not establish facial invalidity.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied beyond a permanent-handicap limitation; a mitigating trait need not have only aggravating relevance to future danger to require an effective vehicle.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 — applied as the reasonable-likelihood method of assessing instructional operation, not as the source of a new substantive rule at 1984 finality.

##### The submitted background evidence also requires effective consideration

**Independent alternative holding:** A capital sentencing system may not prevent a jury from acting on the material culpability significance it finds in a defendant's disturbed upbringing merely because that evidence also bears on future dangerousness or lacks a uniquely severe permanent handicap. The evidence of Graham's mother's recurring mental illness and hospitalizations and his shifting family care supports such a mitigating judgment, which the submitted special issues cannot adequately express; this independently requires the same sentencing remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Parts III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independent background-evidence ground and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 — applied to relevant background proffered as a basis for less than death.
- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied; disturbed upbringing cannot be excluded from mitigating effect as a matter of law.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied; neither a uniquely severe permanent handicap nor complete absence of relevance to the special issues is an exclusive trigger.

##### Positive character as an independent ground

**Authority of the judgment:** The separate contention that Graham's household assistance, religious observance and financial support of his children independently require additional sentencing relief is rejected by all nine Justices, on different grounds. Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter regard its asserted independent culpability significance as insubstantial on this record and its future-behavior use as available under the second issue; White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would bar the requested mitigation relief under Teague. Stone-Zsela joins the denial of this independent component in the judgment only. These grounds do not yield a common general rule or a Marks subset; the youth and background holdings independently require sentencing relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule about positive character evidence or its value apart from the asserted culpability and future-danger uses is decided. The jury remains free to hear constitutionally relevant evidence at the new sentencing proceeding. Neither denial of this independent ground nor the sentencing remedy disturbs the murder conviction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere admission or some future-danger relevance is insufficient when supported culpability mitigation cannot receive effect. The ruling requires neither favorable weight nor a categorical fourth question, finds no psychiatric disability, and imposes no juvenile-execution prohibition or facial invalidity of the scheme. Texas’s later statute is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse sentencing relief denial on youth and background and remand for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate sentencing or a lawful noncapital replacement. Preserve the conviction; no automatic acquittal or immediate release.

### Evidentiary predicates for capital instructions

#### Delo v. Lashley, 507 U.S. 272 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-409.

**Decided:** March 8, 1993.

##### An evidentiary predicate for the requested mitigating circumstance

**Holding:** The Constitution does not require a capital sentencer to receive an instruction on a particular mitigating circumstance when no evidence supports that circumstance; Missouri may require some supporting evidence for its no-significant-prior-criminal-history instruction while leaving the sentencer free to consider and give effect to mitigation actually presented. This production requirement neither requires exhaustive proof of a negative, compels the defendant to testify, authorizes an adverse inference from silence, nor reduces the State's burden to establish aggravating circumstances.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt the rule and reversal of this ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586; Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104; Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302: applied to preserve meaningful consideration of supported mitigation; they do not require this unsupported instruction.
- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993: preserved in its actual controlling scope; supported youth/background culpability evidence must receive effect, without presuming a distinct mitigating fact.
- Hopper v. Evans, 456 U.S. 605: applied by analogy for an evidentiary predicate, not for an obligation to submit unsupported alternatives.
- Walton v. Arizona, 497 U.S. 639: applied to the permitted allocation of mitigation proof, subject to the State's undiminished aggravation burden; Missouri's lesser some-evidence requirement is all that is adjudicated here.

##### The proposed innocence instruction does not sustain this judgment

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming that a presumption of innocence of other offenses applies at capital sentencing, its existence does not automatically require an instruction; the relevant circumstances must create a genuine danger that jurors will attribute other criminal conduct to the defendant on a basis other than lawful evidence. The silent criminal-history record, undiminished reasonable-doubt instructions on aggravation, and absence of a circumstance creating that danger do not require such an instruction here; the Court does not decide the general existence or scope of that sentencing presumption.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices expressly adopt this alternative response. Stone-Zsela and Souter do not join or reach it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Taylor v. Kentucky, 436 U.S. 478: applied for the danger of a verdict based on extraneous considerations, while preserving its context-sensitive instruction rule.
- Kentucky v. Whorton, 441 U.S. 786: applied for the totality-of-circumstances inquiry and rejection of an automatic instruction requirement.
- Herrera v. Collins, January 25, 1993: not extended; its actual record-specific rejection of federal relief supplies no categorical rule extinguishing innocence protections for other purposes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Silence does not prove a no-significant-history mitigating fact. No actual history is found from material the jury did not hear; youth is a different mitigating circumstance. The alternative innocence-instruction holding assumes the protection without deciding its existence or full scope. Strickland merits and the independently rejected defaulted counsel claim are not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse conditional habeas relief on the instruction ground and remand. No counsel-effectiveness or innocence finding, new stay or execution order issues.

### Sentencing for trial perjury

#### United States v. Dunnigan, 507 U.S. 87 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1300.

**Decided:** February 23, 1993.

##### Constitutional permission and the Guideline’s consequence

**Holding:** A sentence increase under U.S.S.G. § 3C1.1 for independently established trial perjury—willfully false testimony under oath or affirmation about a material matter—does not violate the defendant’s right to testify. Once the court properly determines that the defendant committed such perjury, the applicable Guideline requires its two-level obstruction adjustment; neither exercising the trial right nor a guilty verdict alone supplies that predicate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices; all nine Justices adopt this proposition, its explanation and its record application, so it controls directly.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Nix v. Whiteside, 475 U.S. 157 (1986), applied to the right’s boundary: the right to present testimony includes no entitlement to perjury; the decision does not authorize assuming falsehood from an unsuccessful defense.
- United States v. Grayson, 438 U.S. 41 (1978), applied: actual willful trial falsehood may inform sentencing; its rehabilitation discussion is not an exclusive justification and its warning against reflex increases remains operative.

##### Perjury predicates, independent findings and application

**Holding:** When a defendant objects to a § 3C1.1 increase based on trial testimony, the court must review the evidence and independently find willfully false sworn testimony concerning a material matter, excluding confusion, mistake and faulty memory. Separate clear findings for each element are preferable, but a single determination encompassing all the factual predicates is sufficient; the district court’s findings here meet that standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices; all nine Justices adopt this proposition, its explanation and its record application, so it controls directly.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Grayson, 438 U.S. 41 (1978), applied with its safeguard intact: a sentencing response requires an actual assessment of deliberate false testimony, not an automatic consequence of conviction.
- Nix v. Whiteside, 475 U.S. 157 (1986), left intact in its factual boundary: absence of a protected right to perjury does not establish that disputed testimony was perjurious.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No separate perjury prosecution or special verbal formula is required. Neither a guilty verdict nor rejection of a truthful defense establishes perjury. No general proof quantum, universal Guidelines-commentary rule or separate actual-obstruction element from summary-contempt law is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit’s categorical constitutional prohibition and remand; the district court’s independent finding encompasses the required perjury predicates.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-8685.

##### Consistent interpretive commentary is authoritative

**Holding:** Guidelines commentary interpreting or explaining a guideline is authoritative unless it violates the Constitution or a federal statute, or is inconsistent with, or a plainly erroneous reading of, that guideline. ##### The possession exclusion is a permissible interpretation, with temporal application reserved

**Holding:** Amendment 433’s exclusion of unlawful firearm possession by a felon is a permissible interpretation of the crime-of-violence guideline and controls defendants to whom it applies. The appellate judgment sustaining a categorical possession rule is vacated; the governing-version and temporal objection remains for remand, together with properly presented independent sentencing grounds and the consequences of any guideline error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand for temporal applicability, lawful independent sentencing grounds and consequences of any guideline error. No specified replacement sentence or reopening of final cases.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Seminole Rock, 325 U.S. 410: applies the consistency test to Amendment 433.
- Williams, March 9, 1992: preserves statutory error and remand analysis; no automatic replacement sentence.

### Parole consideration and prospective relief

#### Cavanaugh v. Roller

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1510.

**Decided:** 1993-11-30.

For the other questions in Cavanaugh v. Roller, 1993-11-30, No. 92-1510, see Federal Courts — A live claim for the outstanding hearing; Civil Rights — Section 1983 permits this opportunity-only claim.

##### The binding loss of annual reconsideration increases punishment on this record

**Holding:** The Ex Post Facto Clause forbids applying to a pre-enactment offender a later statute that removes every alternate mandatory parole-reconsideration opportunity by doubling the post-denial interval from twelve to twenty-four months, where ordinary parole depends on consideration and no retained ordinary means of obtaining the intervening review is established. This binding loss of a meaningful release opportunity makes punishment more onerous despite unchanged initial eligibility, maximum sentence, and ultimate parole discretion; the prisoner need not prove that the omitted hearing would have produced release.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; six Justices adopt the rule, its application to Roller, and the prospective remedy. Ginsburg's conditional agreement in her separate opinion adds no vote to this controlling coalition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule concerns compulsory loss of every alternate ordinary hearing, not every procedural disadvantage. The State's separate medical power requires a condition associated with life expectancy of no more than one year and is no substitute for general intervening consideration; it has no established application here. The ten-consecutive-years report before release is distinct from hearing frequency. Individual postponement supported by findings, meaningful interim-review systems, voting rules, suitability criteria, abolition of parole and the reach of extraordinary clemency remain undecided. Neither a general probability formula nor proof of the omitted hearing's outcome is required. Act No. 184's January 1 transition does not itself establish later mootness or supersession of this federal holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Weaver v. Graham, 450 U.S. 24 (1981): extends its protection of a legally available avenue to reduced imprisonment to the binding loss of this mandatory consideration opportunity; no vested entitlement to release is required.
- Miller v. Florida, 482 U.S. 423 (1987): applies its distinction between binding adverse legal changes and flexible guidance; unchanged outer limits or a possibly identical outcome are not sufficient answers.
- Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37 (1990): applies its punishment-related limitation and rejects a free-standing rule against every procedural disadvantage; no broader substantial-rights theory is restored.
- Dobbert v. Florida, 432 U.S. 282 (1977): distinguishes permissible procedural alteration from the binding withdrawal of a meaningful opportunity for earlier release on this record.
- Griffin v. State, 315 S.C. 285 (1993): treats its adoption of Roller's analysis and overruling of Gunter v. State, 298 S.C. 113 (1989), as confirming state authority, not an independent substitute for this Court's federal analysis.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's prospective-relief judgment is affirmed, 6–3. Its remand for a declaration restoring annual reconsideration remains in force; the district court may issue an injunction only if necessary to secure consideration. No release, damages, immunity reconsideration, contempt finding or specific hearing date is ordered. The unappealed official- and individual-capacity damages dismissals remain undisturbed.

**Later-authority backlinks:** South Carolina Act No. 184, effective January 1, 1994: offense-date classification and savings/transition terms; no later mootness, completed hearing, judgment vacatur or displacement of the federal holding is established. California Department of Corrections v. Morales, April 25, 1995: applies the protected-opportunity rule to the retrospective three-year postponement supported by individualized findings on the present record. It does not decide every possible interim-review system; the original compulsory biennial-review holding remains unchanged. See Criminal Procedure — Retrospective loss of parole consideration.

### Probation revocation and lenity

#### United States v. Granderson, 511 U.S. 39

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1662.

**Decided:** 1994-03-22.

##### The former proviso requires two to six months’ imprisonment on this record

**Holding:** When controlled-substance possession triggers former §3565(a)'s mandatory probation-revocation proviso on an ordinary record with an initially available zero-to-six-month Guidelines imprisonment range and no established lawful departure, the replacement imprisonment term must be at least one-third of the range's six-month maximum and no more than that maximum: two to six months here. **Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; six Justices support this complete construction, independently of Scalia's and Kennedy's judgment concurrences.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. R. L. C., 503 U.S. 291 (1992): its recognition of a lawful Guidelines ceiling informs the distinct revocation context; its juvenile minimum and interpretive divisions are not transplanted.
- Williams v. United States, 503 U.S. 193 (1992): preserved for actual lawful departure grounds; no departure is invented to sustain twenty months.
- Stinson v. United States, 508 U.S. 36 (1993): preserved; commentary cannot override a federal statute or resolve the proviso by contrary text.

**Independent alternative holding:** After examination of the former proviso's text, sentencing structure and companion enactment, any remaining genuine uncertainty about the Government's proposed conversion of sixty probation months into a twenty-month prison minimum must be resolved against that greater punishment. This residual-lenity ground independently rejects the Government's construction; it neither makes every competing reading ambiguous nor supplies a universal lowest-number rule for different sentencing records.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; the same six Justices adopt this independently sufficient reason to reject the Government's twenty-month construction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336 (1971): applied for fair warning and clear legislative authorization of punishment.
- Bifulco v. United States, 447 U.S. 381 (1980): applied to genuine uncertainty about the authorized penal consequence.
- Chapman v. United States, 500 U.S. 453 (1991): preserved; interpretive disagreement alone does not activate lenity.
- Ratzlaf v. United States, January 11, 1994: its residual-lenity principle is applied to this distinct statutory ambiguity, without importing its offense-specific knowledge requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Materially different original ranges and probation following a substantial downward departure remain unresolved, including the denominator and any distinct lenity question. An actual lawful departure is necessary; no later statutory amendment or probation-to-prison exchange rate is applied.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eleventh Circuit is affirmed, 8–1. Its vacatur of twenty months' imprisonment and release order remain operative, and revocation remains intact. ### Sentencing and collateral attacks

#### Custis v. United States, 511 U.S. 485

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5209.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### The ACCA supplies no general collateral-review procedure at sentencing

**Holding:** Under 18 U.S.C. §924(e), a defendant may not use the federal sentencing proceeding to litigate these constitutional attacks on operative state convictions for ineffective assistance and involuntary counseled pleas. The statute does not supply a general collateral-review procedure; the distinct exception for an unconstitutional complete denial of appointed counsel remains, as do objections to whether the enhancement itself satisfies its statutory requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct joins to this statutory ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lewis v. United States, 445 U.S. 55 (1980): applies the distinction between existing conviction status and an unresolved collateral challenge, without extending its civil-disability rationale to every sentencing defect.
- Beecham v. United States, May 16, 1994: preserves the convicting-jurisdiction selector for §921(a)(20), without adding an ACCA collateral-review procedure.

##### The constitutional counsel exception and the proper review channel

**Holding:** The Constitution does not require the ACCA sentencing court to hear Custis's ineffective-assistance and involuntary-counseled-plea attacks, but a conviction obtained through complete denial of appointed counsel in violation of the constitutional entitlement, without valid waiver, may be challenged there. Custis may pursue available review of the state convictions; successful invalidation permits an application concerning the enhancement through an authorized procedure, without a promised reopening or particular result.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same six Justices join O'Connor's separate constitutional ground and its express remedial reservation; the three dissenters would allow the present attacks under their statutory construction.

**Current-force modification:** Daniels v. United States, April 25, 2001, resolves part of the expired-custody reservation for section 2255: expired counseled state predicates are not reopened for involuntary-plea or ineffective-assistance claims merely because they enhance federal punishment when meaningful original review was available and no external impediment is identified. Complete unconstitutional deprivation of required counsel remains challengeable subject to applicable procedure and adverse enhancement effect. Faultless loss of meaningful review has no controlling resolution; Custis's sentencing rule and its conditional authorized route following independent invalidation remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gideon v. Wainwright, 372 U.S. 335 (1963): preserves the constitutional appointed-counsel entitlement and valid-waiver boundary.
- Burgett v. Texas, 389 U.S. 109 (1967), and United States v. Tucker, 404 U.S. 443 (1972): preserve the exception for unconstitutional complete counsel deprivation; they do not require the broader sentencing forum sought here.
- Parke v. Raley, December 1, 1992: preserves rebuttable regularity and lawful collateral proof rules without deciding the availability of this forum.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 921(a)(20)'s business-regulation exclusions and state-misdemeanor exclusion for offenses punishable by two years or less remain distinct from collateral review. A conviction expunged, set aside, pardoned or followed by civil-rights restoration is excluded under the convicting jurisdiction's law unless the pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibits shipping, transporting, possessing or receiving firearms. No removal event is established here. Expired-custody and no-meaningful-review cases remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit is affirmed, 6–3, leaving the 235-month sentence intact against these attacks. Original-conviction review and a possible authorized application after successful invalidation remain available under their own rules. No stay, automatic reopening or fixed sentence reduction is ordered.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Daniels v. United States, No. 99-9136, April 25, 2001: available-original-review section 2255 restriction and complete-counsel exception, with faultless-review question unresolved.

### Uncounseled misdemeanors at sentencing

#### Nichols v. United States, 511 U.S. 738

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8556.

**Decided:** 1994-06-06.

##### This Guidelines use of a valid fine-only misdemeanor is permissible

**Authority of the judgment:** The Sixth Circuit's sentence is affirmed, 5–4, by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. No complete controlling rationale exists under Marks. The express shared permission below does not make Souter a fifth vote for general overruling of Baldasar or for the plurality's warning rule.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** An uncounseled misdemeanor conviction valid under Scott because no imprisonment was imposed may supply the otherwise authorized criminal-history point in the Guidelines setting presented here: the later prosecution is counseled and the sentencing framework provides a genuine opportunity to contest reliability, relevance and significant overrepresentation through its lawful procedures, including a permitted §4A1.3 departure. This limited permission neither makes a departure mandatory nor adopts a general rule for a scheme that conclusively requires enhanced incarceration without that opportunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's plurality, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, permits this application under a broader valid-conviction rule; Souter's concurrence in the judgment expressly permits this Guidelines application on the narrower reliability-and-opportunity ground. Five Justices support the stated permission at this level; no complete rationale has five joins, and Souter supplies no fifth vote to overrule Baldasar generally.

**Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Under the applicable November 1990 Guidelines, §4A1.1(c) supplies one point per otherwise includable prior sentence outside (a) and (b), capped at four points; (a) gives three for imprisonment over one year and one month, and (b) two for imprisonment of at least sixty days not within (a). A prior sentence follows adjudication of guilt for conduct outside the instant offense. Section 4A1.2(e) counts over-one-year-and-one-month imprisonment imposed within fifteen years of offense commencement or producing incarceration during that period; other sentences count if imposed within ten years; older sentences outside those rules are excluded. Commencement includes relevant conduct; separate juvenile periods are not decided. Note 5 expressly counts DUI rather than minor traffic offenses; no other offense-classification or timing objection is presented. Note 6 excludes already reversed, vacated or constitutionally invalidated convictions without forbidding lawful consideration of underlying conduct. Section 4A1.3 permits departure on reliable information that the category significantly overrepresents seriousness or future-crime likelihood, using a lower category as reference, but not below Category I's lower limit on that ground. A prior arrest record itself cannot support the departure inquiry. Nichols made no such departure request. Point arithmetic is not optional, departure is not mandatory, and no independent proof of the DUI conduct or hypothetical departure is found.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Scott v. Illinois, 440 U.S. 367 (1979): preserves the actual-imprisonment boundary for the original appointed-counsel requirement; the fine-only conviction is treated as valid.
- Baldasar v. Illinois, 446 U.S. 222 (1980): its particular judgment and fractured reasoning remain; only four Justices vote for general overruling, and the Court permits the present Guidelines application without deciding a conclusive mandatory-enhancement scheme.
- Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 (1977): does not convert Blackmun's original-invalidity rationale in Baldasar into five votes for the valid-but-unavailable rationale, or make four present overruling votes five.
- Stinson v. United States, May 3, 1993: preserves commentary's authority subject to constitutional, statutory and Guideline limits; commentary does not itself answer the constitutional objection.
- Custis v. United States, May 23, 1994: distinguishes the ACCA collateral-attack channel from this challenge to a Scott-valid conviction's subsequent use; the counsel-deprivation exception is unchanged.
- Parke v. Raley, December 1, 1992, and Godinez v. Moran, June 24, 1993: preserve their distinct proof/regularity and actual-waiver rules; neither establishes waiver or nonindigency here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No complete rationale commands five votes under Marks. Souter's express permission for the defined Guidelines application is within the plurality's broader permission, but the four do not adopt his conditions as universally necessary. Baldasar's judgment and fractured authority remain; four votes do not overrule it or establish the plurality's broad future-enhancement-warning rule. This is not Custis's challenge to constitutional validity, and no waiver or nonindigency finding is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is affirmed, 5–4. The 235-month sentence and eight-year supervised-release term remain undisturbed; no DUI-point deduction, vacation of the Georgia fine, new hearing, release or fixed 25-month reduction is ordered. The waiver and indigency alternatives are not decided.

### Capital selection factors

#### Tuilaepa v. California and Proctor v. California, 512 U.S. 967

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-5131 (Tuilaepa) and 93-5161 (Proctor).

**Decided:** 1994-06-30.

##### Relevant individualized selection does not require fixed labels or numerical weights

**Holding:** On the unchallenged eligibility premise presented here, the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments permit consideration of understandable, relevant subjects for individualized penalty selection without requiring every consideration to be a yes-or-no factual element, exclusively aggravating or mitigating, or assigned a fixed numerical weight. This rule does not authorize forbidden or wholly irrelevant adverse considerations, exclusion of effective mitigation, or disregard of a separately established selection error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight directly adopt this bounded rule; Stevens and Ginsburg do not join Part I's additional framework discussion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Lawful eligibility narrowing and individualized selection perform distinct functions under Gregg, Woodson and Lowenfield. Arave's authoritative limitation on an eligibility aggravator remains necessary in its setting. Eligibility does not cure a later unconstitutional weighing factor under Stringer and Espinosa. Romano's separate-death-decision responsibility rule and Simmons's future-danger/parole-ineligibility rebuttal rule retain their distinct operation. This decision neither extends nor retreats from those rules and does not approve the entire California capital scheme.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983): applied to relevant individualized selection after unchallenged narrowing; it supplies no exemption for forbidden adverse considerations.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 (1978), and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 (1982): preserved; relevant mitigation must have effective consideration, without guaranteed favorable weight.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 (1990): applied to contextual review and individualized moral judgment, not mechanical factor counting.
- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993, and Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: distinguished in application; their effective independent-mitigation requirement remains fully operative.

##### The circumstances-of-the-crime factor survives both presented challenges

**Holding:** Section 190.3(a), directing consideration when relevant of the circumstances of the crime of conviction and the existence of special circumstances found true under §190.1, identifies an understandable and relevant selection subject and is not unconstitutional on the facial and instructional theories presented by either petitioner. The Court affirms each judgment on that ground without adjudicating §190.2 eligibility or any independently preserved claim of actual forbidden use.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight find no error on each docket's factor-(a) challenge; none of these joins is a harmlessness vote.

**Material application:** Both factor-(a) challenges fail without harmless-error review. The judgment does not decide unrelated guilt-phase comments, every other factor, racial discrimination, the lawfulness of treating absence of a minor-accomplice role as aggravation, or the whole California eligibility scheme. No actual juror mental state is found.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 (1980), and Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 (1988): distinguished; their vague pejorative aggravators differ from identification of the actual crime's circumstances.
- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983): applied; relevant individualized consideration does not require an exclusive aggravating or mitigating label for every circumstance.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 (1990): applied to each complete reported charge; no ordinary word or boilerplate direction automatically cures an actual contradiction.

##### The violent-criminal-activity factor is sufficiently definite in Tuilaepa

**Holding:** Section 190.3(b), when relevant, permits consideration of the presence or absence of criminal activity involving use or attempted use of force or violence, or an express or implied threat to use force or violence. As construed to concern punishable offenses involving force or threatened force against a person, this is an understandable selection subject and defeats Tuilaepa's presented vagueness challenge; Proctor presents no factor-(b) question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight directly sustain factor (b) on no-error grounds; Blackmun dissents on this factor as well as the others.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The trial instruction's proof burden is a record fact, not a new statutory or universal constitutional rule. This holding supplies no finding about an unreported act, no general permission to use unreliable or forbidden evidence, and no factor-(b) ruling in Proctor.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983): applied to relevant offender conduct without requiring a uniform assigned weight.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 (1990): applied; individualized sentencing is not mechanical factual counting.

##### Age is a relevant contextual subject, not an automatic aggravator

**Holding:** Section 190.3(i)'s direction to consider, when relevant, the defendant's age at the time of the crime is sufficiently understandable on Tuilaepa's presented challenge and requires no universal numerical weight or exclusive aggravating or mitigating label. The absence of youth does not automatically aggravate the crime, and the possibility of improper argument does not establish a facial defect or a proved as-applied violation; Proctor presents no age-factor question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V of Justice O'Connor's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight adopt the rule and no-error application; Part VI gives the corresponding affirmances without a new penalty hearing.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds no constitutional error on any presented factor challenge and orders no new penalty hearing. It makes no harmlessness finding and adds no alternative cure holding. If an independent properly presented claim establishes a weighing error, Stringer and Espinosa still require genuine state-authorized reweighing or constitutional harmless-error review with the State carrying Chapman's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden, and a new sentencing proceeding if no lawful cure exists. Nothing here resolves Romano's distinct further-remedy disagreement or alters Simmons's case-specific mandate. Guilt remains outside these sentencing questions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 (1983): applied; contextual relevance does not require an exclusive label or numerical weight.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 (1978), and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 (1982): preserved as protection for meaningful mitigation.
- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993, and Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: their independent-culpability-mitigation rules remain binding; the different California mechanism supports a different no-error application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both California judgments are affirmed, 8–1, on no-error grounds for the presented factor challenges: (a) in both cases and (b) and (i) only in Tuilaepa. No new penalty hearing or harmlessness determination follows. Guilt, the unchallenged eligibility premise and independently available properly presented claims of actual error are not adjudicated; no execution or date is ordered.

### Capital sentencing responsibility and rebuttal

#### Romano v. Oklahoma, 512 U.S. 1

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-9093.

**Decided:** 1994-06-13.

##### Another jury's death decision cannot displace the present jury's responsibility

**Holding:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments forbid the State to present an irrelevant death sentence imposed by another jury in circumstances that substantially invite the present capital jury to treat its own choice of death as already made elsewhere. This contextual rule extends Caldwell's protection beyond an inaccurate description of legal responsibility; it requires more than a speculative possibility of influence, preserves independently admissible prior-conduct evidence, and leaves open whether a focused instruction can prevent the distortion in a different record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Responsibility portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices adopt this rule and its application to the Sarfaty penalty proceeding.

**Material application:** The Thompson judgment disclosed another jury's death selection and execution command despite an offered stipulation to conviction. Its truth, the appeal notice and correct general instructions did not specifically withdraw the suggestion that the decisive choice had already been made. The Court finds the contextual constitutional distortion, not actual abdication by an identified juror.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Caldwell v. Mississippi, 472 U.S. 320 (1985): its responsibility protection is extended to an official disclosure of another jury's death selection that substantially invites displacement of the current choice; the Court does not pretend the earlier facts already decided this question.
- Darden v. Wainwright, 477 U.S. 168 (1986): its guilt-stage setting and remarks increasing responsibility are distinguished; its separate fundamental-unfairness standard remains intact.
- Dugger v. Adams, 489 U.S. 401 (1989): its procedural-default holding is preserved; its description of the ordinary misstatement claim does not preclude the extension adopted here.
- Sawyer v. Smith, 497 U.S. 227 (1990): its collateral-review new-rule holding is preserved; no nonretroactivity bar enters this direct review.

##### Earlier aggravator reweighing does not resolve the responsibility error

**Holding:** The state appellate court's deletion of the prior-violent-felony aggravator and reweighing of the remaining three do not themselves resolve the distinct constitutional error in presenting another jury's death selection. Its judgment is vacated only insofar as it sustains the Sarfaty death sentence, and the case is remanded for further proceedings under the responsibility holding; the Court establishes no controlling choice between a required new penalty hearing and a first constitutional harmless-error inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Common disposition and prior-reweighing portion of Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five adopt this limited proposition and appellate vacatur/remand; their different further-remedy positions are not a single holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No majority determines whether the existing sentence may be retained following a new constitutional harmlessness determination or whether a new penalty hearing is required whenever the State continues to seek death. No independently sufficient ordinary due-process holding, general constitutional evidence code, or Court-wide ruling on the validity of capital punishment is adopted. No actual juror mental state, inevitable execution, harmlessness finding, automatic life sentence, acquittal or release is found or ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Caldwell v. Mississippi, 472 U.S. 320 (1985): its independent-responsibility protection identifies an error distinct from an invalid aggravator; earlier reweighing does not automatically answer it.
- Johnson v. Mississippi, 486 U.S. 578 (1988): the invalid-prior-conviction problem remains distinct from the responsibility error; removing the prior-violent-felony aggravator does not decide the latter.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Oklahoma appellate judgment is vacated only insofar as it sustains the Sarfaty death sentence, and the case is remanded under the responsibility rule. The Court has no controlling choice between a required new penalty hearing and an initial constitutional harmless-error inquiry. It neither separately declares the trial sentence automatically void nor declares it enforceable; guilt and the independent Thompson proceedings remain untouched. No replacement sentence, release or execution order issues.

#### Simmons v. South Carolina, 512 U.S. 154

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-9059.

**Decided:** 1994-06-17.

##### Parole ineligibility must be available to answer the State's future-danger case

**Holding:** When the State places future dangerousness at issue in a capital sentencing proceeding and the only legally available noncapital sentence is life imprisonment without ordinary parole, due process requires an effective opportunity for the defendant to inform the jury of that ineligibility through accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction. A direction to disregard parole and give life imprisonment its ordinary meaning does not satisfy that requirement on this record; the rule neither requires a particular script nor promises immunity from clemency or future legislation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Seven Justices directly adopt this Fourteenth Amendment rule and its application; no combination of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** The operative alternative was life without ordinary parole under §24-21-640's second-or-subsequent violent-conviction rule following separate sentencing for the prior conviction; offenses within one day or one continuous course count as one. The eligibility premise is uncontested here, not a rule that every life sentence excludes parole. The State may still prove danger to inmates or guards and offer truthful information about commutation or pardon.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gardner v. Florida, 430 U.S. 349 (1977): its opportunity-to-answer principle supplies the due-process foundation for responding to material sentencing information.
- Skipper v. South Carolina, 476 U.S. 1 (1986): its future-danger rebuttal principle is applied to a fixed legal sentencing alternative; its independent mitigation rule remains intact.
- California v. Ramos, 463 U.S. 992 (1983): distinguished; permission to give truthful commutation information does not authorize withholding this responsive fact about ordinary parole.
- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993, and Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: their effective-mitigation rules remain controlling within their scope; neither is treated as having already decided this separate legal-information question.

**Later-authority backlinks:** O'Dell v. Netherland, June 19, 1997, No. 96-6867: the confined claim is available against Teague under separate majority old-rule and conditional watershed holdings; eligibility, effective opportunity, error consequence and genuine habeas conditions remain.

##### The error is not harmless on this record and requires a new penalty proceeding if death is pursued

**Holding:** Under Chapman's direct-review standard, the State cannot establish beyond a reasonable doubt that barring the parole-ineligibility explanation did not contribute to this jury's selection of death, given the State's future-danger argument, the complete exclusion of the legal answer and the jury's express parole inquiry. The death-sustaining judgment is reversed and the case remanded for a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding if the State continues to seek death; a lawful noncapital disposition remains available, and the convictions and separate sentences are undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt this record-specific harmlessness determination and mandatory further-hearing direction. O'Connor and Kennedy join reversal but reserve the further remedial determination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court adopts no independently sufficient Eighth Amendment instruction rule. It does not determine an entitlement to parole information where the State has not raised future danger, settle a disputed parole-eligibility question, require a fixed form of words, guarantee against clemency or future legislation, or announce automatic reversal for every omission. The State may pursue a lawful noncapital disposition instead of another capital hearing. No guilt retrial, acquittal, release, invalidation of the separate sentences or general prohibition of capital punishment is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applied; the State must establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt, and fails on this record.
- Romano v. Oklahoma, June 13, 1994: distinguished as an affirmative disclosure of another jury's death decision; this decision neither supplies a missing majority for its further-remedy positions nor changes that case's mandate.
- Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: preserves the distinction between correcting a capital sentencing defect and disturbing guilt; no automatic noncapital sentence follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The death-sustaining judgment is reversed, 7–2, and the case remanded. Five Justices require a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding if the State continues to seek death; the State may instead pursue a lawful noncapital disposition. The existing death sentence cannot be retained by a further harmlessness finding on this omission. Guilt and the separate life and thirty-day sentences remain undisturbed.

### Advisory jury recommendations in capital sentencing

#### Harris v. Alabama

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7659.
**Decided:** February 22, 1995.

##### The Constitution does not require numerical weight for advisory jury sentencing advice

**Holding:** The Eighth Amendment does not require a State to prescribe a numerical or mathematical weight for a jury's advisory sentencing recommendation. That proposition does not resolve whether judicial selection remains adequately guided and reviewable, whether relevant mitigation was considered, or whether a distinct substantive constraint on overriding life advice is constitutionally required.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Tuilaepa v. California and Proctor v. California, June 30, 1994, are applied for relevant individualized selection without fixed numerical weights, preserving their limits on forbidden considerations. Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104, retain effective consideration of relevant mitigation; neither is converted into a numerical scheme.

##### Alabama’s consideration rule and this reviewed sentencing record satisfy the admitted challenge

**Holding:** Where Alabama requires consideration of an unwaived advisory recommendation but assigns final sentencing to the judge, the Constitution does not additionally require Tedder's particular substantive override standard. On Harris's actual individualized findings and appellate review, the absence of a prescribed weight or separate comparative formula for rejecting the life recommendation establishes no constitutional error; this ruling does not excuse disregard of the recommendation, mitigation or a proved independent sentencing defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Spaziano v. Florida, 468 U.S. 447, is applied to judicial final sentencing; Tedder v. State, 322 So. 2d 908, is not made a federal constitutional command. Espinosa v. Florida, June 29, 1992, retains its rule concerning invalid-factor advice carrying required great weight, without deciding every Alabama advice error. Romano v. Oklahoma, June 13, 1994, retains its contextual protection against an irrelevant separate death decision distorting responsibility. Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992, continues to require a lawful cure for an invalid aggravator; no such error is found on this question. Tuilaepa/Proctor preserves independent selection-error review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Alabama Supreme Court is affirmed, 7–2, on the admitted sentencing challenge. No penalty rehearing is ordered, and the guilt judgment is undisturbed. Unanimity on the absence of a numerical-weight requirement does not establish unanimous approval of the sentence.

### Retrospective loss of parole consideration

#### California Department of Corrections v. Morales

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1462.
**Decided:** April 25, 1995.

##### The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here

**Holding:** Applying the 1981 California hearing-deferral provision to Morales’s 1980 offense violates the Ex Post Facto Clause because the actual three-year deferral withdraws offense-date mandatory annual consideration during a period when parole is legally available, without an established ordinary substitute for the meaningful intervening opportunities. The separately reasoned prediction of continued unsuitability and review of that initial prediction do not preserve consideration of later rehabilitation, changed circumstances, or a materially mistaken forecast; relief restores consideration, not release.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cavanaugh v. Roller, November 30, 1993: applies its opportunity rule to this reserved individualized setting; its original holding and rejection of proof of the omitted hearing’s outcome remain in force.
- Weaver v. Graham, 450 U.S. 24 (1981): applies protection of a legally available avenue to reduced custody and the distinction between guaranteed benefits and discretionary alternatives; it supplies no right to parole.
- Miller v. Florida, 482 U.S. 423 (1987): applies scrutiny of binding adverse operation despite unchanged outer limits and a possibly identical result.
- Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37 (1990): preserves the punishment boundary; no free-standing rule against every retrospective procedural disadvantage is adopted.
- In re Jackson, 39 Cal.3d 464 (1985): accepts its separate-reasons construction, permitted factual overlap and initial review; its state-court federal characterization does not displace Cavanaugh or establish ordinary later reopening.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The 1981 amendment retains annual review as the default. It permits a next hearing no later than three years after denial only for a prisoner convicted, in the same or different proceedings, of more than one offense involving the taking of a life, when the Board finds that parole cannot reasonably be expected at a hearing during the intervening years and states its bases. Jackson requires reasons separately directed to postponement, permits their supporting facts to overlap the present-unsuitability reasons, and establishes review of the initial choice. It establishes no generally available later reopening procedure. The rule concerns Morales's actual maximum deferral, not a proved cohort-wide practice; his eligibility arose during the postponed interval.

##### The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes

**Holding:** A prisoner challenging retrospective withdrawal of a parole-consideration opportunity must show the earlier legal entitlement, its retrospective actual withdrawal, and a concrete connection to a legally available avenue to earlier release; inconvenience alone is insufficient, and proof that the omitted hearing would actually have produced parole is unnecessary. After that showing, the State must substantiate any replacement mechanism it invokes by identifying its legal basis and establishing its practical availability and timely capacity to provide the relevant consideration; the prisoner retains the ultimate burden of establishing an unconstitutional increase in punishment.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cavanaugh v. Roller, November 30, 1993: preserves meaningful opportunity loss and the absence of an actual-release prerequisite; this decision expressly clarifies substantiation of an asserted replacement.
- Weaver v. Graham, 450 U.S. 24 (1981): applies its refusal to assume discretionary alternatives compensate for guaranteed benefits; it did not itself prescribe this parole evidentiary direction.
- In re Jackson, 39 Cal.3d 464 (1985): confines review and conceivable advancement to what the opinion establishes; initial review does not become later reconsideration.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns Morales’s demonstrated meaningful loss under the challenged 1981 provision. It does not invalidate every individualized postponement, require an interim hearing in every possible individualized scheme, or constitutionalize every scheduling detail. Maximum deferral is permission, not a statutory command or a proved near-uniform cohort practice. The Court neither requires wholly different facts for the two decisions nor treats the initial forecast as unreviewable.

No universal probability threshold, general presumption transferring constitutional proof to the State, numerical response deadline, mandatory filing procedure, suitability finding, release date, damages award, or right to favorable parole is adopted. Adequacy of a demonstrated materially different arrangement, later amendments and their transition or mootness effects are not decided. The false-information and sentence claims are not reopened. Unresolved credit and matrix calculations establish neither release entitlement nor legal impossibility of earlier release.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit's hearing-frequency judgment is affirmed, 5–4, preserving its remand for offense-date annual consideration. Lawful suitability standards and release discretion remain; no parole, release date or damages are ordered. Rejected false-information and sentence claims remain undisturbed.

### Coordination of sentences for related conduct

#### Witte v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-6187.
**Decided:** June 14, 1995.

##### Future sentence coordination remains conditional and separate

**Holding:** The possibility of an overlapping later sentence does not itself bar this prosecution, and any sentence following conviction must satisfy the then-applicable coordination provisions and independent constitutional limits. ### Double jeopardy and relevant-conduct sentencing

#### Witte v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-6187.
**Decided:** June 14, 1995.

For the other questions in Witte v. United States, decided June 14, 1995, No. 94-6187. see Criminal Procedure — Coordination of sentences for related conduct (Future sentence coordination remains conditional and separate).

##### Relevant conduct is not a prior cocaine prosecution or punishment here

**Holding:** Consideration of relevant uncharged conduct in selecting a sentence within the authorized statutory range for the offense of conviction does not itself constitute prosecution or independent punishment for the uncharged offense. No acquitted-conduct rule, statutory-maximum increase rule, complete sentencing-jury test or same-transaction bar is adopted. The plea-agreement contention remains outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Williams v. Oklahoma, 358 U.S. 576 — applied to consideration of other conduct when fixing lawful offense-of-conviction punishment.

- United States v. Felix — applied by analogy for the difference between use of conduct and prior prosecution; no same-evidence bar is created.

- United States v. Harris — confined to authorized cumulative punishment in one prosecution; it does not cure a valid successive-prosecution bar.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Fifth Circuit reversal of pretrial dismissal, permitting the cocaine indictment to proceed without finding guilt or fixing a sentence. United States v. Angle, June 20, 2002, identifies binding Guidelines ceilings and floors as punishment boundaries requiring element protections for present-offense aggravating facts. Witte's ordinary within-authority sentencing and distinct later-prosecution holdings remain intact; a higher outer statutory ceiling supplies no exemption from that binding-range rule.

### Capital sentencing and collateral review

#### Tuggle v. Netherland, 516 U.S. 10

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-6016.
**Decided:** October 30, 1995.

##### Effect of a surviving aggravator on an independent psychiatric-assistance claim

**Holding:** In a nonweighing capital system, a valid aggravator may preserve death eligibility, but its survival alone neither defeats a properly available claim of denied psychiatric assistance required by Ake nor establishes that an assumed denial did not affect the selection of death. Removing future dangerousness as an aggravator does not itself correct lost assistance in answering evidence the jury heard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous, for the rule and bounded summary vacatur.

**Material application:** The State presented psychiatric future-danger testimony; surviving vileness did not answer how assumed lost assistance affected the jury's further choice between death and life. The 1985 state Ake-error ruling remains part of the procedural history notwithstanding the Commonwealth's withdrawal of its earlier concession.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Violation is assumed only to test the appellate rationale. No favorable missing expert testimony, actual influence or structural classification is found. Required assistance covers evaluation, preparation, presentation, examination, rebuttal and supported mitigation, not a preferred psychiatrist or another examination on request; counting existing examiners does not determine adequacy. The Court reserves actual violation and availability, whether harmless-error review applies, the governing collateral standard, actual effect and ultimate relief. Independent Estelle theories and other petition questions are outside the limited grant without an implied denial or reopening.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the challenged Ake and sentence-review component and remand. Antecedent defenses must be legally available under the 1985 ruling and preservation history; closed issues are not reopened and forfeited defenses are not revived. If an available violation stands, its consequences require consideration on the relevant penalty record under applicable law. No present writ, hearing, guilt retrial, new penalty proceeding, life sentence, release, stay or execution authorization is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Zant is distinguished at its premise of admissible, accurate and explainable evidence; its nonweighing eligibility rule survives. Ake's assistance functions and no-preferred-examiner limit apply without a final violation ruling. Stringer and Richmond's weighing-system correction routes are not transferred. Brecht's preserved-Doyle and O'Neal's preserved-personal-intent Chapman holdings remain category-specific and are not extended to Ake. The separate proposed Chapman extension does not control.

#### Allridge v. Scott

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 93-9137; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied; judgment below, 41 F.3d 213.
**Decided:** January 15, 1996.

##### Ordinary parole availability and a proposed release-prediction instruction

**Holding:** Simmons's right to explain legal parole ineligibility does not require a favorable prediction of eventual release when the noncapital alternative permits ordinary parole. The claimed prediction entitlement fails; this conclusion neither categorically bars truthful parole evidence nor permits a misleading account of the lawful alternative.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I-A, unanimous.

**Independent alternative holding:** Habeas relief on the proposed favorable parole-improbability instruction is independently unavailable because no basis establishes that this different rule was dictated when the conviction became final before Penry in 1989 or that either then-existing Teague exception applies: substantive rules excluding conduct, classes of persons or punishments from state power, or watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Souter's Part I-B; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela does not join this additional ground.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** Affirm rejection, unanimous in judgment, without reaching harmlessness. The information rule neither removes conduct, a class or punishment from state power nor is established as watershed merely because useful against future-danger evidence. A fixed statutory-minimum claim would require separate presentation and retroactivity inquiry and is not decided. Simmons is distinguished on ordinary parole availability without a new prison-danger exception; Teague, Gilmore and Caspari retain their claim-specific finality rule and both exceptions.

##### Effective consideration of actually admitted capital mitigation

**Holding:** A preserved mitigation claim cannot be rejected merely because admitted evidence fails to establish a uniquely severe permanent disability to which the crime is conclusively attributable. Where actual evidence supports material culpability mitigation independent of deliberateness or future danger, the reviewing court must determine from the charge, evidence and trial as a whole whether there is a reasonable likelihood that jurors could not give that mitigation effect while answering the special issues truthfully; mere possibility is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** The father's admitted lay testimony concerning prison abuse and alleged mental illness was rejected under an unlawful categorical severe-disability/crime-linkage requirement. Lack of diagnostic expertise limits what it proves; it does not erase admitted lay evidence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds no psychiatric diagnosis, credibility, causation, material independent mitigating force, inadequate sentencing route or actual instructional violation. The evidence may remain weak, unsupported or adequately usable under the actual charge; the jury need not give favorable weight. No expert-only gate, categorical additional special issue or facial invalidation of Texas's scheme is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate rejection of only the admitted-evidence mitigation component and return it for the functional inquiry and the bounded further review stated below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Graham's independent-culpability, background, youth and prefinality rules apply; its fractured positive-character component is not enlarged. Johnson and Boyde supply whole-charge reasonable-likelihood review. Jurek's conditional approval survives. Lockett and Eddings require effective mitigating use, not compelled favorable weight. Tuggle separates an insufficient reviewing rationale from present entitlement to a writ.

##### Instruction claims resting on unoffered medical evidence

**Holding:** A defendant cannot obtain a mitigation instruction based on the assumed content of medical evidence never presented merely because offering it might also support future dangerousness. That independent instruction claim fails; a supported challenge to actual unconstitutional exclusion or a legal impediment remains distinct and is not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the component's rejection, without a ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Strategic difficulty does not establish an absent diagnosis, admissibility or mitigating significance. The rule does not demand formal presentation of evidence unconstitutionally excluded, create an expert-only requirement or adverse inference from silence, reduce the State's aggravation burden, or decide ineffective assistance.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of this component, unanimous in judgment; the admitted-evidence inquiry remains separate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Delo's supporting-evidence predicate applies without displacing Graham's protection of supported mitigation or foreclosing a supported unconstitutional-exclusion claim.

##### Review correction and eventual punishment-only habeas relief

**Holding:** Vacating the admitted-mitigation review ruling establishes neither an available violation nor entitlement to relief. That component requires review of the actual evidence, full charge, prefinality law, genuinely available defenses and any necessary error-effect inquiry; if a defect ultimately requires correction, the relief concerns punishment through adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence, leaving guilt intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The instruction request establishes no exhaustion or preservation of every reformulated theory. Applicable factual presumptions and genuinely available defenses remain; no forfeited defense revives or settled issue reopens. Graham's established rule cannot be called new merely because another defendant invokes it, but a materially new extension requires its own Teague inquiry. Whether a distinct error-effect inquiry is required, its standard, actual effect and relief are reserved; the full charge, testimony, claim-specific rulings and properly preserved submissions are necessary before a writ.

**Operative remedy or transition:** No present writ, sentencing hearing, release, stay, deadline or execution authorization. Brady, lesser-offense and other unreviewed grounds remain undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Tuggle supplies the review/relief distinction without transferring its Ake subject. Brecht and O'Neal retain their category-specific Chapman holdings; neither they nor Chapman establish the proposed capital-mitigation extension here.

### Conditional community release and revocation

#### Young v. Harper

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1598.
**Decided:** March 18, 1997.

##### Whether preparole community freedom is protected existing liberty

**Holding:** A prisoner released to live at home, work and participate in ordinary community life under parole-like conditions possesses conditional liberty protected by Morrissey. Legal custody, the preparole label, an overcrowding purpose or denial of formal parole does not dispense with revocation process.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Parts II–IV, unanimous.

**Material application:** The August 1991 procedure cannot by itself establish a condition communicated at the October 1990 release or validate the March 1991 telephone return order. Silence in the earlier procedure does not establish all release conditions, prove compliance or make release irrevocable.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Five months is not a constitutional minimum. Formal parole entitlement, short furloughs, work details, ordinary institutional transfers and the later procedure’s prospective validity are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Morrissey protects freedom already conferred; Greenholtz concerns initial release and Meachum ordinary internal transfer. Sandin preserves direct constitutional liberty alongside its sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement route; no exhaustive program-entitlement finding is required.

##### Which preliminary and final safeguards govern withdrawal of that liberty

**Holding:** Revocation of this community liberty requires Morrissey’s preliminary and final protections, with their distinct qualifications. Initial arrest and detention need not await a completed final hearing, but final revocation must rest on constitutionally adequate process.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part III, unanimous.

**Material application:** For arrest and detention pending revocation, a reasonably prompt inquiry at or reasonably near the alleged violation or arrest determines probable cause or reasonable ground to detain for a release-condition violation. The decisionmaker must be uninvolved, but may be a different parole officer rather than a judge. Notice must identify the inquiry, its purpose and alleged violations; the participant may appear, speak, present relevant documents and witnesses and, on request, question adverse informants in person. An officer’s finding that identification would expose an informant to harm permits withholding confrontation of that informant. A summary must state the evidence and reasons without formal judicial findings; supported probable cause permits detention and institutional return pending final decision.

The State must offer a final hearing if desired, within a reasonable time after custody and before effective final revocation, to resolve disputed relevant facts and whether circumstances justify revocation, including denial and mitigation. Minimum safeguards are written notice of violations or grounds, disclosure of adverse evidence, a personal opportunity to present witnesses and documents, confrontation and cross-examination unless specifically withheld for good cause, a neutral and detached decisionmaker, and written evidence and reasons. The decisionmaker need not be a judge or lawyer. Informal letters and affidavits may be considered; matters already decided adversely in another forum, including a relevant conviction, need not be retried.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Preliminary informant-danger and final-hearing good-cause exceptions are separate permissions, not commands to withhold confrontation. No exception, waiver, dispositive admission, prior determination or supported probable-cause decision is established here. Morrissey’s two-month discussion establishes no universal deadline or safe harbor; no new evidentiary burden, automatic-release rule or separate counsel holding is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Morrissey’s flexible administrative safeguards govern in full, without converting revocation into a criminal trial or replacing it with a telephone instruction.

##### What relief follows from the failure to provide revocation process

**Holding:** The Tenth Circuit correctly reversed denial of habeas relief because this termination required Morrissey process. Oklahoma may still establish a legally available revocation ground through adequate procedures or rely on an independent lawful basis for custody.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The procedural violation proves neither sentence expiration, formal-parole entitlement nor compliance with every possible condition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmance returns the case for proceedings under the protected-liberty and process holdings; it orders no unconditional release, release date or new state deadline. Permissible interim detention remains subject to the preliminary and final safeguards.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lynce supplies no unproved exhaustion defect or waiver; Gonzales’s concurrency construction supplies no authority for summary revocation.

For the eight-Justice accepted-rehearing holding in Young v. Harper, No. 95-1598, decided March 18, 1997, see Federal Courts, “Accepted rehearing and certiorari finality.”

### Firearm sentences and existing state imprisonment

#### United States v. Gonzales

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1605.
**Decided:** March 3, 1997.

##### Whether §924(c) permits concurrency with an already imposed state sentence

**Holding:** A federal court imposing a §924(c) term cannot make it concurrent with an existing state or federal term. The reference to any other imprisonment includes state sentences, and §3584’s general discretion cannot override this specific prohibition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices adopt both holdings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide the authority of a later-sentencing state court, independent constitutional objections, duplicative-enhancement claims or a general limit on aggregate punishment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit judgment is vacated and remanded to remove its federal-only concurrency premise, preserving unreviewed convictions and sentencing rulings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Witte permits coordination only within applicable statutes and Guidelines and supplies no exception to §924(c) or cure for an independent successive-prosecution bar. Deal construes the distinct second-or-subsequent-conviction provision; no residual statutory ambiguity here calls for lenity.

##### Which other sentencing choices and service-order questions remain separate

**Holding:** The §924(c) concurrency prohibition neither removes otherwise lawful discretion over non-§924(c) terms nor commands that the firearm term be served first. Correcting this error does not authorize blanket reinstatement of every original sentencing component.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), seven Justices; the same coalition joins both holdings.

**Material application:** Under §3584, simultaneous terms ordinarily run concurrently absent a contrary judicial direction or statutory command, while terms imposed at different times ordinarily run consecutively absent a concurrency direction. Its prohibition on consecutive punishment for an attempt and its sole-objective offense remains distinct and supplies no exception for this firearm sentence; no such pair is found here. Section 3553(a)’s factors govern the remaining discretion under §3584(b), and administrative aggregation under §3584(c) does not prescribe service order.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Credit calculations, administrative sequence and the validity of underlying convictions remain unadjudicated.

### Career-offender maximum and sentence reductions

#### United States v. LaBonte

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1726.
**Decided:** 1997-05-27.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Respondents George LaBonte, Alfred Lawrence Hunnewell and Stephen Dyer have distinct sentencing postures. David Piper is not a respondent and receives no mandate.

##### An applicable enhanced maximum supplies the statutory benchmark

**Holding:** For an offender covered by §994(h) and subject to a lawfully invoked statutory recidivist enhancement, the maximum term authorized is the enhanced statutory maximum. Amendment 506 is invalid insofar as its career-offender benchmark excludes that applicable increase because it rests on the defendant's prior criminal record; the Commission may not replace the legally operative maximum with the unenhanced maximum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; six direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 994(h) concerns a defendant at least eighteen years old with a current qualifying felony and two or more prior qualifying felonies. The current and each prior felony must be a crime of violence or an offense described in 21 U.S.C. §841, §§952(a), 955 or 959, or the Maritime Drug Law Enforcement Act. Eligibility is not disputed here. Potential enhancement eligibility alone does not make an uninvoked maximum applicable. The Court requires no prosecutor to seek an enhancement, approves no improper charging basis, and decides no unpresented constitutional challenge or defect in notice or predicates. The holding concerns Amendment 506's prior-record exclusion, not every conceivable statutory or guideline enhancement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Stinson limits commentary by statute. Neal preserves distinct lawful statutory/guideline measures, not replacement of Congress's benchmark; MCI examines actual delegated power rather than forbidding every consequential policy change. The text resolves this issue without deciding general Commission entitlement to Chevron deference; Auer supplies no contrary rule.

##### The maximum benchmark and proximity remain distinct

**Holding:** Identifying the enhanced maximum does not require imposing that maximum in every covered case. Section 994(h)'s at-or-near language leaves lawful implementation, adjustments and departures within the governing statutes and guidelines; the Court sets no numerical proximity threshold and rejects Amendment 506 on the benchmark defect without a separate quantitative near holding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; six direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No percentage, every-case maximum sentence, aggregated maximum for multiple counts, or probation rule is adopted; §3584 term arrangement remains distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gonzales preserves separately authorized choices at its statutory scope and neither determines this benchmark nor compels every sentence to reach it.

##### Invalid commentary cannot authorize these sentence reductions

**Holding:** Amendment 506's retroactive designation cannot supply lawful authority under §3582(c)(2) for a reduction based on its invalid benchmark. The reviewed sentencing components must be reversed and remanded for proceedings consistent with that invalidity, preserving the separate statutory gates, discretionary choices, unreviewed claims and respondent-specific posture.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; six direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3582(c)(2) requires a sentence based on a range lawfully lowered under §994(o), consideration of applicable §3553(a) factors and consistency with policy statements; the defendant, Bureau of Prisons Director or court may initiate consideration, and an authorized reduction remains discretionary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the affirmed LaBonte reduction from 188 to 151 months and the Amendment 506 premises for Hunnewell's and Dyer's possible-reduction remands, 6–3 each. Dyer's non-sentencing §2255 dismissal and Piper's separate appeal are untouched. No original term is automatically reinstated, replacement sentence fixed or every final career-offender judgment reopened.

### Capital mitigation instructions

#### Buchanan v. Angelone

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-8400.

**Decided:** 1998-01-21.

##### Effective consideration of mitigation under this complete charge

**Holding:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments require an effective sentencing route for the material independent culpability significance of supported mitigation; a reasonable likelihood that the charge, evidence and trial as a whole prevent that use requires correction. This charge supplies that route, so the Constitution did not require the requested additional general mitigation definition or four factor-specific instructions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, controls with six direct votes. Scalia joins the application of governing law while separately reserving his disagreement with its constitutional foundation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls with eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees in rejection of this distinct claim only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No particular favorable weight, universal wording, evidentiary support for every requested factor, mitigation-unanimity ruling, AEDPA temporal holding, new Teague bar or correction standard for an actual error is adopted. **Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed habeas denial is affirmed, 6–3, on the constitutional claim; rejection of the distinct statutory-factor claim is unanimous in judgment. No writ, new sentencing or remand issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

### Capital clemency and retained constitutional protection

#### Ohio Adult Parole Authority v. Woodard

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1769.

**Decided:** 1998-03-25.

##### Required clemency procedures do not create an entitlement to mercy or triallike review

**Holding:** Ohio's required investigation, hearing and advisory recommendation do not create a substantive entitlement to clemency because they do not constrain the Governor to grant it on specified facts. The analogy to a first appeal as of right does not itself require appellate representation, triallike evidence procedure or a new determination of guilt in this discretionary executive process.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion: Part I-A has all eight Associates, with Stone-Zsela also joining the no-right-to-mercy conclusion. Part I-B has O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela joins only the mercy-versus-criminal-trial distinction. Stevens does not join the rejection of the established-process analogy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A further hearing or revised recommendation upon new information is permitted, not mandatory. No right to favorable mercy follows from the required administrative steps.

##### Capital-clemency procedures retain a constitutional minimum, satisfied on these pleadings

**Holding:** An established capital-clemency process must afford minimal safeguards against fundamentally arbitrary procedure, including arbitrary denial of any access or substitution of an entirely arbitrary device such as a coin flip for consideration. The notice and offered interview here, considered with the pleaded restrictions, satisfy that minimum; they establish no entitlement to clemency, trial protections, a favorable recommendation or judicial review of mercy's merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion: Part II-A's minimum safeguard has six direct joins: O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part II-B's sufficient-process application has five: O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens disputes the application; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas agree with rejection of the claim on their distinct entitlement/adjudication ground. Both controlling propositions rest on direct majority joins, without combining separate rationales under Marks.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The application accepts the alleged three-day interview notice, ten-day hearing notice, counsel restrictions, preparation difficulty and inability to testify or submit documents at the hearing. It assumes no additional document channel. The rule fixes no universal procedural checklist and leaves independent constitutional wrongs distinct.

##### The optional interview does not establish compelled self-incrimination

**Holding:** An optional clemency interview does not compel self-incrimination merely because the prisoner must choose between providing potentially harmful information and remaining silent; absence of counsel, confidentiality or immunity does not itself convert this invitation into compulsion.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part III-A, joined by all eight other Justices.

**Independent alternative holding:** The conclusion also holds if an ordinary civil adverse inference from silence is assumed, but it does not authorize an automatic privilege penalty, compelled waiver or criminal use of compelled answers.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** O’Connor’s Part III-B, joined by all seven other Associates; Stone-Zsela does not join this additional ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ohio’s actual use of silence or answers is not found. A genuine threat of an automatic privilege penalty, compulsory waiver or criminal use of compelled answers requires its own analysis.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The due-process remand is reversed 8–1, with Stevens opposing. The self-incrimination remand is reversed unanimously. The independent no-entitlement ruling remains undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Connecticut Board of Pardons v. Dumschat:** Applied to discretionary mercy and the distinction between seeking relief and entitlement to its grant; not extended to extinguish independent constitutional interests.
- **Sandin v. Conner:** Applied at its substantive-constraint rule. Mandatory procedure alone is insufficient, and directly retained constitutional interests remain separate.
- **Doe v. District of Columbia:** Applied to distinguish investigative duties from a substantive entitlement.
- **Johnson v. Rodriguez:** Applied to the same procedure/entitlement boundary and preservation of independent constitutional wrongs; its ordinary initial-parole rule does not displace the retained-life floor.
- **Evitts v. Lucey:** Distinguished at the adjudicatory first appeal as of right; it does not itself require the requested capital-clemency procedures.
- **Ford v. Wainwright:** Its distinction between a protected interest and appropriate flexible procedure informs the minimum-process rule; its separate incompetency protection remains unchanged.
- **Herrera v. Collins:** Preserved as a record-specific innocence disposition; no exclusive-clemency rule is attributed to it.
- **Minnesota v. Murphy:** Applied to distinguish voluntary choices from supported threats of a privilege penalty.
- **Baxter v. Palmigiano:** Applied by the eight Associates to ordinary civil adverse inferences, distinct from automatic punishment or compelled criminal testimony.
- **LaChance v. Erickson / McManus:** Preserved for separately established compulsion and waiver; it supplies no general prohibition on lawful civil inferences.

### Capital lesser-included instructions

#### Hopkins v. Reeves

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1693.

**Decided:** 1998-06-08.

##### Beck does not require creation of a nonincluded offense

**Holding:** Beck does not require a capital trial court to instruct on second-degree murder or manslaughter when those offenses are not lesser included offenses of the charged felony murder under Nebraska's governing elements. Its protection against withholding a legally available, evidence-supported lesser included option remains intact, as does independent constitutional review of a genuinely arbitrary exclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for this rule and explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not approve withholding a genuine, supported lesser included instruction. An unpresented manslaughter-arbitrariness claim, the Teague question, any separate challenge to a presumption of intent, and the independent validity of judge sentencing remain undecided. Judicial selection of the sentence is an additional factual distinction, not a categorical cure for an unreliable guilt choice. The death-eligibility culpability requirement does not itself change the elements of the guilt offense.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit's conditional habeas judgment is reversed on the Beck instruction ground and remanded; the separate rejected due-process ground is not reinstated, and no unrelated claim or execution date is determined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Beck v. Alabama retains its protection against withdrawing legally and factually available lesser included instructions in capital cases; it does not require redesign of generally applicable offense elements.
- Spaziano v. Florida is applied for legal availability of the proposed alternative, without extending its separate judge-sentencing holding.

- Bowersox v. Williams remains limited to genuine supported noncapital options and does not decide their complete absence.

### Chapman governs this available capital-selection misinformation claim

#### Calderon v. Coleman

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-437; Ninth Circuit Nos. 97-99013 and 97-99014; District Court No. C89-1906 RMW.
**Decided:** 1998-12-14.

### Actual sentencing deadlock requires a lawful nondeath sentence

#### Jones v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-9361; federal capital sentencing.
**Decided:** 1999-06-21.

##### Actual sentencing deadlock requires a lawful nondeath sentence

**Holding:** When completed penalty deliberations end without a unanimous recommendation, §3594's otherwise clause requires the judge to impose a lawful nondeath sentence. For this death-resulting kidnapping the alternative is life without release. Section 3593(b)(2)(C) does not authorize another attempt to obtain death solely because the jury deadlocked.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part I-A has eight direct votes from all Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition without joining that rationale.

##### There is no universal deadlock script, but a false sentencing choice must be corrected here

**Holding:** Neither the Eighth Amendment nor a supervisory rule requires a fixed deadlock-consequence instruction in every capital case. In this case, however, the preserved request for an accurate account of the lawful consequence required correction of the charge's materially misleading below-life alternative; refusing that correction requires a new penalty proceeding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I-B's rejection of a universal script is unanimous. Part II's case-specific corrective duty and remedy are directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The Court does not adopt every constitutional or plain-error reason stated in the separate concurrence.

##### Independent aggravator validity and cure are not resolved for the Court

**Limits and questions not reached:** The instruction disposition removes this death sentence. No majority resolves whether factors 3(B) and 3(C) were independently invalid or harmless: O’Connor, Kennedy and Thomas find contextual validity; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg find the specific formulations defective; Scalia, Stone-Zsela and Breyer reserve validity. O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would find narrowly assumed drafting error harmless; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg reject that cure, while Stone-Zsela and Breyer reserve it.

**Authority of the divided positions:** These accounts identify the limits of the Court's decision, not additional factor holdings. No Marks synthesis supplies a validity or cure rule from the divided positions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Simmons v. South Carolina: preserves the effective opportunity to understand the only lawful nondeath alternative in its stated setting; supplies no universal deadlock script.
- Shannon v. United States: distinguishes an ordinary nonsentencing guilt jury; its allocation rule does not excuse an affirmatively misleading capital choice.
- Olano and Johnson: retain separate error, plainness, substantial-rights and discretionary conditions. The four-Justice concurrence's alternative application changes no Court-wide rule and does not decide Johnson's reserved initially unsettled-law setting.
- Boyde, Mills and McKoy: preserve contextual examination of the charge and individual mitigation; no verdict-form typography alone decides the case.
- Tuilaepa/Proctor and Payne: retain understandable relevant individualized selection and permissible impact evidence within their limits; no factor-validity majority arises here.
- Stringer, Sochor, Espinosa, Clemons and Chapman: preserve the distinction between valid eligibility, authorized reweighing and actual harmless-error correction. No new general cure or appellate-resentencing authority is adopted.
- Neder: retains its qualified actual-verdict inquiry; the divided factor applications supply no new universal capital harmlessness rule.
- Strickler: preserves distinct initial Brady materiality for guilt and selection; its burden and record do not decide this preserved instruction issue.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual federal sentencing deadlock invokes §3594's lawful nondeath command, with no replacement jury solely to seek death after that deadlock. There is no universal deadlock script. The specific misleading statutory choice in this charge, identified by the preserved corrective request, requires a new penalty proceeding. The conviction remains intact. The separate constitutional, plain-error, factor-validity and factor-cure applications retain only their stated voting support; existing capital-selection and review rules otherwise continue unchanged.

**Holding:** A sentencing court may not deny an otherwise warranted section 3E1.1 acceptance-of-responsibility adjustment solely because the defendant presents a nonfrivolous legal or constitutional challenge unrelated to admitted factual guilt. **Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The sentencing court may assess actual remorse, candor, sincerity and conduct on supported lawful grounds, with appropriate deference to genuine factual findings. The Court orders no two- or three-level reduction, prescribed term of imprisonment or conclusive finding of acceptance. It does not resolve credibility anew, and supported contrary conduct may outweigh evidence favoring credit.

The applicable statutory exceptions and registration limits remain intact. ### Commencement and adjustment of supervised release

#### United States v. Johnson, 529 U.S. 53

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1696.

**Decided:** March 1, 2000.

##### Actual release begins the supervision term

**Holding:** Under 18 U.S.C. §3624(e), supervised release commences on actual release from imprisonment. Excess custody served before that event neither constructively begins supervised release nor automatically reduces its duration.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; all nine adopt both holdings directly.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not resolve invalid-custody tolling after commencement or an independent remedy for unlawful imprisonment.

##### The separate statutory adjustment powers remain available on their own terms

**Holding:** Excess custody may inform the district court's discretionary exercise of §3583(e), but creates no automatic adjustment. After considering §3553(a)(1), (a)(2)(B)–(D), and (a)(4)–(6), the court may terminate supervision under §3583(e)(1) only after one year of supervised release and when the released person's conduct and the interest of justice warrant termination; §3583(e)(2) separately permits modification, reduction or enlargement of conditions before expiration or termination, and extension of the term if less than the authorized maximum was imposed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; all nine adopt both holdings directly.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Paragraphs (1) and (2) retain the applicable Federal Rules' probation-modification procedure; paragraph (2) also retains the provisions governing original terms and conditions. Reducing conditions is not early termination of the term. Revocation and nonworking-hours home confinement are separate powers not invoked here; no new sentence, damages or retrospective credit follows.

Section 3624(e) separately makes supervision concurrent with any federal, state or local probation, supervised-release or parole term for another offense to which the person is or becomes subject during supervision. Its conviction-connected imprisonment tolling excludes imprisonment for less than thirty consecutive days. Its fine provision conditions release on agreement to an installment schedule for the imposed fine, ordinarily no longer than two years unless special circumstances exist. None supplies precommencement credit.

The other §3583(e) branches are not invoked. Paragraph (3) permits revocation under the applicable Federal Rules upon proof by a preponderance that a supervision condition was violated, with imprisonment without credit for previous postrelease supervision and limits of five years for a class A felony, three for class B, two for class C or D, and one year otherwise. Paragraph (4) permits confinement at the residence during nonworking hours, with telephone or electronic monitoring if ordered, only as an alternative to incarceration. The Court orders neither branch, creates no new sentence, and adjudicates no damages or retrospective sentencing-credit remedy.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The automatic-credit judgment is reversed and remanded. Excess custody may inform a properly presented statutory adjustment request, but the Court requires no motion, hearing, early termination, changed condition or particular result.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

The decision construes §§3624(e) and 3583(e) directly. No earlier Supreme Court holding is overruled or limited. The prior vacatur of Johnson's firearm convictions is accepted as the procedural history of his case; the Court does not decide the substantive §924(c) question.

### Retrospective loss of parole consideration and substitute review

#### Garner v. Jones, 529 U.S. 244 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-137.

**Decided:** March 28, 2000.

##### Whether the actual eight-year deferral establishes loss of a meaningful three-year reconsideration opportunity

**Holding:** Jones's actual eight-year deferral retrospectively removed the offense-date three-year occasion for consideration while parole remained legally available, satisfying Morales's initial showing of a meaningful lost opportunity. He need not prove that the omitted occasion would have produced release; the original prediction of continued unsuitability does not itself preserve consideration of later rehabilitation, changed circumstances or a materially mistaken prediction.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven direct votes applying controlling Morales.

##### How the asserted discretionary expedited-review procedure must be assessed

**Holding:** A written discretionary policy and sworn description of an existing expedited-review procedure must be assessed for legal basis, practical availability and timely capacity to provide the consideration withdrawn; neither the discretionary wording alone nor an assertion that the procedure exists decides its adequacy. Because the appellate court rejected the arrangement on a broader categorical premise and the district court applied a different Morales premise, the judgment must be vacated for the proper inquiry under ordinary procedure.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven direct votes applying controlling Morales.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The prisoner retains the ultimate constitutional burden; officials must substantiate the substitute they invoke. A written policy and affidavit are evidence of an asserted existing process, not a finding of ordinary availability or timeliness. Neither discretionary wording nor asserted existence resolves adequacy. No proof that the omitted occasion would have yielded release is required, and no general significant-risk substitute, invalidity of all longer intervals or guarantee of favorable parole is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Eleventh Circuit judgment 7–2 for application of the existing opportunity-and-substitute inquiry under ordinary procedure. Do not restore officials' summary judgment. If an adequate substitute is not established, restore timely consideration under lawful rules, not release. No automatic discovery, study, hearing date or filing mechanism is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- California Department of Corrections v. Morales governs the actual opportunity and substitute inquiries. Its original authority, burden allocation and consideration-only remedy remain; this decision addresses the materially different Georgia arrangement it left open.
- Cavanaugh v. Roller retains protection against the binding loss of a meaningful avenue to earlier release without proof of the omitted hearing's result.
- Weaver v. Graham supplies the distinction between a legally available opportunity and a guaranteed favorable outcome; theoretical discretion does not establish a compensating substitute.
- Miller v. Florida supplies scrutiny of binding adverse operation despite unchanged sentence ceilings or a potentially identical outcome.
- Collins v. Youngblood preserves the punishment boundary and supplies no categorical exemption for parole procedure.

### Postrevocation supervision under the offense-date statute

#### Johnson v. United States, 529 U.S. 694 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-5153.
**Decided:** May 15, 2000.

##### Original-offense attribution and the inapplicability of subsection (h)

**Holding:** Postrevocation imprisonment and supervision are consequences of the original offense, so that offense supplies the relevant event for determining whether 18 U.S.C. §3583(h) applies. Congress did not direct subsection (h), enacted September 13, 1994, to govern Johnson's October 1993 offense; his sentence must therefore rest on the former statute, and the Court does not decide the constitutional Ex Post Facto challenge to subsection (h).

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I-A, original-offense attribution, is joined by all eight other Justices. Part I-B, statutory nonapplication of subsection (h) without reaching its constitutionality, is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, for eight Justices including Souter. Stone-Zsela does not join Part I-B and states his distinct retrospective-punishment ground separately.

##### Remaining-term authority under former subsection (e)(3)

**Holding:** Former §3583(e)(3) permits a court, upon a qualifying revocation, to allocate part of the supervised-release term actually imposed to imprisonment and retain a remaining part for supervision after imprisonment. The imprisonment must satisfy the applicable felony cap and the combined new imprisonment and supervision cannot exceed that actual imposed term; the provision denies credit for earlier time served on supervision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies a direct seven-Justice majority. Stone-Zsela and Scalia dissent from this construction. Parts III-A and III-B provide the separately joined confirmation described below, not independent alternative sentencing powers.

**Material application:** The thirty-six-month imposed term supports eighteen months in prison and twelve months of supervision. The independent Class D prison limit is twenty-four months, not the combined-term measure; approximately seven months previously spent on supervision do not reduce the imposed-term measure.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The former statute's required consideration of §3553(a)(1), (a)(2)(B)–(D), (a)(4), (a)(5) and (a)(6) remains. Former (e)(3) requires a violation found by a preponderance of the evidence, the applicable probation-revocation procedures and Sentencing Commission policy statements, and its offense-specific imprisonment limit. The Court supplies no new violation finding, credit rule or authority for repeated revocations.

**Proposition-level authority:** The seven Justices joining Part II adopt these limits. In Part III-A, Souter, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer additionally rely on subsection (a)'s supervision-after-prison structure as context. In Part III-B those five and Kennedy also rely on supervision's transition function as confirmation. Thomas joins neither additional discussion; Kennedy does not join Part III-A. Neither discussion independently authorizes a penalty absent the Part II construction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Former (e)(1) allows termination and discharge only after one year of supervision, under the required procedure and when conduct and the interest of justice warrant it. Former (e)(2) retains its power to extend a term shorter than the authorized maximum and to modify conditions before expiration or termination, under its procedural and initial-setting requirements. The Court does not approve extending a term under (e)(2) and then revoking it to enlarge the measure under (e)(3). It leaves different original terms, felony caps and repeated revocations for their own cases. Subsection (h)'s less-than-maximum-prison trigger and authorized-maximum-minus-prison formula are not silently substituted for the former actual-term rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The eighteen-month imprisonment and twelve-month supervision sentence is affirmed under former §3583(e)(3), without a new sentencing hearing. Subsection (h)’s constitutional validity and its equivalence to the former statute are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Landgraf v. USI Film Products; Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc. (April 26, 1994):** Applied their temporal-direction and actual-consequence principles. The common present result does not merge the general organizing framework and the distinct relevant-activity approach or establish that corrective purpose supplies retrospective reach.

### Capital parole information

#### Ramdass v. Angelone, 530 U.S. 156 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-7000.
**Decided:** June 12, 2000.

##### II. Absence of entry does not conclusively defeat the truthful-response claim

**Holding:** When the State places future dangerousness at issue, the absence of formal entry of judgment on a separate guilty verdict does not by itself defeat a claim that due process required a truthful account of that verdict's conditional parole consequence. A case in which substantive adjudication was complete and only entry remained may require that account; mere expectation of conviction, confidence in guilt or a favorable release prediction does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The record does not establish that substantive adjudication was complete. Rule 3A:15 preserved judicial power to set aside the verdict for trial error or insufficient evidence; that power proves neither an actual pending defect nor that every substantive question had ended. Later entry proves neither proposition retrospectively. A verdict facing a genuine unresolved merits issue is outside this rule. Any required account must state the conditional consequence of entry; it may not falsely state that Ramdass was already ineligible during the jury's deliberations. The Court decides no separate arbitrary-manipulation claim or separate claim concerning the April 2 judicial sentencing.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment does not reinstate the District Court's sentencing order or vacate the sentence. It orders no present writ, new penalty proceeding, evidentiary hearing or other evidentiary command, deadline, release, stay or execution-related relief. The conviction and unrelated claims remain untouched. Denial on a properly supported independent ground remains available on remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Simmons v. South Carolina:** Applies its effective-opportunity protection and clarifies that entry alone does not conclusively foreclose a truthful account of a completed adjudication's conditional consequence. Actual entitlement and effect remain unresolved; no false statement of present ineligibility is authorized.
- **Gardner v. Florida:** Applies the right to explain material capital-sentencing information; it supplies the opportunity-to-answer foundation, not a present writ.
- **Skipper v. South Carolina:** Applies the protection for rebutting a State future-danger case; its distinct mitigation holding remains intact.
- **California v. Ramos:** Preserves truthful release-related information and distinguishes it from a misleading account of the lawful alternative; no guarantee against clemency or future legislation is required.
- **Allridge v. Scott:** Preserves the distinction between fixed legal consequences and release predictions. It does not settle whether the Domino's substantive adjudication was complete and does not supply a constitutional holding dated before November 1994.

### Constitutional elements and maximum punishment

#### Apprendi v. New Jersey, 530 U.S. 466 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-478.
**Decided:** June 26, 2000.

##### Present-offense facts necessary for a higher maximum require element protections

**Holding:** Under the Sixth Amendment's jury guarantee, applied to the States through the Fourteenth Amendment, and the Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause, a fact concerning the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to authorize punishment above the maximum supported by the verdict or plea is an element. It must be admitted as an element or proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt; a sentencing label does not remove those requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg. Six direct rationale votes control the rule, both procedural guarantees, the Count 18 application and the bounded remedy. This is a new constitutional holding; the earlier statutory decisions did not establish it.

**Material application:** The plea's express reservation permits this claim; knowledge of possible exposure and admission of an unlawful purpose to frighten or harass do not admit the additional racial purpose. The lower court's view of strong evidence supplies neither an admission nor a jury verdict. The judgment sustaining the enhanced term is reversed and the term vacated, with the limited remand stated below. This application adopts no universal structural-error classification and does not automatically extend Neder's qualified materiality-error inquiry to every sentencing aggravator or plea defect.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Facts of prior conviction are not decided; there is no constitutional prior-conviction exception in this holding. Whether, or in what form, a State must charge a covered fact is not decided, and the Fifth Amendment's grand-jury clause is not applied to the States. The federal charging consequences of the earlier statutory decisions remain intact. Judicial factfinding within punishment already authorized by the conviction remains permissible. Unchanged-maximum mandatory minima, general consecutive-sentence rules, capital eligibility or selection, and materially different sentencing systems, including general Guidelines classifications, remain outside this decision. McMillan and Walton are not overruled. No valid waiver of the reserved claim occurred here; any distinct lawful-waiver question retains its own requirements.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The New Jersey judgment is reversed insofar as it sustains the twelve-year Count 18 sentence. The constitutional rule adopted here requires vacatur of that enhanced sentence. On the existing admitted offense, a sentence within the five-to-ten-year range is permissible. Any further proceeding must have a basis in state law and comply with the governing plea, notice, waiver, sufficiency and double-jeopardy rules; the Court neither orders nor authorizes a new aggravated prosecution or enhancement jury merely by remanding.

The pleas and convictions remain intact. The Court orders no acquittal, withdrawal of the pleas, specific new sentence, consecutive restructuring, aggregate sentence or release. The separate Graves Act question is not decided; any parole consequence must follow the lawful sentence and that provision's own terms rather than automatic retention of the former four-year figure.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Duncan v. Louisiana:** Supplies application of the criminal jury guarantee to state prosecutions; the present rule identifies the additional fact that receives that guarantee.
- **In re Winship:** Supplies the reasonable-doubt protection for offense facts; that burden now governs the covered maximum-increasing present-offense fact.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applies once the constitutional element is identified; the jury must determine that element, while Gaudin alone did not decide this antecedent classification.
- **Mullaney v. Wilbur:** Supports examination of the fact's legal role rather than its label when necessary facts determine aggravated punishment.
- **Patterson v. New York:** Preserves legislative definition of substantive offenses without permitting a label alone to transfer a covered aggravating element away from the jury.
- **Almendarez-Torres v. United States:** Preserves its aggravated-reentry statutory construction and federal charging consequences; neither a general constitutional maximum rule nor a constitutional recidivism exception is attributed to it.
- **Jones v. United States**, No. 97-6203: Preserves its construction of aggravated carjacking grades and associated federal element protections; the constitutional issue reserved there is decided here only at the present-offense scope.
- **Castillo v. United States:** Preserves the statutory element treatment of the former firearm-type aggravators; it is an analogue, not an earlier constitutional holding.
- **Carter v. United States:** Preserves its statutory value-element and distinct offense-comparison holdings; it does not supply a prior constitutional answer or a state charging rule.
- **McMillan v. Pennsylvania:** Distinguished because its minimum left the maximum unchanged; that holding is not overruled.
- **Witte v. United States:** Preserves ordinary sentencing consideration within punishment authorized by the conviction; it supplies no additional maximum here.
- **United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra:** Preserves its within-authority sentencing operation; the Court does not extend it to the fact that first authorizes a higher maximum.
- **Monge v. California:** Preserves its bounded noncapital prior-conviction double-jeopardy classification and reservations of independent charging, jury and proof questions; it supplies no rule for this nonrecidivist fact or automatic authority for a further proceeding.
- **Neder v. United States:** Preserves its qualified actual-verdict inquiry for the materiality errors it decided; strong evidence does not supply an actual admission here, and no universal sentencing-error rule is adopted.
- **Walton v. Arizona:** Leaves its capital rule undisturbed; the present noncapital decision does not resolve its relation to the broader constitutional theories in separate writings.

**Current-force modification:** United States v. Angle, June 20, 2002, extends the present-offense element rule to facts raising either binding end of a punishment range, including Guidelines ceilings and floors below an outer statutory maximum, and overrules McMillan's contrary unchanged-maximum minimum rule. Ring v. Arizona, June 24, 2002, extends the rule to the statutory aggravator necessary for death eligibility and overrules Walton to that extent. Those former reservations no longer leave the covered allocations open; ordinary factfinding within independently authorized punishment, the unresolved prior-conviction and state-charging questions, and the distinction between capital eligibility and final selection remain at their stated scope.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Angle, June 20, 2002, binding punishment ranges and federal charging; Harris v. United States, June 24, 2002, mandatory brandishing minimum and its separate plain-error remedy; Ring v. Arizona, June 24, 2002, death-eligibility factfinding and qualified actual-verdict review.

### Parole information in staged capital sentencing

#### Shafer v. South Carolina, 532 U.S. 36

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-5250.
**Decided:** March 20, 2001.

##### When the parole-information right attaches in a staged capital sentencing procedure

**Holding:** When the State places future dangerousness in issue and a capital jury, after finding the required statutory aggravator, can choose only death or life without ordinary parole, due process requires an effective opportunity to explain that ineligibility through accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction. A lesser sentence available only to the judge if the jury finds no aggravator does not defeat that protection at the later jury sentencing choice; the rule governs this staged procedure and materially equivalent choices.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes for all three propositions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The preliminary aggravator finding does not itself exercise sentencing discretion. The State-raised-danger trigger and accurate legal ineligibility remain necessary; no broader right without future danger or independent Eighth Amendment ground is decided.

##### Whether the complete trial presentation supplied an effective opportunity if future danger was raised

**Holding:** If the prosecution put future dangerousness in issue, the repeated until-death definitions and permitted natural-life defense argument did not provide an effective informational opportunity in this case when considered together with the refusal of the requested statutory parole explanation and the supplemental direction excluding parole eligibility or ineligibility after the jury's questions. This determination concerns the legal adequacy of the complete presentation, requires no particular words, and makes no finding about any juror's actual belief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes for all three propositions.

**Material application:** Repeated until-death instructions and permitted natural-life advocacy did not suffice in combination with refusal of the statutory explanation and the supplemental exclusion of parole consideration. The conclusion rests on the whole presentation, not one phrase or the jury questions alone. It settles legal adequacy conditionally without finding juror belief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No particular wording or guarantee against clemency or future legislation is required. Accurate effective defense presentation or adequate judicial explanation suffices. The State may still offer truthful evidence of danger in prison.

##### What remains for state adjudication and what remedy follows a harmful error

**Holding:** The state court must determine on the trial record whether the prosecution's evidence or argument placed future dangerousness in issue; if it did, informational inadequacy is settled and the State must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the selection of death. If the State fails that burden, a new lawful penalty proceeding is required if it seeks death, or a lawful noncapital sentence may be imposed; if future dangerousness was not in issue, this Simmons due-process claim alone establishes no violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes for all three propositions.

**Material application:** The state court must assess the evidence, its purpose, argument and questions together; a prescribed phrase is unnecessary and qualitative or circumstantial proof cannot be disregarded. The jury's questions alone prove neither the dangerousness predicate nor its absence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded for the actual danger determination and, if present, Chapman harmlessness on the existing record. Informational adequacy may not be reopened. The judgment itself neither vacates the death sentence nor orders an immediate new hearing or substituted sentence; guilt and separate sentences remain intact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The error is not structural. No harmlessness or harmfulness is found; Simmons's own harmfulness finding does not decide this record. Ramdass's collateral-review conditions do not govern direct review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Simmons v. South Carolina, 512 U.S. 154 (1994):** Applied to the actual death-or-life choice in a staged sentencing procedure and to the complete instructional presentation. Its effective-opportunity rule, alternative means of explanation and direct-review remedy remain controlling. Its own harmfulness finding is confined to its record.
- **Gardner v. Florida, 430 U.S. 349 (1977):** Its opportunity to answer material capital-sentencing information supplies the due-process foundation; no new disclosure rule independent of the Simmons predicates is adopted.
- **Skipper v. South Carolina, 476 U.S. 1 (1986):** Its protection for rebutting a State future-danger case supports accurate parole information; its distinct mitigation protection remains intact.
- **California v. Ramos, 463 U.S. 992 (1983):** Distinguished to preserve truthful release-related information without treating hypothetical clemency as ordinary parole or requiring a guarantee against future legal change.
- **Ramdass v. Angelone, 530 U.S. 156 (2000):** Its attention to actual and conditional legal consequences is applied. Its separate habeas requirements and unresolved factual predicates do not control this direct-review remedy or establish error effect here.
- **Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967):** Applied without changing the State's burden to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that a constitutional error did not contribute to the death verdict.

### Capital mitigation mechanisms and confined habeas effect review

#### Penry v. Johnson, 532 U.S. 782

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-6677.
**Decided:** June 4, 2001.

##### Whether section 2254(d)(1) permits relief on the use of the earlier defense-requested psychiatric report

**Holding:** The state court reasonably distinguished a defense-requested competency report used to cross-examine a defense mental-condition expert from Estelle v. Smith's compelled, uncounseled capital examination. Its adjudication is neither contrary to nor an objectively unreasonable application of the federal law established at the February 22, 1995 state-merits cutoff, so section 2254(d)(1) forecloses this psychiatric claim without an actual-effect determination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The report's dangerousness conclusion was elicited over objection and repeated in closing; no absence of objection or universal subject-matter waiver is found. **Treatment of earlier authority:** Estelle v. Smith remains intact in its compelled-examination setting. Buchanan v. Kentucky supplies the defense-initiated evaluation and responsive-use distinction without universal waiver. Williams v. Taylor supplies distinct statutory clauses and objective reasonableness without importing an exclusive allocation proposal lacking a majority.

##### Whether the special issues and mitigation supplement provided a lawful way to choose life

**Holding:** Supported mitigation reducing culpability independently of the factual special issues requires an authorized, coherent means of giving that judgment effect in a life verdict. A supplement allowing a negative answer for mitigation is insufficient when the unchanged form makes that answer certify factual reasonable doubt despite the juror's belief that the fact is proved; in the complete charge and trial context here, the reasonable likelihood of restriction makes the state acceptance objectively unreasonable under established law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. No independent contrary-to holding is adopted.

**Material application:** Jurors could find deliberateness, future dangerousness and lack of reasonable provocation while treating intellectual impairment and childhood abuse as mitigating. The form required at least ten jurors' factual reasonable doubt for a negative answer and unanimity beyond reasonable doubt for an affirmative answer. The judge's similar selection-stage explanations and renewed defense closing were considered but did not reconcile that final certification conflict; neither forgotten instructions nor a separate prosecutorial prohibition is invented.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No favored mitigation weight, prescribed fourth question, categorical disability exemption or separate unanimity holding is required. The error is neither classified as structural nor equated with demonstrated effect.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lockett v. Ohio and Eddings v. Oklahoma protect meaningful culpability mitigation. Penry v. Lynaugh, Graham v. Collins and Johnson v. Texas supply the pre-1995 lawful effective-vehicle requirement. Boyde v. California requires reasonable likelihood in the complete charge and trial. Buchanan v. Angelone, Weeks v. Angelone and Shafer v. South Carolina retain their later, distinct roles and supply no backdated 1995 substantive benchmark.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Atkins v. Virginia, June 20, 2002, establishes a separate categorical bar to execution for established intellectual disability and requires a reliable status procedure. Penry's effective-mitigation protection remains intact; it did not already establish that categorical bar.

##### Which harmless-error burden governs this preserved original-charge mitigation restriction

**Holding:** For a preserved original-charge restriction of this kind on supported capital culpability mitigation, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt on the complete penalty record that the defect did not contribute to death. If it cannot, conditional sentence relief must allow a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence; the guilt judgment is unaffected.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct joins. Stevens and Kennedy support the corrective return but reject this standard; Scalia and Thomas oppose the mitigation disposition. Their distinct effect approaches are not aggregated under Marks.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The error does not automatically establish contribution, and answers to the factual special issues do not establish what an unconflicted mitigation decision would have been. The entire penalty record, including clarifications, must be considered; no present harmlessness or harmfulness finding is made. This new remedial category governs the present return and later applications, not every habeas error, a new retroactivity exception or clearly established substantive law at the 1995 cutoff.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The mitigation certificate disposition is reversed for an issue-specific appeal and remaining full-record effect inquiry, with constitutional error and section 2254(d)(1) eligibility settled. No present writ, sentence vacatur, fixed resentencing deadline, stay, release or execution order is entered; conditional relief follows only if the State fails its burden.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chapman v. California is expressly extended to this defined original-charge capital-mitigation category. Calderon v. Coleman's commutation-error allocation supports the extension without having already established a universal rule. Weeks's response-error standard, burden and uncertainty reservation remain unresolved. Brecht v. Abrahamson, O'Neal v. McAninch and California v. Roy retain their distinct category-specific force.

For reviewability of the certificate denial and the distinction between certification and a present writ, see Federal Courts - Capital habeas certificates and issue-specific appellate return, Penry v. Johnson, No. 00-6677, decided June 4, 2001.

- Solem v. Helm: applied for actual-offense proportionality, corroborative comparison and the difference between ordinary parole and discretionary grace.
- Harmelin v. Michigan: its surviving narrow gross-disproportionality principle governs; its wholesale-quantity application does not decide every completed drug sale.
- United States v. Bajakajian: remains confined to its fines framework and actual-offense application; its monetary proportionality rule is not converted into a prison-term formula.

### Forfeited quantity allegations and jury findings in enhanced sentences

#### United States v. Cotton, 535 U.S. 625

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-687.
**Decided:** May 20, 2002.

For Cotton, May 20, 2002, No. 01-687, on section 3231 jurisdiction and the confined overruling of Bain, see Federal Courts.

##### Which distinct plain-error requirements govern the omitted quantity protections

**Holding:** The enhanced sentences lack the required quantity allegation and jury determination, and the unpreserved objection is reviewed under Rule 52(b), not treated as an intentional waiver or a jurisdictional nullity. Error, plainness, an effect on substantial rights and a serious effect on the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings remain distinct requirements; substantial-rights effect is assumed here without deciding prejudice or structural classification.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine join O'Connor's Part II-A. Part II-B's affirmative intervening-law plainness rule has eight votes: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Scalia assumes plainness without adopting that rule.

**Material application:** An error is plain when controlling law changes settled contrary trial law before direct review ends and it is clear at appellate consideration. Initially unsettled trial law is not decided. Judicial preponderance findings, notice and physical-exhibit stipulations supply neither a missing indictment allegation, jury verdict nor aggravated-offense admission.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The third condition is assumed; the Court decides neither actual prejudice nor structural status. Intentional waiver and preserved-error review remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Apprendi supplies the element protections. Olano separates forfeiture and the four conditions; Johnson supplies the intervening-law rule without erasing the substantive right. Neder's preserved-error inquiry and Vonn's distinct Rule 11 record analysis remain unchanged.

##### Whether this record requires correction at the fourth plain-error condition

**Holding:** The forfeited quantity error does not seriously affect the fairness, integrity or public reputation of these proceedings in the established circumstances: proof that the conspiracy involved at least fifty grams of cocaine base is overwhelming and essentially uncontroverted at that statutory threshold. The enhanced sentences receive no relief on this claim, even assuming an effect on substantial rights; this is a particular appellate-remedy decision, not a substitute jury finding or a universal overwhelming-evidence exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, unanimous.

**Material application:** The jury found the conspiracy; the reported cocaine-base seizures totaled approximately 380 grams. Credibility and individualized Guidelines objections addressed much larger quantities and did not genuinely contest the fifty-gram conspiracy threshold. The original indictment matters only as notice, and no personal admission or general conspiracy-attribution rule is established.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is no substitute verdict, aggravated-offense finding, universal evidence exception or innocence prerequisite. Preserved claims, materially contested threshold evidence and different trial-strategy circumstances retain their own analysis.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the sentence vacatur and remand to implement rejection of these grounds. The enhanced sentences receive no relief on the forfeited claim; the remand does not reopen discretionary correction of the same omissions on the same record. Convictions stand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Olano and Johnson permit the particular fourth-condition denial independently of assumed substantial-rights injury. Apprendi's charging and jury protections remain; Vonn does not require a second discretionary appellate exercise in every forfeited-error case.

### Uncounseled misdemeanor adjudication and suspended jail

#### Alabama v. Shelton, 535 U.S. 654

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-1214.
**Decided:** May 20, 2002.

##### Whether suspended imprisonment requires counsel at the guilt adjudication

**Holding:** A State may not impose a suspended jail sentence upon an indigent defendant tried without counsel unless the defendant knowingly, intelligently and voluntarily waived counsel. The constitutional protection attaches to the adjudication authorizing imprisonment for the original offense, including a term whose execution is deferred; it does not require appointment merely because the statute permits imprisonment when the court selects a lawful noncustodial punishment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas disagree on present suspended-jail invalidation.

**Material application:** Ordinary Alabama revocation does not retry guilt or permit challenge to the assault conviction; counsel then cannot replace trial representation. A complete counseled trial de novo before incarceration is not foreclosed and would be functionally comparable to permissible pretrial probation, but no such procedure is found or ordered.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Safeguards short of complete retrial and counsel for freestanding probation remain undecided. The broader request for counsel whenever prison is statutorily possible was not properly presented.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Argersinger applies to the adjudication actually selecting imprisonment; Scott preserves lawful noncustodial punishment. Gagnon's distinct revocation protection does not substitute for original-trial counsel. Nichols preserves only its defined later counseled Guidelines use, with genuine opportunity to contest reliability and seek an authorized departure; it neither generally overrules Baldasar nor authorizes imprisonment for this earlier uncounseled offense.

##### Whether competent self-representation establishes waiver and which terms survive

**Holding:** Competence to represent oneself and awareness that counsel may be hired do not establish a knowing, intelligent and voluntary waiver of appointed counsel. With no valid waiver established here, the suspended jail term must remain vacated; the conviction and independent fine, restitution and costs remain intact, subject to any necessary state-law adjustment consistent with the bar on activating this jail term from the uncounseled conviction.

**Proposition-level authority:** The six-Justice Souter opinion controls the waiver rule and jail disposition. All nine support preserving the conviction and independent monetary punishment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Alabama's statement that its linked probation falls with the jail term is not a separate freestanding-probation holding. Its late proposed enforcement through counseled contempt, limited to five days or $100, was not passed on below and remains for initial state consideration.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm vacatur of the thirty-day suspended jail term; it cannot be activated from this uncounseled conviction. Preserve conviction, fine, restitution and costs, subject to lawful state adjustment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Godinez distinguishes capacity from actual knowing, intelligent and voluntary relinquishment. The record does not establish advice that appointed counsel would be supplied if unaffordable.

### Excessive fines and combined False Claims Act recovery

#### United States v. Mackby

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit No. 99-15605; review of 261 F.3d 821; no Supreme Court docket is supplied.
**Decided:** June 3, 2002

##### Whether combined fixed FCA penalties and treble damages constitute a covered fine

**Holding:** The False Claims Act's fixed civil penalties and treble damages imposed together for Mackby's false Medicare claims constitute an exaction at least partly punitive and therefore subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. Their compensatory and regulatory purposes remain relevant to the constitutional assessment but do not defeat coverage of this combined recovery.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies nine votes for the complete coverage holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Coverage concerns the combined $555,000 fixed penalties and $174,454.92 treble recovery, not every isolated treble award or materially different statutory combination. It establishes neither actual excessiveness nor application of every criminal-procedure rule. Hudson's Double Jeopardy classification and retained personal-penalty inquiry remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Austin v. United States, 509 U.S. 602: its partly punitive coverage rule and distinction between coverage and actual excessiveness are applied to this direct monetary recovery; its disputed historical forfeiture explanation is not made necessary here.
- United States v. Bornstein, 423 U.S. 303: its recovery and fraud-deterrence functions support examination of the scheme's actual operation rather than treating all multiplied recovery as solely compensation.
- Rex Trailer Co. v. United States, 350 U.S. 148: its compensatory account of liquidated recovery remains relevant but does not eliminate the independent punishment function of this combination.
- Hudson v. United States, decided December 10, 1997: its Double Jeopardy rules, including the retained Halper personal-penalty inquiry, remain distinct and supply no Excessive Fines exemption or universal cost ceiling.
- Vermont Agency of Natural Resources v. United States ex rel. Stevens, 529 U.S. 765: its assigned-claim standing and sovereign-state-defendant holdings are preserved; no separate categorical treble-damages punishment rule is drawn from them.

##### Whether the aggregate demand is governed by gross disproportionality to the actual offense

**Holding:** Under Bajakajian, this combined monetary demand violates the Excessive Fines Clause only if grossly disproportionate to the gravity of Mackby's actual offense, assessed with substantial respect for legislative punishment judgments and appropriate attention to culpability, repeated conduct, harm, compensatory functions and sanction severity. The Ninth Circuit's remand for development and application of that inquiry stands, while established FCA liability remains intact and no replacement award or final excessiveness determination is made.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion of the Court has nine joins for this governing standard, its application and the remand: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The distinct appellate-review paragraph has the narrower authority stated below.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The assessment retains the established false Medicare claims and examines culpability, repetition, harm, compensatory purposes and severity where relevant. These considerations create no exhaustive checklist, fixed multiplier, direct-loss ratio, government-cost ceiling or obligation to prove the precise sum indispensable to deterrence. Legislative authorization and minimum statutory penalties do not conclusively decide constitutionality. The liability judgment remains intact; the remand determines proportionality without a predetermined replacement sum.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bajakajian, 524 U.S. 321: its gross-disproportionality standard, legislative respect and distinct appellate/factual review rules are applied; its particular reporting-offense application does not determine this fraud sanction.

##### Whether the constitutional determination receives independent appellate review

**Holding:** An appellate court independently reviews the constitutional determination whether this combined punitive demand is grossly disproportionate, while reviewing supported underlying factual findings for clear error. Independent constitutional review does not authorize inventing an additional offense or substituting unsupported adverse facts for the findings.

**Proposition-level authority:** The review-standard paragraph of Part II of Breyer's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplying eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela joins Part II's proportionality standard and disposition but not this paragraph.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent constitutional review preserves clear-error review of supported subsidiary findings. It does not permit new offenses or unsupported adverse factual substitutions and supplies no universal standard for every mixed question.

#### Atkins v. Virginia, 536 U.S. 304

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-8452.
**Decided:** 2002-06-20.

##### Execution is unavailable for established intellectual disability

**Holding:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments prohibit execution of a person with established intellectual disability: significantly impaired intellectual functioning, substantial adaptive limitations and developmental onset. The State must provide a reliable procedure to determine that status; a score, diagnostic label or courtroom impression alone does not establish it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; seven Justices adopt the complete rule and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision establishes no numerical cutoff, proof allocation or universal hearing design. It does not resolve retroactivity in another collateral case or other disability categories. Intellectual functioning, adaptive limitations and developmental onset must be evaluated together; individual mitigation alone does not satisfy the categorical restriction.

**Material application:** Atkins’s defense psychologist reported an IQ of 59 and diagnosed disability, while the State expert disputed that diagnosis. The Court does not resolve this conflict or treat the reported score as conclusive.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the death-sentence judgment and remand for reliable individualized determination. Established intellectual disability requires a lawful noncapital disposition. The convictions remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Trop supplies the evolving-standards inquiry using objective domestic evidence. Coker and Enmund supply categorical proportionality grounded in culpability and legitimate punishment purposes. Ford supports lawful procedures implementing a constitutional death restriction without equating incompetence and intellectual disability. Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 (1989), is overruled only insofar as it permits execution of persons with established intellectual disability. Penry v. Johnson retains its requirement of effective mitigation where death remains lawful.

#### United States v. Angle, 254 F.3d 514 (judgment below)

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit Nos. 96-4662, 96-4672 and 99-4187; consolidated appeals of Corey Angle and James Edward Phifer.
**Decided:** 2002-06-20.

##### Present-offense facts changing a binding punishment range receive element protections

**Holding:** A fact concerning the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to raise either the binding ceiling or the binding floor of punishment must be validly admitted as an element or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to a jury, unless a lawful jury waiver permits the judge to determine the element under that same proof standard. A larger outer statutory maximum does not make an enforceable inner Guidelines range discretionary; ordinary judicial assessment remains permissible within punishment independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I-A of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct rationale votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Guidelines remain binding subject to the constitutional conditions. Prior-conviction questions, a general advisory-system remedy and consecutive sentencing independent of the disputed range premise are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Angle's independent findings-based sentence vacatur is affirmed unanimously; six Justices add the constitutional resentencing directions. The lawful range and supported findings within it must be determined without a judicially supplied replacement range.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Apprendi is extended to present-offense facts raising binding Guidelines ceilings or floors. McMillan is overruled insofar as ordinary present-offense sentencing findings may raise a mandatory minimum merely because the maximum is unchanged. Mistretta retains institutional approval of the Commission. Witte and Watts/Putra retain ordinary assessment within independently authorized punishment, subject to the binding-range rule.

##### Federal charging protection accompanies the covered aggravated fact

**Holding:** In a federal prosecution, a present-offense fact performing the aggravating legal work identified in Part I-A must receive the applicable grand-jury charging protection as an element. A quantity-free charge and verdict, ordinary sentencing findings, or notice of possible exposure do not themselves establish that element; a valid element admission or lawful waiver must satisfy its own governing requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I-B of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; five direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins the disposition on this component without adopting the separate charging proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A valid admission or waiver must satisfy its own requirements. The mandate supplies no new charging or jury procedure.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Jones and Castillo supply federal aggravated-element charging consequences while retaining their distinct statutory holdings. Cotton confirms that the constitutional charging omission does not withdraw subject-matter jurisdiction.

##### Phifer's actual drug-count term is erroneous and plain

**Holding:** Phifer's 292-month drug-count sentence exceeds the 240 months authorized by his quantity-free verdict and is constitutional error. Applying the established review-time rule in the settled-law-then-changed setting, the count excess is plain; substantial-rights effect and discretionary correction remain separate requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Thomas and Ginsburg; five direct rationale votes. Scalia agrees that the count is erroneous and assumes plainness without joining the affirmative timing application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The plainness holding applies Johnson's settled-trial-law/intervening-law setting. It does not announce a general rule for initially unsettled trial law or declare the newly decided Guidelines rule settled at original sentencing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Apprendi supplies the verdict-authorized count limit; Johnson supplies limited review-time plainness and Cotton applies that framework to a quantity omission. Olano retains separate substantial-rights and discretionary-correction conditions.

##### An unsupported mandatory aggregate cannot dispose of the remaining plain-error inquiry

**Holding:** A demonstrated lawful and inevitable identical aggregate may bear on substantial-rights effect under Rule 52(b), but unused cumulative statutory exposure or a supposed mandatory total derived from the constitutionally disputed quantity findings does not itself establish that result. Because the categorical no-prejudice ruling depends on that defective range premise, the appellate affirmance of Phifer's sentence must be vacated for the remaining substantial-rights and discretionary-correction inquiry on the actual record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct rationale votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The defendant ordinarily must show an outcome effect. Correction remains separately discretionary and requires an error seriously affecting the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Neither a general sufficiency characterization nor Cotton's different threshold record establishes Phifer's prejudice or correction outcome.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The appellate affirmance of Phifer's sentence is vacated and remanded for the remaining Rule 52(b) inquiry. His sentence is not itself vacated. Both convictions remain intact; the mandate orders no advisory system, enhancement jury or consecutive terms.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Olano preserves all four forfeiture conditions and the ordinary defendant burden. Vonn permits consideration of the relevant existing record. Cotton permits denial at the separate fourth condition on a supported record without transposing its evidentiary result here. Neder's qualified preserved-error framework does not shift the burden to the Government.

#### Harris v. United States, 536 U.S. 545

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-10666.
**Decided:** 2002-06-24.

##### The amended statute uses a mandatory sentencing condition

**Holding:** Amended §924(c)(1)(A) defines the basic firearm offense and assigns graduated mandatory minimums; brandishing is a sentencing condition under that structure, a classification that does not dispense with constitutional element protections. The opening exception applies to the extent a greater minimum is otherwise provided by §924(c) or another provision of law; brandishing requires displaying all or part of the firearm, or otherwise making its presence known to another person, for the purpose of intimidating that person, whether or not the firearm is directly visible.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Scalia's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, supplies nine direct votes for this construction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The construction preserves the five-, seven- and ten-year minimum structure but independently adjudicates only the brandishing condition. No dispute about the greater-minimum exception is decided. The statutory sentencing label neither prescribes preponderance findings nor eliminates constitutional charging protection.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Jones supplies the interpretive method and retains its carjacking construction. Castillo is distinguished because its former firearm-type text differs materially; its statutory-element holding remains intact.

##### Compulsory additional punishment requires element protections

**Holding:** A present-offense fact legally necessary to raise a mandatory minimum must receive the charging protections applicable to the prosecution and must be validly admitted as an element or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to the proper guilt factfinder. A lawful jury waiver permits the judge to determine the element under that same proof standard; it neither enlarges the indictment nor converts an ordinary sentencing finding into the required adjudication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for the rule and its federal application.

**Material application:** Harris's indictment omitted brandishing. The lawful bench trial and jury waiver did not establish the distinct intimidating conduct as an element; the contested preponderance finding was neither a valid admission nor the required aggravated adjudication.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary judicial selection remains lawful within an independently established range. Prior-conviction facts, collateral retroactivity and general sentencing redesign are outside this decision; no constitutional prior-conviction exception is created.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Angle is applied, including its prior overruling of McMillan to the stated extent. Apprendi supplies the punishment-authorization principle; Gaudin and Winship supply proper factfinding and reasonable doubt. Jones and Castillo retain their independent federal charging consequences.

##### This record satisfies all four plain-error requirements

**Holding:** Assuming without deciding that Harris forfeited the constitutional theory, Rule 52(b) requires error, clarity at review in this settled-law-then-changed setting, the defendant's ordinary showing of outcome-affecting prejudice, and a separate determination that the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings before discretionary correction is warranted. Harris satisfies each condition on this record; the Court reverses the sentence-sustaining judgment, vacates the firearm sentence and remands for lawful resentencing while preserving the conviction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Scalia's opinion has the same six direct joins as Part II, including its affirmative plainness application, independent prejudice finding and discretionary correction.

**Material application:** The judge treated disputed brandishing as a close question and imposed the resulting minimum through §2K2.4. The carrying verdict did not decide intimidation purpose beyond a reasonable doubt. Although the judge regarded seven years as appropriate, he declined to select that term independently; the actual record supports a reasonable probability of a different lawful outcome. Genuinely contested conduct without element protections compelled additional imprisonment, independently satisfying the correction condition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither prejudice nor correction is presumed. The Court does not decide preservation because relief follows even assuming forfeiture. Review-time plainness concerns settled law changed on direct review, not initially unsettled law. The Government does not bear the defendant's ordinary prejudice burden.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence-sustaining judgment is reversed, the seven-year firearm sentence is vacated, and resentencing must begin with the five-year minimum and applicable guideline consequence. Any different term requires an independently lawful basis and reasons. The basic conviction stands; further aggravated proceedings require independent charging, waiver, procedural and double-jeopardy authority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Olano's four conditions and ordinary burden are applied; Johnson supplies limited review-time plainness but its refusal of correction is distinguished. Cotton preserves nonjurisdictional treatment and record-specific discretion. Neder retains its distinct preserved-error burden; Vonn permits relevant existing-record consideration without a burden shift.

#### Ring v. Arizona, 536 U.S. 584

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-488.
**Decided:** 2002-06-24.

##### The jury must determine the fact that first authorizes death

**Holding:** A statutory present-offense aggravating fact necessary to make death legally available must be validly admitted as an element or proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt in a jury-tried prosecution. Walton is overruled insofar as it permits a sentencing judge, sitting without a jury, to supply that necessary aggravating fact; existing lawful jury-waiver requirements remain, and waiver does not lower the reasonable-doubt burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplying seven direct votes. Breyer's judgment vote rests on a different Eighth Amendment ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the present-offense aggravator first authorizing death, not mitigation, ultimate selection or the complete weighing process. State grand-jury incorporation, prior-conviction facts and collateral retroactivity remain outside the question. No constitutional prior-conviction exception or new general capital procedure is created. Appellate selection reweighing cannot supply the missing eligibility adjudication.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Apprendi's rule is extended to the expressly reserved capital-eligibility boundary; Angle is applied as supporting range doctrine. Walton's contrary necessary-aggravator allocation is overruled. Winship and Gaudin supply proof and factfinder protections; Harris v. Alabama retains its distinct final-selection holding.

##### The omitted eligibility finding receives qualified actual-verdict review

**Holding:** This omitted statutory eligibility finding is subject to the qualified Neder inquiry: the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the omission did not contribute to the actual verdict, examining the actual instructions, the evidence they directed to the jury, required findings and material conflicts. Necessary equivalent jury findings suffice but are not exclusive; supported whole-record inferences may establish noncontribution only when reliably connected to the actual adjudication, without new credibility findings or substitution of sufficient evidence for a hypothetical verdict.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Kennedy's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Thomas, supplying five direct votes for the method and conditional remedy. Scalia and Ginsburg permit only genuine equivalent jury findings and do not join this broader method; neither Breyer nor O'Connor supplies an additional rationale vote.

**Material application:** Arizona's contention that the robbery verdict necessarily establishes pecuniary gain requires comparison of the actual aggravator, offense elements, instructions and necessary findings, plus any qualifying record inference. The offense label or strong evidence alone does not decide it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State bears the burden throughout. New credibility determinations are forbidden, and appellate selection reweighing cannot replace the actual-verdict inquiry. The Court decides neither harmlessness nor harmfulness on this record.

**Operative remedy or transition:** If the State fails to establish noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt, the death sentence must be vacated; further sentencing needs independent state-law authority and compliance with constitutional and double-jeopardy limits. If the State carries its burden, a consistent sentence judgment may be reinstated. Initial harmlessness remand itself neither unconditionally vacates the sentence nor authorizes a new enhancement trial.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Neder's qualified actual-verdict method is extended to the omitted eligibility finding; Chapman retains the State's burden. Roy and Yates supply the instruction-centered inquiry. Sullivan's distinct structural rule remains intact. Clemons retains lawful appellate reweighing but supplies no missing eligibility adjudication.

##### The appellate affirmance is reversed and the actual review contention returns

**Holding:** The Arizona Supreme Court's judgment is reversed insofar as it sustains Ring's death sentence under judge-only eligibility factfinding, and the case is remanded for proper consideration of the State's actual harmlessness contention. The felony-murder conviction stands; the appellate reversal is distinct from unconditional vacatur of the death sentence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Kennedy's opinion is joined by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six support the reversal rationale; Breyer adds a seventh reversal-and-remand vote on his independent selection ground. Stone-Zsela would reverse the judge-only constitutional ruling, vacate the appellate affirmance and remand, and therefore does not join Part III. His agreement with Parts I and II remains distinct from this formal disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The seven votes to reverse do not enlarge the separate five-Justice harmlessness-method coalition. Stone-Zsela's proposed appellate vacatur preserves the same initial inquiry and conditional sentence relief; Breyer's independent selection rationale supplies no additional eligibility or harmlessness-rule vote.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court reverses the death-sentence affirmance and remands, by seven reversal votes, one vote to vacate and one to affirm. The felony-murder conviction remains intact. The remand leaves any further sentencing to governing constitutional limits and independently available state law.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Walton no longer supports the challenged eligibility allocation. Neder supplies the distinction between reversing an erroneous legal premise and initially applying the proper harmlessness rule below.

#### United States v. Ruiz, 536 U.S. 622

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-595.
**Decided:** June 24, 2002.

##### The asserted legal error must be examined to determine sentence-review authority

**Holding:** Section 3742(a)(1) permits a court to determine whether an asserted constitutional condition connected to a sentence establishes that the sentence was imposed in violation of law; a merely discretionary refusal to depart, when the judge understood the discretion, supplies no such appeal. A mistaken belief that departure authority was absent could supply legal error, but that premise is not present here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court has the direct support of Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela also joins this statutory inquiry. Thomas agrees in his concurrence that the claimed statutory basis can be determined, without joining the Court opinion.

**Material application:** The judge understood departure authority; the rejected condition, withdrawn offer and requested sentencing treatment connected the constitutional allegation to the sentence. Examination of that allegation determines the claimed statutory basis rather than deciding unrelated merits on assumed jurisdiction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mine Workers permits determination of jurisdiction; Steel Co.’s requirement of an actual jurisdictional basis remains. This inquiry creates no general appellate review of discretionary leniency.

##### The condition supplies no ground for vacating this sentence

**Holding:** Because the identified disclosure condition is not unconstitutional at the scope decided, section 3742(a)(1) supplies no authority on this theory to review the understood discretionary refusal to depart; the Ninth Circuit's sentence vacatur and condition-based remand are reversed. Ruiz's unaccepted offer creates no enforceable recommendation, and her later guilty plea without agreement did not itself assent to the rejected waiver; her conviction remains undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Breyer’s opinion, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg: seven direct rationale votes. Thomas concurs in reversal on his separate ground; Stone-Zsela dissents from reversal.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Lack of entitlement to a benefit or rejection of an offer is not itself a rule that an unlawful condition cannot injure. Here the asserted unconstitutional-condition predicate fails. The Court adds no innocence prerequisite or second harmlessness hurdle to a completed constitutional violation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit’s sentence vacatur and condition-based remand, 8–1; the district sentence and conviction remain operative against this challenge.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mezzanatto’s knowing, voluntary and limited impeachment-use agreement remains enforceable; it supplies no universal waiver power and does not convert Ruiz’s rejected offer into an accepted agreement.

The separate impeachment and affirmative-defense disclosure holdings in United States v. Ruiz, No. 01-595, decided June 24, 2002, appear under Criminal Procedure.
