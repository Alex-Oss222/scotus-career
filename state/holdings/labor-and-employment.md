## Labor and Employment

### ERISA employee classification

#### Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, 503 U.S. 318 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1802.
**Decided:** March 24, 1992.

##### Which meaning of employee governs ERISA’s circular definition

**Holding:** ERISA’s circular definition of employee incorporates the general common law of agency, not a particular State’s law or a substitute test of benefit expectations, reliance and bargaining power. Courts must weigh all incidents of the relationship—including control over manner and means, skill, tools, work location, duration, assignment rights, scheduling discretion, payment, assistants, regular-business integration, the hiring party’s business, benefits and tax treatment—with no single factor decisive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court, supported by all nine Justices; all nine Justices adopt the agency construction and multifactor inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A contractual label or single factor is not decisive. Benefits do not replace classification. The FLSA’s broader express definition remains distinct; Darden’s status, vesting, forfeiture, benefits and independent state-contract remedies are not decided. The denied Extended Earnings Plan cross-petition is outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit’s judgment is reversed and remanded for application of all relevant common-law agency incidents and further proceedings consistent with the resulting classification; no employee classification or benefit award is made here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Community for Creative Non-Violence v. Reid, 490 U.S. 730 — applied to ERISA: general common-law agency is the default meaning of an unhelpfully defined employee term.
- NLRB v. United Insurance Co., 390 U.S. 254 — applied: all incidents must be assessed with no single decisive factor.
- NLRB v. Hearst Publications, 322 U.S. 111 — limited as a general interpretive model: purpose alone does not displace agency meaning in ERISA.
- United States v. Silk, 331 U.S. 704 — its general purposive emphasis is not followed for undefined employee terms; Reid supplies the governing presumption.
- Rutherford Food Corp. v. McComb, 331 U.S. 722 — distinguished and preserved: FLSA’s broader express definition supports its different reach.
- Kelley v. Southern Pacific, 419 U.S. 318; Baker v. Texas & Pacific, 359 U.S. 227; Robinson v. Baltimore & Ohio, 237 U.S. 84 — retained as supporting applications of ordinary agency meaning.

### Military leave and reemployment

#### King v. St. Vincent's Hospital, 502 U.S. 215 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-889.
**Decided:** December 16, 1991.

##### Whether 38 U.S.C. §2024(d) contains an implied duration limit on covered military service

**Holding:** Section 2024(d) contains no implicit reasonableness limit on the duration of otherwise covered military service. Neither a categorical three-year exclusion nor a free-ranging durational-reasonableness balance may be added to the provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion; Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter support the proposition unanimously among the eight participants.

**Material application:** The Army required the three-year tour associated with King's position. Neighboring service categories contain express duration limits, while §2024(d) ties protected leave to the period required for covered duty without adding such a cap.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns only an implied duration restriction. It does not excuse failure to satisfy the provision's enacted coverage, notice, service, or return requirements, decide damages, or determine obligations after a failure properly to return.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is reversed and the case remanded with the implied and categorical duration limits rejected.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Morash supplies the requirement to read provisions within the whole enactment. Fishgold supports protective construction of reemployment rights, while text and structure control this decision. Circuit precedent adding a reasonableness condition is rejected. Wolas remains statute-specific.

### Nonemployee organizing access

#### Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, 502 U.S. 527 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-970.
**Decided:** January 27, 1992.

##### When nonemployee organizers may obtain access despite nondiscriminatory exclusion from private property

**Holding:** For a nondiscriminatory exclusion policy, nonemployee organizers must show that employees are practically beyond reasonable offsite communication before the Board may balance a need for entry against the employer's property rights; greater convenience or organizing success does not satisfy that threshold. This workforce's community residence, public approaches and substantial direct contacts defeat the asserted exception, while discriminatory exclusion, employees' own solicitation and genuinely inaccessible workforces remain governed by their distinct rules.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices control both the statutory rule and denial of enforcement.

**Material application:** Employees lived in the surrounding community; organizers used public approaches and obtained direct home-contact information for roughly one fifth of the workforce. Few authorization cards did not establish an inability to communicate. Board expertise remains relevant to actual access obstacles, within the judicially established statutory rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court requires neither organizational success nor exhaustion of every imaginable communication technique. Employees' own solicitation, discriminatory exclusion, lawful public-property advocacy, and genuinely inaccessible workforces retain their separate treatment. No new general deference doctrine or constitutional right of entry is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The First Circuit judgment is reversed and enforcement denied. The Board's order is set aside insofar as it requires nonemployee-organizer access on these facts; no remand for a new balancing exercise is ordered, and the judgment permits no interference with employees' distinct organizing rights or lawful public activity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *NLRB v. Babcock & Wilcox Co.*, 351 U.S. 105 — applied and reaffirmed: employee and nonemployee activity differ, and demonstrated inaccessibility precedes balancing under a nondiscriminatory rule.
- *Republic Aviation Corp. v. NLRB*, 324 U.S. 793 — employees' own organizing rights remain distinct and are not narrowed here.
- *Central Hardware Co. v. NLRB*, 407 U.S. 539 — applied for the prior showing of a need for nonemployee access.
- *Hudgens v. NLRB*, 424 U.S. 507 — accommodation language is preserved within Babcock's nonemployee threshold, not treated as authorization for universal balancing.
- *Sears, Roebuck & Co. v. Carpenters*, 436 U.S. 180 — applied for the heavy burden concerning alternative access and the separate discrimination route.
- *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837, and *Maislin Industries, U.S., Inc. v. Primary Steel, Inc.*, 497 U.S. 116 — general deference remains; a later agency approach must respect a governing judicial statutory construction.
- *Jean Country*, 291 N.L.R.B. 11 — rejected insofar as its universal balancing replaces the antecedent inaccessibility inquiry for nondiscriminatory exclusion of nonemployee organizers; other contexts are not adjudicated.

### Union-constitution enforcement

#### Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71, 502 U.S. 93 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-967.  
**Decided:** December 4, 1991.

##### Whether an individual member-beneficiary may enforce a qualifying interunion constitution under section 301

**Holding:** Section 301 permits an individual union member who is a beneficiary of a qualifying contract between labor organizations to sue for its alleged violation. An international constitution binding the international and its local retains its interunion-contract character when a member invokes it.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; all eight participants.

**Material application:** Section 301 classifies covered contracts rather than limiting plaintiffs to signatory organizations. Uniform federal interpretation applies when a beneficiary enforces the qualifying interunion contract.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary contract predicates remain necessary. The Court does not classify standalone local bylaws, federalize every internal union dispute, or decide breach, defenses, or damages.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The dismissal of the union-constitution claim is reversed and the claim restored for §301 proceedings; no liability or damages are awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Plumbers v. Local 334 supplies the international constitution's interunion-contract status. Smith v. Evening News Association permits individual beneficiaries to enforce covered labor agreements.

For the Seventh Amendment jury holding in Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local 71, decided December 4, 1991, No. 90-967, see Federal Courts — Civil jury right.

### Age motivation and willful discrimination

#### Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins, 507 U.S. 604 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1600.

**Decided:** April 20, 1993.

##### Service-based vesting is distinct from age motivation

**Holding:** ADEA disparate treatment requires age actually to motivate the employment decision and have a determinative influence; pension interference based only on years of service does not by itself establish that requirement. Liability remains possible when pension status is deliberately used as an age proxy or age and pension status both motivate the decision; age-triggered vesting and disparate impact are not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Aikens, 460 U.S. 711, and Burdine, 450 U.S. 248 — applied: actual motivation may be proved circumstantially; no automatic inference from any unlawful motive.
- Ingersoll-Rand, 498 U.S. 133 — distinguished: ERISA pension interference and age discrimination remain distinct.
- McDonnell Douglas, 411 U.S. 792 — preserved; its legitimate-reason terminology does not make every improper motive an ADEA violation.

##### Thurston governs individual acts as well as formal policies

**Holding:** An ADEA disparate-treatment violation is willful when the employer knew or recklessly disregarded whether its conduct violated the Act, whether the decision was individual or a formal policy. Once that showing is made, liquidated damages require no additional outrageousness, direct evidence or predominant-age motive; a good-faith, nonreckless mistake about a statutory permission is not willfulness.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thurston, 469 U.S. 111 — reaffirmed and applied to individual disparate-treatment decisions.
- Richland Shoe, 486 U.S. 128 — applied: negligence and mere awareness of statutory relevance are not willfulness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Pension status is no safe harbor when used as an age proxy. Age-based vesting and disparate impact are not decided. A nonreckless good-faith mistake about a statutory defense or exemption can defeat willfulness without defeating ordinary liability. No direct-evidence, outrageousness or predominant-age requirement is added.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand for reconsideration of ADEA sufficiency and, if needed, willfulness. The younger replacement, confidentiality demands and comments are not held sufficient or insufficient here. Independent ERISA and state-law judgments retain their own effect; no liquidated-damages award is made.

### ERISA preemption of benefit mandates

#### District of Columbia v. Greater Washington Board of Trade, 506 U.S. 125 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1326.

**Decided:** December 14, 1992.

##### Covered-plan content as a mandatory legal measure

**Holding:** ERISA §514(a) preempts this continuation obligation insofar as it incorporates an ERISA-covered health plan’s benefit content as the legal measure of mandatory replacement coverage, even when an employer may administer the replacement benefits separately through an exempt workers’ compensation arrangement. No §514(b) savings claim is decided, and this ruling does not preempt requirements measured solely by an ERISA-exempt governmental or church plan.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; eight Justices adopt the same operative rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Shaw v. Delta Air Lines, Inc., 463 U.S. 85 — applied; its separate-unit permission concerns independently fixed exempt benefits and does not eliminate operative reference to a different covered plan.
- Mackey v. Lanier Collection Agency & Service, Inc., 486 U.S. 825 — applied; specific legal reference is distinguished from ordinary general garnishment.
- Ingersoll-Rand Co. v. McClendon, 498 U.S. 133 — applied; dependence on a covered plan can trigger preemption despite the nominal subject of the state rule.
- Alessi v. Raybestos-Manhattan, Inc., 451 U.S. 504 — applied; regulation through workers’ compensation does not avoid ERISA’s covered-plan restriction.
- Fort Halifax Packing Co. v. Coyne, 482 U.S. 1 — distinguished; a one-time payment without ongoing administration differs from this health plan.
- Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. v. Massachusetts, 471 U.S. 724 — preserved; its insurance-saving holding supplies no additional §4(b) escape from a covered-plan reference.
- Morales v. Trans World Airlines, June 1, 1992 — consistent similar-language analogy only; its aviation holding does not replace ERISA’s own coverage and saving provisions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The obligation's 52-week cap is not a federal safe harbor. A separately maintained workers' compensation, unemployment-compensation or disability-insurance arrangement may remain exempt under §4(b)(3), but that does not exempt the different covered plan used as the mandatory benchmark. Exempt-only governmental or church-plan measures, §514(b) savings claims and ordinary indirect economic effects are outside the holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm preemption in the covered-plan-dependent application; the continuation mandate cannot be enforced to that extent.

### Proprietary public construction contracts

#### Building & Construction Trades Council v. Associated Builders & Contractors (Boston Harbor), 507 U.S. 218 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-261; No. 91-274.

**Decided:** March 8, 1993.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Massachusetts Water Resources Authority v. Associated Builders & Contractors, No. 91-274; consolidated with No. 91-261. The same unanimous holding and reversal/remand govern both dockets.

##### Genuine proprietary project purchases are not state labor regulation

**Holding:** Garmon and Machinists preemption do not prohibit a State's project-specific contracting condition when the State pursues its own proprietary interests as owner and purchaser, the analogous private conduct is lawful, and Congress has not expressly or impliedly displaced that proprietary conduct. MWRA's Bid Specification 13.1 meets those conditions; it governs performance of the Boston Harbor project rather than imposing general labor policy on unrelated conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, joined by the other eight Justices: unanimous controlling authority in both dockets.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- San Diego Building Trades Council v. Garmon, 359 U.S. 236 (1959): preserved as protecting the NLRA/NLRB field against state regulation; genuine proprietary purchasing does not trigger that rule.
- Machinists v. Wisconsin Employment Relations Commission, 427 U.S. 132 (1976): preserved as protecting Congress's intended free play of economic forces; a lawful project purchase is not regulatory displacement of those forces.
- Wisconsin Department of Industry v. Gould Inc., 475 U.S. 282 (1986): distinguished; debarment to enforce labor law against unrelated conduct was regulatory, while this contract concerns the State's purchased performance.
- Golden State Transit Corp. v. Los Angeles, 475 U.S. 608 (1986): distinguished; conditioning a sovereign operating license on labor settlement is regulation, not a purchase of the licensee's services.
- Jim McNeff, Inc. v. Todd, 461 U.S. 260 (1983): applied for construction prehire agreements under § 8(f); no general immunity from the Act follows.
- Woelke & Romero Framing, Inc. v. NLRB, 456 U.S. 645 (1982): applied for the construction-industry subcontracting proviso under § 8(e).
- NLRB v. Iron Workers, 434 U.S. 335 (1978): preserved concerning the § 8(f) proviso permitting employees to use elections under §§ 9(c), (e) to reject representation or union-security arrangements.
- Rice v. Santa Fe Elevator Corp., 331 U.S. 218 (1947), and Maryland v. Louisiana, 451 U.S. 725 (1981): applied as caution against inferring congressional displacement not supplied by the federal scheme.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Public ownership and procurement labels alone are insufficient. The lawful private analogy is undisputed here; §8(e)’s construction proviso and §8(f)’s prehire permission remain distinct, including employee elections under §§9(c), (e). The decision does not make MWRA an NLRA employer or adjudicate §8(d), ERISA, equal protection, due process, Sherman Act or state-law alternatives.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the First Circuit in both dockets and remand. The preliminary injunction cannot rest on the rejected Garmon or Machinists preemption theory; properly remaining independent grounds are not resolved.

#### Chamber of Commerce v. Reich

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 95-5242; no Supreme Court docket assigned.
**Decided:** January 14, 1997.

For the separate questions in Chamber of Commerce v. Reich, D.C. Circuit No. 95-5242; no Supreme Court docket assigned decided January 14, 1997, see Federal Courts — Prospective equitable relief (Review of the implementing officer).

##### The procurement delegation does not authorize this labor-policy sanction

**Holding:** The Procurement Act's general economy-and-efficiency delegation does not authorize Executive Order 12954's government-wide disqualification based on permanent replacement of economic strikers, because that sanction alters the bargaining choices Congress preserved under the NLRA. This statutory holding leaves Congress free to change those choices and preserves ordinary review of materially different proprietary conditions and lawful centralized purchasing standards.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), unanimous.

**Material application:** The restriction reaches contracts above $100,000. Termination for convenience is discretionary and subject to the contracting agency head's objection. Debarment retains dispute-resolution and compelling-reason exceptions and reaches the responsible unit plus affiliates capable of supplying the contract. Resolution factors include settlement or agreed procedures, informal resolution, return of strikers and other evidence that the dispute has ended; the Court does not equate them with a universal union-approval condition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal single-project rule, constitutional limit on Congress, mandatory procurement-findings procedure or guarantee of government business is adopted. The statutory result does not mechanically transplant state labor-preemption doctrine to every federal procurement measure.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The rejection of the challenged implementation is affirmed; prospective officer relief on remand must address that restriction alone. The review-and-waiver holding appears under Federal Courts, Prospective equitable relief.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mackay Radio supplies the protected role of permanent replacements for economic strikers without changing unfair-labor-practice strikers' protection. Insurance Agents and Machinists identify bargaining choices left to the parties. Boston Harbor is distinguished by its supported proprietary bargain; its lawful-project rule survives without a numerical or single-project restriction.

### Scheduled occupational hearing-loss compensation

#### Bath Iron Works Corp. v. Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs, 506 U.S. 153 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-871.

**Decided:** January 12, 1993.

##### Compensation branch and injury date

**Holding:** Noise-induced occupational hearing loss sustained during workplace exposure is a scheduled, presumptively disabling injury governed by §908(c)(13), whether claimed before or after retirement; the injury is complete at last exposure, which supplies the injury date for the wage basis. Later awareness or filing does not make it an occupational disease that does not immediately result in disability under §910(i), and §908(c)(23)’s special retiree formula therefore does not apply.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined by the other eight Justices; nine votes for the rule and application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Potomac Electric Power Co. v. Director, OWCP, 449 U.S. 268 (1980), is applied: scheduled compensation is mandatory within its statutory branch and does not require actual earning loss. Todd Shipyards Corp. v. Black, 717 F.2d 1280 (9th Cir. 1983), is distinguished as a latent-disease case; its rationale does not move the completed hearing injury to the awareness date. The contrary retiree classifications in Ingalls Shipbuilding and Alabama Dry Dock do not survive this statutory construction.

##### The distinct filing protection

**Holding:** Section 908(c)(13)(D) postpones the running of the §912 notice period and §913 claim period for hearing loss until the employee receives an audiogram and its accompanying report indicating hearing loss; it does not postpone the substantive time of injury to awareness. The 1984 amendments accordingly provide a hearing-specific filing protection, independently defeating the contention that congressional concern about late hearing claims necessarily places them in the latent-disease compensation system.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined by the other eight Justices; nine votes for the rule and application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Potomac Electric remains the source of the schedule’s fixed statutory operation. Redick v. Bethlehem Steel Corp., 16 BRBS 155 (1984), cannot justify denying a scheduled claim solely because hearing loss was discovered after voluntary retirement; reference to it in debate does not change either statutory trigger.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The schedule does not require reduced actual earnings and uses two-thirds of the applicable average weekly wage for the prescribed weeks (200 for total bilateral loss, proportionately reduced for partial loss). The special delayed-disease retiree wage formula cannot be combined with the schedule. Retirement audiograms may identify compensable loss but are not mandatory testing duties; no new aging-apportionment dispute is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm Brown’s award. Its 82.4 percent impairment finding is unchallenged; use of the 1985 national wage remains because the recalculation objection was waived, not because that wage is the governing scheduled-loss rule.

### Public employee compensatory time

#### Moreau v. Klevenhagen, 508 U.S. 22 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1.
**Decided:** May 3, 1993.

##### The earlier-hire component: the transition rule supplies the agreement

**Holding:** For employees described in § 207(o)(2)(A)(ii) who were hired before April 15, 1986, the regular compensatory-time practice in effect on that date constitutes the agreement or understanding the statute otherwise requires before the work is performed. This transition rule operates independently of, and does not require, a further individual or collective agreement for that cohort.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s unanimous opinion of the Court, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine support this proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

No prior Supreme Court holding addressed this transition provision; the Court applies its text without displacing any earlier authority.

##### The later-hire component: individual agreements where no representative could bind

**Holding:** Where employees have selected a representative organization that state law does not authorize to enter a binding § 207(o)(2)(A)(i) agreement, the employer may rely on individual § 207(o)(2)(A)(ii) agreements or understandings with the affected employees hired on or after April 15, 1986, rather than lose the compensatory-time option entirely for want of a legally capable collective representative.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s unanimous opinion of the Court, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine support this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general collective-bargaining entitlement, overtime exemption or presumption of individual consent is created. The Court does not decide a practice adopted after April 15, 1986 or employees with a representative legally capable of making the collective agreement.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm on both cohorts: the regular April 15, 1986 practice supplies the earlier-hire agreement; actual individual agreements remain available for later hires lacking a legally capable representative.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

No Supreme Court precedent is overruled, limited, or extended beyond its own terms; the Court decides only the two routes actually presented on this record.

### ERISA equitable relief against nonfiduciaries

#### Mertens v. Hewitt Associates, 508 U.S. 248 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1671.
**Decided:** June 1, 1993.

##### The reviewed compensatory award is not appropriate equitable relief

**Holding:** Section 502(a)(3)’s appropriate equitable relief encompasses traditionally equitable categories of relief, not compensatory damages merely because a court of equity could award them in trust litigation. The requested monetary compensation from this admitted nonfiduciary for the plan’s losses is therefore unavailable under that provision; the Court does not bar every monetary equitable remedy or decide the antecedent, expressly disclaimed cause-of-action question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court; Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The expressly disclaimed antecedent cause of action is reserved, not rejected as jurisdictional. Other equitable monetary remedies are not categorically barred; restitution, unappealed claims and the separate malpractice disposition remain untouched.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of the reviewed compensatory claim under §502(a)(3); no liability finding or award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Curtis v. Loether, 415 U.S. 189: traditional legal compensation distinction applied.
- Chauffeurs, Teamsters & Helpers v. Terry, 494 U.S. 558: remedy classification applied; trust forum alone does not decide the statutory category.
- Firestone Tire & Rubber Co. v. Bruch, 489 U.S. 101: trust-law guidance preserved subject to express statutory limits.
- Clews v. Jamieson, 182 U.S. 461: equity’s monetary powers acknowledged but distinguished from the statutory category.
- Massachusetts Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Russell, 473 U.S. 134: enforcement-structure caution applied; its reserved question is now resolved only within this holding.
- United States v. Burke, May 26, 1992: its fractured judgment supplies no controlling general remedial-range test.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February 26, 1992: appropriate-remedy presumption distinguished because Congress expressly limited this provision to equitable relief.

### Federal supervision of employee benefit trusts

#### Local 144 Nursing Home Pension Fund v. Demisay, 508 U.S. 581 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-610.
**Decided:** June 14, 1993.

##### Section 302 restrains prohibited transactions

**Holding:** Section 302(e) authorizes restraint of violations of the payment, demand, receipt and related prohibitions in §302(a) and (b); §302(c)(5) identifies qualifying trusts rather than a general supervisory remedy for later administration. Retaining these reserves supplies no transfer remedy without an actual prohibited transaction; payment to a nonqualifying trust remains subject to the statute.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, Section 302 restrains prohibited transactions portion; Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The three-Justice pooled-fund rationale is noncontrolling. Separate ERISA asset-transfer and fiduciary claims remain unresolved; lawful receipt does not immunize later fiduciary misconduct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the §302 transfer direction and remand; the court of appeals may consider the unreviewed ERISA claims previously rejected below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Arroyo, 359 U.S. 419 — lawful receipt distinguished from later diversion; contrary supervisory language is dictum.
- Amax, 453 U.S. 322 — fiduciary independence retained; supervisory language does not establish jurisdiction.
- Robinson, 455 U.S. 562 — reasonableness-review rejection retained; reserved supervisory scope resolved.
- Mertens, June 1, 1993 — unchanged; its remedy limit does not resolve these unreviewed ERISA claims.

### Employment discrimination and proof of pretext

#### St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks, 509 U.S. 502 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-602.
**Decided:** June 25, 1993.

##### Pretext alone does not command judgment, and circumstantial evidence need not be supplemented by a special category of proof

**Holding:** After an employer produces admissible evidence of nondiscriminatory reasons, a proven prima-facie case and rejection of the offered explanations do not automatically command judgment for the employee. Those circumstances may support a finding of intentional discrimination without a mandatory additional category of direct or independent proof; the Eighth Circuit’s automatic-liability mandate is vacated, while no majority here adopts a replacement persuasion allocation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Burdine’s persuasion allocation is not replaced. No universal Title VII/§1983 equivalence, clear-error decision, resolution of retaliation, remaining defenses, relief or applicability of the 1991 Act is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the appellate automatic-liability mandate under Title VII and the related §1983 claim against Long and remand. No district-court vacatur, new burden allocation or judgment for either side is ordered. Existing district findings remain subject to proper review; retaliation remains for consideration and the unappealed §1981 dismissal is unchanged.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green, 411 U.S. 792: indirect framework retained; no automatic liability holding adopted.
- Texas Department of Community Affairs v. Burdine, 450 U.S. 248: production stage retained; no five-Justice replacement of its persuasion rule.
- Postal Service Board of Governors v. Aikens, 460 U.S. 711: actual discrimination and circumstantial evidence remain central.
- Hazen, April 20, 1993: prohibited motive remains distinct from general unfairness; no added evidence category follows.

### Hostile work environments

#### Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., 510 U.S. 17

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1168.

**Decided:** 1993-11-09.

##### Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury

**Holding:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considered in all the circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and that the employee actually perceives as abusive; serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis, and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than a merely offensive utterance, and unreasonable interference with work, but no listed consideration is indispensable; psychological effects may inform subjective abuse, while offensive conduct that does not meet the objective and subjective requirements remains insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57 (1986): applies and clarifies its severe-or-pervasive alteration-of-conditions rule; its extreme examples do not impose a psychological-injury element. Its employer-responsibility questions are not enlarged here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere offense remains insufficient. Meritor's employer-responsibility issues and Hicks's persuasion rules are not altered; proposed EEOC regulations and a sex-specific reasonable-person formulation are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for application of the objective and subjective standard without an injury prerequisite; liability, constructive discharge and damages are not decided.

#### Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 95-30510; 83 F.3d 118 identifies the judgment below.
**Decided:** March 19, 1997.

##### Whether Title VII excludes harassment by a person of the employee's own sex

**Holding:** Title VII's protection against discriminatory employment conditions includes same-sex harassment. The employee must still plead and ultimately prove discrimination because of sex and every other element of the asserted claim; common sex supplies neither immunity nor liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Meritor* supplies coverage of discriminatory abusive conditions, extended here to same-sex actors without eliminating any statutory element.

##### Whether sexual desire or an actual opposite-sex comparator is necessary to establish discrimination

**Holding:** Competent direct or circumstantial evidence can establish discrimination because of sex without proof of desire, a particular sexual orientation, or an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual content, contact, or the gravity of conduct does not alone conclusively establish that causal element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** Sex-directed hostility, selective treatment, discriminatory statements, threats, and surrounding circumstances may support the inference. These examples create neither exclusive proof channels nor a compulsory checklist.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds no participant's sexual orientation or motive and decides no independent sexual-orientation coverage question. Personal hostility unrelated to sex is outside this cause of action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Hicks* preserves ultimate persuasion and permissible circumstantial inference; it does not impose a compulsory desire showing or comparator. *Brown v. Polk County* and *Nabozny* retain their distinct statutory and constitutional scopes.

##### Whether a single-sex workplace changes the hostile-environment standard

**Holding:** Sex-based conduct must be severe or pervasive enough to produce an objectively hostile or abusive workplace in context, and the employee must actually perceive it as abusive. Workforce composition informs context but creates no exemption; psychiatric injury and measurable productivity loss are unnecessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** Workplace custom cannot automatically recast forced physical humiliation or rape threats as harmless camaraderie. The allegations remain unproved, and their gravity does not establish unwelcome treatment, sex causation, or attribution.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No single circumstance, diagnosis, treatment, medical proof, or universal reasonable-woman formulation is required.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Harris* supplies the contextual severe-or-pervasive inquiry and its separate objective and subjective requirements, without an economic or psychiatric injury prerequisite.

##### Which attribution and remedial issues survive removal of the categorical bar

**Holding:** Employer attribution, if reached, requires application of *Andrade*: actual delegated supervisory authority must materially aid creation or maintenance of the unlawful environment. A supervisory title alone does not establish that connection, and lack of higher-management notice alone does not defeat this agency route; negligence and a preserved quid-pro-quo theory remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The agency route does not invariably require a completed employment action or economic loss. A quid-pro-quo claim separately requires a supported employment benefit or detriment conditioned on sexual participation. No strict employer liability, new affirmative defense, individual-coworker rule, or finding about Lyons's statutory status is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical ground as to affected Sundowner and Lyons claims and remand under ordinary procedure. Pippen and Johnson's separate not-an-employer dismissal remains outside review, without endorsement of a new immunity rule. Liability, factual sufficiency, constructive discharge, discovery, trial entitlement, and available relief for the 1991 conduct remain undecided; properly presented independent defenses survive.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Andrade* supplies the authority-aided agency rule and preserves negligence as another route. The lower-court *Garcia* same-sex exclusion cannot govern after this decision.

### Insured employee-benefit assets

#### John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank, 510 U.S. 86

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1074.

**Decided:** 1993-12-13.

For the other questions in John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank, 1993-12-13, No. 92-1074, see Administrative Law — An agency interpretation cannot erase the qualified exclusion.

##### The guaranteed-benefit exclusion operates by component

**Holding:** Under ERISA §401(b)(2), a guaranteed-benefit policy itself is a plan asset, while the insurer's underlying assets are not deemed plan assets solely because it issued the policy to the extent the policy genuinely guarantees the amount of benefits. The disputed nonguaranteed free-funds component is not excluded: its plan-asset character subjects Hancock to fiduciary obligations to the extent it exercises authority or control over management or disposition of those assets under §3(21)(A), without deciding breach, causation, loss, or remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, and Souter. Six Justices adopt the component-specific construction and its application to the disputed free funds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- SEC v. United Benefit Life Insurance Co.: used for the distinction between a genuine guarantee and retained investment risk; the ERISA text supplies the governing exclusion.
- Mertens v. Hewitt Associates: kept separate; its treatment of available equitable categories does not decide whether these funds are plan assets or whether Hancock committed a breach.

##### Insurance regulation does not displace ERISA's express direction

**Holding:** McCarran-Ferguson does not bar application of ERISA's express insurance-related fiduciary and guaranteed-benefit provisions to this general-account contract. ERISA's insurance-saving clause preserves compatible state insurance regulation, but does not make a conflicting state obligation controlling over ERISA itself; a concrete conflict must be assessed as such rather than treated as a categorical exemption for all insurer assets.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, and Souter. This six-Justice ground rejects the asserted state-insurance obstacle to affirmance.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. v. Massachusetts: applied to the coexistence of ERISA and state insurance regulation; no blanket withdrawal of federal obligations follows.
- FMC Corp. v. Holliday: applied to the coexistence of ERISA and state insurance regulation; no blanket withdrawal of federal obligations follows.
- Silkwood v. Kerr-McGee Corp.: applied for the distinction between compatible state regulation and a conflicting obstacle to federal law.
- United States Department of Treasury v. Fabe: distinguished because its federal-priority and policyholder-protection issue is different; its holding remains intact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The contract contains guarantees and a residual floor; those do not guarantee the disputed free-funds benefits. A guaranteed reasonable return and fixed conversion protection are indicators, not an exhaustive mandatory formula. No breach, loss or remedy follows from status alone, and no categorical anti-deference rule for inconsistency or litigation positions is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Second Circuit's relevant judgment is affirmed, 6–3. Asset status and applicable fiduciary functions remain distinct from breach, causation, loss and relief, which must be addressed in further proceedings; no monetary or equitable award is made.

### Labor remedies and hearing misconduct

#### ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB, 510 U.S. 317

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1550.

**Decided:** 1994-01-24.

##### Hearing falsehood and the statutory remedy

**Holding:** Under NLRA section 10(c), an employee's deliberate false testimony at the remedial hearing does not automatically forfeit reinstatement or backpay for an established unfair labor practice; the Board may withhold relief for supported misconduct but is not required to apply a categorical forfeiture rule. The separate statutory prohibition applies when the employee was actually suspended or discharged for cause; later hearing testimony does not itself establish that earlier discharge ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices. Scalia and O'Connor agree with the lawfulness of the result in a separate judgment concurrence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Phelps Dodge Corp. v. NLRB: applied to the Board's primary responsibility for connecting relief with the Act's policies.
- Virginia Electric & Power Co. v. NLRB: applied to the judicially enforceable boundary against remedies pursuing unrelated ends.
- Precision Instrument Manufacturing Co. v. Automotive Maintenance Machinery Co.: distinguished; equitable misconduct principles do not impose an extra categorical restriction on section 10(c).
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB: preserved; its binding statutory construction limits Board authority but does not decide remedies for this established unlawful discharge.

##### Review of this remedial choice

**Holding:** A Board remedy remains reviewable for arbitrariness, an inadequate explanation or departure from section 10(c)'s statutory purpose; the Board must recognize material misconduct and give reasons sufficient to connect its selected remedy to the Act, but need not adopt a new sanctions formula or expressly discuss a separate forfeiture sanction in every case. On this record, the Board's recognition of the false account, its accepted finding of antiunion discharge and its stated restorative labor rationale support enforcement without a further explanatory remand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices. Scalia and O'Connor find the action lawful on their narrower statutory-discretion ground, without endorsing the Court's public-remedy assessment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Phelps Dodge Corp. v. NLRB: applied to review the remedy's statutory connection without replacing agency discretion with judicial policy.
- Virginia Electric & Power Co. v. NLRB: applied to preserve meaningful review of the actual remedial explanation and statutory purpose.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB: distinguished on the issue decided; enforcing a statutory limit and reviewing an expressly delegated remedy are different inquiries.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not impose automatic employee entitlement, excuse falsehood, presume a separate Board perjury-sanction inquiry, or require express forfeiture discussion in every case. Independently authorized sanctions remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit enforcement judgment is affirmed. Reinstatement and backpay remain enforceable; no explanatory remand, new sanction or recalculation is ordered. The established unfair-labor-practice finding is outside this limited review.

### Skilled employees and supervisory status

#### NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America, 511 U.S. 571

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1964.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### The Board may distinguish skilled direction from genuine supervisory authority

**Holding:** The Board permissibly interprets NLRA §2(11), in the context of the Act's protection of skilled and professional employees, to distinguish direction incidental to skilled patient care from genuine supervisory authority exercised for the employer. On this record, its functional construction and supported determination that the four licensed practical nurses are employees must be sustained; no occupational exemption or independent judicial substitute for the agency's rationale is adopted.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter. All five directly adopt the permissible-construction ground; Stone-Zsela's proposed mandatory construction appears only in his concurrence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fall River Dyeing & Finishing Corp. v. NLRB, 482 U.S. 27 (1987): applies review for rational interpretation consistent with the Act and substantial evidentiary support.
- NLRB v. Yeshiva University, 444 U.S. 672 (1980): uses its distinction between ordinary professional duties and management, while preserving professional/institutional overlap and the separate managerial question.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, January 27, 1992: preserves binding statutory boundaries; it does not settle this classification tension.
- SEC v. Chenery Corp., 318 U.S. 80 (1943), and National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: permit reliance on the agency's discernible actual rationale, not an invented substitute.

- ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB, January 24, 1994: preserves Board judgment within an enacted delegation, without authorizing disregard of statutory text.

Professional judgment can be independent judgment exercised through genuine supervisory authority; it does not itself establish that authority. ### Independent state rights and railway-labor preemption

#### Hawaiian Airlines, Inc. v. Norris, 512 U.S. 246

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-2058, including the related Finazzo judgment under Rule 12.2.

**Decided:** 1994-06-20.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** The related Finazzo judgment is included under Rule 12.2; the airline and officers actions retain their distinct surviving counts.

##### Independent state rights remain enforceable without interpreting the labor agreement

**Holding:** The Railway Labor Act's exclusive machinery for minor disputes covers claims founded on a collective-bargaining agreement or requiring interpretation of its disputed terms; it does not displace independent state-law claims resolvable through factual questions about conduct or motive without that interpretation. Norris's public-policy and whistleblower theories meet that independence test on the presented preemption question, so both reviewed Hawaii judgments are affirmed, leaving the surviving claims, their ordinary elements and defenses, and any relief for state proceedings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Stevens's opinion for a unanimous Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the complete rule, application, precedent treatment, and bounded affirmance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A statutory or nonnegotiable label does not by itself establish independence. Courts examine the claim's actual elements; a state claim using disputed contractual just cause as its substantive standard differs from the factual retaliatory-motive inquiry here. Overlapping facts, parallel remedies, unfinished grievance steps, or an asserted contract defense do not alone defeat an independent right. The separate federal dismissal of the CBA claim remains intact. State elements, defenses, ordinary limits against duplicate recovery, and genuine arbitration duties remain applicable. Affirmance establishes no unsafe-aircraft finding, wrongful discharge, damages amount, reinstatement, or new FAA cause of action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lingle v. Norge Division of Magic Chef, Inc., 486 U.S. 399 (1988): extends its independent-right and necessary-interpretation distinction from the Labor Management Relations Act context to RLA preemption, without equating the two statutes in every respect.
- Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway Co. v. Buell, 480 U.S. 557 (1987): applies its refusal to substitute RLA grievance machinery for an independent substantive protection; no new federal injury or whistleblower action follows.
- Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Railway Labor Executives' Assn., 491 U.S. 299 (1989): preserves its arguable-contractual-justification test within the major/minor distinction for contractual disputes; it does not answer the antecedent source-of-right question.
- Andrews v. Louisville & Nashville Railroad Co., 406 U.S. 320 (1972): distinguishes its agreement-founded entitlement while preserving compulsory adjustment for that category.
- Elgin, Joliet & Eastern Railway Co. v. Burley, 325 U.S. 711 (1945): disavows its broader suggestion that an omitted contractual case necessarily includes an independent state-law claim within minor disputes; that dictum does not control. Its contractual and representation holdings are not overruled.
- Livadas v. Bradshaw, June 13, 1994: applies the distinction between factual consultation and necessary interpretation, preserving genuinely contract-dependent claims; its separate bargaining-policy and §1983 holdings are not extended here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both reviewed Hawaii judgments are affirmed: the airline action's public-policy preemption disposition and the officers action's public-policy and whistleblower dispositions. Their existing remands remain effective for adjudication of surviving state claims, defenses and relief, subject to ordinary limits on duplicate recovery. The separate federal dismissal of the airline contract claim remains intact. No extra airline-count judgment, liability, damages, reinstatement or renewed grievance is ordered.

### Independent wage rights and collective bargaining

#### Livadas v. Bradshaw, 512 U.S. 107

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1920.

**Decided:** 1994-06-13.

For the other questions in Livadas v. Bradshaw, 1994-06-13, No. 92-1920, see Civil Rights — Section 1983 supplies a channel for this identified federal labor right.

##### An independent wage-penalty claim is not preempted by §301

**Holding:** Section 301 does not displace a nonnegotiable state wage-payment claim whose resolution requires no interpretation of disputed collective-bargaining terms. Consulting an undisputed contractual wage rate to calculate the possible statutory penalty supplies arithmetic, not the contract interpretation needed for §301 preemption; genuinely contract-dependent claims remain different.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this antecedent holding.

**Material application:** Section 201 requires earned unpaid wages immediately on discharge; §203 addresses willful delay, continuing wages at the same rate until payment or commencement of an action for no more than thirty days. Section 219 bars private agreements setting aside those protections but permits more frequent, greater or earlier payment. Section 229 permits wage-collection suits despite arbitration, with a separate exception for disputes over interpretation or application of a bargaining agreement containing arbitration. The broader administrative exclusion is the Commissioner's policy, not §229's command. Contract §18.8 permitted this direct claim through arbitration or an authorized tribunal or agency without contract interpretation. No penalty is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lingle v. Norge Division of Magic Chef, Inc., 486 U.S. 399: applies independence from contract interpretation; use of undisputed wage information does not change the claim's source.
- Allis-Chalmers Corp. v. Lueck, 471 U.S. 202: preserves preemption of genuinely contract-dependent claims; it does not preempt this arithmetic use.
- Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, December 4, 1991: preserves the distinct §301 route for a beneficiary enforcing a qualifying interunion contract, not every union-related claim.

##### California may not penalize completion of protected collective bargaining

**Holding:** The NLRA forbids California's policy withholding otherwise available enforcement of an independent wage right solely because an employee is covered by a collective-bargaining agreement containing arbitration. The employee's protected freedom to bargain includes carrying bargaining through to such an agreement; this rule neither compels employer assent nor generally requires the State to create an enforcement service.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's unanimous Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg adopt this statutory labor-right holding.

**Material application:** The holding does not guarantee identical access through every enforcement route. Proceedings under §§98 and 98.1 differ from representation under §98.3(a), which depends on the Commissioner's assessment of financial inability to employ counsel and a valid, enforceable claim. The employee's own §218 suit and §218.5 fee provision remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Nash v. Florida Industrial Commission, 389 U.S. 235: applies the prohibition on state benefit conditions burdening federally protected labor activity.
- Golden State Transit Corp. v. Los Angeles, 493 U.S. 103: applies protection of the federally secured bargaining process against governmental interference.
- Fort Halifax Packing Co. v. Coyne, 482 U.S. 1: preserves independent minimum labor standards and does not authorize this penalty for protected bargaining.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for source-authorized relief against the official-capacity policy. Monetary-relief and Eleventh Amendment constraints remain distinct. No employer wage penalty, personal damages or automatic reinstatement of a money award is ordered; willfulness, the effect of backpay and other penalty defenses remain unresolved.

### Railroad emotional-injury liability

#### Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Gottshall, 512 U.S. 532

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1956, including Carlisle under Rule 12.2.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Carlisle is included under Rule 12.2. The two reviewed judgments receive different mandatory remand instructions.

##### Negligent emotional injury is actionable within the employee's zone of danger

**Holding:** FELA permits recovery for negligently inflicted emotional injury when the employee sustains physical impact from the employer's negligence or is placed in immediate risk of physical harm by that negligence; on the threatened-impact branch, the emotional injury must result from fear of physical harm to the employee himself. The employee must still establish negligence, legally cognizable injury and FELA causation; seriousness, genuineness and foreseeability alone do not substitute for the physical-danger boundary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Five of nine participating Justices directly adopt the same operative rule; no combination of separate rationales supplies its force.

**Material application:** Whether every otherwise qualifying emotional injury needs physical manifestations, intentional infliction, and the full category of fear-of-future-disease claims remain undecided. FELA negligence and causation are still required independently of the physical-danger boundary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway Co. v. Buell, 480 U.S. 557 (1987): answers its reserved negligent-emotional-injury question; its protection of independently cognizable FELA claims from RLA displacement remains intact.
- Urie v. Thompson, 337 U.S. 163 (1949): preserves occupational-disease coverage; distinguishes that coverage from the boundary for negligent emotional harm.
- Monessen Southwestern Railway Co. v. Morgan, 486 U.S. 330 (1988): applies the importance of common-law background without making every tort rule immutable.
- Ellis v. Union Pacific Railroad Co., 329 U.S. 649 (1947): applies the requirement of negligence and rejection of insurer liability.
- Rogers v. Missouri Pacific Railroad Co., 352 U.S. 500 (1957): preserves FELA's causal-contribution rule without extending it to eliminate duty, breach or the compensable-injury boundary.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Metro-North Commuter Railroad Co. v. Buckley, No. 96-320, 1997-06-23, resolves the reserved symptomless-asbestos contact and future-disease-risk category; see Labor and Employment — Railroad emotional-injury liability.

##### Gottshall's exposure and causal application remain for the court below

**Holding:** The Third Circuit's Gottshall judgment is reversed because it did not apply the zone-of-danger rule, and the case is remanded for that court to apply the rule in the first instance. The Court determines neither whether Gottshall was placed in immediate risk of physical harm nor whether his emotional injury resulted from fear of harm to himself; witnessing and attempting to prevent his coworker's death alone do not establish those predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. The same five Justices adopt this application and remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Buell, 480 U.S. 557: its reservation requires resolution and application of the governing injury boundary, rather than treating emotional injury as already fully defined.
- Urie, 337 U.S. 163: occupational-injury coverage remains effective but supplies no finding about Gottshall's immediate danger or the cause of his distress.

##### Carlisle's work-stress claim is excluded and requires judgment for Conrail

**Holding:** A FELA negligent-emotional-injury claim arising from the prolonged work stress presented in Carlisle, without physical impact or immediate risk of physical harm within the zone-of-danger rule, is not compensable. The Third Circuit's judgment sustaining the $386,500 verdict is reversed, and the case is remanded with instructions to enter judgment for Conrail, not to retry the same excluded claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Five Justices adopt both the legal exclusion and mandatory defendant-judgment remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ellis, 329 U.S. 649: applies the distinction between fault-based liability and workplace insurance without discounting the evidence actually offered.
- Monessen, 486 U.S. 330: applies common-law guidance to the compensable-injury boundary, not to reweigh a jury's supported factual findings.
- Rogers, 352 U.S. 500: preserves relaxed causal contribution but does not let causation evidence replace the required injury category.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Third Circuit judgments are reversed, 5–4. Gottshall is remanded for first-instance application of the zone-of-danger rule, without automatic reinstatement of summary judgment, a trial entitlement or a damages award. Carlisle is remanded with instructions to enter judgment for Conrail, with no new trial of the excluded work-stress claim.

#### Metro-North Commuter Railroad Co. v. Buckley

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-320.

**Decided:** 1997-06-23.

##### I. Symptomless asbestos exposure does not satisfy the physical-danger boundary for this distress claim

**Holding:** Physical contact with asbestos that has caused no manifested disease and presents only a risk of later disease does not qualify as the physical impact required for this FELA negligent-emotional-injury claim. An increased risk of disease in the future likewise does not alone establish Gottshall's alternative requirement of immediate risk of physical harm; the present emotional-distress claim cannot proceed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Thomas's Court opinion is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: six direct votes. Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Ginsburg support reversal of the distress determination on evidence-sufficiency grounds, without joining this rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The threatened-impact branch's fear-for-self requirement is not extended to the actual-impact branch. No universal requirement of psychiatric treatment, mental-health expert testimony or physical manifestations is adopted; later distinct manifested disease or otherwise qualifying injury remains open. The Court does not adopt the three judgment-only evidence-sufficiency positions as an alternative holding.

**Material judgment:** Distress reversal is unanimous in judgment; only the six Justices joining Thomas adopt the physical-danger rationale.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gottshall's reserved toxic-contact category is resolved at this symptomless, future-risk scope while its distinct negligence, injury and causation requirements remain. Buell's reserved emotional-injury inquiry is developed; Urie's occupational-disease protection is distinguished from prospective risk. Monessen supplies common-law guidance, Ellis preserves fault-based liability, and Rogers's causal-contribution rule supplies no missing injury predicate.

##### II. FELA does not authorize the independent, unqualified monitoring damages recognized below

**Holding:** Negligent asbestos exposure, increased future disease risk and the monitoring recommendations in this asymptomatic record do not establish an entitlement under FELA to the independent, unqualified lump-sum medical-monitoring recovery recognized by the Second Circuit. Such damages cannot be attached to the unsuccessful emotional-distress claim; the Court leaves more carefully limited programs, reimbursement of actual necessary costs, later illness and materially different records undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Thomas's Court opinion is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: six direct votes. Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Ginsburg dissent from reversal of the monitoring determination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not find equivalent benefits already available, reject every monitoring remedy, or decide a fund, program, testing schedule, necessary-cost amount or offset.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The appellate monitoring determination is reversed, 6–3, and the case is remanded consistently with both holdings. The mandate orders no automatic trial or adjudication of an unpleaded or unsupported alternative arrangement, and makes no damages award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gottshall keeps cognizable injury distinct from negligence and causation. Monessen permits common-law guidance without freezing all tort rules; Urie and Rogers do not establish an independent monitoring-damages entitlement.

### After-acquired evidence and discrimination remedies

#### McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1543.
**Decided:** January 23, 1995.

##### Later-discovered misconduct does not erase an earlier ADEA violation

**Holding:** Misconduct unknown to the employer when it discharged an employee cannot defeat liability for an age-discriminatory discharge or categorically bar every remedy. The employee must still establish an ADEA violation under the governing liability rules; an employer’s assumption of discrimination for summary judgment is not a final liability finding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hazen Paper’s actual-motive requirement remains controlling; Mt. Healthy and Price Waterhouse are distinguished because they concern reasons involved in the challenged decision. St. Mary’s Honor Center retains its existing proof framework and does not make rejection of an employer’s explanation an automatic liability judgment.

##### Proven independently sufficient misconduct limits appropriate relief

**Holding:** To limit relief through after-acquired evidence, the employer must prove misconduct so serious that it would in fact have discharged the employee for that reason alone had it known, rather than merely showing that discharge was permissible. Once that showing is made, reinstatement and front pay are ordinarily inappropriate, and backpay’s starting measure is the period from unlawful discharge to actual discovery, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances affecting either party’s legitimate interests.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hazen Paper’s knowledge-or-recklessness standard continues to govern any claim of willfulness; this remedial ruling does not lower it. Mt. Healthy and Price Waterhouse supply no complete forfeiture rule for a reason discovered after the challenged discharge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Genuine protected opposition or participation cannot supply a lawful discharge ground; this case announces no general ban on obtaining evidence of discrimination. No final discrimination, willfulness, compensation, liquidated-damages, fee, or reinstatement finding is made. Section 626(c)(1)’s termination of the individual right to bring an action when the EEOC commences an action to enforce that employee’s right remains distinct; no such agency action is established here. The 1991 Title VII amendments supply no new rule for this ADEA discharge.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is reversed and remanded for liability adjudication without treating unknown misconduct as a discharge reason and for relief under the employer's actual-would-discharge showing. No final discrimination or damages determination is entered.

### ERISA plan amendment and corporate authority

#### Curtiss-Wright Corp. v. Schoonejongen

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1935.
**Decided:** March 6, 1995.

##### The company-amendment clause satisfies §402(b)(3)

**Holding:** In this employer-sponsored welfare plan, a written reservation of amendment to the Company provides an amendment procedure and identifies an authorized person under ERISA §402(b)(3), because §3(9) includes corporations and applicable corporate law identifies actors empowered to bind the Company. The statute does not invariably require a list of individual officers; actual additional procedures adopted by the plan remain binding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, 503 U.S. 318 — ordinary legal meaning informs ERISA where the enacted text supplies no contrary rule; no employee-classification test is imported into amendment authority.
- John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank (December 13, 1993) — status and function remain separate from breach and relief; procedural sufficiency establishes no final amendment or immunity.

##### A sufficient procedure still requires authorized corporate action

**Holding:** A legally sufficient reservation of amendment power does not establish adoption of the challenged amendment. The lower courts must determine who had express or implied authority, whether an authorized actor approved the 1983 cutoff, and, if necessary, whether later ratification was legally effective, including its timing and consequences, before entering a conforming benefits judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mertens v. Hewitt Associates, 508 U.S. 248 — the reviewed §502(a)(3) compensation limit remains; it does not bar benefits due under §502(a)(1)(B).
- John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Harris Trust & Savings Bank — the distinction between legal status and ultimate liability is preserved; no new breach or remedy follows merely from identifying amendment power.

**Limits and questions not reached:** ERISA §402(b)(3), 29 U.S.C. §1102(b)(3), requires every plan to provide a procedure for amending it and for identifying the persons with amendment authority. Section 3(9), §1002(9), includes a corporation among persons, as well as an individual, partnership, joint venture, mutual company, joint-stock company, trust, estate, unincorporated organization, association, and employee organization. The plan Constitution reserves to the Company the right at any time and from time to time to modify or amend any or all provisions, in whole or in part, and separately reserves termination. Delaware §141(a) places corporate business and affairs under the board’s management or direction except as otherwise provided by that chapter or the certificate of incorporation. No charter, bylaw, delegation, or approval is established as a fact here. Section 402(a)(1)’s written-instrument and named-fiduciary requirements remain distinct from sponsor amendment authority. Sections 1022 and 1024 retain actual summary, disclosure, and instrument-access duties; an SPD is not proof of authorized amendment. Section 502(a)(1)(B) benefits enforcement is distinct from §502(a)(3)’s appropriate-equitable-relief category.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Third Circuit is reversed and the case remanded to identify authorized corporate actors, determine actual approval and legally effective ratification, including timing and consequences, and enter a conforming benefits judgment. Actual termination and amounts are not decided; rejected lifetime vesting is not automatically reopened.

#### Lockheed Corp. v. Spink, 517 U.S. 882

**Docket or dockets:** 95-809
**Decided:** June 10, 1996

##### Does sponsor adoption of pension amendments create fiduciary prohibited-transaction liability?

**Holding:** Designing and adopting these pension-plan amendments is sponsor conduct, not ERISA fiduciary conduct. Subject to §408's statutory exceptions, §406(a)(1) requires a fiduciary to cause a transaction the fiduciary knows or should know is prohibited; employer party-in-interest status alone is insufficient, and actual implementation must be assessed separately under §3(21)(A)'s functional terms, including asset-disposition control without an additional discretion requirement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), Part I, unanimous; Part IV-A's independent-claims directions likewise unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Amendment power establishes neither compliance with amendment procedures nor lawful administration. Surplus remains plan property. The sponsor-capacity rule forecloses §404 liability resting solely on design or adoption, while actually preserved fiduciary-implementation and §403(c)(1) anti-inurement grounds retain their own predicates and remedies.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed adverse judgment is reversed and remanded, 9–0 overall. Count V, refusal of offensive collateral estoppel and withdrawn Count IV remain outside the relief. Mertens and Peacock retain the distinct predicate and remedy requirements for any preserved claim.

##### Does employer benefit from a required release alone make enhanced retirement payments prohibited transfers?

**Holding:** Genuine enhanced retirement benefits under an otherwise lawful plan are not prohibited transactions under §406(a)(1)(D) merely because employment-related releases benefit the employer. The rule assumes fiduciary implementation for this inquiry and does not immunize sham transfers, kickbacks, an independently unlawful plan or an unenforceable release.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; seven. Souter and Breyer would reserve the question, not decide the payments unlawful.

**Material application:** The accepted complaint allegations describe participant benefits rather than diversion solely because releases have employer value. No sham or kickback is alleged in the decided issue.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No §408 exemption or general net-benefit defense is created. Independent participation, funding, vesting, loyalty and anti-inurement duties survive. Section 406(b)'s distinct prohibitions and particular waiver enforceability under the Older Workers Benefit Protection Act or other law are not decided; no loss, deception or hidden diversion is found.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Keystone's transfer of property to discharge an enforceable funding obligation is distinguished; valuable consideration alone does not equate that transaction with these participant benefits.

##### Does OBRA require credit for Spink's earlier lawful exclusion from pension participation?

**Holding:** OBRA §§9201–9202 do not require credit for service during Spink's earlier lawful nonparticipation. Section 9204(a)(1) applies the new accrual protections to plan years beginning on or after January 1, 1988 and employees with an hour of service in a covered year; a smaller total benefit caused by earlier uncredited service is not itself a reduced accrual rate during a covered year.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III, all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in the judgment only. Dismissal of Counts I–II is restored, 9–0.

**Material application:** The relevant plan year began December 25, 1988. The later calculation date does not turn prior lawful nonparticipation into credited service or establish a separate violation earlier in that calendar year.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 9204(b) separately applies §9203's participation amendments to covered plan years and service performed on or after January 1, 1988; that added service-date condition does not enter subsection (a). Under §9204(a)(2), benefits pursuant to and persons covered by employer–employee-representative collective agreements ratified before March 1, 1986 use the first plan year beginning on or after the earlier of January 1, 1990 or the later of January 1, 1988 and termination of the last qualifying agreement, disregarding extensions after February 28, 1986; no allegation invokes that transition. Section 9204(c) permits formal amendment to await the first plan year beginning on or after January 1, 1989 only if interim operations comply and amendment covers that interval retroactively. Operation under that provision alone does not make benefits or contributions indeterminable or the plan inconsistent with its terms, and does not delay substantive compliance. No general prohibition on using past facts or new default temporal rule is adopted.

### ERISA preemption and hospital-payment measures

#### New York State Conference of Blue Cross & Blue Shield Plans v. Travelers Insurance Co. / Pataki v. Travelers Insurance Co. / Hospital Association of New York State v. Travelers Insurance Co.

For the other questions in this decision, see Federal Courts — Component-specific vacatur and independent relief (The reserved direct-plan claim remains live after limited vacatur).

##### The reviewed 13% hospital differential is not preempted

**Holding:** ERISA §514(a) does not preempt the reviewed 13% hospital differential merely because its price effect may influence a covered plan’s choice of insurer or coverage arrangement. In these commercial-insurance applications the measure neither uses ERISA status or covered-plan benefits as its legal benchmark nor commands plan benefits, eligibility, uniform administration or an alternative enforcement remedy; the Court reserves economic effects sufficiently acute to operate as a substantive coverage command or effectively restrict insurer choice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Shaw v. Delta Air Lines, Inc., 463 U.S. 85: applies the connection-or-reference inquiry and its remote-effects boundary without making every cost consequence preemptive.

- FMC Corp. v. Holliday, 498 U.S. 52: distinguishes its alteration of plan subrogation and benefit calculation; its separate saving/deemer architecture remains undisturbed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2807-c(1) excludes Medicare payments and permits alternative reimbursement methodologies authorized under subdivision four, paragraphs (e)–(i). Paragraph (a) covers state governmental agencies, article 43 insurance corporations’ expense-incurred policies, the described payments by those corporations for foreign-corporation subscribers under subdivision 12(d), and article 44 HMO subscribers. The diagnosis-related-group case payment includes reimbursable operating costs, capital expenses, bad-debt and charity-care allowances, the primary-care allowance, the allowance for financially distressed hospitals, the rate-year trend factor and the subdivision four (a)–(d) case-payment adjustments. Paragraph (a-1) gives local-government payments for local correctional inmates the state-agency rates.

Paragraph (b) covers expense-incurred payments under workers’ compensation, volunteer firefighters’, volunteer ambulance workers’ and no-fault laws; self-insured funds paying hospitals directly, except enrollees covered by a rate-method agreement under paragraph (a) of subdivision two; licensed commercial accident-and-health insurers whose policies cover inpatient services on that basis; and out-of-plan benefits authorized under §4406, except those offered by a not-for-profit corporation or an organization qualifying under Internal Revenue Code §501(c). The payment equals the article 43 case-payment basis, adjusted for uncovered services, increased by 13%, and adjusted under subdivision 11(i) where applicable. A hospital may not refuse a qualifying direct-payment arrangement without due cause. Paragraphs (a) and (b) payors may not choose the lower of charges and the case payment or apportion the case payment by excluding a preexisting or acquired condition that must be treated along with the reason for admission. These are statutory payment rules; they are not findings that an ERISA plan changed its benefits.

Paragraph (c) uses hospital charges for patients without an authorized case-payment arrangement under paragraphs (a) or (b), or coverage under subdivision two (a). The subdivision four (d) short-stay definition applies, with that paragraph’s developed payment increased by 13%. For a charge-paying patient the ceiling is 120% of the paragraph (b) diagnosis-related-group case payment, without the subdivision 11(i) adjustment. A hospital must apply its charge schedule uniformly. This is not another application of the full 24% differential.

##### The additional commercial-insurer 11% charge is not preempted

**Holding:** The reviewed additional 11% commercial-insurer charge likewise is not preempted under §514(a): its revenue destination and additional price differential do not establish a required plan benefit, insurer choice or administrative arrangement on this record. This application adopts neither a numerical safe harbor for combined charges nor immunity for financial pressure that actually operates as a substantive coverage command or effectively restricts insurer choice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Shaw: applies its relation boundary to the additional charge’s actual operation, rather than treating legislative purpose or percentage alone as conclusive.
- Mackey: applies the distinction between ordinary added economic costs and the relation ERISA preempts; it supplies no quantitative exemption.
- Board of Trade: preserves its covered-plan-content benchmark and distinguishes this additional payor charge, whose amount does not use plan benefits as the legal measure.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reproduced subdivision 11(i) applies the additional 11%, totaling 24% with the existing 13%, to the identified commercial-insurer expense-incurred discharges from April 1, 1992 through March 31, 1993. The appendix contains an ellipsis after that provision’s total-24% statement. Subdivision 14-e creates the statewide supplementary pool: hospitals submit the additional component, and the commissioner deposits it and investment income into the general fund. The complete extension text and resulting current rate liability are not decided here.

##### The conditional HMO assessment is not preempted

**Holding:** The reviewed HMO assessment of up to 9%, subject to its actual Medicaid participation, enrollment, exemption, reduction and payment conditions, is not preempted by §514(a) on this record. It affects HMO prices and participation choices without a demonstrated command to an ERISA plan’s benefit structure, eligibility, insurer choice or uniform administration; its conditions receive no categorical immunity from preemption if a different application establishes such a connection or effective compulsion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Shaw: applies connection-or-reference analysis separately to the HMO measure and preserves the possibility that more acute indirect effects could suffice.
- Mackey: applies the distinction between actual administrative commands and economic consequences without dismissing duties merely because imposed through generally applicable law.
- FMC: distinguishes compulsory changes to plan reimbursement rights from the HMO participation and payment conditions shown here, retaining the insured/self-funded distinction where saving and deemer issues must be reached.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No direct self-funded merits ruling, new insurance-saving test, numerical safe harbor, uniform-cost guarantee, FEHBA ruling, stop-loss-letter ruling, Tax Injunction Act holding, or amended-law rate command is adopted. These are record-specific applications of the relation boundary, not categorical immunity for health regulation, price regulation, generally applicable statutes or indirect burdens.

Subdivision 2-a(a) addresses article 44 and article 43 HMO inpatient discharges on or after July 1, 1992. Medicaid-eligible subscribers and participants in the 1988 chapter 703 regional pilot projects are excluded. The base is the subdivision one case payment, subdivision four per-diem rate, or negotiated and approved subdivision two (b) rate, whichever applies. The additional factor is 9%, subject to elimination or reduction. Notice was due June 1 for the 1992 rate period and December 1 for each following annual January rate period. An appeal by May 1 can obtain a July 1 adjustment.

Elimination under subdivision 2-a(b)(i) requires status as a Social Services Law §364-j managed-care provider in each social-services district in the service area and enrollment of at least 90% of the sum of the target numbers of Medicaid recipients not exempt from managed care. The reproduced determination dates are May 1 for the 1992 rate period, November 1 preceding the 1993 rate year, and May 1 when an appeal is taken for the six-month period beginning July 1. Reaching 25%, 50% or 75% of total target enrollment gives the corresponding 25%, 50% or 75% reduction, rather than elimination.

The following nonexclusive good-faith routes apply only to the reproduced 1992 and 1993 rate years. First, the HMO submitted an implementation plan and would have begun enrollment but for the district’s failure or refusal to contract. Second, it submitted letters of intent to all districts, contracted with half by July 1, 1992 and two-thirds by January 1, 1993, and could have contracted with the rest but for Department-agreed implementation delay. Third, the same letters and agreed-delay conditions apply where contracts cover districts containing half and then two-thirds of the service area’s nonexempt Medicaid population on those dates. The same pool and payment procedure apply. ERISA §514(a), subject to subsection (b), supersedes state laws insofar as they relate to a §4(a) plan not exempt under §4(b); its effective date is January 1, 1975, and §514(b)(1) excludes earlier causes of action, acts and omissions. Subparagraph (B) prevents a covered nonexempt plan or its trust from being deemed an insurer, bank, trust company or investment company, or engaged in insurance or banking, for the stated state-regulatory purposes; its express exception is a plan established primarily to provide death benefits. #### De Buono v. NYSA-ILA Medical and Clinical Services Fund

**Decided:** June 2, 1997.

##### A self-insured plan's provider ownership does not preempt this independently triggered tax

**Holding:** ERISA §514(a) does not preempt the reviewed 0.6 percent assessment on these centers' assessable provider receipts merely because the self-insured Fund owns the centers and pays the provider cost directly. Their licensed activity and receipts trigger the measure independently of ERISA status, and its actual operation neither uses covered-plan benefits as a mandatory legal measure nor dictates benefits, eligibility, fiduciary standards, benefit administration or enforcement, or choices through equivalent coercion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Parts II–IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins. Scalia and Thomas withhold present merits disposition, not a contrary merits holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General applicability and the 0.6% rate are not safe harbors. Section 1144(b)(5)(B)(i) preserves ordinary tax-preemption analysis despite the Hawaii exception; insurance saving and deeming create no general self-insured tax exemption.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Travelers is extended to the reserved direct self-funded provider application without discarding its acute-effects reservation. Dillingham supplies the independently operating criterion and actual-compulsion inquiry. Board of Trade's mandatory benefit benchmark is absent. Mackey and Fort Halifax distinguish external obligations from protected administration. Inter-Modal preserves enacted commands without exempting ordinary provider costs.

##### Nonpreemption covers the actual tax base and exclusions only

**Holding:** Nonpreemption governs this challenged provider-tax application with its actual deductions and exclusions, not every receipt or every future tax application. The uncontested exclusion of Fund-to-center transfers as grants and the absence of a challenge to miscellaneous receipts unrelated to participant health services remain boundaries of the decision; no complete present-liability formula is adjudicated.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Parts II–IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2801's definition turns on physician- or dentist-supervised health services, not plan status. It excludes facilities principally treating mental disability and subject to the mental-hygiene department's visitation, examination, inspection and investigation, except distinct parts providing hospital service. The article also excludes institutions principally furnishing services by or under bona fide adherents of a recognized religion whose teachings rely on prayer alone for healing, where services accord with those teachings. Section 2807-d(1)(a) deducts personal-needs allowances and refunds and excludes paragraph (b)'s hospitals; the reproduced appendix omits paragraph (b) and intervening subdivisions, so the Court supplies no substitute text.

They exclude subscriber-service receipts of centers operated by an HMO under Public Health Law article 44 or Insurance Law article 43, and Medicaid-client family-planning receipts where the center has distinct Medicaid family-planning rates. Preserving these boundaries rejects the premise that every dollar reaching the Fund is taxed, without converting the exclusions into an ERISA immunity or a new entitlement to repayment.

##### The assumed payment date fixes the computational starting point

**Holding:** Under 29 U.S.C. §1399(c)(1)(A)(i), the withdrawal-liability installment calculation uses the first day of the plan year after the withdrawal year as the assumed first-payment and computational-interest starting point; it does not capitalize interest for the preceding withdrawal year. ### Nonscheduled compensation and earning-capacity modification

#### Metropolitan Stevedore Co. v. Rambo

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-820.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

##### Economic change can support timely nonscheduled modification

**Holding:** Section 22 permits modification of a nonscheduled disability award upon a demonstrated change in injury-related earning capacity even when physical impairment is unchanged. Modification remains limited to the enacted grounds of changed conditions or mistake in a determination of fact, within one year after the last compensation payment, whether or not a compensation order issued, or within one year after rejection of a claim; true §8(i) settlements remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt the construction and bounded reversal/remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The stipulated award was not a section 8(i) settlement discharging liability with the required best-interest finding. The employer has an actual assessment and reimbursement stake despite Fund payments; the Director's distinct appellate entitlement is not enlarged. Scheduled compensation, medical benefits, untimely reopening and unrestricted revision of final awards are outside the holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- O'Keeffe v. Aerojet-General Shipyards, Inc., 404 U.S. 254 (1971): analogous breadth of fact-correction authority does not erase statutory grounds or deadline.
- Banks v. Chicago Grain Trimmers Assn., Inc., 390 U.S. 459 (1968): applies resistance to restrictions not enacted in the modification text; the mistake ground remains distinct.
- Potomac Electric Power Co. v. Director, OWCP, 449 U.S. 268 (1980): preserves scheduled/nonscheduled statutory branches.
- Bath Iron Works Corp. v. Director, OWCP (January 12, 1993): retains mandatory scheduled-loss operation; no economic-loss condition is added to that branch.
- Director, OWCP v. Newport News Shipbuilding (March 21, 1995): distinguishes general administrative disagreement from an employer's genuine financial interest; no Director appellate entitlement is enlarged.

##### Capacity, proof and the remand

**Holding:** The employer seeking modification must prove the qualifying earning-capacity change; increased wages are probative but conclusive only insofar as they fairly and reasonably represent capacity under §8(h). Otherwise capacity must be determined with due regard to injury, physical impairment, usual employment, other capacity-affecting circumstances and natural future effects of disability; findings retain statutory substantial-evidence review, and the Ninth Circuit must address preserved arguments it did not reach rather than presume termination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt this application/remedy qualification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual earnings are not an automatic recovery presumption or burden shift. The existing findings considered training, several years of crane work, physical demands, work availability, inflation, occupation-specific wage increases and risk of losing the job. Future recurrence and nominal-award doctrine remain undecided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Director, OWCP v. Greenwich Collieries (June 20, 1994): applies persuasion on the modification proponent without a wage-created burden shift.
- Bath Iron Works and Potomac Electric: retain scheduled compensation as a different branch; this capacity inquiry governs the presented nonscheduled award.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's physical-change-only rule and remand for unresolved preserved arguments and lawful review of the existing administrative findings. No immediate termination, unconditional new hearing, new dollar award or finding of complete recovery is ordered.

#### Metropolitan Stevedore Co. v. Rambo

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-272.
**Decided:** June 19, 1997.

##### A significant injury-related future earning-capacity risk permits nominal compensation

**Holding:** For an unscheduled work injury causing no current earning-capacity loss, the Act permits nominal compensation only if the claimant proves a significant, nonconjectural potential that the injury, interacting with reasonably foreseeable economic conditions, will reduce future earning capacity below preinjury earnings. Permanent physical impairment or a merely conceivable possibility alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Parts I-A, II and III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The limitation periods do not themselves create benefits. The holding concerns unscheduled injury; it does not change §22’s grounds or deadlines or reopen true §8(i) settlements.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rambo (1995) reserved nominal compensation and is otherwise preserved. Sections 2(10), 8(c)(21) and 8(h) connect economic capacity with natural future effects; §§13(a) and 22 explain why waiting can foreclose a claim but supply no independent entitlement. Potomac Electric and Bath Iron Works retain the separate scheduled-loss branch.

##### Proof of all qualifying statutory predicates establishes entitlement to nominal compensation

**Holding:** Once the claimant establishes that qualifying disability and all ordinary statutory predicates, appropriate nominal compensation is due. No finding that Rambo has established those predicates, and no current award or amount, follows from this legal rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part I-B, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela’s availability join supplies no additional entitlement vote.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No finding that Rambo meets the predicates and no award or amount is directed.

##### The claimant must prove a significant risk, not that future loss itself is more likely than not

**Holding:** The employer seeking modification must prove the qualifying change in earning capacity, and increased wages alone create no automatic recovery presumption or persuasion shift. Once current earnings fairly represent current capacity, the claimant requesting a nominal order must prove by a preponderance that the work injury creates the significant future potential; this does not require proof that future loss itself is more probable than not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Parts I-A, II and III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No numerical risk threshold is selected. If current earnings do not fairly represent capacity, §8(h) requires consideration of injury, impairment, usual employment, other capacity factors and natural future effects. Section 20(a) applies absent substantial contrary evidence; it is neither a permanent-improvement nor future-loss presumption and does not remove ultimate persuasion after rebuttal. No presumption is found triggered or dispositive here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Greenwich Collieries assigns persuasion to the proponent, preserving operative statutory presumptions and genuine defense burdens. Rambo (1995) requires the employer to prove capacity change without an automatic wage-created shift.

##### The administrative factfinder must determine the significant future risk before an award

**Holding:** A court of appeals may not direct nominal compensation by making the significant-future-risk finding itself or by presuming it from permanent impairment. The administrative law judge must apply the governing inquiry, subject to ordinary substantial-evidence review; a remand does not unconditionally require new evidence where lawful findings on the existing record suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Parts I-A, II and III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Material application:** Training, sustained market employment, wages and physical demands supported the end of present loss, but the prior discussion did not decide the distinct significant-potential inquiry.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand through the Benefits Review Board to the administrative law judge, 6–3, for that inquiry and lawful review. No amount, unconditional evidentiary hearing or nominal award is commanded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sections 21 and 22 preserve factfinding and substantial-evidence review. Rambo (1995) requires lawful treatment of preserved arguments without presumed termination; O’Keeffe and Banks do not authorize reopening beyond §22’s grounds and time limits.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Metropolitan Stevedore Co. v. Rambo, June 19, 1997, No. 96-272: resolves the reserved nominal-compensation question through significant injury-related future-risk proof and administrative application, with a five-Justice entitlement rule on all proved predicates.

### WARN limitations periods

#### North Star Steel Co. v. Thomas / Crown Cork & Seal Co., Inc. v. United Steelworkers of America, AFL-CIO-CLC

##### Source and application of the WARN limitations period

**Holding:** For these WARN claims, courts must borrow an appropriate analogous state limitations period because NLRA § 10(b) is neither a clearly closer analogy nor shown significantly more suitable through federal policy and litigation practicalities. **Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices directly support the rule and application. Scalia joins only the judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Federal borrowing requires both a clearly closer federal analogy and significantly greater suitability from federal policy and litigation practicalities. Section 1658 supplies no period for this 1988 cause. Arbitration, exhaustion, statutory deferral and associated tolling are outside review; the earlier union action does not dispose of the separate employees' claims.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- DelCostello v. Teamsters, 462 U.S. 151: preserves the ordinary state-borrowing rule and confines its federal exception to a genuinely closer analogy and significantly greater federal suitability; its hybrid labor-settlement application is distinguished.
- Reed v. United Transportation Union, 488 U.S. 319: applies its restrictive account of the exception; a labor setting alone remains insufficient.
- Fort Halifax Packing Co. v. Coyne, 482 U.S. 1: applies its separation of independent substantive employment protections from bargaining mechanisms; no arbitration question is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm both Third Circuit judgments: in No. 94-834, preserve reversal of the employees' limitations dismissal; in No. 94-835, preserve rejection of the six-month defense. Both claims are timely under the state candidates presented; no exact period, liability, damages, accrual, tolling or WARN merits exception is decided.

### Railroad hours of service

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1592.
**Decided:** January 8, 1996.

**Holding:** Waiting genuinely incident to transportation from a completed duty assignment to final release is within §21103(b)(4)'s return-transportation interval and counts as neither duty nor required rest. ### Religious accommodation

#### Beadle v. City of Tampa, judgment below 42 F.3d 633

**Docket or dockets:** Eleventh Circuit No. 93-3271; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** February 5, 1996.

##### Neutral scheduling does not alone discharge the accommodation duty

**Holding:** Under 42 U.S.C. §2000e(j), an employer must reasonably accommodate religious observance or practice unless it demonstrates inability to do so without undue hardship on its business. Under Hardison's governing rule, the employer need not incur more than a de minimis cost, including concrete lost efficiency, deficient training, or consequent safety burdens; once an effective reasonable accommodation resolves the conflict, Ansonia does not require the employee's preferred alternative or proof against every alternative.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Title VII supplies no categorical police or public-safety exception. Professional evidence and reasonable operational inferences may establish concrete noncash burdens without waiting for an accident or reducing every burden to dollars. No independent negotiation duty is imposed when an effective accommodation or sufficient hardship defense exists; no compelling-interest or later hardship standard is imported.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hardison's cost and legitimate third-party limits remain. Ansonia's sufficient-accommodation rule remains but is inapplicable to a conflict not actually resolved.

##### A concession about one trainer does not resolve materially different proposals

**Holding:** When the work-religion conflict remains unresolved, a hardship justification must address the supported, materially distinct accommodations and actual operations at issue. A concession that using one trainer would impair training does not establish that every proposed arrangement preserving multiple trainers would cause undue hardship; the accommodation judgment must be reconsidered where that mismatch underlies the decision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** The existing rotation and isolated Saturday leave left the conflict unresolved. Distinct proposals included multiple qualified trainers within Dawson's squad, trainers on compatible nontraining squads and a four-to-eight-week extension. Their feasibility is not found. The seniority provision uses classification seniority when skill, performance and ability are equal and the supervisor finds the task unaffected, but removes seniority from consideration when training affects assignments. No required swap or guaranteed downtown vacancy is established; later practice does not prove earlier availability.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the accommodation judgment and remand for proper application, with further proceedings only as lawfully necessary. The City may prevail on a supported hardship showing. No particular schedule, placement, reinstatement, award or new trial is directed; the distinct release determination remains undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hardison and Ansonia are applied to the actual unresolved conflict and distinct proposals, without creating a right to a fixed Sabbath assignment.

#### Opuku-Boateng v. California

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit No. 94-16542; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied. 95 F.3d 1461 identifies the judgment below.
**Decided:** April 7, 1997.

##### Whether a public-employment applicant receives Title VII accommodation protection

**Holding:** Title VII protects an applicant's known sincere religious practice through the same reasonable-accommodation duty applicable to employees. Public employment creates no exemption or heightened obligation, and prior temporary service is unnecessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Material application:** The known Sabbath conflict and conceded prima facie case bring the refusal to complete the permanent appointment within §§2000e-2(a)(1) and 2000e(j). Operational concerns address accommodation and hardship rather than coverage.

##### Whether good-faith discussion or unsupported operational concerns establish accommodation or hardship

**Holding:** The employer must provide an effective reasonable accommodation or prove hardship exceeding the governing *Hardison* threshold. Without an effective accommodation, the defense must address supported materially distinct proposals in the actual operation; concrete noncash burdens and reasonable supported predictions can suffice, without compulsory experimentation or actual disruption. Good-faith discussion alone supplies neither statutory answer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The employer need not offer the preferred arrangement, exhaust every imaginable alternative, or negotiate as an independent prerequisite to a sufficient defense. Legitimate noncontractual coworker burdens, coverage, efficiency, and security count; religious hostility and abstract future requests alone do not.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Hardison* retains the more-than-de-minimis threshold, protected seniority, and legitimate transferred burdens. *Ansonia* ends the inquiry once an effective reasonable accommodation is offered. *Beadle* and *Brown v. Polk County* allow supported prospective and noncash proof while requiring a connection to the actual proposals.

##### Whether California's completed trial proof establishes the asserted hardship

**Holding:** The completed record does not establish hardship from supported combinations of undesirable shifts and voluntary trades merely through neutral Saturday rotation and the asserted morale and operational concerns. California's failed defense leaves liability resolved without declaring every proposal feasible or all shifts equally burdensome.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Material application:** California identified no protected seniority restriction and failed to establish the burden transferred by the offered night, Sunday, holiday, and other shifts. The poll's results were excluded for their truth; other testimony addressed a mistaken three-day absence or failed to identify the question or respondents. Security testimony did not connect this limited Sabbath absence to concrete loss despite variation on other days. Existing scheduling flexibility required a specific comparison; rejection of one permanent substitute did not resolve supported combinations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Absence of a protected seniority rule does not erase coworker interests, and absence of an experiment does not independently decide liability. No new hardship case is allowed merely because California lost on the proof offered at trial.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Brown* supplies the completed-record distinction; *Beadle*'s remand for unresolved proposals does not require reopening this failed defense.

##### Whether Rule 52 requires deference to the legally and evidentially defective hardship conclusion

**Holding:** Clear-error review protects supported findings and reasonable operational inferences, but cannot sustain a hardship conclusion through excluded-purpose evidence, a material misunderstanding of the requested absence, or treating neutral rotation as accommodation. The remaining proof here is insufficient under the proper predictive standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit, preserving resolved liability and remand for appropriate relief. No new trial opportunity to rebuild the original hardship defense is ordered.

##### What employment and monetary relief follows from the resolved hiring violation

**Holding:** Relief must rest on ordinary Title VII predicates and facts relevant to remedy; liability alone does not establish a present appointment, vacancy, chosen shift, fixed award, or continuing injunction. The employer retains the choice among effective accommodations; a permanent schedule may be prescribed only if lawful and necessary after that opportunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Determine lawful employment and equitable relief, backpay and mitigation, the present basis for prospective relief, and fees and costs. Current staffing and hiring conditions may affect relief without reopening liability for the 1982 refusal. The 1991 Act's new compensatory and punitive damages and dependent jury entitlement do not apply retrospectively to that conduct.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No Free Exercise, individual constitutional-liability, or mixed-motive issue is decided; no general preferred-shift right follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Brown* preserves the conduct-time remedial boundary; its constitutional and mixed-motive holdings do not enlarge this grant.

### Railroad automatic-coupler duties

#### Norfolk & Western Railway Co. v. Hiles, 516 U.S. 400

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-6.

**Decided:** February 27, 1996.

##### Whether ordinary drawbar preparation itself violates the automatic-coupling duty

**Holding:** Former 45 U.S.C. §2 imposes an absolute duty of required coupling performance when equipment is properly set. Ordinary nondefective lateral displacement requiring drawbar alignment does not alone violate that duty; the Act neither prohibits every preparatory task between cars nor separately requires automatic realignment equipment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The enacted duty requires automatic coupling by impact and uncoupling without workers going between the car ends. General safety purpose and custom do not resolve whether an actual condition is ordinary preparation or failed performance. The 1994 recodification supplies no new requirement for this 1990 injury.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Affolder and Carter's proper-setting qualification applies to drawbar alignment as well as knuckle opening; O'Donnell's absolute duty for an engaged coupler remains.

##### Performance-based proof and the limited ordinary-setting explanation

**Holding:** A violation may be proved by failure of required performance without negligence or identification of a mechanical defect. The railroad may show ordinary nondefective misalignment before proper setting, but due care, inspection, later success, contributory negligence and assumption of risk do not erase a proven statutory violation contributing to injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Violation and causal contribution under FELA remain separate. No attempted impact is invariably required where failure otherwise can be shown; an attempted coupling would not itself establish liability. Preparation cannot excuse malfunction by relabeling it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Affolder and Carter preserve performance proof, proper setting and distinct causation. Gottshall's negligent-emotional-injury and zone-of-danger rules do not govern this statutory-performance claim.

##### Whether injury during realignment establishes liability as a matter of law

**Holding:** Hiles's realignment injury alone does not justify directed statutory liability. The displacement's reason, extent and legal character require consideration under the preparation/performance distinction, including only properly preserved performance and causal issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General lateral movement does not prove this displacement ordinary. Excluded inspection and AAR-compliance submissions are proffers, not findings; movement difficulty and disputed curve inferences are not resolved. No new ordinary-negligence claim is supplied.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand; the directed-liability ruling and dependent $492,500 damages foundation fall. Neither final railroad judgment, guaranteed new trial, recovery nor revival of a forfeited issue follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Wagner and Parker preserve examination of actual displacement and performance evidence rather than a conclusive rule for every realignment injury.

### ERISA fiduciary duties

#### Varity Corp. v. Howe, 516 U.S. 489

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1471.
**Decided:** March 19, 1996.

##### The integrated benefit-security presentation was fiduciary administration

**Holding:** ERISA §3(21)(A) attaches fiduciary responsibility to the particular exercise of discretionary plan administration, including plan information communicated in that capacity. Varity exercised that function when it used administrative authority, benefit comparisons and linked assurances of benefit security to induce the class's transfer; employer status, corporate forecasting or participant perception alone would not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Material application:** Current-benefit descriptions and deliberately linked security assurances formed one administrative presentation. Warnings that future conditions depended on MCC's success and that changes might occur did not displace the unchallenged trial findings that the combined presentation was knowingly misleading.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Every employment statement or corporate-solvency prediction is not thereby fiduciary. Corporate restructuring and plan-amendment powers remain distinct from administration.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** John Hancock's particular-function inquiry is applied while keeping status, breach and remedy separate. Curtiss-Wright preserves sponsor amendment authority but distinguishes retained power from authorized exercise; it creates no immunity for this administrative act.

##### Deliberate disloyal deception violates §404

**Holding:** An administrator acting as a fiduciary violates §404(a)(1)'s loyalty duty by knowingly and significantly misleading participants about benefit security to induce surrender of existing plan interests for the employer's benefit. Varity's found deception and the class's reliance meet that rule despite reserved lawful amendment or termination powers.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by the same six Justices as Part I.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This intentional-deception holding decides no general duty to volunteer information, answer every inquiry, disclose every forecast or avoid negligent misstatement. It supplies no substantive rule for the ten retirees' different assignment claim and no permanent-benefits promise.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Firestone's trust-law guidance explains loyalty within ERISA's statutory function, without replacing that threshold. Curtiss-Wright's amendment rule supplies no permission to deceive participants into surrendering present membership.

##### Section 502(a)(3) permits appropriate individual equitable redress for fiduciary breach

**Holding:** Sections 409 and 502(a)(2) do not impose a categorical plan-only bar on §502(a)(3)'s express authorization of appropriate individual equitable relief for a §404 violation. Relief must be traditionally equitable; where another ERISA provision adequately remedies the injury, further equity normally is unnecessary and inappropriate, without an absolute rule governing every alternative pleading.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela's stricter adequate-remedy condition supports the class's judgment but is not the Court's rule.

**Material application:** The class sought restoration after fiduciary deception procured surrender of membership. Rejection of its lifetime-benefits contract theory established neither an adequate §502(a)(1)(B) remedy for that injury nor an automatic bar to restoration.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not classify each monetary item as equitable, including replacement-insurance premiums or all unpaid benefits. It neither revives the rejected $7.6 million and $712,332 compensatory awards nor permits general compensation through trust law. Section 510 and estoppel remain unadopted alternatives.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The clarified restoration decree is affirmed. Reinstatement confers only the rights of the M-F plan as it now exists. Past entitlement may be adjusted for intervening events, with relevant additional evidence and a conforming decree. Future lawful changes remain possible subject to applicable law and preserved retaliation or discrimination objections. No new payment amount or immutable lifetime entitlement is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Russell's plan-centered §409 construction is preserved while its distinct §502(a)(3) question is answered. Mertens's equitable-category limit applies; its reserved antecedent action issue is not treated as an earlier action bar. Peacock's statutory-or-plan-violation requirement is met by the proved §404 breach; its later new-debtor jurisdictional holding does not govern this original action. Firestone supplies guidance within enacted limits.

##### The ten retirees' independent assignment-breach ground was not adequately challenged

**Holding:** The categorical plan-only objection does not defeat the ten retirees' individual equitable recovery, and their distinct lower-court assignment-breach determination remains undisturbed because the petition did not sufficiently present a challenge to it. Affirmance decides no new substantive assignment rule, precise monetary classification or categorical unavailability of §502(a)(1)(B) to those retirees.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela provides a sixth judgment vote on the distinct assignment component without a ground.

**Material application:** The ten had retired before the purported assignment and did not consent. Their unauthorized-administration ground did not depend on the class's presentation or voluntary departure; arguments concerning misrepresentation and lifetime contractual benefits did not identify error in that separate ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the clarified decree for this group on the same existing-plan and adjustment terms stated above, without entering a new amount or reviving terminated-employee, punitive, lifetime-contract, settled-disability or pension claims.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Curtiss-Wright does not transform an amendment reservation into proof of an authorized assignment or permanent vesting; Mertens's monetary-category limits remain without a new classification decision.

### Age discrimination

#### O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp., 517 U.S. 308

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-354.
**Decided:** April 1, 1996.

##### Replacement within the ADEA's protected age group does not defeat the initial inference

**Holding:** An ADEA plaintiff need not show replacement by someone under forty to establish the initial inference of age discrimination. A replacement's membership in the protected group does not itself defeat that inference.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes McDonnell Douglas applies for this question and takes the case as a non-reduction-in-force matter, without settling every ADEA framework or alternative prima facie formulation. Claimant coverage at forty and prohibited age motivation remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hazen Paper supplies the actual-age-motivation inquiry; McDonnell Douglas, Furnco and Teamsters preserve the initial showing's evidentiary function without an under-forty replacement requirement.

##### Relative-age evidence must support an inference of age discrimination

**Holding:** Under the assumed framework, prima facie evidence and each required element must have a logical connection to an inference of age discrimination. A substantially younger replacement may support that inference even if at least forty; an insignificant difference, standing alone, does not supply it.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II has all eight Associates' complete joins. Stone-Zsela joins the inference requirement, positive relative-age proposition, unchanged burdens and reservations, but not the categorical insignificant-gap-alone proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No numerical minimum is prescribed. A substantially younger replacement is not indispensable when other evidence supplies the inference or no replacement sensibly can be identified. Every younger replacement does not suffice; materially different circumstances may defeat the comparison. The sixteen-year gap here establishes neither all initial elements, satisfactory performance nor actual motive. Additional age-related evidence and specific small-gap applications remain open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Burdine and Aikens preserve employer production and the plaintiff's ultimate persuasion burden. Hicks permits circumstantial proof without a mandatory additional category of evidence but does not compel liability merely from a prima facie case and disbelief of produced lawful reasons.

##### Reconsideration under the corrected rule leaves genuinely sufficient alternative grounds available

**Holding:** The reviewed ADEA merits affirmance must be reconsidered under the corrected replacement rule and ordinary proof and summary-judgment standards. The employer may defend the existing judgment on an appropriate alternative ground without a cross-petition, but intertwined lower performance and comparison analysis does not make this legal error immaterial on the disputed timely record.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand the ADEA merits affirmance. A genuinely sufficient remaining ground may still support summary judgment. The separate discovery and Rule 59/60 rulings remain intact; Dennis's postjudgment affidavit remains outside the timely record. No trial, hearing, liability, willfulness, damages, backpay or reinstatement is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Anderson and Celotex require a genuine material dispute and favorable reasonable inferences for the nonmovant, without accepting speculation or making credibility findings. McKennon distinguishes correction of a categorical ADEA bar from proof of liability and lawful relief; its after-acquired-evidence remedy is not extended.

### Agricultural labor and employee coverage

#### Holly Farms Corp. v. NLRB, 517 U.S. 392

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-210.
**Decided:** April 23, 1996.

##### Preservation of the primary-agriculture objection

**Holding:** Section 160(e) bars judicial consideration of an objection not urged before the Board, its member, agent, or agency unless extraordinary circumstances excuse the omission. Raising secondary agriculture did not preserve the distinct primary-harvesting theory, and no extraordinary circumstance excuses the omission here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), Part I, joined by all seven other Associates; Stone-Zsela joins only the disposition declining consideration.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether poultry catching can be primary agriculture remains undecided. The preserved secondary-agriculture claim remains reviewable.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Woelke & Romero* supplies the agency-preservation requirement and its extraordinary-circumstances qualification.

##### Secondary agriculture and these live-haul occupations

**Holding:** Secondary agriculture requires both a practice performed by a farmer or on a farm and a relationship incident to or in conjunction with the relevant farming operations. A nonfarmer's employees may qualify through the on-farm alternative, but location alone is insufficient; preparation for market and delivery to storage, market, or carriers remain subject to both requirements. Courts independently identify those statutory boundaries before reviewing an unresolved agency construction for permissibility.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court opinion, unanimous.

**Material application:** Ownership of birds, employer identity, and integration within one business are not conclusive. Work may serve farming and processing simultaneously; exclusive farming purpose is not an additional requirement.

##### Classification of these live-haul drivers

**Holding:** The Board lawfully classifies these substantially off-farm live-haul drivers as covered employees. Transport serving Holly's separate processing operation does not become agriculture because the birds were raised on farms, Holly continuously owned them, or the trip begins there.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the Court opinion, unanimous.

##### Classification of these catchers and forklift operators

**Holding:** The statute does not unambiguously exempt these on-farm catchers and forklift operators. The Board permissibly treats their catching, cooping, and loading as a separately organized removal operation serving processing rather than as incident to or in conjunction with the growers' farming, on its actual, substantially supported integration rationale.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of the Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; six Justices. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas reject this application while joining the shared statutory predicates and driver holding.

**Material application:** Independent growers raise the birds to marketable size. Holly separately organizes and supervises removal according to processing shifts, with handling continuing until plant receivers take over. Those facts support the Board's actual rationale; the Labor Department's poultry-preparation examples retain the statutory conditions and do not compel exemption. Substantial evidentiary support remains distinct from judicial statutory interpretation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision mandates no classification for every poultry arrangement, farmer's own employees, other contractors, or mixed-duty drivers, and does not hold that the opposite driver classification on identical facts would also be permissible. It neither makes employer identity conclusive nor declares all post-growing work nonagricultural or a processing plant incapable of being a market. No new proof burden, general remand rule, or *Gissel* standard is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm enforcement within the classification question; the Board's existing order remains effective. No unit reconsideration, bargaining-order remand, election, hearing, or monetary award is ordered. Tyson's separate successorship issue and unrelated unfair-labor-practice issues are not reopened; genuinely remaining matters retain their ordinary lawful channels.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Farmers Reservoir* distinguishes farming from a separately organized enterprise serving it while preserving both statutory gateways. *Bayside* rejects attribution of independent growers' farming through processor ownership and supports the driver holding; its on-farm reservation is resolved only for this catcher/loader arrangement. *Chevron* requires independent boundary review before permissible interpretation. *Town & Country* preserves each express exclusion within bounded employee classification; *Health Care* supports functional construction on the agency's actual rationale without importing its supervisory test. *MCI* forbids eliminating a statutory condition; the on-farm alternative remains effective. No precedent is overruled.

### Majority status and collective-bargaining agreement enforcement

#### Auciello Iron Works, Inc. v. NLRB, 517 U.S. 781

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-668.
**Decided:** June 3, 1996.

##### Does the NLRA entitle an employer to a postacceptance assessment period for previously known majority-status doubt?

**Holding:** At *Chevron* step one, the NLRA does not unambiguously entitle an employer to leave its offer open and then repudiate the accepted agreement on previously known, unasserted good-faith majority-status doubt. This timing question remains within the range of permissible Board choices, subject to the Act's employee-choice and majority-representation safeguards.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part I, unanimous; all nine Justices join Parts I–IV and every qualification and remedy below.

**Material application:** Sections 7 and 9 protect employee choice and majority representation, and §§8(a)(1), (2) and (5) prohibit interference, unlawful support and refusal to bargain, but none provides the claimed grace period. Section 8(d) requires good-faith bargaining and execution of an agreement reached upon request, without compelling assent to an unaccepted proposal or a concession. This established incumbent retained a rebuttable presumption after expiration of its prior agreement. Contract formation under the Board's criteria, which need not coincide with general contract law, establishes neither actual majority support nor an independent answer to the statutory objection; doubt and actual minority remain distinct.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither silence nor a presumption proves employee support. The 1977 certification creates no renewed conclusive certification year in 1988. Actual-minority merits, including the Board's reserved question of a postacceptance actual-minority claim based on previously known events, are not decided; nor is the lawfulness of continued bargaining when both parties know actual minority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. NRDC*, 467 U.S. 837, requires independent statutory construction before permissibility review. *MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T* and *Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB* preserve binding statutory boundaries before deference; *Lechmere*'s access holding supplies no majority-status rule. *NLRB v. Town & Country Electric, Inc.* and *Holly Farms Corp. v. NLRB* apply statutory-first review while keeping classification, actual facts and relief distinct. *Brooks v. NLRB*, 348 U.S. 96, and *Fall River Dyeing & Finishing Corp. v. NLRB*, 482 U.S. 27, preserve continuity and orderly employee-choice routes without fresh numerical proof at every bargaining act. *International Ladies' Garment Workers' Union v. NLRB*, 366 U.S. 731, is distinguished as initial recognition of an actually minority union; its employee-choice and §8(a)(2) limits remain intact.

##### Was the separate actual-minority objection preserved for review?

**Holding:** NLRA §10(e) bars an objection not urged before the Board unless extraordinary circumstances excuse the omission. Preserving a good-faith-doubt objection did not preserve actual-minority merits, and no qualifying excuse permits review of that separate objection here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This threshold ruling does not dispose of the preserved doubt claim, establish actual minority, or resolve the Board's reserved postacceptance actual-minority question.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Holly Farms Corp. v. NLRB* supplies §10(e)'s preservation requirement with its extraordinary-circumstances exception; the exception is retained but unsatisfied here.

##### May the Board ordinarily use acceptance as the cutoff for a good-faith-doubt defense based on earlier-known information?

**Holding:** At *Chevron* step two, the Board may ordinarily require an employer to act on majority-status doubt based on previously known information before an established incumbent accepts its still-outstanding offer. The explained stability policy reasonably protects accepted agreements, subject to the distinct protected-period, representation-route and virtually simultaneous first-information qualifications below; the Act does not compel that policy for every future Board.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** The Board may prevent unilateral uncertainty and postacceptance litigation generated by an employer's previously uncommunicated doubt. Its supplemental decision itself supplies that policy explanation, so no substitute judicial rationale or explanatory remand is necessary. These prospective policy risks are not findings of strategic concealment, bad faith, actual employee support or insufficient doubt. The asserted need to assess reports does not require leaving an accepted agreement defeasible.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Before acceptance an employer may use legally available election procedures, assert legally sufficient doubt in response to a refusal-to-bargain charge, or continue good-faith bargaining while investigating. Investigation does not suspend bargaining; refusal may draw a charge and requires its own lawful defense, and no election is automatically available. The discussion of offer withdrawal assumes without deciding that withdrawal here would not support an unfair-labor-practice charge implicating the blocking-charge rule; it creates no categorical immunity for withdrawing an offer or recognition. The cutoff does not depend on converting that assumption into a right.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Fall River* supports rational Board policy within the Act, not automatic approval of presumptions. *NLRB v. Curtin Matheson Scientific, Inc.*, 494 U.S. 775, preserves the distinction between legal policy and evidence; its striker-replacement rule decided neither this cutoff nor Auciello's doubt sufficiency. *ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB* supplies explained statutory discretion and arbitrariness review, not a majority-status rule. *NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp.* and *Holly Farms* retain actual statutory predicates and the agency's own reasons rather than a judicial substitute.

##### Which presumption limits and representation procedures qualify the acceptance cutoff?

**Holding:** The acceptance cutoff preserves the distinct certification-year, contract-term and postexpiration presumptions, recognized unusual-circumstances qualifications, and lawful representation procedures. The Board separately reserves case-by-case treatment of doubt-producing information first received virtually simultaneously with acceptance; later assessment of earlier-known information is not that circumstance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part III, unanimous.

**Material application:** Ordinary certification protection lasts one year; ordinary contract protection lasts the term up to three years. Illustrative, nonexhaustive unusual circumstances include dissolution or defunctness of a certified union and a schism transferring substantially all members and officers to another local or international. After protection ends, actual nonmajority requires its own numerical showing; sufficient objective good-faith doubt may rest on circumstantial evidence where that route is lawfully available. For agreements of three years or less, the stated ordinary representation or decertification petition window is more than 60 but less than 90 days before expiration. Healthcare and seasonal operations receive different treatment, with no substitute window decided here. Permission to petition does not permit unilateral refusal to bargain during a contract.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No dissolution, schism, petition or employee vote is adjudicated here. Whether the Board could generally abolish the doubt defense remains open. First presentation of signed anti-representation petitions virtually when acceptance arrives illustrates the simultaneous-information reservation but does not dictate the hypothetical result. That reservation differs from protected-period unusual circumstances and §10(e)'s extraordinary-circumstances excuse. Timely lawful challenges, fraud and material postacceptance events are not foreclosed, but require their own lawful predicates and procedures; none supplies an automatic exception terminating protected obligations or establishes relief in this case.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Brooks* retains protected bargaining stability, its unusual-circumstances qualification and orderly employee-choice routes without renewal of the certification year. Neither *Brooks* nor *Fall River* converts the incumbent's presumption into proof of actual support.

##### Does the accepted timing rule sustain the existing enforcement judgment without a doubt-sufficiency remand?

**Holding:** Because Auciello knew the relevant information before acceptance and the virtually simultaneous first-information reservation does not apply, the Board's permissible timing rule defeats the preserved defense without deciding the evidence's sufficiency or a reasonable assessment period. The entire enforcement judgment therefore stands on that policy ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, unanimous.

**Material application:** The settled findings establish that the November 17 offer remained open, the negotiating committee could accept without membership ratification, and management already possessed the information later invoked. The holiday-week assessment account does not turn earlier knowledge into simultaneous first receipt. Picket-line crossings, union resignations, asserted disaffection and reported new jobs are not interchangeable with opposition to representation. Neither sufficient objective doubt nor actual minority was found or conceded.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not adopt the Board's alternative contention that Auciello had adequate assessment time even under the employer's proposed approach, fixes no period, and makes no finding that sufficient doubt existed on November 18, 21 or 23. That calendar argument is not an independent alternative holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** On the Union's request, Auciello must put the accepted agreement in writing, sign and effectuate it retroactively to November 27, 1988, and comply with the existing make-whole and cease-and-desist order. No new payment amount, liability determination, election, automatic decertification, doubt-sufficiency or compliance hearing is prescribed. Any genuinely remaining lawful implementation issue keeps its ordinary scope; affirmance creates no additional proceeding.

**Material judgment:** First Circuit enforcement judgment affirmed in its entirety, 9–0; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate writing or partial join.

### ERISA preemption of apprenticeship wage incentives

#### California Division of Labor Standards Enforcement v. Dillingham Construction, N.A., Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-789.
**Decided:** February 18, 1997.

##### The wage criterion does not refer to ERISA plans

**Holding:** ERISA §514(a) does not preempt the challenged public-works wage application as a reference to ERISA plans: the lower apprentice rate turns on state approval of an apprenticeship program, not on its ERISA status or the benefit content of a covered plan. The Court accepts this program's covered status for purposes of deciding preemption without holding that every apprenticeship or training arrangement is an ERISA plan.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The particular program's coverage is assumed; not every apprenticeship arrangement is classified as an ERISA plan, and not every California approval requirement is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Travelers governs independently operating state cost rules. Board of Trade is distinguished because the wage criterion does not use covered-plan benefit content as a mandatory measure.

##### The wage choice does not establish a preempted plan command

**Holding:** On this record, §514(a) does not preempt California's requirement that contractors pay prevailing journeyman wages when they do not use apprentices from an approved program. The approval-linked wage incentive neither dictates covered-plan benefits, funding, structure, administration or remedies nor is shown to have economic effects so acute that the retained wage choice becomes an effective plan command; a direct command or demonstrated coercive application remains outside this holding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Material application:** A contractor can retain an unapproved program and pay journeyman wages. The record does not establish effects that eliminate that choice; later program approval is nonretroactive.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Direct regulation, an ERISA-specific benchmark, competing enforcement remedies and demonstrated acute coercion remain outside the holding. No percentage safe harbor or categorical immunity for wage laws, apprenticeship or public purchasing is created. Section 514(d) and the Fitzgerald Act saving theory need not be reached.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversal removes the ERISA ground only. The independent NLRA claim, contingent §1983 damages, genuinely remaining fees, nonpreemption defenses and wage calculation retain their lawful posture. The specific §1988 fee denial for an ERISA right not pursued under §1983 is not automatically reversed, and previously unpreserved contractual estoppel is not revived.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Travelers' distinction between ordinary influence and direct or effective plan command is applied with its acute-effects reservation intact. Boston Harbor and Chamber of Commerce v. Reich provide no public-contract immunity and decide none of the remaining NLRA or damages questions.

### Hostile work environments and retaliation

#### DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers' Association

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 93-8700.

**Decided:** January 6, 1997.

##### The credited course of publication does not meet Title VII's objective threshold

**Holding:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when the complete circumstances establish conduct sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions, objectively hostile or abusive and actually perceived as abusive; no physical advance, superior-subordinate relationship, psychological injury or measurable work loss is indispensable. Crediting this verdict record and favorable reasonable inferences, the supported course of newsletter commentary falls below that objective threshold, so the Association's hostile-environment judgment is affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; six direct joins. The seven-Justice hostile-environment judgment includes Stone-Zsela, who does not join the statutory ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court disapproves requirements of destroyed equal opportunity or palpable performance deterioration. General commentary, workplace distribution and favorable verdict inferences count; contextual evidence of good ratings and support is not an indispensable defense. The First Amendment defense is not reached and no newsletter immunity is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harris is applied without adding a prerequisite; Meritor's alteration-of-conditions boundary remains controlling. No constitutional speech precedent is extended by this holding.

##### The complaint-related publications establish no actionable retaliatory consequence here

**Holding:** Protected activity and hostile commentary do not alone establish Title VII retaliation without a cognizable adverse consequence. The mocking EEOC reference, routine minutes reporting the complaint and announced intention to sue if the action proved groundless establish no denial of an employment or Association benefit, concrete imposed penalty or comparably supported material disadvantage on this record; judgment for the Association is therefore affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not confine retaliation to ultimate employment decisions or categorically exclude threats, harassment or public shaming on stronger facts. No comprehensive retaliation standard is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harris's distinction between offensive treatment and legally sufficient discrimination is preserved at its hostile-environment scope; its injury rule is not converted into a comprehensive retaliation test.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm judgment for the Association: hostile environment 7-2, retaliation unanimously. No liability or damages are restored.

### Public-employee salary-basis exemptions

#### Auer v. Robbins

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-897.
**Decided:** February 19, 1997.

For the separate questions in Auer v. Robbins, No. 95-897 decided February 19, 1997, see Federal Courts — State immunity and local public bodies (The Board's local relationship does not make it an arm of Missouri); Administrative Law — Interpretation of agency regulations (The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds).

##### The salary-basis rule validly implements the public-employee exemption

**Holding:** Section 213(a)(1)'s express power to define and delimit the exemption permits the Secretary to require salary-basis compensation for these public executive and administrative employees. Section 207(k)'s separate law-enforcement hours treatment, public-accountability absence provisions and the need for police discipline do not require a general exemption for short disciplinary deductions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Under then-effective §541.118(a), the employee generally receives the full predetermined salary for any week with work; a week without work need not be paid. Employer- or business-caused lack of work permits no deduction when the employee is ready, willing and able to work. A day or more of personal absence other than sickness or accident may be deducted. Day-or-more sickness or disability deductions must follow a bona fide plan, policy or practice compensating salary loss from both sickness and disability; salary need not be paid before qualification, after leave exhaustion or for compensated days. Alternatively, deductions for a working day or more are permitted under a state sickness-and-disability insurance law or private sickness-and-disability insurance plan when benefits follow that law or plan. Industrial-accident compensation must comply with the applicable compensation law or plan, and the employer must also provide a plan for other sickness and disability. Jury service, witness attendance and temporary military leave permit no salary deductions, but the specified fees or military pay for that week may be offset. Good-faith penalties for major-safety infractions are permitted where the rule prevents serious danger to the plant or other employees.

Section 541.5d(a) preserves otherwise qualifying public employees under a pay system established by statute, ordinance, regulation or a policy or practice based on public accountability, which accrues both personal and sick leave and requires reduced pay or unpaid leave for absences shorter than a workday for personal reasons, illness or injury when accrued leave is unused because permission was not sought or was denied, leave was exhausted, or the employee chose unpaid leave. Under §541.5d(b), a budget-required furlough disqualifies salary status only in the workweek in which it occurs and pay is reduced. These exceptions remain distinct from correction of an improper deduction.

**Material application:** Residency discipline is not a major-safety penalty: that exception concerns serious danger to the plant or other employees. Salary amount and the appellate duties determination are uncontested, and no rulemaking petition or refusal to amend is found.

##### Practical exposure requires an actual practice or an effective particular policy

**Holding:** An employee is subject to improper salary reduction when an actual practice of such deductions exists or an effectively communicated, sufficiently particular policy creates a significant practical likelihood of them. A merely theoretical disciplinary possibility is insufficient, but an actual deduction from each employee is unnecessary; the mixed manual and Guzy's exceptional negotiated sanction do not themselves establish the required policy or recurring practice here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of the Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No numerical deduction threshold, compulsory notice format or general compliance presumption is established. Competing examples and evidentiary-burden contentions remain disputed submissions; the Court directs no general reopening and leaves ordinary preservation rules intact. The later residency rule made termination exclusive but did not repeal all disciplinary authority.

##### Correction eligibility requires actual reimbursement and future compliance

**Holding:** Under §541.118(a)(6), an improper deduction that is inadvertent **or** made for reasons other than lack of work may qualify for correction; reimbursement **and** a promise of future compliance are both necessary to preserve exemption by that route. Guzy's isolated intentional disciplinary deduction qualifies under the second alternative, without an immediate-upon-discovery deadline, but corrected exempt status cannot support disposal of the affected salary claim until both acts are satisfied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V of the Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Correction does not transform a settled recurring practice into an isolated error. General deductions when work is unavailable indicate absence of salary-basis intent and defeat exemption during that period.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmance is expressly conditioned on actual reimbursement and a future-compliance promise before disposing of the affected salary claim on restored exemption. Neither act is found complete; without both, that ground remains unavailable and lawful compensation consequences must be determined. No timetable, fixed overtime amount, restoration of the earlier $9,284 award or reopening of duties is ordered.

### Religious accommodation and completed mixed-motive proof

#### Brown v. Polk County

**Docket or dockets:** Eighth Circuit No. 93-3313.
**Decided:** March 17, 1997.

##### Whether the four religious activities establish accommodation or undue hardship

**Holding:** Under §2000e(j), the employee must establish sincere practice, conflict with employment, employer knowledge and the adverse treatment implicated by the claim; the employer must show effective reasonable accommodation or inability to accommodate without more than de minimis hardship under the governing rule. Supported professional predictions and concrete noncash burdens can suffice without an accident, experiment or complaint, an effective accommodation need not be preferred, and no independent duty to negotiate or try every alternative remains once effective accommodation or sufficient hardship is shown.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** Paid secretarial Bible-study work imposes a sufficient resource burden. The particular prework office prayer also establishes no protected access because arrival started paid work, personal resource use was prohibited, and no forum or comparable allowed personal use was shown; off-clock prayer is not categorically barred. The voluntary, spontaneous, irregular meeting prayers and single work-ethic reference require separate consideration. The completed proof did not connect them to alleged favoritism or polarization; supervisor status and infrequency are relevant but neither proves hardship nor confers immunity.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual disruption is unnecessary, but a proved burden for secretarial work cannot be transferred to every religious practice. Knowledge and failure to attempt accommodation alone do not resolve liability; the Court permits no new hardship trial.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hardison supplies the governing hardship threshold, Ansonia permits an effective nonpreferred accommodation, and Beadle requires practice-specific assessment while permitting supported predictions and noncash costs.

##### Whether the pre-1991 same-decision defense warrants another liability determination

**Holding:** Under the original Price Waterhouse regime, proof by a preponderance of direct decisional evidence that protected religion was a substantial factor actually relied upon shifts to the employer the burden to prove by a preponderance that it would have reached the same decision without that consideration. Meeting that defense defeats liability itself; here its narrow trigger is satisfied and the County’s failure on the completed trial record leaves religious-discharge liability settled without another evidentiary or same-decision opportunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), Part III, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela supports this disposition without joining its rationale.

**Material application:** The decisionmaker’s acknowledgment that the religious reprimand played a role, coupled with the connected evidence and protected conduct within it, meets both O’Connor’s narrower Price Waterhouse trigger and the plurality’s. Legitimate performance, budget and organizational reasons remain evidence but do not prove the same discharge would have occurred without religion; the whole completed account cannot carry that counterfactual burden.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere awareness, stray remarks and unprotected resource use are insufficient. Uncertainty is not invariably a dispositive admission, and no sole-cause requirement, special evidence category or formulaic answer is imposed. The Court preserves Rule 52’s ordinary protection of permissible findings and resolves no other disagreement among the Price Waterhouse writings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The five-day trial supplied the opportunity to prove this preexisting defense; no relevant exclusion or newly imposed burden warrants reopening. The affirmed remand is for relief only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Price Waterhouse’s original liability-stage defense governs at the narrow common trigger. Landgraf prevents importing the 1991 Act’s changed legal consequences for earlier conduct absent an express retrospective command. Warner-Jenkinson’s contextual treatment of a newly express patent burden supplies no reset for this completed employment defense.

##### Which statutory remedies remain after liability is established

**Holding:** The statutory remand concerns backpay and reinstatement under their ordinary amount, mitigation and equitable conditions, without reopening liability or guaranteeing reinstatement. The 1991 Act’s new §102 compensatory and punitive damages and dependent jury election do not apply retrospectively to this conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), Part IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Preexisting statutory relief and independently available constitutional compensation remain distinct. The coordinated Iowa disposition is preserved without independent state-law construction; the affirmed race-discrimination judgment is outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmance preserves the appropriate-relief remand without a new liability trial, fixed award or automatic reinstatement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf and Rivers bar retrospective §102 damages and jury consequences without eliminating preexisting employment relief.

For the constitutional invalidity and distinct constitutional remedies in Brown v. Polk County, Eighth Circuit No. 93-3313, decided March 17, 1997, see First Amendment, “Religion-specific public-workplace restrictions.”

### Supervisory harassment and employer attribution

#### Andrade v. Mayfair Management, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 95-2476.
**Decided:** February 26, 1997.

##### Whether delegated authority can support employer liability without higher-management notice

**Holding:** Title VII permits attribution of a supervisor-created hostile environment to the employer when authority actually delegated to the supervisor materially assists the harassment. Rank and workplace proximity alone do not suffice, but lack of actual or constructive notice to higher management does not defeat this agency route; negligent failure to correct known or reasonably knowable harassment remains a separate basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), unanimous; all nine Justices join both holdings.

**Material application:** Hiring, firing, discipline, threatened personnel action and daily control of working conditions can supply the necessary connection. An express threat, completed personnel action or economic loss is not always required. The administrator’s hiring and firing powers therefore required examination despite his reporting to a corporate officer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision adopts neither automatic liability for every supervisor’s private misconduct nor a new affirmative-defense regime. Whether an actionable environment exists remains distinct from whether the employer is responsible for it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson requires an actual agency inquiry rather than either automatic imputation or an exclusive notice test. Harris v. Forklift Systems and DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers’ Association govern the separate severity-or-pervasiveness inquiry.

##### How the erroneous notice-only premise affects the employer’s Rule 50 judgment

**Holding:** A Rule 50 judgment that forecloses supervisory-agency attribution solely because higher management lacked notice must be reconsidered under the correct attribution rule, drawing permissible evidentiary inferences for the verdict winner. That legal error does not itself establish attribution or resolve preserved alternative grounds.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Evidentiary sufficiency, instructions and preservation remain for proper consideration below; constructive discharge is outside the grant.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed employer-attribution judgment is vacated and remanded. The $25,000 verdict is not automatically restored, no new trial is automatically required, and the constructive-discharge disposition is undisturbed.

#### Faragher v. City of Boca Raton

**Docket or dockets:** Eleventh Circuit No. 94-4878; no separate Supreme Court docket number is supplied.

**Decided:** September 8, 1997.

##### Whether actual delegated authority establishes attribution without higher-management notice

**Holding:** Actual delegated supervisory authority materially aiding creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment supplies an independent ground for employer responsibility under Title VII; the plaintiff must establish that connection. Supervisory rank or workplace proximity alone is insufficient; higher-management notice, an express threat, a completed personnel decision and tangible economic loss are not invariably required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary employment scope, apparent authority and negligence remain distinct alternatives and are not decided. An actionable environment and discriminatory causation remain separate predicates. Personal purpose and violation of an employer prohibition do not alone negate actual aid.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor requires agency analysis and rejects both automatic supervisory liability and notice-only immunity. Andrade supplies the plaintiff-proved actual-power connection; Oncale preserves distinct causation, environment and attribution inquiries. Harris and DeAngelis govern hostile-environment severity, not attribution or constructive notice.

##### Whether the supported operational findings establish material aid in this work unit

**Holding:** The established allocation and exercise of assignments, discipline, daily duties and supervision in this work unit materially aided maintenance of its accepted hostile conditions. These supported bench findings establish the actual-authority ground without a new threat, fear or employment-penalty finding, and do not require a further attribution proceeding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight votes. Stone-Zsela joins Part I only and opposes this conclusive application and restoration mandate.

**Material application:** Actual control over daily work, assignments and discipline in the geographically separate unit supported the connection to maintaining its hostile conditions. Historical findings receive clear-error review; legal significance receives independent review.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new subjective fear, discharge threat, order to submit, lost wage or employment-penalty finding is added. Remoteness and failed policy communication are relevant circumstances, not universal prerequisites or substitutes for actual aid.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor's unresolved findings and Andrade's Rule 50 questions warranted their own remands; Oncale's allegation-stage posture likewise does not require renewed factfinding on these adequate bench findings.

##### Whether the ineffective policy and eventual response defeat this proved attribution

**Holding:** In this case, the combination of a policy not communicated to the relevant workforce, failure to complain to distant higher management and correction after another employee's eventual report does not defeat the proved authority-aided liability. The Court leaves open whether genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse could establish a complete defense, including its elements, burden, relationship to tangible employment action and any distinct limitation on particular relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight votes. Stone-Zsela joins Part I only and opposes this conclusive application and restoration mandate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general complete defense, categorical rejection of every defense, burden-shifting formula, tangible-action exception, universal complaint duty or freestanding dissemination duty is adopted. Effective measures, negligence, apparent authority and avoidable-loss issues retain significance when their predicates exist; dissemination failure alone is not negligence per se.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor does not make paper policy and noncomplaint conclusive. Andrade's reserved defense question remains open; later correction does not retroactively erase earlier attributable misconduct.

##### What relief the supported Title VII findings require

**Holding:** The appellate judgment is reversed only insofar as it reversed Faragher's Title VII judgment against the City, and the case is remanded for reinstatement of the existing one-dollar judgment without a new merits trial or further agency findings. No individual or state-law award, remaining appellate disposition, fee entitlement or additional remedy is adjudicated.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight votes. Stone-Zsela joins Part I only and opposes this conclusive application and restoration mandate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reinstate the existing one-dollar City judgment without another trial or attribution findings. Individual §1983 and battery awards, other state claims and remaining appellate dispositions are undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf prevents new §102 compensatory and punitive liability for the 1985–1990 conduct and forecloses its dependent new jury right. Farrar distinguishes nominal relief from reasonable fees under §1988, supplying only an analogy; Title VII fees retain their own requirements.

#### Harrison v. Eddy Potash, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** Tenth Circuit Nos. 96-2045 and 96-2065; no separate Supreme Court docket number is supplied.

**Decided:** September 15, 1997.

##### Whether material aid requires alter-ego or hire-and-fire authority

**Holding:** An employer may be responsible under Title VII when actual delegated supervisory power materially aids creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment; the plaintiff must prove that connection by a preponderance of the evidence. Hiring or firing authority, alter-ego identity, higher-management notice, an express threat, a completed personnel action and tangible economic loss are not invariably required, while rank, employment or workplace proximity alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), Parts I–V, unanimous.

**Material application:** Evidence that the foreman used daily work orders to isolate Harrison supported this route without proving every alleged incident. Personal purpose, a harassment prohibition and rejection of status-alone liability do not remove the requirement to instruct an adequately supported theory.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Harris's objective-and-subjective severe-or-pervasive standard and Oncale's sex-causation requirement remain distinct; psychological injury, treatment or lost productivity is not mandatory and grave misconduct alone proves neither causation nor attribution.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor supplies agency analysis; Andrade, Oncale and Faragher require actual delegated power materially aiding the wrong without categorical management-notice or personnel-action barriers. Harris and DeAngelis retain the contextual environment standard.

##### Whether the charge prejudicially excluded the supported actual-authority route

**Holding:** Considered as a whole against this record, the preserved charge was prejudicially incomplete because it made apparent authority and reasonable reliance cumulative requirements, imposed an alter-ego barrier and omitted the supported actual-authority/material-aid route. If a distinct apparent-authority theory is properly submitted, it requires reasonable belief traceable to the employer's words or conduct and reasonable reliance on that appearance, not merely the supervisor's claimed power; that alternative is not a condition of actual-authority attribution.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), Parts I–V, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The whole charge receives independent legal review with prejudice required; the choice of particular proposed wording remains a different inquiry. No special verdict establishes an independent correct ground for the employer verdict. The separate tort judgment against Brown proves no complete statutory case against Potash. Apparent-authority factual sufficiency is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The actual-authority omission independently sustains the instructional reversal; no second appellate prejudice determination is required.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Restatement §27 supplies employer-attributable manifestations and reasonable reliance for a distinct apparent-authority theory. Faragher did not decide that alternative; its actual-authority rule is not extended to every assertion of power.

##### Whether policy and response cure the omitted route or establish a complete defense

**Holding:** A prohibition of harassment and correction after notice do not themselves negate earlier material aid from actual delegated power or cure the prejudicially incomplete charge. The Court preserves Faragher's reservation concerning a general complete defense based on genuinely effective prevention and unreasonable employee nonuse, including its elements, burden, relation to tangible employment action and any distinct limitation on relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), Parts I–V, unanimous.

**Material application:** The posted policy discovered after some incidents, its omission from new-hire materials, and the later investigation, discipline, crew change and contact restrictions may matter to their proper inquiries; they do not themselves remove earlier actual aid.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No complete defense is supplied to Potash. Universal policy ignorance, unavailable reporting routes, unreasonable nonuse and inadequate response are not found. No policy-publication duty, general reporting duty or new burden-shifting formula follows; dissemination failure is not negligence per se.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Faragher's general-defense reservation remains unchanged. Meritor distinguishes unwelcome conduct from apparently voluntary participation; the investigator's account is no judicial finding that the conduct was welcome.

##### Whether the asserted contractual grievance requirement bars this independent Title VII action

**Holding:** A contractual grievance assertion does not remove federal subject-matter jurisdiction over an independent Title VII claim. Potash cannot obtain dismissal for nonexhaustion on this appellate record, which includes neither its CBA nor the district court's exhaustion ruling and identifies no agreement covering arbitration of statutory claims or delegation of the antecedent question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), Parts I–V, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The appellate omissions do not establish that no contract existed or that the employer waived every claim. The Court declines a blanket CBA/FAA rule, reserves the general employment-contract exclusion and validity of every explicit collective statutory-arbitration agreement, and reaches no conditional post-delegation review question.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of the employer's cross-appeal in No. 96-2065; no new contract-development or exhaustion proceeding is ordered to cure the unsupported assertion.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gardner-Denver preserves independent Title VII rights despite contractual grievance arbitration. Gilmer enforces an actual statutory-arbitration agreement without supplying missing terms. First Options requires independent judicial determination of assent and clear, unmistakable delegation. Livadas and Hawaiian Airlines protect rights whose adjudication does not require disputed contract interpretation without creating immunity from every arbitration agreement.

##### What mandate and remedial limits govern further Title VII proceedings

**Holding:** The Tenth Circuit's reversal of Potash's Title VII verdict and remand in No. 96-2045, and its rejection of Potash's cross-appeal in No. 96-2065, are affirmed. The remand contemplates a new Title VII trial under corrected instructions, subject to lawful resolution of genuinely preserved independent grounds; if the surviving claim is submitted to a jury, the supported actual-authority route must be accurately instructed, with the prejudicial error already resolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), Parts I–V, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The 1993 conduct is outside Landgraf's pre-enactment bar but establishes no damages entitlement. Section 102 compensation excludes backpay, backpay interest and other §706(g) relief. Punitive damages require malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights. If available §102 compensatory or punitive relief is sought, either party may demand a jury, which must not hear the caps. No amount, cap calculation or punitive predicate is found; duplicate compensation remains prohibited.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm both appellate results. The employer claim proceeds toward retrial under corrected instructions, subject to lawful genuinely preserved independent grounds. Brown's $102,500 compensatory and $40,000 punitive tort awards remain undisturbed; no employer award, fee, reinstatement or new termination claim is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Faragher's nominal restoration on accepted bench findings supplies no jury-case findings. Landgraf retains its separate compensation, punitive and jury conditions without limiting this later conduct to nominal relief.

### Title VII employee counting

#### Walters v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises, Inc. / Equal Employment Opportunity Commission v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-259 and 95-779.

**Decided:** January 14, 1997.

For the other questions in Walters v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises, Inc. / Equal Employment Opportunity Commission v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises, Inc., Nos. 95-259 and 95-779, decided January 14, 1997, see Federal Courts - Preservation and question-presented limits (The unpresented promotion-year issue receives no relief).

##### Count genuine employment relationships on each working day

**Holding:** For Title VII's requirement of fifteen or more employees for each working day in each of twenty or more calendar weeks in the current or preceding calendar year, count an individual on every working day during an existing employment relationship, whether or not that person worked or was paid that day. Payroll ordinarily evidences that relationship but is not conclusive: a person not yet hired, already separated, an independent contractor or within an express statutory exclusion does not become an employee merely by appearing on a list.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Numerosity's jurisdictional character is not decided. Payroll supplies evidence, not conclusive status. Title VII's own employer and employee exclusions govern.

The industry-affecting-commerce requirement and employer-agent language remain. Employer exclusions remain for the United States, wholly United States-owned corporations, Indian tribes, District of Columbia departments or agencies subject by statute to competitive-service procedures, and bona fide private membership clubs other than labor organizations exempt under §501(c). The first-year-after-March-24-1972 transition for employers with fewer than twenty-five employees does not govern this period. The separate employee exclusions remain for elected state or local public officers, their chosen personal staff, policymaking appointees and immediate advisers concerning the office's constitutional or legal powers; those exclusions do not cover employees subject to applicable state or local civil-service laws. Section 2000e(f)'s inclusion of United States citizens employed abroad does not alter this domestic count. No exclusion is found to cover the two part-time employees here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Darden's genuine-relationship framework is applied by analogy at its common-law scope; Town & Country's attention to compatible employment and enacted exclusions is preserved without transplanting NLRA exclusions into Title VII.

##### The stipulated minimum satisfies the 1990 numerical requirement

**Holding:** Under relationship counting, the stipulated 1990 record establishes at least thirty-eight weeks in which Metropolitan had the required employees on each working day, exceeding the twenty-week requirement. That minimum resolves the 1990 numerical objection to the retaliatory-discharge claim without a remand to recompute an exact total or litigate an unsupported worker-status dispute.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Thirty-eight qualifying weeks suffice. The Court does not calculate an exact greater total, classify every payroll entry or reopen stipulated employment relationships; the different nine-week compensation count is not the same set of weeks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Darden and Town & Country preserve the distinction between legal status and an evidentiary list; neither requires relitigation of these stipulated relationships.

##### The remand concerns retaliation, with the numerical objection resolved

**Holding:** The compensation-based judgment is reversed within the retaliatory-discharge dispute in the consolidated cases, and that dispute is remanded for unresolved liability, genuinely remaining defenses and appropriate relief under the correct counting rule. The 1990 numerical objection is resolved, while the promotion dismissal remains undisturbed and this mandate supplies no authority to reopen its calendar years, preservation or unknown 1988 count.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV, unanimous as to retaliation reversal and remand; eight Associate joins on the promotion explanation, with Stone-Zsela agreeing only in its disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Coverage establishes no retaliation, discrimination, causation, damages or final liability; the mandate resolves only the numerical objection and preserves ordinary adjudication of genuinely remaining issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Zafiro's distinction between nonreview and merits adjudication governs the promotion limit; Darden and Town & Country provide no automatic liability or remedial award from employee classification.

#### Inter-Modal Rail Employees Association v. Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway Co.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-491.

##### Section 510 includes prospective welfare-plan rights

**Holding:** ERISA §510 protects against a covered discharge or other prohibited action undertaken for the purpose of interfering with attainment of a right a participant may become entitled to under an employee welfare benefit plan, even though the benefit is not subject to ERISA's statutory vesting requirements. **Holding:** The Ninth Circuit's welfare-interference dismissal is vacated because its categorical nonvesting ground is erroneous, and the case is remanded for consideration of the properly remaining grounds. **Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by all seven other Associates; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela would reverse and direct reinstatement of the welfare claims instead.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Eligibility, purpose, causation, mixed motives, business justification, defendants and authorized relief require adjudication. ### Pension succession and survivor protection

#### Boggs v. Boggs

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-79.
**Decided:** June 2, 1997.

For the federal benefits route in Boggs v. Boggs, June 2, 1997, No. 96-79, see Federal Courts — ERISA benefits jurisdiction.

##### The protected survivor annuity cannot be recovered through a succession accounting

**Holding:** Section 1055 preempts enforcement of the sons' Dorothy-derived claim to any part of Sandra's protected survivor-annuity entitlement. The sons may not obtain that annuity share directly, after an installment is paid, through an accounting, or through an equivalent lump-sum award replacing the share.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Material application:** The sons actually demand Sandra's annuity share, including its equivalent capital value; §1055 protects that entitlement against a change in collection form. The statutory qualified joint-and-survivor benefit pays the survivor for life at least 50% and no more than 100% of the joint-life annuity, with required actuarial equivalence; that range does not authorize outsiders to take part of Sandra's benefit.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Travelers and Dillingham are applied to an actual conflict with a protected benefit rather than mere economic effects or a categorical family-property exception.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This holding concerns Sandra's protected survivor annuity, not the coverage of every other asset under §1055. The waiver rule requires written spousal consent acknowledging the election's effect, witnessed by a plan representative or notary, with the statutory protection against changing the beneficiary designation without further consent unless the consent permits that change. The consent exception requires establishment to the plan representative's satisfaction that there is no spouse, the spouse cannot be located, or another Treasury-prescribed circumstance prevents obtaining consent; consent or its excusal is effective only for that spouse. No such relinquishment is established here. The optional one-year marriage condition does not exclude Sandra on the stipulated chronology. The Court applies the protection governing this annuity without applying later consent amendments backward or deciding any other asset's coverage or waiver.

##### The distributed-property claims depend on an impermissible antecedent transfer

**Holding:** ERISA preempts these claims to pension installments paid to Isaac, the rollover IRA and the distributed ESOP shares because each depends on giving effect to Dorothy's asserted succession-based transfer of an interest in then-undistributed plan benefits outside the applicable federal beneficiary and anti-alienation provisions. Subsequent distribution changes the asset's custody or form but does not validate that antecedent transfer; the rule does not decide a claim arising from a genuinely independent later obligation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion on the pension-receipts, rollover-IRA and distributed-ESOP claims, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent from these three holdings.

**Material application:** Each claim derives from Dorothy's asserted interest in undistributed benefits passing at her death, rather than an independent later bargain, tort, loan or gift. Changing custody or property form does not cure that source. The Court need not hold that the IRA remains an ERISA plan or immunize all pension-traceable assets.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Guidry preserves the pension prohibition without an equitable exception; Mackey preserves the pension/welfare distinction. Travelers and Dillingham retain concrete-conflict analysis and their remote-effects boundary. Board of Trade shows that separate administration alone cannot save an obligation intruding on protected benefits. Inter-Modal preserves independent statutory commands without becoming a succession rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse rejection of preemption for each of the three claims, 5–4, and remand to implement that rule. No accounting fraction, valuation, compensation or new administrator proceeding is ordered.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 1056(d)(2) excepts a voluntary, revocable assignment of no more than ten percent of a benefit payment and an irrevocable assignment or alienation executed before September 2, 1974, but not an assignment made to defray plan administration costs. A loan to a participant or beneficiary secured by the participant's accrued nonforfeitable benefit is not an assignment if exempt from the prohibited-transaction tax under 26 U.S.C. §4975(d)(1). None describes the transfer asserted here.

A qualified domestic relations order under §1056(d)(3) can create or recognize an alternate payee's right to support, alimony or marital property for a spouse, former spouse, child or other dependent through state domestic-relations law, including community-property law. It must give the participant's name and last known mailing address, if any; each alternate payee's name and mailing address; the amount or percentage or means of determining it; the number of payments or period; and each plan affected. It may not require an unprovided benefit type, form or option, increased actuarial benefits, or payment already required to another alternate payee under a previously qualified order. A qualifying alternate payee is a beneficiary, and §1144(b)(7) preserves the linked preemption exception. The Court does not hold that the label probate by itself disqualifies every order.

The distinct REA §303(d) transition requires treatment of a pre-January 1, 1985 domestic relations order as qualified when the administrator was paying benefits pursuant to it on that date. The administrator may treat another earlier order as qualified even without compliance with the amendments' requirements. The 1980 succession order's date alone establishes neither the required payment predicate nor exercise of the permissive authority; permission is not a command. The Court does not direct qualification or a new administrator proceeding, and the ordinary qualification, segregation and determination provisions do not themselves establish that this order was recognized or paid.

The operative succession claim arose with Dorothy's 1979 death and the 1980 order; earlier employment alone does not establish an independent pre-1975 cause of action or act preserved from ERISA. Section 408(g) governs application of the IRA tax section without regard to community-property law; §408(d)(6) separately governs tax treatment of a transfer incident to divorce. Neither supplies the Court's property rule here. The Court decides no taking, preservation of a takings claim, compensation, precise vesting date, separate life-insurance claim, asset valuation or final state-law accounting fraction. No administrator recognition or payment is established beyond the record described above.

##### The changed merits premise permits discretionary fee reconsideration

**Holding:** Reversal of Sandra's merits loss permits consequential consideration of her §1132(g)(1) fee request on remand under that provision's discretion; it does not award fees, fix an amount or create an automatic prevailing-party entitlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees only to leave fees for proceedings below.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Consequential §1132(g)(1) consideration returns with the case; no entitlement, amount or new fee test is established.

### Religious accommodation and notice before discipline

#### Chalmers v. Tulon Co. of Richmond

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 95-2594; review of 101 F.3d 1012.

**Decided:** 1997-06-12.

##### Assess notice against the actual religious conflict and remaining prospective response

**Holding:** An employer's knowledge may be direct or indirect and need not come through prescribed words or a formal request. Familiarity with an employee's faith does not identify every practice, but absence of an exact request before a first manifestation does not by itself defeat a claim when the employer understands the religious practice before discharge and a prospective response remains materially in issue; the timing of knowledge remains important when injury is irreversible or no prospective response can address the conflict.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's common portions, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins to both rules, their applications and the minimum set-aside-and-return consequence. No Marks synthesis supplies the rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General familiarity with faith alone is insufficient. An employer need not anticipate every manifestation, forgive irreversible harm or accept every religious explanation before discipline as sufficient notice. No duty of lesser punishment follows.

##### Supported prospective limits must be considered before this categorical hardship judgment

**Holding:** The employer must supply an effective reasonable accommodation or demonstrate inability to accommodate without more than a de minimis hardship on its business, addressing supported materially distinct possibilities. Concrete noncash costs, reasonable evidence-supported predictions, supervisory pressure and third-party burdens may suffice without an accident, completed harassment violation or experiment; offense, supervisory status or a possible lawsuit alone does not establish the defense. Under Rule 56, this record does not conclusively establish the claimed inability to continue employment under prospective limits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's common portions, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins to both rules, their applications and the minimum set-aside-and-return consequence. No Marks synthesis supplies the rule.

**Material application:** The letters and their concrete supervisory and third-party burdens matter. The record nevertheless leaves material inferences about continued employment under prospective boundaries unresolved. The Court does not rely on the subordinate’s later unsworn account as competent summary-judgment evidence or find a warning or another arrangement feasible.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both grounds sustaining the existing summary judgment require correction; neither is an independently sufficient ground for the Court’s result. No every-alternative, negotiation, experimental-accommodation or least-severe-sanction duty arises. No liability, right to send further letters, compulsory warning, reinstatement, award, constitutional or state-law ruling follows; the abandoned disparate-treatment theory stays abandoned.

**Material judgment:** Five set aside summary judgment and return the accommodation claim. Stone-Zsela would reverse and require trial; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate for ordinary further proceedings; O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm. Neither formal vacatur nor a mandatory trial has five votes.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Remand under the controlling notice and hardship rules without compelling trial despite another lawful disposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Trans World Airlines, Inc. v. Hardison:** Applies the then-governing more-than-de-minimis hardship threshold and its protection of legitimate operational and third-party burdens. No heightened hardship standard replaces it.
- **Ansonia Board of Education v. Philbrook:** Preserves the employer's choice among effective reasonable accommodations and the end of further alternatives inquiry once one resolves the conflict. Mere neutral discipline does not establish that stopping point.
- **Beadle v. City of Tampa:** Applies its distinction between neutral rules and accommodation, its attention to supported materially distinct possibilities, and its permission for concrete noncash costs and professional predictions. Its remand does not establish the feasibility of a particular proposal or require trial in every accommodation case.
- **Brown v. Polk County:** Applies the statutory accommodation part's practice-specific burden and limits on treating supervisor status or offense as conclusive. Its public-employer constitutional holdings and completed-trial mixed-motive liability determination do not decide this private-employer summary-judgment claim.
- **Opuku-Boateng v. California:** Applies the distinction between discussion and effective accommodation, supported prospective hardship and evidentiary limitations. Its completed-trial liability determination does not establish Tulon's liability or entitle Chalmers to a particular accommodation.

### Voluntary affirmative action and layoffs

#### Piscataway Township Board of Education v. Taxman

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit Nos. 94-5090 and 94-5112.
**Decided:** June 16, 1997.

##### Constitutional recognition of an educational interest supplies no Title VII exemption

**Holding:** Selecting which equally qualified and equally senior employee loses an existing position because of race requires permission under Title VII; the employment tie does not remove the discrimination question. Constitutional recognition of a diversity interest does not itself create an exception to that employment statute, and this statutory case decides no equal-protection question.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No equal-protection claim is decided. Hopwood's supported public-university diversity interest and strict scrutiny of actual means remain intact; its reservation of employment and other educational levels is neither statutory permission nor a constitutional prohibition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hopwood and its preserved Bakke competitive-consideration proposition do not amend Title VII or import admissions proof and remedy rules. Adarand's federal constitutional rule supplies no local employment exemption.

##### The nonremedial race-selected discharge violates Title VII on two independent grounds

**Holding:** Title VII's voluntary-affirmative-action accommodation does not authorize choosing an incumbent for discharge because of race solely to preserve departmental diversity, without the remedial connection to a manifest imbalance in a traditionally segregated job category described in Weber and Johnson. A qualifying remedial plan need not rest on proof that this employer committed an actionable past violation; diversity alone does not supply the missing remedial premise here.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: five Justices.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming a diversity purpose could qualify under Title VII, this nonremedial policy unnecessarily trammels the displaced employee's protected interests by assigning her the loss of an existing tenured job because of race, without a remedial limiting goal and despite an available nonracial tie procedure. This record-specific burden independently establishes the violation; it does not invalidate every race-conscious arrangement involving some burden or every remedial layoff plan.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** The same five-Justice Parts II–III coalition directly adopts the unnecessary-trammeling ground as independently sufficient.

**Material application:** The equally qualified, equally senior employees faced a required staff reduction. Taxman bore loss of an existing tenured position under a departmental-diversity policy with no asserted remedial imbalance or remedial limiting goal; a nonracial tie procedure was available.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither every remedial layoff arrangement nor every plan imposing some burden is invalidated. The decision does not require proof of the employer’s own past actionable discrimination for a qualifying remedial plan.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm federal liability and the $134,014.62 award for backpay, benefits and prejudgment interest. The independent NJLAD liability, $10,000 emotional-suffering award and restored seniority remain; prior rehiring makes a new reinstatement order unnecessary. Broader injunction and punitive-damages denials are undisturbed. Stone-Zsela joins the federal rationale and only the disposition of the independent state-law component; no new NJLAD rule is announced.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Weber and Johnson retain their voluntary remedial accommodation, manifest-imbalance premise and protection against unnecessary trammeling; neither is overruled. The Court declines to extend that accommodation to this nonremedial discharge. Wygant’s burden discussion does not become a controlling Title VII or constitutional ruling here.

### Incumbent-union polling and agency evidentiary standards

#### Allentown Mack Sales & Service, Inc. v. NLRB

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-795.

**Decided:** 1998-01-26.

##### The announced reasonable-doubt polling threshold is permissible

**Holding:** The Board may require an employer to possess a good-faith reasonable doubt, supported by objective considerations, of an incumbent union's majority support before conducting a safeguarded poll. Using the same prerequisite for polling, withdrawing recognition and seeking a Board election is not, by itself, irrational or inconsistent with the Act; objective doubt is uncertainty about majority support, not proof that the union has lost it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This threshold is permissible, not obligatory for every future Board; §§7 and 8(c) supply no unconditional right to poll. Existing safeguards require a majority-claim inquiry purpose, its communication, assurances against reprisal, secret ballot, no unfair practices or coercive atmosphere, and advance time-and-place notice. Certification, contract and representation protections retain their distinct predicates.

##### The presented challenge reaches the standard actually applied

**Holding:** A challenge expressly directed to the amount of evidence demanded before polling fairly includes whether the Board applied its announced reasonable-doubt standard to the order under review. That inquiry reaches the associated whole-record application here; it creates no general permission to add an independent factual question merely because merits briefs discuss it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, supplies five direct votes.

##### An announced doubt standard cannot silently become minority proof

**Holding:** Under NLRA §§10(e)–(f) and APA §706, the Board's finding must be supported by substantial evidence on the record as a whole under the substantive standard actually governing the case. When the announced predicate is objective reasonable doubt, the agency cannot sustain its order by effectively demanding proof approaching actual loss of majority support, or silently convert recurring evidentiary outcomes into a different substantive or proof rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Parts III–IV have Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, providing five direct votes for this framework and its application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The agency’s §10(c) preponderance burden differs from whole-record substantial-evidence review. Lawful expressly adopted presumptions remain possible; expertise cannot silently alter the governing standard, and no systematic-manipulation finding or automatic credit for unverified reports follows.

##### This record requires denial of enforcement

**Holding:** The finding that Allentown lacked objective reasonable doubt when it announced the poll is unsupported by substantial evidence on the whole record. The enforcement judgment must therefore be reversed with instructions to deny enforcement of the challenged Board order; the record does not call for another agency weighing under the same or a newly demanding threshold.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part IV has Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, supplying five direct votes for the application and complete mandate.

**Material application:** The individually accepted reports together with Marsh, Bloch and steward Mohr establish objective uncertainty without a headcount of actual minority support. Their cumulative relation to the continuing workforce controls; the later 19–13 poll provides no retroactive justification.

##### The separate remedy objection remains outside review

**Holding:** Section 10(e) bars an objection not urged before the Board unless extraordinary circumstances excuse the omission. The separate objection to the bargaining order's explanation and decertification bar was not preserved, and no such excuse is established; that objection is not reviewed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion: Part V has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in leaving the objection unreviewed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The facial challenge is rejected by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The different majority of Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reverses enforcement and directs its denial. No second weighing of the unchanged record, retroactively higher threshold, new poll or permanent decertification follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Auciello's accepted-offer cutoff remains intact. Its statutory limits, distinction between circumstantial doubt and actual minority, and separate §10(e) requirement are applied; it had not resolved incumbent polling or the weight of these reports.
- Fall River supplies separate review of rational Board policy and supported factual application. Its continuity and successor principles are not reopened, and its rebuttable presumption does not become proof of employee preference.
- Universal Camera supplies whole-record substantial-evidence review, including contrary evidence and the examiner's actual findings, while retaining deference between fairly conflicting views. The Court applies that standard to this unsupported finding rather than replacing it with de novo factfinding.
- Gissel retains its protection of noncoercive expression and its attention to the employment setting. Its reservation concerning mandatory permission for reasonable inquiry is preserved; the Court derives no unconditional polling entitlement from it.
- Hubbard's distinction between supporting arguments and new questions is applied to the wording and challenged order here. No exception based merely on merits briefing is adopted.
- Struksnes's procedural safeguards and the additional advance-notice requirement remain operative. Their satisfaction does not eliminate the separately permissible incumbent-polling prerequisite.

### Pension withdrawal-liability collection and limitations

#### Bay Area Laundry and Dry Cleaning Pension Trust Fund v. Ferbar Corp. of California

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-370.

**Decided:** 1997-12-15.

##### Collection accrual requires a payment violation

**Holding:** Withdrawal creates liability under 29 U.S.C. §1381, but a collection cause does not accrue until the plan has assessed liability, set a schedule and demanded payment, and the employer fails to pay an installment that is due. Section 1451(f) supplies the longer of six years after accrual or three years after the plaintiff acquired or should have acquired actual knowledge of the cause; in fraud or concealment, an action may be brought within six years after discovery of the cause. No fraud or concealment finding is made here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts I–II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The plan must still assess and demand payment as soon as practicable under §1399(b)(1); proper delay objections remain available. Payments commence no later than sixty days after demand notwithstanding review or appeal. No exhaustion requirement postpones payment.

##### Installments accrue separately unless valid acceleration changes maturity

**Holding:** Each missed installment gives rise to a separate collection claim and its own limitations period. An expired first-payment claim does not extinguish later timely installments; after a legally effective election to accelerate, the period on the accelerated debt runs from that exercise. A later aggregate demand cannot revive installments already barred.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts I–II, unanimous.

**Material application:** Nonpayment default requires failure to cure within sixty days after written notice; a plan-defined alternative default must indicate substantial likelihood of inability to pay. Default does not itself elect acceleration. Whether the complaint’s alternative full-debt request accomplished acceleration remains open.

##### The Fund cannot revive its first-payment claim on this petition posture

**Holding:** Having obtained review on its representation that the conflicting installment rules determined the case and that the single-period rule barred its suit, the Fund may not now recover the first $345.50 installment while obtaining reversal for the later installments.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees only in excluding the first installment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The $345.50 exclusion rests on this petition posture, not a found receipt date, universal February 1 maturity date or rule that alternative arguments always forfeit a claim.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded for installment-specific timeliness and remaining assessment, acceleration and remedy questions, excluding the first $345.50 installment. No award, full-debt payment or automatic prospective injunction is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Milwaukee is applied for the distinction between computational assumptions and actual payment, default and acceleration; its installment-calculation rule remains unchanged. Concrete Pipe's neutral adjudication and lawful factual and actuarial rebuttal burdens remain intact; the assessment is not conclusively validated here. North Star's borrowing rule is distinguished because Congress enacted the relevant limitation. Inter-Modal's distinction between substantive plan rights and enforcement predicates is consistent with this decision but supplies no preexisting withdrawal-accrual rule. No Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

### False denials in federal employment discipline

#### LaChance v. Erickson; LaChance v. McManus

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1395, including the Rule 12.4 McManus companion.

**Decided:** 1998-01-21.

##### Due process protects a meaningful answer, not proved knowing falsity

**Holding:** The Fifth Amendment's opportunity to answer an agency misconduct charge does not immunize a knowingly false factual denial from an otherwise lawful employment falsification charge or penalty consideration. An unsuccessful defense is not itself a knowing lie: the agency must establish the actual false statement and requisite culpability under the governing employment rules, while preserving good-faith factual contest, mistakes, uncertain recollection, legal disagreement and procedural nonadmission.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion has all nine Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No criminal jury, criminal burden or universal materiality element is added to employment proceedings. Shared evidence may prove misconduct and falsification without a different-source or corroboration condition.

##### The Civil Service Reform Act supplies safeguards without denial immunity

**Holding:** Sections 7513 and 7701 do not confer categorical immunity for a proved knowingly false factual denial. Their cause, procedure, proof and review safeguards continue to govern every particular charge and sanction; rejection of immunity establishes no employee's falsity, service nexus, regulatory liability or final penalty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion is joined by all seven other Associate Justices, providing eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejection of the statutory immunity claim.

**Material application:** Section 7513 ordinarily requires thirty days’ advance written specific notice, with the crime exception requiring reasonable cause to believe an imprisonable crime occurred; an answer period of at least seven days, representation and a reasoned written decision follow. An agency hearing is permissive by regulation, distinct from Board rights. Ordinary misconduct uses preponderance proof; designated performance and senior-executive matters retain substantial-evidence proof. Harmful procedural error, prohibited personnel practice or legal invalidity prevents sustaining action. Section 7703 appellate review remains distinct from agency proof, including designated discrimination de novo review; OPM review retains its statutory predicates.

##### Penalty consideration and impeachment remain conditional

**Holding:** A proved knowing false denial may receive otherwise lawful consideration in selecting an employment penalty or assessing credibility; the categorical bars resting on denial immunity cannot stand. Such use is permitted, not compelled, and an adverse finding on the underlying misconduct alone establishes neither falsification nor an entitlement to enhance a sanction or impeach the employee.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela additionally joins its penalty-consideration proposition and its actual-proof and lawful-procedure qualifications, but agrees only in the disposition removing the distinct impeachment bar.

##### The reporting branch and self-incrimination require their own predicates

**Holding:** The Barrett/Roberts reporting disposition is set aside only insofar as it rests on categorical immunity from providing information because it would implicate the employee; further proceedings must address the actual duty and every supported defense. Rejecting a right to lie neither compels waiver of a valid privilege nor decides a genuine compelled-incrimination claim, and it creates no general duty to confess.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Souter's opinion has all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela joins only the proposition preserving separate compelled-incrimination review and prohibiting compulsory waiver of a valid privilege; he agrees only in the reporting disposition itself.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 5.4 requires information for designated civil-service bodies or representatives but excepts disclosure prohibited by law or regulation. Garrity bars criminal use of compelled employment answers; Gardner protects refusal to waive use and fruits protection while preserving its narrow official-duty distinction. No actual compulsion, waiver, silence penalty or general ban on lawful civil inferences is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both appellate judgments are reversed insofar as they adopt denial immunity and dependent penalty and impeachment bars. The reporting branch is set aside only to the extent dependent on that premise. Unreviewed false-time findings remain; no original sanction is automatically restored and no employee’s final sanction is selected.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Loudermill is applied to preserve meaningful notice and response, adequate later process and the difference between inaccuracy and knowing deception. It supplies no immunity for a proved lie.
- Bryson supplies the bounded principle against deliberate falsehood as a response to an official inquiry; its criminal statutory elements and proof conditions are not transferred to employment discipline.
- Garrity and Gardner remain operative for compelled testimony, criminal-use protection and impermissible waiver demands, with Gardner's official-duty distinction intact. No respondent-specific privilege claim is adjudicated.
- Gaudin remains a criminal element-allocation holding, not a command for an administrative jury or criminal burden. Lanier preserves conduct-time warning in its stated setting; today's decision supplies no missing prior notice for an individual charge.
- Jeffries, Richenberg and Valot retain their actual-conduct, meaningful-rebuttal, lawful-process and bounded-remedy rules. None confers categorical denial immunity or creates a general investigation checklist.
- Hudson retains its distinct civil/criminal classification inquiry and supplies no complete due-process answer here. Lee's supported-duty and threshold-remand rulings establish neither employee falsity nor an individual reporting obligation.

### Age-discrimination waivers and tender

#### Oubre v. Entergy Operations, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1291.

**Decided:** 1998-01-26.

##### A deficient ADEA waiver cannot be enforced through retention or prior tender

**Holding:** A release failing the applicable minimum requirements of 29 U.S.C. §626(f) cannot bar an ADEA action merely because the employee retains its consideration, whether the asserted bar is called ratification, prior tender or estoppel resting on that same retention. The party asserting waiver validity retains the statutory burden of proving a knowing and voluntary waiver under the applicable paragraph. The ruling neither invalidates a compliant waiver nor decides a distinct fraud, contract or equitable claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** This release omitted a specific ADEA reference, allowed fourteen rather than twenty-one days for consideration and lacked seven-day postexecution revocation. Signature, attorney consultation or later awareness cannot supply those missing protections. No group program triggering forty-five days and disclosure duties, or settlement of an already filed charge or action under paragraph (2), is established; the latter retains paragraph (1)(A)–(E) and a reasonable consideration period.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The entire contract is not declared absolutely void. Lawful restitution, recoupment, setoff, other rights and genuine distinct equitable conduct remain open; no automatic deduction or employer recovery follows, and repayment cannot be a condition to suit. The employee must prove discrimination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded, 7–2, permitting the ADEA action without prior tender and without awarding compensation or ordering repayment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mezzanatto retains its bounded evidentiary-waiver rule and right-specific statutory limits. It does not permit common-law consent principles to replace OWBPA's express requirements or assigned burden.
- McKennon retains its separation of ADEA liability from supported remedial limitations. Its after-acquired-misconduct proof requirement and relief rules do not decide severance accounting.
- Brooklyn Savings' protection against private defeat of a public statutory safeguard remains consistent with the right-specific distinction; no universal prohibition of statutory settlements is adopted.

### Agency-fee arbitration and nonconsenting objectors

#### Air Line Pilots Association v. Miller

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-428.
**Decided:** 1998-05-26.

##### Whether nonconsenting fee objectors must exhaust the union’s private arbitration procedure

**Holding:** Nonmember employees who did not agree to arbitrate need not exhaust this union-provided impartial arbitration procedure before bringing their compulsory-fee claims in federal court. Hudson's requirement that a union offer a prompt impartial opportunity to contest fees does not make that private procedure a compulsory preliminary step; the ruling does not displace express statutory exhaustion requirements, appropriate administrative exhaustion or an actual arbitration agreement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven direct supporters including Souter.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Participation solely under protest supplies no assent. Objectors must identify their objections sufficiently, and the union must establish the challenged expenditures’ relation to collective representation. Courts may manage actual duplication, including a balanced pause for prompt arbitration, without making arbitration universal or preclusive. Consenting parties, audit adequacy, particular expenditures and the final fee amount remain undecided. Hudson’s conceded application here does not decide every private-union arrangement.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmed, 7–2, on exhaustion, preserving the D.C. Circuit’s bounded remand for independent adjudication of the remaining 1992 fee dispute. Earlier unappealed agency-shop challenges and other years are outside review. No refund, fee percentage or discovery plan is directed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Chicago Teachers Union v. Hudson, 475 U.S. 292:** Applied as a requirement to provide an adequate fee explanation, a reasonably prompt impartial opportunity and escrow for reasonably disputed amounts; it is not converted into compulsory exhaustion or preclusive arbitral review.
- **AT&T Technologies v. Communications Workers, 475 U.S. 643:** Applied to preserve agreement as the source of contractual arbitration authority.
- **First Options of Chicago v. Kaplan, May 22, 1995:** Applied to antecedent assent; a protest appearance does not itself establish consent. Its arbitrability rule does not decide every noncontractual exhaustion duty.
- **McCarthy v. Madigan, March 4, 1992:** Applied to the actual statutory scheme and institutional function. Its surviving judicial-exhaustion framework is neither overruled nor expanded into a universal no-consent exception.

### COBRA continuation and other group coverage

#### Geissal v. Moore Medical Corp.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-689.

**Decided:** 1998-06-08.

##### Existing other coverage is not a post-election event

**Holding:** Otherwise eligible COBRA continuation cannot be denied or terminated under the applicable 29 U.S.C. §1162(2)(D)(i) merely because another group plan already covered the beneficiary before election. That branch requires the beneficiary first to become covered after election, as an employee or otherwise, under another group plan containing no exclusion or limitation concerning any preexisting condition of that beneficiary; continuous coverage or a change in its primacy is not a new coverage event.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's statutory-construction portion, joined by Stone-Zsela and all seven other Associates, supplies nine direct votes for this rule and explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The statute's condition-specific preexisting-condition qualification does not authorize an overall comparison of insurance adequacy, deductibles or coverage gaps. The reported absence of an Aetna exclusion for Geissal's condition establishes neither post-election acquisition nor full reimbursement. The ruling removes this particular denial ground. Eligibility, proper election, premiums, duration, other lawful termination grounds, actual plan benefits and lawful coordination remain separate. The separate Medicare-entitlement branch of section 1162(2)(D)(ii) has no application established here. The later HIPAA addition was not effective for this dispute and does not replace the applicable text.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The eight Associates adopt vacatur of the reviewed Rule 54(b) judgment and remand without an independent estoppel decision or automatic benefit award. The statutory error does not adjudicate waiver, document liability, fiduciary deception, inter-plan reimbursement or a damages amount. Count I is vacated by the eight Associates; Stone-Zsela would reverse and remand it. All nine vacate Count II, with Stone-Zsela agreeing only in that disposition. Counts III and IV were outside the reviewed judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Inter-Modal Rail Employees Association v. Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway Co. is applied for enforcing actual statutory protection rather than substituting an assumption about nonvested benefits; it does not create permanent continuation rights.
- Varity Corp. v. Howe preserves its distinct fiduciary-function, loyalty and equitable-relief requirements. This timing decision supplies no new fiduciary violation or equitable award.


### ADEA enforcement, working owners and compulsory retirement

#### Equal Employment Opportunity Commission v. Johnson & Higgins, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** 96-1743; Second Circuit No. 95-6216.

**Decided:** 1998-09-21.

##### Private opposition does not disable the Commission's public enforcement action

**Holding:** The EEOC may enforce the ADEA under 29 U.S.C. §626(b) without an affected individual's charge, consent or request for recovery, and §626(f)(4) prevents a private waiver from extinguishing that public authority even if the waiver is valid. The Commission must attempt informal conciliation, conference and persuasion before suit; the actual efforts here satisfy that condition without a duty to repeat an unproductive offer indefinitely.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg). Part I of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins.

**Material application:** Section 626(b) incorporates public enforcement powers and remedies; §626(d)'s charge and waiting requirements govern an individual's action, while §216(b)'s party-plaintiff consent requirement does not govern the Commission. General Telephone Co. of Northwest v. EEOC supports independent public enforcement in its Title VII setting, and Gilmer v. Interstate/Johnson Lane Corp. preserves Commission authority despite an individual's separate arrangement. The ADEA's own text decides this case. Notice of investigation, a determination, the July 1992 meeting and invitation, and renewed September and December information requests gave the company the actual violation theory and opportunities to resolve it before the August 5, 1993 suit. Its resistance explains why repetition was unnecessary; categorical denial alone is not a general exception to the statutory attempt requirement. Opposing director affidavits therefore do not bar this suit. Oubre v. Entergy Operations preserves enacted waiver safeguards, but the Court need not determine each release's private effect because §626(f)(4) independently protects enforcement. No automatic private damages, recovery for unwilling persons, repayment or double-recovery ruling follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- General Telephone supports independent agency enforcement in its statutory setting without replacing the ADEA text.
- Gilmer preserves independent agency enforcement despite an individual's arrangement, without replacing the ADEA text.
- Oubre's private-waiver holding remains distinct; this decision neither enlarges nor retreats from it.

##### Employee status depends on the complete relationship

**Holding:** The circular employee definition in 29 U.S.C. §630(f) invokes ordinary common-law agency and requires consideration of all relevant incidents of the actual relationship. Corporate form, an employee label, director title, ownership, profit-based compensation or an exclusive three-factor formula cannot alone establish or defeat employment; a genuine proprietor remains outside employee coverage, while actual employment may coexist with ownership and directorship.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg). Part II has all nine joins.

**Material application:** Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden supplies the ordinary-agency presumption and the all-incidents inquiry. Relevant matters include control over work and removal, skill, tools and location, duration, assignment and scheduling authority, compensation, assistants, business integration, benefits and tax treatment; in this setting, governance powers, equity and profit-and-loss arrangements must also receive their actual weight. NLRB v. Town & Country Electric permits compatible roles while preserving actual control and statutory boundaries. Walters v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises likewise distinguishes a genuine relationship from an evidentiary label, and Robinson v. Shell Oil requires attention to the particular statutory setting. Those decisions do not classify every working owner as an employee. Incorporation does not create categorical coverage, and the company's partnership analogy does not erase a distinct employment relationship. ADEA incorporation of FLSA remedies does not import the FLSA's broader definition of employment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Darden's common-law method applies to the ADEA's circular definition.
- Town & Country preserves compatible roles and actual service within its statutory boundaries; unrelated exclusions are not transplanted.
- Walters supports genuine-relationship inquiry at its statutory scope without importing unrelated Title VII holdings.
- Robinson supports attention to statutory context without importing unrelated Title VII holdings.

##### These directors continue covered employment alongside their ownership and governance

**Holding:** On the complete undisputed relationship, the employee-directors remain ADEA employees while holding stock and exercising substantial collective governance. Their actual full-time operational service, individual review, compensation determination and removal controls establish employment that election to the board does not extinguish.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg). Part III has six direct votes: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate and remand classification and the dependent liability and injunction.

**Material application:** The directors are selected from existing senior officers and continue their ordinary managerial work. Six senior directors conduct annual reviews; compensation depends on performance and an annually determined profit percentage; each director submits a resignation exercisable by two thirds of the board. These controls operate on each individual despite collective ownership. The Court gives substantial weight to the contrary features: required shares, voting and appointment powers, authority over merger or dissolution, profit participation, mutual succession terms, compulsory share sale on departure and the ten-year consulting/noncompetition arrangement. Those features establish genuine ownership and governance, but do not eliminate the continuing operational employment subject to other senior actors' control. Darden requires this complete assessment, rather than acceptance of the lower court's categorical phrasing or exclusive selection of three indicia. The undisputed incidents permit decision now. No inactive director becomes an employee by title alone, and no rule places all partners, shareholders or full-time proprietors within the Act.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Darden applies its complete inquiry to the actual dual relationship without a per se rule for corporate owners.
- Town & Country preserves compatible roles in applying that inquiry without a per se corporate-owner rule.

##### A sensible reason for selecting age is not a factor other than age

**Holding:** For a covered employee, compulsory retirement at the end of the year age 62 is attained, or earlier at the end of the year both age 60 and fifteen board-service years are attained, is an age-based employment decision. Succession planning does not make those express age conditions reasonable factors other than age under §623(f)(1); the corporate-need extension available only on the age-60/service branch does not remove the age trigger.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg). Part IV.A's statutory rule has nine direct joins. The six-Justice coverage coalition applies it to liability here; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join only on the express condition that employee status is established.

**Material application:** Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins distinguishes years of service actually independent of age from a decision motivated by age or using a criterion as its proxy. These alternatives expressly require age. The chairman's corporate-need finding, approved by the executive committee, permits only year-by-year continuation under the earlier branch and never beyond the end of the year age 62 is attained. The company's reasonable desire for succession therefore cannot satisfy a provision requiring a criterion other than age. It may pursue another legally permissible criterion or separate lawful governance retirement from covered employment. No heightened constitutional age tier is created, and no bona fide occupational qualification, foreign-law conflict or qualifying benefit/seniority-plan defense is established on this record.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hazen Paper's actual-age/non-age distinction controls. Its willfulness rule is preserved; facial liability does not itself establish knowledge or reckless disregard or award liquidated damages.

##### The executive-retirement provision does not authorize these ages

**Holding:** Section 631(c) permits compulsory retirement only for an employee who has attained age 65, following bona fide executive or high-policymaking employment throughout the two immediately preceding years, with an immediate nonforfeitable aggregate annual retirement benefit of at least $44,000 from the employer's pension, profit-sharing, savings or deferred-compensation plans, or their combination. This policy's ages 60 and 62 fail the age condition regardless of the unresolved benefit valuation; executive status alone supplies no exemption.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg). Part IV.B's statutory construction has the eight Associates. The five Associates affirming coverage apply it here. Stone-Zsela agrees in rejection of this exception without adding a ground; the three classification dissenters retain their coverage condition and do not join the ultimate liability judgment.

**Material application:** The exception contains cumulative conditions, not a general freedom to retire any executive. For benefits paid in a form other than a straight-life annuity without ancillary benefits, or involving employee contributions or rollovers, §631(c)(2) requires the prescribed adjustment to the equivalent of a straight-life annuity without ancillary benefits under a plan without employee or rollover contributions. Neither the ten-year contract nor a general description of generous retirement benefits establishes those requirements. The age condition alone disposes of the asserted applicability to the maintained policy; no benefit amount or annuity valuation is found. The Court neither requires a proprietor to remain in governance nor extends the exception below its enacted age.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The statutory exception is applied according to its own terms; no earlier decision is displaced and no general executive exclusion is created.

##### The prospective order protects employment without guaranteeing ownership or board tenure

**Holding:** The Second Circuit's affirmance of liability and the bounded employment injunction stands. The order bars the unlawful employee-retirement policy, agreements by incoming employee-directors to that unlawful retirement, and covered employment termination because of age; it requires rescission and submission of a compliant policy on a date to be set below, while leaving separately lawful ownership and board arrangements available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg). Part V's affirmance has the same six-Justice coalition as Part III. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate the dependent commands pending correct classification; all nine accept that coverage alone establishes no monetary or governance entitlement.

**Material application:** Review under §1254(1) reaches the §1292(a)(1) injunction appeal following partial summary judgment; no final damages award is before the Court. The maintained policy and asserted right to continue it provide a concrete preventive basis under incorporated §217 and §626(b). Federal Election Commission v. Williams requires that independent prospective basis, but supplies no ADEA limitations or tolling rule. The identified policy and described prohibitions satisfy the presented specificity objection; the order is not a general command to obey every employment law. The company may separate board or equity retirement from employment if statutory law permits, but may not use that separation indirectly to end covered employment because of age. Monetary relief, willfulness, lawful effects of individual agreements, setoff, repayment and any proper individual remedy remain for their own adjudication. No automatic damages, reinstatement, lifetime board seat, equity retention or recovery for an unwilling person is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Williams's prospective-remedy requirement applies at its actual scope.
- Oubre preserves the distinction between statutory protection and independently authorized monetary consequences.

### False Claims Act retaliation and independent protected activity

#### United States ex rel. McKenzie v. BellSouth Telecommunications, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** 97-850.

**Decided:** 1998-09-28.

##### The employee's retaliation claim has independent statutory conditions

**Holding:** The then-operative §3730(h) protects an employee against covered employment retaliation because of lawful acts in furtherance of an FCA action, including qualifying investigation or assistance before an action is filed; protection does not depend solely on a successful qui tam recovery or survival of the public-disclosure bar. McKenzie's allegations permit the required inferences of protected activity, employer knowledge and causation at the reviewed dismissal stage, so the Sixth Circuit's reversal and retaliation remand are affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas). All nine Justices adopt Thomas's Part III; nine direct rationale votes.

**Material application:** McKenzie alleged persistent reporting of the fraud beyond ordinary dispatcher tasks, presentation to supervisors of a newspaper account of similar federal qui tam litigation, and responsive threats and harassment. These facts permit an inference that BellSouth understood her conduct as connected to contemplated federal enforcement. The employer need not hear the precise statutory label when the alleged facts provide the required notice. Ordinary performance complaints without such a connection are not automatically protected. Robinson supports reading retaliation protection in its statutory context while keeping prohibited conduct, notice and causation distinct; its Title VII rule does not supply FCA elements. Discovery may disprove any predicate. The judgment finds neither actual fraud nor retaliation, disability causation or an entitlement to reinstatement, back pay or damages. The claim proceeds under its own statutory conditions rather than falling automatically with a distinct qui tam jurisdictional ruling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Robinson v. Shell Oil Co. supplies contextual treatment of a retaliation provision while its Title VII coverage holding and limits remain distinct.

Related questions in United States ex rel. McKenzie v. BellSouth Telecommunications, Inc., 1998-09-28, dockets 97-850, appear under Federal Courts — False Claims Act public disclosure and original-source jurisdiction.

### Title VII attribution for unfulfilled supervisory threats

#### Burlington Industries, Inc. v. Ellerth

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-569.
**Decided:** 1998-06-26.

##### Unfulfilled threats may support a hostile-environment claim under the existing actual-authority rule

**Holding:** An employee who rejects sexual advances and suffers no completed tangible retaliation may pursue a Title VII hostile-environment claim, but must prove discrimination because of sex, an objectively and subjectively abusive environment sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions, and employer attribution. For the actual-authority route, the employee must prove by a preponderance that delegated supervisory power materially aided creation or maintenance of that environment; rank, employment or workplace proximity alone is insufficient, while final hiring or firing power, a fulfilled threat, economic loss and higher-management notice are not invariably necessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by all eight Associate Justices; nine direct joins to the rule, application and limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Employer negligence, employment scope and apparent authority remain distinct routes. Apparent authority requires an employer-attributable manifestation and reasonable reliance; the supervisor's own assertion is insufficient, and those requirements do not become elements of actual authority. No universal tangible-action definition, automatically sufficient single threat or distinct quid-pro-quo claim is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor's agency requirement is applied through Andrade, Faragher and Harrison's actual-power/material-aid rule; Harris and Oncale retain their independent environment and discrimination predicates.

##### Policy knowledge and nonreporting alone do not supply a complete defense

**Holding:** Ellerth's knowledge that a harassment policy existed and her failure to report during employment do not alone eliminate earlier material aid from delegated authority or establish a complete defense. Whether genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse can establish a general complete defense remains open, including its elements, burden, relationship to tangible employment action and any distinct limitations on particular relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices; nine direct joins. The three-Justice concurrence's additional recoverability proposal is not part of this holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds neither policy effectiveness nor employee reasonableness. No burden shift, universal reporting or publication duty, tangible-action exception, negligence safe harbor or automatic erasure of incurred harm is adopted. The three-Justice concurrence's avoidable-loss proposal creates no governing recovery limitation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Faragher's complete-defense reservation and Harrison's preservation of it remain unchanged; Meritor does not make a policy and noncomplaint conclusive.

##### The appellate reversal stands, with ordinary further proceedings

**Holding:** The Seventh Circuit's judgment reversing employer summary judgment on the appealed attribution claim is affirmed, and the case is remanded for further proceedings under Parts I–II. The legal error in categorical nonliability establishes no final liability, damages or entitlement to trial; genuinely preserved independent grounds may be resolved under ordinary procedures, and the separately dismissed constructive-discharge claim remains outside this disposition absent established preservation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices; nine direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The categorical attribution error alone establishes no final claim element. The 1993–94 conduct does not trigger Landgraf's pre-1991 temporal bar, but statutory damages, punitive predicates, fees and jury entitlement remain unadjudicated. No compulsory trial, second agency hearing, amendment, new discovery or revived constructive-discharge appeal is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Andrade's bounded correction and Faragher's distinct findings-based remedy are applied at their actual scope; Harrison's procedural distinctions and Landgraf's temporal limit remain intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The case returns through the Seventh Circuit for ordinary proceedings on the appealed Title VII claim. Ellerth must establish the required predicates under the governing rules; Burlington may present genuinely preserved independent grounds. No factual issue, defense, damages, fee award, jury entitlement or constructive-discharge claim is resolved by the mandate.
