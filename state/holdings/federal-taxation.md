## Federal Taxation

### Bankruptcy fiduciaries’ filing and payment duties

#### Holywell Corp. v. Smith, 503 U.S. 47 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-1361 and 90-1484.
**Decided:** February 25, 1992.
**Companion or consolidated matters:** *United States v. Smith*, No. 90-1484, consolidated with *Holywell*; both challenge the declaration of no filing or payment duty.

##### Whether the assignee of substantially all corporate property must file corporate returns and pay the associated tax

**Holding:** An assignee holding all or substantially all of a corporation’s property must make its income-tax return under §6012(b)(3), whether or not the corporation dissolves or the assignee continues its business. Smith’s receipt of the corporate estate property satisfies that category, and §6151(a) requires payment of the tax due with those returns.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; unanimous statutory construction.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The plan assigned substantially all corporate property to Smith for liquidation and distribution. The filing duty follows the statutory assignee category and does not depend on operating the business or dissolving the corporations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No alternative statutory classification, exact income, deduction, penalty or tax amount is decided. The duties concern returns and accompanying payment after appointment, not taxes already due before appointment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand in both dockets for Smith to file the prescribed corporate returns as assignee and pay the tax due with them under §6151; amounts and implementation are left to ordinary tax and bankruptcy procedures.

##### Whether Smith must file and pay as fiduciary of the separate liquidating trust holding Gould’s former estate property

**Holding:** Smith must file the separate liquidating trust’s return under §6012(b)(4) and pay the associated tax under §6151: the plan vested Gould’s estate property directly in a separate trust organized to liquidate and distribute the transferred assets and authorized Smith to perform those functions, making him its fiduciary despite limits on his discretion. Because the property did not first revest in Gould for contribution to the trust, the asserted grantor-trust theory does not displace Smith’s duty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; unanimous trust-specific construction and application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The plan created a separate trust to liquidate and distribute transferred assets within Treasury Regulation §301.7701-4(d); Smith’s powers satisfy §7701(a)(6) and Regulation §301.7701-6 despite limits on his discretion. The estate transferred Gould’s property directly to the trust; Gould did not first recover and contribute it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal tax treatment for all liquidating trusts is adopted. Grantor provisions retain their operation when their predicates exist; the result of a different revesting-and-contribution arrangement is not decided. The Court computes no tax amount.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand in both dockets for Smith to file as trust fiduciary and pay the associated tax, with computation and implementation left to ordinary procedures.

##### Whether confirmation of a plan silent about taxes and the United States’ failure to object excuse later statutory duties

**Holding:** Confirmation under §1141(a) of a plan silent about taxes, coupled with the United States’ failure to object, does not excuse the statutory return and payment obligations imposed on this assignee and trust fiduciary after appointment. The binding treatment of properly encompassed preconfirmation claims does not establish an exemption from these later obligations.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; unanimous rejection of the confirmation defense.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The government seeks recognition of Smith’s postappointment duties, not payment from him of taxes already due before appointment. The plan neither stated nor adjudicated an exemption; §6151 connects payment to the required return’s due date.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes, without deciding more broadly, that confirmation can bind creditors concerning properly encompassed preconfirmation claims. Preappointment taxes and express adjudication of existing tax claims remain outside the disposition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The no-duty declaration is reversed in both dockets; confirmation supplies no defense to implementation of the postappointment filing and payment obligations.

### Capital expenditures

#### INDOPCO, Inc. v. Commissioner, 503 U.S. 79 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1278.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether a separate asset is necessary for capitalization and whether these acquisition expenses are currently deductible

**Holding:** Creation or enhancement of a separate and distinct asset is a sufficient ground for capitalization under §263, not an indispensable condition; the character, duration and extent of a transaction’s benefits also matter. The professional expenses facilitating this acquisition’s established enduring corporate restructuring benefits are capital expenditures, not immediately deductible §162(a) operating expenses; mere incidental future benefit alone does not compel capitalization.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; unanimous statutory holding and application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The investment-banking, legal and related expenses facilitated a negotiated acquisition producing established corporate benefits beyond the acquisition year: access to Unilever’s resources, simplified ownership and conversion from independent public corporation to subsidiary. Those benefits do not rest solely on a shareholder’s estate-planning preference or speculative profit estimates.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No automatic capitalization rule for every future benefit, acquisition or professional fee is adopted. Hostile acquisitions, failed transactions, unrelated fees and later cost-recovery periods are not decided; the ordinary taxpayer burden to establish a deduction remains unchanged.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm denial of the disputed immediate deductions and preserve capital classification. No new amortization schedule or additional tax assessment is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Commissioner v. Lincoln Savings & Loan Association*, 403 U.S. 345, is clarified: creating a separate asset is sufficient but not necessary for capitalization. *Welch v. Helvering*, 290 U.S. 111, supplies the contextual distinction between necessary business outlays and current expenses. *Woodward v. Commissioner*, 397 U.S. 572, and *United States v. Hilton Hotels Corp.*, 397 U.S. 580, connect professional costs to the capital acquisition they facilitate. *Commissioner v. Idaho Power Co.*, 418 U.S. 1, preserves capitalization despite an otherwise available expense category. *Interstate Transit Lines v. Commissioner*, 319 U.S. 590, leaves the taxpayer’s deduction burden unchanged.

### Income exclusions

#### United States v. Burke, 504 U.S. 229 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-42.
**Decided:** May 26, 1992.

##### Whether the identified pre-1991 Title VII settlement payments are excluded from gross income under §104(a)(2)

**Authority of the judgment:** The Sixth Circuit’s exclusion ruling is reversed, 6–3: White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter support reversal; Stone, O’Connor and Thomas oppose it. The identified payments may not be excluded in the refund action. No controlling statutory rationale exists under *Marks*. Blackmun, White, Stevens and Kennedy rely on the old Title VII remedial scheme; Scalia separately construes the statute around physical or mental health; Souter accepts the regulation, recognizes competing tort and contract analogies, rejects making intangible remedies indispensable, and relies on narrow construction of uncertain exclusions. Neither concurrence is established as a logical subset of the plurality’s remedial classification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The judgment creates no general remedial-range test, health-injury requirement or discrimination-recovery exclusion. No separate FICA wages issue, punitive-damages classification, ordinary wage settlement, distinct tort allocation, amended statutory regime or Title VII jury-trial question is decided. The settlement establishes no adjudicated discrimination finding. No independently sufficient alternative holding has five votes. Further proceedings must give effect to reversal, without enlarging the recovery or valuation; no tax amount, separate withholding theory or reopening of the settlement is ordered.

### Intergovernmental tax immunity

#### Barker v. Kansas, 503 U.S. 594 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-611.
**Decided:** April 21, 1992.

##### Whether Kansas may tax military retirement while exempting comparable state and local retirement benefits

**Holding:** For 4 U.S.C. §111, the military retirement benefits at issue are deferred compensation for past service, comparable to exempt Kansas state and local retirement benefits. Continued military status, restrictions and possible recall do not justify taxing the entire military retirement stream while exempting those comparable benefits; the asserted difference must directly relate to and justify the unequal tax treatment.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; nine votes control. Stevens and Thomas also concur separately without limiting their joins.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Rank and completed years of service principally determine these payments. Continued military status, discipline, employment restrictions and possible recall do not explain taxing the entire pension stream while exempting comparable state pensions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Compensation specifically tied to new service, other military-status consequences, materially different tax classifications and preserved retroactivity or refund issues remain undecided. Other constitutional and state-law claims supply no independent holding; McKesson and Beam are not modified.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Kansas Supreme Court judgment is reversed and remanded for compliance with §111 and consideration of preserved retrospective-relief questions under then-governing law. No refund amount is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Davis v. Michigan Department of Treasury, 489 U.S. 803 — applied: retirement income receives §111 protection and a tax-relevant significant difference must justify unequal treatment.
- Phillips Chemical Co. v. Dumas Independent School District, 361 U.S. 376 — applied through Davis: the difference must directly relate to and justify the disparity.
- United States v. Tyler, 105 U.S. 244 — confined to its statutory pay-increase context; continuing military status does not establish current compensation for all purposes.
- McCarty v. McCarty, 453 U.S. 210 — distinguished: it reserved the characterization question and its community-property prohibition was displaced by the Uniformed Services Former Spouses’ Protection Act; no surviving rule authorizes this tax disparity.

### National Firearms Act making tax

#### United States v. Thompson/Center Arms Co., 504 U.S. 505 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-164.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

##### Whether the genuine unassembled dual-use Contender package incurs the short-barreled-rifle making tax

**Holding:** The National Firearms Act does not clearly treat the segregated Contender pistol and conversion package, which has genuine lawful pistol and long-rifle uses and has not been assembled as a short-barreled rifle, as making that regulated firearm. Because the same definition carries criminal consequences, the remaining ambiguity is resolved for the manufacturer in this civil refund action as well.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's plurality opinion joined by O'Connor, Scalia's concurrence joined by Thomas, and Stone's concurrence expressly share this case-specific construction and uniform residual lenity. These five form an actual majority at this level of generality; no complete opinion controls and no synthetic *Marks* rationale combines their different accounts of the ambiguity.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the identified genuine dual-use package and the shared residual-lenity proposition.

**Material application:** Section 5845's rifle definition addresses design, making and intended shoulder firing, while other firearm definitions expressly address combinations of parts. The language and related definitions leave ambiguity for this package's lawful pistol and long-rifle configurations alongside its regulated short-rifle capability.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No complete opinion controls the broader division between permissible and regulated combinations. Actual short-rifle assembly, pure regulated-firearm kits, sham lawful configurations, miscellaneous or incomplete parts, unrelated firearm categories and separate criminal offenses remain unadjudicated. No general intent-to-assemble element, new criminal proof burden, storage protocol or exemption for all parts kits is created. Souter's broader utility taxonomy, Scalia and Thomas's separate shoulder-firing account and Stevens's civil-notice exception remain noncontrolling.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Federal Circuit's refund judgment, 5–4, with Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas supporting affirmance and White, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy opposing. The $200 making tax is not owed on the segregated unassembled unit; no broader injunction or new remand command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Crandon v. United States*, 494 U.S. 152, supplies one construction of criminally consequential language in civil litigation without changing criminal proof. *United States v. R. L. C.*, March 24, 1992, preserves its adult-Guidelines ceiling without establishing a shared new lenity method or firearms rule. *Marks v. United States*, 430 U.S. 188, supplies no synthesized broader rationale where the exact case-specific proposition already has five express supporters.

##### Whether making a firearm invariably requires final mechanical assembly

**Holding:** The National Firearms Act's references to manufacturing and otherwise producing a firearm are not confined to the final mechanical act of putting its parts together. The Federal Circuit's universal actual-assembly prerequisite is rejected without deciding tax liability for an unpresented pure-parts or sham arrangement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter and O'Connor expressly adopt the construction; White's dissent, joined by Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy, adopts the same predicate; and Stone's concurrence expressly rejects a universal final-assembly requirement while reserving pure-parts and sham liability. These seven directly support the exact proposition across writings. Scalia and Thomas disagree. This is an actual majority proposition, not a *Marks* synthesis or a join in the plurality's broader taxonomy.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated, without a categorical rule for the reserved arrangements.

**Material application:** Section 5845(i) separately identifies manufacturing, otherwise producing and putting together. Requiring final assembly for every form of making would deprive the broader production language of work. Production must still meet the enacted definition; regulatory purpose alone does not impose a tax.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No holding classifies every miscellaneous collection, incomplete kit or genuine alternative-use set as a firearm. Liability for pure-parts kits, sham configurations, actual short-rifle assembly and separate offenses remains outside the decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The narrow refund judgment remains affirmed; the Federal Circuit's universal actual-assembly rationale does not survive as the governing construction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *United States v. Drasen*, 845 F.2d 731, supports the manufacturing construction without adjudicating its kits or adopting its broader notice and criminal-responsibility discussion. *Thompson/Center Arms Co. v. United States*, 924 F.2d 1041, is affirmed in judgment but its universal actual-assembly rationale is rejected.

### Assessment limitations for pass-through income

#### Bufferd v. Commissioner of Internal Revenue, 506 U.S. 523 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7804.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### Which return starts limitations on the shareholder’s individual assessment

**Holding:** For an assessment of an S corporation shareholder’s individual income tax, the return that starts the ordinary § 6501(a) limitations period is the shareholder’s return, including applicable extensions and statutory exceptions. Expiration of the period associated with the corporation’s § 6037 information return does not bar correcting pass-through items in a timely shareholder assessment; the corporate return remains operative for corporate-level taxes to which it applies.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion for the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas. All nine Justices adopt this rule and its explanation; it controls by a direct majority.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Automobile Club of Michigan v. Commissioner, 353 U.S. 180, 188 (1957), applied by analogy: a filing insufficient to compute the relevant taxpayer’s liability does not trigger that taxpayer’s assessment period; the holding is not a rule that informational completeness alone transforms one person’s return into another’s.
- Germantown Trust Co. v. Commissioner, 309 U.S. 304 (1940), distinguished in scope: a sufficient return for the same taxpayer may trigger limitations despite its form; it supplies no corporate-return substitute for a shareholder’s own return.
- Holywell Corp. v. Smith, February 25, 1992, left undisturbed: distinct fiduciary and trust filing duties do not decide the limitations question here.
No Supreme Court precedent is overruled. The contrary corporate-period rule applied in Kelley v. Commissioner, 877 F.2d 756 (9th Cir. 1989), cannot govern this shareholder-assessment question. No broader C corporation rule is decided.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ordinary three-year period remains subject to the shareholder’s applicable extensions and statutory exceptions. The corporate return retains its corporate-tax function. Special audit regimes, the unpressed wording objection to the individual extension and any general C corporation rule are not decided; later unified-audit rules do not govern the 1979 liability.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Second Circuit: the timely shareholder assessment is not barred by expiration of the corporate-return period.

### Depreciation of acquired intangible assets

#### Newark Morning Ledger Co. v. United States, 507 U.S. 546 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1135.

**Decided:** April 20, 1993.

##### Proof controls depreciation of the acquired customer intangible

**Holding:** Under section 167 and Regulation §1.167(a)-3, a taxpayer that proves an acquired intangible’s ascertainable value and limited useful life estimable with reasonable accuracy may depreciate it despite its resemblance to expected continued patronage. The taxpayer bears the substantial proof burden; unsupported assertions, indefinite or self-regenerating value and double allocation do not qualify, and goodwill remains nondepreciable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Massey Motors, 364 U.S. 92 — applied: depreciation matches capital cost with useful contribution.
- Houston Chronicle, 481 F.2d 1240; Miller, 537 F.2d 446; Donrey, 809 F.2d 534 — persuasive proof-based reasoning adopted, not a categorical customer-list exemption.
- INDOPCO, February 26, 1992 — distinguished: capitalization and later cost recovery are separate questions.
- Red Wing Malting, 15 F.2d 626 — preserved for nonwasting goodwill; not extended to defeat proof of a distinct wasting asset.

##### The established subscriber findings satisfy the rule

**Holding:** The finite acquired subscriber cohort here satisfies the separate-value and reasonably measurable limited-life requirements on the existing trial findings. The useful-life and straight-line stipulation becomes operative only after those prerequisites are met; reversing the categorical goodwill rule neither supplies an unconditional concession nor requires proof of the entire case anew.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, II; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Houston Chronicle, 481 F.2d 1240 — applied through its evidentiary requirements; actual findings matter.
- Golden State Towel, 373 F.2d 938 — distinguished: the acquired cohort is found not to be a self-regenerating mass.
- Massey Motors, 364 U.S. 92 — applied to the proved finite-life asset; no universal valuation method adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The finite purchased subscriber cohort excludes later automatic replacement customers; indefinite or self-regenerating goodwill remains nondepreciable. The useful-life and straight-line stipulation is conditional on the proved prerequisites. No universal valuation method, customer-list exemption or duplicate basis allocation is authorized.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical goodwill judgment and remand consistently with the existing separate-value and finite-life findings and conditional stipulation. No later amortization regime or new tax amount is imposed.

### Home-office deductions

#### Commissioner v. Soliman, 506 U.S. 168 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-998.

**Decided:** January 12, 1993.

##### Comparative principal-place-of-business inquiry under former §280A(c)(1)(A)

**Holding:** Under then-effective §280A(c)(1)(A), identifying the principal place of business requires comparing the relative importance of activities performed at each business location and the time spent there; necessary administration or the lack of another office alone does not make a home office principal. Customer contact or service delivery receives great but nonconclusive weight, special facilities may add weight, time is particularly useful when the functions do not settle the comparison, and a home office does not qualify by default when no place emerges as principal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O’Connor and Souter: six votes for this formulation and its application. Thomas and Scalia agree only with the judgment on their separately stated approach; Stevens dissents. The Court opinion controls without a fractured-decision inquiry.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Malat v. Riddell, 383 U.S. 569 (1966), and Crane v. Commissioner, 331 U.S. 1 (1947): applied for ordinary meaning in revenue statutes; neither is displaced.
- Welch v. Helvering, 290 U.S. 111 (1933): retained for the fact-sensitive character of deduction questions; it does not prevent articulating consistent statutory considerations.
- The Fourth Circuit’s necessity/substantial-time/no-other-office test is rejected because it fails to compare locations; no earlier Supreme Court principal-place test is overruled.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Exclusive and regular business use remain prerequisites. Patient or client visits are not indispensable under subsection (A); subsection (B)’s normal-course meetings and subsection (C)’s unattached structures remain separate unadjudicated alternatives. The employer-convenience condition applies to employee use, not this self-employed taxpayer. No fixed arithmetic weighting or default home-office qualification is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit and disallow the 1983 principal-place home-office deduction on the hospital-service and time facts. Other deductions, including unappealed travel determinations, are untouched.

### Minimum-tax depletion basis

#### United States v. Hill, 506 U.S. 546 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1421.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### Mineral-interest basis under former §57(a)(8)

**Holding:** For tax years governed by former § 57(a)(8), the adjusted basis offset against percentage depletion is the adjusted basis of the taxpayer’s § 614 interest in the mineral deposit, determined without that year’s depletion deduction; it excludes separately depreciable tangible drilling and development improvements identified in Treasury Regulation § 1.612-4(c)(1). Lawfully capitalized intangible costs recoverable through depletion remain in depletable basis; this classification does not erase distinct statutory provisions governing other costs or later taxable years.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s opinion for the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas. All nine Justices adopt this rule and its explanation; it controls by a direct majority.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for liabilities governed by former §57(a)(8), including the 1981–1982 years adjudicated. The Energy Policy Act of 1992, Pub. L. 102-486 §1915(a), (d), enacted October 24, 1992, removes §613A(c) depletion deductions from amended §57(a)(1) for taxable years beginning after December 31, 1992. Its coordinated §56(g)(4)(F)(ii) exception likewise removes those deductions from clause (i) and subparagraph (C)(i) for those taxable years; clause (i)’s general cost-depletion rule retains its separate condition for property placed in service in a taxable year beginning after December 31, 1989. The holding does not supply a contrary rule for the amended years.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Energy Policy Act of 1992, Pub. L. 102-486 §1915(a), (d), 106 Stat. 3023–3024; enacted October 24, 1992; applicable to taxable years beginning after December 31, 1992; prospective depletion-preference and adjusted-current-earnings exceptions. This is noncase-law scope information, not a judicial holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- INDOPCO, Inc. v. Commissioner, February 26, 1992, left undisturbed and distinguished by issue: capitalization can be required without creating a separate asset; Hill concerns allocation among already distinct depreciation and depletion accounts. No restrictive separate-asset prerequisite is adopted.
The controlling classification follows the coordinated statutory and regulatory text. No Supreme Court precedent is overruled or made into a general rule against multiple tax benefits. Technical Advice Memorandum 8314011 supplies no precedent under then-§ 6110(j)(3); its § 616 deferred-development setting also has an express basis direction different from the costs here. The memorandum is not a judicial holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The former-law classification does not combine depreciation and depletion accounts because assets are commercially integrated. Properly capitalized depletable intangible costs remain in mineral basis; no general ban on multiple tax benefits is adopted. Technical Advice Memorandum 8314011 is nonprecedential and its §616 provision has a distinct express basis direction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Federal Circuit and remand for implementation of the mineral-interest basis calculation for the 1981–1982 liabilities. The prospective 1992 amendment does not govern those years; no new factual hearing is directed.

### Priority of federal tax liens

#### United States v. McDermott, 507 U.S. 447 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1229.

**Decided:** March 24, 1993.

##### Federal notice precedes choateness in after-acquired property

**Holding:** A filed federal tax lien takes priority over a previously docketed judgment lien in land acquired only after federal notice, because the judgment lien becomes choate in that land only upon acquisition. Both liens attach when the debtor acquires the property; priority here follows the federal notice rule, not fictional earlier attachment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- New Britain, 347 U.S. 81 — applied: choateness requires established lienor, amount and property.
- Glass City Bank, 326 U.S. 265 — applied: federal liens reach after-acquired property upon acquisition.
- Vermont, 377 U.S. 351 — distinguished: no separate levy is required for a lien already fixed in existing property; future acquisition remains different.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The priority concerns the September 23 land and substituted proceeds; statutory protections for other creditors and financing interests remain. An asserted earlier contract interest was not preserved within this dispute and is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Tenth Circuit and implement federal-lien priority in the escrowed proceeds; no unrelated lien or property is adjudicated.

### Pension property transfers and prohibited exchanges

#### Commissioner v. Keystone Consolidated Industries, Inc., 508 U.S. 152 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1677.
**Decided:** May 24, 1993.

##### The established tax meaning includes property exchanged for release of a funding duty

**Holding:** In §4975(c)(1)(A), the established tax meaning of sale or exchange includes an employer’s transfer of property in satisfaction of an enforceable pension-funding obligation. An unencumbered transfer meeting that condition is not excluded merely because it is called a contribution.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Part III-A; White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Helvering v. Hammel, 311 U.S.504: settled tax treatment of obligation-discharging transfers applied.
- Sorenson v. Secretary of Treasury, 475 U.S.851: consistent tax-language construction applied.
- Atlantic Cleaners & Dyers, Inc. v. United States, 286 U.S.427: contextual qualification preserved; it does not justify a different meaning here.
- Commissioner v. Lester, 366 U.S.299: internal consistency of tax language supports the established-meaning rationale.
- Albernaz v. United States, 450 U.S.333: prior settled construction informs the meaning Congress selected.

##### Discharge supplies consideration even without importing a technical tax meaning

**Independent alternative holding:** Independently of a settled technical tax meaning, transferring property to discharge an employer’s pension-funding duty is an exchange under §4975(c)(1)(A): the plan receives property and the employer receives release from its obligation. Section4975(f)(3)’s inclusion of property subject to a mortgage or similar lien assumed by the plan or placed by a disqualified person within ten years does not exclude other exchanges supported by consideration.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Blackmun’s Parts III-C and III-D; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The settled-tax-meaning and ordinary-exchange grounds are independently sufficient. Gratuitous contributions, unpresented exemptions, correction conditions, valuation and computation remain distinct; no agency-deference holding is necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand under the correct transfer classification and applicable tax conditions; unencumbered status alone supplies no exclusion. No automatic second-tier assessment or final tax computation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- This independently sufficient ground rests directly on §4975(c)(1)(A) and (f)(3); it requires no additional treatment of an earlier decision.
