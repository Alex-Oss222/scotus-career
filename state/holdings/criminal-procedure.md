## Criminal Procedure

### Appellate finality

#### United States v. Ibarra, 502 U.S. 1 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1713.  
**Decided:** October 15, 1991.

##### Whether a timely reconsideration motion renewing an abandoned suppression ground postpones appellate finality

**Holding:** A single timely motion asking a district court to alter a suppression ruling renders that ruling nonfinal for appeal purposes until the motion is decided, even when the motion renews a ground the Government earlier abandoned. The full appeal period begins upon disposition of the motion; the rule does not decide whether the renewed ground remains waived on the merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Per curiam opinion joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; unanimous eight-Justice participating Court.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The Government's January 30 notice of appeal was filed within thirty days after the January 3 denial of reconsideration.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Bad-faith and successive motions, the substantive effect of abandonment, and the Fourth Amendment validity of the search are unresolved. Existing appeal periods and preservation rules are unchanged.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit's dismissal of the appeal is vacated, and the case is remanded for further appellate proceedings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** United States v. Healy supplies suspension of finality on a timely criminal rehearing request. United States v. Dieter forecloses an exception based on the kind or strength of the asserted error. Department of Banking v. Pink is distinguished because its filing sought no substantive alteration of adjudicated rights.

### Capital sentencing

#### Sochor v. Florida, 504 U.S. 527 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-5843.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

For the preservation bar on the jury-heinousness instruction claim in *Sochor v. Florida*, June 8, 1992, No. 91-5843, see Federal Courts — Adequate and independent state grounds.

##### Whether the judge's conscious-victim strangulation application supplies a sufficiently narrowed heinousness aggravator

**Holding:** A sentencing judge may apply an otherwise vague heinousness aggravator through an authoritative and sufficiently specific state narrowing construction. Florida's consistently recognized conscious-victim strangulation construction supplies adequate guidance here; inconsistent applications in other factual settings do not establish that this judge lacked guidance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-B of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; seven Justices. Blackmun and Stevens do not join this disposition because they would require correction of the separate jury defect.

**Current force:** Remains controlling at the stated judicial application and record.

**Material application:** Florida's identified narrowing decisions address the fear and conscious suffering of strangulation. The victim's resistance, fear and strangulation support that concrete construction; the judge did not merely repeat the statutory adjectives.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court approves neither every Florida heinousness decision nor the bare instruction given to this jury, and it does not decide a different victim's condition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reject the preserved constitutional challenge to the judge's narrowed heinousness application; that factor supplies no basis for relief.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Godfrey v. Georgia*, 446 U.S. 420, and *Maynard v. Cartwright*, 486 U.S. 356, require meaningful narrowing. *Walton v. Arizona*, 497 U.S. 639, permits reliance on authoritative state constructions and presumes the judge's familiarity with them. *Proffitt v. Florida*, 428 U.S. 242, preserves a narrowed heinousness category without approving every later application.

##### Whether submission of a lawful but unsupported coldness aggravator establishes that a general advisory jury recommendation relied on it

**Holding:** Submitting a legally available aggravating circumstance without evidentiary support does not itself establish that a Florida advisory jury relied on it in a general death recommendation that identifies no aggravator findings. The asserted inference of jury-coldness error is rejected without a finding that the jury actually rejected the factor or resolution of a legally invalid instruction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Souter's opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices. Stone reserves this independent question, Blackmun and Stevens would include the submission in correction, and Scalia does not join this holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling at this narrow factual-insufficiency inference and general advisory recommendation.

**Material application:** Cold, calculated and premeditated murder was a lawful aggravator, but proof of its heightened premeditation requirement failed. The jury identified no aggravator findings, so submission alone cannot establish its reliance. The judge's express coldness finding establishes a separate error.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding makes no factual jury finding, does not assimilate every capital recommendation to a guilt verdict, does not decide that all instructional errors are harmless, and leaves broader capital-jury doctrine open. Legally invalid alternatives remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Griffin v. United States*, December 3, 1991, is extended narrowly through its factual-versus-legal distinction to this asserted inference from a general advisory recommendation. *Stromberg v. California*, 283 U.S. 359, and *Yates v. United States*, 354 U.S. 298, retain their rules for legally invalid alternatives. *Estelle v. McGuire*, December 4, 1991, retains whole-charge reasonable-likelihood review, which neither removes the preservation bar nor supplies an automatic inference of factual reliance here.

##### Whether the judge's express weighing of the evidentially unsupported coldness aggravator creates constitutional error

**Holding:** When a Florida capital sentencing judge expressly weighs an aggravator that the state supreme court holds unsupported by the evidence, that invalid factor creates Eighth Amendment error in the weighing decision. Lawful reweighing, constitutional harmless-error review or resentencing is required; other valid aggravators do not themselves cure the error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-B-1 of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; eight Justices. Scalia rejects the constitutional weighing-error premise.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for documented reliance on an unsupported aggravator.

**Material application:** The judge expressly found and weighed heightened coldness, which Florida held unproved. Proof of ordinary murder premeditation does not establish this additional aggravating requirement. Surviving death eligibility therefore does not eliminate the actual weighing error.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The convictions and mitigation findings are not disturbed. Removal of an aggravator does not itself require a life sentence or a new penalty jury. The no-mitigation finding does not mean no mitigating evidence was presented.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence requires a supported determination under *Chapman*, lawful reweighing if Florida law authorizes it, or resentencing. The separate holding below identifies why the existing appellate treatment fails to provide that correction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Clemons v. Mississippi*, 494 U.S. 738, requires adequate correction of capital weighing error. *Parker v. Dugger*, 498 U.S. 308, applies that principle to unsupported Florida aggravators and the actual sentencing record. *Stringer v. Black*, March 9, 1992, supplies the correction principle for this documented reliance; its habeas retroactivity and default reservations do not bar direct review. *Zant v. Stephens*, 462 U.S. 862, is distinguished because its nonweighing setting does not make surviving eligibility an automatic cure. *Chapman v. California*, 386 U.S. 18, supplies constitutional harmlessness with the State bearing the burden.

##### Whether Florida's proportionality discussion and surviving-factor count constitutionally cure the weighing error

**Holding:** A state appellate court affirming this sentence must substantively establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the invalid aggravator did not contribute to the sentence, or provide another authorized constitutional cure. Florida's existing proportionality discussion, count of surviving factors and mixed citations do not establish that inquiry; no verbal formula is either required or sufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts III-B-2 and IV of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Kennedy; six Justices. White and Thomas accept the error framework but find the existing review sufficient; Scalia would affirm on a separate ground.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Proportionality compares sentences across cases; it does not itself determine whether this invalid factor influenced this sentencing balance. Three valid factors and no found mitigation may bear on harmlessness but do not establish the missing inquiry. The State bears the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither undertakes reweighing nor determines the outcome of lawful correction. It requires no prescribed phrase and rejects a bare harmless-error label as sufficient. No automatic life sentence, mandatory new penalty jury, new mitigation finding or new habeas standard is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the judgment sustaining the death sentence and remand to Florida for supported *Chapman* review, lawful reweighing if Florida law permits, or resentencing. The convictions remain intact; the state courts must choose and perform an adequate lawful correction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Chapman v. California*, 386 U.S. 18, retains the State's burden to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute. *Clemons v. Mississippi*, 494 U.S. 738, and *Parker v. Dugger*, 498 U.S. 308, require corrective analysis of the sentence and actual record rather than proportionality alone. *Stringer v. Black*, March 9, 1992, preserves the inadequacy of factor counting and proportionality as standalone cures.

#### Morgan v. Illinois, 504 U.S. 719 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-5118.
**Decided:** June 15, 1992.

##### Whether an automatic-death juror is disqualified from a capital sentencing jury

**Holding:** When a State provides a jury to decide a capital sentence, due process requires an impartial jury. A juror who would automatically impose death upon conviction regardless of the facts and required consideration of mitigation is subject to challenge for cause; if such a juror serves and death is imposed, that sentence cannot be executed.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices adopt this proposition. Stone concurs in the judgment without a formal opinion join; Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Illinois requires consideration of aggravation and mitigation. A juror who fixes death in advance regardless of the evidence refuses that task; the conclusion a qualified juror reaches after genuine consideration is different.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State need not provide jury sentencing, but a jury it supplies must be impartial. General support for capital punishment is not disqualifying, and the Court prescribes no weight for mitigation or required punishment. Witt's substantial-impairment standard remains intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The death-sentence affirmance is reversed and remanded, 7–2. The conviction is undisturbed; no automatic life sentence or constitutional right to jury sentencing is imposed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wainwright v. Witt, 469 U.S. 412 (1985), and Adams v. Texas, 448 U.S. 38 (1980): applied to views disabling lawful performance; their governing substantial-impairment rule is not replaced.
- Witherspoon v. Illinois, 391 U.S. 510 (1968): its impartiality principle applies to the opposite categorical disability; ordinary death-penalty views remain permissible.
- Ross v. Oklahoma, 487 U.S. 81 (1988): its automatic-death-juror principle is expressly adopted; its result rested on removal of the identified juror.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 (1978), and the received mitigation line: retained as requiring a real opportunity for individualized consideration, not a prescribed result.

##### Whether requested inquiry must expose automatic-death views despite general assurances of fairness and obedience

**Holding:** On a capital defendant’s request, voir dire must adequately inquire into whether prospective sentencing jurors would automatically impose death regardless of lawful consideration of the facts and mitigation. General promises of fairness and obedience to instructions did not cover that subject here; the denied inquiry requires reversal of the death-sentence affirmance and remand, without disturbing the conviction.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices adopt this proposition. Stone concurs in the judgment without a formal opinion join; Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Generic assurances can conceal a prospective juror's belief that automatic death is fair. One juror's volunteered disqualifying answer and removal do not show adequate subject coverage for the others. The defendant need not prove answers to an inquiry he was forbidden to make.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No actual bias is found in a seated juror. Trial judges retain control over wording and follow-up, and no fixed questionnaire or new proof quantum is required. Unrelated voir dire omissions, collateral review and retroactivity remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The death sentence is set aside for lawful further sentencing proceedings without this defect. If Illinois uses a future capital jury, selection must comply with the inquiry rule; the Court does not require jury sentencing, order a guilt retrial or impose life imprisonment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wainwright v. Witt, 469 U.S. 412 (1985): its requirement of demonstrating bias through questioning supports the necessary opportunity to inquire.
- Mu’Min v. Virginia, 500 U.S. 415 (1991): distinguished; discretion over the detail of an inquiry does not authorize complete omission of this necessary subject.
- Lockhart v. McCree, 476 U.S. 162 (1986): its recognition of the opportunity to identify disqualified jurors is applied to the defendant’s corresponding challenge.
- Turner v. Murray, 476 U.S. 28 (1986): its protection against unacceptable capital-sentencing risk supports required subject coverage and sentence-only relief.

#### Espinosa v. Florida, 505 U.S. 1079 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7390.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

##### A legally influential recommendation cannot rest on an inadequately narrowed aggravator

**Holding:** When a weighing State requires its sentencing judge to give great weight to a jury's recommendation, an inadequately narrowed aggravating instruction to that jury can infect the final sentence even if the judge directly finds only valid aggravators. The especially wicked, evil, atrocious or cruel instruction given here lacks the guidance the Eighth Amendment requires; the judge's separate findings do not by themselves cure the recommendation's legal influence.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt the defect and corrective requirements. White favors argument and casts no present merits vote; Scalia dissents.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The jury returned an 11–1 general death recommendation under the unelaborated instruction. The judge gave operative weight to four aggravators and found two mitigators. No factor-specific jury finding is inferred; the required legal weight of the recommendation supplies the indirect channel of error.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 (1980), Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 (1988), and Shell v. Mississippi, 498 U.S. 1 (1990): apply their requirement of meaningful guidance for a capital aggravating category; the instruction here is insufficiently narrowed.
- Stringer v. Black, simulated March 9, 1992: applies the distinction between valid eligibility and constitutionally defective weighing; surviving valid aggravators alone do not remove the error.
- Sochor v. Florida, simulated June 8, 1992: applies its weighing-error rule while distinguishing its independent state preservation bar to a different jury claim and its separate factual-invalidity issue.
- Mills v. Maryland, 486 U.S. 367 (1988): applies the refusal to presume that jurors disregard an instruction's constitutionally defective route; no factor-specific verdict is invented.
- Walton v. Arizona, 497 U.S. 639 (1990): preserves genuine judicial sentencing and the presumption that judges apply legal narrowing; distinguishes a system in which a separate unguided recommendation carries required weight.
- Baldwin v. Alabama, 472 U.S. 372 (1985), and Spaziano v. Florida, 468 U.S. 447 (1984): preserve States' latitude in allocating sentencing responsibility; that latitude does not validate influential unguided weighing.
- Tedder v. State, 322 So.2d 908 (Fla. 1975), Smith v. State, 515 So.2d 182 (Fla. 1987), and Grossman v. State, 525 So.2d 833 (Fla. 1988): supply the state-law requirement to give great weight to jury recommendations of life or death.
- Smalley v. State, 546 So.2d 720 (Fla. 1989): rejects its premise that the jury's advisory designation alone makes this vague instruction constitutionally immaterial.

##### Correction must address the recommendation's influence on the death sentence

**Holding:** Reversal of the rejection of this sentencing claim requires further proceedings that remove the influence of the invalid jury recommendation through lawful reweighing or harmless-error review satisfying Chapman beyond a reasonable doubt. If no lawful cure is available, a new sentencing proceeding is required; valid remaining aggravators alone do not establish a cure, and neither an automatic life sentence nor disturbance of the guilt judgment follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt the defect and corrective requirements. White favors argument and casts no present merits vote; Scalia dissents.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No automatic reduction to life, retrial of guilt, adjudication of unrelated aggravators, factor-by-factor jury finding, or collateral-review gateway rule follows. The petition's severance and mitigation/time/funding/proportionality questions are not granted or adjudicated in this limited summary action; no separate denial of them is invented. No prior Supreme Court decision is overruled.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court grants limited review and reverses the rejection of the sentencing claim. Remand requires lawful reweighing or Chapman review addressing the recommendation's influence, and new sentencing if no lawful cure is available. Guilt and the ungranted severance and mitigation/time/funding/proportionality questions remain outside the disposition; no separate denial or automatic life sentence follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 (1990): applies lawful appellate reweighing or harmless-error correction; merely counting valid aggravators does not cure the error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applies harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt; no lesser standard or burden on the defendant is created.
- Stringer v. Black, simulated March 9, 1992, and Sochor v. Florida, simulated June 8, 1992: apply actual cure requirements to the recommendation's transmitted influence; no unreasoned harmlessness label suffices.
- Parker v. Dugger, 498 U.S. 308 (1991): applies review grounded in the real sentencing record and mitigation, not an assumed alternative record.

### Capital sentencing and habeas retroactivity

#### Stringer v. Black, 503 U.S. 222 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6616.
**Decided:** March 9, 1992.

##### Whether the Mississippi vague-aggravator claim seeks a new rule after February 19, 1985 finality

**Holding:** For a sentence final on February 19, 1985, Godfrey already prohibited the unguided heinousness aggravation later addressed in Maynard, and applying that protection to Mississippi’s weighing system does not create a new rule. Teague therefore does not bar consideration of this claim on the ground that Maynard or the corrective principles discussed in Clemons postdated finality.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding is confined to the old-rule application established by Godfrey for this finality date. No Teague exception or unresolved procedural-default defense is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit’s Teague disposition is reversed and remanded for consideration of the preserved procedural-default defense and adequate state correction. If relief otherwise becomes warranted, a conditional writ may permit lawful resentencing or authorized appellate correction. No unconditional release, automatic life sentence, or finding that a particular state cure failed is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288 — applied, not replaced: the claim seeks an old rule at the relevant finality date.
- Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied: both novelty of the later rule and novelty of its application must be considered.
- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 — applied: meaningful guidance was already required for the heinousness aggravator.
- Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 — treated as applying Godfrey’s old rule to equivalent language.
- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 — applied as identifying permissible correction, not a newly created protection in this setting.

##### What correction a constitutionally invalid aggravator requires in a capital weighing system

**Holding:** When a capital sentencer weighs a constitutionally invalid aggravating factor against mitigation, remaining valid aggravators do not by themselves cure the error. The state judicial system must supply genuine reweighing without the invalid factor, where authorized, or constitutional harmless-error review establishing that the invalid factor did not affect the sentence; the adequacy of any cure here remains for further proceedings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Genuine authorized reweighing and constitutional harmlessness are distinct corrective routes. The latter places on the State the burden of proving harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt. Remaining valid aggravators or bare proportionality review alone do not cure the error; death eligibility is not automatically negated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit’s Teague disposition is reversed and remanded for consideration of the preserved procedural-default defense and adequate state correction. If relief otherwise becomes warranted, a conditional writ may permit lawful resentencing or authorized appellate correction. No unconditional release, automatic life sentence, or finding that a particular state cure failed is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 — distinguished: its nonweighing holding does not automatically cure a weighing-system error.
- Barclay v. Florida, 463 U.S. 939 — distinguished: its actual review and state-law invalidity do not justify uncorrected constitutional aggravation.
- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 — applied: genuine appellate reweighing or constitutional harmlessness can correct a weighing error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 — applied to the harmless-error route: the State must establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.
- Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231 — distinguished: duplication in narrowing does not answer vague-factor weighting and cannot supply a prefinality reliance premise.

### Compelled medication and fair trial

#### Riggins v. Nevada, 504 U.S. 127 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-8466.
**Decided:** May 18, 1992.

##### What justification is required to continue antipsychotic medication over a pretrial detainee’s objection

**Holding:** Once a pretrial detainee objects to continued antipsychotic medication, the State must establish medical appropriateness and a need sufficient to override the protected refusal interest, considering reasonable less intrusive means. The unexplained order here supplies neither an essential justification nor findings about alternatives and cannot authorize the compelled treatment through trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this rule and application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Continued medication became involuntary after objection. Medical appropriateness was accepted, but the unexplained order established neither essential safety need nor the insufficiency of less intrusive means; testimony indicated that competence might continue without Mellaril.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No incompetency finding, fixed treatment instruction, categorical medication prohibition, universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula or rule making every medication error structural is adopted. The complete conditions for competency-restoration medication, competency waiver, the Fifth Amendment demeanor theory and an unpreserved Eighth Amendment theory remain undecided. Chapman’s harmless-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt formulation appears only in Stone’s concurrence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence are reversed and vacated, 7–2; the case is remanded for a new trial if the State proceeds. Renewed compelled treatment requires constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Washington v. Harper, 494 U.S. 210 (1990): applied for the protected refusal interest and requirement of overriding justification and medical appropriateness; its convicted-prisoner procedures do not automatically govern trial medication.
- Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970): relied on for the State's legitimate trial interest and the possibility of necessary courtroom measures; no comprehensive competency-medication exception is adopted.

##### Whether the unsupported treatment and unreconstructable trial risk require a new trial

**Holding:** When unjustified compelled antipsychotic medication through trial creates a supported, substantial risk of impairing the defense that the record cannot reliably reconstruct, the defendant need not demonstrate exactly how an unmedicated trial would have differed. The risk here extends beyond visible demeanor to testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel; explanatory expert testimony does not cure it, and the convictions and dependent sentence must be vacated for a new trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this record-specific rule and remedy. Kennedy concurs in the reversal and new-trial judgment on a distinct trial-integrity ground.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The supported risk concerned attention, testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel as well as demeanor. Expert explanation cannot restore an altered conversation or response, and an unmedicated trial cannot reliably be reconstructed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No incompetency finding, fixed treatment instruction, categorical medication prohibition, universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula or rule making every medication error structural is adopted. The complete conditions for competency-restoration medication, competency waiver, the Fifth Amendment demeanor theory and an unpreserved Eighth Amendment theory remain undecided. Chapman’s harmless-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt formulation appears only in Stone’s concurrence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence are reversed and vacated, 7–2; the case is remanded for a new trial if the State proceeds. Renewed compelled treatment requires constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970): applied by analogy to potentially prejudicial compelled trial measures and the need for an essential justification.
- Estelle v. Williams, 425 U.S. 501 (1976): applied by analogy to effects of compelled trial appearance that cannot be fully reconstructed from a transcript; its particular waiver disposition is not extended.

### Competency hearings

#### Medina v. California, 505 U.S. 437 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-8370.
**Decided:** June 22, 1992.

##### Whether California may assign the defendant the preponderance burden at a competency hearing

**Holding:** Due process permits California to require a defendant asserting incompetence to prove it by a preponderance at an adequate competency hearing with counsel and access to psychiatric evidence. This allocation determines the outcome when evidence is evenly balanced; it neither authorizes trial of a defendant established to be incompetent nor excuses a hearing when constitutionally sufficient doubt arises.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy, White, Scalia and Thomas expressly adopt this proposition in the principal opinion; O'Connor and Souter expressly adopt the same California-specific burden and adequacy conclusion in O'Connor's concurrence in the judgment. Six support the exact operative proposition, although no whole opinion has five joins. The methods are not logical subsets yielding a broader Marks rule; the categorical exclusion of Mathews from criminal procedure has only four supporters and does not control.

**Current force:** Remains controlling at the stated California preponderance-burden and adequate-hearing scope; no exclusive criminal due-process method controls.

**Material application:** The full hearing, counsel and psychiatric evidence supply constitutionally adequate protection despite the risk that incapacity may impair the defendant's ability to help prove it. No finding establishes actual incompetence or equipoise in Medina's hearing.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Dusky's competence definition, the duty to inquire, the prohibition on trial while incompetent and the prosecution's burden on offense elements remain unchanged. No higher proof quantum, insanity allocation, harmlessness of an invalid burden, retrospective feasibility or other trial error is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the California judgment; no new competency inquiry, retrospective hearing or trial is required.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pate v. Robinson, 383 U.S. 375, and Drope v. Missouri, 420 U.S. 162, preserve necessary competency inquiry and the bar against incompetent trial without settling this persuasion allocation. Dusky v. United States, 362 U.S. 402, preserves rational consultation and rational and factual understanding as the functional standard. Patterson v. New York, 432 U.S. 197, permits state procedural choices, but its exclusive-framework extension here has only four supporters. Ake v. Oklahoma, 470 U.S. 68, preserves necessary psychiatric assistance; Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319, is not categorically excluded. Leland v. Oregon, 343 U.S. 790, concerns insanity rather than present competence. In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358, preserves proof beyond reasonable doubt of offense elements. Foucha v. Louisiana and Riggins v. Nevada, both May 18, 1992, retain their psychiatric-custody and compelled-medication rules without deciding this burden; Wright v. West, June 19, 1992, supplies no replacement direct-review standard.

##### Whether the associated statutory presumption of competence may remain after sufficient doubt requires a hearing

**Holding:** California may retain its statutory presumption of competence insofar as it means that the party asserting incompetence must prove it by a preponderance. Sufficient doubt requiring inquiry does not itself prove incompetence or invalidate that burden-allocating presumption.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy, White, Scalia and Thomas expressly adopt this interpretation in the principal opinion; O'Connor and Souter expressly adopt the same allocation and affirmance in their concurrence. Six support this narrow rule, without a controlling broader method or inference of additional evidentiary weight.

**Current force:** Remains controlling only as a restatement of the permitted persuasion allocation.

**Material application:** California's Supreme Court construed §1369(f) to allocate persuasion, rather than make the presumption psychiatric evidence. Precautions during the inquiry protect unresolved capacity without establishing its factual answer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision approves no irrebuttable presumption, added evidentiary weight, increased proof burden or authority to proceed after a finding of incompetence. It makes no finding of actual incompetence or equipoise and supplies no harmlessness or retrospective-feasibility ruling.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The California judgment and challenged presumption remain in force at this limited scope; no new hearing or trial is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pate, 383 U.S. 375, is preserved and distinguished as governing the duty to inquire. Dusky, 362 U.S. 402, continues to govern the competence being determined; the presumption does not alter that definition.

### Conditional pleas

#### Doggett v. United States, 505 U.S. 647 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-857.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

For the Sixth Amendment scope and delay holdings in *Doggett v. United States*, June 24, 1992, No. 90-857, see Criminal Procedure — Speedy trial.

##### Whether the express conditional plea preserves the speedy-trial claim and defense-impairment argument

**Holding:** A guilty plea under Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 11(a)(2), with the Government's express consent to reservation of the adverse speedy-trial ruling, preserves the claim and its defense-impairment argument for appellate review. Doggett's admission of guilt does not waive the claimed consequence of unconstitutional delay that his plea expressly reserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy; six Justices adopt this threshold application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the expressly reserved claim and consented conditional plea.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Unreserved objections and the factual admissions establishing guilt are not reopened. No new general exception to plea waiver is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Effective appellate review remains available; success on the preserved Sixth Amendment claim requires the dismissal and vacatur stated under Speedy trial.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rule 11(a)(2) is applied according to the express reservation and prosecutorial consent. United States v. Broce, 488 U.S. 563, is distinguished because an unconditional plea's consequences do not defeat this reservation. Strunk v. United States, 412 U.S. 434, supplies the consequence of success on the reserved claim.

### Confrontation and hearsay

#### White v. Illinois, 502 U.S. 346 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6113.
**Decided:** January 15, 1992.

##### Whether accepted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions require prior production or proof of unavailability

**Holding:** Where the granted review assumes that statements were properly admitted under the firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions, the Confrontation Clause does not require the prosecution first to produce the declarant or establish unavailability. The accepted exception foundations remain necessary; the ruling does not validate a disputed foundation or every police interview.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; nine Justices endorse this rule.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The limited grant accepts the state-law exception foundations for the child's statements as its premise; the Court makes no finding that disputed foundations are satisfied. Their spontaneous and treatment settings supply evidentiary value that later trial testimony cannot recreate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No blanket approval of police questioning, automatic child-necessity rule, harmless-error holding, or ruling on state-law admissibility outside the granted premise is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Illinois Appellate Court judgment is affirmed on the limited Confrontation Clause claim; no new trial or evidentiary hearing is required on the unavailability contention.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Inadi*, 475 U.S. 387 — extended from coconspirator declarations to the two accepted exceptions because later testimony does not replace their contextual value.
- *Ohio v. Roberts*, 448 U.S. 56 — preserved for its received confrontation framework; its former-testimony unavailability analysis is not universalized.
- *Idaho v. Wright*, 497 U.S. 805 — preserved for constitutional reliability outside firmly rooted exceptions; no unsupported judicial assurance substitutes for its requirements.
- *Bourjaily v. United States*, 483 U.S. 171 — firmly rooted exceptions retain their established reliability significance.
- *Coy v. Iowa*, 487 U.S. 1012, and *Maryland v. Craig*, 497 U.S. 836 — distinguished as trial-witness presentation cases; their safeguards remain intact.

##### Whether confrontation is categorically limited to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes

**Holding:** The Confrontation Clause is not categorically confined to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes, so that limiting definition does not supply an alternative basis for admitting these statements. The Court resolves the accepted exceptions under existing confrontation law and leaves any broader reconstruction of that law open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices reject the proposed categorical narrowing. Scalia and Thomas withhold that join.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not hold that every hearsay statement violates the Constitution or decide the treatment of a formal investigative accusation on a disputed record. No new categorical testimonial rule replaces the received framework.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Mattox v. United States*, 156 U.S. 237 — retained as evidence of confrontation's substantive protection and its coexistence with established exceptions.
- *California v. Green*, 399 U.S. 149, and *Dutton v. Evans*, 400 U.S. 74 — preserved; the Court's treatment of out-of-court statements is not displaced by a formal-affidavit-only definition.
- *Inadi* and *Roberts* — applied as exception-sensitive confrontation decisions, not replaced with a new categorical testimonial rule.

### Detention credit

#### United States v. Wilson, 503 U.S. 329 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1745.
**Decided:** March 24, 1992.

##### Who initially computes §3585(b) detention credit and when

**Holding:** Section 3585(b) does not authorize a district court to compute detention credit at sentencing; the Attorney General through BOP must make the initial computation after the federal sentence commences. Administrative review and proper subsequent judicial review remain available, so the calculation is not unreviewable discretion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices adopt this proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A process capable of granting detention credit is distinct from McCarthy’s damages-only claim and compensation-incapable grievance procedure. The decision leaves proper administrative and judicial review available.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit’s direction to the sentencing court to award credit is reversed. Initial computation remains with the Attorney General through BOP after sentence commencement, subject to proper review; no days of credit or new detention order are awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Otte v. United States, 419 U.S. 43 — applied: Congress’s choice of verb tense informs statutory timing.
- Gwaltney of Smithfield v. Chesapeake Bay Foundation, 484 U.S. 49 — applied for the same temporal-text principle.
- Russello v. United States, 464 U.S. 16 — applied as a rebuttable inference, overcome here by the rewritten text and administration scheme.
- Robertson v. Railroad Labor Board, 268 U.S. 619 — applied: a departure from long-established procedure is not lightly inferred.
- McCarthy v. Madigan, March 4, 1992 — distinguished: its damages-only process could not provide the requested relief, unlike a credit computation process.

##### Whether detention already credited to another sentence may also receive federal credit

**Holding:** Section 3585(b) forbids federal detention credit for time already credited against another sentence when the federal sentence begins. A defendant cannot avoid that condition merely because the federal sentencing hearing occurred before the other court awarded its credit.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices adopt this proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Tennessee credited 429 days before the federal term began, although after the federal sentencing hearing. The Court does not fix a numerical federal credit, define every form of official detention, or decide a challenge to a completed BOP calculation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit’s direction to the sentencing court to award credit is reversed. Initial computation remains with the Attorney General through BOP after sentence commencement, subject to proper review; no days of credit or new detention order are awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Turkette, 452 U.S. 576 — applied: avoid an arbitrary statutory result unsupported by the text’s purpose.

### Double jeopardy

#### United States v. Felix, 503 U.S. 378 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1599.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

##### Whether prior evidentiary use places a defendant in jeopardy for distinct uncharged substantive offenses

**Holding:** The use of evidence of an uncharged crime in one trial does not itself place the defendant in jeopardy for that crime. The later Oklahoma substantive charges concern different acts from the Missouri attempt and are not barred merely because evidence of those acts helped prove intent in Missouri.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of White's opinion of the Court, supported by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; all nine Justices adopt the distinct-prosecution rule.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Missouri attempt concerned different acts from the earlier Oklahoma offenses. Ordinary objections to the admissibility of particular evidence remain.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment reversing substantive counts 2–6 and conspiracy count 1 is reversed, restoring the prosecution’s judgments against the double-jeopardy objection, subject to preserved unresolved appellate issues. The separate disposition of travel counts 9–10 is undisturbed; no automatic new trial or evidentiary ruling is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Dowling v. United States, 493 U.S. 342 — applied: evidentiary use does not itself constitute prosecution for the described offense.
- Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S. 508 — applied and confined to essential-conduct analysis; its express rejection of a same-evidence test remains operative.

##### Whether overlapping substantive acts bar prosecution of the distinct conspiracy agreement

**Holding:** A conspiracy and its substantive objects are distinct offenses; prosecution of the substantive crime does not bar prosecution of the agreement merely because the same acts help prove both. The overlapping Missouri conduct therefore does not bar this Oklahoma conspiracy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of White's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt this rationale. Stevens and Blackmun concur only in the conspiracy judgment on a narrower ground.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Grady remains in force; no same-transaction requirement or new evidentiary exemption is adopted. A materially different single-incident successive prosecution remains for its own case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment reversing substantive counts 2–6 and conspiracy count 1 is reversed, restoring the prosecution’s judgments against the double-jeopardy objection, subject to preserved unresolved appellate issues. The separate disposition of travel counts 9–10 is undisturbed; no automatic new trial or evidentiary ruling is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bayer, 331 U.S. 532 — applied: the agreement is distinct from its execution.
- Pinkerton v. United States, 328 U.S. 640 — applied: substantive crime and conspiracy remain separately punishable offenses.
- Iannelli v. United States, 420 U.S. 770 — applied: conspiracy’s independent character survives overlapping proof.
- Garrett v. United States, 471 U.S. 773 — applied by analogy to the treatment of multilayered criminal conduct.
- Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S. 508 — clarified without overruling: it does not abrogate the established conspiracy/substantive-offense distinction.

### Entrapment and evidentiary insufficiency

#### Jacobson v. United States, 503 U.S. 540 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1124.
**Decided:** April 6, 1992.

##### Whether the Government proved predisposition independent of its inducement beyond a reasonable doubt

**Holding:** When the Government induces the charged crime and entrapment is at issue, it must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant’s predisposition existed before and independently of its cultivation; evidence obtained after contact may prove that earlier state of mind. Even giving the prosecution its reasonable favorable inferences, the lawful earlier purchase and responses generated during this twenty-six-month operation do not establish that independent predisposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** White, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, Thomas: six votes control.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Later-collected evidence may establish prior readiness; its collection date and investigative persistence do not alone decide entrapment. No knowledge-of-illegality element, reasonable-suspicion prerequisite, general bar on undercover opportunities, or objective misconduct defense is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Eighth Circuit’s judgment and return the case for entry of acquittal. The prosecution may not retry the charge to supply missing proof of independent predisposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sorrells v. United States, 287 U.S. 435 — applied: the Government may offer opportunities but may not manufacture the disposition and prosecute its product.
- Sherman v. United States, 356 U.S. 369 — applied: the record can establish entrapment as a matter of law despite an eventual willing transaction.
- United States v. Russell, 411 U.S. 423 — preserved: subjective predisposition remains the inquiry; no objective misconduct defense is substituted.
- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307 — applied: reasonable prosecution-favorable inferences remain subject to proof beyond reasonable doubt.
- Mathews v. United States, 485 U.S. 58 — preserved: a routine offered opportunity does not automatically support an entrapment instruction.

##### Whether insufficiency of proof overcoming entrapment permits retrial

**Holding:** Because the prosecution’s proof was legally insufficient to overcome entrapment, the proper disposition is acquittal rather than a new trial. Burks bars a second opportunity to supply the missing evidence of independent predisposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** White, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, Thomas: six votes support the remedial direction.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This disposition rests on insufficiency, not an erroneous instruction or another correctable trial error. A separate outrageous-government-conduct claim and statutory amendments are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Eighth Circuit’s judgment and return the case for entry of acquittal. The prosecution may not retry the charge to supply missing proof of independent predisposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Burks v. United States, 437 U.S. 1 — applied: insufficiency requires acquittal and bars retrial to repair the proof.
- Sherman v. United States, 356 U.S. 369 — applied: matter-of-law entrapment supports termination, not a second prosecution opportunity.

### Evidence and jury instructions on habeas review

#### Estelle v. McGuire, 502 U.S. 62 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1074.  
**Decided:** December 4, 1991.

##### Whether admission of prior nonaccidental-injury evidence violates due process when the earlier abuser is unidentified

**Holding:** On federal habeas review, evidence of a child's prior nonaccidental injuries may be relevant to prove that the charged death was intentional even when the identity of the person who caused the earlier injuries is unknown. Admission for that nonidentity purpose does not itself violate due process, and a defendant's choice not to contest accident does not remove the State's burden to prove intentional killing.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, joined on this proposition by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; all eight participants.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The repeated injuries supported the inference of nonaccidental harm without themselves identifying McGuire as the abuser. The evidence-alone theory could not support habeas relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** State-law admissibility alone is not reviewed. The Court does not decide a general constitutional prohibition of propensity evidence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lewis v. Jeffers and Pulley v. Harris exclude habeas relief for state-law error alone. Lisenba and Dowling preserve the fundamental-unfairness boundary. In re Winship supplies the prosecution's continuing burden to prove every element.

##### Whether the prior-act instruction created a reasonable likelihood that jurors bypassed elemental proof

**Holding:** An ambiguous instruction violates due process only when, read with the charge and trial as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood the jury applied it in a way that relieved the State of proving an element. This instruction did not create that likelihood because it conditioned any inference on the jury's finding that McGuire committed the prior acts and expressly prohibited a bad-character inference.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion joined at this level by Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; five of eight participants. Stone, Stevens, and O'Connor join only the distinct prior-injury-evidence proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The complete charge neither directed a finding that McGuire caused the earlier injuries nor shifted the prosecution's burden on identity or any other element.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not approve every prior-act instruction or decide a general propensity-evidence rule. Because the controlling majority finds no constitutional error, it does not reach Teague, Rose, or Chapman or require harmless-error review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit judgment is reversed. The habeas writ remains denied; no acquittal, retrial, or release is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cupp supplies whole-charge review, and Boyde supplies the reasonable-likelihood standard. Francis and Sandstrom are distinguished and preserved for mandatory presumptions or shifts in elemental persuasion.

#### Wright v. West, 505 U.S. 277 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-542.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

##### Whether the actual larceny trial record satisfies Jackson's constitutional sufficiency requirement

**Holding:** On the trial record here, the quantity and recency of West’s possession of distinctive items from the same theft, considered with the jury’s permissible rejection of his incomplete account, allow a rational trier of fact to find the elements of larceny beyond a reasonable doubt; the exhausted Jackson insufficiency claim therefore warrants no habeas relief. The reviewing court must assess the evidence and permissible credibility choices favorably to the prosecution, without retrying guilt, shifting the State’s burden or treating possession as an invariably sufficient substitute for proof of theft.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas and Scalia in the principal opinion, White in his express endorsement of its sufficiency demonstration, O'Connor with Blackmun and Stevens in their concurrence, Kennedy in his concurrence, and Stone in his separate concurrence expressly adopt this same record-specific Jackson proposition: eight Justices. Souter is excluded because his ground is Teague-only. No complete opinion commands a majority, and no broader rule is synthesized under Marks.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** More than fifteen distinctive items from the same theft were found in West's residence two to four weeks afterward, and the jury could reject gaps in his incomplete flea-market account. Affirmative possession evidence and permissible credibility choices together sustain the inference; disbelief alone supplies no missing element. The offense threshold was $100 and the prior felony was credibility material, not propensity proof of this theft.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new corroboration requirement, proof quantum, burden shift or categorical possession-to-theft inference is adopted. The facial validity of every possession inference, an independent instruction claim, the Elkins new-evidence claim and a general habeas legal-review standard remain undecided. The broader separate writings do not establish a new Teague rule or replace independent review with general acceptance of a reasonable state decision; Teague's existing operation, Miller's legal/fact distinction and the then-existing §2254(d) remain. Cosby's contextual considerations do not become a constitutional five-factor test. A Teague bar and merits denial are not combined under Marks.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's direction to issue a writ on insufficiency grounds is reversed. Remand preserves other properly remaining claims, including the Elkins claim, under their own requirements. No new trial, acquittal, release, expansion of custody or disposition of all habeas claims is ordered.

**Material judgment:** All nine Justices support reversal and remand; eight expressly support the narrow sufficiency holding and Souter supports the judgment on his separate noncontrolling Teague ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307 — applied unchanged to the actual record with the prescribed weight for rational jury inferences.
- In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358 — retained; the prosecution must prove every element beyond a reasonable doubt and no burden shifts to West.
- Barnes v. United States, 412 U.S. 837 — permissive-inference principle retained as background, not an automatic equation of any possession with theft.

### Former testimony

#### United States v. Salerno, 505 U.S. 317 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-872.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

##### Whether Rule 804(b)(1) requires both opportunity and similar motive despite prosecutorial immunity power and asserted fairness

**Holding:** A party offering an unavailable witness's former testimony under Federal Rule of Evidence 804(b)(1) must establish that the opposing party had both an opportunity and a similar motive to develop that testimony; the Government's power to immunize the witness at trial and an appeal to adversarial fairness do not dispense with either requirement. Similar motive must be considered on the actual proceedings and arguments; this decision neither finds that condition satisfied here nor establishes a categorical exclusion of grand-jury testimony.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven of nine participating Justices adopt this rule and explanation. Stone concurs in the judgment on his separately stated compatible rule and qualifications; he is not a formal opinion joiner. Stevens dissents from the remand on actual-motive application, not on an exemption from the Rule.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The court of appeals dispensed with similar motive because the Government could immunize the witnesses at trial and did not fully consider the competing actual-motive accounts. Disclosure of grand-jury material to the defense is distinct from admitting it for its truth.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds neither actual similar motive nor ultimate inadmissibility and adopts no categorical exclusion of grand-jury testimony. Compelled immunity, other hearsay exceptions, independent preserved constitutional grounds, witness truthfulness and guilt remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the dispensation with similar motive and remand its actual satisfaction, admissibility and judgment consequences for Anthony Salerno, Vincent DiNapoli, Louis DiNapoli, Nicholas Auletta, Edward Halloran, Alvin O. Chattin and Aniello Migliore. Preserve independent appellate grounds; Ianniello's separate relief lies outside the writ. No categorical exclusion, unconditional reinstatement of every conviction or immediate retrial is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Green v. Bock Laundry Machine Co., 490 U.S. 504 (1989): applied for enforcing the enacted evidentiary rule rather than altering it for a preferred litigation result; no revision of Green's separate impeachment rule.
- Dennis v. United States, 384 U.S. 855 (1966): distinguished as a disclosure decision under Rule 6(e); its protection of access to relevant grand-jury material remains fully operative without creating automatic trial admissibility.
- United States v. Abel, 469 U.S. 45 (1984): its respect for the enacted evidentiary scheme remains consistent with this application; its distinct bias-impeachment rule is unchanged.

##### Whether offering independent contradictory evidence forfeits the Rule 804(b)(1) objection

**Holding:** When the Government offers independent evidence contradicting unavailable witnesses' grand-jury accounts, but does not present any version of those accounts to the trial jury, that act alone does not forfeit its Rule 804(b)(1) objection. Even assuming a privilege-forfeiture analogy is available, mere contradiction is not disclosure of the protected communication; this decision does not establish that every hearsay objection is a testimonial privilege.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices expressly reject the asserted forfeiture ground at this limited level of generality. This is a distinct controlling answer to the defendants' alternative admissibility theory, not a finding of ultimate inadmissibility.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The Government offered contractors, recordings and documents, without presenting any version of DeMatteis's or Bruno's grand-jury account to the trial jury. The selective-disclosure premise of the asserted United States v. Miller, 600 F.2d 498 (5th Cir. 1979), analogy is absent; that lower-court authority does not supply an adopted Supreme Court privilege rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The consequences of actual selective presentation of former testimony and any general equivalence between hearsay and testimonial privileges remain undecided. Rejection of this alternative admissibility theory does not determine actual similar motive or ultimate admissibility.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Actual motive and the resulting admissibility and judgment consequences remain for the seven-respondent remand; the independent-contradiction act alone supplies no forfeiture ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

The decision does not change White v. Illinois, January 15, 1992, or United States v. Williams, May 4, 1992. Their exception-sensitive confrontation and confined grand-jury presentation rules remain distinct from trial admissibility and do not create automatic admission.

### General verdicts

#### Griffin v. United States, 502 U.S. 46 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6352.  
**Decided:** December 3, 1991.

##### Whether factual insufficiency on one lawful conspiracy object requires reversal of a general verdict supported on another object

**Holding:** A general guilty verdict on a federal multiple-object conspiracy need not be set aside merely because the evidence is inadequate for one legally valid object when another object is supported sufficiently under the unchanged beyond-a-reasonable-doubt standard. This rule does not govern a legally invalid object, failure of proof on every route, an element-diluting instruction, or a properly presented independent unanimity defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion for the Court, joined on the proposition by Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; seven of eight participants. Blackmun's judgment-only concurrence is not counted as a join in this reasoning.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the multiple-object conspiracy verdict. *Sochor v. Florida*, decided June 8, 1992, extends the factual-versus-legal distinction only to rejecting an inference of reliance on a legally available but factually unsupported aggravator from a general Florida advisory death recommendation; it does not settle broader capital-jury error rules.

**Material application:** Both alleged §371 conspiracy objects were legally permissible. The Government conceded insufficient evidence connecting Griffin to the DEA object, but the IRS object had sufficient evidentiary support.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The excluded legal-invalidity, all-routes-insufficiency, elemental-instruction, and independent unanimity questions remain unresolved. Preferred withdrawal of an unsupported route on timely request creates no independent constitutional reversal rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is affirmed; the supported IRS object sustains the conviction against this challenge. No retrial or acquittal is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Turner v. United States is applied to factual insufficiency. Stromberg v. California and Yates v. United States are distinguished and preserved for legally forbidden verdict grounds. In re Winship preserves proof beyond reasonable doubt, and Jackson v. Virginia preserves the rational-factfinder sufficiency inquiry.

**Later-authority backlinks:** *Sochor v. Florida*, 504 U.S. 527 (1992), No. 91-5843, decided June 8, 1992, question concerning inferred jury reliance on the coldness aggravator; see Criminal Procedure, the Sochor entry.

### Grand-jury presentation and supervisory dismissal

#### United States v. Williams, 504 U.S. 36 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1972.
**Decided:** May 4, 1992.

For the other questions in *United States v. Williams*, May 4, 1992, No. 90-1972, see Federal Courts — Preservation and questions passed upon below (reviewability) and Criminal Procedure — Grand-jury presentation and supervisory dismissal (disclosure duty and dismissal authority).

##### What known evidence a federal prosecutor must disclose to avoid materially distorting a grand-jury presentation

**Holding:** Before seeking an indictment, a federal prosecutor must present or otherwise disclose to the grand jury personally known, substantial evidence directly negating the charged offense when its omission would materially distort the grand-jury presentation. This duty requires neither an investigative search for undiscovered favorable evidence nor presentation of every possible defense; a violation alone does not establish entitlement to dismissal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Parts II and III, joined by Blackmun, O’Connor and Thomas, adopt the personally-known/direct-negation duty. Stone’s separate merits opinion adopts that duty where omission materially distorts the presentation. Under Marks, these five judgment supporters establish the stated common requirement; the four-Justice formulation does not remove Stone’s material-distortion qualification from the controlling rule.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The duty concerns substantial evidence personally known to the prosecutor, not an investigative search or a preliminary trial of every defense. Misconduct and charging prejudice are separate inquiries.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The grant concerns authority, not the accounting documents’ specific character. Affirmance does not establish that every intent-related item meets the duty or that the prosecutor fabricated evidence. Negligent discovery, state-grand-jury duties, constitutional disclosure, immunity and fabrication claims remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit’s dismissal without prejudice is affirmed, 5–4: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor and Thomas. A lawful new indictment remains available; no acquittal, dismissal with prejudice or reindictment command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Calandra, 414 U.S. 338 — its protective function and enforceable process limits support the confined duty; ordinary evidentiary freedom remains.
- Bank of Nova Scotia v. United States, 487 U.S. 250 — its misconduct and prejudice inquiries remain separate; violation does not automatically warrant dismissal.
- Costello v. United States, 350 U.S. 359 — preserved for competence and sufficiency challenges to an otherwise lawful presentation; distinguished from the specified prosecutorial omission.
- Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 — applied to the judgment supporters’ shared direct-negation duty with the material-distortion qualification retained.

##### When a materially misleading omission permits supervisory dismissal without prejudice

**Holding:** Federal supervisory authority permits dismissal without prejudice when a prosecutor withholds personally known, substantial evidence directly negating the charged offense, the omission materially distorts the presentation, the evidence would plainly preclude a finding of probable cause, and the violation substantially influenced the indictment or leaves grave doubt that the indictment was free from that influence. This establishes the stated sufficient ground for dismissal; it requires neither a search for undiscovered favorable evidence nor presentation of every potential defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s merits Parts II and III, joined by Blackmun, O’Connor and Thomas, supply the narrower judgment-supporting rule. Stone concurs in affirmance under the broader material-distortion formulation, covering known evidence substantially undermining an essential element with established Bank of Nova Scotia prejudice. Under Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188, the four-Justice rule is the controlling logical subset: every known, directly negating omission that plainly defeats probable cause, materially distorts the presentation and causes the stated prejudice also satisfies Stone’s broader rule. The five judgment supporters together establish its force. Stone is not represented as formally joining the narrower qualification. The four-Justice and broader one-Justice applications are not combined to remove that qualification from the sufficient ground recognized here. Neither the plurality nor the five judgment supporters adopt an exclusive rule forbidding relief in every other circumstance.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The narrower rule adds evidence plainly precluding probable cause to the shared known-evidence, direct-negation and material-distortion requirements, and independently requires Bank of Nova Scotia prejudice. The Government presented a categorical authority objection rather than renewed review of the particular evidence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is a sufficient dismissal class, not an exclusive prohibition on relief outside it. The plurality does not find the accounting documents plainly defeat probable cause; the evidentiary application was outside the grant. Stone’s broader application does not become general law. Guilt, negligent discovery, state grand juries and constitutional disclosure remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit’s dismissal without prejudice is affirmed, 5–4: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor and Thomas. A lawful new indictment remains available; no acquittal, dismissal with prejudice or reindictment command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bank of Nova Scotia v. United States, 487 U.S. 250 — its prejudice requirement is retained; limited supervisory power is extended to the specified known-evidence omission. Its evidence-reliability prohibition survives for lawful presentations.
- Costello v. United States, 350 U.S. 359 — distinguished: the confined misconduct inquiry is not a challenge to the competence or sufficiency of evidence in an otherwise lawful presentation. Its hearsay and ordinary adequacy rules remain.
- United States v. Calandra, 414 U.S. 338 — preserved for grand-jury independence and freedom from trial evidence rules; no exclusionary-rule extension occurs.
- United States v. Mechanik, 475 U.S. 66 — its completed-trial harmlessness holding is not extended to erase a prejudicial violation challenged and resolved before trial; the charging-prejudice formulation adopted in Bank remains operative.
- Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83 — not transplanted into grand-jury proceedings; its trial disclosure duty remains unchanged.
- Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 — applied to the genuine logical subset of the five judgment supporters’ rules; the shared authority supports the sufficient dismissal ground, without creating a single majority merits opinion or an exclusive boundary on other relief.
- United States v. Page, 808 F.2d 723 — the judgment applying it in Williams is sustained against the categorical no-authority challenge; Page’s full disclosure formulation and any rule requiring every fact relevant to a possible defense are not adopted.

### Guidelines departure review

#### Williams v. United States, 503 U.S. 193 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6297.
**Decided:** March 9, 1992.

##### Whether reliance on a forbidden departure factor incorrectly applies the Guidelines

**Holding:** A departure based on a factor that the Commission has forbidden, or has already fully considered in the applicable range, rests on an incorrect application of the Guidelines under §3742(f)(1). A policy statement expressly prohibiting a sentencing action is authoritative guidance in applying the relevant Guideline, and an arrest record itself is not a permissible departure ground here.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices support the proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The policy-statement holding concerns an express prohibition guiding the relevant Guideline, not every policy statement for every purpose. Whether nonsimilar old convictions justify departure is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence-affirmance judgment is vacated and remanded to the Seventh Circuit. It must decide causal harmlessness and independently reasonable extent on the whole record; if either required condition fails, it must remand for resentencing. The convictions remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- No earlier Supreme Court decision is overruled; this is a construction of §§3553(b), 3742(f)(1) and the applicable §4A1.3 prohibition.
- Griffin v. United States, December 3, 1991 — kept distinct: unsupported facts under a valid legal theory do not establish the rule for a prohibited sentencing factor.

##### What showing permits affirmance despite reliance on an invalid departure ground

**Holding:** Once the challenger establishes reliance on an invalid departure factor, remand is required unless the reviewing court determines from the whole record that the district court would have imposed the same sentence without that factor. The challenger need not additionally prove that the error determined the sentence; the party defending the sentence must persuade the reviewing court that the error was harmless.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices support the proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The inquiry asks whether this judge would have imposed the same sentence, not whether that sentence could lawfully have been imposed. Chapman’s constitutional proof standard is not adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence-affirmance judgment is vacated and remanded to the Seventh Circuit. It must decide causal harmlessness and independently reasonable extent on the whole record; if either required condition fails, it must remand for resentencing. The convictions remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 52(a) — applied as the governing harmless-error rule for this nonconstitutional sentencing error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 — not imported: the Court adopts no beyond-reasonable-doubt burden for this statutory error.

##### Whether departure reasonableness is separate from causal harmlessness

**Holding:** Even when an invalid departure ground was harmless under §3742(f)(1), a reviewing court must separately determine under §3742(f)(2) that the departure’s extent is reasonable in light of the valid grounds actually identified by the sentencing court. A reasonable sentence that the district court could have imposed does not establish that the erroneous factor was harmless.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices support the proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Review concerns valid grounds actually identified by the sentencing court; comments used to select a sentence after departure do not become new departure grounds. The lawful use of nonsimilar old convictions and the ultimate reasonable magnitude remain unresolved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence-affirmance judgment is vacated and remanded to the Seventh Circuit. It must decide causal harmlessness and independently reasonable extent on the whole record; if either required condition fails, it must remand for resentencing. The convictions remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Solem v. Helm, 463 U.S. 277 — applied for the primary sentencing role of the trial court, not to add a proportionality issue.

### International abduction and treaty limits on prosecution

#### United States v. Alvarez-Machain, 504 U.S. 655 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-712.
**Decided:** June 15, 1992.

##### Whether the United States–Mexico Extradition Treaty bars prosecution following this officially sponsored, protested abduction

**Holding:** The United States–Mexico Extradition Treaty does not prohibit this abduction or make extradition its exclusive means of obtaining a defendant’s presence. ### Official-right extortion

#### Evans v. United States, 504 U.S. 255 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6105.
**Decided:** May 26, 1992.

##### Whether Hobbs Act official-right extortion requires an independent demand or initiation by the official

**Holding:** Official-right extortion under 18 U.S.C. §1951 does not require that the public official initiate, demand or separately induce the payment when he obtains money to which he is not entitled knowing that it is given in return for official acts. The required interstate-commerce element and proof beyond a reasonable doubt remain.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–II of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, O’Connor and Souter; five Justices adopt this statutory ground. Kennedy and Stone independently support the judgment without joining those parts, and their reasons are not necessary to make this holding controlling.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Knowing receipt of unauthorized payment in return for official acts does not become lawful merely because the payer proposed it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere gratuities, cases lacking the required undertaking, entrapment and collateral evidentiary disputes are not decided or reopened. **Holding:** A charge requiring a public official knowingly to accept money in exchange for a specific requested exercise of official power, while excluding a contribution standing alone even when the donor has pending business, satisfies McCormick’s exchange requirement on this record. The offense is complete upon receipt in return for the official undertaking; actual fulfillment of the promised act is not an additional element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–II of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, O’Connor and Souter; five Justices sustain the instruction and distinguish agreement from later performance.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** The charge excluded a contribution standing alone, even where the donor had pending business, and required knowing acceptance for a specific requested exercise of official power. Receipt for the undertaking completes the crime without completed rezoning assistance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere gratuities, cases lacking the required undertaking, entrapment and collateral evidentiary disputes are not decided or reopened. The tax conviction is undisturbed; the affirming coalition does not reach its conditional challenge, and Scalia and Thomas reject disturbing it on preservation grounds. **Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McCormick v. United States, 500 U.S.257 — applied: legitimate campaign support remains protected absent the required official exchange; completed performance is not required.
- Glasser v. United States, 315 U.S.60 — applied for the favorable-to-verdict treatment of supported factual inferences; statutory construction and charge review remain legal questions.

##### Whether official-right extortion requires a false assertion of lawful entitlement to a fee

**Holding:** Official-right extortion is not confined to obtaining payment by falsely asserting that law entitles the official to collect it. A knowing unauthorized payment in return for official acts can violate the official-right branch without deceiving the payer that the payment is a lawful fee.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter; five Justices decide this construction. O’Connor does not reach it, and Stone does not join or supply an additional ground on it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere gratuities, cases lacking the required undertaking, entrapment and collateral evidentiary disputes are not decided or reopened. ##### Whether Trevino preserved a pre-Batson equal-protection objection to racial strikes

**Holding:** A pre-Batson motion alleging a historical pattern of racial peremptory strikes, renewed after the challenged exclusions and carried forward on appeal with a Fourteenth Amendment reference, preserves the equal-protection objection despite imperfect labels. #### Georgia v. McCollum, 505 U.S. 42 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-372.
**Decided:** June 18, 1992.

For the State's standing to assert excluded jurors' rights in *Georgia v. McCollum*, June 18, 1992, No. 91-372, see Federal Courts — Standing to assert third-party rights.

##### Whether a criminal defendant's purposeful racial peremptory strikes are attributable to the State

**Holding:** A criminal defendant’s invocation of state peremptory authority to obtain judicial exclusion from a governmental jury is state action for that specific act, and purposeful racial exclusion violates the prospective juror’s equal-protection right. This attribution does not make defendants or defense counsel state actors in their ordinary representation, advice or investigation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, joined by White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; five of nine Justices adopt the rule and its limited attribution.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Material application:** Georgia creates the peremptory power, summons and compensates jurors and judicially gives exclusion legal effect. Selection of the governmental criminal jury is the specific public function at issue.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary defense representation, advice and investigation remain private. The decision does not abolish peremptories or constitutionalize every defense act, and it decides no actual strike, criminal charge or nonracial classification.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The pretrial Georgia judgment is reversed and remanded for selection and further proceedings under the constitutional prohibition; no conviction is reversed or particular juror ordered seated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete Co., 500 U.S. 614 — extended from private civil peremptories to the specific criminal-defense selection act.

- Powers v. Ohio, 499 U.S. 400 — applied for the excluded juror’s equal-protection interest against purposeful racial exclusion.
- Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79 — extended to purposeful racial exclusion by the defense during the state selection process.

**Holding:** When the State establishes facts supporting a prima facie inference of purposeful racial defense strikes, the defense must give race-neutral explanations and the judge must determine whether the State has proved purposeful discrimination; the ultimate persuasion burden remains with the State. **Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, joined by White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; five Justices. No general privilege-resolution rule or unconscious-bias test is adopted.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A strike against a minority juror alone does not require explanation. Genuine race-neutral challenges and meaningful inquiry into actual disqualifying bias remain available. A concrete privilege dispute, a general privilege-resolution rule and unconscious-bias safeguards remain undecided; confidential judicial consideration is possible without a categorical exemption.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Jury selection on remand must follow the ordinary Batson sequence; no purposeful discrimination is found in this event.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79 — extended with its purpose requirement, staged production and ultimate persuasion burden unchanged.
- Trevino v. Texas (April 6, 1992) — applied for ordinary Batson administration, without presuming discrimination.
- Ross v. Oklahoma, 487 U.S. 81 — applied to distinguish statutory peremptories from the impartial-jury guarantee.

### Speedy trial

#### Doggett v. United States, 505 U.S. 647 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-857.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

For the conditional-plea holding in *Doggett v. United States*, June 24, 1992, No. 90-857, see Criminal Procedure — Conditional pleas.

##### Whether post-accusation defense impairment is protected when the accused remains free and unaware

**Holding:** Once an indictment or other official accusation triggers the Sixth Amendment, the speedy-trial inquiry must consider defense impairment even though the accused remains free and unaware of the charge. Absence of incarceration or anxiety removes those possible injuries on this record but does not exclude defense impairment from the Barker balance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy; six Justices adopt this scope rule.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for an operative official accusation.

**Material application:** Doggett's indictment remained pending throughout the challenged delay. His freedom and ignorance did not eliminate the independent interest in fair adjudication of that outstanding prosecution.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No limitations period running from the offense or independent right to repose is created. Preaccusation delay, periods after dismissal and independently available due-process protection retain their separate treatment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The protected defense interest enters the balance that establishes the violation and dismissal consequence below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Barker v. Wingo, 407 U.S. 514, supplies fair adjudication as an independent protected interest. Moore v. Arizona, 414 U.S. 25, rejects an indispensable actual-prejudice requirement. United States v. Marion, 404 U.S. 307, is distinguished as to preaccusation delay; United States v. MacDonald, 456 U.S. 1, as to periods following dismissal; and United States v. Loud Hawk, 474 U.S. 302, as to absence of an operative prosecution and justified interlocutory delay. Those decisions do not impose a categorical custody-or-anxiety restriction after accusation.

##### Whether extraordinary negligent delay and unrebutted defense impairment establish a violation requiring dismissal

**Holding:** Under Barker, extraordinary post-indictment delay from sustained governmental negligence can violate the Sixth Amendment without identified trial prejudice when the accused did not knowingly acquiesce and the resulting inference of defense impairment is not persuasively rebutted. The eight-and-one-half-year delay here, including approximately six avoidable years while Doggett lived openly, establishes the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy; six Justices adopt the rule, application and dismissal consequence.

**Current force:** Remains controlling at the stated contextual scope under Barker; no fixed deadline or automatic violation from elapsed time alone is established.

**Material application:** Ordinary investigation could have found Doggett living openly, and accepted findings establish ignorance of the indictment before arrest. Prompt assertion after arrest supplies no knowing acquiescence. The Government's answers to particular alleged losses do not persuasively rebut the accumulated inference from prolonged neglect; the Court does not find the lost-tape or youthful-offender claims proved or attribute every foreign period to American fault.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Delay sufficient to trigger inquiry is distinct from the ultimate balance of duration, reasons, assertion and prejudice. Negligence weighs less than deliberate obstruction but increasingly against the Government as unjustified years accumulate. Diligence, justified foreign obstacles, knowing evasion or materially shorter neglect may yield different results. No universal transfer of the ultimate constitutional burden, preaccusation rule or independent right of repose is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Eleventh Circuit and remand through it for dismissal of the indictment and consequent vacatur of the conditional conviction and sentence. The violation is established; no additional trial-prejudice finding, retrial, sentence-only adjustment or new factual hearing is authorized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Barker, 407 U.S. 514, is applied and specified for extraordinary negligent delay while preserving the distinct threshold and contextual balance. Moore, 414 U.S. 25, defeats a categorical actual-prejudice prerequisite. Loud Hawk, 474 U.S. 302, retains the relevance of justified delay and the insufficiency of possible prejudice alone. Strunk, 412 U.S. 434, requires dismissal and vacatur rather than sentence credit or a new trial.

**Decided:** December 1, 1992.

##### An actual cure is necessary

**Holding:** When a death sentence in a State that weighs aggravation against mitigation rests on an unconstitutionally vague aggravating factor, remaining valid factors do not alone cure the error. The sentence may stand only after an authorized sentencer actually makes a constitutionally valid new sentencing determination, including lawful appellate reweighing, or the State establishes constitutional harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: eight votes. Thomas also writes separately; Scalia dissents.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 (1990), applied: lawful state appellate cure is permitted, automatic factor-count affirmance is not. Stringer v. Black (March 9), Sochor v. Florida (June 8) and Espinosa v. Florida (June 29), all 1992, applied at their controlling levels: actual weighing error requires actual constitutional correction. Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967), preserves the State’s beyond-reasonable-doubt harmlessness obligation.

##### Arizona’s adjudication did not cure this sentence

**Holding:** On the parties’ premise that a majority of the Arizona Supreme Court had to cast valid curative votes, this sentence cannot stand: even assuming the two Justices retaining the narrowed aggravator lawfully reweighed, the two other affirming Justices relied conclusively on the remaining criminal history without a new balance of mitigation. The denial of sentence relief is reversed for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutional correction or vacation of the death sentence and imposition of a lawful lesser sentence.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: eight votes. Thomas also writes separately; Scalia dissents. No rule demanding identical reasoning from every member of a state appellate majority is adopted.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Walton v. Arizona, 497 U.S. 639 (1990), and Lewis v. Jeffers, 497 U.S. 764 (1990), preserved: narrowing constructions and appropriately reviewed state applications may be valid, but do not supply a curative vote absent here. State v. Gretzler, 135 Ariz.42, 659 P.2d1 (1983), remains the state narrowing source; its proper application by Holohan and Hays is not decided. Clemons and the operative weighing-error decisions require the stated remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Arizona is a weighing State. A general duty of independent review is not actual reweighing; no prescribed verbal formula is required, and a federal habeas court cannot perform the missing state sentencing balance. The validity of the Holohan–Hays narrowing and reweighing is assumed, not decided. The accepted state-majority premise establishes no requirement that every curative vote use identical reasoning.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the denial of sentence relief and remand for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutional correction or vacation of death and imposition of a lawful lesser sentence. The conviction is untouched; no automatic life sentence is imposed.

### Collateral challenges to prior convictions

#### Parke v. Raley, 506 U.S. 20 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-719.

**Decided:** December 1, 1992.

##### Initial production burden

**Holding:** In Kentucky’s collateral challenge to a final counseled conviction used for sentence enhancement, due process permits the Commonwealth to prove the judgment and require the defendant to produce evidence of infringement or procedural irregularity. If the defendant refutes regularity, the Commonwealth must affirmatively establish constitutional validity under Kentucky’s procedure; a missing old transcript alone, without alleged official misconduct, does not establish invalidity.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight votes at this level. Blackmun agrees with the result in a separate writing limited to sentencing.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Boykin v. Alabama, 395 U.S. 238 (1969), distinguished as direct review without weakening its valid-waiver requirement. Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458, 468–469 (1938), applied to collateral regularity. Burgett v. Texas, 389 U.S. 109 (1967), preserved for constitutionally defective uncounseled convictions. Medina v. California (June 22, 1992), distinguished: its narrow competence-burden holding does not establish a controlling categorical ban on practical due-process analysis.

##### Measure of proof

**Holding:** Due process does not require the clear-and-convincing extrarecord proof demanded by the Sixth Circuit to establish validity of an old guilty plea in this collateral sentencing proceeding. Kentucky’s retention of the ultimate persuasion burden does not entail that heightened constitutional quantum; the fact of prior conviction and the validity inquiry remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight votes at this level. Blackmun agrees with the result in a separate writing limited to sentencing.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Boykin is not extended into a clear-and-convincing collateral proof requirement. Zerbst supports the distinction between constitutional validity and a universally heightened burden. The Sixth Circuit’s Dunn v. Simmons, 877 F.2d 1275 (1989), cannot govern insofar as it demands that constitutional quantum and shifts the entire production burden solely because no transcript survives.

##### The 1981 plea

**Holding:** Under then-applicable 28 U.S.C. §2254(d), a competent state court’s factual determination, evidenced by written findings, an opinion or other reliable and adequate written indicia after a merits hearing involving the applicant and the State receives a presumption of correctness subject to the conditions and exceptions below; the ultimate federal question whether a plea was knowing and voluntary remains independently determined. On the established earlier warnings, contemporaneous 1981 charge form and jury warning, representation, education, unimpaired condition and testimony here, Raley’s 1981 plea was knowing and voluntary and the Sixth Circuit’s conditional new-hearing remedy is unwarranted. The presumption does not govern if the applicant establishes, it otherwise appears, or the State admits that (1) the state hearing did not resolve the merits of the factual dispute; (2) its factfinding procedure was inadequate to afford a full and fair hearing; (3) material facts were not adequately developed; (4) the state court lacked subject-matter jurisdiction or jurisdiction over the applicant; (5) an indigent applicant was denied appointed counsel in deprivation of his constitutional right; (6) the applicant did not receive a full, fair and adequate hearing; or (7) the applicant was otherwise denied due process in that proceeding. Under paragraph (8), the presumption also does not govern when the pertinent state record is produced and the federal court, considering that record as a whole, concludes that the factual determination lacks fair record support. At a federal evidentiary hearing, once the state factual determination is duly proved, and absent those circumstances, the applicant must establish its error by convincing evidence. That federal factual-review burden is distinct from Kentucky’s allocation and measure of proof of a prior plea’s constitutional validity. Inferences receive deference only when fairly supported by the facts; no presumption attaches to the ultimate federal legal conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight votes at this level. Blackmun agrees with the result in a separate writing limited to sentencing.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Marshall v. Lonberger, 459 U.S. 422, 431–438 (1983), applied to supported historical inferences and independent legal review. Boykin and North Carolina v. Alford, 400 U.S. 25, 31 (1970), applied to an informed voluntary choice. No later habeas deference standard enters.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ultimate federal validity of the plea remains independently determined. No universal collateral proof quantum or categorical prohibition on collateral review is adopted. Kentucky's separate proof of the fact of prior conviction beyond a reasonable doubt and prosecution burdens on the present offense remain. Earlier experience alone does not establish knowing waiver.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit conditional-writ judgment concerning the 1981 plea; the actual evidentiary showing warrants no new hearing. The unchallenged 1979 determination is undisturbed.

### Effective capital mitigation under Texas special issues

#### Graham v. Collins, 506 U.S. 461 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7580.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality

**Holding:** Teague does not bar Graham's claim that Texas's submitted sentencing questions prevented effective use of his youth and unstable background as material mitigation independent of deliberateness or future danger. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated that functional requirement before his conviction became final on September 10, 1984; no new procedural-rule exception is created.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this prefinality-law holding, its application to youth and background, and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288, and Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied; the relevant result must be dictated at finality, not merely supported by a later plausible extension.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — preserved as qualified approval; it does not immunize every application of the special issues.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied as the pre-1984 effective-mitigation rule.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied as recognition of that existing rule, not as law inserted retroactively into 1984.
- Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992 — applied at its existing Teague scope; disagreement alone does not establish novelty.
- Franklin v. Lynaugh, 487 U.S. 164, and Saffle v. Parks, 494 U.S. 484 — distinguished; evidence whose mitigating use fits the questions, and unrestricted sympathy, differ from material culpability mitigation excluded by them.

##### Youth requires an effective route for its independent mitigating force

**Holding:** When the record supports a material mitigating judgment about culpability that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the submitted mechanism prevents that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Graham's youth at seventeen has that independent significance notwithstanding deliberate conduct or predicted future danger, and the three submitted special issues provide no adequate route for it here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground for sentencing relief and its conditional remedy.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied to youth's independent culpability significance; jurors may assign weight but cannot be legally prevented from giving it effect.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — its conditional framework remains; this defective application does not establish facial invalidity.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied beyond a permanent-handicap limitation; a mitigating trait need not have only aggravating relevance to future danger to require an effective vehicle.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 — applied as the reasonable-likelihood method of assessing instructional operation, not as the source of a new substantive rule at 1984 finality.

##### The submitted background evidence also requires effective consideration

**Independent alternative holding:** A capital sentencing system may not prevent a jury from acting on the material culpability significance it finds in a defendant's disturbed upbringing merely because that evidence also bears on future dangerousness or lacks a uniquely severe permanent handicap. The evidence of Graham's mother's recurring mental illness and hospitalizations and his shifting family care supports such a mitigating judgment, which the submitted special issues cannot adequately express; this independently requires the same sentencing remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Parts III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independent background-evidence ground and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 — applied to relevant background proffered as a basis for less than death.
- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied; disturbed upbringing cannot be excluded from mitigating effect as a matter of law.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied; neither a uniquely severe permanent handicap nor complete absence of relevance to the special issues is an exclusive trigger.

##### Positive character as an independent ground

**Authority of the judgment:** The separate contention that Graham's household assistance, religious observance and financial support of his children independently require additional sentencing relief is rejected by all nine Justices, on different grounds. Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter regard its asserted independent culpability significance as insubstantial on this record and its future-behavior use as available under the second issue; White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would bar the requested mitigation relief under Teague. Stone-Zsela joins the denial of this independent component in the judgment only. These grounds do not yield a common general rule or a Marks subset; the youth and background holdings independently require sentencing relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule about positive character evidence or its value apart from the asserted culpability and future-danger uses is decided. The jury remains free to hear constitutionally relevant evidence at the new sentencing proceeding. Neither denial of this independent ground nor the sentencing remedy disturbs the murder conviction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere admission or some future-danger relevance is insufficient when supported culpability mitigation cannot receive effect. The ruling requires neither favorable weight nor a categorical fourth question, finds no psychiatric disability, and imposes no juvenile-execution prohibition or facial invalidity of the scheme. Texas’s later statute is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse sentencing relief denial on youth and background and remand for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate sentencing or a lawful noncapital replacement. Preserve the conviction; no automatic acquittal or immediate release.

### Evidentiary predicates for capital instructions

#### Delo v. Lashley, 507 U.S. 272 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-409.

**Decided:** March 8, 1993.

##### An evidentiary predicate for the requested mitigating circumstance

**Holding:** The Constitution does not require a capital sentencer to receive an instruction on a particular mitigating circumstance when no evidence supports that circumstance; Missouri may require some supporting evidence for its no-significant-prior-criminal-history instruction while leaving the sentencer free to consider and give effect to mitigation actually presented. This production requirement neither requires exhaustive proof of a negative, compels the defendant to testify, authorizes an adverse inference from silence, nor reduces the State's burden to establish aggravating circumstances.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt the rule and reversal of this ground.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586; Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104; Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302: applied to preserve meaningful consideration of supported mitigation; they do not require this unsupported instruction.
- Graham v. Collins,January 25, 1993: preserved in its actual controlling scope; supported youth/background culpability evidence must receive effect, without presuming a distinct mitigating fact.
- Hopper v. Evans, 456 U.S. 605: applied by analogy for an evidentiary predicate, not for an obligation to submit unsupported alternatives.
- Walton v. Arizona, 497 U.S. 639: applied to the permitted allocation of mitigation proof, subject to the State's undiminished aggravation burden; Missouri's lesser some-evidence requirement is all that is adjudicated here.

##### The proposed innocence instruction does not sustain this judgment

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming that a presumption of innocence of other offenses applies at capital sentencing, its existence does not automatically require an instruction; the relevant circumstances must create a genuine danger that jurors will attribute other criminal conduct to the defendant on a basis other than lawful evidence. The silent criminal-history record, undiminished reasonable-doubt instructions on aggravation, and absence of a circumstance creating that danger do not require such an instruction here; the Court does not decide the general existence or scope of that sentencing presumption.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices expressly adopt this alternative response. Stone-Zsela and Souter do not join or reach it.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Taylor v. Kentucky, 436 U.S. 478: applied for the danger of a verdict based on extraneous considerations, while preserving its context-sensitive instruction rule.
- Kentucky v. Whorton, 441 U.S. 786: applied for the totality-of-circumstances inquiry and rejection of an automatic instruction requirement.
- Herrera v. Collins,January 25, 1993: not extended; its actual record-specific rejection of federal relief supplies no categorical rule extinguishing innocence protections for other purposes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Silence does not prove a no-significant-history mitigating fact. No actual history is found from material the jury did not hear; youth is a different mitigating circumstance. The alternative innocence-instruction holding assumes the protection without deciding its existence or full scope. Strickland merits and the independently rejected defaulted counsel claim are not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse conditional habeas relief on the instruction ground and remand. No counsel-effectiveness or innocence finding, new stay or execution order issues.

### Fourth Amendment protection of possession

#### Soldal v. Cook County, 506 U.S. 56 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-6516.

**Decided:** December 8, 1992.

##### A seizure of a home protects possession independently of privacy

**Holding:** Meaningful government-attributable interference with possession of a home is a seizure under the Fourth Amendment even without a search, invasion of privacy or liberty, or criminal investigation. The family’s alleged forcible dispossession meets that threshold, but coverage alone does not establish unreasonableness or every element of §1983 liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; nine Justices adopt the same rule.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Jacobsen, 466 U.S. 109 — applied; possession and privacy remain independently protected interests.
- United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696 — applied; seizure scrutiny does not require an accompanying search.
- Arizona v. Hicks, 480 U.S. 321 — applied; plain-view seizure remains subject to the Fourth Amendment.
- Adickes v. S. H. Kress & Co., 398 U.S. 144 — applied to the summary-judgment joint-action inference, without a final conspiracy finding.
- Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 — preserved; seizure coverage does not establish municipal liability.
- Wyatt v. Cole, May 18, 1992 — preserved; attribution does not settle immunity or a private merits defense.

##### A due-process characterization does not displace the specific seizure claim

**Holding:** A court may not reject an otherwise applicable Fourth Amendment claim merely because the same conduct also concerns a deprivation of property under the Due Process Clause. Each applicable constitutional protection must be examined on its own terms; this disposition does not adjudicate the family’s separate substantive-due-process claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; unanimous holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Graham v. Connor, 490 U.S. 386 — applied; the specific Fourth Amendment source governs its covered conduct and is not displaced by a dominant-character theory.
- Parratt v. Taylor, 451 U.S. 527 — distinguished; postdeprivation procedural remedies do not answer the distinct seizure claim.
- Hudson v. Palmer, 468 U.S. 517 — confined to its prison setting for the relevant coverage proposition; no rule excluding possession of a home follows.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Private action alone is insufficient. Ultimate reasonableness, municipal policy, causation, immunity, damages and the separate substantive-due-process claim remain undecided. The holding addresses a home, without deciding coverage of every property category.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse adverse summary judgment and remand the seizure claim; no final finding of unreasonableness or award of damages.

### Fugitive disentitlement and appellate review

#### Ortega-Rodriguez v. United States, 507 U.S. 234 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7749.

**Decided:** March 8, 1993.

##### A sufficient appellate connection is required

**Holding:** When a defendant's flight and recapture both precede a timely appeal, fugitive status alone does not justify dismissal: the sanction must respond to a sufficient connection between that misconduct and the appellate process. Courts may adopt reasonable rules for recurring classes of appellate disruption, and may dismiss for sufficiently established interference, inability to conduct meaningful review, or prejudice to a legally available retrial; retrial prejudice cannot justify dismissing a sufficiency appeal whose successful resolution would prohibit retrial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices adopt the rule, qualifications and remand.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Molinaro v. New Jersey, 396 U.S. 365: retained for flight connected to pending review; its disentitlement rationale does not establish automatic forfeiture for every completed preappeal flight.
- Estelle v. Dorrough, 420 U.S. 534: retained for deterrence and appellate order in its pending-appeal setting; it does not settle the distinct preappeal boundary.
- Smith v. United States, 94 U.S. 97, Bohanan v. Nebraska, 125 U.S. 692, and Eisler v. United States, 338 U.S. 189: their enforcement rationale remains applicable to fugitives beyond the reviewing court's effective control, a condition absent here.
- Allen v. Georgia, 166 U.S. 138: postappeal escape may support continued dismissal after recapture; that setting remains distinct.
- Thomas v. Arn, 474 U.S. 140: applied to require reasonable appellate supervision; reasonable circuit rules need not be identical.
- United States v. Holmes, 680 F.2d 1372: its automatic conviction-appeal forfeiture rule is rejected insofar as it dispenses with a sufficient appellate connection; its exception for absence beyond the defendant's control and its preservation of sentencing-error review do not cure that defect.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The issue is appellate supervision, not absence of a live controversy. District-court escape sanctions, appeal deadlines and disentitlement for flight during appeal remain available. No least-restrictive-sanction requirement, constitutional right to appeal or automatic review despite actual appellate disruption is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate dismissal and remand for the sufficient-connection inquiry, including any actual significance of separate codefendant appeals. The conviction appeal remains undecided.

### Ineffective assistance and displaced substantive law

#### Lockhart v. Fretwell, 506 U.S. 364 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1393.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### Loss of a displaced substantive rule is not cognizable prejudice here

**Holding:** Counsel's failure to obtain a favorable application of a substantive rule subsequently rejected does not establish Strickland prejudice where the lost benefit is one the governing law does not recognize and the omission otherwise deprived the defendant of no fair or reliable sentencing protection. This limited rule does not replace ordinary reasonable-probability analysis, excuse deficient performance, or require an additional showing of unfairness in the usual ineffective-assistance case.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt the rule and its application to the Collins objection.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S.668 (1984): applied and clarified for loss of a subsequently rejected substantive rule; ordinary reasonable-probability analysis remains the usual test.
- Nix v. Whiteside, 475 U.S.157 (1986): applied by analogy for the distinction between a changed outcome and loss of a legally protected benefit; no finding of perjury by Fretwell is made.
- Kimmelman v. Morrison, 477 U.S.365 (1986): distinguished; a procedural bar on direct habeas consideration does not eliminate a meritorious underlying claim, unlike the substantive premise here.
- Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S.231 (1988): applied for constitutionally sufficient narrowing at the guilt stage despite duplication at sentencing.
- Collins v. Lockhart, 754 F.2d258 (8th Cir.1985), and Perry v. Lockhart, 871 F.2d1384 (8th Cir.1989): Collins's displaced double-counting rule supplies no cognizable benefit; Perry's rejection of that rule supplies the accepted litigation premise. Whether Collins could independently be revived is not decided.

##### Teague does not preserve the displaced rule for this prejudice inquiry

**Holding:** Teague's limitation on new rules supporting collateral relief does not require a federal habeas court to disregard a subsequent legal correction favorable to the State when deciding whether an omitted objection caused cognizable Strickland prejudice. Performance remains assessed from counsel's perspective at the time, while the prejudice inquiry here does not preserve an entitlement to the rejected Collins rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this answer to the temporal objection.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S.288 (1989): distinguished from an affirmative entitlement to an obsolete rule; its collateral finality limitation is not made reciprocal.
- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S.668 (1984): preserves the contemporaneous perspective for evaluating performance without mechanically fixing the prejudice inquiry to every favorable rule then available.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice remains the usual rule; no separate unfairness requirement is added in ordinary cases. Deficient performance is conceded. The Court does not revive Collins, decide the Solicitor General’s separate §2254(a) theory or determine the unresolved penalty-preparation claims. Thomas’s state/federal hierarchy observation is not a majority holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Eighth Circuit grant of relief on the Collins-only ground. Unresolved independent claims retain their own treatment; no new sentence, stay or execution is ordered.

### Interstate detainers and trial-period commencement

#### Fex v. Michigan, 507 U.S. 43 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7873.

**Decided:** February 23, 1993.

##### Article III(a): actual receipt starts the trial period

**Holding:** When a prisoner invokes Article III while serving a term of imprisonment and an untried charge in another participating State is the subject of a lodged detainer, the 180-day trial period begins only when the required written notice and request for final disposition have actually reached both the prosecuting officer and the appropriate court in that State. Delivery to the custodian alone does not start it; a necessary or reasonable continuance may be allowed for good cause shown in open court with the prisoner or counsel present, Article VI(a) tolls the period while the prisoner is unable to stand trial as determined by the court having jurisdiction, and Article V(c) requires dismissal with prejudice when the applicable period is violated.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by White, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies six votes for this rule and its application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carchman v. Nash, 473 U.S. 716 (1985): applied for the compact’s federal-law character and attention to operative language; its exclusion of probation-violation detainers remains intact.
- Houston v. Lack, 487 U.S. 266 (1988): distinguished because a prisoner’s own appeal-filing deadline differs from commencement of the receiving State’s prosecution period; its mailbox rule remains in force within its setting.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The custodian’s prompt forwarding and Article III(c) notice obligations remain; actual receipt does not excuse official delay. Separate notice remedies, constitutional claims and a continuance first sought after expiration are not resolved. There is no automatic grace period or bad-faith prerequisite.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm: trial began 177 days after both required recipients received the papers, so Article III(a) supplies no dismissal with prejudice here.

### Joint trials, severance and claim preservation

#### Zafiro v. United States, 506 U.S. 534 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-6824.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### Concrete prejudice and effective relief under Rule14

**Holding:** For properly joined defendants, mutually antagonistic defenses alone do not require severance; the district court evaluates whether a joint trial presents a serious risk of compromising a specific trial right or preventing a reliable individualized verdict, and chooses effective relief in its sound discretion. Severance is not required for every showing of prejudice if instructions or other safeguards adequately cure the risk, but instructions are not presumed to cure every risk; Garcia, Soto and Martinez have shown no uncured risk requiring separate trials here.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; eight Justices adopt the rule and its application. Stevens’s narrower concurrence supplies no join in this general formulation.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Richardson v. Marsh,481 U.S.200 — applied for joint-trial benefits and the ordinary effectiveness of instructions, not a universal cure.
- Bruton v. United States,391 U.S.123 — preserved: a specific confrontation risk may require exclusion, redaction or severance when instructions are inadequate.
- United States v. Lane,474 U.S.438 — applied only to Rule 14’s discretion over effective prejudice relief; no Rule 8 misjoinder ruling.
- Schaffer v. United States,362 U.S.511 — applied to adequate individualized jury instructions on this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Serious risk may arise from another defendant’s evidence, markedly different culpability in complex proof or unavailable exculpatory evidence; the examples are not exhaustive. A better chance of acquittal alone is insufficient. Rule 8 joinder and independent confrontation rights remain distinct; review of effective Rule 14 relief is for abuse of discretion. Gloria’s sufficiency and collateral questions are not reached.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm on petitioner-specific grounds: Gloria’s claim is not reviewed; Garcia, Soto and Martinez receive no new trial because the individualized instructions adequately address the risks shown.

### Petty offenses and the jury-trial right

#### United States v. Nachtigal, 507 U.S. 1 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-609.

**Decided:** February 22, 1993.

##### Whether the national-park DUI penalties overcome the petty-offense presumption

**Holding:** An offense carrying no more than six months’ imprisonment is presumptively petty for the constitutional jury-trial right, but the defendant may overcome that presumption by showing that the additional authorized penalties, considered with the maximum incarceration, are so severe that they clearly reflect a legislative judgment that the offense is serious. For this national-park DUI scheme, the six-month imprisonment ceiling, $5,000 fine and alternative probation of up to five years with its authorized conditions do not rebut the presumption; the defendant has no constitutional jury entitlement on that basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; all nine support both the complete Blanton rule and this application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Blanton v. North Las Vegas, 489 U.S. 538 (1989), applied: the six-month presumption remains rebuttable after considering additional authorized penalties; the governing legislature’s choices, rather than other jurisdictions’ practices, supply the relevant seriousness judgment.
- United States v. Craner, 652 F.2d 23 (9th Cir. 1981), displaced to the extent its jury-entitlement rule for this federal offense conflicts with Blanton as applied here. Delegated rulemaking does not remove Congress’s enacted imprisonment ceiling from the analysis.
No Supreme Court precedent is overruled. This decision establishes no universal rule that financial sanctions or probation can never overcome the petty-offense presumption.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Authorized probation conditions, including treatment, community service, residence or movement restrictions and monitored home confinement, are considered; intermittent custody remains bounded by the offense’s imprisonment ceiling. Gain-or-loss-based alternative fines lack an established predicate on this record; a different triggered penalty package is not decided. No universal rule excludes fines or probation from rebutting the petty-offense presumption. The separate acquittal remains outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit jury-entitlement judgment and remand; other challenges to the conviction remain outside this decision.

### Presence at commencement of trial

#### Crosby v. United States, 506 U.S. 255 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-6194.

**Decided:** January 13, 1993.

##### Trial cannot begin under the voluntary-absence exception without initial presence

**Holding:** Rule 43 does not permit a criminal trial to begin in a defendant’s absence merely because that absence is voluntary; its voluntary-absence continuation exception requires that the defendant was initially present when trial began. Crosby’s convictions from such a trial must be set aside and the case remanded for proceedings complying with the rule; no constitutional minimum or change to another enumerated exception is decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; nine participating Justices adopt the rule and explanation.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Diaz v. United States, 223 U.S.442 — applied at its actual scope: voluntary departure permits continuation after initial presence, not commencement in absence.
- Taylor v. United States, 414 U.S.17 — applied: no special warning is required for voluntary midtrial absence; its initial-presence premise remains essential.
- United States v. Crosby, 917 F.2d362 — rejected on Rule 43: practical burdens and knowing pretrial flight cannot replace the rule’s express commencement condition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Other express Rule 43 exceptions and lawful independent flight sanctions are unchanged. The Court decides neither constitutional waiver nor a general harmless-error rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the judgment sustaining Crosby’s convictions and remand for proceedings complying with Rule 43; no flight sanction or revised restitution award is entered.

### Protected silence and habeas harmless-error review

#### Brecht v. Abrahamson, 507 U.S. 619 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7358.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### The protected-silence error remains constitutional trial error

**Holding:** Using Brecht’s silence after Miranda warnings to impeach his trial explanation violates Doyle’s due-process protection; permitted impeachment with prewarning silence is analytically separate. **Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Jenkins, 447 U.S. 231, and Fletcher, 455 U.S. 603 — distinguished: prewarning impeachment remains permissible in the circumstances presented.
- Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279 — applied: this evidentiary trial error permits harmlessness analysis; structural defects remain distinct.

##### Chapman governs this preserved Doyle claim on habeas

**Holding:** For this preserved Doyle trial error properly before the federal habeas court, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the constitutional error did not contribute to the verdict. **Limits and questions not reached:** The Chapman ruling covers this preserved Doyle claim properly on habeas, not every constitutional collateral claim. ### Sentencing for trial perjury

#### United States v. Dunnigan, 507 U.S. 87 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1300.

**Decided:** February 23, 1993.

##### Constitutional permission and the Guideline’s consequence

**Holding:** A sentence increase under U.S.S.G. § 3C1.1 for independently established trial perjury—willfully false testimony under oath or affirmation about a material matter—does not violate the defendant’s right to testify. Once the court properly determines that the defendant committed such perjury, the applicable Guideline requires its two-level obstruction adjustment; neither exercising the trial right nor a guilty verdict alone supplies that predicate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, and Thomas; all nine Justices adopt this proposition, its explanation and its record application, so it controls directly.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Nix v. Whiteside, 475 U.S. 157 (1986), applied to the right’s boundary: the right to present testimony includes no entitlement to perjury; the decision does not authorize assuming falsehood from an unsuccessful defense.
- United States v. Grayson, 438 U.S. 41 (1978), applied: actual willful trial falsehood may inform sentencing; its rehabilitation discussion is not an exclusive justification and its warning against reflex increases remains operative.

##### Perjury predicates, independent findings and application

**Holding:** When a defendant objects to a § 3C1.1 increase based on trial testimony, the court must review the evidence and independently find willfully false sworn testimony concerning a material matter, excluding confusion, mistake and faulty memory. Separate clear findings for each element are preferable, but a single determination encompassing all the factual predicates is sufficient; the district court’s findings here meet that standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, and Thomas; all nine Justices adopt this proposition, its explanation and its record application, so it controls directly.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Grayson, 438 U.S. 41 (1978), applied with its safeguard intact: a sentencing response requires an actual assessment of deliberate false testimony, not an automatic consequence of conviction.
- Nix v. Whiteside, 475 U.S. 157 (1986), left intact in its factual boundary: absence of a protected right to perjury does not establish that disputed testimony was perjurious.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No separate perjury prosecution or special verbal formula is required. Neither a guilty verdict nor rejection of a truthful defense establishes perjury. No general proof quantum, universal Guidelines-commentary rule or separate actual-obstruction element from summary-contempt law is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit’s categorical constitutional prohibition and remand; the district court’s independent finding encompasses the required perjury predicates.

### Forfeited error on criminal appeal

#### United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1306.
**Decided:** April 26, 1993.

**Holding:** Rule 52(b) permits correction of a forfeited legal error only if it is plain and affects substantial rights; valid intentional relinquishment of a known right extinguishes error, while mere failure timely to assert a right does not. - Brecht, April 21, 1993: distinguished; preserved Doyle habeas review does not control forfeited Rule 24(c) error.

##### Correction remains discretionary

**Holding:** Even when plain forfeited error affects substantial rights, correction under Rule 52(b) is permitted, not automatic, and should occur only when the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. **Holding:** For these alternates, instructed not to participate, mere presence during deliberations neither establishes prejudice nor warrants a presumption of prejudice under Rule 52(b). Without a specific showing of participation or a chilling effect, respondents have not shown an effect on substantial rights; the per se new-trial ruling must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 24(c) waivability, errors first clarified on appeal, outcome-independent categories, appropriate prejudice presumptions, the preserved-objection burden, and Rule 606(b) hearing limits remain undecided. ##### Direct review permits the necessary sentence correction

**Holding:** Because this is direct review of the preserved mitigation claim, Teague does not bar relief. The death sentence must be reversed for an effective sentencing vehicle permitting the material youth mitigation to affect punishment without a false answer to a factual special issue; Texas may provide adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence, while the guilt judgment remains intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, Direct review permits the necessary sentence correction portion; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The whole-charge reasonable-likelihood standard, not mere possibility, governs. No categorical age bar, facial invalidation of Texas sentencing, right to favorable weight or unrestricted mercy instruction is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse only the death sentence and remand for constitutionally adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence. Guilt remains intact; no exact instruction wording is prescribed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague, 489 U.S.288 — collateral-review restriction inapplicable to this direct appeal.
- Graham, January 25, 1993 — merits and remedy principles followed; collateral-review limitations do not bar this direct appeal.
- Jurek, 428 U.S.262 — authority to structure sentencing retained subject to effective mitigation.

### Competence and informed waiver

#### Godinez v. Moran, 509 U.S. 389 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-725.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### A common capacity standard and a separate actual waiver

**Holding:** Dusky supplies the constitutional mental-capacity standard for standing trial, pleading guilty and choosing to waive counsel: sufficient present ability to consult with counsel with rational understanding, and rational as well as factual understanding of the proceedings. A competent defendant may relinquish the rights only through a knowing, intelligent and voluntary waiver; competence does not establish that the particular choice was informed and uncoerced.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Dusky v. United States, 362 U.S.402: the functional competence standard governs each presented choice.
- Faretta v. California, 422 U.S.806: legal skill is distinct from competence to choose representation.
- Boykin v. Alabama, 395 U.S.238, and Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S.458: actual informed and voluntary waiver remains separately required.
- Westbrook v. Arizona, 384 U.S.150: additional waiver inquiry preserved; a higher constitutional capacity quantum is not required.

##### Existing findings do not eliminate the distinct inquiry on remand

**Holding:** A further competence determination is required when circumstances give the court reason to doubt present competence; no automatic hearing is required for every plea or waiver. Reversal of the higher-standard ruling leaves the existing claims about adequate inquiry and informed, voluntary relinquishment for consideration under the correct standards and the applicable pre-AEDPA treatment of state findings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** States may provide further safeguards. Execution competence and a distinct standard for actually conducting a trial without counsel remain open. Medication does not itself establish incapacity, and earlier findings are neither ignored nor conclusive after material change; reliable retrospective review and actual waivers remain for consideration.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the higher-standard basis and remove the associated conditional-writ direction; remand under proper capacity, inquiry, waiver and pre-AEDPA state-finding rules. No automatic validation or restoration of the ex-wife murder death sentence already reduced to life.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pate v. Robinson, 383 U.S.375, and Drope v. Missouri, 420 U.S.162: reasonable doubt concerning present competence triggers inquiry.
- Medina, June 22, 1992, and Riggins, May 18, 1992: their limited rules are preserved, not enlarged into conclusive findings here.
- Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S.37: the Court may decline to introduce an unraised Teague defense.

### Successive prosecution after criminal contempt

#### United States v. Dixon, 509 U.S. 688 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1231.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

##### Nonsummary punitive contempt triggers double-jeopardy protection

**Holding:** A completed nonsummary criminal-contempt prosecution places the accused in criminal jeopardy; private initiation and an order’s protective purpose do not categorically remove that protection. Whether a later prosecution is for the same offense remains a separate inquiry; coercive civil sanctions, summary courtroom contempt and separate sovereigns are not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bloom v. Illinois, 391 U.S.194: criminal character of punitive contempt applied.
- Gompers v. Bucks Stove & Range Co., 221 U.S.418: punitive/coercive distinction applied.
- Young v. United States ex rel. Vuitton, 481 U.S.787: independent judicial enforcement authority retained, not a categorical exception from criminal protection.

##### Previously prosecuted necessary offenses cannot be tried again

**Holding:** When a criminal-contempt adjudication necessarily prosecutes the substantive offense forbidden by the order, the Government cannot prosecute that same substantive offense again. Dixon’s drug offense and Foster I’s November assault were necessarily prosecuted in the respective contempts and are barred; this shared proposition does not supply a common analysis of Foster II–V.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brown v. Ohio, 432 U.S.161: successive greater/included-offense protection applied.
- Harris v. Oklahoma, 433 U.S.682: necessary substantive offense cannot be erased by an abstract description of the first charge.
- Blockburger v. United States, 284 U.S.299: elements inquiry retained; disagreement over its broader application separately recorded.

##### Grady remains governing successive-prosecution law

**Holding:** Grady is not overruled: beyond Blockburger’s elements protection, a later prosecution is barred when establishing an essential element requires proof of conduct constituting an offense for which the defendant has already been prosecuted. Mere reuse of evidence or a common episode does not suffice, and Felix’s independently punishable conspiracy holding remains; no five-Justice rationale determines Grady’s application to Foster II–V in this fractured decision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Grady survives; evidentiary overlap and a common episode do not alone establish a bar. Summary contempt, coercive civil sanctions and separate sovereigns are not decided. Foster II–V have no controlling rationale, as stated separately below.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the bar for Dixon’s drug charge and Foster I (6–3), leaving the contempt judgments intact. Reverse the bar for Foster II–V and remand (5–4); threat charges remain subject to a properly established Ashe bar from actual acquittals and other defenses. Foster V may proceed without authorizing a barred simple-assault conviction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S.508: expressly retained; no overruling by this decision.
- United States v. Felix, March 25, 1992: conspiracy and evidentiary-overlap distinctions preserved, not recast as an overruling.
- Illinois v. Vitale, 447 U.S.410: successive-prosecution concern beyond formal elements supports retention.
- Brown v. Ohio, 432 U.S.161: finality and greater/included-offense protection retained.
- Ashe v. Swenson, 397 U.S.436: independent issue-preclusion rule preserved for the remanded threat charges.

##### Whether Foster II–V may proceed under a common double-jeopardy rationale

**Authority of the judgment:** Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reverse the bars to Foster II–V and remand, 5–4. No controlling rationale exists under Marks: the four-Justice elements-only rationale would overrule Grady, while Blackmun retains Grady but treats judicial-order enforcement differently. Those incompatible grounds are not logical subsets. The judgment permits these counts without creating a general contempt exception or displacing the separately majority-supported retention of Grady.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Foster II–IV remain subject to any actual-acquittal issue preclusion established under Ashe and other preserved defenses. Foster V’s distinct assault-with-intent-to-kill charge may proceed; the judgment does not authorize a barred simple-assault conviction or disturb the contempt dispositions.

### Excessive fines and federal civil forfeiture

#### Austin v. United States, 509 U.S. 602 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-6073.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

##### These statutory forfeitures are covered fines

**Holding:** Forfeiture to the United States under 21 U.S.C.§§ 881(a)(4) and(a)(7) constitutes payment in kind to the sovereign at least partly as punishment and is subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. Its civil designation, proceeding against property, and concurrent remedial aims do not remove coverage; coverage does not establish excessiveness or import all criminal-procedure safeguards.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Browning-Ferris v. Kelco Disposal, 492 U.S.257: government prosecution/receipt distinguishes this fine from private punitive award.
- United States v. Halper, 490 U.S.435: partly punitive function recognized by analogy; separate Double Jeopardy analysis not imported.
- One 1958 Plymouth Sedan v. Pennsylvania, 380 U.S.693: lawful property is not contraband merely because used in crime.
- One Lot Emerald Cut Stones v. United States, 409 U.S.232, and United States v. One Assortment of 89 Firearms, 465 U.S.354: remedial customs/contraband characterizations distinguished, not abolished.
- Buena Vista, February 24, 1993: statutory owner defenses remain provision-specific; no § 881(a)(7) or constitutional innocent owner holding imported.

##### Historical in rem form does not negate punishment

**Holding:** The historical treatment of statutory in rem forfeiture includes punishment and does not establish categorical exemption from the Excessive Fines Clause for these forfeitures. The traditionally limited common-law innocent-owner defense does not prove that forfeiture was solely remedial; the constitutionality of forfeiting a truly innocent owner’s property remains unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Peisch v. Ware, 4 Cranch 347: punitive understanding and stranger-removal qualification applied.
- The Palmyra, 12 Wheat 1: no prior personal conviction required, not an absence of punishment holding.
- J.W. Goldsmith, Jr.-Grant Co. v. United States, 254 U.S.505, and Calero-Toledo v. Pearson Yacht, 416 U.S.663: punitive/deterrent features recognized; truly innocent owner question reserved.

##### Remand leaves excessiveness distinct from statutory connection

**Holding:** The lower court must evaluate whether Austin’s actual forfeiture is excessive; statutory facilitation and coverage as punishment do not themselves decide that question. The Court adopts no comprehensive excessiveness test and does not limit the remand to instrumentality alone: the property’s connection to the offense may matter, and other relevant considerations may also be examined.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Coverage does not decide excessiveness, incorporate all criminal safeguards or establish state incorporation. Truly innocent ownership, taxation and solely compensatory recovery remain distinct; neither a comprehensive formula nor instrumentality alone is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for an excessiveness inquiry on a developed record. No automatic property return, value finding, ratio or fixed cap; the lower court’s concern was not a completed excessiveness determination.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Browning-Ferris, 492 U.S.257: fine coverage principle applied without inventing a comprehensive proportionality formula.
- Calero-Toledo, 416 U.S.663: statutory/historical connection not converted into a final excessiveness answer.

### Excessive fines and criminal RICO forfeiture

#### Alexander v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1526.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

For the traditional-prior-restraint classification and independent First Amendment examination in *Alexander v. United States*, June 28, 1993, No. 91-1526, see First Amendment — Postconviction forfeiture of expressive enterprises.

##### Whether punitive criminal RICO forfeiture requires a distinct Excessive Fines inquiry

**Holding:** A criminal RICO forfeiture extracting property as punishment is subject to the Excessive Fines Clause independently of the validity of the convictions or the permissibility of the prison term. The lower court must examine the extent of the exaction in relation to the offenses supporting it; it may not reject that inquiry simply because the sentence is less than life imprisonment without parole.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas; six Justices adopt coverage and remand. Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy accept the alternative excessiveness remand without joining Part III or adopting a proportionality formula.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court leaves actual excessiveness to the lower court, with appropriate record development. The inquiry is not reduced to counting the seven titles adjudged obscene, and it may not presume that every asset or dollar of lawful business is criminal activity. No numerical cap, comprehensive proportionality formula or binding list of excessiveness considerations is adopted. The independent First Amendment inquiry remains necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate approval of the challenged forfeiture and remand for an Excessive Fines examination independent of the First Amendment inquiry. All nine accept this remand, but only the six Part III Justices adopt its coverage rationale; Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy accept alternative relief without joining that rationale. No final excessiveness finding, dollar cap or return of property is ordered; the convictions remain undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Browning-Ferris Industries v. Kelco Disposal, 492 U.S.257: its distinction between private awards and governmental punishment supports coverage of this criminal forfeiture; its private-award holding remains intact.
- Solem v. Helm, 463 U.S.277: no rule about imprisonment displaces the separate Excessive Fines inquiry; the Court adopts no general prison-sentence holding here.
- Austin v. United States, June 28, 1993, is an uncoordinated same-day decision, not an earlier source of this holding. No logical priority or adoption of its separate writings is inferred.
