## Bankruptcy

### Estate exclusions for pension trust interests

#### Patterson v. Shumate, 504 U.S. 753 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-913.
**Decided:** June 15, 1992.

##### Whether an ERISA-enforceable antialienation restriction excludes this beneficial trust interest under §541(c)(2)

**Holding:** Section 541(c)(2) preserves a restriction on transfer of a debtor's beneficial interest in a trust when that restriction is enforceable under applicable nonbankruptcy law, including federal law. This established ERISA-qualified trust contains an enforceable antialienation restriction, so Shumate's interest is excluded from the bankruptcy estate and cannot be turned over to its trustee as estate property.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt the statutory exclusion. Scalia's separate concurrence does not limit his full join; Stone concurs in the judgment without a formal opinion join.

**Material application:** Plan trust status, ERISA qualification and federal enforceability are established. Shumate's control, which defeated the lower state-spendthrift analysis, cannot impose a state-law limitation absent from §541(c)(2). Section 522 retains work for interests not already excluded; policy observations supply no independent alternative holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The required beneficial trust interest, enforceable transfer restriction and applicable nonbankruptcy law remain essential. Other plans, nontrust interests, statutory exceptions, distributed benefits, avoidance remedies and the plan-fiduciary question preserved in Guidry are not adjudicated or displaced. No general pension immunity is created. The alternative §522(b)(2)(A) exemption and §522(d)(10)(E)'s coverage of presently payable benefits versus undistributed corpus remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 9–0. The qualifying interest is excluded from the estate and cannot be turned over as estate property; no payment amount or new factual inquiry is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Guidry v. Sheet Metal Workers National Pension Fund, 493 U.S. 365 (1990): applied for enforceability and the refusal to create an equitable exception; its statutory exceptions and reserved plan-fiduciary issue remain intact.
- Union Bank v. Wolas, simulated December 11, 1991: applied for giving effect to an enacted bankruptcy provision despite a proposed policy limitation; its ordinary-course preference holding remains unchanged.
- Dewsnup v. Timm, simulated January 15, 1992: distinguished as a context-specific construction preserving an allowed real-property lien, not a restriction of §541(c)(2) to state law; no holding is overruled.

### Exemption objections

#### Taylor v. Freeland & Kronz, 503 U.S. 638 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-571.
**Decided:** April 21, 1992.

##### Whether an untimely objection may defeat a scheduled exemption lacking a colorable statutory basis

**Holding:** Under 11 U.S.C. §522(l) and the applicable Bankruptcy Rule 4003(b), property claimed as exempt becomes exempt when no party in interest objects within 30 days after the creditors' meeting concludes or a relevant amendment to the exemption list is filed, whichever is later, and the court grants no further time within that period. The trustee cannot later contest the claimed exemption merely because it lacked a colorable statutory basis; this holding concerns the scheduled proceeds claimed here and does not decide a different claim's scope, an unscheduled asset, or an unpreserved fraud or §105(a) theory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion for the Court, joined in full by Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Eight participating Justices adopt the rule and its explanation at the same level of generality; no fractured-opinion analysis is needed.

**Material application:** Taylor knew of the lawsuit and its potentially substantial proceeds. Uncertainty about value allowed timely inquiry, objection or a request for extension; it did not suspend the deadline until collection. Rule 9006(b)(3) confines enlargement to Rule 4003(b)’s conditions; the August 1, 1991 supplemental-schedule wording does not reopen the expired period.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide §105(a), unpreserved fraud, unscheduled property, differently limited exemption claims, retroactivity, alternative valuation, liens or good faith. Rule 4003(c)’s burden on the objector remains applicable in a properly presented objection proceeding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Third Circuit is affirmed. Its reversal of the $23,483.75-plus-interest turnover order stands; the scheduled proceeds remain exempt against this untimely theory. No alternative recovery, sanction or new award is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, Inc., 489 U.S. 235 — applied: a coherent Bankruptcy Code provision is enforced according to its terms; no general power to replace explicit procedure with an equitable exception follows from bankruptcy administration.
- Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, March 9, 1992 — consistent with its text/coexistence rule: substantive exemption eligibility and the procedure for contesting a claim perform different work. Its separate bankruptcy appellate-jurisdiction holding remains unchanged.
- In re Dembs, 757 F.2d 777, and In re Peterson, 920 F.2d 1389 — rejected insofar as their colorability requirement permits a late objection of this kind; that requirement cannot govern the §522(l) consequence decided here.

### Liens and claim valuation

#### Dewsnup v. Timm, 502 U.S. 410 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-741.
**Decided:** January 15, 1992.

##### Whether §506(d) reduces an allowed real-property lien to collateral value in the presented Chapter 7 case

**Holding:** In the Chapter 7 setting presented, §506(d) does not reduce a lien on real property to its current judicially determined value when the claim is allowed under §502 and secured by that lien. Section 506(a)'s valuation for treatment of a claim does not, on these facts, compel avoidance of the lien's excess under §506(d); other statutory settings and express exceptions remain outside the decision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion, joined by White, Stevens, O'Connor and Kennedy; five of eight participating Justices control the statutory construction and affirmance.

**Material application:** The debtor sought to reduce the lien securing an allowed debt of approximately $120,000 to the established property value of $39,000. Section 506(a) continues to value secured and unsecured amounts for claim treatment; that valuation does not automatically avoid the excess lien here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not adopt the Tenth Circuit's abandonment or estate-interest rationale, determine estate administration, or decide other subsections, express statutory exceptions, reorganization-specific restrictions, constitutional taking questions, or future-valuation questions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit judgment is affirmed and the dismissal with prejudice remains in place; the allowed lien is not reduced under §506(d) to the property's judicially determined value.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Long v. Bullard*, 117 U.S. 617 — the ordinary survival of liens through personal discharge remains an interpretive premise, not immunity from every express avoidance provision.
- *Johnson v. Home State Bank*, 501 U.S. 78 — applied for the distinction between extinguished personal liability and a surviving claim against property.
- *Farrey v. Sanderfoot*, 500 U.S. 291 — lien survival informs the Code's operation subject to its actual avoidance provisions.
- *United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, Inc.*, 489 U.S. 235 — preserved; the Court finds contextual uncertainty here rather than displacing the priority of clear statutory text.
- *United Savings Association v. Timbers of Inwood Forest Associates*, 484 U.S. 365 — §506(a)'s valuation function remains operative; it does not decide this §506(d) question.
- *Union Bank v. Wolas*, simulated December 11, 1991 — its §547(c)(2) rule remains intact; no lien-avoidance rule is drawn from its treatment of debt maturity.

### Ordinary-course preference exception

#### Union Bank v. Wolas, 502 U.S. 151 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1491.  
**Decided:** December 11, 1991.

##### Whether payments on long-term debt are eligible for the contemporaneous section 547(c)(2) ordinary-course exception

**Holding:** Payments on long-term debt may qualify for the then-current §547(c)(2) ordinary-course exception; debt maturity is not an additional categorical exclusion. Under §547(g), the creditor must prove that the debt was incurred in the ordinary course of both parties, that payment was made in their ordinary course, and that payment accorded with ordinary business terms.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion states this statutory proposition for all nine Justices. Scalia's separate writing agrees with this holding and judgment.

**Material application:** Congress removed the former 45-day restriction while retaining the three statutory requirements. Eligibility alone does not establish that the particular interest and commitment-fee payments satisfy them.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No presumption of ordinariness attaches to long-term payments. The decision does not categorically exempt interest or commitment fees or decide challenges the maturity ruling bypassed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for review of remaining challenges to the statutory elements. Judgment for the Bank is not itself restored.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises supplies adherence to operative Code text unless demonstrably at odds with congressional intent. The Ninth Circuit's categorical CHG International construction is rejected; no Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

### Preference transfer timing

#### Barnhill v. Johnson, 503 U.S. 393 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-159.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

##### When an ordinary check transfers the debtor’s account interest for §547(b)’s preference period

**Holding:** For §547(b), an ordinary check transfers the debtor’s relevant bank-account interest when the drawee honors it, because delivery gives the payee no assigned interest in that account. The November 20 honor date therefore satisfies the ninety-day timing element here, without deciding other preference elements or defenses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The trustee must establish every other preference element; properly presented §547(c) defenses remain available. Neither timing under §547(c) nor nonordinary payment instruments is decided. Section 547(e) does not relate the transfer back before an interest in the account passed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit’s honor-date judgment is affirmed. November 20, the honor date, falls within the ninety-day period; the decision does not automatically avoid every check honored within that period.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McKenzie v. Irving Trust Co., 323 U.S. 365 — applied: federal transfer law operates on property interests identified by state law.
- Butner v. United States, 440 U.S. 48 — applied: state law defines the relevant property interest absent a contrary federal rule.
- Union Bank v. Wolas, December 11, 1991 — left intact: ordinary-course eligibility and proof remain distinct from §547(b) timing.

### Excusable neglect and claim deadlines

#### Pioneer Investment Services Co. v. Brunswick Associates Limited Partnership, 507 U.S. 380 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1695.

**Decided:** March 24, 1993.

##### Excusable neglect can include carelessness

**Holding:** For a Chapter 11 proof-of-claim deadline subject to Bankruptcy Rule 9006(b)(1), neglect includes inadvertence, mistake or carelessness as well as circumstances beyond the actor's control, but relief after expiration requires a motion and excusability. Excusability depends on all relevant circumstances, including prejudice to the debtor, length of delay and its effect on proceedings, the reason for delay including reasonable control, and good faith; this authority does not enlarge deadlines governed by Rule 9006(b)(2)'s prohibitions or (b)(3)'s specific-rule limitations.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion for the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy: five.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Taylor v. Freeland & Kronz, April 21, 1992, decision: distinguishes the Rule 4003(b)/9006(b)(3) exemption-objection limit; preserves its strict operation.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S.871 (1990): distinguishes the requirement of a supported post-deadline request; does not confine every qualifying neglect to uncontrollable events.

##### Counsel's conduct is attributed to the creditor

**Holding:** A creditor seeking relief under Rule 9006(b)(1) is accountable for the acts and omissions of its freely selected counsel; the court must evaluate the neglect of both client and lawyer together. Reasonable client reliance on counsel does not create an exception from that attribution or independently require an extension.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Court opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy: five; the dissent does not dispute attorney attribution, but its disagreement about excusability does not create another rationale majority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Link v. Wabash Railroad Co., 370 U.S.626 (1962): applies attribution of freely selected counsel's conduct to the client.
- United States v. Boyle, 469 U.S.241 (1985): applies the rejection of reliance on an agent as an automatic filing excuse.

##### This record supports allowing the late proofs of claim

**Holding:** With counsel's conduct attributed to the creditors, the unusually placed and ambiguous bar-date notice, the twenty-day delay, and the undisturbed findings of no prejudice, no adverse administrative effect and good faith support excusing the missed Chapter 11 deadline here. The late claims may be filed; the decision does not establish their substantive validity or amount, excuse ordinary disregard of a clear notice, or give dispositive weight to counsel's office disruption.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Court opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy: five; the Sixth Circuit's judgment is affirmed on the corrected attribution analysis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Link v. Wabash Railroad Co., 370 U.S.626 (1962): applies full attorney attribution while permitting the separate excusability inquiry authorized by this rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 9006(b)(2) forbids enlargement under Rules 1007(d), 1017(b)(3), 2003(a) and (d), 7052, 9023 and 9024. Rule 9006(b)(3) permits enlargement only on the conditions of Rules 1006(b)(2), 1017(e), 3002(c), 4003(b), 4004(a), 4007(c), 8002 and 9033. Neither Chapter 7 claims nor Taylor’s exemption-objection deadline gains a general equitable exception. No factor is dispositive: good faith or no prejudice alone is insufficient, and ordinary ignorance or inadvertence ordinarily does not excuse a clear deadline.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm permission to file these late Chapter 11 claims with counsel’s conduct fully attributed. The unusual notice and undisturbed equitable findings support relief; claim validity, priority and amount are not established.

### Protection of home-mortgage rights

#### Nobelman v. American Savings Bank, 508 U.S. 324 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-641.
**Decided:** June 1, 1993.

##### Valuation does not remove the protected rights of this mortgage holder

**Holding:** When a creditor retains a secured portion of an allowed claim secured only by a security interest in real property that is the debtor’s principal residence, §1322(b)(2) protects the holder’s rights in the mortgage claim as a whole, notwithstanding §506(a) valuation and bifurcation. The debtor may not modify the deficiency portion in a way that alters those protected repayment and enforcement rights; independent statutory restrictions and the express cure authority remain effective.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section1322(b)(5) permits cure within a reasonable time and maintenance of payments during the case when the final claim payment falls after the last plan payment. The automatic stay operates independently. Wholly unsecured liens and classification of ancillary deed collateral remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of the proposed stripping treatment; other lawful plan treatment remains available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Butner v. United States, 440 U.S.48: state law identifies the property and contract rights to which federal protection attaches.
- United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, Inc., 489 U.S.235: the text of each Bankruptcy Code provision receives independent effect.
- Dewsnup v. Timm, January 15, 1992: Chapter 7 §506(d) rule distinguished; §506(a) valuation is not displaced.

### Interest on mortgage cure arrearages

#### Rake v. Wade, 508 U.S. 464 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-621.
**Decided:** June 7, 1993.

##### Section 506(b) supplies interest on these oversecured arrearages

**Holding:** For an allowed secured claim whose collateral value, after recovery under §506(c), exceeds the claim, §506(b) allows interest within that surplus even if the agreement does not provide interest on arrearages. In these plans the parties accept application through confirmation; reasonable fees, costs and charges remain subject to the separate requirement that the agreement provide for them.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, Inc., 489 U.S.235: statutory interest distinguished from agreement-dependent reasonable fees, costs and charges.
- Nobelman v. American Savings Bank, June 1, 1993: protected mortgage rights coexist with independent Code provisions; contract silence does not displace §506(b).

##### Deferred cure payments must preserve present value under the applicable confirmation alternative

**Holding:** When a plan provides for an allowed secured arrearage claim through deferred payments, the creditor objects and the debtor retains the collateral, §1325(a)(5)(B) requires lien retention and distributions worth at least the allowed claim as of the plan’s effective date. Cure and maintenance under §1322(b)(5) do not exempt those arrearages from that requirement; creditor acceptance or surrender remains a distinct statutory alternative.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Preconfirmation application is accepted for these plans, not a universal temporal endpoint. Cure under §1322(b)(5) requires a reasonable time and maintained payments on debt extending beyond the plan. No rate, universal undersecured rule or agreement-free fee right is decided; accrual and present-value calculations must not duplicate recovery for the same period.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm entitlement to §506(b) interest within the collateral surplus and the distinct present-value protection under §1325(a)(5)(B).

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Nobelman, June 1, 1993: the anti-modification rule and express cure authority retained without erasing the separate confirmation requirement.
- Ron Pair, 489 U.S.235: distinct Bankruptcy Code provisions receive their own operative effect; the present-value holding rests on §1325(a)(5)(B), not §506(b) alone.
