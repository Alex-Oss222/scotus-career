# AGENTS.md — Stone-Zsela Supreme Court term simulation

This repository is a simulated Supreme Court of the United States, run one October Term at a time. The user controls one Justice, Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela, who replaces Rehnquist at the opening of October Term 1991 (October 7, 1991). Everything else — the other Justices, the Court's actions, and the law that results — is produced under the Engine.

You are the Engine's operator for one task at a time. Begin with the applicable task instructions below and honor their reading boundaries. Open, Run, and Close follow `foundation/ENGINE.md` and read the other governing files each stage uses, within that stage's boundary; no general pre-read overrides a stage's scope. Render reads only its authorized Render Input, the Render Contract, and this file's Render form, Holdings writing standard, and Voice sections.

## Files that govern every task

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `foundation/ENGINE.md` | The Supreme Court Term Simulator Engine. Controls adjudication, chronology, participation, coalitions, records, and term close. |
| `foundation/RENDER_CONTRACT.md` | The Judicial Turn Output Render Contract. Controls how a completed event is presented. Presentation only. |
| `foundation/COURT_COMPOSITION.md` | Who sits on the Court, seniority, seat lines, and circuit allotments, OT1991–OT2026. |
| `foundation/CASE_BRIEF_TEMPLATE.md` | The structure of a case brief: Section I neutral packet, Section II Stone position, Section III historical comparator. |
| `foundation/templates/` | The instructions for the three trackers. |

Treat `foundation/` as governing infrastructure. Do not change it during an ordinary term task. A user-authorized architecture revision made before a term opens may update it; such a revision must preserve existing adjudications unless the user separately authorizes a correction. The user has expressly authorized the bounded reconstruction revision in Engine §14 to apply prospectively during the open OT1995 term and future terms. This specific exception preserves completed adjudications and frozen handoffs; it is not general permission to revise infrastructure during a Run.

## Current state

`state/` holds the three trackers the Engine calls Holdings, Standards and Tests, and Standing State. They are the term-opening baseline for the term in progress. From OT1993 forward, Holdings is also split into canonical doctrinal reading volumes under `state/holdings/`; `state/HOLDINGS.md` is the generated continuous compatibility view. Between chunks, current-term law and procedure live in the term's `workspace/` projections, not in `state/`. The trackers are replaced only at term close, as one coordinated set, under the Close protocol below. Standing State holds only the Court's setting: roster and seniority, circuit allotments, standing practices, and the docket of cases brought up; it never carries positions, dependencies, or history.

## Term folders

`terms/OT<year>/`

- `case-list.md` — the user's term-wide inventory of cases and Court actions (the Engine's "lightweight term-wide inventory"). From OT1996 forward, whatever form the user supplies it in, it is saved in the form of `foundation/templates/CASE_INDEX_TEMPLATE.md`: the OT1994 case index with an Area of law column.
- `briefs/` — the user's case briefs, one file per chunk of about ten cases (`OT_<year>CHUNK<n>.md`). Inputs. Never edit them; Stone's words are never altered.
- `runtime/` — derived current-chunk handoffs. From OT1993 forward the normal set is `_NEUTRAL`, `_COMMITMENTS`, `_COMPARATOR`, `_RECONCILED`, and `_STONE`; the latter four are created or exposed only at the stage that may read them. Runtime splits are regenerated from the approved brief before each Run and must not be hand-maintained.
- `entering-law/` — derived per-chunk reading slices created immediately before modeling from the term-opening trackers plus every effective current-term Record. A slice is never authority; it is a verbatim or tightly linked reading copy of the authority the case actually implicates.
- `freeze/` — committed non-Stone commitment and reconciliation handoffs, plus durable validation summaries. The frozen copies are authoritative; a same-named `runtime/` file is only a working copy and never overrides them. These artifacts are internal and never enter the public render.
- `workspace/` — from OT1993 forward, four small coordinated projections: `manifest.md`, `ledger.md`, `continuity.md`, and `neutral-projection.md`. Earlier terms may retain a single `workspace.md`. Records remain authoritative over every workspace projection.
- `records/` — one Canonical Decision Record or Admitted Source Record per completed event, named by natural key: case or docket, event type, and date. One file per event. A user-authorized correction replaces the file in place and states concise truthful lineage; repository history carries the detailed edit history.
- `render-inputs/` — generated from each Record's bounded Public Projection section. The generated eleven-block handoff is the renderer's exclusive substantive input and must not be independently rewritten.
- `output/` — written by Render: the public render of each chunk under the Contract. One file per chunk: a correction replaces the event’s entry in place; the other entries stay byte-identical.
- `close/` — staged close work, one canonical `AUDIT.md`, the Term-Close Dossier, and generated `SEPARATE_WRITINGS.md`, `DEPARTURES.md`, and `CONSEQUENCES.md`. Candidate tracker files and temporary pass notes are removed after a successful Commit pass; superseded bytes remain in Git history.

## The four tasks the user gives

**"Open October Term <year>."** Validate `state/` as the opening trackers against the Engine's term-opening baseline rules, including `python tools/holdings_volumes.py check`. Read `case-list.md`; from OT1993 forward run `python tools/open_term.py OT<year>` to seed `workspace/manifest.md`, `ledger.md`, `continuity.md`, and `neutral-projection.md`, then add validated Standing State carryovers and replace the two projection stubs with the actual opening continuity and sanitized neutral state. The term is open only when `workspace/manifest.md` exists and those four files are validated. Report any conflict or missing inventory item; do not invent one.

**"Run October Term <year>, chunk <n>."** The user gives one command, but the operator must execute physically separated internal stages. First run `python tools/split_chunk.py` on the approved chunk brief and build the operator-selected entering-law slice with `python tools/build_entering_law.py`; the selections identify relevant authority but the tool only copies it. Then: (1) a neutral/model context sees only the Current-Term Neutral Projection, entering-law slice, neutral packet, and Engine rules needed for modeling; it writes `_COMMITMENTS` and commits the freeze; (2) a reconciliation context receives the frozen neutral packet, frozen commitments, and `_COMPARATOR`, but never `_STONE`; it writes `_RECONCILED` and commits the freeze; (3) an assembly context receives the frozen reconciled commitments and `_STONE`, plus the opinion-assignment statements in the current and preceding terms' Records that Engine §8 counts, tests final compatibility, writes the Canonical Decision Records, and updates the workspace projections. After assembly, run `python tools/rebuild_ledger.py OT<year>` and `python tools/rebuild_manifest.py OT<year>` (passing any stopped or carried matters explicitly), then update the continuity and neutral projection from the committed Records. Generate the chunk Render Input with `python tools/build_render_input.py`; it copies each Record's Public Projection and derives chunk metadata, and is never separately authored. Run `python tools/check_term.py OT<year>` before considering the chunk committed. A stopped matter is listed with its exact blocker and left open unless a dated terminal event or an expressly authorized continuity closure resolves it.

**"Render October Term <year>, chunk <n>."** A separate task. Read only `render-inputs/OT_<year>CHUNK<n>.md`, `foundation/RENDER_CONTRACT.md`, and the Render form, Holdings writing standard, and Voice sections below. Write `output/OT_<year>CHUNK<n>.md`. Do not open briefs, records, or sources; do not research, revisit votes, change coalitions, or add holdings. If a Render Input lacks something the Contract needs, render the entry as far as it goes and name the exact gap in the operator-facing Workflow Blockers section.

**Closing a term** is staged, one task per pass; see "Close protocol" below. Nothing in `state/` changes until the Commit pass.

The user may also say, in plain words, that the Court issues an order or takes up a lower-court case. Record it the way the Engine requires — an Admitted Source Record, a manifest change, or a Standing State entry — then continue.

## Mode

From OT1993 forward, isolated-context mode is the default for substantive Run stages. The non-Stone modeling context may not receive the historical comparator or Stone material. The historical-reconciliation context may receive the comparator but not Stone. Stone enters only in assembly after reconciled non-Stone commitments are frozen. If tooling genuinely cannot provide fresh scoped contexts, use the Engine's disclosed single-conversation fallback and record that limitation internally; never claim blindness that was not achieved. Public renders never mention mode, prompts, approval history, model names, commits, or operator workflow.

## Research

Follow Engine §14, including its adopted incomplete-and-counterfactual-record procedure. Distinguish unavailable historical documents from follow-up created by simulated orders; use supported published recitations or bounded reconstruction premises within that rule, and identify any remaining material assumption specifically. Neither missing research files nor reconstructed procedure establishes litigant default or the merits. Preserve separate neutral, modeling, reconciliation, assembly, and Render contexts. Sources in order of preference, grouped by what each is actually for — go straight to the group that matches the need rather than working the full list top to bottom:

**Full case record** (petition, both merits briefs, joint appendix, opinion) — nothing else below has the petition or briefs, so start here whenever the record itself, not just the opinion, is in question:

1. The Internet Archive's Records and Briefs collection (`us-supreme-court`; one item per docket, holding the petition, briefs, joint appendix, and opinion as text PDFs). Check this first and check it thoroughly — search its own listing (browsable at https://archive.org/details/us-supreme-court) by docket number and case name, not just by guessing a URL, and read the full text of the petition, both merits briefs, and the joint appendix before concluding a specific fact or transcript citation isn't there; a citation to a lower-numbered page range is often quoted directly in a party's brief even when the joint appendix itself is short. For terms from roughly 1990 through 2006, expect it to hold the overwhelming majority of cases (roughly 95%, by observed experience) with occasional real gaps; from 2007 onward its coverage is comprehensive. Do not spend disproportionate effort re-searching this collection for the rare genuinely missing case — confirm the docket has no matching item, then move to source 4 for the opinion text itself.

**Full-opinion verification** (confirming exactly what a specific Justice wrote, every writing, every footnote) — these are faster than source 1 for this narrower need and carry no truncation risk, since each serves the opinion as its own page or a bounded single page rather than one huge combined-document fetch; go here directly when the record itself (briefs, JA) isn't in question:

2. Cornell LII and Oyez for opinions and argument audio. Cornell LII's older docket-based URL scheme (`law.cornell.edu/supct/html/<docket-no>.Z<letter>.html`) is especially useful for full-reading verification: each separate writing is served as its own clean page rather than one combined document, keyed by suffix — `ZS` syllabus, `ZO` opinion of the Court, `ZC`/`ZC1`/`ZC2` concurrences in filing order, `ZD`/`ZD1` dissents in filing order. Fetch the syllabus first (`ZS`) to confirm the docket number and get the exact lineup of who filed what, then fetch each writing's own page rather than extracting a range from a single large combined-text fetch — this avoids truncation and off-by-offset uncertainty about which text belongs to which Justice.
3. supreme.justia.com (`supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/<volume>/<page>/`) as a cross-check against sources 1 and 2: the syllabus and every opinion — majority, each concurrence, each dissent — sit on one continuous page, so there's no missing-writing risk, but also no per-Justice URL split, so read the whole page rather than trying to extract a range (the same truncation/offset risk source 2's scheme exists to avoid). It loads fine through an actual rendering browser; a plain automated HTTP fetch is what gets blocked (see below). Its separate "Opinions & Dissents" tab shows Justia's own AI-generated per-Justice summaries, not primary text — the page itself discloses "[s]ome case metadata and case summaries were written with the help of AI, which can produce inaccuracies" — never cite or rely on that tab; read only the actual opinion text block.

**General opinion and judgment-below text** (fill gaps the sources above don't cover, or confirm a lower court's holding):

4. CourtListener and the Caselaw Access Project (courtlistener.com search and API; static.case.law) — the judgment below in every case, and any earlier opinion in full text, including each Justice's date-eligible opinions and joins.
5. openjurist.org (`openjurist.org/<volume>/<reporter>/<page>`, e.g. `openjurist.org/514/us/549`) — a good supplement to source 4 for full opinion text at both the Supreme Court and lower-court level, with useful citation-network context (later decisions citing the case, what it relies on) alongside the opinion itself.
6. loc.gov — the United States Reports; and the 1988 edition of the United States Code for statutory text as it stood in 1991.
7. supremecourt.gov — opinions, orders, Journals, transcripts. Strongest from 2007 onward.

**Statutory and legislative text:**

8. govinfo.gov — the 1994 edition of the United States Code (the closest full edition after 1991), the Statutes at Large, and the Federal Register from 1994; congress.gov for legislative history.

**Last resort, for a narrow statutory gap nothing above fills:**

9. HathiTrust (babel.hathitrust.org) only as a last resort, for a state code or session law whose exact 1991 text matters and is not quoted in the opinion or briefs.

Do not rely on Justia alone, and never through a plain automated HTTP fetch (it blocks those) — only through an actual rendering browser, per source 3 above. Do not rely on any aggregator alone. Send a browser User-Agent when a site refuses the default one. Download a PDF and read its text in full; do not skim the first pages and infer the rest. Scholarship and commentary are context only, never authority, and anything written after the event's date is not in-world. Record the research cutoff in every Decision Record.

## Render form

Use the Render Contract's adaptive presentation within the existing render structure. Choose the form from the supplied decision's complexity and material distinctions, without consulting the historical comparator or publishing a "Render form: ..." annotation.

- For merits entries, keep a visible **Judgment** block with the disposition, count, and named **Supporting** and **Opposing** Justices as applicable under the Contract, preserving any supplied limit on public disclosure. A single disposition may use a compact block even at 5-4. Use component judgment tables for mixed dispositions, and opinion topology tables for partial joins, judgment-only agreement, fractures, and any other relationship a concise statement would leave unclear. Certiorari and other procedural actions retain the Contract's Public Action forms; do not expose or infer an undisclosed tally or lineup.
- Preserve every supplied substantive element: argued and decided dates, route to the Court, questions, vote by component with names, every writing with author and joins, separate positions, limits and questions not reached, precedent treatment and resulting law, and mandate, remedy, and stage. A common coalition may be stated once when its scope is clear; do not repeat an identical roster for every holding.
- Keep holdings and their reasoning, treatment of earlier precedent, and resulting law as distinct jobs in the existing structure. Avoid restating the same explanation in each section, but retain every material rule, change in a precedent's force, and consequence for the law after the decision.
- Selected separate-writing summaries must explain the writing's substantive grounds, its disagreement or qualification, and the components and joins to which they apply. The Record supplies that substance; the render projects it and adds no ground the Render Input lacks. Merely noting that a Justice concurred or dissented is not a substantive summary.
- Renders publish only what the Court publishes: dispositions, opinions, and noted statements. Never the conference or certiorari poll.
- **No workflow metadata in a render.** The public render carries no version numbers, commit hashes, lineage, correction labels, modern research-retrieval dates, approval language, model names, or words such as "simulated" or "user-directed" when describing the Court's own law or action. A corrected entry reads exactly as the Court's decision of its date. Provenance lives only in the internal record, freeze/audit artifacts, and repository history. Source Notes contain only public-source support and legally relevant source limits. A Workflow Blockers section appears only if a blocker is still open; otherwise omit it.

## Records

Every Canonical Decision Record begins with these exact four labels, each on its own line: `**Case and dockets:**`, `**Event and date:**` (including `YYYY-MM-DD`), `**Result:**`, and `**Version / lineage:**`; then the substantive record. It carries an adaptive audit annex: each non-Stone Justice's frozen commitment and ground, historical-comparator reconciliation, and any departure with a concrete Justice-specific changed premise. For each material departure ask: what legal, factual, procedural, remedial, record, or prior-simulated premise differs from the premise supporting the historical position, and why could that difference matter to this Justice? If no such difference is supportable, history remains strong evidence and the departure requires further research or correction. This is a qualitative legal inquiry, never a script-generated yes/no rule. When a component materially departs from history, include one natural-language `**Historical departure:**` line in the audit annex stating the changed premise and Justice-specific basis. When a decision expressly displaces a predicate of a later historical matter, include one `**Consequence:**` line identifying that predicate and matter. These labels feed generated close indexes only; they do not decide substance. End each Record with a bounded `## Public Projection` containing the eleven Render blocks only, in the Contract's exact order; tooling copies that section to `render-inputs/` without altering substance.

**Completeness checks** (added September 15, 2026, from the user’s Casey version 1.1 handoff; they bind every record, Render Input, and render from now on, and later briefs): state every material exception, qualification, alternative, and triggering condition the analysis uses, in its own terms; never write “the specified exception” or “the stated qualification” unless the same entry supplies the terms; keep permission and obligation distinct (“may be omitted” never becomes “must be omitted”); distinguish a statutory command from a lower-court finding about how it operates; keep distinct provisions distinct (an exception in one section is not the exception in another); trace every disposition to its stated legal ground and its remedy; if a material qualification is genuinely unresolved, name the omission and the source needed rather than filling it by inference.

## Holdings writing standard

The render can only project what the record holds, so write the law of the decision at full depth in the record's controlling-holding section and carry it into the Render Input's Holdings block unchanged. For every controlling proposition and every independently sufficient alternative holding:

- **Holding and operative rule.** The complete rule — trigger, what is required or forbidden, the legal consequence, any operative qualification — in one or two sentences a later court could apply without reading anything else.
- **Authority.** The writing, the Justices who join it at that level of generality, and why it controls. One common coalition declaration may cover multiple holdings if its scope is explicit; identify any different or narrower joins separately.
- **Controlling explanation.** An explanation in the Court's voice, within the applicable ceiling in Render Contract §2, rule 10, containing only reasoning the controlling coalition adopted: the precedents relied on and what each supplies; the application to this record; why the principal contrary argument fails; what the Court expressly leaves open. No dicta, no Stone-only reasoning, no later law.
- **Precedent treatment.** One line per earlier decision the holding materially relies on, distinguishes, limits, extends, or overrules: the treatment in ordinary words and its consequence for that precedent's present force.

Follow the Contract's presentation rules and retain all four substantive elements whether they appear in a labeled block or in prose. Length follows the reasoning the decision requires, with no minimum; a routine application still gets an explanation but may need less space than a rule change. Do not pad or repeat an explanation merely to fill a section. Keep the distinct precedent-treatment and resulting-law work required by the Contract.

## Voice

Write the render the way the Court writes for the public, not the way a treatise writes for specialists: plain declarative sentences; the rule first in ordinary words, then its precise legal formulation; every cited precedent followed by a clause saying what it supplies here; terms of art only when needed, explained on first use; no Latin where English will do; the Court's voice ("The Court holds ..."), never "the model" or "the simulation." A careful non-lawyer should be able to follow every step; a lawyer should find nothing imprecise.

## Special consideration matters

The user may designate a matter for special consideration. Its Run and Render follow every rule above and these additional ones:

- **Statute in terms.** The neutral packet quotes each challenged provision from the statute’s own text, with every exception and qualification, from the source file the user supplies or the official text. A brief’s paraphrase is not enough.
- **Enhanced record for every Justice on every component.** The Engine’s enhanced issue-specific comparison (§7) is mandatory throughout: for each non-Stone Justice and each separable component, the two-path adversarial test, the strongest counterargument, the join barriers, and the narrower or broader positions, each resting first on that Justice’s own date-eligible writings and simulated positions earlier in the term, with the historical comparator used only to confirm or to record a departure. Cite the writing each row rests on.
- **Explicit join test.** After Stone’s position enters, record for every part of every opinion each Justice’s join or refusal, the recorded objection, any revision answering it with the basis and scope of the resulting join, and any remaining disagreement (§8). Assignment follows §8 and is stated.
- **Separate writings at depth.** Each published separate writing receives a substantive summary in the record’s continuity section and in the Render Input: framework, each component, authorities, and the published grounds of disagreement or withheld assent. Apply the designated separate-writing ceiling in Render Contract §2, rule 10, with no minimum; preserve the richer coverage the designation requires.
- **Extended holdings.** The user names the holdings that carry the decision’s doctrine; their controlling explanations receive extended treatment within the designated ceiling in Render Contract §2, rule 10, with no minimum. All other holdings use the ordinary ceiling in that rule. One dedicated holding states the standard lower courts apply after the decision and the controlling status of each component.
- **Reconciliation checks.** Every vote count and join in the opinion topology reconciles to the judgment on each component, whether presented in a compact block or a table; every Marks claim shows both rationales and why one is a logical subset of the other; no historical authorship or joinder fact is imported.
- **Coalition and vote audit.** The record’s audit annex carries a vote audit: before-and-after vote tables naming the Justices for every controlling proposition and every provision; for each proposed new join, the Justice’s recorded objection, the revised reasoning that answers it, and the exact scope of agreement (complete-framework support, support for particular protections, or judgment-only agreement); an explanation for every changed vote; and the author and joins of every controlling portion. Where the user asks what would follow if a proposal obtained five votes, the annex adds a clearly labeled hypothetical successful-adoption scenario that names the additional judicial choices it assumes and is kept apart from the support the record establishes; nothing from that scenario enters the decision, the Render Input, the render, or current law.
- **Render depth.** The Render carries the substantive separate-writing summaries and extended holdings the Render Input supplies, within the Contract's ceilings. The **Event** block of the Record's Public Projection carries one nonprinting line, `Length designation:`, naming the extended holdings, the designated separate-writing summaries, and any case-specific ceiling the user set, so the Render Input conveys the designation without another step. Apply the Contract's form and table rules; designation alone does not require a redundant table or repeated coalition roster.

## Close protocol

Term close runs only after every manifest item is completed, corrected, or expressly carried forward. It is staged so that no single context must hold the whole term; each pass is its own task, committed before the next. Records control; renders are cross-checks only. Do not change any adjudication during close — a defect goes back to a correction record.

1. **"Close October Term <year>, Holdings pass <k>."** Passes cover chunks in order (1–5, 6–10, 11–14). Each reads the records for its chunks and appends their controlling propositions to `terms/OT<year>/close/HOLDINGS.candidate.md` under the Holdings instructions: area-first organization, natural authority anchors, the entry form, current force, limits, operative remedy. A correction record is placed by its effective date. Nothing noncontrolling enters.
2. **"Close October Term <year>, Standards and Tests pass."** Derive `close/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.candidate.md` independently from the term-opening register and the records — never from the Holdings candidate — under the Standards and Tests instructions. Reconcile navigation references to the Holdings candidate only after the substance is complete.
3. **"Close October Term <year>, Standing State pass."** Standing State is the Court's setting, not a running record. The next-term Standing State contains only: the header; the Current Court (roster and seniority at the next term's opening, from the Composition); the circuit allotments; the Court's standing practices already recorded (the referral practice); and a docket list of cases brought up — the user's lower-court additions and any matter the Court itself left open at term's end, one line each. No noncontrolling-positions register, no dependency, filing-condition, or history sections, whatever the template's later sections describe. A Justice's simulated prior-term positions are consulted from that term's public renders in `output/`, not from Standing State.
4. **"Audit October Term <year>."** Run `python tools/check_term.py OT<year>` first, then the remaining deterministic checks: inventory-to-record-to-render reconciliation; effective-date order; participation/vote/join arithmetic; runtime-split freshness; Public Projection/Render Input identity; link and anchor resolution; commit-hash existence; candidate cleanup; and open-matter carry-forward. Only after those pass does a fresh AI audit review legal substance: votes, coalitions, holdings, precedent treatment, remedies, continuity, and Justice-specific historical departures. Write or replace only `close/AUDIT.md`. Provenance review verifies concise lineage claims against Git but does not require narrative commit archaeology.
5. **"Commit October Term <year> close."** Only when the audit lists no unresolved discrepancy. Write `close/TERM_CLOSE_DOSSIER.md`; publish the three synchronized candidates to `state/`; then run `python tools/holdings_volumes.py split` and `python tools/holdings_volumes.py check` so the doctrinal Holdings volumes and continuous compatibility view match. Run `python tools/build_close_indexes.py OT<year>` to generate `INDEX.md`, `close/SEPARATE_WRITINGS.md`, `close/DEPARTURES.md`, and `close/CONSEQUENCES.md`. Verify publication, then delete candidate files and temporary pass notes from `close/`. Repository history and the dossier preserve superseded bytes. The generated indexes are navigation and audit aids, never sources of law.

## Rules that never bend

1. Stone's approved position controls Stone. The Engine may not fill a missing Stone choice; stop the matter and say exactly what is missing.
2. A completed adjudicative entry is never silently rewritten. A correction the user authorizes replaces the superseded entry in place — in its record file, in its chunk’s Render Input, and in its chunk’s render. The internal record names what it corrects and carries the lineage line (versions and commit hashes); the Public Projection, Render Input, and public render carry only the corrected public substance, without correction labels or lineage. Every earlier version stays in repository history. During staged close, candidate trackers and `close/AUDIT.md` are replaceable working artifacts so the directory contains only the current versions. After Commit, candidates and temporary pass notes are deleted and one final `AUDIT.md` remains. The Term-Close Dossier and generated close indexes remain. (Convention changed September 15, 2026 at the user’s direction; the Holmes, Suter/Montana, Alaska and Harris corrections predate it and remain as separate files.)
3. Do not invent quotations, votes, dockets, dates, findings, concessions, or sources.
4. Only controlling law changes current doctrine. A render never changes substance.
5. No scores, dashboards, ideology labels, win-loss framing, or predictions. No fictional conference dialogue.
6. Use the shell to read files, fetch sources, and check arithmetic, chronology, and cross-references. Tools check consistency; they never decide legal meaning.
7. If anything needed to continue is missing or in conflict, stop the affected matter, name the exact blocker, and finish everything else.
